So I fell down a rabbit hole this week reading old arguments about the Axiom of Choice vs the Axiom of Determinacy, the kind of thing that pops up on math forums where people argue about which axiom 'reality' actually satisfies. AD supposedly patches that (every set of reals becomes measurable, no more sphere-doubling nonsense) but apparently breaks other things mathematicians care about too, like a well-ordering of the reals or certain compactness results people rely on elsewhere.

Can you write me a dialogue between two set theorists at a whiteboard, one a committed AC-defender and the other an AD-advocate, where they actually engage with each others strongest points instead of just talking past each other? I want the AC person to defend the well-ordering/Zorn's-lemma intuitions and push back on this "measurability at any cost" framing, and I want the AD person to press hard on how genuinely bizarre Banach-Tarski is and question whether "it's useful for algebra" is actually evidence that an axiom is true rather than just convenient. Bonus points if they eventually get into whether this whole dispute is really about truth at all, versus just which axiom system is more fruitful for the kind of math you personally care about.
