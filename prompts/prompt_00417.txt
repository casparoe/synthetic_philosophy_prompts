As a retired physicist who has recently been reading about the foundations of mathematics, I've been puzzling over the Banach-Tarski paradox. It seems to show that the axiom of choice lets you take a solid ball, cut it into a handful of pieces, and reassemble them into two balls identical to the original — which looks like a flat violation of conservation of mass, one of the most secure principles in all of physics. Given this, why hasn't the axiom of choice simply been discarded as empirically falsified, the way we would reject any physical hypothesis that predicted matter could be duplicated out of nothing?
