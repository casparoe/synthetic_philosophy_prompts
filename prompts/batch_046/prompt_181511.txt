People in my seminar keep comparing the end of slavery or dueling to a bank run on honor — once elites stop treating the code as social currency, the whole norm collapses without anyone actually arguing about morality. They lean on this to push the Williams/Appiah line that prestige and hidden interests do the work, not reasoning. Can you lay out the correspondance: what's the bank, the deposit, the panic, the reserve? Then where it breaks, especally with Appiah's honor-world framing and Williams's claim that economic motives hide behind moral talk. Does the analogy smuggle in that norms are just coordination games? And does the "arguments don't matter" conclusion survive once the disanologies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the whole load?
