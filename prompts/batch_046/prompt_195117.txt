Rank the following five explanatory strategies for the emergence of collective linguistic normativity in a small-scale community according to two dimensions: first, tier them (S, A, B, C, F) by how well they operate as Dennett-style cranes — building the phenomenon from simpler, independently motivated parts without invoking an unexplained skyhook; second, assign each a greedy-reductionism vulnerability score from 0 (no risk of dissolving the target phenomenon) to 10 (the explanation eliminates what it explains). The strategies are: (i) an evolutionary-psychology module account that derives norm-following from fitness-maximizing cooperation heuristics; (ii) a cultural-evolutionary crane using iterated learning and prestige bias, with no dedicated language faculty assumed; (iii) a rational-choice equilibrium reconstruction treating norms as self-enforcing conventions in repeated interaction; (iv) a phenomenological account that treats normativity as irreducibly intersubjective, requiring a non-reductive horizon of meaning; (v) a hierarchical predictive-coding model that explains normative judgment as cortical error-minimization.

For each strategy, justify its tier and its score in one sentence that cites the specific mechanism or explanatory commitment at stake, not merely the philosophical school. Then identify the pair of strategies that a reader is most likely to mis-rank — specifically, the pair where one is wrongly treated as greedy reductionism merely because it is mechanistic, while the other is wrongly protected as a crane merely because it sounds holistic — and explain in one sentence why that misclassification occurs.
