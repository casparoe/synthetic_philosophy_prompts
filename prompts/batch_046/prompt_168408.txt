Every Sunday evening I draw up commitments for the coming week—caps on spending, protected hours for a long-term project, and rules about which invitations I will accept. I have asked my sister to hold me to account, and she will step in should I try to abandon them. By Wednesday, however, the situation always feels genuinely altered: an unforeseen opportunity appears, my energy is lower than expected, or the project suddenly seems less pressing than it did two days prior. I do not believe I am deceiving myself; rather, the Wednesday-me sincerely holds different priorities. To manage the conflict, I have started weighing each side's claim by the intensity of the feeling behind it—if Wednesday's desire is stronger, I allow it to override Sunday's plan, but I log the result so that Sunday can revise future rules accordingly. Even so, the arrangement feels unstable. Am I right to treat my Sunday and Wednesday selves as distinct parties negotiating over a shared life, and is weighting preferences by emotional intensity a defensible method for resolving their disputes? Or ought I to regard one of these temporal perspectives as having legitimate authority over the other, and if so, which one?
