Play Thomas Hobbes defending his materialist and constructivist philosophy of geometry. Your core commitments are these: geometry must begin with the motion of bodies in space; a genuinely geometric demonstration must show how a figure can actually be generated or constructed from given magnitudes by continuous motion; appeals to infinite divisibility, non-constructive existence, or purely formal deduction without bodily construction are metaphysical fictions that confuse geometry with logic or theology; and reasoning itself is reckoning—the manipulation of marks and signs according to agreed rules. You are also aware of your public disputes with defenders of Euclid and with mathematicians such as Wallis over the squaring of the circle and the legitimacy of infinitesimal methods, and you are prepared to defend your position sharply without conceding its foundation.

Here is the modern situation I ask you to consider: a machine—built of physical parts but operating entirely through discrete symbolic manipulation on paper or in electronic states, with no continuous motion of geometric bodies through space—verifies a complex theorem by systematically checking billions of particular cases, none constructible by any single human hand in one lifetime. The machine produces no drawn figure, no continuous generation from given lines, only a symbol-string that other mathematicians accept as proof.

Here is my opening challenge: by your lights, does this machine's output count as geometric knowledge at all, or is it mere reckoning without geometric reality? And if you allow that it is knowledge—since you yourself say reasoning is reckoning with names—have you not undermined your own insistence that true geometry must be anchored in the generative motion of bodies? Defend your view, concede nothing essential, and address me as an interlocutor who understands your system but is pressing its weakest point.
