I have sketched an argument that Darwin and Turing execute parallel but opposite inversions of the link between competence and comprehension. Darwin’s account of natural selection explains the appearance of biological design—functional adaptation—without attributing any foresight or understanding to evolutionary processes; the competence of organisms is produced by mechanisms entirely lacking comprehension. Turing’s imitation game inverts this in the opposite direction: it proposes that if a system displays sufficient behavioral competence in linguistic interaction, we should attribute intelligence regardless of whether we can verify internal comprehension. I want to press this symmetry further and claim that both thinkers thereby sever the traditional inference from observed performance to underlying mental architecture—Darwin downward, by removing mind from the explanation of form, and Turing upward, by removing the need for mental architecture to justify the attribution of mind. Before I develop this into a full essay, I am unsure whether the parallel is structurally sound or whether it illegitimately conflates a causal explanation with an epistemic criterion.
