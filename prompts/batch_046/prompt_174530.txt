Question: State the causal exclusion argument against non-reductive physicalism, and assess whether appealing to token-identity of mental and physical events, together with a principle of downward causation, successfully answers it.

Student answer:
The causal exclusion argument, formulated by Jaegwon Kim, begins from the causal closure of physics: every physical event has a complete physical cause. Non-reductive physicalists hold that mental properties—like beliefs and intentions—are real and not reducible to brain states, yet they also accept closure. So when I raise my arm, neurons provide a full causal explanation. If my intention also causes the movement, we have two sufficient causes for one effect, which Kim treats as unacceptable overdetermination. The mental cause is therefore excluded.

One response is token-identity: each mental event is identical to some physical event, so there are not two events competing. Davidson's anomalous monism uses this idea—mental events are physical events described differently, and because mental descriptions are anomalous, they do not enter into causal laws that conflict with physics. This avoids exclusion because there is only one causal chain, just described in mental and physical vocabulary.

But Kim points out that token-identity does not solve the problem at the property level. Even if the event is one, the mental property and the physical property are still distinct. So downward causation is added: the mental property of the event exerts causal influence downward onto the physical level. This means the mental property genuinely contributes to the effect, not redundantly, because it shapes how the physical properties operate. Since the event is token-identical, closure is technically preserved—physics describes the event fully—while mental properties still do causal work through downward influence.

However, downward causation seems to violate closure in practice, because if a mental property determines part of the physical outcome, then the physical cause was not sufficient on its own. So the combination of token-identity and downward causation does not really save non-reductive physicalism; it either collapses into property-reduction or abandons closure.
