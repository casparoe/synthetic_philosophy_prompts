I am working through my position on sphexishness, mechanical rigidity, and free will, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. Here are my commitments, labeled (1) through (6):

(1) Sphexishness is a structural property of behavioral execution—context-blind, invariant repetition of a script regardless of feedback that should rationally alter it—not reducible to determinism, simplicity, or absence of consciousness.

(2) Free will consists in context-sensitive flexibility: the capacity to revise one's action-plan in light of reasons. It does not require causal indeterminacy or some special form of self-causation.

(3) Determinism is therefore compatible with free will, because determinism governs the causal origin of an action, not whether the causal architecture permits branching, reason-responsive pathways.

(4) First-order sphexishness—rigid execution of particular tasks—is compatible with free will, provided second-order flexibility remains intact: the agent can still revise or suspend the task-rules themselves.

(5) Total second-order sphexishness—complete inability to revise one's own revision-rules—eliminates free will and moral responsibility entirely, even if the agent originally authored those rules during a brief prior window of flexibility.

(6) In actual humans, however, that prior second-order flexibility is rare, fleeting, and causally overdetermined by stress responses, social priming, or neurochemical states; it is therefore too fragile and isolated to ground robust moral responsibility for subsequent first-order sphexish habits.
