Second, I think large-scale social planning should be conducted by technical experts using predictive models and formal decision theory, with ideological and metaphysical commitments explicitly bracketed, because only empirically assessable claims should enter the planning apparatus. Third, I hold that democratic legitimacy requires affected populations to retain a veto over planning outcomes that violate their core values, even when those values cannot be translated into the planner's formal framework and even when the veto conflicts with technically optimal strategies. Fourth, I believe value disagreements are ultimately non-cognitive—expressions of practical attitude rather than propositions capable of rational resolution—though they can be managed through procedural negotiation. Fifth, I want political discourse reconstructed so that pseudo-questions, which depend on metaphysical commitments violating the principle of tolerance, are eliminated, while genuinely practical disagreements are preserved and clarified. Sixth, I reject both the idea that there is one uniquely rational plan that all reasonable agents must converge on, and the idea that pure majoritarian preference should determine outcomes, since both ignore the distinction between cognitive justification and practical choice.
