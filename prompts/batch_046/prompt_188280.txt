I'm trying to figure out where I actually stand on methodological individualism vs holism, going by my gut on cases. Explaining why my office switched to that weird project-management software: the individual managers each had their own incentives, but the firm also had a culture that made them all lean the same direction — I think you need both levels and its not reducible downward. Explaining the fall of the Roman Republic: individual senators made choices, but the structural pressure from slave-based latifundia made those choices predictable — I feel okay citing the structural pressure without listing every senator's motives. Explaining why that protest turned violent last Tuesday: I think you have to go individual, because the structural conditions were present for months but only this crowd flipped — a holistic explanation feels like hand-waving here. Explaining the spread of double-entry bookkeeping in 14th-century Italy: the trade networks and institutions shaped it, but individual merchants invented the techniques — I think holism is okay for the spread, individualism for the invention, but I'm not sure if thats consistent. Explaining why my friend quit smoking: purely individual psychology, no social structure needed. But then there's pension reform that got stuck not because of any current actor's preference, but because of legacy contracts signed decades ago — that feels holistic, but also feels like just pushing the individual explanations back in time. And there's the weird case of open-source license legacy: the GPL choice made in 1991 constrains current contributor behavior without any present agent enforcing it — is that holism or just delayed individualism? Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close, and what does it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on — explaining the 2008 crisis as "systemic risk culture" vs tracing it through specific trader incentives?
