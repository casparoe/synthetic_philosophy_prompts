I have recently begun to think seriously about whether to have a second child, and what was once an abstract philosophical puzzle—the ethics of creating people—has become genuinely personal. I am trying to determine what I actually believe about population axiology by examining my reactions to concrete cases, in the hope that a principle might emerge.

Here are my verdicts. A world of ten billion people living lives barely worth living strikes me as worse than a world of one billion people living flourishing lives; yet I am unsure whether it is worse than an empty world. If I must choose between creating one child with welfare level 50, or creating two children each at level 30, I prefer the latter; but if the choice is between one child at 50 and ten children each at 6, I prefer the former, despite the greater total welfare in the latter case. A world containing ten billion people at welfare 100 and a single individual suffering at level -100 seems worse to me than the world without that sufferer, yet I hesitate to say that preventing that one suffering justifies preventing the ten billion good lives—unless the suffering is extreme enough to dominate the sum. I also find myself resistant to a policy that avoids a minor harm to existing people by forgoing the creation of a very large number of people who would have lived clearly good lives; but if those potential lives would have been only barely above neutrality, the resistance fades. There is one invented case that I keep returning to: a population of "stroboscopic" beings, each of whom experiences intense well-being only in brief flashes separated by long unconscious intervals, such that the total summed welfare equals that of a smaller, continuously conscious population. Even with identical totals, the vast stroboscopic population feels less valuable to me than the smaller continuous one.

Is there a coherent principle—or a small family of principles—that accommodates all of these judgments? If so, please state it precisely, indicate which clause handles which case, and say what it predicts about a scenario I have not considered: a world containing infinitely many people, each with welfare exactly zero. If no single principle captures them all without contortion, please identify the verdict that is most likely the outlier and explain what a simpler axiology would say about the stroboscopic case.
