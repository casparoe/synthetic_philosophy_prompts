I'm serving as the senior grader for a graduate examination in philosophy of cognitive science. Our rubric requires distinguishing "stimulus poverty"—the empirical scarcity and noise of the linguistic input a child actually receives—from "inductive poverty," the logical underdetermination of any universal grammar by a finite sample of sentences, however large or clean that sample might be. With that distinction firmly in mind, please evaluate the following exam response.

Question presented to the student: "Explain Chomsky's poverty-of-the-stimulus argument for innate grammatical knowledge. In your answer, address (a) how children acquire hierarchical, structure-dependent rules for phrase formation without explicit pedagogical guidance, and (b) the problem of negative evidence in learning grammatical constraints. Conclude by stating whether a successful argument establishes only domain-specific linguistic knowledge or also entails that the language faculty is informationally encapsulated in Fodor's sense."

The student's answer:

"Chomsky's argument is that children learn grammar despite an input environment that is too sparse and degraded to support inductive learning. For hierarchical phrase structure, children master rules like subject-auxiliary inversion in embedded questions even though caregivers almost never model transformations inside nested clauses, and children receive no explicit instruction about tree-structured representations. This demonstrates stimulus poverty: the environment simply does not contain enough relevant data.

On negative evidence, children reliably avoid ungrammatical constructions—such as agreement violations in deeply embedded contexts—despite the fact that parents rarely correct their syntactic mistakes. Because the absence of correction cannot explain why children reject these forms, they must already possess innate constraints that rule them out.

If the argument succeeds, it proves that grammar is innate and specific to the language domain. But it also establishes Fodor's modularity thesis. Only an informationally encapsulated module could acquire such precise structural rules without recruiting general intelligence, analogical reasoning, or world knowledge. So domain-specific innateness and full encapsulation come together in the same faculty."

Your task: Assign a specific letter grade or percentage score, and provide point-by-point comments that justify it. Specifically, indicate where the student correctly identifies the negative-evidence problem; where they conflate stimulus poverty with inductive poverty by treating empirical data scarcity rather than logical underdetermination as the core issue; where they mischaracterize the hierarchical-structure example; and where they slide illegitimately from domain-specific innateness to full Fodorian modularity without an intermediate argument. Do not offer a general impression; assess each component separately, and frame your evaluation using the stimulus-poverty versus inductive-poverty distinction where relevant.
