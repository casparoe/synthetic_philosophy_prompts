In AI safety and alignment, there has been a persistent debate about whether avoiding side effects can be formalized without committing to a complete specification of human values. Proponents of reachability-based and counterfactual-baseline methods have argued that one can define an agent's impact purely in terms of state-space dynamics—measuring which future states become accessible or inaccessible relative to a counterfactual trajectory in which the agent takes no action—without encoding any preference over which states are desirable. Critics respond that selecting the state-space representation, the distance metric, and especially the counterfactual baseline already requires normative judgments about which features of the world are worth preserving, so that value-neutral impact measures merely hide value commitments in technical parameters. This exchange has repeated for years with remarkably little convergence: formalists produce new metrics, critics point out hidden normative premises in the metric's construction, and both sides claim the other has missed the point. My working hypothesis is that this is not a single disagreement but several layered ones that share vocabulary: one about whether causation can be separated from evaluation in physical dynamics, one about whether a baseline is a descriptive counterfactual or a normative benchmark, and one about whether low impact is a coherent single desideratum or a conflation of distinct goals—preserving autonomy, preserving option value, and minimizing physical change. Is that right? Diagnose the stall concretely: is there a hidden empirical or formal question—for instance, whether every possible baseline selection rule implicitly orders states by some value criterion—that could resolve it? Or is the disagreement verbal, turning on incompatible uses of value-neutral? Or are the sides answering different questions, with formalists addressing algorithmic feasibility and critics addressing normative justification? I want a diagnosis that engages with the actual structure of the arguments rather than a survey of proposals, and I want an honest assessment of what progress would look like: a theorem showing that impact metrics entail preference orderings, a conceptual distinction that disentangles causal footprint from normative significance, a demonstration that the debate conflates multiple independent problems, or an argument that the impasse is unavoidable because the concept of side effect is inherently indexical to a value framework.
