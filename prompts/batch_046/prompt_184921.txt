Write an essay on the controversy over whether truth commissions that exchange amnesty for full disclosure—think South Africa's TRC—do more harm than good to the historical record. Contrast that with Argentina's annulled amnesty laws and Rwanda's gacaca courts, but don't just compare institutions; I want you to argue whether the disclosure-for-immunity bargain creates what we might call epistemic amnesty (where perpetrators recieve legal silence in exchange for partial truth) or whether it produces a kind of performative accountability that criminal trials never reach. Reach a real conclusion—does linking amnesty to testimony serve justice, or does it corrupt both justice and memory? Lead with the bottom line up front. Keep it under 800 words max, and don't bother with citations or a bibliography; just reason it through. There's probably a typo or two in this prompt, just roll with it.
