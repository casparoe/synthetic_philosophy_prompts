I'm serving on the governance committee of an open-source foundation that currently elects its five-person technical steering board by approval vote. A growing faction wants to replace elections with random selection: anyone who passes a technical skills audit enters a pool, and five are drawn by lot to serve a term. They argue that in a technically homogeneous community, voters systematically overweight charisma and recent visibility, so a randomly selected board from the qualified pool will make collectively better decisions than an elected one. Opponents counter that removing elections eliminates democratic accountability—there is no way to vote out a poor performer—and that replacing representation with a bureaucratic lottery violates the principle that governed people should choose their governors, even if the pool is pre-filtered for competence. I am genuinely unsure how to weigh these claims. Is there a coherent philosophical framework for deciding between election and lot-based selection when every candidate in the pool is demonstrably capable, or am I just conflating procedural familiarity with legitimate authority?
