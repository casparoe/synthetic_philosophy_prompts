Here is my opening challenge. Consider two neighboring regions, A and B. Both regions report that their core commitments are now better served—A protects more sacred land than it could have protected alone, and B secures a development pathway that carries social legitimacy rather than coercive force. According to this hypothesis, neither side adopts the other's metric of value; instead, they construct a temporary "exchange grammar" that allows each to register a net gain strictly within its own framework. I take this to show that moral compromise can yield real, non-illusory benefits for both parties without reducing incommensurable values to a single common denominator. How, from your position, do you account for both sides sincerely believing they have advanced their own values—without dismissing them as merely deluded?
