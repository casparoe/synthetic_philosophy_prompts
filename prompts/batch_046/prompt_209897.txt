Her calls so far: sand heap losing grains one by one, yes; bald guy plucking hairs one by one, yes; tax law with a hard income cutoff at exactly $50k, no; different languages drawing "blue" over different shades, no; biological species with hybrid zones, yes; computer code with a sharp if/else at 100 but rounding errors near the boundry, no. Now she's facing this made-up case: a virtual land registry merges parcels into an "estate" automatically every time the running total hits a round number, but the smart contract never defines "estate" — there's just the math, and users start treating 500 parcels as one estate, then 499, with zero dispute about the numbers but also zero rule in the code. Will she call that vagueness? What's the rule she's actually running — needs continuous physical gradation, needs a missing social convention, or needs a sharp hidden fact we can't access? And what one further case would actually seperate those three?
