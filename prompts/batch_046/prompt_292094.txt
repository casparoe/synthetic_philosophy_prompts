I read an encyclopedia entry summarizing the logical omniscience problem in formal epistemic logic. The entry said that in possible-worlds semantics an agent automatically believes every proposition true in all worlds they consider possible, so belief is closed under logical consequence and real human cognitive limits cannot be represented. It claimed the standard resolution, developed in the 1980s, was to replace semantic models with purely syntactic ones: belief is defined as a set of sentences closed only under explicitly listed inference rules, and since people do not apply rules instantly, omniscience disappears. The entry concluded that the deep philosophical problem was therefore dissolved—what remains is only the technical task of selecting the right inference rules for a rational repertoire, not any normative question about whether rational belief requires logical closure. This sounds suspiciously tidy. Which parts accurately describe the formal problem and its history, which parts conflate syntactic approaches with awareness-based or impossible-worlds responses, and does the claim that the normative issue was settled in the 1980s actually follow? I suspect the question of whether rationality demands closure is still very much alive, but I need help locating exactly where the summary overreaches.
