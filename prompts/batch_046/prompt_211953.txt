I run a grad phil workshop — about 12 people, 50 minutes, one session. I want an in-class activty that makes them feel Dewey's experimentalism about democracy: that democracy is collective inquiry, not preference aggregation. To keep it from being something they can just quote back, frame the core idea as "institutional fallibilism under distributed ignorance" — the group must solve a practical problem where nobody holds the full spec, and the democratic procedure needs mechanisms for revising the very decision-framework, not just picking among pre-set options.

Design a runnable game or simulation: rules, one-page printable materials, a point-based payoff scheme with no real money, and roles arranged so participants run into the failure mode themselves (probably they default to voting and miss they're solving the wrong problem) before anyone names it. Then a debrief mapping specific moments onto Dewey's idea of democracy as social intelligence vs majority rule. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first run and how to handle it, and be honest about which part of Dewey's long-run temporal experimentalism this 50-minute format just can't reproduce and you'll have to supply verbally. Make sure the idea shows up in the rules and incentives, not just in what I say afterwards.
