Construct a thought experiment to the following specifications. Design a realistic, single-paragraph scenario involving a closed agricultural community of fixed size that must choose between two nutritionally equivalent food systems: System B, based on grass-fed beef, and System C, based on grain-fed chicken. Use only stipulated quantitative relationships—such as hectares of pasture, hectares of tilled cropland, numbers of cattle and chickens raised, and counts of indirectly killed wild vertebrates and soil invertebrates—without citing empirical studies or asking for bibliographic support. Engineer the case so that three specified positions deliver three specified verdicts: (1) a direct-harm view that counts only the farmed animals intentionally killed per unit of human nutrition favors beef, because far fewer individual animals are slaughtered; (2) a wild-animal-inclusive consequentialist view that weights suffering of all sentient vertebrates killed indirectly by land use but assigns zero moral weight to soil invertebrates favors chicken, because grain cultivation requires less total land and therefore kills fewer wild rodents and birds than pasture maintenance and its associated predator ecology; and (3) when the scenario injects a stipulated niche condition—the Tillage-Nematode Assumption, which holds that plowing for grain kills large numbers of soil nematodes with non-negligible moral status—the wild-animal view reverses and now favors beef, since pasture avoids tillage altogether. Hold constant human population, total caloric output, and land area across both systems. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that each specification is satisfied: state the precise verdict of each view, identify the comparison method, specify the relevant counterfactual worlds or adjustments, and explain why each view gives the result it does. Then provide an honest analysis of which feature does the work—namely, the interaction between feed-conversion efficiency and the moral status of organisms killed indirectly—explain what happens to the verdicts if the Tillage-Nematode Assumption is removed, and describe how a defender of the chicken-favoring verdict could attack the case without invoking outside sources. Write the entire response in American English.
