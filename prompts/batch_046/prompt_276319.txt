Following a lengthy argument with a friend about diachronic identity, I want to examine a specific objection that keeps recurring to me. On the perdurantist account, I am a four-dimensional worm composed of distinct temporal parts, each existing at only one time. Consequently, the stage that signs a contract on Monday and the stage that performs it on Tuesday are not numerically identical; they stand only in relations of causal and psychological continuity. From this, it seems to follow that no single entity both incurs and discharges the obligation—the Monday-stage generates the duty, yet the Tuesday-stage is literally a different thing that pays. Endurantism avoids this fragmentation because the very same three-dimensional object is wholly present on both days. So unless the perdurantist can demonstrate that obligation can be genuinely borne by a non-identical successor stage without reducing long-term commitment to a mere causal chain, does not perdurantism undermine the coherence of practical commitment across time? I suspect I am conflating numerical identity with what is sometimes called practical identity, but I want a precise diagnosis of exactly where this reasoning goes wrong.
