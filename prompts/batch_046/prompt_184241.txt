The gender-equality paradox—the finding that societies with stronger formal gender equality often exhibit larger gender differences in career and study choices—raises competing conceptual interpretations about nature, culture, and justice. Suppose you are directing a three-year conceptual research program in philosophy and normative theory, not empirical data collection, on what this phenomenon implies. Which open lines of inquiry should take priority?

Compare and rank at least three: first, whether the paradox undermines liberal-egalitarian arguments that gender-neutral opportunity structures will tend to produce balanced outcomes; second, whether one can conceptually separate genuine preference divergence from structurally channeled choice without presupposing the very distinction the paradox is meant to illuminate; and third, whether justice in this domain should be reconceived around something like preference-autonomy rather than distributive equality. For each, defend its place in the ordering: explain its tractability for philosophers rather than social scientists, its dependency relations with the other questions, and what kind of conceptual resolution would cause you to reorder your priorities. Do not propose empirical studies or list sources; deliver a defended agenda for conceptual labor.
