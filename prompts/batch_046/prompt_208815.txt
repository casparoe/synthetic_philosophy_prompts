A colleague dismissed this by saying, "it is wrong to bring suffering into existence, but not wrong to miss bringing pleasure into existence," as if that single line settled the weighing problem without further argument. I suspect the sentence is hiding several distinct philosophical claims. Can you lay out exactly five precise theses it could be expressing—ranging from a tautology about counterfactual harm to a substantive axiology of non-being—say for each whether it is defensible and whether it actually supports dismissing my selection argument? Present the result as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
