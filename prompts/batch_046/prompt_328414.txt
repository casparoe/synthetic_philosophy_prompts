Hey, I'm working on a paper for my philosophy of language seminar and I keep running into this move when people talk about Quine's gavagai stuff in ethics or AI contexts. The pattern goes like this: first they invoke translation indeterminancy to say we can't really know whether some other culture (or an AI system) means 'wrong' or just 'bad-for-us' or whatever - there's no fact of the matter about which manual is right. But then, right after, they pick one specific manual - usually the one that makes the other side look irrational or deficient - and build an argument about what the culture 'really' thinks, as if that interpretation had been defended. I saw it last week in a discussion about cross-cultural moral psychology and again in an AI alignment thread. It's not plain hypocrisy because they never claim determinacy; it's more like they need the ambiguity to block essentialism but need determinacy to do the actual critique. Is there a name for this specific double-move? If not, can you define its boundaries precisely - how it differs from just changing your mind, and whether it's a misapplication of Quine's thesis to normative domains? I don't need citations, just want to know if I'm describing something real or inventing it.
