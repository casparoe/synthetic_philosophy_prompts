The framing question is whether habitat destruction tends to reduce wild animal suffering, but I've come to think that the answer depends critically on a distinction I haven't seen clearly articulated: the difference between standing welfare—the summed experience of all currently living wild animals in a given habitat at a given time—and flow welfare—the summed experience across all lives that will be brought into existence or prevented from being brought into existence over an ecological time-scale. Habitat destruction clearly collapses standing populations, but its effect on flow welfare depends on replacement rates, life-history strategies, and whether the destroyed habitat is replaced by ecosystems with lower reproductive throughput and different welfare profiles.

This raises several open questions that I need to prioritize.
