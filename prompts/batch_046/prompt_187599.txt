Design a 75-minute seminar activity for 14 graduate students in philosophy of mind that makes them experience the conceptual pressure of Molyneux’s problem—not in its familiar cube-and-sphere formulation, but in a constructed variant that strips away linguistic mediation. The setting is a single session with no external materials beyond paper, envelopes, and blindfolds.

The framing should be injected as follows: participants must distinguish between recognition without inference and recognition via covert reasoning in cross-modal matching. Stage a task in which half the group learns three irregular convex and concave forms only through haptic exploration (blindfolded, ten minutes, with explicit rules forbidding naming, counting aloud, or verbal encoding). After removing blindfolds, these same participants must immediately point to the corresponding visual silhouettes among distractors that share identical verbal descriptions but different geometries. The matching phase must be conducted in silence, and a reputation/payoff scheme should reward speed and confidence, creating social pressure to claim that they “just saw” the correspondence. The other half serve as silent observers who record, in real time, any verbal or gestural justification traces they witness.

Include the debrief questions that force participants to examine whether their successful matches relied on spatial inference—such as recalling orientation, counting edges, or comparing relative size—rather than on direct visual-haptic correspondence.
