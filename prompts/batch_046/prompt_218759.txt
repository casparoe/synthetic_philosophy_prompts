I am drafting my philosophical profile on consciousness for a small discussion group, and I would like a rigorous consistency audit before I present it. Please approach this as a professional philosopher of mind with particular expertise in global workspace theory and the access–phenomenal distinction; think carefully, begin with a brief brainstorm of the logical relations among the commitments below, and then proceed to the audit itself.

Here is where I have landed. First, I accept Global Workspace Theory as the best functional account of access consciousness: information becomes conscious in the access sense precisely when it is globally broadcast and made available to multiple cognitive subsystems. Second, I maintain a sharp distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness, holding specifically that phenomenal experience can occur in the absence of global broadcasting—for instance, in what I call “isolated integration pockets”, local neural coalitions that achieve feature-binding without ever entering the workspace. Third, I nevertheless hold that whenever information is globally broadcast within a system, there is necessarily something-it-is-like associated with that state; I deny the possibility of “phenomenally empty” broadcasts. Fourth, I am a physicalist about phenomenal consciousness: phenomenal properties supervene on physical properties, and there is no non-physical ontology. Fifth, I take phenomenal concepts to be conceptually irreducible to physical or functional concepts, but I deny that this generates a metaphysical explanatory gap—the gap, on my view, is purely epistemic and concerns the structure of our conceptual schemes. Sixth, I deny that there is anything it is like to be a thermostat, yet I am genuinely open to the idea that minimal recurrent architectures—say, a simple two-layer feedback loop with adaptive weights—might possess extremely dim, unstructured phenomenal states despite lacking full workspace integration. Seventh, I regard the so-called hard problem as primarily a problem about how our concepts are organised rather than about any deep ontological mystery.

Please audit this package. Which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be in tension but dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision that would restore consistency? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious. For each genuine tension, specify exactly which commitment would have to give and how expensive that revision would be. Be specific about whether my notion of “isolated integration pockets” is doing hidden work that conflicts with my physicalism or with my claim that broadcast necessarily carries phenomenal character, and check whether my openness to minimal architectures having faint phenomenal states undermines my denial about the thermostat in a principled way or merely draws an arbitrary line. I do not know where the problem lies and do not wish to signal which single commitment I would be most willing to abandon; the deliverable should be an ordered audit, not a lecture.
