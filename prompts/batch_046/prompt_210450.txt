I have been working through George Berkeley's writings on money and economic value on my own, without formal training, and I am trying to determine the exact principle he applies when deciding whether an instrument counts as true money or only as a private agreement. As I understand his judgments, he treats minted gold and silver coins as genuine currency; he accepts paper notes issued by a public bank provided they are directly redeemable for land or specie; he rejects direct barter in which goods are swapped with no mediating token or record; he rejects private promissory notes circulated among merchants, even when widely trusted; and he allows that small communal tally-books can serve local exchange, yet he denies they are true money because they lack both a sovereign guarantee and a portable physical form.

What ties these cases together is unclear to me. One possibility is that money must be a tangible token combined with authorization by a public authority. Another is that sovereign backing and legal enforcement are sufficient on their own, regardless of physical form. A third is that the decisive factor is public recognition of the instrument as representing stored labor or goods, with state backing being only one way to secure that recognition.

To test this, I have constructed a hypothetical case. A government creates a system of pure account entries: there are no coins, no paper notes, and no metal reserves. The entries are maintained in a public register kept by sworn clerks, declared legal tender for all debts and taxes, and transferred among citizens by updating the records. Nothing can be held in the hand, but the system is fully backed by the state's promise and enforced by law.

Would Berkeley recognize this system as money? Predict his verdict, lay out the two or three competing rules that best account for his earlier judgments, indicate which rule you think he is actually running and why, and then identify the single further case that would best distinguish those rules from one another. I am not looking for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the reasoning derived directly from the pattern of cases itself.
