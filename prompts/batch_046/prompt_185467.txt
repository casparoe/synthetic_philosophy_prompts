I've come to accept that moral sainthood is asymptotically impossible — not just undesirable like the usual boringness objection, but structurally self-defeating. The idea is that maxing out moral goodness requires stripping away the partial attachments and emotional biases that make moral action recognizably human; past a certain point you're not a saint, you're just an optimization process running on altruism. So there's a ceiling you can't break through without ceasing to be a moral agent in the normal sense. What would count against this? I want the serious defeaters, not just "but some people are really nice." Specifically: could there be a form of maximal goodness that preserves partiality? Does empirical psycholgy show extreme altruists stay emotionally intact? And has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability? Rank them fatal to awkward, and say what a defender of the asymptotic view would concede.
