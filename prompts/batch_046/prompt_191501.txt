She said laïcité works like noise-cancelling headphones: the state actively filters out religious 'noise' in public spaces so citizens can hear each other as bare equals, and the silence isn't emptiness but engineered neutrality. Anglo multiculturalism, she said, is like an audio mixer: the state doesn't mute any track, it just balances volumes so every cultural signal stays audible in the public mix. She's using this to argue that both models are equally respectful of equality, just through different mechanics -- subtraction vs amplification.

But I'm suspicious that the metaphor is carrying too much weight. Can you audit this properly? Give me the correspondence table -- what's the headphones, the noise-canceling algorithm, the listener's ear, the mixer, the tracks, the sound engineer? Then lay out where the mapping actually breaks: does laïcité filter or just set a default volume? Is framing religion as 'noise' smuggling in that it's inherently disruptive to public reason? Does the mixer hide that some tracks might still drown others out, or that the engineer picks which ones exist? And crucially, does her conclusion -- that both are neutral tools serving equality -- survive once those disanalogies are on the table, or was the tech metaphor doing all the normative lifting? Also, if there's a better analogy that captures the republican commitment to a shared civic identity without treating culture like audio interference, I'd like to hear it.
