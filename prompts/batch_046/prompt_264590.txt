i sit on the advisory board for our city's Direct Democracy Pilot — we're deciding wether a randomly-selected citizens panel should get binding authority on zoning and budget disputes, or stay advisory only. board meets tuesday so i need a memo to circulate before then recommending one course with a concrete timeline.

the philosophical problem is right there in the design: critics cite three athenian failure modes. the Mytilene debate, where the assembly voted for mass execution then reversed next day only because a ship was delayed — showing emotional momentum plus procedural haste equals cruelty that moderation can't fix in real time. the Sicilian Expedition, democratic overreach driven by charismatic rhetoric and the illusion that collective desire equals strategic wisdom. and the trial of Socrates, where a democratic jury executed dissent under populist pressure.

proponents say modern panels with trained facilitators avoid all that. i need the memo to take that seriously but recommend we either (a) approve binding authority only if the charter includes a "Mytilene protocol" — any irreversible or punitive decision gets a mandatory 48-hour delay with a written minority dissent report distributed to all members — plus an "Alcibiades check" requiring strategic claims be vetted by an independent technical board and advocates disclose conflicts, plus protected dissent slots for minority voices; or (b) reject binding authority entirely and keep the panel advisory.

pick one. defend it with reasoning that honestly engages the strongest consideration on the losing side — probably that these safeguards paralyze direct democracy and turn it into bureaucracy. write it as a formal memo with clear recommendation and timeline. don't ask for citations or reading lists. avoid referencing obscure modern theorists i wouldn't have memorized; stick to the classical dynamics and the logic of the safeguards.
