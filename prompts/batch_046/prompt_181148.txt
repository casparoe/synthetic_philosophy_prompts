Exam question: Compare Mencius and Xunzi on the origin and development of moral virtue. Specifically: (a) How does each thinker account for the psychological origin of moral failure? (b) What role does ritual (li) play in moral development for each—corrective, constitutive, protective, or some combination? (c) Be precise about the metaphysical status of the four sprouts (si duan) in Mencius and the relation between human nature (xing) and artifice (wei) in Xunzi.

Student answer: Both thinkers begin from the observation that human beings naturally pursue their own interests, but they diverge sharply on whether this tendency exhausts human nature. For Mencius, nature is fundamentally good: the four sprouts—compassion, shame and dislike, courtesy and deference, and the sense of right and wrong—are already present as complete moral capacities within every person. Moral failure therefore arises primarily from external deprivation: famine, bad governance, or social disorder suppress these innate virtues and prevent them from reaching maturity. Ritual plays a largely protective function for Mencius; it creates stable conditions so that the sprouts can unfold spontaneously without corruption.

Xunzi takes the opposite view. He holds that human nature contains no innate moral orientation whatsoever—only raw appetites for profit, sensory gratification, and competitive advantage. Left to themselves, these appetites inevitably produce social conflict. Ritual was invented by the ancient sages precisely as an external mechanism of restraint: it imposes order on a nature that is permanently opposed to it. Consequently, the relation between xing and wei is fundamentally antagonistic. Moral development under Xunzi's scheme is not the fulfillment of an internal potential but the sustained suppression of one's original desires through disciplined adherence to social rules.

In short, Mencius treats morality as an internal reality that needs shelter, whereas Xunzi treats it as an artificial construction that must dominate nature from without.
