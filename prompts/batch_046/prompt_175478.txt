I want a dialogue between two philosophers — call them R and S — about whether self-deception is even possible, but not the usual split-mind replay or the Freudian unconscious stuff we've all seen a million times. Instead, I want them fighting over this specific angle: R thinks self-deception needs what she calls epistemic partitioning — not literal split minds, but a sustained functional separation between a belief and the evidence that'd kill it, kept alive by active attentional control. S thinks that's just motivated reasoning wearing a costume, because real deception needs an intentional deceiver who knows the truth and hides it; in one unified agent that collapses into straight contradiction, or at least into plain old irrationality.

Have them actually talk to eachother. R should say intentionality can be spread across time and that knowing the truth in one register while believing its opposite in another isnt contradictory if the registers dont interact. S should hit back that she's just redefining weakness of will about belief to save the phenomenon, and ask what conceptual work partitioning does that motivated irrationality doesnt cover. They should also touch on whether there's even a real explanandum here — maybe it's all just belief-formation gone wrong with fancy labeling.

Both should be equally sharp, no strawmen. Real back-and-forth, not talking past eachother.
