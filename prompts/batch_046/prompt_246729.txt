Been thinking about that meat-eating vs wild animal suffering debate that keeps cycling through EA forums. One side points to all the field mice and birds killed by crop farming and pesticides, the other says beef is worse because of feed conversion ratios and pasture land use. The numbers get thrown around but nobody updates much, and I feel like we're talking past eachother.

My working theory is that it's not actually an empirical stall — it's that people are answering two different questions under one heading. One question is about total ecological footprint per calorie; the other is about marginal consumer effect, which depends on agricultural elasticity and is totally different for beef vs grains. There's also this hidden move about whether displacement harm through land use counts the same as direct harm, and whether replaceability of farmed animals breaks the causal chain.

So is this a substantive disagreement about facts, a verbal mess about what causing suffering means, or two groups answering different questions? And if it's the framing or the causal concept that's broken, what would actually count as progress — a better elasticity model, a distinction between direct and displacement harm, or should we just drop the per-calorie comparison entirely?
