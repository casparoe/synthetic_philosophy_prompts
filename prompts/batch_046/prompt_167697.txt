In a working group on psychiatric taxonomy I have been observing a debate that has stalled despite broad agreement on the underlying clinical facts, and I want a diagnosis of what kind of impasse it actually is rather than an adjudication. One camp argues that the DSM-5's dimensional model for personality disorders—rating patients on continuous trait domains such as negative affectivity, detachment, and antagonism rather than assigning categorical diagnoses—was implicitly engineered to weaken Ian Hacking's looping effects: without a discrete kind-label like 'borderline personality disorder,' they claim, there is no categorical identity for a patient to internalize and perform back to clinicians. The opposing camp insists that dimensions loop just as forcefully, except now patients loop on quantitative self-profiles ('I score high on antagonism') and clinicians loop on arbitrary cutoff thresholds that reproduce categorical distinctions in practice; they maintain that looping requires only an institutionally stabilized classification that feeds back into behavior, not a nominal kind-label per se, and that the dimensional reform therefore changed nothing metaphysically important. Both sides accept the same ethnographic reports and longitudinal data, yet neither has conceded ground in years. My working theory is that the disagreement is partly verbal—about whether looping is definitionally tied to kind-membership—but also masks two different projects: one metaphysical, asking whether psychiatric kinds are stable enough to support looping, and one pragmatic, asking which classification format minimizes therapeutic harm. Is there also a hidden empirical disagreement, perhaps about whether continuous scores generate less identity consolidation over time, or is the stall a sign that the core question is ill-posed? And regardless, what would count as genuine progress here: a conceptual distinction that separates metaphysical stability from clinical feedback, a specifically designed study, or a reframing of the problem altogether?
