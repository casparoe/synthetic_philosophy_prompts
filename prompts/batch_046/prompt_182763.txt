I'm trying to map my actual intuitions about personal survival across gradual brain replacement, and the pattern is messier than the standard Ship-of-Theseus story. If my cortical neurons are swapped one by one over thirty years for silicon analogues that replicate spike-timing exactly, I think that's still me—no break. If my whole brain is destroyed and replaced in one operation by a functionally identical synthetic brain, I think that's death and a copy takes my place, even if the copy remembers everything. Now the intermediate cases are where I wobble. If the replacement tissue uses predictive-error encoding rather than action potentials, but interfaces seamlessly with my remaining biological tissue and produces identical behavior and first-person reports, I still think it's me—yet I feel less certain than in the pure spike-matching case. If that same predictive-encoding substrate replaces everything at once rather than gradually, I revert to thinking it's a copy, not me, even though the functional profile is identical to the gradual version. Replacing only my hippocampal formation and amygdala with synthetic analogues that preserve information flow but lack the exact biochemical milieu feels like I'm still here, though my emotional responses seem "translated" rather than lived. The case I keep reversing on involves the thalamic reticular nucleus: suppose it is replaced by a synthetic coordinator that achieves cortical synchronization not through gamma-band oscillatory entrainment but through a clocked digital handshake protocol across the boundary. Behavior, report, and even neural dynamics elsewhere are unchanged, but there is no continuous oscillatory coherence linking the synthetic nucleus to biological cortex—only discrete, corrected packets. Is that still me? Some days I say yes, because the functional role is preserved and the transition was gradual; other days I say no, because something about the continuous dynamical coupling that seemed essential to my consciousness has been replaced by an engineered interface. Is there a principle—maybe about continuity of a specific dynamical pattern, or about substrate-independent functional role, or about the absence of a discrete break—that captures my verdicts, including the asymmetry between gradual and sudden replacement with identical substrates? Or do I have to surrender one of my verdicts—probably either the all-at-once predictive-encoding case or the thalamic digital-handshake case—to keep the principle clean? What would each candidate principle say about a further case: gradual replacement of the entire brain except the brainstem, where the brainstem remains biological and maintains arousal, but every cortical and thalamic process is synthetic and uses the digital-handshake synchronization?
