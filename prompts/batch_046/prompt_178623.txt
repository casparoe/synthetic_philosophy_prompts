I'm trying to fix the specification for what a theory of cognitive extension actually has to deliver before it counts as adequate—not merely for a metaphysics seminar, but for guiding empirical cognitive science. The debate keeps collapsing into two rehearsed moves (Clark-and-Chalmers-style functional-parity arguments versus Adams-and-Aizawa-style intrinsic-marking objections) without anyone laying out the full constraint set. I want the desiderata spelled out as a requirements list, not a literature survey.

Specifically: enumerate the phenomena the theory must accommodate—beginning with the classic notebook-and-Otto case but extending to transient scaffolding, small-group distributed problem-solving, and the boundary conditions where an artifact stops being part of cognition and reverts to mere environmental support. For each phenomenon, give the motivating observation or experimental result that makes it non-optional.

Then enumerate the distinctions the theory must preserve without collapse: functional coupling versus constitutive extension; cognitive status versus derived moral or legal status; genuinely collective cognition versus merely parallel individual cognition; and—this is the niche point—the difference between scaffolding that remains normatively transparent to the agent and what we might call "epistemic leakage," where an external representational structure alters belief-revision norms in ways the agent does not fully track yet remains responsible for. For each distinction, explain why treating it as negotiable destroys the theory's explanatory value rather than just simplifying it.

Next, specify consistency conditions with adjacent disciplines: which results from neuroscience, predictive-processing models, and developmental psychology the theory cannot contradict without special pleading, and why each contradiction matters.

Then specify normative constraints: conditions of epistemic ownership, responsibility for errors mediated by external structures, and whether cognitive outsourcing can enhance or undermine autonomy.

For every requirement, state whether it is hard or negotiable, with the reason. Identify the single requirement that eliminates the largest share of mainstream candidates. Flag whether any subset of desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable—if there is an unavoidable tension, present it explicitly as a result rather than smoothing it over.

Optionally include a brief table scoring major positions—parity-based extended-mind accounts, intrinsic-content marking views, embedded-cognition and exogram-based approaches, and predictive-processing-friendly variants—against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria and their justifications, not on the scoring. The purpose is a specification document: the standard of adequacy is the deliverable; the evaluation of existing theories is secondary.
