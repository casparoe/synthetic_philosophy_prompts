So I've been arguing with my coworker about this focus-credit thing we do at our little design collective, and I feel like I'm missing something deeper but I can't quite name it. The setup is pretty simple: you log uninterrupted deep-work blocks, and each block earns you a veto token against meetings. The rules never say the work has to be contigious or that you can't split one project across days, so lately I've been breaking my four-hour jobs into four seperate one-hour chunks spread through the week. That nets me four veto tokens instead of one, which feels like smart optimization within the rules.

My coworker is furious. She says if everyone fragmented their tasks like this, the veto pool gets inflated, meeting protection becomes meaningless, and the whole point of the system—guarding deep work—gets undermined by the very people using it. I told her that's just a slippery-slope worry, not a real rule break: a world where everyone splits work into tiny pieces is totally conceivable, it just means more bookkeeping. She keeps insisting there's a structural difference between "this can't happen" and "this can't be consistently willed without destroying the purpose it serves," and she talks about willing the end and the means together, which sounds to me like she's smuggling consequentialism through the back door. Is there actually a hidden consistency problem in my reasoning, or is she just dressing up a practical complaint in fancy language?
