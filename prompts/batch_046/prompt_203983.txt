I was grading papers for my philosphy course on metaphysics and logic — I started TA'ing this semester and suddenly this stuff is personaly relevant because I keep arguing with my roommate about whether sentences are "held together" by some invisible glue. Anyway, on the midterm I asked students to explain Bradley's regress argument against treating relations as real, using the sugar-is-sweet example, and then to lay out Russell's distinction between internal and external relations and say whether that solves the worry about the unity of the proposition.

One student wrote the following, paraphrased from their paper. Bradley starts with sugar being sweet and asks how sweetness gets linked to sugar. If we introduce a relation R that connects them, then R is just another term, so we need R2 to link R to sugar, then R3, and so on forever. Because this regress never stops, relations can't actually exist — not even in appearance, since appearance is just a wrong picture of reality. Russell stops the regress by saying some relations are external: they don't depend on the nature of the terms, so they can link sugar and sweetness directly without themselves being terms that need linking. Internal relations are the ones Bradley was right about, because if a relation is internal to its terms then it collapses into them and can't do linking work. So Russell saves external relations, that fixes the infinite regress, and it also solves the unity problem because now we don't need extra glue to hold the proposition together.
