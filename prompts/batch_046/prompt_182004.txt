okay so my friend dragged me into this microhistory vs macrohistory thing and now i have an argument that seems to prove state-level history is literally impossible. i want to keep the intuition but i know i'm being stupid somewhere, probably an equivocation i can't name.

(1) A historical explanation is legit only if it could in principle be confirmed by someone experiencing the event from the afffected side.
(2) State-level events (treaties, fiscal reforms, wars) are experienced by populations as opaque structural forces, not as intential actions they can observe.
(3) So state-level explanations are never confirmable from the affected side; they rely on administrative archives that record elite intentions, not lived effects.
(4) If an explanation relies solely on sources that the affected population couldn't have produced or verified, it's epistemically parasitic on elite perspective and should be replaced.
(5) Therefore any macro-state explanation is just elite projection, and we should prefer microhistories that recover subaltern archives.
(6) But microhistories are locally dense and don't aggregate deterministically: knowing one village's reaction to a tax doesn't entail knowing the fiscal crisis of the realm.
(7) So if we reject macro-explanations, we can't explain large-scale outcomes at all.
(8) But we obviously do explain large-scale outcomes (like why a particular tax reform triggered revolts across regions).
(9) So (1) must be false, and microhistory doesn't have special epistemic authority.

where's the slip? i feel like there's a modal slide or scope shift between (3) and (4), or maybe (1) confuses "confirmable" with "causally complete." if i have to choose, i'd rather keep (1) than (5), but then i'm stuck accepting (7). what does rejecting (4) cost for the microhistory program? and is keeping (1) while dropping (8) just being a nominalist about states?
