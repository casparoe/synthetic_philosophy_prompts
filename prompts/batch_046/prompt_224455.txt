I'm drafting an essay for a philosophy of mind seminar on mind uploading and the gradual-replacement scenario—specifically, the Ship of Theseus variant in which biological neurons are replaced one at a time with functionally equivalent silicon substrates. Rather than rehearsing the familiar debate about whether functional continuity suffices for personal identity, I want to explore what I am provisionally calling "phenomenological debt": the possibility that each micro-replacement introduces a sub-threshold alteration in qualitative character that is individually undetectable but collectively transforms the subject's experiential economy, much as creeping environmental degradation can alter an ecosystem without any single change being perceptible. The essay argues that if phenomenological debt accumulates linearly with substrate turnover, then the gradual-replacement subject might preserve first-person continuity—reporting no discontinuity—while having undergone a systematic qualitative replacement that undermines the intuitive claim that "the same consciousness" survives. I am particularly interested in whether this framing genuinely challenges functionalist accounts of personal identity, or whether it merely smuggles in a non-functional criterion—perhaps a form of substrate essentialism—under the guise of first-person phenomenology.

Given this framework, I would appreciate your comments on three things. First, is the concept of phenomenological debt coherent, or does it collapse into either an unverifiable mysticism about "what it's like" or a redundant restatement of functionalist claims about qualitative role? Second, does the creeping-normality analogy hold up under scrutiny, or is there a relevant disanalogy between ecological systems and conscious subjects—perhaps involving the unity of consciousness or the transparency of mental states—that makes the comparison misleading? Third, I am unsure whether my argument avoids begging the question against substrate-independent theories: if the functionalist insists that qualitative character just is the functional organization, then any talk of "debt" that outstrips functional change seems to presuppose what needs to be shown. Please also indicate how confident you are in your assessment, and identify the specific point about which you are least certain.
