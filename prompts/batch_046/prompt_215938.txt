Consider the conceptual triangle formed by the Protestant work ethic’s moral framing of labor as a calling, the 35-hour work week as an institutionalized rights-based ceiling on labor time, and karoshi—death by overwork—as a limit case in which both market incentives and regulatory protections collapse. You are advising a philosopher of labor and social policy who has roughly two years to produce a monograph that is neither pure intellectual history nor a policy white paper, but rather a conceptual clarification of which rights-claims are genuinely in conflict here.

What are the four or five main open conceptual questions in this triangle—for instance, whether leisure should be conceived as a negative liberty (freedom from labor) or as a positive entitlement (guaranteed, structured rest); whether the work ethic functions today mainly as a coercive cultural script or as a genuinely internalized virtue that makes hour-caps feel like violations of autonomy; whether karoshi represents an extreme deviation or the logical endpoint of a particular rights-structure that privileges production over reproduction; and whether algorithmic scheduling and platform labor have created a form of temporal fragmentation that makes traditional leisure rights structurally unenforceable?

Of these, which two should form the backbone of the monograph, and in what order should they be addressed? Defend your prioritization with explicit criteria: conceptual tractability for philosophical analysis (as opposed to empirical sociology), dependency relations among the questions (which answers must be settled before others can even be formulated clearly), and the practical stakes of resolving them one way or the other. Also identify one currently overrated line of inquiry—perhaps one that collapses into empirical disagreement about labor markets rather than conceptual disagreement about rights—and one underexplored niche issue, such as how hustle culture operates as a secularized afterlife of the Protestant ethic that generates voluntary overwork in ways formal hour-caps cannot capture, that should be woven in precisely because standard treatments neglect it.

The deliverable should be a defended agenda: an ordered list with reasoning, trade-offs acknowledged, and a frank assessment of what argument or evidence over the next year would cause you to reorder the priorities.
