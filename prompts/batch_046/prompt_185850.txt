I'm working out a position on automation and the welfare state for an essay and want a consistency check before I commit to it. I hold four things together: (a) that work is meaningful chiefly through social recognition of contribution rather than through the intrinsic value of its output, so even administratively useless jobs can be genuinely fulfilling if they are socially validated; (b) that automating away all necessary drudgery is therefore desirable, but only if society replaces wage-labor with voluntary collective projects—call them commoning institutions—that preserve that recognition-structure without economic coercion; (c) that welfare should be entirely unconditional, with no workfare, behavioral means-testing, or fraud surveillance, because conditionality corrupts the recognition-relation that gives contribution its meaning; and (d) that dignified leisure in a post-work society must be embedded in shared ritual or collective maintenance rather than pure private consumption, which gives the state a legitimate interest in organizing and funding commoning, though never in mandating participation. Do these hang together? In particular, does my rejection of conditionality in (c) undermine the organizational ambition in (d)? Is there a hidden tension between (a) and (b)—if recognition requires an audience of fellow contributors, doesn't eliminating all necessary labor dissolve the very social frame that makes recognition possible? And is the apparent conflict between wanting to abolish wage-work and wanting to preserve recognition-structures only superficial, or does resolving it require abandoning one side? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what I would have to revise and at what cost, and clearly flag any commitment that looks problematic but actually survives scrutiny. Finally, say what specific argument—perhaps about the psychology of recognition or about the fiscal feasibility of unconditional funding—would change your verdict, and point to where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
