Write a final take-home essay prompt (1,500 words, 48 hours) for an upper-division philosophy of religion seminar on Kierkegaard's account of Abraham and the teleological suspension of the ethical. The prompt must require students to do three things: first, explain how Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac suspends the universal ethical order rather than merely violating it, using the conceptual distinction between the knight of infinite resignation and the knight of faith; second, analyze why Abraham must remain silent, specifically distinguishing this silence from both mystical ineffability and strategic concealment; third, assess a counterfactual in which the angel does not intervene and Isaac dies—namely, whether Abraham's act then collapses into tragic guilt or retains its status as faith, and what this outcome reveals about whether the ethical is genuinely suspended or only temporarily overridden. Provide a model answer of roughly 600–700 words that names the specific moves a top-grade response must contain (for example, recognizing that faith is the paradox of the particular being higher than the universal, and that the absence of the ram exposes whether Abraham relates to the absolute or to an ethical calculation in disguise). Include a marking scheme out of 30 points that distinguishes an A-range answer (which grasps the paradox, addresses the counterfactual's bearing on resignation versus faith, and avoids reducing Abraham to a moral fanatic or a subject of divine command theory) from a B-range answer (which correctly describes the suspension but conflates faith with resignation or treats silence as mere emotional inarticulacy) and from a failing answer (which offers narrative summary without conceptual analysis). Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
