I need help thinking through a disagreement with my roommate about our kitchen stove. Every evening I turn the knob, the electric igniter clicks, and a blue flame appears; I have always taken this as a straightforward causal chain in which the spark and gas produce combustion. Last Tuesday, however, the igniter clicked repeatedly and nothing lit—there was a blockage in the gas line. My roommate now argues that this single failure proves there was never a real causal link to begin with. He claims the flame and the spark are just separate events that nature, or perhaps some deeper ordering principle, habitually pairs together, and that the blockage only reveals the pairing was never necessary. He even alludes to an older idea—something about fire not genuinely burning cotton, but rather each occurrence being arranged separately—that he thinks supports his view. I feel there must be a meaningful difference between a mechanism that is broken and one that never possessed causal power, yet I cannot articulate what that difference is. Is he right that one counterexample dissolves every causal claim? Or is there a principled way to defend the reality of the stove's power without ignoring the exception, and if so, does that mean science describes genuine forces rather than only very stable patterns of conjunction that we mistake for causes?
