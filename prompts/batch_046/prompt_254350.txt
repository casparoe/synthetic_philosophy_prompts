I have recently been disturbed by a failure of self-transparency that now feels philosophically consequential: I spent an entire afternoon convinced I was calmly focused, only to realize afterward that I had actually been in a sustained state of low-grade anxiety. This experience has made me take seriously the question of luminosity—the claim that if one is in a mental condition, one is in a position to know that one is—and its relation to the KK principle, which holds that knowing p entails knowing that one knows p. I would like a poem in the form of an Italian sonnet, with an octave and a sestet separated by a clear volta at line nine, that uses this structure to enact the conceptual problem rather than merely illustrate it. The octave should present the intuitive case for luminosity: the speaker asserts direct, unmediated access to their own calm, treating the mental as if it were self-illuminating. At the volta, the poem should turn on the recognition that knowledge, on a safety-theoretic account, requires being safely distant from nearby error; consequently, precisely at the boundary where anxiety shades imperceptibly into calm—the very region the speaker occupies—there can be no luminous knowledge, and the KK principle fails not through mere ignorance but through the instability of marginal knowledge. The sestet should then trace the aftermath: the speaker now doubts not only their calm but their knowledge of that doubt, leaving open whether mental self-knowledge is ever fully transparent or is always already deferred by a margin of error. The rhyme scheme should follow ABBA ABBA CDE CDE, and the diction should be precise enough that a reader familiar with the anti-luminosity arguments and the debate over self-knowledge transparency will recognize the philosophical architecture, while remaining coherent as a lyric poem for someone unfamiliar with the technical vocabulary. Please do not resolve the tension with consolation or resignation; let the failure of transparency itself be the poem’s true subject and its final unresolved note.
