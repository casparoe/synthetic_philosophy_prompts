so at the rationalist meetup last weekend two friends got into it over beers about whether the hinge-of-history idea is actually useful or just narrative candy. (1) Maya said it's mostly a storytelling bias — we call 1989 or 1968 hinges because neat arcs sell books, but run serious counterfactuals and structural forces (tech curves, demographics) mean outcomes converge anyway. (2) Leo pushed back with this taxonomy he'd been tinkering with: micro-hinges (single patents or elections that cascade), meso-hinges (institutional forks like Portugal's 2001 decriminalization), and macro-hinges (civilizational shifts). He said Maya was only attacking macro and ignoring that micro and meso are genuinely contingent and well-documented. (3) Maya said the taxonomy just moves the goalposts — if a hinge is just "any event with divergent counterfactuals," you've redefined causation, not discovered a special category. She said Leo was smuggling in signifigance by labeling. (4) Leo said no, the point is predictive robustness: macro has low robustness (everything converges), micro has high robustness, and meso is where policy lives — Portugal's shift genuinely altered European overdose trajecties, so it's not retrofitting. Can you adjudicate move by move? Rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say what both are missing, and tell me whether Leo's taxonomy is doing real work or just renaming.
