A study-group handout summarizes F.H. Bradley's argument about relations and the unity of the proposition in the following way: Bradley argues that breaking a judgment into distinct terms—such as a subject and a predicate—requires an additional relation to unite them into one thought, and that this unifying relation itself needs a further relation, generating an infinite regress; he therefore concludes that reality cannot contain genuinely independent parts and must be an indivisible Absolute, making ordinary relational judgments appearances rather than truths about reality. The handout then claims that Russell solved this problem by treating relations as purely external, meaning they do not modify their terms and therefore need no 'glue,' which allows plural propositions without contradiction and renders Bradley's monism unnecessary. How fair is this account? Distinguish Bradley's specific notion of internal relations from Russell's technical use of external relations, identify whether the regress targets the structure of propositions or the ontology of properties, and say whether Russell's response actually answers Bradley or simply rejects the premises that generate the regress. Present your evaluation as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion. Write in American English.
