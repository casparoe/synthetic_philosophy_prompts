I've been arguing with my roommate about the Malcolm-Dennett debate — whether dreams are actual experiences during sleep or just stories we stitch together the second we wake up. I'm now holdin a kind of hybrid view that feels weirder than either side: dreams aren't experiences in the moment, but they're not pure confabulation either; instead they're "offline predicitive updates" — compressed adjustments to the brain's internal models that happen during REM, which only get translated into a pseudo-narrative with colors and feelings once waking consciousness comes back online. So when someone says "I was flying over a city," what's really being reported is a retrofitted summary of prediction-error minimization that never had any phenominal character of its own. If I'm right, then vivid dreams and certain false waking memories should be structurally identical except for the metabolic context, and there shouldn't be any in-principle way to distinguish them from first-person report alone.
