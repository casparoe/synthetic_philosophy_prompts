Arpaly's view requires that the agent be moved by the right object—here, the stranger's suffering—because it is valuable, and specifically requires de re registration of that particular suffering as the reason. Because the lifeguard only tracks a general distress-type through her dispositional schema, without having this victim's need in mind as what moves her, she is not moved by the right feature for the right reason. Finally, Markovits holds that genuine responsiveness requires the agent to be able to articulate the reason justifying her action and to adjust her behavior in light of specific situational features.
