Compose a deadpan satirical tract—approximately 700 to 900 words—written in the voice of a fictional Enlightenment disciple of Jeremy Bentham, Dr. Silas Merrick, who applies Bentham's actual utilitarian framework to the moral evaluation of modern intimate and romantic practices. The satire must derive from exaggerating three genuinely Benthamite commitments rather than inventing them: first, that moral judgment is reducible to a calculus of pleasures and pains that disregards social convention or appeals to nature; second, that the psychological springs of action can be precisely taxonomized and weighed; and third, that restrictions on private conduct require proof of demonstrable harm to others, the very principle Bentham invoked to argue against the criminalization of non-procreative sexuality. Invent a pseudo-technical vocabulary—such as a Sympathy-Discount Rate or a Pleasure-Debt Ledger—that sounds authentically Benthamite but corresponds to no real concept in his writings. Include a footnote in which Merrick rebuts a fictional conservative opponent by demonstrating that the opponent's objection collapses into an unexamined ascetic prejudice once subjected to consistent felicific calculation. Maintain sustained irony without breaking character: the prose should remain grave, precise, and period-appropriate, with absurdity emerging only from the relentless granular quantification of emotional labor, dating algorithms, and domestic scheduling. Do not cite modern scholars, do not provide a bibliography or reading list, and do not refer to any named living person.
