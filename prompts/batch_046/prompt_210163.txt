So I'm putting together the final for my upper-level seminar on Descartes and the self — 45 mins, about 800 words, 30 marks out of 100. I need one essay question on the cogito that students cant answer by just summarising the Meditations.

Write a question that asks exactly what kind of existence is established by the claim that I think therefore I exist. Make them do three things. First, explain why the cogito is immune to hyperbolic doubt and whether it's an inference with a hidden premise or something more direct like an intuition or performative contradiction. Second, have them tackle the thin self problem: does the proof only establish that some thinking is occuring right now, or does it also prove there's a persistent substance that owns these thoughts? They should mention the Lichtenberg-style worry that all we can really say is it thinks, like it rains, and explain how Descartes might reply that I is necessarily tied to the act of thinking itself. Third, inject this twist: imagine a minimal system — say a split-brain subject or a very bare AI — that can produce the token I think without having unified diachronic consciousness. Could that system genuinely perform the cogito? Or does the proof depend on a kind of first-personal access that such a system lacks? Tell them not to just say no because its not human; they need to name the specific feature, if any, that requires that access.

The question should end by asking whether the cogito actually proves more than a momentary, subject-less occurance of thought, and if not, whether thats a failure or just the right scope for the argument.

Then please write a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B by listing the specific moves each grade needs — for example, correctly distinguishing inference from intuition, handling the Lichtenberg objection without misattributing it, and actually engaging with the minimal-system twist instead of dismissing it. The scheme should also flag the common error of conflating the cogito with the wax-argument proof of substance dualism.
