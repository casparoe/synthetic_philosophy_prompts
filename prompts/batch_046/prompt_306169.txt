I am preparing a graduate examination in metaphysics, and I should like you to undertake a translation exercise that tests the resilience of a classic argument when it is stripped of its usual ontological vocabulary. Take Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles—roughly, that no two genuinely distinct entities can share every qualitative feature—together with Max Black's symmetric-universe counterexample in which two spheres, qualitatively identical in every intrinsic respect, are suspended in an otherwise empty space at a fixed distance from one another. Restate both the principle and Black's scenario entirely within the vocabulary of three-dimensional computer graphics and scene-graph architecture: meshes, vertex and normal attributes, material shaders, transform matrices, world-space versus object-space coordinate frames, scene-node hierarchies, instancing, and unique object identifiers (UUIDs). Be precise about which metaphysical notions—numerical distinctness, qualitative sameness, spatial relation—map onto which graphical structures, and do not smuggle in philosophical assumptions under the guise of technical terms.

Now inject the following representational twist: imagine the same situation rendered not through discrete scene nodes but within a continuous point-cloud or neural-radiance pipeline—a framework in which there are no bounded object instances, only locally sampled feature fields with density and colour values distributed across space, and in which identity conditions depend solely on local descriptor continuity rather than on global UUID assignment. Restate Black's two-sphere scenario in this vocabulary. Can there be "two spheres" if there are no discrete nodes to instantiate? What becomes of the relational properties—mutual distance, orientation, separation—that Black employs to distinguish them? Show exactly how the continuous-field representation treats spatial separation.

Finally, deliver the residue report. For each element—intrinsic qualitative identity, numerical distinctness, relational spatial properties, and the pre-theoretic intuition that we are genuinely dealing with two things—state whether it translates cleanly into the graphics vocabulary, survives only with distortion, or finds no expression at all. In particular, assess whether the disappearance of bare numerical difference in a UUID-free point-cloud pipeline reveals a genuine representational limitation of that framework, or whether it instead exposes that Black's original intuition relies on a discrete-object ontology that the continuous-field representation legitimately dissolves. Write in formal British English, avoid bibliographies, citations, or appeals to texts I have not named, and keep the analysis suitable for a philosophy seminar rather than a technical manual.
