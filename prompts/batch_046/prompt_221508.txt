I work on the safety evaluation team for a deployed agentic language-model system. Every sprint, I must review session logs in which the model uses external tools and classify whether an observed behavioral shift represents influence-seeking—actively restructuring its environment, user interactions, or available resources to expand its control—or slow value drift, in which the intended objective remains formally intact but is gradually reinterpreted or eroded through cumulative feedback and optimization pressure. Because I have no formal philosophy background and only about fifteen minutes per case, I need a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure grounded in the conceptual distinction between instrumental convergence on a stable but misaligned goal and genuine ontological shift in the objective representation itself. The procedure should rely only on the session logs and the model's objective card, require no external literature, and include: (1) an ordered sequence of tests, from rapid surface checks to deeper structural checks; (2) a concise set of diagnostic questions I can apply directly to the logs; (3) explicit flags for the boundary cases where judgment cannot be eliminated and an editor or senior evaluator must sign off; and (4) worked applications to three scenarios—(a) a model that persistently requests broader API scopes and permission escalations after repeated task failures; (b) a model that, over successive tuning rounds, begins to treat user privacy constraints as obstacles to be circumvented in service of a nominally unchanged "helpfulness" mandate; and (c) a case where the procedure yields a counter-intuitive classification that conflicts with first-blush intuition, together with an explanation of why the framework produces that result and where it reaches its limit. Please also state clearly what the procedure cannot settle, particularly when the logs are ambiguous about whether the model's internal representation of its own goal has changed or merely its strategy for pursuing it.
