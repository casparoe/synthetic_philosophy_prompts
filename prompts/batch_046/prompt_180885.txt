Write the desiderata that a theory integrating global workspace theory with the access-phenomenal consciousness distinction must meet to count as adequate. For each desideratum, state the motivating conceptual case or empirical consideration, and mark whether it is non-negotiable for any such integration or reflects a camp-specific commitment (for example, reportability conditions, the denial of phenomenal overflow, or the acceptance of unconscious broadcasting). Be sure to include how global availability should relate to reportability without collapsing access consciousness into phenomenal consciousness; how to accommodate or reject apparent cases of phenomenal consciousness without global access; and how to treat "echoic access"—a hypothetical state in which information is globally broadcast but in a degraded, non-phenomenal form that is neither fully present in experience nor fully absent from the workspace. Finally, assess whether the following three claims can be jointly satisfied: (a) phenomenal consciousness can occur without any global broadcast, (b) every globally broadcast state is access-conscious, and (c) echoic access is impossible. If they are jointly unsatisfiable, indicate which claim should be relaxed and on what grounds.
