Write an essay addressing the controversy over whether Daniel Dennett's notion of the self as a centre of narrative gravity genuinely eliminates substantial selfhood or merely reinstates a functional analogue of the Cartesian ego. Focus particularly on the challenge raised by severe dissociative fragmentation, in which multiple, apparently autonomous narrative streams seem to operate within a single biological organism. If each stream generates its own centre of narrative gravity, does Dennett's framework commit us to the literal existence of several selves within one body? Or does it require us to privilege one stream as the authentic self, thereby reintroducing a substantial criterion of personal identity? Reach and justify a clear conclusion about whether the framework can maintain its anti-substantialist commitments consistently under such conditions. Finally, state explicitly what argument or empirical discovery—perhaps concerning the integration or competition of narrative streams—would most likely lead you to abandon or significantly revise that conclusion, and indicate where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
