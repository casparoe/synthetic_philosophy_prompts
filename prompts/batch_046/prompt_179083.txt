I just finished a long, exhausting argument with a friend about a design pattern that is becoming standard in certain recommendation and public-policy algorithms. The pattern works like this: a system records a user’s revealed preferences—clicks, votes, consumption choices, survey answers—but then runs them through an automated “coherence and reflection filter.” The filter discards decisions made under time pressure, emotional arousal, sleep deprivation, or contexts that the classifier labels “inconsistent with the user’s long-run profile,” and it treats the remaining, cleaned dataset as the user’s authoritative preference set for optimization. The user is never asked to ratify the filtered result; the system simply acts on it, on the grounds that these are the preferences the user “would have held” under conditions of full information, coherence, and calm deliberation. My friend insists this is just pragmatic paternalism with no deeper metaphysics. I think it quietly commits to a very specific philosophy of practical reason, personal identity, and normative authority.

What philosophical commitments does that two-layer practice actually presuppose? Does it require something like coherent extrapolated volition—the idea that there is a single, determinable “idealized self” whose preferences have normative priority over the actual, fragmented agent—or can it be justified by a thinner ideal-observer or informed-desire account? Reconstruct the implicit theory of preference, autonomy, and temporal authority: does the practice treat the impulsive temporal slice as less genuinely “the person” than the reflective slice, and if so, what view of personal identity or diachronic agency does that depend on? Is the practice philosophically underdetermined, so that a pluralist about the self, or a deflationary view that treats incoherence as constitutive rather than erroneous, could still license the same filtering mechanics for purely instrumental reasons? And if we replaced its underlying view with one that denies there is any “true” preference behind the noise—say, a view that takes the unfiltered, contradictory set as the authentic expression of agency—what exactly in the system’s architecture, and in the institutional norms around consent and transparency, would have to change?

If the framing above conflates things that should be kept apart—for instance, if the filter does not actually presuppose any metaphysics of an idealized self, or if invoking “coherent extrapolated volition” here is a category mistake—please say so plainly rather than constructing an answer around a confused premise.
