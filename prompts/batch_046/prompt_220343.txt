I have been working through David Pearce's vision of the hedonistic imperative—the idea that biotechnology could eventually abolish suffering altogether—and I keep circling back to a problem that the standard popular debates rarely address with any precision. Most discussions ask whether engineered happiness would be authentic, or whether evolution has made suffering indispensable for survival; these are familiar frames. But in a course on philosophy of mind and value, I found myself wondering about a more specific tension: if suffering is not merely an unpleasant feeling but also a functional signal that tracks damage, risk, and normative failure, then abolishing it entirely might do more than change our affective landscape. It might eliminate a distinctive mode of cognition—what we might call adversarial tracking—through which organisms come to know that something is wrong in a direct, non-inferential way. The controversy I want to explore is whether biotechnology can genuinely separate the informational role of aversive experience from its negative phenomenology, or whether the hedonistic imperative necessarily entails giving up that form of knowledge altogether.

Write an essay addressing the following question: If suffering were abolished through biotechnology, could the cognitive and normative functions it traditionally serves—alerting us to harm, motivating correction, marking moral urgency—be fully preserved by valence-neutral or positively valenced signaling systems? Or does the phenomenology of suffering contribute something irreplaceable to these functions, such that a post-suffering civilization would operate with a genuinely transformed and possibly impoverished epistemic and motivational architecture? Weigh the main arguments on both sides, including the possibility of synthetic analogues or virtual simulations that preserve learning without harm, and the counter-claim that once negative valence is decoupled from evolutionary function, the concept of damage itself becomes obsolete and adversarial cognition is no longer necessary. Reach and defend a definite conclusion—do not end on a non-committal note.
