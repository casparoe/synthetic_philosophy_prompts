For a twelve-month research grant in philosophy of mind and cognitive science, I need a proposal that argues for a specific conceptual intervention rather than surveying the field. The problem sits at the intersection of global workspace theory and the access-phenomenal distinction, but I want to avoid the standard framing in which “access consciousness” is treated as a unified achievement and phenomenal consciousness is appended as an unexplained extra. Instead, I propose splitting global availability itself into two modes: “dispositional broadcasting,” in which information resides in the workspace and is retrievable by subsystems on demand, and “actualized integration,” in which that information is actively woven into the system’s current global state and modifies its dynamics in real time. The precise question is whether phenomenal consciousness tracks actualized integration rather than broadcasting per se, and whether a system—say, a transformer-based architecture with attention-driven state updates—could exhibit full access consciousness (reportability, reasoning, action-guidance) without phenomenal consciousness if its broadcasting remains purely dispositional.

Write the proposal so that it takes a committed position on this framing. State precisely why standard global workspace accounts leave this internal split unexamined; defend using artificial architectures as the primary analytical case rather than human neuropsychological dissociations like blindsight, and explain why that methodological choice is better suited to isolate the structural feature in question; describe what a successful result and a failure would look like—success being a determinate argument that the split either dissolves the explanatory gap or relocates it productively, failure being the discovery that the distinction collapses back into the familiar access-phenomenal binary; and include an honest assessment of scope for one year, identifying the specific conceptual or technical obstacle that could derail the project. Do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations. The tone should be formal and suitable for a grant committee, and you should not present the dispositional-versus-actualized distinction as one tentative option among others: defend it as the right lens, explain what hangs on its being correct, and say clearly what conclusion you would reach if the split turns out to be unstable.
