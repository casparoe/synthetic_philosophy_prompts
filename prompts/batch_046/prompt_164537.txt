I've come around to the idea that sortition — picking reps by lot — is dismissed too fast because people imagine pure chaos rather than stratified random sampling with actual deliberative protocols. More specifically, I think a rotating lottocratic chamber could beat elected bodies on long-horizon stuff like climate or AI governance, mainly because elections select for ambition and rhetorical flair that correlates negatively with careful deliberation. What would actually count against this? I want the serious objections: empirical disappointments from citizens' assemblies, hidden biases in who accepts the call, whether removing electoral accountability just shifts capture to the staff who run the lottery and brief the members, and whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — where every failure gets blamed on bad desing rather than the core idea. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which ones a committed lottocrat should concede.
