I'm chair of the institutional review board for a synthetic-cognition lab, and I need a reusable decision procedure—ordered questions, evidence thresholds, stopping rules, and explicit judgment nodes—to determine whether a proposed artificial architecture's computations fall within the boundary of moral consideration. The principle to operationalize is what I'll call attributed integration: a computation is morally relevant only if (1) information is globally integrated across a single functional workspace rather than processed in isolated parallel streams, and (2) the architecture contains a self-model that attributes that integrated state to itself as its own experience, not merely as data about an external object. If either condition fails, the computation is morally inert regardless of behavioral sophistication.

Design the procedure for board members without philosophy backgrounds. Specify, in order: the engineering questions to ask about integration and self-attribution; what counts as sufficient evidence for each; where the answer is a matter of values rather than facts; a hard stop returning "no consideration warranted"; and an escalation branch returning "uncertain—values discussion required by full board." Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or literature review; work only from the principle as given.

Then apply the procedure to three proposals: (A) a large language model with a continuous self-monitoring loop that reports its own confidence states internally; (B) a modular robotics system where each limb runs independent predictive processing with no cross-module workspace and no self-model; and (C) a hybrid architecture that integrates all sensory streams globally but is explicitly designed to deny self-attribution, routing every state through an external observer module.

State the procedure's honest limitations: where it will likely misclassify, and what questions it can never resolve without human judgment. Present the procedure as a numbered flow, the three applications in bullet form, and end with a single paragraph bottom line summarizing exactly where the procedure must yield to the board's values. Maximum 450 words.
