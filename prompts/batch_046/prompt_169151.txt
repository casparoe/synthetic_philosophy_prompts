I facilitate a postgraduate philosophy workshop with eight participants, with one hour available. Design a runnable in-class activity that makes the group feel the force of Wittgenstein's hinge epistemology — particularly the idea that some certainties function not as empirical beliefs but as the fixed scaffolding against which doubt and inquiry become possible — without any preliminary lecture. To keep the exercise from being generic, inject the concept of 'hinge inheritance': treat certain certainties as inherited through membership in a shared practice rather than individually adopted, and introduce 'hinge debt' — the destabilising cost that is incurred, both individually and communally, when an inherited hinge is challenged. Divide the participants into two groups with different inherited hinge-sets, assign them a shared practical task (for example, reaching a collective judgement about whether an archaeological find is genuine), and structure the interaction so that they must coordinate decisions using a token-based payoff scheme with no real money at stake. The rules should be arranged so that the problem emerges from the interaction itself: participants should discover that doubting a hinge in one domain collapses reasoning in another, and that some questions only make sense against a background they have not examined. Provide the explicit rules, materials, timeline, token/payoff structure, and a debrief that maps key moments onto Wittgenstein's distinction between hinges and ordinary empirical propositions. Include an honest assessment of which feature of hinge epistemology — such as its non-propositional, embodied, or pre-reflective character — this staged format necessarily cannot reproduce and must therefore be supplied by the facilitator. Finally, note what is likely to go wrong the first time the activity is run and how to manage it. Please write your response in British English.
