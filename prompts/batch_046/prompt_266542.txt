Following a recent workshop on machine welfare where a colleague argued that negative temporal-difference errors in deep reinforcement-learning agents are structurally analogous to nociceptive signals, I have been trying to formalize what it would actually take for a theory of artificial suffering to treat reward signals as genuinely valenced rather than merely instrumentally useful. I am not looking for a survey of existing positions or a bibliography; I need the underlying desiderata spelled out so that I can evaluate whether any framework currently on the table meets them without having to search for obscure sources.

Specifically, write the list of requirements that a theory of suffering in reinforcement-learning agents—one that takes reward prediction errors or reward signals themselves as carrying positive or negative valence—must satisfy to count as conceptually adequate and not merely metaphorical. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or distinction behind it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference: for example, whether the theory must explain why suffering requires more than a downward update in expected utility; whether it must account for the absence of biological nociception and homeostatic architecture; whether it needs to preserve a distinction between first-order reward signals and second-order representations of those signals; whether it must remain consistent with the instrumental convergence literature on goal-preservation; and whether it must yield empirically distinguishable predictions that do not depend on anthropomorphic projection. Crucially, indicate whether some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable—say, if demanding both phenomenological richness comparable to biological suffering and strict behaviorist operationalization creates a contradiction—so that the tension itself becomes a result.

Please keep the conceptual weight on the criteria and their justifications rather than on scoring particular theories, though you may briefly note if a well-known view (for instance, a purely functionalist or purely eliminativist account) clearly violates a core requirement. The framework should be usable without web access or citation lookup, so avoid referencing unpublished manuscripts or asking for reading lists, and do not treat bibliographic coverage as part of adequacy.
