After a long and circular argument with a friend about whether the omnipotence paradox reveals a genuine contradiction or merely a confused task-description, we agreed to stop talking past each other and formalize exactly where our disagreement lives. Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the omnipotence paradox, written in a deadpan, journal-formal voice as if for a philosophy collaboration forum.

Camp One (the Logical-Coherence Camp) holds that omnipotence is the power to perform any task that is logically possible; that descriptions like “create a stone so heavy that its creator cannot lift it” specify logical contradictions analogous to a round square; and therefore that an omnipotent being’s inability to perform them is not a limitation but a rational boundary condition, leaving omnipotence fully consistent.

Camp Two (the Essentialist-Task Camp) accepts the logical-possibility distinction but contends that this reply either renders omnipotence vacuous or commits its defender to a costly metaphysics: if the being’s essential properties—necessary omnipotence, necessary benevolence, necessary identity—are held fixed, then the class of “tasks logically possible for that being” is already tautologically constrained by those essences, so the paradox is not dissolved but relocated into whether an agent can possess essential properties that are genuinely independent of its power.

Deliver the statement as a single JSON object with exactly this schema:
