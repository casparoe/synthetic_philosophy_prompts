I've been thinking about how scientists build models that are obviously wrong in detail but still seem to work. I read that the economist Milton Friedman defended this with what people call an "F-twist": the realism of a model's assumptions doesn't matter, only whether it predicts correctly. What if the unrealistic assumption isn't just a simplification but actually hides a mechanism that only matters under different conditions? Imagine a disease model that assumes everyone mixes randomly; it predicts hospital loads well in one city, but if the real pattern is clustered and a new variant exploits those clusters, the model's success was brittle. Does Friedman's defense survive when false assumptions mask causally fragile mechanisms rather than merely simplifying them?
