so i've been going in circles with wittgenstein's private language argument, especially the beetle-in-the-box scenario and the diary entry marked S. for decades people split into two camps: one reads him as a verificationist saying if nobody else can check your inner state then talk about it is just noise, the other insists that's a mis-reading and he's really arguing about normative conditions for correct use — that without public criteria you don't even have the idea of getting the word wrong. but honestly nothing new has come out of this fight in ages. my working theory is that the stalemate isn't really about verificationism vs normativity, but about whether we can hold a calibration-independant concept of mental content — like whether your grasp of pain can be fixed solely by an occurence of feel without any triangulation with behavior, language, or shared practice. is that the hidden fault line? or is there a disguised empirical claim about cognitive architecture doing all the heavy lifting? i want a real diagnosis, not another survey: is this disagreement verbal, about which cases count as language, about standards of evidence for mental content, or about starting intuitions nobody can argue for? and if it's substantive, what kind? also be honest — could a new conceptual distinction actually end it, or is the question ill-posed in a way that means we should just change the subject? tell me what progress would even look like here.
