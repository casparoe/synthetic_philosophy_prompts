Write a dialogue between a foundationalist and an infinitist sitting in a diner arguing about whether belief justification really needs a bedrock stoping point. Don't do the generic textbook version—have them trace a concrete chain: justifying that a specific bridge won't collapse tomorrow, running through an engineer's report, then the material-science textbook behind it, then the calibraton logs for the testing gear, then the perceptual reliability of the technician who read the gauge, and so on with no obvious ground in sight. The foundationalist should argue that without a self-justifying or direct perceptual base, this chain isn't real justification but just an infinite deferral of responsability—like writing checks with no bank behind them. The infinitist should reply that "deferral" is the wrong image: each link genuinely supports the next, and demanding a ground is like asking for the first number in an infinite sequence. Crucially, make them respond to actual moves: when the foundationalist presses that a single hidden flaw in the chain could undermine the whole bridge-belief, the infinitist shouldn't just switch to talking about coherence but should answer whether local damage really propagates all the way up in an infinite regress. Keep the tone casual—use contractions, maybe a typo or two, no lecture-hall stiffness. Don't ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and don't name-drop obscure papers.
