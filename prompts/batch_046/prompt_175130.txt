I spent thirty years in condensed-matter physics before retiring, and in the last few years I have been working through philosophy of science and metaphysics on my own, mostly without formal coursework. One argument that genuinely troubles me is the line from special relativity to the so-called block universe—the claim, associated with Rietdijk and Putnam, that because simultaneity is frame-relative there can be no objective global present, and therefore past, present, and future events all exist equally. I understand the basic physical setup well enough: two observers in constant relative motion construct different planes of simultaneity for a distant event, and if presentism is true, one observer’s “now” includes events that for the other are already past or still future, which seems incoherent. Yet the popular literature tends to collapse everything into a simple “eternalist versus presentist” dichotomy, and I want something more granular.

Please construct a formal taxonomy—structured as a decision tree with labeled branch points—of the positions one might take on whether special relativity genuinely entails eternalism. The tree should begin with a concrete physical scenario rather than an abstract philosophical label: imagine observer A and observer B in constant relative velocity, each assigning a plane of simultaneity to a distant supernova, and then branching on successive diagnostic questions that separate positions without naming them in advance. The branches should ask: (i) whether their disagreement about simultaneity is merely epistemic (they lack access to some hidden true frame) or ontological (there is genuinely no fact of the matter); (ii) if ontological, whether temporal passage can survive in a frame-relative or indexical form rather than as a global flow; (iii) whether one is willing to posit a cosmologically privileged reference frame—perhaps tied to the cosmic microwave background or to some other large-scale structure—to salvage an absolute present; and (iv) whether one accepts that the block view eliminates genuine change, or instead reinterprets change as four-dimensional variation across a static manifold.

The leaves should include named positions where they exist—block eternalism, presentism defended via a privileged frame, a relativistically adapted moving-spotlight view, growing-block eternalism restricted to Lorentz-invariant hypersurfaces, and any other coherent combination—plus “unnamed” for logical spaces that appear unoccupied. Each leaf should carry the position’s name (or “unnamed”) and one sentence describing its most awkward commitment: for example, the privileged-frame presentist must explain why local physics never reveals the frame; the relativistic spotlight view must explain how a single “lit” region can be assigned without violating Lorentz covariance. Please also flag any pair of leaves that your tree genuinely cannot separate—where two positions make identical physical predictions despite different metaphysical packaging.

In addition, inject one somewhat niche distinction into the tree: I have been wondering whether a “point-presentist” position—holding that only events lying on one’s own world-line are real, with no spatially extended present slice at all—counts as a coherent rescue of presentism, or whether it collapses into a kind of solipsism about distant events. Include that as an explicit branch point or leaf, with a brief diagnosis of whether anyone actually occupies it and what its central difficulty is. Keep every node phrased as a question an intelligent non-specialist could answer without already knowing the philosophical labels. No bibliography, no citations, no request for further reading—just the tree itself.
