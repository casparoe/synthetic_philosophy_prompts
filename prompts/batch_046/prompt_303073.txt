I've been pretty bought in on the Kelly / geometric rationality thing ever since the old LessWrong threads about it — the idea that if outcomes compound multiplicatively, maximizing expected log utility isn't just a gambler's heuristic but the actual rationality constraint, and that linear EV is basically leaving survival capital on the table. Lately I've been pushing it into weirder territory in my head: not just betting or portfolio growth, but epistemic updating where your credences compound over time like capital, and even altruistic allocation where you're distributing resources across people facing their own multiplicitive risks. I think geometric rationality should govern all of that, but I want to actually stress-test the view instead of just nodding along.

What would genuinely count against it? I'm not asking for the standard "Kelly is too conservative for one-shot moonshots" line — I know that one, and I think the reply about parallel lives or repeated structure mostly handles it. I want the deeper defeaters: formal results showing geometric rationality collapses into ordinary expected utility under conditions that are actually widespread; empirical cases where agents approximating Kelly systematically do worse than EV-maximizers in some structured environment; or moral scenarios where log-utility aggregation across people gives answers that feel not just conservative but actually wrong in a way defenders have to bite. Also, does the view risk being insulated by saying "oh, that's not a multiplicitive domain" whenever it mispredicts — and if so, is that insulation a fatal flaw or just the cost of having a domain restriction?

Please rank these from "would make me genuinely update downward" to "merely awkward," and be honest about what committed Kelly defenders themselves concede is a real cost rather than just a critic's misunderstanding. I'm especially curious whether there's a result in decision theory or economics that quietly undermines the uniqueness claim — like, if there's a broad class of preferences where geometric and linear rationality are observationally equivalent, then maybe the whole framework is just aesthetic dressing. Don't hold back if the position turns out to be well-insulated; I want to know whether that's a feature or a bug.
