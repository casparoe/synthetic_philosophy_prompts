"The lexical-threshold view does solve the problem because it restricts incommensurability to only the worst suffering. Standard negative utilitarianism fails because it treats all suffering as infinitely outweighing pleasure, which makes pleasure completely worthless and also creates problems with the repugnant conclusion in population ethics. By allowing pleasure to matter below the threshold, the hybrid view preserves the intuition that a life with moderate happiness and no severe pain is good. However, the view collapses into standard welfarism because once you admit that pleasure and pain can be traded off on a common scale below the cutoff, you've already conceded that they share a common metric, so the lexical ordering above the cutoff is just an arbitrary discontinuity. Also, incommensurability means the values can't be compared at all, not just that they can't be summed, so the hybrid is incoherent: it says they're comparable in one region and literally incomparable in another, but comparison is a transitive relation, so if A compares with B and B with C, A must compare with C."
