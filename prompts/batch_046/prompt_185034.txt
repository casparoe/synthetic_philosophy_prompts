You are designing the final examination for an upper-level undergraduate course on Hume and the Scottish Enlightenment. The exam lasts 60 minutes; the essay question below carries 25 marks and should be answerable in roughly 45 minutes of writing.

Write one essay question that forces students to engage with the internal mechanics of Hume's monetary theory in his essays Of Money and Of Interest, rather than allowing them to reproduce a summary of his conclusions. The question must turn on a specific conceptual tension in Hume's account: he treats money as purely representational, comparing its function to that of oil that smooths the motion of machinery, yet he also maintains that a sudden increase in the physical stock of specie—such as the influx of American silver—can temporarily stimulate labor and production in Europe before prices have adjusted upward. The task should ask students first to reconstruct how Hume attempts to resolve (or leaves unresolved) this tension using only the conceptual resources internal to his essays—specifically his distinction between gradual and sudden changes, his proportionality thesis linking prices to the quantity of money, and his implicit account of how nominal expectations affect real labor supply.

Second, the question must inject a hypothetical scenario that Hume never discusses: a sovereign introduces a fully transparent fiat currency in a closed economy, distributing the new units evenly among all agents with perfect common knowledge of the total supply and of the future price level. Students must determine whether Hume's conceptual apparatus predicts any temporary stimulus to industry in this case, or instead predicts immediate monetary neutrality, and they must defend their answer by identifying precisely which elements of Hume's mechanism depend on the physical presence of coin, which depend on information asymmetry or expectation formation, and which depend on the velocity of circulation.

Provide the following:

1. The essay question itself, clearly worded for students and approximately 300 to 400 words in length.
2. A model answer of about 600 words that demonstrates the required analytical moves.
3. A marking scheme totaling 25 marks, broken into specific must-hit points (for example: accurate reconstruction of the representational claim; correct deployment of the gradual versus sudden distinction; precise analysis of the fiat-currency hypothetical; correct isolation of the informational or expectation-based variable). For each point, state the marks allocated and include a brief one-line note on the characteristic error students make. The scheme should clearly distinguish an A-level response, which correctly maps each mechanism onto the hypothetical case, from a B-level response, which summarizes Hume accurately but fails to separate the physical-coin component from the information component.

The prompt must not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to secondary literature. Do not include verbatim quotations from Hume's texts unless verified against a source; paraphrase his claims without quotation marks.
