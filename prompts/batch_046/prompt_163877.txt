I run a philosophy workshop at a local library, about 14 people, ages range from college kids to retirees, 90 minutes. I want them to physically feel the idea that creativity isn't inventing from scratch but more like turning knobs on a theme you already have - Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model. To make it concrete and a little weird, have them redesign a "goodbye ritual" for strangers (like leaving a store or ending a phone call) but with one rule: they must keep the eye-contact parameter locked - it can't be removed or replaced, only adjusted in intensity or timing. Give them rules with hidden incentives using points they can trade for snacks at teh end, arranged so that early on everyone copies the safest version, then they realize the locked parameter forces them to twist other things instead. I need the full rules, materials, a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money, a debrief that maps the copying phase to theme-retrieval and the twist moment to parameter-tuning, and be honest about which part of Hofstadter's view this can't reproduce (like the unconscious selection of knobs).
