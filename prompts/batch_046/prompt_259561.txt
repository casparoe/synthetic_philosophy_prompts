Write a sestina on Skolem's paradox, narrated from inside a countable model of set theory by an element that considers itself genuinely uncountable. Use the six end-words countable, set, map, outside, cardinal, and model, arranged in the standard sestina rotation. Throughout the stanzas, let these terms bear their internal meanings—the element asserts there is no internal map pairing it with the naturals, so its cardinal exceeds that of any internal countable set—while gradually allowing an external perspective to shadow them: an outside observer can enumerate the entire model. The envoi should contain all six words and should state, without sermonizing, that being countable is frame-dependent rather than absolute. Make the form itself enact the paradox: the same vocabulary returns in altered order, just as the same relations are described differently from within and without. The poem should be accurate to the Löwenheim-Skolem phenomenon—someone who knows it should recognize the misalignment between internal cardinality and external enumerability—but should contain no citations, references, or bibliographic apparatus.
