Write a villanelle exploring whether a first-person perspective—an "I"—can be genuinely distributed across two brains linked by a sustained bidirectional cortical interface, using Douglas Hofstadter's idea that selfhood consists in a strange loop of symbolic patterns rather than in a single physical substrate. Employ exactly these two refrains: "The loop is strange, and I am not alone" and "The pattern holds across two skulls of bone." Structure the six stanzas so that each repetition of a refrain shifts the pronoun's reference—singular, then plural, then ambiguously collective—mirroring the conceptual slippage between individual and distributed identity. In the closing couplet, state precisely what survives when autobiographical memory is symmetrically shared: not two selves, not one merged self, but a loop that requires both nodes to close. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Hofstadter's view of pattern-based persistence recognizes the mechanism, not merely the name.
