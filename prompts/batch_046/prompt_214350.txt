Crucially, action is not merely the fulfillment of predictions but epistemic foraging—the agent samples its environment to minimize expected free energy—so that belief and movement are inseparable. Do not reduce this to a pop-science brain-as-computer metaphor; render the mechanism with enough specificity that a specialist in precision-weighted inference would recognize it, yet keep the poem legible to a reader who has never encountered Friston.

Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets followed by one quatrain, with two refrains drawn from the opening tercet that alternate as the final line of each subsequent tercet, and both refrains standing together in the closing quatrain. Start by outlining your two refrains and deciding how the quatrain’s pairing will represent the co-constitution of prior and error rather than a victory of either. One refrain should voice a generative prior or model; the other should voice a precision-weighted prediction error or the drive to sample. The form’s compulsory repetition should do the philosophical work: trace how the prior is continuously revised yet never abandoned, and show that the only resolution available is continued epistemic foraging—movement—rather than convergence on a final truth. Leave the tension open. Maintain a loose iambic rhythm, but observe the strict refrain pattern exactly.
