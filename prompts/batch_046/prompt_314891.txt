I've been chewing on the Orwellian versus Stalinesque framing of retrospective experience revision, particularly in light of the color phi phenomenon and the broader literature on the timing of conscious awareness. If I understand the distinction correctly, an Orwellian account holds that the present moment of experience is literally rewritten by later inputs—so that what it feels like to be conscious at time t is determined partly by events at t plus delta—whereas a Stalinesque account holds that the original experience at t is preserved intact but subsequently misrepresented in memory or report. The color phi effect seems like a clear case of Orwellian revision: subjects report seeing an intermediate color and motion between two discrete dots, yet there is no physical stimulus corresponding to that intermediate state, which suggests the content of awareness at the first location is overwritten once the second dot arrives. From this I have been led to a more general worry: if even low-level perceptual content can be postdictively inserted in this way, then doesn't it follow that there is never a genuinely present "now" in consciousness at all, but only a constantly reconstructed narrative? And if that is right, then doesn't the Stalinesque model become incoherent when applied to the stream of consciousness, since it presupposes an original experiential record that could be airbrushed, but on the Orwellian view there was never any such record—only the rewritten version? I also wonder whether this leads to a double-revision problem: if the experience at t is revised by t plus delta, might not the experience at t plus delta itself be revised by a later input, generating an infinite regress in which no moment is ever stable? In particular, is there a coherent middle position in which some layers of experience are subject to Orwellian revision while others retain a Stalinesque structure—perhaps with immediate phenomenal presence being Stalinesque and higher-order integration being Orwellian—and if so, what empirical or conceptual criteria would distinguish them?
