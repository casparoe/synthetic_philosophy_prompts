For a philosophy-of-science seminar on nature and nurture, I am trying to articulate my position on heritability and need a consistency check before I present it. Here is where I have landed. (1) Heritability coefficients are purely descriptive statistics about variance decomposition within a specific population-environment matrix; they carry no modal or causal force regarding what is biologically fixed or environmentally changeable for individuals. (2) The heritability fallacy—inferring immutability from a high h²—is a genuine error, but it arises only when one treats heritability as a causal-power metric rather than a variance ratio; if one genuinely adopted that causal-power interpretation, then high heritability would indeed entail resistance to environmental intervention, so the fallacy diagnosis itself depends on rejecting that interpretation. (3) I think evolutionary explanations for behavioral differences across human populations must establish that the differences are heritable within each population, because without heritable variance there is no genetic substrate for selection to act upon. (4) At the same time, I hold that heritability is causally inert: knowing that h² equals 0.80 for a cognitive trait in a given sample reveals nothing about the causal pathways from genotype to phenotype, and nothing about whether altering the environment could shift the population mean. (5) I also think that in public-health ethics, conditions with low heritability should be prioritized for environmental intervention, since a small genetic share of variance indicates that environmental factors dominate and are therefore tractable. (6) Finally, I reject the idea that heritability expresses a deep, environment-relative metaphysical property of a trait; I take it to be merely a statistical artifact of the particular sample, so that measuring the same trait in a different environment yields a different number that does not uncover a hidden feature of the trait itself.

I am especially concerned that (3) and (4) pull in opposite directions—using heritability as a necessary condition for evolutionary explanation seems to grant it causal relevance that (4) denies. I am also unsure whether (5) commits me to a reverse heritability fallacy, and whether (6) undermines the environment-specific framing I relied on in (1). Could you audit these commitments?
