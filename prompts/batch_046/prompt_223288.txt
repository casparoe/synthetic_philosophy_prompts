Write a deadpan satirical parody of a modern corporate human-resources performance-review form—approximately 600 words, formatted with numbered competency sections—applying Benjamin Franklin’s system of thirteen moral virtues as an employee-evaluation framework. The satire should turn on actual, specific features of Franklin’s project: the ordered sequence of virtues (beginning with Temperance and ending with Humility), the small-book grid-tracking method he used to record weekly failures, his secular and pragmatic justification for improvement rather than religious salvation, and his own admitted inability to master Order and the paradoxical difficulty of achieving Humility. Invent an internally consistent scoring rubric in which an employee is rewarded for demonstrating Humility yet penalized for taking pride in that demonstration. Do not invent fictional virtues, attribute religious motives to Franklin, or misrepresent the purpose of the grid; keep his actual logic intact even as the bureaucratic form exaggerates it. Include brief, deadpan “manager notes” that cite an employee’s failure at Silence and Frugality using Franklin’s practical definitions—avoiding trifling conversation and waste—without inventing direct quotations. The piece should be readable to someone unfamiliar with Franklin’s autobiography but should reward readers who recognize the precise structure of his original plan. Write in American English.
