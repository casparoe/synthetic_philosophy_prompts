(1) If something is a reason for me to act, it has to be reachable from my current motivational set—otherwise it's not really my reason.
(2) Internal reasons satisfy this easily since they just extend my existant desires.
(3) External reasons don't: they ask me to act on values I haven't adopted, which violates psychological inertia.
(4) So if rationality means following all my reasons, and all my reasons are internal, then I have no rational obligation to care about strangers.
(5) But that's wrong: if I see a kid drowning near my telescope and feel nothing, I should still jump in.
(6) So either (1) or (3) is false.
(7) If I reject (1), reasons can be totally alien to my psychology, making rationality like mind-control.
(8) If I reject (3), then rationality can demand I rebuild my core values on the spot.
(9) Both feel absurd, yet (4) and (5) both seem right.
(10) So there must be external reasons that don't violate inertia, but I can't see how.

Which step do I actually drop? I'd prefer to keep (3) and (5). What's the usual way out, and what's the price of giving up either?
