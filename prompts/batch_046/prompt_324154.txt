Here is a line of reasoning I constructed for a workshop and now distrust because it seems to show that accepting the Allais pattern destroys not only expected utility theory but any normative account of belief. (1) In the classic Allais setup, agents prefer L1 over L2 and L4 over L3, violating the independence axiom. (2) The usual defense appeals to a certainty effect: the psychological value of guaranteed outcomes breaks the linear weighting that independence requires. (3) But independence is not merely one axiom among others; it is the decision-theoretic analog of probabilistic factorization—preferences over acts should decompose over states just as joint probabilities factor over independent events. (4) So if psychological framing can violate independence for monetary lotteries, consistency requires allowing analogous framing effects for credences: for instance, being more confident in hypothesis H when background condition B is settled with certainty than when B is left open, even though B is evidentially irrelevant to H. (5) Call this epistemic Allais framing. Once permitted, it undermines Jeffrey conditioning, because conditioning requires that updating on E leave probabilities over E-independent propositions unchanged regardless of whether other background conditions are certain or probabilistic. (6) Without Jeffrey conditioning, there is no unique rational update rule for partial beliefs; credences become arbitrarily frame-sensitive. (7) Therefore anyone who treats the Allais preference pattern as rationally permissible must also accept that rational belief is globally frame-sensitive and lacks any stable update rule. (8) But that conclusion is absurd: it makes scientific reasoning, legal inference, and everyday belief revision systematically arbitrary. (9) Hence the Allais pattern cannot be rational, and independence must be preserved even at the cost of denying the intuitive appeal of certainty-seeking choices.

Which step should I reject? I am especially reluctant to give up (3), the structural isomorphism claim, and I want to keep (8) that global frame-sensitivity is unacceptable. If I must choose, I would rather deny (4) or (5), but I want to know exactly what rejecting them costs—whether it requires treating belief and preference as governed by fundamentally different norms, or whether there is a more subtle equivocation between state-dependence in acts and evidential independence in credences. Please also say where your reasoning is most likely to fail and what consideration—perhaps a formal model of framing-sensitive belief update that avoids arbitrariness—would change your verdict.
