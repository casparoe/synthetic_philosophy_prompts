I have been trying to work out whether dreams are actually experiences that happen while we sleep, or whether they are only stories we assemble the moment we wake up. I know there is an old philosophical disagreement about this—Malcolm's point that we lack any criterion for dream-experience during sleep, and Dennett's response that an experience might occur but be misremembered or inserted right at waking—but I want to defend a more specific version that I haven't seen stated exactly this way. Call it the coherence-gate view. It holds that being an experience requires not merely neural activity but a minimal continuity with the subject's waking conceptual framework, which I will call the authorization condition. During deep or REM sleep, this framework is largely suspended; therefore whatever neural events occur cannot be authorized as experiences belonging to the sleeper. The waking report does not recall an experience but retroactively constructs a pseudo-experience out of raw neural noise, borrowing the grammatical form of first-person experience reports without the corresponding phenomenological substrate. I think this avoids Dennett's worry about insertion-at-waking, because I am not claiming the experience happens at waking; I am claiming there was no experience at all, only unstructured neural material that the waking mind shapes into a narrative. But I am unsure whether this is consistent with lucid dreaming, where subjects seem to maintain conceptual continuity, or with coherent sleep-talking that tracks dream content.

So I want an honest map of this view's exposure: what would actually count against it, sorted from fatal to merely awkward? Please cover empirical findings about cognition during sleep, philosophical objections that the authorization condition is too demanding even for waking experience, conceptual tensions like whether I have simply redefined experience to win by fiat, and any commitments this view creates in other areas—say, about memory consolidation or personal identity over time. I also want to know which defeaters a defender of the coherence-gate view could honestly concede without abandoning the core claim, and whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability; if it does, say whether that is a problem for a claim of this kind. One more thing: if my framing is confused—say, if the authorization condition is just Malcolm's criterion argument with extra jargon, or if the task as posed can't be done well because the view collapses into one of the standard positions—please say so plainly rather than complying politely. Don't treat the view as well insulated unless it genuinely is; tell me whether it risks anything real.
