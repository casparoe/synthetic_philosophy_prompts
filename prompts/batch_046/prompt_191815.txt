As an adviser recently appointed to a refugee resettlement board, I have become preoccupied with whether equitable distribution requires prioritising the worst-off or risks justifying harm to the better-off without corresponding gain. Write a dialogue between a prioritarian philosopher and an egalitarian critic of the levelling-down objection, debating whether a resettlement policy that caps the welfare of the best-off families—without improving conditions for the worst-off—could be morally justified. Both interlocutors should be equally rigorous; each must respond directly to the other’s specific arguments rather than speaking past them. Keep the exchange focused on the conceptual tension between weighted priority and the intuition that equality achieved solely by levelling downward possesses no intrinsic value.
