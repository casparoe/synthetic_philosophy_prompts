Here are my takes on eternal recurrence and I'm not sure they hang together. First, when Nietzsche has that demon tell you you'll live your life over identically, I think he's doing more than a therapy excercise—he's proposing something like a cosmological thesis, even if he knew the physics was shaky. Second, I think the point is ethical: you should live so you'd want it repeated. Third, there's this weird tension—if it's literally true that every cruelty repeats exactly, then my avoiding one cruel act doesn't actually reduce suffering across eternity; it just changes my local experience. So the ethical motivation seems to need that my choices matter across cycles, but literal recurrence says they don't, they just replay. Fourth, there's an interpretation I find kind of appealing where only your 'peak' moments of intense will-to-power recur, and the mundane stuff is just filler—this saves the ethical problem but feels like I'm importing something Nietzsche never said. Fifth, if recurrence is purely psychological—just imagine it—then why does he spend so much time in those late notebook fragments on energy conservation and finite force? I think those notes matter, but I'm not sure they prove literal recurrence either.

So is there one account that saves all five verdicts? Or which one should I drop? And what does the best version predict for someone who lives a totally mediocre life with no peak moments—does recurrence apply to them at all, or do they escape the cycle? Also please seperate what's settled about the texts from what's genuinely contested—don't pretend there's a consensus where there isn't.
