Many large East Asian corporations employ collective consensus mechanisms for strategic decisions—no single executive formally approves a proposal, and failure is attributed to the group rather than to any individual—while simultaneously operating rigorous individual performance-review systems that determine promotion, compensation, and termination. What conception of the relation between person and community does this institutional pairing presuppose? Must it assume that the collective is ontologically prior and the individual is merely a relational node, or can it be read as treating collective harmony as purely instrumental to individual flourishing? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely fits it well, and say whether replacing collective decision-making with direct executive authority—while retaining the individual reviews—would necessarily commit the firm to a Western liberal individualist ontology, or whether such a change could still be accommodated within a Confucian relational framework.
