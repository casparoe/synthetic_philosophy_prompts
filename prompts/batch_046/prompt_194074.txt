The knowledge norm, like Timothy Williamson defends, says you should only assert p if you actually know p, not just believe it. The truth norm is easier: your assertion just needs to be true, even if you got there by accident. The belief norm comes straight out of Grice's cooperative principle — it just requires that you sincerely beleive what you're saying. A big problem with the belief norm is that it lets people assert false stuff as long as they really mean it, which feels wrong for conversation. Also, Williamson thinks knowledge is needed because asserting without knowing breaks Grice's maxim of quality, and since knowledge is basically justified true belief, the knowledge norm is really just Grice's rule made stricter. So belief is too weak since it doesn't require truth or justifcation.
