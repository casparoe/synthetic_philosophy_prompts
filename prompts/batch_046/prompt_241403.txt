In my philosophy of mind reading group we have spent the past term circling a disagreement about consciousness as a user illusion—specifically, whether treating phenomenal experience as an interface-level representation in the predictive-processing sense eliminates the hard problem or merely relocates it into the architecture of the model. One faction holds that if consciousness is literally a user-illusion analogous to a desktop icon, then no phenomenal residue remains: the binding problem dissolves because there never was a unified phenomenal field to bind, only a compressed predictive summary delivered to higher-order systems. The opposing faction accepts the interface metaphor but insists it presupposes a rendering substrate—the actual predictive dynamics that generate the illusion—and that explaining why those dynamics feel like something rather than nothing is precisely the hard problem concealed behind the metaphor, not dissolved. Despite mapping every standard distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness, transparency and opacity, and every relevant empirical finding, neither side has advanced. I hypothesise the stall is structural rather than factual: perhaps they apply different success conditions for explanation, with one demanding ontological elimination and the other demanding a constructive account of subjective presence; or perhaps they answer different questions—one ontological, one epistemological about introspection—while sharing the vocabulary of illusion. Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is? Is there a hidden conceptual distinction—say, between interface transparency and phenomenal transparency—that exposes the misalignment? Or does the impasse rest on bedrock intuitions about whether subjective presence requires a substrate, with no prospect of resolution? Please lead with your bottom-line diagnosis in the first sentence, then lay out your reasoning, identifying each side’s commitments, whether a reconceptualisation or distinction could move things forward, and if not, why we should expect permanent stalemate.
