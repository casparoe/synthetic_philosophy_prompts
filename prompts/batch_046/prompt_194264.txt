I am preparing a philosophical profile on panpsychism and its ethical implications for a discussion group, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. My commitments are as follows:

(a) I accept a non-emergentist panpsychism: fundamental physical entities possess minimal phenomenal properties, and macro-consciousness arises through a phenomenal-bonding relation among them rather than through brute emergence.
(b) I hold that suffering requires negatively valenced integrated information; consequently, isolated fundamental entities do not suffer, because they lack both the organizational integration and the valence structure necessary for suffering.
(c) I maintain that moral patiency depends specifically on the capacity to suffer, not on phenomenal consciousness per se; therefore, fundamental entities are not moral patients, and we bear no direct moral duties toward them.
(d) I also hold that macro-subjects are metaphysically constituted by these fundamental entities in such a way that harming a macro-subject necessarily involves altering the phenomenal states of its constituent micro-subjects, which gives macro-harm a distributed phenomenal substrate.
(e) Furthermore, I believe that large-scale physical interventions—such as altering cosmological constants or inducing widespread quantum decoherence—would affect an astronomically large number of proto-phenomenal states, and that this creates an indirect, aggregate ethical consideration that is not reducible to direct duties to individual entities.
(f) At the same time, I reject summative micro-ethics: the total moral value of the universe is not calculable by aggregating individual micro-experiences; only macro-level suffering and well-being possess non-derivative moral weight.

Please audit this set. For each pair of commitments, indicate whether there is a genuine tension, an apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, or no tension. Where there is real conflict, rank the tensions by severity, specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency, and state what that revision costs me philosophically. In particular, examine whether (d) and (e) can be maintained alongside (c) and (f) without either collapsing into direct obligations to micro-subjects or rendering the notion of "indirect aggregation" incoherent.
