Write the desiderata an adequate theory of the paradox of tragedy—the enjoyment of artworks that elicit genuinely negative emotions—must satisfy. For each requirement, state the phenomenon or case that motivates it (e.g., the distinction between appreciating a tragedy and merely admiring its craft; our willingness to seek sad films but not real-world analogues; the temporal dynamics by which negative affect resolves into positive evaluation) and flag whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Crucially, include one rarely discussed constraint: the theory must account for what can be called "modal isolation," namely the collapse of tragic pleasure when the sorrowful component (a mournful melody, a grief-stricken expression) is experienced without narrative or contextual framing, even though the negative affect itself remains intact. Identify any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—particularly any tension between requiring an authentically felt negative emotion and requiring a globally dominant aesthetic pleasure that nullifies it. Briefly indicate how cathartic, conversion-based, and control-based accounts fare on the modal-isolation criterion, but keep the conceptual weight on the criteria rather than on scoring the contenders. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
