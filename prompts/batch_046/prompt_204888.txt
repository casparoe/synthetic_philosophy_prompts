Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of the norm governing assertion must meet. Specify the explanatory targets, the case distinctions the theory must preserve, and the puzzles it must dissolve, with the motivating consideration behind each requirement.

Your list should address at least the following: (1) the lottery-paradox tension between high probabilistic confidence and the impropriety of asserting; (2) Moore-paradoxical utterances in which a speaker sincerely asserts p while simultaneously manifesting belief that they do not know p; (3) the boundary among lying, reckless guessing, and honest error, particularly with respect to whether the governing norm targets knowledge, truth, or reasonable belief; (4) assertions issued under conditions of deliberately manufactured ignorance, where a speaker avoids an easily available check and then asserts, and whether this violates the norm more severely than asserting on weak but non-culpable evidence; and (5) the asymmetry between withdrawing an assertion because one learns it was false versus withdrawing because one learns it was unjustified yet accidentally true.

For each desideratum, indicate whether it is non-negotiable or can be relaxed in exchange for broader explanatory success. Identify any pair you regard as jointly unsatisfiable, and explain the tension. Conclude by briefly assessing how a knowledge norm, a truth norm, and a belief-based norm each stand against your criteria, without invoking specific bibliographic citations.
