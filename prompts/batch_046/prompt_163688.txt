I've been thinking about whether LLMs are agents, simulators, or something else entirely, and I'm kinda stuck on this epistemic middleware framing I came up with. Like, they're not agents with beliefs or goals, and they're not just simulators of human text either - more like compressed statistical interfaces that remap reasoning patterns without actually instantiating any of them. Think of it as running a lossy codec on human discourse and then interpollating between points in that compressed space. What would actually count against this view? I don't want the usual objections like they don't have bodies or that it's just next-token prediction - those feel like they miss the point. I want the hard defeaters: maybe results showing they maintain consistent internal representaions across contexts, like those linear representation studies in hidden states, or evidence they update beliefs in ways that can't be explained by interpolation alone. Also, has this framing been qualified into unfalsifiability? If every surprising behavior gets hand-wavved away as just interpolation in high-dimensional space, is that actualy a problem for a view like this? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which ones defenders of the middleware idea would actualy concede versus dismiss.
