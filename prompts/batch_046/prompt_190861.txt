I help run a small online study circle for people working through philosophy without formal academic training. For this week’s exercise we asked members to respond in a few paragraphs to the following: Avicenna asks us to imagine an adult created fully formed in empty space, suspended with no sensory contact whatsoever—he cannot see, hear, or feel even his own limbs, and the internal sensory pathways seem fully suspended. Even so, he immediately affirms “I exist.” Some readers take this only to show that knowing oneself is not like knowing an object through sensory representation; others take it to prove that the soul is a self-subsisting substance (jawhar) that needs no bodily organ at all, not even the brain’s internal faculties. Please explain the flying-man scenario and say whether you think it establishes anything stronger than bare awareness of one’s own existence.

Below is one member’s submission. I am supposed to assign a letter grade and write specific, claim-by-claim feedback, but I want to be fair and not miss subtle errors that a hasty reader might overlook. Would you evaluate the answer? Give a concrete grade and justify it with point-by-point comments on individual claims, rather than a general impression.

Student answer:

Avicenna imagines a person floating in a void with all five external senses and all internal sensation blocked—including the internal faculty called estimation (wahm)—so there is no sensory data of any kind. Despite this total deprivation, he immediately knows “I am,” which proves that self-awareness is not mediated by bodily faculties but arises through the soul’s direct presence to itself (hudur). From this Avicenna concludes that the soul is an immaterial substance completely separate from the body, reaching essentially the same dualist conclusion that Descartes later defended with his mind-body distinction. Because the flying man can affirm his existence with no brain activity and no sensory input whatsoever, the argument demonstrates that the soul can persist and think with absolutely no physical substrate at all.
