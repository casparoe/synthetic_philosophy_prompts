Me and a friend got into it over pints about why duelling vanished in Britain. He reckons its all Bernard Williams' point: duelling made sense inside an honour world and once that social framework crumbled the practice just became conceptually empty, not immoral. I countered with Appiah's line on foot-binding and slavery — honour codes shift when they stop serving anyone's practical interests, not because of some sudden moral insight. He asked why people then felt shame rather than just indifference. I said shame's just the emotional hangover when an interest-based norm collapses. He called that reducing morality to economics. Then he brought up abolition: he claimed slavery ended because gentlemanly honour changed, not because sugar got less profitable. I said the economic argument isn't that profits vanished, but that the relative cost of coercion rose against wage labour. He said thats just post-hoc rationalising. Can you go through the moves and rate who's right on each bit? I don't want a simple winner — tell me what both of us are missing too.
