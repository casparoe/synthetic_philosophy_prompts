I've been trying to work out whether structural realism collapses into ontic structural realism and I think I've built an argument that proves way too much but I can't find the leak. (1) Structural realism says we only have epistemic access to the mathematical structure of theories, not to the nature of the unobservable entities that realize that structure. (2) If that's right, then for any accepted theory there's an infinite family of ontologically distinct realizers—permutation variants that swap all intrinsic natures while keeping relations intact. (3) Since empirical evidence only constrains structure, no experiment can ever discriminate between a model and its variants. (4) So if structural realism holds, theory change is never a move toward the true ontology; it's just selecting one arbitrary member of a structural equivilance class. (5) But structural realism is usually motivated by the no-miracles argument: science succeeds because its structures approximate real structures. (6) If every variant is ontologically on par, there's no fact of the matter about whether our structures approximate reality's ontology—only whether they match its structure. (7) So structural realism either kills the no-miracles argument or admits scientific success is a miracle, since structure-matching alone doesn't explain predictive success without ontological approximation. (8) Therefore structural realism is either self-defeating or redundant with empiricism. Which step is wrong? I'd like to keep (1) and (5) but I need to know the price.
