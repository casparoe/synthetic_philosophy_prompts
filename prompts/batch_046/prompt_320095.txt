I am writing as a senior in an independent-study philosophy seminar, and I have developed a view that I want to stress-test before presenting it to my advisor. Drawing on Dennett's two-bitser argument against Searle's claim that original intentionality is biologically intrinsic, I hold that there is no metaphysically significant boundary between original and derived intentionality: evolutionary design is structurally identical to engineering, only slower, so human aboutness is derived from natural selection in precisely the way that a computer's is derived from its programmers. On this functionalist account, intentionality is constituted by sustained predictive tracking, normative error-correction, and coherence across contexts rather than by any internal biological property. Consequently, I maintain that a large language model with sufficient contextual persistence and self-monitoring exhibits the same structural kind of aboutness—derived, yes, but no less real—that Searle grants to books and calculators.

What would have to be true for this position to be wrong? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a restatement of the Chinese Room intuition, which I think confuses syntax with sustained functional coherence. Specifically, which empirical findings in neuroscience or artificial intelligence—perhaps about the necessity of embodied sensorimotor loops, or about specific architectures of self-modeling that artificial systems consistently fail to achieve—would count as decisive defeaters rather than merely awkward accommodations? Furthermore, has my view qualified intentionality so broadly—extending it to any system with predictive tracking—that it has become unfalsifiable, and if so, is that insulation a philosophical vice or a defensible feature of a functionalist framework? I am especially interested in whether there is any intermediate case—say, a synthetic neural cluster lacking evolutionary history but exhibiting genuine normative commitment—that would force me to concede that original intentionality is more than a useful fiction.
