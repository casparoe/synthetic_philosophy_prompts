I'm in a history-philosophy discusing grp and we got stuck on the Hitler counterfactual, but I think I pushed it into a corner. Here's the argument I built and now it feels like it eats itself.

(1) If structural factors alone (Versailles, depression, weak Wiemar institutions) were enough to produce a fascist German state, then anyone filling the "charismatic nationalist leader" slot would have done roughly the same job.

(2) Call that slot the "functional role" — the person who merges the party, controls the SA, and becomes chancellor.

(3) Now suppose some other guy, call him X, fills that role in 1933. X doesn't have Hitler's specific antisemitic obsession or his particlar strategic instincts.

(4) So X probably doesn't launch the Holocaust in the same form, and maybe doesn't invade the USSR in 1941.

(5) But then "there would have been a Nazi Germany without Hitler" is true in one sense (a fascist regime) and false in another (the actual Holocaust and war path).

(6) If structuralists say "yes, there'd still be a Nazi Germany," they're only right about the regime-type, not about the actual historical tragedy we're usually asking about.

(7) So either structuralism is trivial (it predicts some fascist regime but misses all the specifics that matter morally), or it has to claim that the Holocaust and Barbarossa were structurally inevitable regardless of who filled the role.

(8) But the second horn is implausible — surely X could have stopped at authoritarian nationalism without genocide.

(9) Therefore structural explanations either collapse into triviality or overclaim inevitability, and the Great Man view wins by default.

Where's the slip? I feel like there's an equivocation hiding between "Nazi Germany" as a kind of state and "Nazi Germany" as this specific chain of events, but I can't tell if that's the real problem or if I'm just dodging the hard choice in (7). If I have to pick, I'd rather save the idea that structures constrain without determining every detail, but I don't see how to do that without making structuralism useless for explaining why this particular horror happened. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost me?
