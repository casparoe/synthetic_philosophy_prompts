I'm trying to figure out if there's a term for this move I keep seeing in populist rhetoric. A leader or movement says they speak for "the will of the people" and points to big rallies or election wins as proof. But then when polls or referenda go against them, they don't say they were wrong about the will - they say the people have been lied to by elites, or that the opponents aren't "the real people" anyway. So support counts as direct revelation of the popular will, but opposition counts as contamination that needs filtering out. It's not just confirmation bias, because there's a specifc asymmetry in how democratic procedures are treated: winning validates the leader's access to the people, losing reveals that the procedure was captured. Is there an established concept for this exact double-standard, maybe in democratic theory or populism studies? And if not, how would you distinguish it from ordinary post-hoc rationaliztion?
