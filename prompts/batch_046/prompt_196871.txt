I hold the view that "collapse"—whether applied to the Late Bronze Age palace economies, the Classic Maya lowlands, or Easter Island's monument-building society—is a misdescription born of state-centrism: societies do not fail but undergo organizational decoherence, in which hierarchical integration dissolves while social networks persist in altered form. What would genuinely count against this position? Provide a committed, not ecumenical, assessment: rank the kinds of evidence or argument that would be decisive versus merely awkward, specify what a defender of this view should herself concede as damaging, identify whether the position makes any risky predictions that its rivals do not, and judge whether it has been qualified into unfalsifiability—if so, whether that insulation is a conceptual virtue or a defect. I am especially interested in whether archaeological or ethnographic cases exist that resist being redescribed as mere organizational change, and whether the distinction I draw relies on a contestable ontology of social form.
