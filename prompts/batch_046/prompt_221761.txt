I'm serving on the examination committee for our upper-level metaphysics seminar, and I need a 90-minute essay question that forces students to manipulate the conceptual machinery of material constitution rather than simply reproduce the standard statue-and-clay exchange from the literature. To that end, please design a problem built around a deliberately engineered case: an installation artwork titled Iterative Form consists of four hundred fungible ceramic modules arranged in a precise geometric configuration. The work is maintained by an automated protocol that continuously removes degraded modules and replaces them with new ones, storing retired pieces in a warehouse. Every ten years, the entire assembly is completely disassembled into storage bins, the warehouse of retired modules is emptied and recycled, and the geometric form is rebuilt—potentially with an entirely different physical set of modules—while a single original ceramic module remains sealed, untouched, in a separate vault.

The exam prompt should ask candidates to determine whether there is a statue (or artwork-object) distinct from the aggregate of modules present at any time, whether it survives the total disassembly and reconstruction, and whether the classic arguments for non-identity—the modal argument about differing persistence conditions and the coincidence argument about distinct properties—still apply when both the putative statue and the aggregate share the same disassembly conditions and when the material substrate is itself fungible and replaceable. Please provide: (1) the exam question itself, calibrated for 90 minutes and worth 20 marks; (2) a model answer that articulates at least three defensible positions (for example, a strict constitution view that privileges organizational form, an eliminativist view that denies any object over and above the modules, and a revised functionalist view that makes persistence depend on continuous organizational history rather than on continuous material overlap); and (3) a marking scheme built around five must-hit analytical moves, with marks allocated out of 20, plus a one-line note on the characteristic failure mode for each. The difficulty should come from applying the framework to ambiguous persistence conditions, not from recalling philosopher names, specific texts, or bibliographic references.
