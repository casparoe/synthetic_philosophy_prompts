Since joining the AI governance review board last month, I have been trying to understand our implicit criteria for formalizing "low-impact" or "side-effect-avoiding" AI without requiring a complete specification of human values. Our track record on formal proposals is as follows: we approved a system that bounded physical and informational displacement against a null-intervention counterfactual; we rejected one that defined side effects negatively as any state-change outside the task specification, on the grounds that delineating the task boundary already imports normative commitments; we conditionally approved a metric based on comparative statics with a frozen-world baseline, but restricted it to closed-domain robotics where the baseline is well-defined; we rejected a proposal that used predicted human-approval divergence as the impact measure, arguing that approval patterns embed contested values; and we approved, with reservations, an energy-and-compute footprint cap, noting it captures resource side effects but misses semantic and social ones.

We now face a proposal for an open-ended scientific-research agent. It introduces a "descriptive-ontology preservation" constraint: the agent computes the Kolmogorov complexity of world-state descriptions relative to a pre-deployment ontology and is forbidden from taking actions that force the introduction of new primitive predicates into that ontology. The proponents claim this avoids side effects without specifying values, because it restricts only the vocabulary needed to describe outcomes, not the outcomes themselves.

If we want to be consistent with ourselves, should we approve, reject, or conditionally approve? Which earlier ruling is the closest analog? I want the rule we are actually running—whether it is that mechanical measurability is necessary but insufficient, that counterfactual comparison requires a static reference frame, or that any non-trivial boundary condition smuggles in normative content—together with the alternative rules that fit our track record equally well. Finally, specify one further hypothetical proposal, with its core mechanism, that would force us to choose between those rules.
