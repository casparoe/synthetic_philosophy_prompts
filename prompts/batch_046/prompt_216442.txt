I was discussing democratic backsliding with colleagues in a comparative-politics reading group, and we kept talking past one another because we seemed to be using the same word—backsliding—to track different phenomena: some meant procedural erosion of formal checks, others meant substantive collapse in pluralism and rights-protection, and others treated the label itself as a strategic partisan accusation rather than an analytical category. I am constructing a conceptual taxonomy, structured as a branching decision tree, that sorts actual positions by the answers people give to concrete diagnostic questions rather than by which country case they invoke or which theoretical camp they claim. The tree should begin with a split on whether backsliding is defined procedurally (measurable degradation of electoral rules, judicial independence, and institutional checks) or substantively (decline in liberal values, minority inclusion, and democratic culture regardless of formal continuity).

From there, branch further on whether identifying backsliding requires an authoritative expert arbiter with comparative baselines, whether it can be recognized through purely domestic partisan contestation, and whether intentional authoritarian design is a necessary condition or whether "institutional drift"—cumulative legal-but-norm-violating changes that no single actor orchestrates—counts as genuine backsliding on its own. I need leaves that accommodate at least these commitments: the view that Hungary and Poland represent clear backsliding while recent US developments do not; the view that Turkey demonstrates irreversibility once judicial capture is complete; the view that in all four cases the concept functions primarily as a partisan weapon with little independent analytical content; and the view that backsliding can occur without malign intent through structural drift alone. For each leaf, provide either a recognized label (e.g., institutionalist, partisan-skeptic, drift-theorist) or mark it "unnamed," and include one sentence on its most awkward commitment—something its defenders must accept but rarely emphasize. Please flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, especially where procedural and substantive criteria overlap in practice, and phrase every node as a question answerable without prior knowledge of the taxonomy's labels.
