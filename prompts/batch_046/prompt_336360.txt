For an upper-level seminar exam on rationality (30 minutes, approximately 600 words, 30 marks), write a single essay question that cannot be answered by summarizing Herbert Simon. Present this concrete scenario: during a prolonged multi-casualty disaster, a triage system assigns patients priority scores, applies an aspiration-level cutoff for receiving care, and—after each round of resource depletion—automatically lowers that cutoff rather than re-optimizing allocations across all remaining patients and resources. Ask the student to determine whether this adaptive lowering remains an instance of Simon-style satisficing, collapses into constrained optimization, or exemplifies a distinct failure mode—such as ecological rationality turned pathological, or aspiration-level revision that undermines the stability conditions required for genuine satisficing because it destroys the reference class needed to judge outcomes adequate. Provide a model answer that identifies the four conceptual moves required for full marks: (1) distinguishing adjustment of an aspiration level from revision of underlying preference orderings; (2) relating the cascading cutoff to Simon’s claim that rationality is bounded by and adapted to environmental structure; (3) assessing whether downward adjustment preserves the logic of accepting the first option that meets a threshold, or instead creates a feedback loop that eliminates its own benchmark; and (4) identifying which factor—cognitive limitation, environmental match, or procedural rigidity—does the explanatory work in this case. Include a marking scheme that allocates marks to each move and lists specific misconceptions that should cost points, for example treating satisficing as synonymous with any non-optimal heuristic, or interpreting bounded rationality as mere cognitive deficiency rather than an adaptive strategy. The response must state how confident it is in its framing of Simon’s conditions for satisficing and must single out the specific point about which it is least certain. Keep the entire response within 450 words.
