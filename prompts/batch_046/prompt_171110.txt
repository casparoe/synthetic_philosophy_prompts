At my uni the cognitive science dept has this weird institutional split. The human subjects ethics form literally makes you check a box saying you'll describe participants using belief-desire language — like the subject believed the cup was hidden — and if you write purely mechanistic descriptions your protocol gets kicked back. But in the neuroscience lab manual there's a no folk psychology rule: you have to describe the same experiments with only neural and behavioral terms, and using he wanted or she thought gets marked down. What philosophical commitments about minds does that split presuppose? Is it assuming folk psychology is real but unscientific, or that its just a practical fiction for ethics? And if eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires is correct, should we drop the belief-desire checkbox from ethics forms, or is there some non-ontological reason the practice makes sense anyway? Also, is the split even consistent — can you really treat beliefs as mandatory for moral review but banned from scientific explanation without contradicting yourself?
