Compare F. H. Bradley's regress argument against the reality of external relations with Bertrand Russell's theory of judgment as an account of propositional unity, concentrating specifically on how each framework accounts for the directedness of asymmetric relational propositions—such as the distinction between A is taller than B and B is taller than A. Then consider a hypothetical amendment to Bradley's view, according to which relations possess an intrinsic directional character that binds relata without requiring a further relating relation; call this the intrinsic directionality thesis. Evaluate whether this amendment allows Bradley's position to avoid the infinite regress while still explaining propositional asymmetry, or whether it collapses into a structure functionally equivalent to Russell's appeal to the directional sense of a mental act. In your response, clearly distinguish the metaphysical commitments of the original Bradleyan account, Russell's psychological analysis, and the proposed amendment.
