You serve as the senior clinical ethicist and philosopher of medicine advising the hospital’s Joint Institutional Review and Clinical Ethics Board. Before drafting the document, please think carefully and begin with a brief outline: identify the two principal philosophical accounts of why pain is harmful—one that locates the harm in its intrinsic phenomenal quality and one that locates it in its motivational or aversive force—and explain briefly how congenital pain asymbolia, in which patients register tissue damage without emotional distress or protective avoidance behaviour, bears on each account.

We are meeting on Thursday to decide on Protocol SAS-09, a pilot study proposed by the neurosurgical research group. The protocol employs targeted, reversible neuromodulation to suppress limbic and anterior cingulate responses to nociceptive input during necessary orthopaedic procedures, while leaving primary somatosensory pathways intact. Patients would remain conscious, able to report the location and intensity of surgical stimuli, but would describe these sensations as lacking urgency, unpleasantness, or threat—functionally replicating an acquired, temporary state analogous to congenital pain asymbolia. The investigators argue that because the badness of pain resides entirely in its affective-motivational dimension, removing that dimension eliminates ethical harm; they further claim that preserving raw sensation allows intraoperative feedback and avoids the cardiorespiratory risks of general anaesthesia. A coalition of anaesthesiologists and patient-advocacy representatives contends that the phenomenal quality of pain is itself harmful regardless of emotional response, and that inducing a dissociation between bodily damage and protective motivation creates a distinct, poorly understood harm: a form of alienation from one’s own defensive interests, potentially leaving patients vulnerable to unnoticed injury and undermining the integrity of the body-mind relation.

Please draft the formal written opinion—no more than 1,200 words—that I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with an explicit time frame: either conditional approval with defined safeguards and a mandatory review milestone within ninety days, or rejection with a clear pathway and criteria for resubmission. Your recommendation must be actionable by the Board. The reasoning should engage the investigators’ philosophical claim seriously, not dismiss it as wordplay, and should treat the strongest consideration on the opposing side with equal intellectual honesty. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the Board needs reasoning it can evaluate without external reference. Write in polished, formal British English, suitable for circulation to clinicians, legal counsel, and lay board members.
