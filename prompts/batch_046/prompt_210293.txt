I am drafting a scene for a philosophical novel in which a physicist-philosopher, confined since birth to a monochrome laboratory, has mastered every propositional fact about color vision—including an obsolete chromatic taxonomy reconstructed from a lost Boreal lexicon—yet has never experienced hue. Write a Petrarchan sonnet in her voice, spoken at the moment she steps outside and sees cerulean. The octave should catalog her knowledge in enclosed interlocking rhymes (ABBAABBA), while the sestet should open into CDECDE as she confronts the phenomenal quality itself. Let the volta at line nine turn not on mere surprise but on the precise inadequacy of her taxonomy: she identifies the wavelength she calculated, yet the experience overflows every entry in her reconstructed lexicon. Render the knowledge argument with precision—the claim that complete physical information leaves phenomenal knowledge untouched—so that the rhyme scheme’s shift from enclosure to openness structurally mirrors the gap between knowing-that and knowing-what-it-is-like. A reader familiar with Mary’s room should recognize the parallel; a reader unfamiliar should still find a coherent lyric about the limits of description.
