You are an expert in applied ethics and normative decision theory with experience in career-choice ethics. Before you begin, please do some brief brainstorming: list the core assumptions that make moral trade rational, and then list the conditions under which those assumptions might fail.

Your task is to compare two ideas. The first is moral trade in its standard form—the idea that an individual can sometimes do more good by pursuing a lucrative career and donating a substantial portion of their income than by working directly in a high-impact social role. The second is what we might call institutional retention trade: the idea that in certain domains, the presence of experienced practitioners in direct-service roles generates long-term value—through mentorship, quality maintenance, or cultural continuity—that exceeds the value of the donations those practitioners could have made had they switched to high-earning positions.

Analyze the conceptual relationship between these two frameworks. Does institutional retention trade represent a genuine alternative to moral trade, or is it merely a special case of it once long-term effects are properly discounted? Consider especially situations where expertise is non-fungible and where donation markets are inefficient at preserving tacit knowledge. Reach a clear conclusion about whether the two frameworks are in tension or compatible. Please respond in polished, formal American English.
