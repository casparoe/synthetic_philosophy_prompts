I am a philosophy professor preparing to present my thesis to an interdisciplinary colloquium on moral psychology and metaethics. My thesis is that the documented divergence between WEIRD and non-WEIRD populations in the weighting of harm, purity, and fairness norms does not defeat moral objectivity, but requires reconceiving it as domain-indexed constructivism: within each culturally stabilized moral grammar, objective normative constraints operate, yet no culture-transcendent metric exists to adjudicate conflicts across grammars. I need a Q&A preparation sheet, not a defense of the view: list the objections I am most likely to face, in the order they will arise and by descending damage to my position. For each objection, phrase it as the specific kind of interlocutor—realist, anthropologist, or experimentalist—would phrase it; indicate what I should concede, what I should hold, and where my reply is weakest. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks, specifically the challenge that asks me to specify non-circular criteria for ranking one moral grammar as objectively superior to another without smuggling universalist premises back in. The framing should remain conceptual; do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and target the argument rather than my familiarity with specific studies.
