The central claim is that L eliminates the paradox of instantaneous self-nullification—where an amendment could revoke its own authorizing rule—but introduces a distinct conceptual flaw: during the lock period, the system operates under an authority that has been democratically suspended yet remains legally binding, creating a gap between legitimation and enforcement.

- Does L actually generate a logical discontinuity in legal authority, or only a temporal delay that authority can bridge?
- Is democratic legitimation necessarily continuous, such that any interim period without active authorization undermines validity?
- To what extent does your judgment depend on treating legal authority as a persistent state versus an ongoing flow?
