As a journalist covering the resurgence of interest in long-range economic philosophy, I have been returning to John Maynard Keynes's well-known essay on economic possibilities for our grandchildren, not for its familiar prediction of a drastically shortened working week, but for its under-examined account of human flourishing once material scarcity is solved. Keynes draws a sharp distinction between absolute needs, which are naturally satiable, and relative needs, which are fueled by social comparison and can expand indefinitely; he further suggests that the intense drive for wealth accumulation is essentially a mental morbidity appropriate to an age of deprivation, and that true civilization begins when societies turn from money-making to the cultivation of leisure, art, and culture. Yet there is a tension in this vision: Keynes treats labor as primarily instrumental, a necessary evil to be minimized by technology, without fully confronting the possibility that work might also serve as a foundational source of social identity, moral structure, and collective purpose.

For a feature I am preparing on automation and the meaning of work, I would like you to write an essay that examines whether Keynes's conception of the good life is coherent in light of this tension. Specifically, weigh his optimism about post-scarcity culture against the argument that automating labor away might not liberate people for higher pursuits but instead destabilize the psychological and social conditions that make a meaningful life possible. Reach and defend a clear conclusion: either Keynes was correct that abundance naturally redirects human energy toward aesthetic and intellectual fulfillment, or his framework collapses because it underestimates the non-instrumental value of necessary work and overestimates our readiness for unstructured leisure. In your justification, identify precisely which element of Keynes's psychology of needs, his aesthetics of leisure, or his sociology of status is carrying the weight of your argument, and explain how a defender of Keynes might respond to the charge that he ignored labor's role in constituting personal identity.
