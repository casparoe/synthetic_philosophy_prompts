I am preparing the final examination for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in philosophy of history. Please draft one essay question, together with a model answer of approximately 800 words and a marking scheme. The question must address the competing cyclical and linear accounts of historical change associated with Polybius, Ibn Khaldun, Spengler, and Toynbee, but it must be unanswerable by mere summary: students should have to adjudicate whether these four frameworks share a single cyclical logic or divide into structurally incompatible kinds—specifically, between mechanistic cycles driven by institutional decay or group-solidarity erosion, and organic cycles driven by vitalist cultural maturation or spiritual exhaustion. To prevent regurgitation, embed a hypothetical scenario: a society that achieves permanent technological and institutional stability without any corresponding spiritual, artistic, or cultural decline. Ask students to apply each thinker's framework to this case and determine whether it counts as a counterexample to cyclical determinism, reveals hidden linear-progressive assumptions within the theory, or collapses the cyclical/linear distinction altogether. The model answer should identify that Polybius's anacyclosis and Ibn Khaldun's asabiyyah cycle are mechanistic—dependent on constitutional rotation and the erosion of collective solidarity, respectively—while Spengler's culture-to-civilization trajectory is organic and deterministic, governed by an internal life-cycle rather than institutional mechanics; it should further argue that Toynbee's challenge-and-response model is fundamentally linear-progressive, treating cyclical setbacks as temporary failures of creative response rather than inevitable phases of a closed loop. The marking scheme should allocate 25 marks total, specify the five analytical moves required for an A-grade response with point values for each, and name the specific misconception or omission that typically separates an A from a B answer—such as conflating Ibn Khaldun's sociological mechanism with Spengler's vitalist morphology, treating Toynbee as purely cyclical rather than teleologically progressive, or failing to use the hypothetical stasis-case to test determinism versus contingency. The exam is 90 minutes; answers should be about 800 words. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the answer must stand on conceptual analysis alone.
