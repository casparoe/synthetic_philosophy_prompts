Please make the case that contemporary naturalist theories of life's meaning—exemplified by Wolf's fittingness view and objective accounts requiring modal robustness—solve a pseudo-problem created by conflating two normative layers: the internal coherence of valuable projects ('project-meaning') and the non-derivative justification for sustaining conscious existence through them ('existential warrant'). Argue that Camus-style absurdism undermines only the second layer, not the first, and that treating 'meaning in life' as a unified optimization target mistakes the collapse of cosmic purpose for a crisis of local value. I'm writing for a rationalist audience that is skeptical of vague existentialism but open to precise normative distinctions; the argument should specify exactly which kind of reason survives the denial of transcendent purpose and which kind genuinely evaporates.
