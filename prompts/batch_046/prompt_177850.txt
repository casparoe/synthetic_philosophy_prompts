My study group got into this after our phil of rel seminar and now I'm stuck. I think I've build an argument that breaks both James and Clifford at once, but I can't find the seperate flaw. (1) Clifford's rule is that it's always wrong to believe p without sufficient evidence. (2) But 'sufficient' is itself a normative standard, and adopting any standard requires believing that standard is the right one. (3) That belief can't be justified by the standard itself without circularity, and there's no neutral ground outside all standards from which to justify it. (4) So Clifford's own view commits him to holding a core belief without sufficient evidence. (5) James allows belief without full evidence only when the option is live, forced, and momentous. (6) Picking an epistemic framework is live (pragmatism, evidentialism, etc. are real alternatives), forced (you can't just have no standards), and momentous (it governs every other belief you form). (7) So James actually permits Clifford to hold his evidentialist commitment. (8) But then Clifford's universal ban collapses, since he's using the very method he forbids. (9) Even worse, if James allows this move for Clifford, he allows it for anyone: every dogmatist can claim their core creed is a live, forced, momentous framework choice. (10) So James's criteria don't restrain belief at all, they just license dogmatism with extra steps. I keep thinking the slip is between (3) and (4) — maybe holding a standard isn't the same kind of belief Clifford targets? — or in (6), where maybe framework choice isn't 'forced' in James's strict sense. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd really like to keep (1) and (5) intact, but I need to know what I'm paying.
