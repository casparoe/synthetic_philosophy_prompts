(1) Kant's catagorical imperitive says never treat people as mere means.
(2) If the council approves a tax that helps schools but hurts pensioners, they're using pensioners as a means to educate kids.

(5) But councils clearly can do right things, so Kant must be wrong about this.

Where does this go wrong? I really want to keep (1) — it feels right that you can't just use people — but (4) feels like it proves too much. My teacher mentioned 'threshold deontology' but I don't get how that helps. Is there a way to save Kant here without saying government is impossible?
