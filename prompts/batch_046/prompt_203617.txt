My friend and I have just finished a long, circular argument about whether prioritarianism can survive the leveling-down objection, and I want us to stop talking past each other by producing the adversarial-collaboration document we should have written from the start. One of us defends prioritarianism: she holds that giving greater weight to benefits for the worse-off never entails harming the better-off, and that leveling-down scenarios misrepresent the view. The other of us thinks the objection is decisive—if well-being has diminishing marginal value, then equality achieved by reducing the best-off can be better even with no compensating gain, and prioritarian aggregation must endorse it.

Please draft our joint statement. First, include a numbered section of claims both of us can sign: about the formal structure of prioritarian aggregation (how weights depend on absolute well-being levels), about what a pure leveling-down scenario actually involves, and about the distinction between ex post evaluation of outcomes and ex ante evaluation under risk. Second, provide a disagreements table containing the exact formulations of our remaining differences, each phrased in wording its own side accepts, with the cruxes separated out. I take the cruxes to be (i) whether prioritarian weighting ever generates a reason to actively reduce well-being for equality’s sake when no one benefits, and (ii) whether shifting to an ex ante, risk-weighted framework dissolves the intuitive force of the objection. For each crux, specify a concrete case or line of reasoning both of us agree is relevant—for instance, a choice between a safe but unequal distribution and a risky but more equal one—and record in advance what each side predicts it will show. If part of our dispute turns out to be verbal—for example, over whether prioritarianism names only an individual decision rule or also an institutional design principle—identify which part and why. Keep the document focused and formal; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
