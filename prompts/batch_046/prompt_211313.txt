For a graduate seminar on epistemic ethics, lay out the desiderata that a normative theory of belief-formation must meet to resolve the tension between William James's defense of voluntary belief-adoption in live, forced, and momentous options, and W.K. Clifford's evidentialist prohibition against believing on insufficient evidence. For each criterion, state the specific conceptual or practical consideration that motivates it — for instance, whether the theory must accommodate the phenomenological involuntariness of perceptual belief, whether it must distinguish between direct and indirect doxastic control, or whether it must preserve a distinction between pragmatic and evidential reasons without collapsing them. Mark each desideratum as either non-negotiable or as a camp-specific preference associated with either the Jamesian or Cliffordian position. Finally, assess whether any subset of the non-negotiable criteria is jointly unsatisfiable given well-established constraints on human cognitive architecture; that assessment is the central deliverable. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; brief remarks on where James's and Clifford's own positions violate specific desiderata are welcome but secondary.
