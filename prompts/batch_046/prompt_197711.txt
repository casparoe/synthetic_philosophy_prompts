Right, since I retired from the physics department and started reading philosophy of science for fun, I've been trying to pin down exactly where I stand on Wigner's old claim about the unreasonnable effectiveness of mathematics. I want to check whether my views actually cohere before I bore my friends with them at the pub. So here's my package:

(1) I think Wigner was onto something real — mathematics doesn't just describe nature conveniently, it captures deep structural features with a precision that goes far beyond any practical need, and that genuinely needs explaining.

(2) But I also think the best explanation is evolutionary: our mathematical cognition evolved because it helped our ancestors navigate space, quantity, and pattern, so maths is essentially formalised common sense tuned by natural selection, not some mystical access to abstract realms.

(3) Despite that, I'm drawn to mathematical Platonism — I think numbers, sets, and structures exist mind-independantly, regardless of whether anyone thinks about them.

(4) At the same time, I'm fairly instrumentalist about physics: I take physical theories as powerful predictive instruments rather than literal descriptions of an underlying reality.

(5) And I reckon that if we ever met aliens with radically different evolutionary histories, they'd have developed a completely different mathematics, even though the physical laws they encounter are universal.

So does this hang together? It feels like (1) and (2) pull in opposite directions — if the effectiveness is just evolved common sense, why call it unreasonnable? And (3) with (2) looks shaky: if maths is evolved brain-stuff, why believe in mind-independant Platonic objects? Meanwhile (4) makes me wonder what the effectiveness of maths in physics actually demonstrates if physics itself isn't describing reality. Then there's (5): if maths is contingent on our evolution, but physics is universal, how can maths be so tightly woven into physics?

That said, maybe some of these tensions are only apparent? I'd like an audit, ordered by severity: which pairs are genuinely in conflict, which ones just look awkward but can be reconciled without too much cost, and what's the least painful revision if I want to keep the core of my view. No need for bibliographies or a reading list — I can't chase down papers easily, and I'm not asking you to cite anyone. Just tell me where the cracks are and what I'd have to give up.
