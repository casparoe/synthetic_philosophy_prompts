Before drafting, please think carefully about what makes a collaboration document genuinely joint rather than a compromise, and begin with brief brainstorming: list the categories of claims both sides must be able to sign without reservation, then distinguish verbal disputes from substantive ones.

My seminar has hosted a year-long exchange between two camps on the coherence and acceptability of national character. Camp A—the Dispositional Cluster Theorists—holds that populations can exhibit stable, non-essentialist statistical regularities in values and behavioral dispositions, and that a carefully circumscribed concept of national character is sometimes epistemically and politically useful if properly qualified by internal variance. Camp B—the Anti-Generalization Constructivists—holds that any ascription of shared character to a people is either conceptually incoherent given internal heterogeneity and historical fluidity, or normatively impermissible because it risks reification, stereotyping, and the erasure of minority voices, regardless of statistical qualification.

Please draft the joint statement they should have produced. Use this structure: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims both camps can sign; (2) a disagreements table rendering each camp’s precise position in neutral wording that both sides accept as accurate to their own view; (3) a list of cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp; and (4) for each crux, one concrete case or body of evidence both sides agree bears on it, together with each camp’s explicit prediction about what that case shows.

In particular, separate essentialism (a metaphysical claim about necessary properties shared by all members) from statistical clustering (an empirical claim about modal distributions), and clarify whether predictive utility can ever justify normative risk in cross-cultural description. Address the modal profile problem I have introduced: if a disposition is held by a clear statistical majority but actively rejected by substantial minority subcultures within the same polity, does that pattern still constitute a national character, or does it collapse into a description of the dominant subgroup? Keep the document formal, precise, and entirely free of bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. The deliverable should be a constructive text both parties could put their names to, with every residual disagreement stated exactly.
