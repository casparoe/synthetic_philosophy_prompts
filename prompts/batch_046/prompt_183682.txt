Write an essay on whether multiple realizability genuinely undermines functionalism's ambition for a unified mental science, or whether it only requires a stratified taxonomy that distinguishes realization-insensitive functional roles from realization-sensitive architectures. Center your analysis on a specific conceptual tension: if octopus nociception and human nociception exhibit parallel avoidance and protective-signaling behaviors despite radically different neural substrates, do they share one mental kind—pain—or does the substrate divergence reveal that "pain" denotes a family of locally analogous but globally dissimilar states? Evaluate the defense that functionalism can survive by retreating to domain-specific local functionalism, and the counter that such retreat abandons the cross-species generality that made functionalism attractive. Reach a justified conclusion—perhaps that mental kinds are interest-relative rather than natural, or that functionalism must embrace a multilevel ontology in which architectural differences bear on kind-identity.
