Compare the standard Chalmers paradox of phenomenal judgment with a variant I'm calling the 'mute witness' problem. Classic version: if consciousness is epiphenominal, our beliefs about it are physically caused with no causal link to the experience itself, so why trust them? Mute-witness version: imagine a cognitively isolated stream that actually has the phenomenal experience but can't affect speech or behavior, while our verbal stream reports correctly but feels nothing — it's tracking via some physical correlation, not causation from experience. Which one actually breaks epiphenomenalism, and does the mute-witness case accidentally save it by showing tracking doesn't require causal efficacy? Bottom line up front, then the meat.
