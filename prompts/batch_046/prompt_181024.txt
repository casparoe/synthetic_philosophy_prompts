Defend the claim that the formal equivalence between Solomonoff induction, Kolmogorov complexity, and Occam's razor conceals a substantive metaphysical commitment rather than delivering a purely epistemic principle. Specifically, argue that privileging hypotheses with lower Kolmogorov complexity implicitly assumes that the data-generating process is computable within a universal Turing-machine architecture, thereby embedding a digital-ontological bias into what is usually presented as a neutral prior. Address how the invariance theorem—showing that complexity measures differ by only an additive constant across universal machines—fails to eliminate this bias, and explain why this matters for whether Solomonoff induction genuinely justifies scientific preference for simplicity or merely encodes a contingent physical ontology. In doing so, distinguish your position from standard Bayesian defenses of simplicity and from purely syntactic interpretations of algorithmic information theory.
