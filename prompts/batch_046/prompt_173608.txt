I am a novelist who occasionally teaches an upper-level seminar in philosophy, and I am drafting the final examination for my course on well-being and value. I need one essay question—approximately seven hundred words, seventy-five minutes—on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: what, if anything, all pleasures have in common. The question must be unanswerable by reciting Mill’s distinction between higher and lower pleasures or Feldman’s attitudinal account. Present a concrete triad: the pleasure of finally grasping an abstract proof, the pleasure of easing a chronic itch, and the pleasure taken in a rival’s misfortune. Ask students to reconstruct the strongest hedonist defense of treating these three as contributing to well-being in a single currency despite the absence of any shared introspectively detectable quality, specifically by evaluating a reply that appeals not to phenomenal uniformity but to sub-personal “positive-valence anchoring” or to functional equivalence within a reward system. Then require them to determine whether that reply saves hedonism, collapses it into value pluralism, or renders it circular through a hidden normative filter on which pleasures “count.” Please provide the exam question, a model answer of about seven hundred words, and a marking scheme totaling twenty points that lists five specific moves a top answer must contain, assigns marks to each, and notes the characteristic error that typically costs points on each. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations; the answer should stand entirely on its own reasoning.
