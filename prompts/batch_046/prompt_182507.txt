There's this idea people in metaethics call free-floating rationales -- reasons that exist without any reasoner, like normative force just hanging out in the void with no agent to relate too. Some philosophers treat it as obvious once you ditch subjectivism; others say it's pure mysticism, reasons are inherently relational and need a subject. The debate's been stuck for ages. One camp keeps refining accounts of how reasons can be "real" independent of agents, the other keeps saying thats just re-labeling intuition. I think they're answering different questions -- one about ontology (do reasons exist as abstract entities?), one about practical authority (can something count as a reason for me if I'm not related?). But maybe I'm wrong and there's a hidden conceptual disagreement. Can you diagnose what's actually stalling this? Is it verbal, substantive, or are they just talking past eachother? And be honest: could any new distinction or argument actually end it, or is this just going to loop forever?
