hey, i keep seeing people say Solomonoff induction is basically "science as zip file" — like, the best theory is just the program that compresses all your observations into the shortest possible string, and Occam's razor is literally the 2^-length weighting in the universal prior. people use this to claim that LLMs minimizing cross-entropy are approximating ideal induction, or that we finally have an objective, formal "pick the simplest explanation" rule. i want to audit that anology.

lay out the correspondence table: what plays the role of the zip algorithm, the compressed file, the decompressor, the byte stream? then where it breaks — Solomonoff needs an uncomputable prior over all programs, real compression schemes depend on specific encoding languages, there's the lookup-table problem where a huge hardcoded table can be "short" in one language, and the invariance theorem only saves uniqueness up to an additive constant that can be enormous in practice. also, does the analogy smuggle in that there's a single correct "file format" for reality, when the prior is actually relative to your choice of universal Turing machine?

then check the conclusion people draw from it — that induction is "solved" normatively, or that simplicity gets an objective exponential bonus. once the disanologies are on the table (uncomputability, no oracle access to program space, resource-bounded agents can't approximate the prior well), does that conclusion survive, or was the compression picture doing all the argumentative work?

please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know this stuff — for instance, whether resource-bounded versions like MML or MDL rescue anything normative, or whether LLM training has any meaningful structural overlap with Solomonoff induction versus being a totally different beast. don't present a contested view as the consensus.
