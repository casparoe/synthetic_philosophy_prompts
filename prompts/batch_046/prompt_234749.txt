For our closing meeting, I'd like to present a Petrarchan sonnet that uses the form itself to do philosophical work. The octave should present tactile knowledge of a cube and a sphere through verbs of manipulation and resistance, with rhymes that pair these motor terms — for instance, grasp with class, turn with learn — so that the rhyme scheme embodies the internal coherence of the tactile field. The closing couplet should leave the problem suspended: not choosing between Locke's negative answer and the modern positive one, but suggesting that if tactile knowledge is genuinely motor-intentional, the question dissolves rather than receives an answer. Please make the imagery precise enough that someone familiar with the contemporary literature on sensory substitution and affordance theory would recognize the move, but do not cite specific authors, texts, or experiments, and do not resolve the tension with a sermon or a shrug. The poem should work for an audience of philosophy students who have been arguing about this for weeks.
