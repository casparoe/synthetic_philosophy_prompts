A philosophy blogger summarizes the classical debate on human nature like this: “Mencius holds that people are born with fully formed moral virtues—compassion, shame, deference, and judgment—that society only risks corrupting, so education is mainly protective. Xunzi holds that human nature is pure self-interested desire with no innate moral capacity whatsoever; the heart-mind is a blank instrument, and ritual propriety is a purely artificial technology invented by sages to restrain innate brutality. The disagreement is therefore between moral nativism and blank-slate constructivism, and modern evolutionary psychology supports Mencius because infants show innate empathy.” How fair is this summary? Go claim by claim: identify what a careful reader of the texts would accept, where the blogger conflates a nascent tendency with a completed virtue or ignores the necessity of active cultivation, what Xunzi actually grants about the heart-mind’s capacity for learning and approval, and whether framing the debate through evolutionary psychology is a legitimate extension or an anachronistic overreach. Finally, does the blogger’s conclusion that Mencius “wins” survive once the misrepresentations are corrected?
