I have noticed a recurring configuration in discussions of dispositions that I cannot place within the standard taxonomy. Here are two concrete instances.

First, consider a person with a genuine disposition to panic in emergencies. Precisely because they recognize this trait, they have undergone training that now suppresses the panic response whenever an emergency actually occurs. The training exists only because the panic disposition exists, yet it blocks the manifestation in exactly the conditions that should trigger it, while the underlying disposition remains fully intact. Second, consider a material that is chemically disposed to corrode in the presence of moisture, but the corrosion process itself generates a passivation layer that seals the surface from further moisture. The protective layer is present solely because the corrosive disposition is present, and it prevents the very outcome that defines the disposition.

In both cases, the blocker is not an independent external factor—like an added poison pill or protective sheath—nor is it a case where the disposition simply vanishes when the conditions for manifestation are met. Rather, the disposition persists unchanged, but produces its own obstacle.

Does this have an established name—perhaps a reflexive mask, an endogenous antidote, or a homeostatic fink? If not, how should it be distinguished from Martin-style finks, Johnston-style masks, and Bird-style antidotes, and does the conditional analysis of dispositions require a separate amendment for this structure? I am looking for the precise boundaries of the concept, not just a label.
