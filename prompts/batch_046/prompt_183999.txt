I recently joined a municipal sentencing review board, and I am trying to map the implicit philosophy of our chair, a retired appellate judge whose recommendations I must anticipate when drafting opinions. Over the past year she has ruled as follows: in an embezzlement case where the offender fully repaid the funds and showed genuine remorse, she recommended restorative mediation with no incarceration, on the ground that the purpose of state response is social repair rather than the infliction of suffering for its own sake; in a violent recidivist assault with minimal provocation, she insisted on the maximum statutory term, holding that when culpability is high and the act expresses contempt for others, the response must register that gravity independently of any future rehabilitative benefit; in a negligent homicide by a sober but distracted driver with no prior record, profound remorse, and a victim’s family requesting leniency, she approved a suspended sentence with community service, reasoning that punishment should not compound tragedy when risk is nil and repair is possible; in a corporate toxic-exposure case harming hundreds but lacking direct malice—only deliberate cost-cutting that ignored known risks—she imposed heavy fines and an industry ban but rejected prison, arguing that systemic harms call for structural correction rather than bodily confinement; and in a juvenile arson case driven by peer pressure, with the minor now in intensive therapy within a stable family, she directed rehabilitation with no record, maintaining that the state’s duty is to form character, not to balance a moral ledger.

The pending case is this: a first-time adult offender committed a premeditated, ideologically motivated hate assault causing serious but non-permanent injury, widely publicized and targeted at a protected group. Since conviction he has completed extensive psychological rehabilitation, expresses sustained remorse, and both the direct victim and representatives of the affected community have jointly requested a restorative justice process rather than incarceration; risk assessments indicate near-zero recidivism. The board’s concern is that a non-carceral response might fail to register the wrong to the broader public or to affirm the dignity of the targeted group.

Predict her recommendation. Identify at least two plausible implicit rules—such as direct retributivism scaled by culpability, contextual proportionality with victim-mediated mitigation, or indirect normativity mediated by idealized deliberation over the social rules rational agents would endorse for this class of wrongs—that jointly fit her track record, and state what each predicts here. Give your best guess, with reasons for preferring it, name the closest analog among her prior verdicts, and say, for each possible recommendation, what broader principle about punishment the board would be adopting. Finally, specify one further hypothetical case that would distinguish the rules you have identified.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning with no preamble and without restating the question.
