I am writing an essay for my philosophy of logic module on the problem of propositional unity, and I want to move beyond the standard classroom rehearsal of Bradley's regress—where relating a relation to its relata seems to generate an infinite hierarchy of further relations—together with Russell's reply that relations can relate externally without further mediation. The conceptual issue I need explained is whether an external relation genuinely resolves the unity problem or merely relocates it, particularly when we consider proposals that invoke a primitive, non-relational tie or nexus to bind constituents into a single proposition. Please take a committed position and defend it with reasons rather than laying out options neutrally: either argue that such a tie can coherently block the regress without becoming either redundant or itself a constituent in need of further binding, or argue that any such move either collapses back into Bradley's internal-relations monism or implicitly reintroduces the regress at the level of the tie's own application. In either case, I want you to show precisely where the chain is broken—or why it cannot be—without smuggling in a hidden relational glue.
