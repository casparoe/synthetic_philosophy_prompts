For a small philosophy reading group I have been asked to write down my current profile on conceivability and modal reasoning so the others can probe it. Here is the set of commitments I currently hold; I am not sure they all hang together.

1. I am a modal rationalist, but restricted to what I call "structural conceivability": if I can clearly conceive a scenario's complete relational and causal architecture without contradiction, then that architecture is metaphysically possible.

2. I think zombie worlds—physical duplicates lacking phenomenal consciousness—are descriptively conceivable but not structurally conceivable. Any full structural specification of conscious cognition, I claim, necessarily includes phenomenal properties within its causal network, so leaving them out is leaving out part of the structure. Hence zombies are impossible, and this defuses the standard anti-physicalist argument.

3. I think inverted-spectrum scenarios—identical physical and functional organization with swapped qualitative characters—are structurally conceivable, and therefore represent genuinely distinct metaphysical possibilities.

4. I hold that phenomenal concepts are irreducibly first-personal and indexical. This indexicality explains why no purely third-person physical description ever seems sufficient to entail phenomenal facts, but I deny that there is any corresponding ontological or metaphysical explanatory gap.

5. I maintain that if two worlds share every relational and causal property and differ only in the intrinsic, non-relational nature of certain phenomenal properties, then they are not two worlds at all, but one world described with different indexical labels; intrinsic differences that make no relational difference are metaphysically redundant.

6. I think that mathematical falsehoods such as 2 + 2 = 5 are descriptively conceivable only in a thin, superficial sense, but structurally inconceivable, since arithmetical structure belongs to the relational backbone of any world; hence they are impossible.

Go through these commitments and audit them for joint tenability. Identify which pairs are in real tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say exactly what has to give and what the cheapest revision would be, noting what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Finally, tell me which single revision—dropping or altering one commitment—would clean up the largest number of problems with the least collateral damage. I am not signaling which commitment I am most willing to abandon, and I do not want a general lecture on modal epistemology: I want an audit of this specific profile.
