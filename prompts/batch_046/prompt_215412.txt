My commitments are:
(1) Karma is best understood as a normative theory of moral desert — present circumstances are appropriate to the moral quality of past actions — rather than as a naturalistic causal law.

(3) Karmic accountability is nevertheless justified, because psychological continuity (not substantial identity) is sufficient for moral desert.
(4) A practitioner can alter the experiential quality or intensity of ripening karmic fruits through present mental discipline (e.g., cultivating metta or mindfulness) without retroactively cancelling the underlying moral debt.
(5) Genuine moral desert requires libertarian free will: the agent must have been able to do otherwise in a robust sense at the moment of the original action.
