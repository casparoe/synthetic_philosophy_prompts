I've been thinking about the ethics of rembering since my mom died last winter and I've been sorting through her house. Here are my gut verdicts. Keeping her letters and visiting the grave feels like a real duty I owe her. Tossing her broken appliences without guilt also feels fine—I don't need to hoard every object. A friend who screwed me over years ago: I've mostly forgotten the details and that feels healthy, not like a moral failure. But my sister refuses to ever talk about our dad, who was abusive, and that rubs me wrong; it feels like she's erasing something that matters. Meanwhile I don't think I have to dwell on every stupid thing I did in college. The case I keep going back and forth on: my mom and I had a nasty fight the week before she died, and I'm not sure whether I should hold onto it clearly or let it fade. Is there a principle that captures when remembering is obligatory and when forgetting is okay or even good? What does it say about that fight? And which of my other verdicts—especially the sister versus the friend—would it force me to drop? Also, what would it predict about whether I should remember the good parts of someone who hurt me badly?
