Consider the state of debate about dispositions after the standard objections involving finks—interferers that remove a disposition precisely when its triggering conditions are met—and masks—interferers that block manifestation without removing the underlying disposition. Most treatments treat these as technical puzzles solved by conditional analysis with ceteris paribus clauses or by powers ontologies, yet several deeper conceptual issues remain unsettled and rarely treated as live priorities.

Assume you are advising a metaphysician with about three years of research time, a background in philosophy of science, and no access to laboratory collaboration. Which open questions in this area deserve the most attention, and in what order? Do not provide a survey or a reading list; deliver a defended agenda. For each priority, specify whether it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is through purely conceptual or formal work, what kind of result would count as progress, and how the risk of circularity in specifying "normal conditions" without presupposing further dispositional attributions affects its urgency. In particular, assess whether extending fink- and mask-like interference to compound, institutional, or artificial capacities is a productive research line or a distraction.

Rank your choices with explicit criteria—dependency on other debates, tractability for non-empirical work, neglectedness, and relevance beyond pure metaphysics—explain the trade-offs between them, and say what conceptual finding over the next few years would cause you to reorder them.
