For my school's philosophy seminar, I am writing on whether epistemic akrasia—maintaining a belief while judging one's own grounds for it insufficient—can ever be rational when driven by higher-order evidence. Focus on the following case: a student learns from reliable psychological studies that her assessments of moral responsibility are consistently skewed by outcome bias, yet she continues to believe that an agent in a specific hypothetical scenario is fully blameworthy, convinced that the direct arguments for blame survive even if her cognitive processes are fallible. One view holds that rationality requires her to suspend judgment until she can correct for the bias; the opposing view holds that if the first-order reasoning remains intact, retaining the belief alongside acknowledged self-doubt is a permissible form of epistemic steadfastness rather than irrationality. Write an essay weighing these positions, reach a clear conclusion, and justify it with conceptual reasoning rather than appeals to authority.
