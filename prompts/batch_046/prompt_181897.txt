My opening challenge introduces a case not in your original letter: suppose a person born blind spends a decade using a sensory-substitution device that translates visual edges into patterns of vibration on the tongue, building rich spatial representations through tactile mediation. She then receives a retinal implant that restores conventional vision. Would you argue that this prolonged technological mediation has created the associative bridge between tactile and visual ideas of shape, allowing her to recognize the cube and sphere immediately? Or would you maintain that conventional vision still presents an entirely new kind of idea, leaving her no better off than Molyneux's suddenly-cured subject until she learns by trial?
