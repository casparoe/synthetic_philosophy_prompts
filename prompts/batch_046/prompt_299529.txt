ok so me and my friend were arguing for like three hours about euthanasia and assisted dying and i noticed this weird move people keep making. when the case is terminal cancer with physical pain, everyone talks about compassion, ending suffering, not wanting to be a burden, all that. but then if you bring up psychiatric assisted dying or someone with early dementia who wants to die before they get worse, suddenly the argument shifts hard to autonomy and self-ownership, like its not about comparing pain levels at all. and then if you attack the autonomy side by saying maybe depression is clouding their judgment, they slide right back to "well mental suffering is real suffering though". so its like there are two separate moral justifications running in parallel, and they take turns covering each other's gaps. is there an established name for this? i dont think its just equivocation, because nobody is secretly using a different word meaning; its more like the argument has modular parts. and its not quite motte-and-bailey either, since both the suffering version and the autonomy version are being defended at full strength, not a defensible core with a wild extension. my friend said its just pragmatic reasoning but that doesnt capture the structure. so does this pattern have a name in bioethics, and if not, what should we call it? i was thinking "justificatory relay" but i want to know where its edges are and what it isnt.
