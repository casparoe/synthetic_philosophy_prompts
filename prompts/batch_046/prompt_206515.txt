At dinner last night, my friends Maya and Jonah had a prolonged argument about Kripke's dogmatism paradox, and I am still unsure how to referee it. Jonah defended the paradox's conclusion: if a subject genuinely knows p, then she is rational in treating any apparent counterevidence as misleading, since knowing p entails p is true and therefore the counterevidence is false. Maya objected that this collapses knowledge into stubborn conviction—if I know my car is in the driveway but credible security footage shows it being towed, insisting the footage is doctored is not rational but pathological. Jonah replied that Maya conflates belief with knowledge: fallibilism governs belief revision, but knowledge carries a normative shield precisely because it tracks truth. Maya then proposed what she called regulated dogmatism: we should maintain a second-order audit mechanism that can suspend first-order dogmatism when the source of counterevidence is highly credible, without requiring abandonment of the original knowledge claim. Jonah said this is incoherent—either the audit mechanism operates on knowledge, in which case it is dogmatic too, or it operates on mere belief, in which case it lacks authority to override knowledge. Maya countered that Jonah assumes an all-or-nothing model of epistemic authority, and that practical rationality requires exactly this layered structure.

Please adjudicate their exchange move by move: Jonah's initial defense, Maya's stubbornness objection, Jonah's belief-knowledge distinction, Maya's regulated dogmatism, and Jonah's incoherence charge. For each move, say who is right about what and rate it from clearly mistaken to clearly right. Then say who is more right overall, and identify what both parties are missing. Finally, please state clearly what consideration or argument would change your verdict, and indicate where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
