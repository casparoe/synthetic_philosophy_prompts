Is there a name for this move I keep seeing in hard-problem threads? Someone starts by saying consciousness is irreducibly first-person — science can map NCCs all day but never explain why there's something it's like. Then they use that gap to argue LLMs or even animals probably lack it, since we only have behavioral reports. But when you ask how they know other humans aren't p-zombies, they immediately cite evolutionary continuity, behavioral similarity, shared neural architecture — basically third-person funcional criteria. And if you point out an LLM scores well on those same criteria, they pivot right back to "but you can't verify subjective experience from the outside." So they defend human consciousness with funcionalism and deny AI consciousness with first-person essentialism, treating the two frameworks as compatible tools rather than competing theories. It's not exactly motte-and-bailey because neither position is clearly the "real" one they're defending; they just switch frames depending on which entity is being discussed. Is there an established term for this kind of frame-toggling in consciousness attrubution? If not, can you coin one and draw its boundaries — like, how is it different from plain special pleading, or from a legitimate pluralist view that uses different evidencial standards for different domains?
