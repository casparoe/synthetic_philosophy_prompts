Two friends of mine got into it at lunch over whether Bostrom's simulation argument should cool down longtermist fanaticism about the far future. Maya said if we're likely in a sim then getting obsessive about humanity in year 5000 is like worrying about the ending of a save file that might just get deleted -- the ontology's shaky, so extreme commitments to distant futures don't hold up. Leo pushed back that simulated suffering is still real suffering, and that nested simulations actually amplify the stakes since there'd be far more conscious beings in simulated far futures than in base reality, so the argument doesn't dampen urgency it just complicates where the urgency points. Maya said Leo was just moving the goalposts to base reality simulators and nested copies, which actually proves her point: the argument destabilizes exactly what longtermists think they're protecting. Leo answered that Maya was treating we might be simulated like we might be dreaming and using it to dismiss practical reasoning altogether, but we still plan for retirement without knowing we aren't brains in vats. They asked me to refree and I honestly couldn't tell who was dodging what. Can you go through their exchange move by move -- rate each step from clearly off to clearly right -- and say what both are missing? In particular, is there a way to keep longtermist urgency without needing metaphysical confidence in this specific timeline?
