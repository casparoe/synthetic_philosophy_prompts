me and my roommate got into it about whether generations — boomers, millennials, gen z — are real kinds or just marketing boxes. i said they're closer to Mannheim-style cohorts, groups shaped by shared formative events, so the kind has causal weight, not just statistical convenience. she said they're administrative kinds, like tax brackets: Pew draws the millennial line at 1981, other shops use 1980 or 1982, gen z starts at 1997 or 1995 depending on who you ask. if the boundary shifts with the vendor, there's no natural joint.

i said fuzzy boundaries don't kill reality — biological species are fuzzy under phylogenetic definitions, but lions are still real. she said false analogy: species have reproductive isolation mechanisms, generations have nothing analogous. there's no generational isolation preventing a 1996 birth from being identical in every relevant trait to a 1998 birth except the label. then i said they might be homeostatic property clusters — traits hang together via shared causal history, not hidden essence. she said thats just naming the correlation. without disentangling age, period, and cohort effects in APC models, saying millennials are anxious might just mean being 25 in 2025 is hard. so treating them as a kind is premature.
