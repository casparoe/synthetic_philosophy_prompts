Construct a thought experiment in which a self-referential claim about its own truth-status is realized inside a simple, physically describable automaton—one that writes a single symbol onto its own display, where the symbol’s content is fixed solely by the automaton’s subsequent behavioral rule—such that a correspondence theorist, a deflationist, and a revision theorist who permits iterative stabilization of truth-values each reach a different verdict: false, malformed or empty, and unstable or oscillating, respectively. Constraints: the mechanism must be explainable in ordinary physical and logical terms without invoking Tarskian language hierarchies, exotic physics, omniscient observers, or stipulative semantic rules; present the scenario in one paragraph; and ensure the divergence is generated by the theories’ own structural commitments rather than by an ad hoc framing. After the scenario, state the belief or claim at issue, describe the method of evaluation available to the system, specify the nearby worlds or iterations relevant to each theory, and explain precisely why correspondence, deflationism, and revision theory deliver their respective judgments. Identify the single feature of the setup—likely the combination of self-reference with the absence of an external grounding state—that does the work in forcing the split, and show what happens to all three verdicts if that feature is removed, for example by redirecting the symbol to an external environmental condition. Finally, explain how the defender of the theory that comes out worst, presumably the one forced to declare the system semantically pathological, would attack the case: which premise they would reject, whether they would deny that the symbol expresses a genuine proposition, and whether removing the offending feature collapses the scenario into an unproblematic case that all three theories can agree on. Keep the reasoning explicit and avoid appealing to bibliographic authority.
