Two grad students in my seminar, Maya and Leo, got stuck arguing about program equilibrium with a weird twist neither had seen in the literature. They imagined agents submit source code, but there's a "reflection window" where the opponent can run your code in sandbox for exactly a million steps before the real game starts. Maya said this breaks the equilibrium: the sandbox is truncated, so your opponent might see cooperation in the window but defect later, meaning transparency doesn't guarantee cooperation. Leo said that's wrong if agents are geometrically rational -- maximizing expected log payoff -- because truncation error decays exponetially and geometric rationality punishes tail risk so hard they'd still cooperate. Maya fired back that geometric rationality is misapplied: the payoff is binary (cooperate/defect), not compound growth, so taking logs is just a distortion, not a rationality constrant. Leo replied you can apply geometric rationality to any utility via exponential transformation, so the variance argument survives. They asked me to referee. Please adjudacate move by move: rate the truncation argument, the binary-payoff objection, and Leo's transformation reply. Say who's right about what, and flag the bigger thing both miss about whether sandbox verification actually changes the equilibrium conditions or just shifts them.
