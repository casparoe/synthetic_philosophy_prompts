As a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of science, I am preparing to moderate a podcast interview with a philosopher of physics—call her Dr. A—who defends an unconventional reading of Bell’s theorem. She holds that the theorem is not principally a constraint on spatiotemporal locality, but rather demonstrates that any deterministic hidden-variables account must abandon counterfactual definiteness in a specifically modal register; and she argues that the standard “locality versus realism” dichotomy is an artifact inherited from pre-quantum mechanics that obscures the actual logical structure of quantum correlations. Write me a sequence of eight to ten interview questions suitable for a scientifically literate but non-specialist audience. The questions should not ask her to restate the view, nor should any request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. I want at least one question that presses how her modal reading of counterfactual definiteness differs from the usual statistical-independence formulation of Bell locality, with a follow-up anticipating her likely appeal to possible-worlds semantics and then pressing whether that semantics is empirically distinguishable from a parameter-dependent non-local model. I want one question on why she treats outcome independence, rather than parameter independence, as the condition that must be relinquished, with a follow-up keyed to her probable reply that outcome independence already smuggles in a classical joint-probability framework. I want one question asking what specific experimental result—beyond the loophole-free Bell tests she already accepts—would genuinely weaken her position, with a follow-up that asks how she distinguishes an empirical refutation from a mere reinterpretation of the correlation data. Finally, I want a concluding question about whether her rejection of the locality-realism framing leaves any residual conceptual work for “local causality” in relativistic quantum field theory, and what that work would be. Each question should anticipate the standard reply and include the immediate follow-up that makes the deflection harder.
