I'm writing a feature on synthetic consciousness for a science-and-ideas magazine. Read Ridley Scott's Blade Runner through the debate over original versus derived intentionality, specifically the clash between Searle's claim that true aboutness requires biological brains and Dennett's counter that intentionality can be derived and then amplified, including Dennett's speculative figure of the two-bitser — a system whose every mental state is initially borrowed or designed-in yet might, through sufficient organizational complexity, come to possess something functionally indistinguishable from original intentionality. Apply this to Roy Batty and Rachael: are they merely two-bitsers running Tyrell's programmed narratives, or has derived intentionality crossed a threshold into something genuine? How should we interpret the Voight-Kampff test in these terms — does it detect biological empathy or only a sophisticated derived simulation? And does the film's deliberate ambiguity about Deckard's own nature vindicate Dennett's continuum or defend Searle's hard biological boundary? I want a reading that treats the replicants as more than philosophical illustrations, that notes where the film's visual and narrative logic pushes back against both philosophers, and that concludes with what Blade Runner contributes to the debate that pure argument cannot.
