I'm especially hung up on three tensions that seem live but I can't tell which is upstream. First, there's the scaling problem: Dewey wrote mostly about face-to-face cooperative inquiry, but democratic life now happens in asynchronous, algorithmically sorted publics where there's no shared temporal frame or clear feedback loop. Does his idea of growth through shared problem-solving break down here, or can it be reconstructed for distributed networks? Second, there's the institutional design gap: Dewey wanted democratic structures that institutionalize fallibilism without becoming either rigid proceduralism or constant revolutionary chaos, but he never really specified the middle architecture. Is solving that a conceptual task or just an empirical political science problem? Third, there's the education loop — Dewey said democracy is primarily a form of associated living, which seems to require a specific pedagogical model, but modern mass education doesn't look like that at all. Is this mismatch a fatal flaw in applying experimentalism to large societies, or just a practical scaling issue?
