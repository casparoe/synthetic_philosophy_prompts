(1) Schrodinger says there's really only one consciousness—individual minds are like waves that think they're seperate but are all the same ocean. (2) If that's true, then the boundry between me harming you and me harming myself is just a perspectival glitch, not a real metaphysical line. (3) So every wrong act is literally self-harm done by the one consciousness through one "window" onto itself. (4) That means retributive punishment is incohearent: it's one aspect of the self punishing another aspect for what the whole self did. (5) And if there's only one agent, then either I deserve credit and blame for every human act ever, or the concept of desert never applies to anyone. (6) But we clearly do hold people accountable, and we don't think I'm guilty of a murder in Tokyo just because we're "the same consciousness." (7) So either the Vedantic picture is false, or our whole moral framework is a useless fiction. Where does this break? I'd like to keep (1) and (2) if I can, but maybe I have to drop (4) or (5)?
