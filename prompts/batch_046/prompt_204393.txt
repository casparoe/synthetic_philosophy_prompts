i'm in my historiography module and we're arguing about presentism — whether historians can ever escape judging the past by today's standards. here's where my gut lands:

- slating 18th-century surgeons for not washing hands before germ theory existed: presentist, unfair.
- judging roman slavery purely through modern human rights language: presentist, but at least it's explicit.
- praising a medieval queen for being 'ahead of her time' on gender: presentist, definitely.
- analysing feudal tax records with modern econometric models: not presentist, it's just analytical tools.
- refusing to call chattel slavery immoral because 'they didn't know better': not presentist, but it makes me really uncomfortable.
- condemning victorian factory owners for ignoring safety rules that actually existed then, but measuring the harm with modern occupational-health stats: i'm going back and forth on this one.

is there a principle that sorts all of these, or am i just being inconsistent? specifically, what does it say about the victorian factories case, and about a historian who uses present-day climate data to argue that medieval farmers 'should have' managed soil rotation differently?
