Exam question: In The Present Age (part of A Literary Review), Kierkegaard diagnoses the modern public and the press. Explain the link between anonymity, reflection without action, and "leveling." Then apply the framework to algorithmic content feeds: what corresponds to the anonymous journalist, the "numerical" crowd, and the escape route of the "single individual"?

Student answer:
Kierkegaard says the press makes an abstract public by letting writers avoid personal commitment — they can say anything because they never have to stand behind it as individuals. This creates leveling: real qualitative differences get crushed into quantitative sameness, like treating every person as just one more unit in the crowd. The core mechanism is reflection without action, where people constantly discuss things they never act on. If we map this to algorithmic feeds, the anonymous journalist becomes the anonymous user or bot, and the crowd becomes engagement metrics that treat every click as identical numerical input. The leveling is actually worse now because algorithms flatten qualitative judgment into pure quantity automatically. The escape is still the single individual who opts out of consumption and returns to inward religious commitment, standing outside the abstract crowd just like Kierkegaard wanted.
