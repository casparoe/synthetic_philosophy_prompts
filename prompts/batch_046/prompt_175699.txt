I have just finished a long, somewhat circular argument with a close friend about Dennett’s Wandering Two-Bitser and its bearing on Searle’s distinction between original and derived intentionality. To check whether I have understood anything rather than merely accumulated talking points, I want to present the summary my friend offered and ask you to audit it, because I suspect it smuggles in assumptions that neither philosopher actually holds.

Here is how my friend characterized the disagreement. Dennett’s scenario involves two physically identical devices—one engineered to detect quarters, the other engineered to detect Panamanian balboas of the same dimensions and weight—whose internal states are token-for-token identical but differ in what they are “about” solely in virtue of their design histories. From this, my friend inferred that Dennett is arguing that non-conscious artifacts can possess original intentionality directly, without any derivation, because evolutionary or engineering history alone is sufficient to ground aboutness. On Searle’s side, my friend said that Searle denies both devices have original intentionality and insists they possess only derived intentionality; however, my friend added that for Searle this derivation is not satisfied by the original designer’s past intentions but requires a conscious interpreter actively present at the moment of interpretation. Consequently, if no one is currently thinking about the balboa-device, it lacks intentional content entirely at that time. My friend concluded that Dennett treats original intentionality as a historical property of artifacts, while Searle treats it as a present-tense property of conscious minds, so the two are not really disagreeing about the same phenomenon.

I am inclined to think this framing is mostly right but subtly distorts both positions—perhaps by conflating Dennett’s intentional stance with a metaphysical claim about design history, and by misrepresenting Searle’s notion of derivation as requiring an ongoing conscious observer rather than an original intentional source. But I am also trying to apply a distinction of my own: I wonder whether reading the two-bitser as direct proof that artifacts have intrinsic original intentionality, rather than as a challenge to the very sharpness of the original-derived boundary, is a recognizable category mistake with an established name, or whether I am inventing a conceptual tool I do not need. Please evaluate the fairness of my friend’s summary carefully—separating accurate characterization, acceptable pedagogical simplification, and genuine misrepresentation—particularly regarding how Dennett uses the scenario and what Searle means by derivation. If the premise of my audit request is itself confused—for instance, if I am wrong to think the two-bitser argument can be read as supporting original intentionality in artifacts at all—say so plainly and correct the framing rather than accommodating it politely.
