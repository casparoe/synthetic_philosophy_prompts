Write an essay addressing whether well-known cases of simultaneous independent discovery—calculus by Newton and Leibniz, evolutionary theory by natural selection by Darwin and Wallace, and non-Euclidean geometry by Gauss, Bolyai, and Lobachevsky—support the thesis that major scientific discoveries are historically inevitable. Weigh this inevitability argument against counterarguments emphasizing the contingency of individual genius, conceptual framing, and social conditions for research. Reach and defend a clear conclusion. In your argument, specifically consider whether the inevitability thesis applies with equal force to formal mathematical structures and to empirical theories grounded in observation, and explain any relevant difference.
