Compare Skolem's resolution of the Löwenheim-Skolem paradox—grounded in the distinction between internal and external cardinality relative to a particular model—with the structuralist interpretation that treats the paradox as evidence for the model-relativity of all set-theoretic properties, including the notion of countability itself. Focus in particular on whether the structuralist reading genuinely dissolves the paradox or merely relocates it into a question about the determinacy of reference for mathematical vocabulary. To sharpen the evaluation, introduce a hypothetical 'canonical grounding' constraint: the thesis that an intended interpretation of the membership relation can be fixed independently of model-theoretic satisfaction, thereby restoring absolute cardinality judgments. Examine whether this constraint can be sustained without circularly presupposing the set-theoretic framework whose determinacy it is meant to secure, and conclude with an assessment of which of the two original resolutions remains more defensible once the canonical-grounding option has been considered and rejected.
