I've been trying to think through whether moral status really comes in degrees, or whether talk of "degrees" is just an inconsistent way of applying a binary distinction. I began with the familiar ladder—insects, pigs, people—but kept running into configurations that don't align with a single scale. There's the hive, where collective decision-making looks intelligent yet no individual bee seems to possess the corresponding experience. There's a human in deep, dreamless sleep, temporarily lacking nearly every cognitive capacity but not seeming to drop to partial status. There's a smart thermostat that modifies its own rules to avoid damage, displaying something like aversion without any biological substrate or evolutionary history of survival. And there's the hypothetical case of synthetic neural tissue that processes nociceptive signals yet lacks any drive to preserve itself. Rather than asking which of these entities "have rights," I want the full specification first: what must an adequate theory of gradated moral status actually deliver?

Please lay out the hard requirements—the specific cases it must accommodate without collapsing them into one another; the distinctions it must preserve, such as between possessing some status and possessing more of it, or between status that belongs to a unified subject and status that might be distributed across parts of a system; the empirical constraints from biology or psychology that it cannot contradict; and which single requirement does the most damage to the simplest one-factor theories (pure sentience, pure cognitive capacity, pure relational status). I am especially interested in whether such a theory can allow status to be present in parts or in collective behavior without being fully present in any single part, and whether that conflicts with the idea that moral status must always attach to one unified subject of experience. If any two of these requirements turn out to be jointly unsatisfiable, flag the tension explicitly and explain what has to give. I am not asking for named authors, a literature survey, or citations; I simply want to know whether the conceptual space I'm describing—continuous but non-additive, capable of being distributed yet not arbitrarily transferable—is coherent, and if so, where its exact boundaries lie.
