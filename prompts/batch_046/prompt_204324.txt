As a retired condensed-matter physicist who has only recently begun to read philosophy of science in earnest, I have become preoccupied with a slogan I encounter repeatedly amongst discussions of scientific realism: "only structure survives scientific change, so only structure is real". At a recent seminar on ontic structural realism, a speaker invoked it specifically to argue that quasi-particles such as phonons or holes ought not to be granted ontological status, since they vanish under alternative Hamiltonian representations, whereas the Hamiltonian structure itself should be retained as the proper object of realist commitment. I wish to understand precisely what work this one-liner is capable of doing.

Could you please disentangle the distinct precise theses it might be expressing? I have in mind at least the following possibilities, though I expect there are others: an epistemic claim that we can know only structural relations and never intrinsic natures; an ontic claim that there literally are no entities, only relations; a claim about diachronic continuity, that mathematics alone persists across theory change and therefore alone merits belief; and a methodological claim that we should commit only to those entities that are invariant across all empirically equivalent representations. For each reading, please indicate whether it amounts to a tautology, a substantive but defensible position, or a falsehood, and explain whether it genuinely supports the speaker's specific move — that representational redundancy in the Hamiltonian formalism entails the ontological dispensability of phonons, whilst leaving Hamiltonian structure untouched.

In particular, I am interested in whether any coherent formulation of the slogan can draw a principled line between "representational redundancy" (as in gauge freedom) and "ontological dispensability" (as with emergent quasi-particles), or whether the two collapse into one another once the slogan is made precise. I do not require a bibliography, reading list, or citation; I am looking for a conceptual map of the logical space the slogan occupies, together with a verdict on which of its precise incarnations actually license the argument about quasi-particles I heard.
