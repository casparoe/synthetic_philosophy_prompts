The philosophical comparison between Darwin's evolutionary mechanism and Turing's computational model has produced a curiously static disagreement about the phenomenon in which complex functional success—adaptive biological design in one case, apparently intelligent behavior in the other—emerges without internal understanding, intentional representation, or semantic grasp. One tradition reads these as two expressions of the same structural inversion, contending that once blind natural selection and syntactic rule-following are shown capable of generating sophisticated outcomes, explanations appealing to purpose, consciousness, or intrinsic meaning become redundant. The opposing tradition denies that the inversion is shared, arguing that evolutionary adaptation depends on irreducible historical contingency, population-level dynamics, and deep environmental coupling that have no parallel in formal computation, while computation depends on explicit symbol-manipulation architectures that evolutionary processes do not instantiate. Despite decades of repetition, both sides accept the core empirical claims—that evolution produces complex adaptation, that machines can exhibit intelligent behavior, and that neither process involves a conscious grasp of its outputs—and yet the disagreement remains unresolved, with the same analogies and counter-analogies recurring in the literature.

Diagnose the nature of this stall. Is it primarily verbal, turning on competing definitions of comprehension, function, or inversion? Is it substantive but concealed, resting on a hidden empirical question about whether evolutionary dynamics are fully computable or on a formal question about whether functional explanation requires syntactic structure? Or are the disputants answering different questions—one about the metaphysics of biological purpose, the other about the conditions for artificial cognition—under a single misleading heading? Might it instead reflect bedrock normative commitments about what kinds of explanation are intellectually satisfying, commitments that neither side can argue for from shared premises?

Then state precisely what would count as progress: a conceptual distinction that separates the two domains, an empirical or formal result that resolves the hidden question, a reframing that dissolves the shared heading, or a reasoned argument that no further progress should be expected. Do not present a neutral survey; take a definite position and defend it.
