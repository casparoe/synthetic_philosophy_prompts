I'm trying to map my moral-status judgments across a gradient of systems, from unambiguous persons to things that merely maintain homeostasis, and I want to know whether they trace a coherent principle or just collapse into ad hoc distinctions.

My verdicts:

- A conscious adult human and a sleeping human: full direct moral status; the sleeping state is temporary absence of experience, not absence of the subject.
- A pig in acute distress: very high status—wrong to harm without overwhelming reason, though perhaps not exactly coextensive with human rights.
- A bumblebee with activated nociceptors after injury: minimal but nonzero status. There's likely a dim something-it-is-like, but the moral weight is negligible compared to the pig—closer to zero than to the mammal.
- A Venus flytrap closing on prey via calcium signaling: zero direct status, even though the biochemical cascade is more elaborate than some animal reflex arcs.
- A bacterium swimming away from a toxin via chemotaxis: zero status. The feedback loop is simpler than the bee's, but functionally analogous—this is where I feel tension.
- A domestic thermostat with predictive occupancy learning: zero status, despite having more parameters and predictive processing than the bacterium.
- A distributed sensor grid—ten thousand simple nodes with no local experience, collectively exhibiting global self-modeling and integrated behavior: I lean strongly toward zero, but the case gives me more pause than the thermostat, which suggests I might be tracking complexity rather than consciousness.
- A philosophical zombie—behaviorally identical to a suffering human, but with no phenomenal consciousness: zero direct status. Harming it might be wrong for indirect reasons (deception, corruption of norms), but not because it suffers.

The hard pair is the bee versus the bacterium: both involve homeostatic avoidance mediated by signaling, yet I assign minimal status to one and none to the other. Meanwhile the distributed grid has more integration than either, yet I still deny it status—unless integration without phenomenology is sufficient, which I don't want to grant.

Is there a coherent principle here—perhaps one requiring both valenced phenomenology and a unified global workspace, with a non-arbitrary threshold? Which verdicts must be sacrificed for consistency? And what should I say about a nematode with 302 neurons that avoids heat but shows no centralized processing: slight status like the bee, or none like the bacterium?
