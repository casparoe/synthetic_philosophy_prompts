I was eavesdropping on two people in my study group arguing about Ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood and they got totally stuck. One said if Ubuntu actualy means I am because we are, then cutting all social ties should make you less of a person -- maybe not a full person at all. The other said thats wrong, Ubuntu doesnt deny you have an inner life, it just says that inner life is constituted by relationships, not that it disappears in isolation, so a hermit is still fully human. First guy came back: 'But then youre sneaking in an intrinsic property -- the capacity to relate -- which is exactly what liberal individualism does. If that capacity lives inside you, relationships are just the environment, not the source of personhood.' Second guy said the capacity itself is relational: its the capacity to be addressed and to address others, so even alone youre oriented toward people. But then he admitted if you permanently destroy that orientation, maybe personhood is scalar rather than binary. First guy jumped on that: 'So you agree some people are more persons than others? Thats a dangerous slope, and it also means the slogan is misleading since it sounds like an all-or-nothing claim.' They wouldnt budge. Whos right about what? Is the first guy right that any intrinsic-capacity move collapses Ubuntu into liberalism? Is the second guy right that orientation-to-others saves it? And what are they both missing about whether Ubuntu allows for voluntary withdrawal, or about the difference between being isolated and being unaddressable?
