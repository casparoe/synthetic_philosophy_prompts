A friend summed up Locke's labor-mixing view for me after skimming some IP stuff: she said you own whatever you mix your mental labor with, so ideas are just mental land you can fence off. Since ideas are non-rivalrous, taking one doesn't leave others with less, which means the Lockean proviso — leave enough and as good left for others — is automatically satisfied and never limits idea-ownership. She said thats why Locke would back strong perpetual patents, and that copyright is even more justified than patents because copying a book steals the authors physical labor-product, while an independent invention is just parallel mental farming. Then she added this modern twist I hadn't heard: owning an idea also encloses the mental commons by monopolizing attention-space in thier heads, so if you take attention-scarcity seriously, the proviso actually fails for strong IP and Locke should have opposed patents altogether.
