Two people in my studey group got stuck arguing about Newton's bucket after class. (1) He said it proves space is real: the water climbs the sides when the bucket spins even after it's at rest relative to the bucket walls, so it's rotating against something absolute, not just other objects. (2) She said that's Mach's principle -- the concavity comes from rotation relative to distant matter, and in an empty universe there'd be none, so space isn't a substance. (3) He replied that relationism is unfalsifiable: you can always postulate hidden distant stars, and GR kept absolute structure anyway in the metric field, it just curved it. (4) She said the metric field is dynamical and coupled to matter, not an empty container, and Mach's principle constrains the laws, not our observations of hidden stuff. Can you adjudicate move by move, rating each from clearly mistaken to clearly right? And what's both sides missing about whether the argument already sneaks in a specific law of inertia?
