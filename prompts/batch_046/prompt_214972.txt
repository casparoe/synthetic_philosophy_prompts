I'm working up a blog post about the "DNA is a code" metaphor and whether it actually holds water. I want a sorting instrument — like a decision tree or taxonomy — for readers to figure out which camp they really belong to, not just which scientist they like. Start with the commitment that genomes carry info that directs development. Then branch on concrete answers, not labels:

- Is the mapping from codons to amino acids arbitrary the way a word-to-thing mapping is, or is it chemically fixed?
- Does explaining that mapping require an interpreter with intentions or goals, or can chemistry alone do the work?
- Put the same DNA sequence in two different cellular enviroments — do you get the same result?

From those combinations, locate the actual positions: the literal semantic/code camp, the algorithmic/information-theory camp, the blueprint/template camp, and the "no special code, DNA is just one resource" camp. Note any combo that's logically possible but unoccupied — for instance, arbitrary mapping plus no interpreter plus context-independent results — and diagnose whether it's incoherent or just unexplored. Also flag which branches your tree can't cleanly separate (maybe the algorithmic and blueprint views overlap on some answers?). Keep it to about six leaves, write for smart general readers, phrase every node as a concrete claim or case someone can answer without knowing the jargon, and don't ask for citations or reading lists — I'm writing this for an audience that won't have library access.
