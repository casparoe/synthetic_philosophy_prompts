Next Thursday I'm defending my Quine interpretation at a phil colloquium - mostly analytic ethicists plus a couple phil-science folks who think Quine just said moral values are cultural posits with no real cognitive standing. My thesis: if you take Quine's holism and naturalism seriously about norms, moral principles aren't just conventional fixtures; they sit in the web of belief and get revised for pragmatic coherence with behavioral science and predictive success, not for correspondence to moral facts but also not for pure sociology. So morality gets a kind of second-order empirical standing - part of our best total theory, like the Duhem-Quine thesis applied to normative frameworks. The audience knows Two Dogmas and Ontological Relativity but will think I'm smuggling in cognitivism through the back door. I need a Q&A prep sheet: objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way an analytic ethicist or a Quine scholar would put them, which ones actually hurt, what I should concede up front, what I have to hold, and the one reply that has to land or the proposal dies. Also flag the weakest spot in my own position I should defend rather than hide. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks - probably whether Quine's own near-total silence on ethics just kills my reading, or whether this collapses into straight utilitarianism. Keep it like a cheat sheet, not an essay.
