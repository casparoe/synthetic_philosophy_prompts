Write an essay on whether maximal moral goodness is genuinely desirable. The standard debate assumes that moral sainthood is unattractive because it requires suppressing humor, aesthetic life, and personal projects in favor of relentless duty. Set that framing aside and examine a different possibility: a pluralist saint whose moral and non-moral commitments are not in competition but integrated within one coherent practical identity—someone for whom art, friendship, and justice express the same underlying evaluative structure rather than being traded off. If this figure is conceptually coherent, does the undesirability of sainthood disappear? Or does it reveal a deeper problem, namely that maximal moral goodness collapses practical reason's domain-specificity, turning every choice into a moral choice and erasing the category of the merely permissible? Reach and defend a conclusion. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then develop the reasoning with no preamble and without restating the question.
