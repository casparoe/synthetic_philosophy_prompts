Please design a decision-tree taxonomy for the debate over norms of assertion, intended for readers familiar with forecasting norms and bounded-rationality arguments but not necessarily with the analytic epistemology literature. The root node should ask whether an assertion must express knowledge, or whether a weaker epistemic relation—true belief, justified belief, or calibrated confidence—will suffice. From there, branch on whether the required relation is factive: must the asserted content actually be true, or is justified false belief permissible? Next, split on how the view handles statistical-justification cases of the lottery-paradox kind, where a speaker has excellent evidential support for a particular ticket losing but lacks knowledge because of the reference-class base rate. Then add a branch that distinguishes categorical assertions ('X will occur') from explicit probability ascriptions ('my credence in X is 0.87'), asking whether the same epistemic norm governs both or whether calibrated forecasting statements are subject only to a calibration norm rather than a knowledge or truth norm. At each leaf, identify the position by a standard name if one exists—knowledge norm, belief norm, truth norm, calibration-contextualism—or label it unnamed, and give one sentence describing its most awkward commitment, such as being forced to treat a well-calibrated forecaster's explicit credence report as not an assertion at all, or being forced to condemn a speaker who asserts a statistically likely falsehood that happens to fall in the error tail. Ensure every branch is phrased as a concrete diagnostic question answerable without knowing the philosophical labels in advance, flag any pair of leaves that collapse under a resource-bounded epistemology, and avoid asking for citations or reading lists.
