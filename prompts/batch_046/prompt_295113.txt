I'm drafting a short conceptual essay arguing that consciousness across animals should be mapped by what I call counterfactual integration depth (CID) — the capacity to maintain an internal representation that conflicts with immediate sensory input and deploy it across a temporal gap — rather than by taxonomic rank. Below is the core argument.

The standard view treats consciousness as a gradient running from simple invertebrates up through mammals, with proximity to human neuroanatomy as the implicit benchmark. I propose instead that CID clusters non-linearly across phylogeny. For instance, an octopus navigating a maze may suppress immediate tactile feedback in favor of a remembered spatial layout, exhibiting high CID in that domain; certain corvids planning for distant tool use hold a representation that conflicts with present drive, showing comparable depth. By contrast, some mammals with large cortical volumes may show lower CID where behavior remains tightly coupled to immediate cues. Because CID requires specifically representational conflict plus cross-modal integration, it differs from general intelligence or associative learning.

The implication is ethical: moral consideration should track demonstrated functional capacity rather than species membership. The chief vulnerability is measurement circularity — without independent neural markers, we risk defining consciousness through the behaviors we intend to explain.

Identify the weakest point in the framing and suggest one revision that would strengthen it without abandoning the core claim.
