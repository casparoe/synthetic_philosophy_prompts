I keep seeing people use the sphex wasp thing to argue about free will - you know, she drags the cricket to the burrow, checks inside, and if you move the cricket she loops forever dragging it back and forth. The usual take is that shes running a hardwired script, so real freedom must be the ability to adapt when circumstances change, like humans do. But I read this flipped take that says the wasp isnt actually rigid, shes just running a shallow check - she verifies the burrow is empty but doesnt represent the crickets actual position. So the analogy gets flipped: maybe human flexibility isnt about escaping mechanism, its just about having deeper nested checks in your behavior loop. Can you lay out the mapping - what in human decision-making corresponds to the burrow-check, the cricket-drag, the loop? Where does the analogy break down, especially if we accept that deeper checks are still mechanical? And does the argument that free will needs representational depth survive, or does the sphex case just show that more layers of checking dont buy you anything metaphysically interesting?
