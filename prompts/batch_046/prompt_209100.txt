Write a short story that takes the doomsday argument seriously but puts it inside a tired family drama. The main character is a demographer who just had twins and can't stop applying the self-sampling assumption to her own life — not as an abstract puzzle, but like it's actually going to determine whether she has a third kid. She starts treating milestones as data about humanity's total lifespan, and she has to decide whether to warn her partner that according to her reference-class math they're probably closer to the middle than the end, so maybe stop now. The partner should push back with the presumptuous-philosopher worry — that she's just picking a reference class that gives her the scary answer she wants — but phrased like a tired spouse arguing over dishes, not a seminar. Don't make it a lecture; let the philosophy come through them being defensive and slightly drunk on cheap wine. Add one weird niche detail: she works for a tiny census bureau that only tracks people born on leap days, which is how she originally got obsessed with sampling bias. Keep it in the weeds, and let her be definatly wrong about some of the math but still emotionally convincing.
