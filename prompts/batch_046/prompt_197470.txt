I've noticed a recurring argumentative shape in discussions of Goodman's grue, and I'm unsure whether it's a recognized variant or just entrenchment in a fancier coat.

The pattern runs like this. First, someone grants that 'all emeralds are green' and 'all emeralds are grue' have identical evidential support up to time t, but insists green is still the rational projection because it participates in a cross-domain cluster: green co-varies with chlorophyll, spectral properties, evolutionary function, and so on. Grue, by contrast, is isolated — it doesn't link to independent theoretical structures. Second, an interlocutor objects that we can always manufacture a parallel cluster for any gerrymandered predicate ('grue-photosynthetic', etc.), so the cluster itself is just as contrived. Third, the first speaker doubles down not on the cluster's existence but on its counterfactual stability: if we intervened to make emeralds stay blue past t, the green-cluster predictions would fail together in a unified way, while the grue-cluster failures would be disjoint and ad hoc.

This seems different from ordinary language-entrenchment or simplicity-based solutions, but I can't tell if it's a named position — maybe a form of structural realism applied to induction, or a variant of theory-ladenness — or if I'm inventing a distinction without a difference. So: does this specific three-move dialectic have an established label? How exactly does it differ from Goodman's own entrenchment, from natural-kind realism, and from standard appeals to theoretical unity? If it lacks a name, what would be the right boundary conditions for coining one? Keep it under 250 words; I'm reading this offline, so no citations needed.
