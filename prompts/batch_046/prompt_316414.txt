Write an exam question for an upper-level undergraduate philosophy seminar—together with a model answer of about 600 words and a marking scheme—on Albert Camus’s opposition to capital punishment and its relationship to his philosophy of rebellion. The question should not be answerable by summarizing Reflections on the Guillotine alone. Instead, it should require students to resolve an internal tension: Camus claims that state execution is an absolute wrong because it asserts a divine sovereignty over life, eliminates the possibility of redemption, and operates as premeditated bureaucratic murder; yet he endorsed lethal violence in the Resistance and argued that rebellion asserts a common human value that applies even to the rebel. Ask students to articulate the principle that distinguishes legitimate killing in rebellion from illegitimate killing by the state, using Camus’s concepts of the limit, solidarity, and administrative murder. Then require them to apply this principle to a modern analog: whether Camus’s framework would condemn life imprisonment without parole as equally destructive of the possibility of redemption, or whether the temporal structure of execution makes it uniquely wrong. The model answer must contain three specific moves: first, explaining why rebellion’s violence respects the limit because it affirms a shared value that constrains the rebel, whereas state execution denies that value to the condemned; second, showing how the bureaucratic, hidden, and premeditated nature of the guillotine distinguishes it from the immediate, risky, and acknowledged violence of rebellion; and third, arguing either that life without parole fails the same test because it permanently forecloses redemption without immediate physical death, or that execution remains singular because it claims absolute finality over life. The marking scheme should be out of 20 points, identifying five must-hit moves each worth 4 points, the specific error that costs each point, and a one-line distinction between an A-grade answer—which uses the concept of the limit to resolve both the historical tension and the modern analog consistently—and a B-grade answer—which notices the tension but resolves it only by appealing to context or by mischaracterizing Camus as an absolute pacifist. Do not ask for bibliographies or citations; the answer should rely on conceptual analysis alone.
