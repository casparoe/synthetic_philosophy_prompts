I'm planning a thirty-minute session for first-year philosophy students who arrive thinking Rand simply endorses selfishness. I want to teach Rand's critique of altruism—not as a defense of cruelty, but as the rejection of self-sacrifice as a moral ideal—using the distinction between psychological altruism and ethical altruism as the framing device. Plan the explanatory arc: what idea must be in place before altruism can be distinguished from benevolence; the one concrete scenario that carries the distinction, which must involve a genuine cost to the agent and a benefit to others yet allow both an altruistic and a benevolent response; the misconceptions students will raise in sequence and the point in the arc where each should be headed off; how to check whether they have grasped the normative structure rather than merely reacting to the word "selfishness"; and which part of Objectivist meta-ethics should be explicitly left out at this level and said so.
