I am a PhD student in history and philosophy of science, working on predictive theories in political economy and their evaluation across extended time horizons. I keep returning to the problem informally described as being right but early—the idea that a structural theory, such as the Malthusian dynamics linking population growth to resource constraints, can suffer repeated apparent falsification over decadal scales without being genuinely refuted, because its intended domain of application may lie further in the future. I want to understand whether this judgment is conceptually coherent and, if so, what conceptual work is needed to defend or refine it rigorously.

To organize my thinking, I distinguish three clusters of open problems. First, the epistemology of temporal scale: by what criteria do we assign a predictive theory its proper testing horizon, and how do we distinguish a premature evaluation from a genuine counterexample? Second, the logic of theoretical rescue: when we introduce auxiliary conditions—such as technological substitution, institutional adaptation, or spatial redistribution—to preserve core dynamics, are we engaging in legitimate parameter adjustment or transforming the theory into an unfalsifiable framework? Third, the normative and methodological downstream effects: if we accept that a long-run structural theory can be right but early, what should change in how we weigh precautionary reasoning about current resource and demographic policy?

Given roughly three years of dissertation-level work, which of these clusters, or which specific open questions within them, should I prioritize, and in what order? I am less interested in a survey than in a defended agenda: for each priority, explain why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophical rather than empirical or modeling work, and what kind of conceptual result over the next few years would cause me to reorder the list. Please also identify any apparent tensions between these clusters that dissolve on closer inspection, and specify the criteria—dependency, tractability, neglectedness, practical stakes—you are using to rank them. Please provide your answer in American English.
