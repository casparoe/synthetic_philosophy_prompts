I've been working through the tight-versus-loose-culture idea—how societies calibrate the strictness of rule-following—but I want to operationalize it into a reusable decision flowchart rather than a vague intuition. To do that, I'm injecting a framework I'll call normative permeability: every rule has an internal elasticity score (how much deviation it can absorb before collective trust fractures) and every case receives a cohesion-threat index (how severely the specific context raises the stakes for coordinated action). Please turn this into a step-by-step procedure I can actually run: ordered tests, explicit decision nodes, and clearly flagged judgment calls that cannot be disguised as mechanical steps. The output should tell me whether to recommend strict enforcement, calibrated remediation, or a formal proposal to revise or loosen the rule itself. It must handle hard conflicts—when elasticity and cohesion-threat point in opposite directions, and when a student violated the rule because a subculture's norms directly clash with the school's. Then apply it to three cases: (1) a first-year student shares homework answers with a roommate during exam week—low cohesion-threat, high emotional stakes; (2) a senior forges a doctor's note to skip a widely resented mandatory assembly—high visibility, but low perceived legitimacy of the rule; and (3) a student from a community with very different collaboration norms is caught using unauthorized help on a group project—where I expect the procedure will recommend something that conflicts with my own preference for uniform standards. Finally, state precisely what the procedure cannot do and where it will systematically misfire, so I don't overtrust it.
