Consider an interpreter of the Zhuangzi who has ruled consistently on the philosophical appropriateness of responses to death. Her verdicts are as follows. First, when a master dies of old age after living in harmony with the Dao, she praises disciples who celebrate with music rather than weep, describing the event as borrowed form returning to its source. Second, when a young soldier is killed in battle, she tells the family not to mourn the biological transformation itself, yet she condemns the state that ordered the war for treating bodies as instruments. Third, when a criminal is executed for theft, she calls the execution neutral—like cutting timber for use—but observes that the criminal's terror reveals a failure to understand transformation, which she describes as a death within life. Fourth, when a farmer dies peacefully in sleep, she approves only minimal burial without ritual, arguing that elaborate mourning treats the body as private property. Fifth, when a beloved friend dies suddenly of illness, she permits brief tears but warns that prolonged grief is clinging to a shadow.

Now consider a new case. A philosopher-engineer arranges for her consciousness to be gradually migrated, upon clinical death of her biological body, into a distributed synthetic ecosystem that maintains continuous subjective experience without preserving a discrete personal identity; she asks her family to celebrate her completion rather than hold a funeral, and she describes the biological death as merely one phase in a longer transformation.

What should the interpreter recommend if she wishes to stay consistent with her track record? Identify the closest analog among her five verdicts, and be explicit about whether the precedents genuinely determine the outcome. Then reconstruct the two most plausible organizing rules—one that turns on whether the transformation is natural and continuous versus artificial and discontinuous, and one that turns on whether the deceased and survivors are releasing or clinging to identity and form. For each rule, state whether she should approve or reject both the migration procedure and the requested celebration, and name the principle she would be adopting either way. Finally, specify one further hypothetical case, involving a different variable, that would best distinguish these two rules.
