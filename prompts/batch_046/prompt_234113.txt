Next Thursday I'm speaking at an interdisciplinary workshop on democratic decline — audience is mostly comparative-politics people who run large-N institutional datasets, plus one Dewey scholar who knows his 1927 Public and Its Problems inside out. I'm defending the view that democratic backsliding gets misdiagnosed if we treat it purely as institutional decay. Drawing on Dewey's experimentalism, I argue democracy is better understood as a mode of collective inquiry, so 'backsliding' is really a collapse in shared experimental conditions, not just broken checks and balances. To make this concrete I draw a line between procedural experimentalism (testing policies) and ontological experimetalism (testing who the democratic 'we' even is) — I think the backsliding literature conflates the two.
