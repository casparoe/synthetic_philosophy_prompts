I was talking with a friend in clinical psych training about Hacking's looping effects — the idea that when psychiatry classifies people it actually reshapes their behavior, so the category kind of chases it's own tail. She pointed out this weird institutional loop in adult ADHD assessments: insurers and clinics usually won't diagnose unless you show "significant functional impairment" at work or school, but the rating scales they use to measure that impairment mostly ask about the exact same symptoms that define ADHD (missing deadlines, disorginization, trouble sustaining focus). So the gate requires impairment, yet impairment is operationalized as basically having the disorder.

What philosophical commitments about mental disorder does that practice presuppose? Does it assume there's a real biological dysfunction sitting underneath, independent of whatever social roles you're failing in? Or is it implicitly treating ADHD as a kind of fixed cognitive profile that only becomes a disorder when it clashes with specific institutional demands? Reconstruct the implicit theory here — dysfunction, maladaptation, looping kind, or something else — and say whether the practice actually needs one specific view or is philosophically compatible with several. Given Hacking's point that classification reshapes the people it sorts, could the "functional impairment" check be partly measuring the effects of prior labeling rather than some pre-existing condition? And tell me what a diagnostic system that took looping seriously would have to change: drop impairment gatekeeping entirely, redesign the checklists so they don't circularly reference the disorder they're meant to validate, or something else?
