Compare two proposed resolutions to the Löbian obstacle facing self-trusting agents. First, the monomodal deflationary view, which holds that the obstacle is largely an artifact of conflating proof-theoretic derivability within a single formal system with rational acceptance across successive versions of an agent; on this view, self-trust need not be internalized as an object-language reflection principle of the form □(□φ → φ), and the apparent paradox dissolves once reasoning is separated from commitment across agent-stages. Second, the stratified-reflection view, which treats the obstacle as genuinely constraining but attempts to navigate it by indexing trust relations to discrete ordinal ranks of self-modification, allowing an agent at rank α to rationally endorse its immediate successor at rank α+1 without licensing the universal Löbian closure that generates the contradiction. In your comparison, evaluate which framework more coherently preserves both the normative force of self-trust and the logical consistency of successive self-modification. Specifically, address whether the stratified approach genuinely dissolves the obstacle or merely relocates it to the limit ordinals, and whether the deflationary approach succeeds in preserving self-trust without collapsing into an arbitrary separation between an agent's reasoning and its practical commitments.
