I hold that the omnipotence paradox dissolves if omnipotence is understood not as an unrestricted quantifier over task-descriptions but as maximal power within a consistent modal framework: an omnipotent being can perform any action whose description is not self-contradictory, and "creating a stone too heavy for that same being to lift" describes a contradictory object-state rather than a genuine task. On this view omnipotence is analogous to maximal computational expressiveness within a consistent formal system—there is no "program that computes its own non-computation"—rather than to an ability that could fail at any particular job. What would count against this position? I want the arguments, formal results, or conceptual distinctions that should move me, sorted by how decisive they are: for instance, whether there are coherent task-descriptions that are not strictly contradictory yet are incompatible with omnipotence; whether the logical/physical impossibility distinction collapses in ways that restore the paradox for contingent omnipotence; or whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, such that any counterexample can be dismissed as a hidden contradiction. I also want an honest assessment of what defenders of this view must concede—e.g., whether omnipotence necessarily excludes powers traditionally attributed to God, such as the power to sin or to annihilate oneself—and whether those concessions are damaging or merely clarifying. Please distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence that is merely awkward, say whether you think the position risks being well-insulated in a problematic way, and include what a critic who accepts the basic logical framework would still press against it.
