Write a dialogue between two philosophers — call them A and B — arguing about whether numbers should count in moral decisions. A takes a Taurek-style line: saving five over one is only justified if those five are genuinely distinct subjects, and they suspect aggregation is basically a statistical illusion. B thinks thats wrong, each life adds independent moral weight, end of story. To avoid the usual trolley fatigue, frame the case around an AI triage system that has to pick between treating five patients running on one distributed consciousness (so its genuinely unclear if there are five minds or just one spread out) versus a single patient with a standalone brain. Both should be smart, respond directly to there points instead of talking past eachother, and work through the reasoning without citing anyone or dropping references. Keep the tone casual — maybe slip in a typo or two.
