I'm sorting out my free will views for a discussion thread and want a consistency check before I post. Here's my profile:

1. I buy the standard Frankfurt story — Jones is responsible for voting Democrat even if Black never steps in, so PAP in its naive form is dead.

2. I reject the flicker defense, but specifically because the prior indicator (the blush, the twitch) is causally inert with respect to the decision mechanism, not because counterfactual possibilities never matter.

3. I actually think Fischer's alternative-sequence version of PAP is salvageable: responsibility requires that in nearby worlds where different reasons are present you react differently, not that you have an open future right at the moment of action.

4. I'm convinced by manipulation arguments. If a neuroscientist engineers my values from scratch I'm off the hook, and determinism feels structurally analagous — like being programmed by physics and upbringing.

5. At the same time I'm a reasons-responsivness compatibilist: determinism is okay if my actions are properly sensitive to my reasons, which they can be even in a deterministic world.

6. In hierachical Frankfurt cases with a second-order controller, I think the agent lacks responsibility, but not because alternatives are missing. It's what I'd call "source fragmentation" — the first-order will isn't genuinely owned by the second-order structure.

So which of these actually pull against each other? I feel like 1 and 3 might clash but maybe they don't have to. And 4 and 5 seem like they should conflict, though maybe I'm missing how manipulation breaks reasons-responsiveness while determinism doesn't. There's also something weird between 2 and 3: if I care about counterfactual sequences in 3, why do I dismiss the flicker in 2 just for being causally disconnected?

Can someone audit this? Rank the tensions by severity, say which ones are only apparent, and tell me the least painful revision. Don't just list them all as equally broken — I want to know what's actually inconsistent vs what just looks it.
