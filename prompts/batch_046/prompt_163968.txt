I work as a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I have been considering a specific analogy between Quine's rejection of the analytic-synthetic distinction and the architecture of formal languages. In programming, there appears to be an operational separation: the rules enforced by a compiler or type-checker are fixed by the language specification and seem true in virtue of that specification alone, whereas the runtime behavior of a program depends on contingent inputs, hardware states, and external systems. From this, I am inclined to hold that within a formal system there is a robust analytic-synthetic boundary—analytic claims are those entailed purely by the formal specification, while synthetic claims involve the contingent execution environment. Before I commit to this view, I want an honest map of its exposure. Not just the standard Quinean holist point that even logical rules can be revised in principle, but which specific observations, formal results, or conceptual distinctions would genuinely count against it? For example, does the existence of self-modifying code, reflective meta-programming, or the fact that language specifications themselves evolve in response to practical failure blur the line in a decisive way? Would a defender of this formal-systems analogy have to concede that dependent type theories, which embed computational content in types, collapse the syntax-semantics separation? Which of these potential defeaters are fatal—showing that the analogy collapses entirely into conventionalism—versus merely awkward, requiring only local qualification? I am particularly interested in whether this position risks reducing to a purely pragmatic distinction about how engineers choose to organize tools, and whether that outcome should be counted as a defeat or just a refinement. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish those that defenders of the view should concede from those they can reasonably dismiss, and say whether the position is well insulated or genuinely exposed.
