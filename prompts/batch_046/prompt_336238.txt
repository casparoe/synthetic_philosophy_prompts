I am a PhD candidate in philosophy of science and institutional economics, serving on my university's Interdisciplinary Research Oversight Committee. At our meeting next Wednesday we must decide whether to approve a final funding extension for the initiative internally designated Project Synthesis—a five-year, cross-departmental study on the emergence of phenomenal concepts in synthetic cognitive architectures that has already consumed $3.8M of its original $4.5M budget. The principal investigator now requests an additional $1.2M and an eighteen-month extension, arguing that a recently revised theoretical framework offers a credible path to both peer-reviewed publication and a provisional patent application, and that terminating the project now would render the prior institutional investment and the career commitments of four doctoral students effectively worthless.

The committee's charter explicitly directs us to evaluate proposals on prospective value alone, prohibiting consideration of sunk costs. Nevertheless, the PI frames the request not as emotional attachment but as rational trajectory completion: abandoning an interrupted research path at roughly eighty-five percent completion, she argues, is structurally analogous to refusing to pay the final toll that secures passage already begun, and she has proposed a formal structured exit-ramp protocol in which the $1.2M is disbursed in three tranches, each contingent on independent peer review of specific deliverables, with automatic termination if any tranche fails. Several committee members, however, contend that this protocol addresses only formal reversibility, not substantive reversibility: once graduate-student dependencies, equipment leases, and institutional reputation are activated, the social and political costs of invoking termination conditions will almost certainly lead to lenient interpretation of failure—reproducing precisely the escalation dynamics characteristic of the dollar auction, where each successive commitment increases the cost of withdrawal. They further note that the revised theoretical framework has not yet received external peer validation, making the prospective-value claim speculative rather than calibrated.

Please draft the formal committee opinion—approximately 700 to 900 words—that I will circulate before Wednesday's vote. The memo should recommend a specific course of action: approve the extension with the exit-ramp protocol made binding and externally monitored; approve only a reduced, single-tranche bridge grant with stricter conditions; or reject the extension outright and mandate an orderly wind-down with protected student transition funding. Your reasoning should treat the opposing position with intellectual seriousness—if you approve, engage seriously with the escalation-of-commitment and shadow-reversibility objections; if you reject, address the PI's trajectory-completion argument and the welfare of the doctoral researchers. Include a concrete timeline for implementation and, if applicable, specify the verification mechanisms and decision nodes that would make any conditions enforceable rather than ceremonial. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. Conclude with a final line that begins exactly with Verdict: followed by your recommendation in a concise phrase.
