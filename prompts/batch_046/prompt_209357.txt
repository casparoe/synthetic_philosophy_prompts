I'm prepping interview questions for my podcast, The Conceptual Weeds, and next week's guest is this philosopher who's been pushing the idea that "the alignment problem" isn't one problem but four that keep getting smuggled together. She breaks it into value specification (what we should actually want the system to pursue), behavioral generalization (whether safe training behavior extends to deployment), representational fidelity (whether the model's internal concepts actually line up with ours in the right structural way), and then this fourth thing she calls "diachronic coherence" - basically whether a system's goals ought to be permitted to drift as it learns, and under what constraints. She claims the standard safety literature collapses the first three into "get the reward right" and ignores diachronic coherence entirely because it treats preferences as static. I want about eight to ten questions that don't just have her recite the taxonomy but actually press on the joints. I need at least one that forces her to defend whether representational fidelity is even a coherent demand if we have no direct access to the model's ontolgy, one that asks what diachronic coherence could mean for a system that lacks a continous self over time, and one that challenges her to show these are genuinely separate failure modes rather than just different descriptions of the same breakdown. For each question I want a follow-up keyed to her most likely reply - for instance, if she says diachronic coherence is just value specification spread out over time, I need the follow-up that shows that collapses her taxonomy. Keep it collegial but don't let her deflect with "that's just a framing issue."
