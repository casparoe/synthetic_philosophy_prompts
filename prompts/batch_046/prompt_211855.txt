I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of science working on evolutionary explanations of cognition. Daniel Dennett's distinction between cranes—incremental, mechanistic, bottom-up explanatory structures—and skyhooks—mysterious, top-down explanatory devices—has become a central tool for diagnosing greedy reductionism, the view that every higher-level phenomenon can be exhaustively accounted for by lower-level selectionist mechanisms without explanatory remainder. A recent but still underexplored argument in evolutionary linguistics proposes that language acquisition involves a temporary explanatory scaffold: a heuristic that permits apparently discontinuous functional integration during ontogeny, yet is itself ultimately reducible to mechanistic developmental and selectionist processes, after which the scaffold dissolves and leaves only standard cranes. Given Charles Darwin's well-known resistance to saltationist jumps in evolutionary history, alongside his willingness to employ analogical functional reasoning when discussing human mental capacities, how do you think Darwin would classify such a scaffold? Would he regard it as a legitimate variant of a crane—a temporary structural support that remains fully naturalistic—or as a disguised skyhook that smuggles non-gradualist causation into evolutionary explanation? Furthermore, if Darwin were to accept this scaffold as a crane-variant, do you think he would take it to show that greedy reductionism can accommodate discontinuous cognitive capacities without collapsing into eliminativism about those capacities, or would he instead see it as evidence that reductionism must permanently tolerate irreducible intermediate explanatory levels?
