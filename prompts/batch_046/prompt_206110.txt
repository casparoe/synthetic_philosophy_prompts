Okay so I've been going back and forth on what "societal collapse" actually means, and I want to check if my gut verdicts point to one idea or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Cases:
- The Late Bronze Age breakdown: palaces burn, writing vanishes, long-distance trade dies. Definately collapse.
- The Classic Maya lowlands: cities abandoned, monument-building stops, but people keep farming in the hinterlands. Also collapse, even though it's not genocide.
- Easter Island: population crashes, statue-building stops, but they adapt into smaller societies without losing social order. So severe change, not collapse.
- The Western Roman Empire in the 5th century: collapse, even though the East keeps going. The western half lost its institutional backbone.
- The Indus Valley: writing and big cities disappear, but it feels gradual and peaceful, so mabey not collapse — just long decentralization.
- The weird one: imagine a modern state deliberately abolishes its own government, redistributes everything, and GDP/literacy crash because they rejected the old metrics. I wouldn't call that collapse either, since it was chosen.

So is there one principle — mabey "involuntary decoherence of hierarchical institutions" — that saves all six? Or do I have to drop one? And what should it predict for a climate shock that forces a nation-state to split into autonomous regions that keep tech but lose central law — collapse or change?

Also please push back hard if I'm just projecting a modern fear of disorder onto messy history and there's no real distinction here. I want an honest read, not a polite one.
