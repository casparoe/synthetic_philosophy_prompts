I'm writing teh eulogy for my uncle who died tuesday. He was a logician who loved Gödel's incompleteness theorems but always rolled his eyes at Gödel's metaphysical turn -- especially the idea that an afterlife is rationally required because a well-ordered universe wouldn't waste conscious minds on a single brief life. The audience is split: half are mathematicians who know Gödel's ontological proof and his Leibnizian optimism, half are family who think modal logic sounds like a band name. I need about five minutes. The speech should use Gödel's rationalist frame -- that coherence demands survival -- as real structural work, not decoration: it should determine exactly what I can and can't claim about him being at peace or still present without betraying his strict materialism or comforting the family with false promises. He believed mathematics is discovered, not invented, and he lived like meaning is built into things even without God. I want the mathematicians to catch the Gödel reference and the family to just feel that his skepticism and his sense of order aren't in tension. Don't quote anyone, and please don't turn it into a lecture on modal logic.
