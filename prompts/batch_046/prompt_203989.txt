root case: three committee members vote on premises P and Q and conclusion R, with R following logically from P and Q. the vote comes back: P yes (2/3), Q yes (2/3), R no (2/3). now build me a tree.

if unified mind -> second branch: is logical consistency a constitutive norm on that state?

    - derive R from the aggregated premises -> leaf: premise-based aggregation. awkward commitment: the group ends up endorsing R even though a clear majority of members explicitly rejected it.
    - use a distance-minimizing rule to drop the outlier judgment -> leaf: judgment-distance restoration (mostly unnamed). awkward commitment: you might have to discard an accurate majority vote on one premise just to keep the other two consistent.
  - no -> leaf: collective dialetheism (unnamed / barely defended). awkward commitment: the group rationally asserts P, Q, and not-R together, treating contradiction as an acceptable feature of collective cognition.

  - expertise-weighted -> leaf: weighted premise aggregation (unnamed). awkward commitment: you need a principled way to assign weights before you know which premise is actually contested, otherwise you're just stacking the deck for whoever happens to be right.
