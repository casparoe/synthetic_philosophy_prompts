I cover science and ideas, and I'm preparing a feature on whether large language models understand language. A philosopher I interviewed offered the following chain, which seems to prove too much: it forces us either to deny human understanding or to grant it to machines on purely mechanistic grounds. Here is the argument as I reconstructed it.

(1) The stochastic-parrot framing claims that LLMs lack understanding because they produce language solely by computing statistical correlations over training tokens, with no causal coupling to referents, no intentional states directed at meanings, and no world-model beyond the distribution.

(2) Predictive-processing accounts in neuroscience hold that human brains generate language and perception by computing statistical predictions—minimizing prediction error—using weights learned entirely from prior sensory and linguistic exposure, without any separate "understanding module" that transcends the statistics.

(3) If a system's outputs are fully explained by statistical prediction over prior data, and if such prediction is inherently insensitive to present-world causation and to semantic reference, then those outputs do not manifest understanding.

(4) Human linguistic outputs are fully explained by statistical predictive processing (per 2).

(5) Therefore, human linguistic outputs do not manifest understanding—unless there is a relevant difference in kind between neural prediction and silicon-based correlation.

(6) But denying that humans understand language is performatively self-defeating and conflicts with direct first-person knowledge.

(7) So either predictive processing in brains yields understanding while structurally identical computation in silicon does not—which is an unmotivated dualism—or LLMs understand language too, and the stochastic-parrot framing collapses as a criterion for understanding.

The conclusion feels wrong: I want to keep some version of (1) and definitely (6), yet I don't want to be pushed into granting understanding to LLMs by default. Where does this go wrong? Is the flaw an equivocation in (3) between token-level correlation and embodied prediction-error minimization? A scope shift in (4) about what "fully explained" allows? Or is the argument actually valid, so that the real cost lies in giving up (2) or (3)—and if I reject (2), what does that commit me to about the neuroscience of language? I'd like the diagnosis, with the price of each alternative clearly marked.
