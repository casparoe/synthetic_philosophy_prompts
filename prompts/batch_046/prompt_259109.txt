I have been thinking, in conversations with friends who are not philosophers, about how one ought to make a serious moral decision when knowledge is partial and reasoning is biased. Some people seem to hold that you should simply calculate, with the information you actually have, the direct consequences of each available option and choose the best outcome. Others maintain that you should avoid case-by-case calculation altogether and instead follow rules or cultivate character traits that tend to produce good results over time—what I gather is called indirect normativity. A further idea keeps surfacing: that the correct choice is whatever an idealised version of yourself—fully informed, free from self-deception, and reasoning without time pressure—would endorse. I do not understand exactly how these three ideas relate, and I want to avoid answering by memorising labels.

Could you please construct a taxonomy, structured as a decision tree or branching quiz, that sorts the actual positions people hold on this cluster of issues? Each branch should turn on a concrete commitment that a non-specialist could answer without knowing philosophical terminology: for example, whether the value of an action is fixed by its actual results or by its conformity to a rule; whether an idealised deliberator should be modelled with your present values or with corrected ones; whether there can be legitimate moral residue when an indirect rule yields a worse result in a particular case; and whether indirect norms govern only action-types or also the very process of deliberation itself. The leaves should carry named positions where they exist—such as direct act-consequentialism, rule-consequentialism, virtue ethics with indirect norms, ideal-observer contractualism, and the less familiar view that idealisation ought to be applied ex post rather than ex ante—while any unoccupied combinations should be noted and briefly diagnosed (whether incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). Please flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate, phrase every node as a question I could ask myself without already knowing which answer leads where, and write the response in British English. I should also ask that you do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, since I am working without access to external sources.
