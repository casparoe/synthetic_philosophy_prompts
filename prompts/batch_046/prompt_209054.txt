I've had this long commute for about six months now — same highway, same exit, same playlist — and lately I've started noticing something that feels like a glitch in my head. Usually I get home and can't recall the last fifteen minutes, which is whatever, people talk about zoning out. But last week there was a sudden road closure with detour signs I've never seen before, and apparently I navigated this whole back-road maze, avoided a pothole, and turned my blinker on at every intersection like I knew exactly where I was going. The weird part is I have zero memory of seeing the closure, deciding to turn, or even being in the car during those ten minutes. My partener even said I mentioned the detour at dinner, but I don't recall saying a word. So it's not just autopilot on a familiar route — I solved a brand new navigation problem and executed it smoothly, yet there's no "me" in the loop that I can access afterwards.

Is there an established name for this specific configuration? I know philosophers talk about the absent-minded driver, but that example always seems to be about routine actions, like turning left out of habit when you meant to go right. Here the action wasn't habitual at all; it was genuinely adaptive to an unexpected situation. It's also not quite the same as being in "flow," because flow still feels like you're fully present, just not judging yourself. This is more like I was competently present enough to drive well, but absense enough that I formed no conscious intentions or memories of the choices. If there's no specific term, what would you call it, and how should I think about the boundary between this and ordinary distracted driving, or between this and dissociation? I want to understand whether the issue is about memory failure, about missing intentions, or about something else entirely.
