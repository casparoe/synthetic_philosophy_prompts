Compare Bradley's argument that genuinely external relations generate an infinite regress with Russell's response through logical form and the multiple-relation theory of judgment, focusing specifically on what each view implies for propositions that seem to unify terms that do not actually co-exist—false judgments, counterfactual claims, and negative existential statements. Bradley avoids the regress by denying external relations and locating all real unity in an undifferentiated Absolute, but this appears to dissolve the very distinctness that makes false or modal judgment intelligible. Russell preserves the distinctness of terms by treating judgment as a direct mental relation to multiple objects arranged by logical form, yet he still owes an account of how that form itself binds the terms into a single proposition rather than leaving them as an unordered collection.
