Write me the joint statement that a structuralist historian — who thinks Weimar’s collapse was overdetermined by the Dawes Plan reparations cycle, the 1928 agricultural crisis, and the presidential emergency-article machinery — and a contingency historian — who thinks Hitler’s 1932 refusal of the vice-chancellorship, his specific manipulation of the 1933 Enabling Act vote, and the choreographed Night of the Long Knives were irreplaceable — could both sign after a real adversarial collaberation.

Format: 1. Agreed claims (numbered, about 200 words): concrete facts about Weimar’s fiscal and institutional state in 1930–33 and about Hitler’s actual moves in 1933–34 that both sides accept without reservation. 2. Disagreement table (about 250 words): each row a precise disagreement phrased in wording its own side accepts — for instance whether functional equivalents for Hitler’s rhetorical and tactical skills existed inside the NSDAP leadership, and whether the 1934 consolidation was path-dependent on his physical presence. 3. Cruxes and predictions (about 300 words): name the two or three sub-questions that would actually shift someone’s view. For each, state a concrete counterfactual or archival case both agree is relevant — I’m thinking of (a) Goering dying in the 1933 Reichstag fire and the SA never being purged in 1934, and (b) the 1932 presidential election going to a different conservative candidate — and write out each side’s explicit prediction about what that case shows. 4. Verbal or real? (about 100 words): say plainly whether any part of the dispute is just a wording fight over ‘inevitable’ versus ‘highly probable’.

Keep it in British English (behaviour, programme, centre). Dont ask for a bibliography, reading list, or citations — I want something a model can reason through cold, without guessing at sources. Make the formulations sharp but keep the tone casual; a typo or two is fine.
