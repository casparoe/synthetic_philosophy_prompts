Restate Bernard Williams's integrity objection—particularly the claim that moral theories demanding impartial weighing of all projects alienate agents from their ground projects, and the associated scenario in which saving a spouse is defended only after running a full moral calculus—using only that vocabulary. For each core component (integrity as coherence with one's projects; the corruption of agency by calculative reasoning; the normative force of the objection that such reasoning involves one consideration too many), show precisely how it maps onto evolutionary concepts such as unconditional commitment devices, kin-selected local altruism, the evolutionary instability of purely conditional strategies, and the separation of proximate psychological mechanisms from ultimate fitness outcomes. Be explicit about every point where you must insert a non-evolutionary assumption to preserve the argument's force.

Then provide the residue report. Identify which parts translate cleanly into evolutionary terms, which survive only through distortion—for instance, treating integrity as a fitness-enhancing commitment device rather than as a first-person normative condition on authentic agency—and which have no expression at all within evolutionary biology, especially the idea that a ground project is authoritative for an agent regardless of its contribution to inclusive fitness or social equilibrium. Conclude by assessing whether the untranslatable residue indicates a genuine gap in evolutionary explanation—namely, an inability to represent normative authority over one's own life—or whether it reveals that Williams's objection is best understood as a phenomenologically rich description of an evolutionary stable commitment strategy.
