Consider the project of naturalizing mental content through teleosemantics, particularly under the conceptual pressure created by predictive-processing and distributed-architecture models of cognition, in which there is no discrete “consumer” mechanism that validates representations and in which error is computed locally rather than by a dedicated functional endpoint. Within this framing, several open lines of work seem especially urgent: first, whether evolutionary or learning-based selection histories can ground normative representational content without presupposing a designer or a clearly individuated token-state with a single proper function; second, whether a pluralist teleosemantics—permitting genetic, developmental, and culturally acquired selection histories to assign different contents to the same neural or computational state—can be rendered coherent, or whether it collapses into an unacceptable relativism about mental content; third, whether the distinction between misrepresentation and mere malfunction can survive in architectures where representation and represented state are not cleanly separated; and fourth, whether teleosemantics can accommodate genuinely novel representational capacities—such as abstract mathematical or normative concepts—that appear to lack direct evolutionary selection histories but inherit functional roles from more basic cognitive mechanisms.
