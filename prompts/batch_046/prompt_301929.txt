I'm preparing remarks for a graduate seminar on the regress problem and want an audit of my own position before I present it. Here is the package I currently defend: (1) Foundationalism is correct for empirical knowledge—there are basic beliefs that do not depend for their justification on other beliefs. (2) I reject every form of givenism; basic beliefs are not justified by intrinsic phenomenal character or non-conceptual qualitative presence. (3) The justification of basic beliefs comes instead from the reliability of the processes that produce them, but I also hold that judgments about process reliability are not themselves basic; they depend for their support on coherence with broader theoretical commitments. (4) Coherence is necessary for justifying non-basic beliefs and can augment the justification of basic ones, yet it is never sufficient by itself. (5) I accept infinitism for mathematical knowledge: there is no self-evident foundation, only an open-ended chain of derivations, and the regress is benign because each link is locally justified and there is no vicious circularity. (6) I think the structural features that make mathematical infinitism benign—local justification, absence of vicious circularity, and the practical openness of the chain—are present in empirical belief systems as well, but I deny that infinitism applies to empirical knowledge. (7) The distinction between basic and non-basic beliefs is gradational rather than binary: beliefs can be more or less basic, but there is no sharp cutoff where inferential dependence drops to zero. Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severely. For each real tension, specify the cheapest revision that restores consistency and what it costs me. Rank the tensions by severity, and then identify the single revision that would clean up the most problems at once. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Write in American English.
