The student's answer was: "(a) The case involves both testimonial and hermeneutical injustice, though testimonial injustice is primary. This is not merely implicit bias, because implicit bias operates at the individual cognitive level without necessarily carrying the social-structural weight of identity-based prejudice that Fricker identifies; testimonial injustice, by contrast, is structurally embedded and ethically significant regardless of individual intent. (b) Hermeneutical injustice is also present. The residents possess local terminology—'heavy air' and 'tired blood'—but these concepts are not part of the shared institutional hermeneutical pool used by the clinic. However, this does not amount to hermeneutical marginalization in Fricker's precise sense, which occurs when a group lacks the conceptual resources altogether to understand their experience. Here, the residents understand their experience perfectly well; the problem is that the institution refuses to recognize their framework, which is better described as a testimonial failure applied to collective narrative authority rather than a hermeneutical gap.
