I'm a PhD student in axiology, and I've been playing with a kind of hybrid view about Nozick's experience machine. I think the standard intuition pump works not because people care about 'contact with reality' in some metaphysical sense, but because the usual machine strips away narrative agency — you're just a passive recipient of pre-written experiences. But if we imagine a tweaked version where you're actively co-authoring the script with an adpative system in real-time (so your choices genuinely shape the trajectory, but it's still all simulated), then the anti-machine intuition weakens a lot. So my position is that well-being is basically subjective satisfaction plus narrative agency, and reality-contact only matters instrumentally to sustain that agency. What would actually count against this? I don't want the usual 'but you'd miss real relationships' line — I want defeaters specific to this framing. For instance, is there empirical work showing people still reject co-authored simulations? Or a conceptual argument that narrative agency inside a simulation is still 'fake' in a well-being-undermining way regardless? Also please say what would change your mind — like an argument that agency requires some non-simulated causal chain, or data that people reject it even with co-authorship — and flag where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
