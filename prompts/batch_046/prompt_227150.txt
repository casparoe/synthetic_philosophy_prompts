I'm preparing discussion materials for a graduate seminar on axiologies of well-being, where we have been contrasting hedonism, desire-satisfactionism, and the less frequently taught view of tranquilism—the position that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, while positive pleasures or fulfilled desires are at best instrumentally useful and carry no direct axiological weight. For our next session I want a joke, structured perhaps as a brief dialogue or deadpan anecdote, that plays specifically on the internal logic of tranquilism rather than on generic philosophy stereotypes. The punchline should turn on a genuine feature of the view: for instance, the counterintuitive consequence that a patient kept in a permanent, craving-free vegetative state is in a maximally good condition, whereas a fulfilled artist experiencing intense joy but zero suffering is axiologically no better off; or, more specifically, the idea that a tranquilist pharmaceutical consultant would reject a drug that eliminates suffering but also induces mild euphoria, treating that euphoria as axiologically superfluous—like shipping a package with unnecessary padding that adds weight without delivering value.

Please avoid jokes that depend on philosophers being out of touch with reality or on wordplay around the word "tranquil." The humor should arise from the view's own arithmetic of absence, so that someone familiar with the distinction between tranquilism, negative utilitarianism, and Buddhist-inspired ethics of cessation recognizes the joke as conceptually precise rather than a straw man. Keep it deliverable in about a paragraph, suitable for a discussion board post, and do not include bibliographic references or requests for further reading.
