I have been asked to present my philosophical profile on constructivism and logic for a working seminar, and I want an internal consistency audit before I present. Here is the set of commitments I currently hold. (1) Intuitionistic first-order logic is the correct logic for all mathematics, specifically because the law of excluded middle fails for propositions involving infinite totalities. (2) I accept the BHK interpretation of the intuitionistic connectives—proof conditions for conjunction, disjunction, implication, negation, and the quantifiers—without reservation. (3) I hold a strict surveyability constraint: every mathematical object I am willing to admit must be in principle constructible and surveyable by a human agent, and any proof must be reducible to such constructions; I reject any ontology that outstrips explicit constructibility. (4) I nevertheless accept Brouwer's free choice sequences as indispensable for intuitionistic analysis of the continuum, including sequences that are not governed by a law. (5) I maintain that intuitionistic logic should govern empirical reasoning about physical measurement, not only pure mathematics, because physical properties are constituted through observation protocols that are themselves constructive acts. (6) For statements restricted to finitely presented algorithmic processes—where a decision procedure is available—I hold that the law of excluded middle is valid and that classical logic applies without restriction. (7) I deny that coherence with established scientific practice or reflective equilibrium can serve as a constraint on logical commitment; logical principles are prior to empirical science and are not revisable in light of it.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine logical or philosophical tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and what the least costly revision would be for each real tension. Rank the tensions by severity. In doing so, separate what is well-settled in the constructivist literature from what remains genuinely contested among specialists. Lead with your bottom-line verdict in the first sentence, then provide the reasoning; do not preface the answer with a restatement of my question, and do not present any disputed interpretive claim as if it were the consensus view.
