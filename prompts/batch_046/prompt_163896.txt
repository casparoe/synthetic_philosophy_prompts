Write a dialogue between a transcendental arguer and an evolutionary naturalist about whether transcendental arguments actually establish anything about the world or just trace the structure of our thinking. The arguer should defend the claim that even denying objective causation requires a hidden appeal to the transcendental unity of apperception — specifically, that comparing your present skeptical belief with your past evolutionary story already presupposes a single subject persisting through time, which is exactly what the naturalist claims biology can explain away. The naturalist should push back that this is just a linguistic trick, and that diachronic identity can be modeled functionaly without smuggling in Kantian machinery. Make sure they actually respond to each other's moves, neither side is dumb, and don't let them talk past one another. Keep it focused, both voices sharp, no strawmen.
