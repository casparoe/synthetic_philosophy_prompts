As someone whose recent diagnosis has made the statistical fragility of embodied life feel suddenly intimate rather than cosmologically distant, I want a villanelle—nineteen lines, iambic pentameter, with two repeating refrains (A1 and A2) and two rhymes (A and B)—that treats the cosmological fine-tuning argument not as an inference to design but as a meditation on contingent existence. Let the first refrain voice the weak anthropic principle: that observation selects for conditions compatible with observers, rendering the calibration tautological rather than explained. Let the second refrain voice the multiversal selection account: that physical constants vary across domains, and ours is unremarkable among the observed ones. Use the form’s compulsory returns—the refrains alternating through the six tercets and finally converging in the closing quatrain—to enact the philosophical stalemate: neither interpretation resolves whether the constants are genuinely improbable or merely filtered, yet both must accommodate the same precise values. The turn, if there is one, should occur not toward theism but in the recognition that biological vulnerability and cosmic calibration share a structure of improbable maintenance. The poem should be conceptually precise enough that a philosopher of science recognizes the distinction between observational selection and explanatory multiverses, yet it must avoid naming specific physicists, invoking bibliography, or treating the argument as settled. I am writing this for private reflection, not for publication, and I want the conceptual tension rendered entirely through the formal constraint of repetition and convergence.
