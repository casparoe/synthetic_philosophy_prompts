Since retiring from biology I've gotten into philosophy, and two people in my reading group got stuck arguing about whether dreams are actually experiance. (1) Sam said they can't be, following Malcolm -- there's no criteron for being wrong about a dream-apple being red, since you can't go back and check the dream. So calling it an "experience" is a category mistake. (2) Jordan said that's old news, Dennett was right: the dream is just a narrative we spin up the second we wake, there's no inner movie playing during sleep. (3) Sam then brought up leg cramps: people often dream the pain maps exactly onto the body part that actually hurt in real life. If there's no experiential stream during REM, how does the sleeping brain get the spatial mapping right for the thier waking story? (4) Jordan said that's just peripheral signals being woven into the report, like background noise filtered into a narrative. It proves nothing about an inner show running in real time. (5) Sam said but waking perception is also partly reconstructed post-hoc -- the brain fills blind spots, delays signals, builds a model. So if Jordan uses post-hoc construction to deny dream-experiences, he should deny waking ones too, which is absurd. They kept circling. Can you go through the five moves, rate each from clearly wrong to clearly right, and say what both are missing? I keep wondering if memory consolidation blurs the line but I'm not sure how.
