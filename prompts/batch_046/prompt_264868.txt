Before I evaluate particular illusionist accounts of phenomenal consciousness, I want the full specification of what such an account is being asked to deliver. Write the desiderata: the introspective and experimental cases it must get right, the distinctions it must preserve, the neuroscience and cognitive-science results it must accommodate, and the conceptual puzzles it must dissolve — with the motivating consideration behind each. Be sure to include the challenge of accounting for reports of phenomenal absence (the blind spot, inattentional blindness, sensory deprivation) without treating absence as a negative phenomenal property, and the further constraint — call it the cognitive-overreach condition — that the theory must separate representational error about phenomenal properties from representational self-reference that altogether lacks phenomenal anchoring. Flag which requirements are non-negotiable for any illusionist position and which belong to specific camps (eliminativism about phenomenal concepts versus revisionism, compatibility with predictive-processing architectures, preservation of first-person authority). If some subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — for example, if explaining reliable absence reports without real phenomenal properties conflicts with maintaining first-person authority — identify that tension explicitly; it is the part I want most clearly mapped. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard, though you may briefly indicate how Dennett-style and Frankish-style illusionism stand against the list.
