I'm presenting a job-market paper at a comparative-philosophy workshop next week. My thesis is that the Zhuangzi passages on death—specifically the story in which Zhuangzi drums and sings at his wife's funeral and the butterfly-dream episode—should not be read as either (a) a metaphysical dissolution of the self into the Dao that makes individual death illusory, or (b) a therapeutic program recommending emotional detachment from mortality. Instead, I argue that these texts enact a deliberate performative contradiction: Zhuangzi stages an attitude of perfect acceptance toward death while being manifestly alive, embodied, and affectively engaged, and the point of the staging is to show that any philosophical stance toward death—including Daoist "equanimity"—must be adopted from within life and therefore cannot claim the transcendental authority it pretends to have. I am using the notion of performative contradiction only as an analytic heuristic drawn from ordinary-language philosophy, not attributing it to Zhuangzi. The audience will be about twelve people: Sinologists committed to textual fidelity in the received text and wary of anachronistic Western frameworks; analytic philosophers of religion who expect a death-stance to deliver either rational justification or demonstrable therapeutic efficacy; and one or two recent defenders of a "skeptical pragmatist" reading who think Zhuangzi is undermining epistemic certainty rather than making metaphysical claims. They care especially about not importing speech-act theory into classical Chinese material. Prepare me for the objections I will actually get, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way each subgroup would phrase them: what has real force, what I should concede up front, where each reply is weakest, and which objection I am hoping nobody asks. In particular, address whether my heuristic collapses into the very therapeutic reading I reject, and whether I can defend the "performative contradiction" interpretation without claiming Zhuangzi intended anything like Austin's framework. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material, and do not present a contested interpretive view as the consensus.
