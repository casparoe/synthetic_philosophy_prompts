I'm in this philosophy of tech seminar and we've been arguing about whether virtual things can actually be harmed, and my intuitions are all over the place. Here are four cases where I have a strong verdict but can't see the pattern.

One: I pay real cash for a virtual plot in an online world, and the company deletes my account by mistake. That's a real harm, full stop — like property being taken. Two: I spend months building a virtual museum of my late grandmother's pottery designs in a sandbox world, and griefers burn it down. That also feels like real harm, closer to destroying art or a memorial. Three: I'm playing a competitive shooter where stealing gear is literally the point of the game, and someone takes my sword. Not harm at all — it's just playing. Four: in a non-competitive world with no combat rules, another player uses a hidden glitch to erase my museum. I think that's harm, just like the griefers did it. So I have two cases that look structurally identical — virtual objects destroyed by another agent — but one feels like legitimate gameplay and the other like vandalism. The only difference I can point to is whether the world-rules allow it, but that feels circular.

There's also a wierd case I'm unsure about. There's a virtual therapy environment where patients interact with simulated memories of lost loved ones. If the server corrupts one permanently, is that harm to the patient? My gut says yes, but I'm not sure if that's about the virtual object or just the psychological hit.

So what's the actual principle? Is it real-world investment, institutional rules, psychological dependency, or something else? Don't just lay out options — take a definite stand, defend it, and be honest about where my intuitions have to bend.
