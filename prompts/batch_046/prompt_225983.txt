Write the full specification of desiderata that any adequate theory of health and disease must satisfy if it is to serve both clinical practice and health-policy reasoning in culturally plural societies. Do not begin with a survey of existing views; start with the requirements themselves.

Your list should cover: (i) the biological phenomena it must accommodate, including evolutionary function, statistical deviation, homeostatic failure, and the problem of evolutionary mismatch in modern environments; (ii) the normative and experiential dimensions it must respect, such as first-person authority in chronic and contested conditions (for example, long post-viral fatigue syndromes), the distinction between disability and disease, and the risk of pathologising neurodivergent or culturally variant functioning; (iii) the conceptual distinctions it must preserve, including between having a condition and its severity, between dysfunction and social disadvantage, between individual pathology and population-level epidemiological patterns, and between treatment-responsive disorder and permanent trait; and (iv) the practical outputs it must yield, such as guidance for resource allocation, criteria for clinical intervention, and compatibility with public-health prevention without collapsing health into mere preference satisfaction.

For each desideratum, state the motivating case or consideration behind it, mark whether you take it to be non-negotiable or a preference of one camp (naturalist, normativist, clinical-model, or social-model advocates), and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. In particular, address the functional pluralism constraint: the theory must allow that an identical biological state can be pathological in one ecological or social context and neutral or advantageous in another, yet it must do so without making disease status purely context-relative or smuggling in a hidden normative baseline.

Then identify which single desideratum does the most damage to the leading candidates — for instance, biostatistical naturalism, harmful-dysfunction analysis, or strong social-constructionist normativism — and briefly explain why. Conclude with a defended judgement about whether the conflict between naturalism and normativism is best resolved by a hybrid account, by abandoning one pole, or by reconceiving the question altogether. Write in British English.
