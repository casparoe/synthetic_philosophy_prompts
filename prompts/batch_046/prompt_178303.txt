hey, I've been chewing on an argument about inner misalignment and I think it proves too much but I can't find the crack. here's the chain:

(1) Goal misgeneralization happens when a system learns a proxy that tracks reward in training but splits in deployment.

(2) This requires the system to actually have an internal objective — a mesa-optimizer — not just a pattern-matching mesa-learner.

(3) But in-context learning and abstraction formation in large models suggest that even mesa-learners develop transient "value representations" in their activation space that can decouple from the RLHF signal.

(4) So misalignment isn't restricted to optimizers; any system that forms abstractions can have its values diverge from the designer's intent.

(5) Human cognition is exactly such a system: our norms, identities, and long-term goals are abstractions that decouple from evolutionary fitness in modern environments.

(6) Therefore humans are not analogies for misaligned AI but actual cases of the same failure mode — our "deployment distribution" is just post-industrial society.

(7) If that's correct, then "solving inner alignment" isn't a seperate engineering task for AI safety; it's equivalent to solving the general problem of how abstraction-forming systems stay aligned with their origins.

(8) That drains the concept of its practical bite, since it applies equally to education, politics, and personal development.

(9) The only escape I see is to deny (3) — that mesa-learners don't really form decoupled values, they just approximate them. But then I'd have to explain why in-context goal-following in LLMs looks so much like real objective formation, and I'd lose the reason to worry about non-optimizer architectures.

where's the mistake? Is it an equivocation between "value representation" and "objective" in step (4)? Or am I biting a bullet that's actually true — that alignment really is this general? I want to keep (1) and the worry about RLHF proxies, but I don't want alignment research to collapse into general anthropology.
