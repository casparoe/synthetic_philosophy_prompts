Consider the philosophical thesis that value pluralism entails genuine incommensurability: some goods share no common measure, so that selecting between them is not an optimization problem but a hard choice resolved through rational commitment rather than comparative weighing, and that such commitments can leave a structurally unavoidable form of regret even when the choice is correct. I work in welfare economics and mechanism design; my native vocabulary is Pareto efficiency, Arrow-type impossibility results, ordinal utility, preference aggregation, and strategy-proofness, and I have no philosophical training. Translate the thesis above entirely into that vocabulary, preserving conceptual structure without importing philosophical terms by stealth. Then deliver the residue report: what survives intact, what must be distorted to fit (for instance, recasting normative commitment as an exogenous preference revision), and what — particularly the notion of rational, non-erroneous regret — lacks any economic analog. Conclude by judging whether these residues point to a genuine deficiency in economic language or to an ambiguity in the original philosophical claim.
