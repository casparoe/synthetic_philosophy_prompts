I am writing a term paper for my philosophy of mind course on the status of propositional-attitude psychology, and I want to calibrate my own intuitions against a fully decomposed judgment. What is your actual credence—expressed as a specific number between 0 and 1—that eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires is correct in something like Paul Churchland’s strong form: that a mature cognitive neuroscience will ultimately displace, rather than reduce or preserve, our ordinary belief-desire explanations of human action? I do not want a conditional answer that hides behind decision-theoretic ambiguity. Instead, decompose the credence into the following sub-questions, assigning each a rough probability and a one-line justification: (1) that folk psychology is best understood as an empirical theory rather than as a pragmatic interpretive practice or normative framework; (2) that connectionist or predictive-processing architectures in the brain can in principle account for the full range of behaviors currently explained by belief-desire attributions without positing discrete, syntactically structured representational states; (3) that the likely absence of such discrete syntactic structures in neural tissue genuinely undermines the ontological commitment to beliefs and desires, as opposed to merely requiring a revised implementation-level story; and (4) that pragmatic, predictive, or ethical reasons for retaining folk-psychological talk are insufficient to preserve the ontology even if the scientific explanation succeeds. Combine these terms into an overall figure. Then identify which of the four components you are least confident in, explain what empirical discovery or conceptual argument would most substantially shift your overall number, and state whether your credence sits closer to the eliminativist or the retentionist pole in contemporary philosophy of mind.
