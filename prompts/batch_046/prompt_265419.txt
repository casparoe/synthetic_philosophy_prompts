Read E.B. White's Charlotte's Web through the ethical lens that weighs the suffering of farmed animals against the hidden suffering of wild animals killed in crop cultivation, and that compares the total suffering footprint of pork production with that of poultry production. The novel makes Wilbur's impending slaughter feel like an unbearable injustice, and it invites the reader to see Fern's intervention as morally obvious; but apply to Fern's farm the kind of accounting that asks how many small mammals and insects die to grow the feed corn and soy, and whether replacing pigs with chickens would reduce or merely redistribute that death toll. I want a reading that takes both White's narrative craft and this comparative framework seriously: examine the scenes where Wilbur is saved by the ax, where he is exhibited at the fair, and where Charlotte dies leaving her egg sac behind, asking in each case whether the text's sympathy is tied to the visibility and naming of individual victims, and whether that sympathy can survive being scaled up to the statistical suffering that sustains the farm economy. Be honest about where the comparison strains the novel—does anthropomorphizing every barnyard creature illuminate the moral difference between intentional slaughter and collateral wild-animal death, or does it collapse that distinction by giving every creature a human-like voice? Does the book's celebration of Charlotte's maternal sacrifice and Wilbur's continued life inadvertently support the idea that farmed animals matter more than the unnamed wild creatures destroyed by agriculture? And does Charlotte's Web contribute something to this debate that a direct argument about beef versus chicken and crop casualties cannot—perhaps about the moral weight of particular relationships across species, or about how narrative identification shapes our sense of obligation? Write in continuous prose only, with no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables, as if you were composing a long letter to a thoughtful friend who is preparing to teach the novel.
