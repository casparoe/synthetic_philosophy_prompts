We are now attempting to predict her verdicts on three borderline cases, but the real aim is to reverse-engineer the governing rule or rules, since her judgments appear jointly consistent with more than one principled framework. The cases are: (i) a human cerebral organoid cultivated from induced pluripotent stem cells that displays spontaneous gamma-band oscillations and synaptic plasticity but has never received sensory input or directed motor output; (ii) a lobster subjected to thermal nociception protocols, given her prior exclusion of fish but her inclusion of cephalopods; and (iii) a hypothetical distributed bio-synthetic mesh in which living cortical neurons are integrated with silicon sensors across a non-hierarchical grid, lacking any central processor but maintaining continuous global metabolic regulation and adaptive behavior sustained over months.

Please provide your best prediction for each verdict, but prioritize identifying the competing principles—such as central neural integration with architectural exceptions, biological homeostasis coupled to temporal continuity of self-modeling, or a hybrid criterion involving sustained metabolic autonomy—that could jointly explain her entire track record. For each principle, state what it predicts about the three cases, indicate which case best separates the interpretations, and propose one further hypothetical scenario—perhaps involving isolated hemispheres in a split-brain subject or a synthetic substrate that precisely replicates biological metabolism—that would decisively adjudicate between them. Work only from the logical structure of her verdicts; do not invoke specific named theoretical frameworks that depend on external citation.
