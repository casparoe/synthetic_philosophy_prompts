As a novelist working on a literary fiction about a disgraced metaphysician, I have become preoccupied with how Bradley's regress might be dramatized not merely as an abstract logical puzzle but as a crisis of narrative form. In the novel, my protagonist argues with her editor—herself a former philosopher—who claims that if relations cannot genuinely bind terms without spawning an infinite hierarchy of meta-relations, then any story attempting to link events into a coherent plot is committing the same structural fraud. I want the protagonist to reply with a substantive philosophical position rather than mere aesthetic consolation.

Please construct a rigorous argument defending the view that Bradley's regress is resolved by treating propositional unity as an intrinsic, self-articulating feature of predicative structure: the relation between subject and predicate is not an external connector requiring further unification, but is constituted in and through the very act of predication itself, so that differentiation and unity are co-original rather than sequentially ordered. Your reasoning should explain precisely why this avoids collapsing into Bradley's undifferentiated Absolute on one side and Russell's pluralism of external relations on the other, and it should address the strongest objection—that calling predication "primitive" merely renames the mystery of relation without dissolving the regress. Frame the argument as if it were the protagonist's prepared defense for her editor, precise enough to sustain a philosophical exchange without relying on appeals to textual authority or bibliographic reference, and allow the narrative context of plot-coherence to inform the exposition without substituting metaphor for logical analysis.
