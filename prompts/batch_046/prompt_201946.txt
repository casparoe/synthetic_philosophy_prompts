I serve on the research ethics board of an interdisciplinary institute that studies long-term decision-making and public deliberation. Before us is a proposal for a "preference coherence protocol" designed for municipal resource-allocation consultations: participants state initial preferences, then undergo structured reflection and exposure to counter-arguments, after which the system generates an "extrapolated preference profile" meant to represent what they would endorse if more informed, more consistent, and less subject to framing effects. The investigators describe this as an operationalization of idealized preference aggregation, drawing on the philosophical idea of coherent extrapolated volition. Several board members argue that treating reflective stability as normative authority collapses descriptive psychology into prescriptive ethics, and that the protocol implicitly privileges one controversial conception of rational autonomy without democratic mandate. Others contend that standard polling is already distorted by ignorance and inconsistency, so refining inputs is a methodological correction rather than a covert imposition of values. Please draft the board's decision memo — approve with conditions, reject, or approve outright — recommending a concrete course of action with a time frame for implementation or revision. The memo should include criteria reviewers could apply to distinguish legitimate methodological refinement from normative engineering, and should engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than dismissing it.
