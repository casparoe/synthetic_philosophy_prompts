What is your credence—give me an actual number between 0 and 1—that in such a system the meaning of the output resides primarily in the frame message (the user's interpretive context and intentions) rather than in the outer message (the literal tokens) or in any inner message (a stable internal representational state)?

Decompose your answer into three parts with rough probabilities: (a) how likely it is that Hofstadter's tripartite framework applies analogously to non-conscious, stateless generators; (b) given that it applies, how likely it is that current models genuinely lack an inner message; and (c) how likely it is that the outer message is semantically underdetermined without the user's frame. Show roughly how these combine into your overall number, then identify which term is your weakest and what kind of evidence—computational, philosophical, or conceptual—would move it the most.
