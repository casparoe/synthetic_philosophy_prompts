Restate the core logic of superrationality as it appears in the Platonia dilemma and in the Luring Lottery entirely within the vocabulary and conceptual machinery of distributed consensus. Then translate and extend a variant I will call the Byzantine Lottery. In this scenario, a cluster of nodes shares an identical reasoning architecture and must choose between committing to a shared cooperative state or forking to an individual defect state. A large collective reward is released only if all nodes reach unanimous agreement on cooperation. However, a minority subset—Byzantine nodes—possesses corrupted or heterogeneous reasoning architectures. Any standard node that detects these Byzantine nodes may break quorum, defect unilaterally, and capture a smaller, guaranteed immediate payout, but in doing so it forfeits the collective reward for the entire cluster.

For both the standard cases and this Byzantine variant, provide a residue report: identify which claims translate cleanly into distributed-systems terms, which survive only with significant distortion, and which have no expression at all in that vocabulary. Conclude by saying whether the residue points to a genuine gap in distributed-systems theory (for example, a missing form of cross-node counterfactual reasoning) or whether it reveals that the philosophical argument was relying on a metaphor that dissolves under technical formalization.

Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts. Do not invoke obscure philosophical formalisms unfamiliar to a scientifically literate general reader. Work only with the concepts and vocabulary I have specified above.
