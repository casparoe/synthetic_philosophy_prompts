I'm serving on the Institutional Review Board for the Municipal Governance Innovation Lab. We must decide on a proposal to deploy an automated "normative synthesis module" in a pilot zoning-reform process. The module is explicitly designed not to model the beliefs, values, or intentions of residents, planners, or officials; instead, it generates structurally coherent policy justifications—what the applicants call "free-floating rationales"—that are not attributable to any particular reasoner or collective agent. The applicants argue that the normative force of a rationale depends solely on its logical and evidential structure, not on its origin in a human mind, and that substituting such rationales for deliberative output is legitimate if the rationales are sound. Opposing board members contend that a reason without a reasoner lacks genuine normative authority, that deploying such rationales in public decision-making severs accountability from justification, and that no structural quality can compensate for the absence of a responsible agent behind the recommendation. We need to choose among approving the pilot (possibly with binding safeguards), rejecting it outright, or requiring a fundamental redesign that anchors every output to an identifiable human judgment process.

Draft a formal recommendation memo addressed to the full board. State your recommended course of action clearly, including any specific conditions or monitoring requirements that should attach to it. The memo should contain: (1) an operational criterion that distinguishes permissible use of synthesized reasoning from impermissible replacement of human judgment in this context; (2) the reasoning supporting your recommendation, treating the applicants' structuralist position with full charity; and (3) a substantive, honest account of the strongest argument for the option you do not recommend, explaining exactly what that option gets right and why it nonetheless loses. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or appeals to authority; ground the analysis in the conceptual structure of reasons, agency, and democratic accountability as they apply to this pilot.
