Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a philosopher who accepts Dennett's argument that qualia are an incoherent posit—using the Chase-and-Sanborn coffee-taster intuition pump as the central case—and a philosopher who rejects that argument and defends the coherence of intrinsic phenomenal properties.

Structure the document with the following sections:
1. Agreed claims: a numbered list of propositions both sides accept about the Chase-and-Sanborn scenario (that both tasters initially agree on the taste of Maxwell House coffee; that they later diverge in preference without any detectable change in the coffee blend or in their perceptual apparatus; that the scenario is designed to probe whether there could be an intrinsic qualitative change that leaves all observable and functional features fixed; and that if qualitative properties are defined as intrinsic and immediately known, then undetectable inversion is conceptually possible).
2. Disagreements table: state the core divergence in neutral wording, then restate each position in wording its own side would accept. Cover (a) whether the possibility of undetectable inversion undermines the very concept of qualia or merely demonstrates the indirectness of qualitative access; (b) whether qualitative judgments can in principle be corrected by third-person behavioral or neural evidence; and (c) whether talk of an invariant phenomenal character can avoid circular appeal to the very judgments being explained.
3. Cruxes and predictions: identify two cruxes. First, whether a perfect behavioral and neural duplicate of a conscious subject could lack phenomenal experience altogether—apply this to the tasters by asking whether duplicating Chase's neural state in a system with no subjective taste experience would show that his original experience was not an intrinsic property. Second, inject the following hypothetical case: Chase and Sanborn undergo a fully monitored, reversible neural modulation that inverts their preference reports and associated verbal behavior, yet each consistently reports that the taste remains personally identical across every phase, with intact memory of prior reports. Specify what each camp predicts this demonstrates—whether it reveals the incoherence of an invariant intrinsic quality or rather shows that phenomenal properties can remain stable despite complete behavioral inversion.
4. For each crux, name one concrete conceptual test or argument both sides agree bears on it (for the first, the coherence of applying a zombie-style argument specifically to taste experience; for the second, the consistency of first-person reports under systematic behavioral inversion) and record each side's explicit prediction.

Keep the document formal, do not request citations or bibliographies, and avoid dependence on specific publication dates or page numbers. The task should require precise conceptual analysis of the intuition pump and the injected hypothetical, not recitation of a standard summary.
