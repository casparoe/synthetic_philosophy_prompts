For a philosophy of science seminar, construct a decision tree that classifies stances on whether Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity vindicate, replace, or undermine Occam's razor in scientific reasoning. The root should be the acceptance that Kolmogorov complexity is uncomputable and that the Solomonoff prior weights hypotheses by 2 raised to the negative Kolmogorov complexity. From there, branch by whether one treats this prior as normative (a rational constraint on belief) or descriptive (a statistical regularity of prediction). Next, within each branch, split on whether algorithmic simplicity should be interpreted syntactically (shortest program in a universal language) or semantically (fewest independent ontological kinds), and then on whether uncomputability is a fatal practical flaw, a harmless idealization, or an explanation of induction's difficulty. Every internal node should be phrased as a concrete claim a student could evaluate without knowing the label of the view it leads to. Each leaf should carry a named position — for example, a normative syntactic view that embraces the idealization, or a descriptive semantic view that treats uncomputability as fatal — together with its most awkward commitment, such as having to explain why scientists rationally prefer general relativity over a finely tuned empirical fit with identical predictions. Note any pair of leaves that your tree fails to distinguish and diagnose any unoccupied combinations (e.g., a normative semantic view that treats uncomputability as fatal) as either incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored.
