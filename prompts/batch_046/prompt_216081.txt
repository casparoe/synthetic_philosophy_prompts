Write an essay evaluating whether the moral logic of deplatforming and coordinated social sanction is better captured by a model of distributed punishment or by a model of reputational boundary maintenance. Examine the argument that, because these sanctions lack a centralized authority, predetermined procedures, and formal proportionality scales, they fall outside the normative framework that governs state punishment and should not be judged by retributive or procedural constraints. Then examine the counter-argument that functional equivalence—deliberate harm inflicted in response to perceived wrongdoing, with expressive and deterrent aims—subjects them to analogous moral requirements regardless of institutional form. In reaching a defended conclusion, specifically address whether the absence of an appeal mechanism and the asymmetry of re-entry costs, whereby the sanctioned party bears the full burden of rehabilitation without any structured path to reinstatement, undermines the legitimacy of the practice, or whether these very features are what allow social sanction to serve a distinct moral function that formal punishment cannot replicate. Your conclusion should be explicit: either that social sanction is genuinely punitive and must therefore be constrained by proportionality and due-process norms, or that it is a categorically different practice whose legitimacy depends precisely on escaping those constraints.
