I keep encountering the slogan “animals are just machines” in discussions about whether causing animals pain is morally serious—usually offered as if it ends the debate. Without a philosophy background, I have been trying to unpack what that claim could precisely mean, since it seems to range from “they lack an immaterial soul” to “they are literally built of gears” to “their behavior is fully explicable by reflex without any inner experience.” Could you lay out the distinct theses that slogan might express, marking which are trivial, which are substantive and defensible, and which are plainly false? For each reading, please say whether it actually supports dismissing animal suffering, since that is the work the slogan was doing when I heard it. I also keep running into my own comparison: if being a “machine” here means that every behavior is mechanically explicable without consciousness, then wouldn’t a human in a permanent vegetative state be a “machine” in exactly the same sense? Does that analogy expose a weakness in any of the readings, or is there a version of the claim that can block it?
