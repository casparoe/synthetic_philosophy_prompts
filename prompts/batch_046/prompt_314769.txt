(1) Any philosophical theory can be formalized as a constraint-satisfaction problem over its core claims. (3) A rational agent should not maintain beliefs whose global consistency she is unable to verify. (4) Human cognition is physically bounded and cannot solve NP-hard or undecidable verification problems for its own large belief sets. (5) Therefore no human rationally holds a complex philosophical theory; we hold only heuristic approximations we mistake for genuine commitments. (6) Yet the same reasoning applies to scientific frameworks, mathematical conjectures, and ordinary belief networks, implying that rationality is impossible everywhere, which is absurd. (7) So one premise must be rejected. Which is the weakest link—the formalization in (2), the rationality norm in (3), or the cognitive bound in (4)—and what does giving it up cost the broader project of using complexity theory to critique philosophy?
