In discussions of historical sociology, people often reduce Weber's well-known argument to a single compressed slogan: "the Protestant ethic caused modern capitalism." The phrase gets used to defend cultural explanation, to dismiss it as unscientific, and to settle disputes about whether values can appear in causal accounts at all. I want that slogan taken apart precisely.

Please lay out the distinct theses this one-liner could actually be expressing, separating and evaluating at least the following readings: (1) a counterfactual-dependence claim about regional economic trajectories—would capitalism have failed to emerge, or taken a substantially different form, absent this value-complex? (2) a motivational-psychological claim about the individual dispositions and calculative habits of early capitalists; (3) a selection or functional claim about which value-orientations survived competitive market pressure and were therefore retained in institutional niches; (4) a constitutive or hermeneutic claim that capitalism as a social form required a particular ethical vocabulary to become stable, intelligible, and reproducible across generations; and (5) any other live reading you can identify in Weber's own argument or in its standard philosophical reception.

For each reading, state whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or conceptually confused, and specify what kind of evidence, comparative case, or counterfactual scenario would actually support or undermine it. Then address the standard objection that "culture" or "values" cannot be causes in the same sense as material conditions: for each reading that survives this objection, explain exactly how it restructures the explanatory claim—whether by appealing to mechanism-based causation, to counterfactual intervention on institutional conditions, to selection dynamics, or to some other framework—so that values do genuine explanatory work without violating standard conditions on causal explanation (e.g., independent manipulability, counterfactual dependence, or non-redundancy).

Finally, indicate which of these readings is typically being invoked when someone uses the slogan to defend the autonomy of cultural explanation against economic reductionism, and which is being invoked when an opponent uses the same slogan to caricature Weber as claiming that religious beliefs literally produced factories, banks, and balance sheets.
