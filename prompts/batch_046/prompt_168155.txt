My friend maintained that Marx's nineteenth-century categories are too historically embedded to capture a situation in which the machinery of coordination is itself an immaterial form of constant capital that operates without physical presence, while I contended that real subsumption was precisely intended to describe cases where capital reshapes the labor process itself rather than simply inserting machines into an otherwise intact craft. We left the disagreement unresolved, but it raised a precise question I would like you to address carefully.

Assume, for the sake of the exercise, that Marx were confronted with this specific configuration—algorithmic management treating worker availability as a continuously variable input to be optimized by predictive models rather than as a fixed labor cost. Drawing on the conceptual structure of his writings about machinery, the factory system, and the extraction of surplus value, predict how he would likely analyze its implications for worker autonomy and for the dynamics of capital accumulation. In particular, address whether he would regard this as an extension of real subsumption, and how he might respond to the common management claim that such systems increase worker flexibility and therefore represent a departure from the coercive dynamics he described.
