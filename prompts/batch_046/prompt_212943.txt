I have been reflecting on the well-known philosophical argument—often associated with Susan Wolf—that a moral saint, understood as a person whose life is entirely governed by moral reasons, would be an undesirable or unattractive figure, lacking personal projects, playful humor, and non-moral virtues. This seems to generate a puzzling structural problem for axiology: if moral value is genuinely good, yet its unrestricted maximization apparently lowers the overall value of a life, then value should be non-monotonic with respect to moral achievement. Could the resolution be that Wolf is not describing a formal feature of value at all, but is instead relying on a substantive normative claim—perhaps that human flourishing has lexical priority over moral perfection—that, if rejected, dissolves the tension? Or is there a coherent formal model of non-monotonic moral value that I am missing?
