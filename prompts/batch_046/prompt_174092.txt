Compose a villanelle in which the two mandatory refrains embody the opposing sides of Frege's puzzle about identity: one refrain expressing the tautological form in which an object is identical to itself and no new information is gained, the other expressing the informative form in which two distinct names or descriptions denote the same object yet yield cognitive value. Let the refrains alternate through the middle stanzas and finally converge in the closing couplet without collapsing into one position, so that the poem's formal structure — its returning lines and their ultimate adjacency — enacts the persistent tension between sameness of reference and difference of sense. The poem should show accurate command of the distinction between sense (Sinn) and reference (Bedeutung), and it should weave in the less commonly emphasized variant that occurs with fictional entities, letting the recognition that two apparently different fictional characters are in fact the same invented figure serve as the volta or turning point. Write in precise, formal English; include no citations, bibliographies, or footnotes; and construct the verse so that a reader familiar with Frege's argument recognizes its shape in the rhyme and refrain pattern, while a reader without that background still encounters a self-contained lyric about the strangeness of discovering that what you already knew, under one description, was true under another all along.
