(c) I deny that there is any diachronically persisting "executive self" outside this sequence of momentary preference states; agency consists only in the causally linked chain of these states, bound by memory, anticipation, and the shadow of future rewards.

(d) I hold that moral blame for an akratic act is appropriate only if the agent had access to a feasible precommitment strategy — such as a Ulysses contract, a cooling-off rule, or an external commitment device — and failed to deploy it; bare failure in the heat of the moment, without such access, is not blameworthy.

(e) I believe reflective equilibrium requires treating one's long-term, cool-headed evaluations as normatively authoritative over present-biased impulses, but I also claim that this authority derives solely from the evaluation being shared across more temporal slices, not from any metaphysically privileged standpoint.

(f) I think that if hyperbolic discounting is genuinely hardwired and not correctable by reasoning alone, then the rational plan is just the one that maximizes the agent's actual discounted utility, which implies that the reflective evaluation in (e) is authoritative only when it aligns with what a precommitment strategy would secure.
