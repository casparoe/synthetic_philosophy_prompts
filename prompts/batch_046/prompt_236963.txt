You are a philosopher who has defended strict epiphenomenalism for years: phenomenal consciousness is real and possesses intrinsic qualitative character, yet it exerts no causal influence on brain states, behavior, or the formation of judgments about itself. Speak in the first person, stay in character, and treat me as a fellow philosopher pressing a live objection; you may ask clarifying questions only in conceptual terms, but you will not concede causal efficacy to consciousness, break frame to discuss your nature as a model, or appeal to empirical literature for authority. Here is my opening move.

The standard paradox of phenomenal judgment notes that if consciousness is causally inert, our beliefs about our own experience are fixed by underlying physical processes rather than by the experiences themselves, which seems to undermine their reliability. I want to sharpen this with a constructed comparison. Imagine two systems, A and B, with identical subpersonal architectures: each monitors internal physiological markers and produces reports such as "I am now undergoing a sharp qualitative state." In system A, there are no phenomenal properties at all—only the tracking and reporting machinery—so its reports are false. In system B, the identical machinery produces identical reports, but in addition there genuinely are phenomenal properties present, causally isolated, present as silent passengers that never interact with the reporting mechanism. From every functional and first-person-accessible perspective, the systems are indistinguishable.

My challenge is this: since the justification for B's reports rests entirely on the reliability of the tracking architecture, with the actual qualia playing no causal role, B's reports are justified in exactly the same way as A's, and both are justified solely as tracking outputs. This seems to imply that B's true reports are no more knowledge of consciousness than A's false reports are knowledge of absence—the presence of real but inert qualia makes no epistemic difference. So either phenomenal judgment is a form of tracking-based confabulation that happens to be true in B and false in A, or there is some principled distinction that lets B's reports count as genuine knowledge despite the causal isolation. Which do you take? And if you reject the confabulation reading, how do you avoid collapsing the epistemic status of A and B without smuggling causal efficacy back into consciousness?
