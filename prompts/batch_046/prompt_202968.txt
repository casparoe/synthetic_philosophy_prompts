If Nozick's experience machine demonstrates that well-being requires an irreducible "contact with reality" component, and if objective-list theories already encode this component through criteria like genuine achievement and non-deceptive relationships, then doesn't modifying hedonism to include a "causal-origin filter"—counting only pleasures whose intentional objects are actually realized in the external world—simply turn hedonism into an objective-list theory in subjective clothing? Consider specifically a two-stage machine: Stage One delivers pure simulated pleasure, but Stage Two activates a "reality-check protocol" that lets the subject verify whether her achievements and relationships are causally connected to real states of affairs, and automatically downgrades simulated satisfactions to zero well-being value. If a hedonist endorses Stage Two as the correct model of well-being—because it preserves pleasure-maximization while filtering out deception—has she not implicitly accepted that the normative work is being done by reality-contact rather than by pleasure? Given that informed-desire theorists can apply the same filter through an epistemic constraint, is the experience machine best interpreted not as a localized objection to mental-state theories, but as proof that any adequate theory of well-being must be structured around reality-tracking conditions, rendering the subjective/objective distinction merely taxonomic?
