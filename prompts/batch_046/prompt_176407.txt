I have been working through Quine's attack on the analytic-synthetic distinction and the various attempts to rehabilitate or replace it, and before I commit to any particular defense I want a clear specification of what such a theory is actually being asked to do. Would you write out the full list of desiderata that any adequate post-Quinean account of conceptual or semantic distinction—whether it rehabilitates analyticity, replaces it with conceptual-role semantics, or abandons it for a graded continuum—must satisfy? I want the requirements spelled out in detail: which phenomena of ordinary linguistic practice it must accommodate, such as the felt difference between learning that bachelors are unmarried through conceptual mastery versus learning that gold has atomic number seventy-nine through empirical investigation; which puzzles it must dissolve or explain away, including the circularity worries that Quine raised about defining synonymy and the apparent existence of revisable "definitions" in mathematics, law, and science; which distinctions it must preserve, for instance between conceptual error and empirical error, or between a change in meaning and a change in belief; and which results from philosophy of science or cognitive psychology it cannot flatly contradict without special pleading, particularly regarding how conceptual boundaries actually shift under theoretical pressure. I am especially interested in whether there is a hidden tension between the demand that the distinction be sharp enough to do genuine explanatory work in epistemology and the demand that it be flexible enough to survive historical cases like the reclassification of Pluto or the revision of geometric axioms—if these two demands turn out to be jointly unsatisfiable, I want that flagged explicitly, since that conflict itself may be the most important result. For each desideratum, please indicate whether it represents a widely shared constraint or rather a preference of one particular camp, and please identify which single requirement, if taken seriously, eliminates the largest number of competing theories.

Please keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a full scorecard, though I do want a brief assessment of how descriptivism about meaning, a two-dimensional framework separating primary and secondary intension, and a purely pragmatist or deflationary account fare against the list. Write this entirely in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as you would in a long and careful letter to a colleague, and please take care to separate what is genuinely settled among philosophers working on this topic from what remains actively contested, without presenting any one camp's reading of Quine or of the distinction itself as if it were the consensus view.
