ok so i've been chewing on nozick's experience machine but with some tweaks that make the usual answers feel shaky. here are my gut verdicts:

1. classic full plug-in, no idea it's fake, perfect life: i still wouldn't do it. i want to actually do things, not just feel like i did.

2. same machine but you keep full meta-awareness that it's simulated, and you can toggle out whenever: i think i'd actually plug in here. the reality-contact worry is gone because i know, and i can leave.

3. now imagine a 'layered' machine where your beliefs stay anchored to the real world — you know you're in a pod, you know your family is aging out there — but your emotional and sensory life is replaced by blissful synthetic ones. i think this is worse than case 1, not better. being emotionally disconnected from reality feels like a kind of betrayal, even if you're lucid.

4. but then there's this variant: you enter the machine, live a life of genuine artistic achievement (you actually write the symphonies in the sim, they're real in the sim, and you remember the struggle), but the 'struggle' was algorithmically smoothed. i think my well-being here is high, maybe as high as real life, even though i didn't 'really' suffer.

5. finally, the 'drift' case: you're in a machine for 50 years, then pulled out with false memories of having lived a real life. at extraction you realize it was all fake. do you retroactively lose well-being? i think no — the years felt good — but also yes, because the narrative you built your identity on collapses.

so my pattern is: no to 1, yes to 2, strong no to 3, yes to 4, and split on 5. is there one principel that captures all this? maybe it's about emotional calibration to real events, or about narrative ownership, or maybe i'm just being inconsistent. specifically, can any well-being theory — hedonist, desire-fulfillment, objective list — deliver that 2 is okay but 3 is awful, given that both involve full awareness?

also, what should my best principle predict for a case i haven't listed: a machine that gives you real-world causal powers (you can actually send messages out, affect people) but all your internal experiences are synthesized? and be honest: which of my verdicts has to go if i want the simplest account?
