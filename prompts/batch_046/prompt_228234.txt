“The reasoning begins correctly: if Friday were the day and it had not happened by Thursday night, then Friday morning everyone would rationally expect it, violating the condition. However, the paradox is resolved once we see that surprise requires total ignorance that the event will occur at all. Because the students knew an exam was coming, they were never in a position to be genuinely surprised—only misled about the timing. In particular, by Wednesday morning the backward induction has already eliminated Wednesday, so they should rationally expect it then; the fact that they feel surprised is just a psychological reaction, not a logical contradiction.

This structure is identical to the unexpected hanging paradox, and both demonstrate that any self-referential prediction necessarily destroys the conditions it describes. Therefore the teacher’s announcement contains an internal contradiction: if the class applies correct reasoning, no day can satisfy the condition, so the exam cannot exist.
