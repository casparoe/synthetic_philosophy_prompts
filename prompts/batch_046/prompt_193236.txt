Here's an argument I cooked up after reading about Moore's paradox and now it feels like it eats itself. (1) If it's irrational to assert p but I don't believe p, that's because asserting p commits you to believing p. (2) The same commitment runs upward: asserting I believe p commits you to believing that you believe p, and so on. (3) So for any n, rationaly asserting p requires having an nth-order belief that p. (4) By induction, rational assertion requires an infinite hierachy of beliefs about p. (5) But no finite mind actually holds infinitely many explicit beliefs. (6) Therefore no finite being can rationaly assert anything. That can't be right, but every step looks like a natural extension of Moore's point. I'd rather keep (1) and (2) than swallow (6), but I don't see where the slide happens. Is the induction illegitimate? Does commitment change meaning as we go up? Tell me exactly which step to kill and what killing it does to the original Moore intuition. Don't just list options -- take a stand and defend it.
