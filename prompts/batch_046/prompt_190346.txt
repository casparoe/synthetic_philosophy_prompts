I've been sitting through zoning board hearings for a project near my place, and it's honestly surreal—neighbors object that new apartments will "change the charcter" of the block, and the board makes developers do shadow studies and traffic reports, but they just waived all that for a luxury condo with twice the height. What philosophical commitments about who the city belongs to does that practice presuppose? Does treating "neighborhood character" as a protected interest only work if you think current residents have a collective property right in the spatial experience of the area—something like a spatial version of Locke? And why is affordable housing treated as a harm that needs mitagation, while luxury development gets tax breaks—what theory of urban justice or "the right to the city" makes both of those moves hang together? Or is the board just running on incoherent premises about property, community, and who counts as "the public"?
