I was arguing with my friend last night about whether generations are actually real kinds or just something marketters and sociologists made up to sell books. I'm taking a course on social ontology this semester and it's got me confused, becuase I keep flipping back and forth on cases that should be straightforward. Here are my gut reactions on different groupings and I want to know if there's a coherent principle behind them or if I'm just being inconsistent.

I think Boomers are a real kind, or at least closer to one, becuase they share this specific historical trajectory: post-war economic boom, Vietnam draft, the moon landing, all hitting at formative ages. But Millennials feel fake to me, like the birth years are totally arbitrary (1981 to 1996?) and someone born in '81 has almost nothing in common with someone born in '96 except maybe owning a smartphone. Gen Z feels a little more real becuase they actually grew up with the internet from birth, not just adopted it, but then again that's more about technology diffusion than a shared social identity. I also think calling Gen Alpha a generation is premature, like we're projecting a kind onto toddlers who haven't developed any collective consciousness yet.

But here's where I get tangled. I think the "COVID cohort" — people who turned eighteen between 2020 and 2022 — is genuinely a real group, even though it slices right through Gen Z and Millennials. And "the 9/11 generation," people who were old enough to understand 9/11 but young enough to have their worldview shaped by the security state and forever wars that followed, feels more ontologically solid to me than "Millennial" does, even though it cuts across Boomers, Gen X, and Millennials. Meanwhile, "Xennials" (around '77 to '83) just feel like a marketing bridge, not a kind, even though they share the experience of analog childhood and digital adulthood. So my verdicts are: Boomers yes, Millennials no, Gen Z maybe-but-probably-not, COVID cohort yes, 9/11 cohort yes, Xennials no, Gen Alpha premature/no. Is there a principle here about formative historical events occurring during critical developmental windows, or about collective self-identification, or about demographic clustering? Or am I just picking favorites? Please lay out what principle captures the pattern, which verdicts it forces me to drop if any, and what it predicts for a case I haven't mentioned — like whether "the remote-work generation" (people who started their first jobs fully remote in 2020-2021) counts as a kind. And please, no bullet points or headings, just write it out like you're emailing me back. Also, if the principle relies on shared consciousness, does that mean generations don't exist in societies that don't have a concept of generations? And what about people born in the same year in totally different countries — is there one global kind or are there local ones? My friend kept saying that all demographic categories are just statistical clusters with fuzzy edges, like "tall people" or "redheads," and I feel like there's a difference but I can't articulate it without sounding like I'm just being essentialist about birth years.
