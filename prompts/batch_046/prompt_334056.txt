In a debate about replacing a career legislature with term-limited offices filled partly by random selection, an interlocutor kept shutting down defenses of term limits alone by repeating, "Election is aristocratic, lot is democratic." I want to press back, but I need the slogan unpacked first. Can you lay out the distinct precise theses that line could be expressing—distinguishing, for instance, whether "aristocratic" means rule by a self-selecting elite, rule by those with superior campaign skill, or rule structurally disconnected from the governed; whether "democratic" requires equal ex ante chance of holding office, descriptive representativeness, or the absence of professional career incentives; and whether the claim is conceptual (defining democracy), empirical (predicting institutional outcomes), or normative (assigning legitimacy)? For each reading, say whether it is plausible, whether it actually undermines term limits without sortition, and which version someone using the line to dismiss term-limit proposals probably intends. Give me the readings as a menu rather than defending one, and flag which ones are trivial, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false.
