I'm a neuropsych intake clinician, I see about four patients a week with dissociation or weird perceptual reports. I buy the idea that consciousness is basically a user illusion -- not a window but a dashboard the brain builds for control, and usually it's functionally transparent so you never notice the construction. I need a reusable 20-minute checklist I can run in intake to classify a first-person report like "the filter dropped and I saw things as they really are" or "my self-model feels broken." I want to distinguish three outcomes: the illusion is intact but misinterpreted (therapy route), the illusion machinery is damaged (neuro referal), or there's interface leakage where the model starts representing its own construction (uncertain / both).

The procedure should have ordered tests I can do without scans, mark where I'm just making a judgment call dressed up as a step, and handle cases where the patient is highly articulate vs barely verbal. Apply it to three hypotheticals: (1) a philosophy grad student who describes seeing "raw sense data without conceptual overlay" after meditation; (2) a stroke survivor with right-parietal damage who says "the world feels flat and made of cardboard"; (3) one where the checklist points to neurology but the patient desperately wants therapy and will feel betrayed by the referal. Also tell me what the procedure flat-out can't do -- especially if the illusion framework is wrong and there's actually direct access somewhere. Keep it honest about which steps are just me guessing.
