Hey, I'm a retired ecologist who only started reading philosophy last year, so apologies if I'm botching the jargon. At my book club we got stuck on Davidson's radical interpretation idea. Two of us — me and a guy named Marcus — couldn't agree, and the others asked us to shut up. Can you adjudicate?

Marcus said: if we radicallly interpret an alien, Davidson's charity principle means we maximize belief agreement. So if they point at snow and call it 'fire' but never flinch near flames, charity forces us to say they don't mean fire — they mean snow, and we're mistranslating.

I said: that's mixing up belief with meaning. Charity is about rationality, not translation. They could genuinely think snow is dangerous and call it 'fire' because their taxonomy is different. Davidson doesn't let us rewrite beliefs just to save agreement.

Marcus came back with triangulation: meaning gets fixed by shared causal interaction with the world and other speakers. If we both point at snow together, the word should anchor to snow regardless of their vocabulary.

I replied: but triangulation needs a shared form of life, right? In my old fieldwork we tracked animals by thermal signatures, not sight. If the aliens sense infrared, 'snow' for them might truely track 'cold-radiating-object', not the white stuff. So charity plus triangulation might actually push us to reinterpret, not converge.

Marcus said I'm just sneaking in relativism Davidson rejects — he thinks there's one correct interpretation that makes them mostly true.

So who's right move by move? Is charity about belief-rationality or meaning-fixing? Does triangulation solve this or just push the problem back to whose causal chain counts? And where are we both missing something? Please go through each step. Also I spelled radicallly with three l's — don't mind me.
