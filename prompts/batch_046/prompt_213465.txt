I'm a retired ecologist who recently got into philosphy and I'm stuck on something that came up at our watershed board. We had grant money to either fully protect one old family farm from flooding—it's been in the same family since the 1800s—or do partial mitgation for five seperate smallholdings downstream. The board went with the five, arguing that more people protected means more good, full stop. But I kept wondering: if the flood actually hits, the harm to that one farm is total loss of land and livelihood, just like each of the five faces its own partial ruin. In ecology we learn that stressors aren't always additive—two ponds hit by the same pollutant don't suffer "twice" the damage of one, they suffer in different ways. So is counting heads (or farms) missing something essential about how harm is distributed? I feel like there might be a real philosophical argument that saving the greater number isn't automatically right when the damage to each unit is identical, but maybe I'm just overfitting my science brain onto ethics. What's your take? I don't need citations, just trying to think it through.
