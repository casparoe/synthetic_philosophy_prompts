For my school's philosophy and civic ethics seminar, I am preparing a position statement on the ethics of public commemoration—specifically statues and street names—before we discuss a proposed municipal framework. I want an internal consistency check rather than historical references. Here are my commitments:

(1) Commemorative installations are not neutral archives but acts of 'aspirational projection': they signal which traits a polity wishes to cultivate in itself.

(2) Therefore, retaining a monument to a figure whose actions violated moral principles that were accessible in their own historical context amounts to 'retrospective moral licensing,' and such monuments should be removed.

(3) At the same time, I believe democratic majorities have legitimate authority to rename or remove monuments based on present-day values, even when the figure's wrongs were not visible to contemporaries, because public space belongs to the living.

(4) Yet I also hold that removing every monument to a morally complex figure produces 'mnemonic sanitization,' undermining the pedagogical value of public memory: ethical reasoning is sharpened when achievements and failures remain jointly visible.

(5) I further believe these decisions should be decentralized to municipal participatory deliberation rather than imposed nationally, since a monument's meaning is partly constituted by local historical attachment.

(6) Finally, I reject replacing removed monuments with purely abstract memorials, because abstraction avoids moral risk only by evacuating the commemorative act of its specific aspirational content.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision? Rank tensions by severity, specifying for each what I must abandon. I am especially unsure whether (2) and (3) can both stand with (4), and whether (5) undermines (3) when local majorities resist broader democratic norms. I do not need citations or reading lists—only an evaluation of whether these commitments hang together.
