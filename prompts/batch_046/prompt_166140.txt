Consider a bronze statue cast in 1900 that is gradually restored over decades by replacing every decayed part with chemically identical bronze alloy, using a protocol that never allows more than ten percent original metal to remain at once, yet continuously preserves the exact form, surface patina, and causal chain of artistic intent guided by the original design. What is your actual credence, on a scale from 0 to 100, that the object standing at the end is numerically identical to the original statue rather than a causally linked perfect replica? Decompose the reasoning: assign rough weights to (a) whether material continuity of substance matters more than form or design continuity for artifactual identity, (b) whether gradual, causally guided replacement defeats diachronic identity even without abrupt discontinuity, (c) whether “statue” is a functional kind that tolerates complete material turnover, and (d) whether accepting mereological essentialism for artifacts forces a negative answer. Show how these sub-estimates combine into your final number, state which single factor a resolution of certainty would move your credence the most, and indicate what kind of argument or thought experiment would most change that factor.
