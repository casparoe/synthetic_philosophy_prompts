I am developing a normative account of valid consent for my dissertation. I hold that consent is valid only if two conditions are met beyond the usual absence of coercion and presence of competence: first, what I call architectural transparency—the consenter must understand not only the propositional content of the act (what will happen, risks, benefits) but also the structural power asymmetry that frames the transaction, such as that the institution is designed to extract value from this class of agreements or that the other party controls the interpretive framework. Second, competence requires not just rational capacity but architectural literacy: the ability to represent these framing conditions rather than merely process information about the act itself. On this view, deception about the act's nature invalidates consent conventionally, but obscurity or misrepresentation about the architectural framing invalidates it equally, even when all propositional disclosures are accurate. Coercion is narrowly defined as direct threat to basic welfare interests, so economic pressure or social expectation do not vitiate consent unless they reach that threshold.

What would count against this position? I want the defeaters ranked by decisiveness: which observations about actual consent practices, which counterexamples—perhaps involving agents who lack architectural literacy yet seem to consent validly, or agents with full architectural knowledge who nonetheless seem coerced by non-threat structures—and which theoretical results in other domains, such as autonomy or contractual fairness, it stands or falls with. Which of these would a defender of architectural transparency concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss as missing the point? Is there a risk that the view can be qualified—by redefining architectural framing or expanding coercion—into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that insulation a virtue or a vice for a normative theory of consent? I am especially interested in whether the distinction between propositional and architectural disclosure can be maintained without collapsing into a general epistemic requirement that makes all imperfect information vitiate consent.
