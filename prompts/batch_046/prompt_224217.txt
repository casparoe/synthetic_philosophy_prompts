I'm helping design a module on Marx for a philosophy course that emphasizes conceptual distinctions over historical narrative, and I've noticed that students tend to treat exploitation and alienation as two names for the same misery. To help separate them, I'd like you to write a joke—dry, seminar-style rather than stand-up—that turns on a deliberately engineered divergence between the two. Construct a scenario involving a gig-economy warehouse worker whose labor is governed by an algorithm that compensates every minute at its precise exchange value, so that the rate of surplus value approaches zero and an accountant could report that, structurally, no exploitation is occurring. At the same time, arrange the work so that the worker experiences all four dimensions of alienation—estrangement from the product, the production process, species-being, and fellow workers—to an extreme degree.

The punchline should arise from the collision between these two Marxist diagnostics pulling in opposite directions: one metric says the worker is being treated with perfect economic fairness, while the other says the worker's human condition is maximally degraded. The joke should be intelligible to anyone who knows the basic framework—surplus value extraction versus the fourfold estrangement described in Marx's early writing—but should not depend on obscure secondary literature, precise textual citations, or direct quotations. Avoid bibliographic references altogether; the goal is conceptual clarity through absurd juxtaposition, not scholarly attribution.
