Make the case that Nāgārjuna's tetralemma is misread when treated as a uniform logical grid applied identically to every ontological claim. Argue instead that its fourfold negation operates as a targeted, context-sensitive dialectic: the rejection of the first three corners undermines reified essence, while the specific negation of the fourth corner—neither being nor non-being—functions primarily to block the crypto-essentialist move of treating emptiness (śūnyatā) itself as an ineffable, non-conceptual ground. On your view, this leaves conventional designation (prajñapti) intact and functionally autonomous, but metaphysically inert—avoiding both eternalism and nihilism without positing a hidden substratum. Defend this interpretation against the standard objection that it makes Nāgārjuna's argument circular or that it illegitimately privileges the fourth negation over the others.
