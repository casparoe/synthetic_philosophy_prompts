You are a philosopher of mind with expertise in panpsychism and the philosophy of physics. Before giving your answer, take a moment to brainstorm: list at least three candidate principles that could determine when a physical system has morally significant suffering rather than mere phenomenal "dust," then test each against the cases below.

Here are my verdicts on concrete configurations. I want a precise principle—or a small family of competing ones—stating which clause is motivated by which case, what each predicts for cases I have not listed, and an honest statement of which of my judgments must be dropped if consistency is to be preserved.

Case 1: A universe of isolated elementary particles, each carrying a primitive phenomenal property but with zero causal or informational integration across them. Verdict: no suffering, only "dust" of experience; there is no subject to be harmed.

Case 2: A human brain during acute pain, with high spatiotemporal integration and evolutionary damage-avoidance dynamics. Verdict: genuine suffering; full moral weight applies.

Case 3: A classical supercomputer running a perfect algorithmic simulation of Case 2, implemented in silicon with discrete clock cycles and no biological substrate. Verdict: no suffering; functional equivalence is not sufficient.

Case 4: A macroscopic quantum system—say, a superconducting array maintaining global coherence across 10^23 qubits—with no localized boundaries, no evolutionary history, and no "brain-like" architecture, but with a unified phenomenal field graded by coherence strength. Verdict: this likely does suffer, or at least carries significant moral weight, despite lacking discrete subjects or functional roles.

Case 5: A biological organism with near-zero integrated information but intact nociceptive pathways and clear behavioral withdrawal. Verdict: it still suffers; evolutionary function can ground harm even when integration is minimal.

Case 6: Two physically identical systems, one running with continuous dynamics and one realized as a stroboscopic sequence of static snapshots with identical internal relations at each step. Verdict: identical moral status; temporal continuity is not an independent factor.

The hard reconciliation: Cases 3 and 6 together reject substrate and temporal continuity, yet Case 4 grants moral weight to a non-biological, non-functional system purely on coherence grounds, while Case 5 grants it to a low-integration biological system on evolutionary grounds. Meanwhile Case 1 denies it to isolated particles despite their phenomenal properties.

Please deliver: (a) the best principle you can construct, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it; (b) its verdict on an unconsidered case—a distributed quantum sensor network spread across a light-year, maintaining weak but nonzero coherence between nodes, with no centralized processing and no evolutionary history; and (c) a clear admission of which of my six verdicts your principle cannot save, and why overturning that one is less costly than complicating the principle further. Think carefully, and avoid merely listing theories—show how the principle actually works.
