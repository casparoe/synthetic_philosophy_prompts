Write a dialogue between Mara, an endurantist, and Leo, a perdurantist, arguing about Judge K. She served ten years, then over three years about 60% of her brain was gradually replaced with synthetic analogues, then she died for 90 seconds, then was revived with 40% synthetic tissue and kept serving six more months before dying permanently. The core issue is whether the revived person is the same one who made an earlier controversial ruling, whether she should finish her term, and whether she bears responsibility for it.

Make them actually respond to each other's points — no strawmen. Mara should defend that there's literally one enduring thing that can survive part-replacement and even brief non-existence, kinda like a computer turned off and back on, and she needs to address Leo's push that if you allow gaps then you've basically admitted temporal stages are doing all the work. Leo should explain why moral and legal responsibility sticks to the earlier temporal parts rather than the revived stage, but he also has to deal with Mara's objection that this collapses contract law and judicial continuity into chaos. He should also explain how he handles the 90-second gap without secretly smuggling in an enduring substrate.

Keep it casual — a typo or two is fine, maybe write "recieve" or "occurance" by accident. Don't let either side sound dumb. At the end, say what specific argument or discovered fact would change your mind about which view of persistence is right, and flag exactly where your reasoning is shakiest — for instance, if you're quietly assuming psychological continuity is more basic than bodily continuity, or if you're leaning on legal conventions that don't actually track metaphysical identity.
