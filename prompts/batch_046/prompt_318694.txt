I've been convinced that the repugnant conclusion is basically unavoidable if you're consistent about totalism, but I also think our horror at it comes from mixing up cosmological populations with the kind of personal narratives we actually care about — like, we imagine being one of the zillions, not just that there are zillions. So my position is: totalism is right, our resistance is a kind of scope-neglect bias dressed up as moral intuition, and the real mistake is treating population ethics as continuous with personal ethics. What would count against this? I want the arguments or observations that should actually move me — not just 'that feels wrong' — including whether there's a formal result showing totalism collapses into something worse, or empirical work on moral cogniton that undermines the bias story, and whether defenders of person-affecting views have a reply that doesn't just beg the question. Also, has this kind of 'it's a bias' defense been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so is that fatal?
