I've been trying to wrap my head around the procreation asymmetry for a population ethics course I'm taking, and a friend in my study group came up with this argument that seems to prove way more than it should. Here are the steps:

(1) The asymmetry holds: there's a moral reason not to bring into existence someone who will suffer, but no corresponding reason to bring into existence someone who will be happy.

(2) If an action is wrong in one possible world, and the only relevant difference is whether the affected individual actually exists in our world, then the wrongness is anchored to the action-type rather than to any token victim—so reasons don't vanish just because the victim is counterfactual.

(3) For any actual happy person S, there's a nearby possible world where S is conceived and lives a suffering life.

(4) In that world, the asymmetry gives us a clear reason not to conceive S.

(5) By (2), that reason transfers across: in the actual world we still have a reason not to have conceived S.

(6) So for every actual happy person who could have suffered, there's an active reason against their conception.

(7) But if there's a reason against conceiving someone and we went ahead anyway, then conceiving them was wrong, and their existence is a kind of moral loss we should register.

(8) Therefore, if the asymmetry is true, then for nearly everyone who's actually happy, their parents did something wrong in conceiving them, and we ought to regret that they exist.

That can't be right, right? I really want to keep (1) and (6) if I can, but I can't see exactly where the slide happens. Is it (2) with that modal anchoring thing? Or is (3) smuggling in a counterfactual identiy that isn't legit? Or maybe (5) is doing a scope shift I'm missing? Tell me which premise to drop, why it seemed okay at first, and what giving it up costs for the usual positions—like person-affecting accounts vs totalist ones, or anyone who tries to formalize the asymmetry with world-indexed deontic reasons. I feel like there's an equivocation between 'reason not to create suffering' applying to actions vs applying across worlds, but I'm not sure where it bites.
