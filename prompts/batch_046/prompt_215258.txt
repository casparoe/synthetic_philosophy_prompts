I'm trying to articulate a position on political organization that draws on Simone Weil's critique of parties, and I'd like a consistency check before I present it. Here is my package of commitments:

(a) Political parties are not primarily representations of social interests but affective technologies—structural mechanisms for manufacturing collective emotional intensity around abstract symbols. This feature is constitutive, not a corruption that reform could fix.

(b) Therefore, permanent parties should be abolished. Legitimate collective action should take the form of temporary, mandate-bound associations that automatically dissolve once their explicit purpose is fulfilled.

(c) The central cognitive failure of parties is not error but the suppression of internal disagreement. The best organizational form is therefore one that institutionalizes dissent and treats every policy claim as provisionally revisable.

(d) Yet effective action oriented toward justice requires an unconditional, non-instrumental commitment—what might be described as a supernatural orientation—that transcends cost-benefit reasoning and cannot be revised without destroying its motivational force.

(e) Finally, I hold that the value of such an association should be measured not by its policy achievements but by its capacity to maintain cognitive friction: a structural resistance to the smooth assimilation of contradictory information, since without such friction epistemic openness collapses into passive consumption.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to conflict? For each real tension, rank them by severity and identify the least costly revision—whether that means abandoning one commitment, weakening it, or adding a qualification. In particular, I want to know whether (c) and (d) can be stabilized by an ends-means distinction, whether (b) undermines the permanence required by (d), and whether (e) is compatible with (c) or secretly pulls in the opposite direction. Do not simply list every possible conflict as equally bad: discriminate between superficial friction and deep structural incompatibility, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems at once.
