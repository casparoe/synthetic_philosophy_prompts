After a long argument with a friend about whether Bradley's regress is a genuine metaphysical obstacle or merely a linguistic puzzle about how we form judgments, I want you to build a designed thought experiment that forces the issue in a practical setting rather than in abstract ontology. Construct a realistic, one-paragraph scenario—no science fiction, no demons, no perfect predictors, and no generic bureaucratic failure—set in an ordinary institutional context such as a museum conservation board or a legal archive that must decide whether a heavily restored composite artifact is identical to the original entry registered decades earlier. The case must turn specifically on comparative judgments of resemblance between complex composite objects (not on simple predication like color attribution), since Bradley treated comparison and asymmetrical relating as posing a distinct form of the regress. Engineer it so that a metaphysical view treating relations—such as 'resembles in structure to' or 'is a constituent part of'—as real, external entities that themselves require further relations to connect them back to their relata produces an operational paralysis: the board can never finalize its judgment because every proposed relating step demands another meta-relating step. By contrast, a view that treats the board's final judgment as an irreducible propositional unity, or that treats relations as internal modifications of the terms rather than independent connectors, should allow the board to reach a stable verdict without that loop. The paralysis must fall out naturally from the ontological commitment, not from an obviously suboptimal rule adopted by the board. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that each specification is met: state the precise judgment at stake, the method by which terms are combined, the relevant nearby worlds in which one further relating step succeeds or fails, and why each metaphysical position delivers its verdict. Then identify the exact structural feature—perhaps the need to relate an asymmetrical comparative relation back to both composite relata simultaneously—that makes the regress vicious here rather than benign; say what happens if that feature is removed and whether the case then collapses into an ordinary coordination problem. Finally, explain how a defender of the relational-realist position that comes out badly would attack the case, specifically addressing whether they can deny that practical paralysis tracks ontological dependence or whether they can invoke a non-relational tie or an internal-relation reply. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; frame everything so that a model without web access can follow it, but keep the reference to Bradley's argument in Appearance and Reality (1893) precise enough that the variant of the regress being tested is unmistakable.
