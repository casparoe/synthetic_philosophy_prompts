I am constructing a taxonomy of historiographical positions on whether long-term geographical and climatic structures—what Braudel called the longue durée—carry independent explanatory weight in historical change, or whether contingent events and individual agency do the real work. Please design a decision tree (or flowchart-style taxonomy) whose branches are labeled by concrete diagnostic commitments rather than by school names, so that a reader can trace their own answers without knowing the labels in advance.

1. The collapse of the Western Roman Empire: is the decisive factor long-term ecological and climatic stress (cooling, agricultural contraction, and epidemiological pressure), the contingent strategic and leadership choices of specific barbarian commanders and Roman generals at particular moments, or a structural overextension of fiscal and military systems that made collapse inevitable regardless of who acted?
2. The British Industrial Revolution: is it best explained by the prior geographical availability of coal and centuries of capital accumulation, by a narrow cluster of contingent institutional shifts and individual inventions, or by a feedback loop in which geographical conditions shaped institutions that then transformed the use of those conditions?
3. The regional mortality patterns of the Black Death: do differences in death rates follow primarily from long-term ecological and trade-route structures, from contingent local political decisions about quarantine and movement, or from an interaction in which structures created systemic vulnerability but local events determined severity?

From the combinations of answers, derive at least six terminal positions. The tree must include:
- a hard structural-determinist leaf (events are epiphenomenal noise);
- a structural-constraint-with-event-trigger leaf (slow structures set conditions, specific events determine form and timing);
- an event-first or agency-first leaf (geography and climate are background conditions with no independent causal force);
- a scale-dependent pluralist leaf (the correct explanation depends on whether the question is framed at century-scale or decade-scale);
- and at least one rarely articulated but coherent leaf: a co-evolutionary view that treats climate and geography not as static external constraints but as slowly changing variables that co-develop with social institutions, so that the separation between “structure” and “event” is an analytical artifact rather than an empirical finding.

For each leaf, provide the standard name if one exists (e.g., Braudelian structuralism, event history, methodological pluralism) or mark it “unnamed.” Then identify any logical combinations of answers that the tree leaves unoccupied—such as pure determinism paired with decisive weight given to individual tactical choices—and briefly diagnose each gap: is it incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored?
