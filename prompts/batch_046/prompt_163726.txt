Analyze the Shepard-Metzler mental rotation experiments from the 70s through the lens of Fodor's language of thought, but dont just replay the usual analog-vs-propositional script. I want you to work out a specific discrete implementation: a symbol system that stores a structured description of the block figure and applies a sequence of small-angle rotation rules, each taking constant processing time, so the linear RT slope comes from counting steps rather than traversing a continuous mental space. Map that onto the actual study—what the 3D block stimuli looked like, what the same-vs-mirror-image task required, why the linear slope with rotation angle is the headline result, and where 'tacit' geometric knowledge would have to sit in the symbol system. Then be honest about where LOT is silent or stretched: can it explain why people report a continuous 'moving' image, does the constant-time-rule assumption survive the fact that larger angles feel harder not just longer, and what does the whole episode actually tell us about whether mental representations need analog format? Separate whats settled about the data from whats genuinely contested in the interpretation, and dont present one camp as the obvious consensus.
