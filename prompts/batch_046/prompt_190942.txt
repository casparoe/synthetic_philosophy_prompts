I'm a novelist, and I've come to hold a stark view: the self isn't just like a character in fiction, it literally is Dennett's center of narrative gravity, but interpreted as an autofictional construct with zero ontological weight outside its own plot coherence. There's no hidden author behind the first-person pronoun—just grammatical habits, memory revision, and what I'd call "retroactive continuity" in autobiography: we retcon our past to keep the story coherent, and the "I" is just the unreliable narrator that emerges. When someone confabulates wildly, or a split-brain patient reports two competing centers of agency, I don't see that as revealing a hidden true self; I see it as the manuscript falling apart, or two drafts being written at once.
