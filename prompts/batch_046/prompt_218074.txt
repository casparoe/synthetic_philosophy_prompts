Split-brain cases have been around since Sperry and Gazzaniga and the basic results are text-book — left hand grabs the object, right hemisphere can't talk, left hemisphere confabulates. Yet the argument about whether there's genuinely one consciousness or two in there hasn't budged in decades. My hunch is that it's not really about consciousness but about what counts as a 'binding mechanism': one side treats the corpus callosum like a synchronization protocol that creates a single workspace, the other treats it as just a high-bandwidth data bus, and they never agree on whether cutting it splits a stream or just isolates modules. So is this stall verbal — we mean different things by unity — or substantive about neural architecture, or are they answering different questions, one about phenomenal experience and one about information integration? Give me the diagnosis using what each side actually says about autonomy and reportability, and tell me honestly what would count as progress here. Would it take a split-brain patient whose right hemisphere suddenly writes sentences, a formal model of interhemispheric inhibtion, or should we just drop the 'how many minds' framing entirely?
