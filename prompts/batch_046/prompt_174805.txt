I sit on the review board for our institutes AI safety protocols, and we're deadlocked on a pilot proposal from the alignment team. They want to run structured red-team debates where one side is allowed to present reasoning through indirect argumentation — embedding claims inside nested analogies, ambiguous framing, and hypothetical chains — to test whether the judge model can still extract and evaluate the core position. Their reasoning is that a misaligned system in the wild won't argue in bullet points; it'll argue obliquely, so our debate framework should be stress-tested against that. Half the board says this defeats the purpose of debate: transparency is what makes adversarial testing a safety tool, and if the argument is deliberately hidden, you're just doing hermeneutics, not oversight. We also don't have staff to run a full interpretive audit on every round, so whatever we recommend has to be feasable with current resources. I need a memo for tueday's vote: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a clear recommendation and reasoning the alignment team can actually respond to. Please take the transparency objection seriously — don't just restate it and move on — and include an honest account of the strongest point on the losing side. If you recommend conditions, specify a concrete pilot scope and timeframe.
