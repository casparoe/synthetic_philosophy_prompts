I've been trying to sort out Dennett's four kinds of minds for a side project, and my intuitions are kind of a mess. Here are my gut verdicts on concrete cases, and I'd like to know if there's one principle behind them or if I have to drop some.

A population of bacteria that evolves heat resistance across generations through random mutation and selection: Darwinian, no question.

A rat in a Skinner box that learns to pull a lever to avoid shock: Skinnerian, clearly.

A chess program that plays out thousands of board states inside itself before making one real-world move: Popperian, since the bad moves die in simulation, not in reality.

Now the hard part. I think a human child learning language is Gregorian, not Popperian — they're inheriting a cultural toolkit, not running private mental experiments. But then I look at a deep RL agent that trains on millions of self-play games and updates its weights. It also runs internal simulations and lets bad strategies die without real-world cost, so part of me wants to call it Popperian. But its learning is really just gradient descent on rewards, which feels Skinnerian or even Darwinian at the paramater level. So which box?

Even weirder is this creature I made up: a colony of insects where no individual can learn, but they leave chemical traces that others read and modify. The colony selects behaviors over time, but no single insect does. Is that Darwinian at the colony level, Skinnerian at the individual level, or something that breaks the taxonomy?

And here's the pair that really bugs me. I think an octopus figuring out how to open a jar by trial and error is Skinnerian, pure and simple. But I think a human scientist running mental simulations of an experiment before doing it is Popperian, even though they also get rewarded or punished for actual results. Both learn from consequences, yet I put them in different boxes. What's the principle — internal representation of counterfactuals? Or where the selection pressure applies?

One more I keep going back and forth on: a language model trained with RLHF. The selection happened during training on its outputs via human feedback. Now it generates text with no further selection happening. Is it Gregorian because it outputs language? Darwinian because its weights were shaped by selection? Popperian because during training it predicted and updated?

Can someone lay out the principle, or maybe a small family of them, that captures as many of these as possible? I want each clause tied to the case that motivates it, plus what it predicts for cases I haven't listed — say, a bird that learns a song by copying adults but also improvises variations that get rewarded by mates. And please be honest: which of my verdicts have to go, especially that octopus/scientist split? If there's no coherent account that saves them all, say so and tell me which one to overturn.
