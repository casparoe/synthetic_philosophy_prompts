You are preparing a speaker for the question period following a seminar presentation delivered to a joint audience of historians of early modern science and analytic philosophers of religion. The speaker defends the thesis that Galileo's hermeneutic principle in his letter to the Grand Duchess Christina — that Scripture teaches how one goes to heaven, not how the heavens go — should be interpreted not as a straightforward division of labour between science and religion, but as a proto-Kantian claim that cosmological passages in Scripture function as regulative fictions accommodating necessary limits on human cognition of divine intentions, rather than as descriptions of natural reality. The speaker further argues that this reading exposes a hidden tension: Galileo simultaneously treats the mathematical structure of nature, read through observation and geometry, as directly intelligible, which appears to depend on the very epistemic confidence his scriptural hermeneutic denies.

The audience comprises three groups. First, historians of the Scientific Revolution who favour a standard accommodationist reading and are hostile to anachronistic philosophical framing, particularly one that imports later conceptual distinctions into a seventeenth-century Catholic polemical context. Second, philosophers of religion who worry that interpreting Galileo in this way collapses his view into a form of non-cognitivism about biblical cosmology, undermining the theological authority he sought to preserve. Third, one prominent critic of Whiggish historiography who will press on whether this interpretation projects modern epistemological categories onto Galileo's text without direct warrant.

Draft a Q&A preparation sheet for the speaker. Order the objections by how likely and damaging they are, phrased exactly as members of this audience would phrase them. For each objection, specify: what the speaker should concede immediately; the core reply that must land for the thesis to survive; and an honest note on where that reply is weakest. Include the objection the speaker is most hoping nobody asks — specifically, the charge that if Galileo's hermeneutic is genuinely proto-Kantian in this sense, then his confidence in reading the mathematical structure of nature directly becomes circular, since that confidence presupposes the same cognitive transparency his accommodation principle denies for scriptural language.

Write the response in British English. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Ensure the sheet is practical for a live seminar defence, not an abstract essay.
