Restate the Bayesian problem of old evidence — particularly whether evidence already held in an archive can rationally confirm a hypothesis formulated after its acquisition, and the distinction between logical learning (restructuring credences over a fixed body of knowledge) and empirical updating (adding new observations) — using only the vocabulary and reasoning patterns of provenance research. Translate faithfully: do not disguise Bayesian priors as institutional confidence or reduce archival judgment to numerical updating. Then report the residue. Which philosophical claims have no analog in how archivists actually weigh a pre-existing conservation report against a newly proposed authorship theory? Where the translation breaks down, is the gap a deficiency in archival practice — for example, the absence of explicit prior probabilities — or does it show that parts of the Bayesian framing are formal apparatus without substantive counterpart in provenance work?
