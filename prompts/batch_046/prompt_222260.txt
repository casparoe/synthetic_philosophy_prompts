For my distributive justice seminar I want to move past textbook versions of the leveling-down debate and see where the real fracture lies. Write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors, Mira and Jordan, structured as exactly five exchanges with no framing text outside the dialogue itself; in each exchange both speak once, each in no more than two sentences. Mira should defend a threshold-sensitive prioritarianism: she applies steeply diminishing welfare weights only below a decent minimum, and holds that once everyone is above that threshold equality carries no further prioritarian value, so leveling the well-off downward is never justified. Jordan should press a relational-status version of the objection: equality matters non-instrumentally because unequal social status expresses disrespect regardless of welfare effects, which means Mira’s refusal to level down treats comparative welfare as the sole currency of justice and quietly smuggles in an egalitarian commitment about the threshold itself. In the second exchange Mira should distinguish weighting welfare from honoring status; in the third Jordan should challenge whether status can be separated from comparative welfare without circularity; in the fourth Mira should concede that plural values exist but deny they justify harming anyone to achieve equality; and in the fifth they should converge on whether their disagreement is fundamentally about value pluralism or about how welfare ought to be measured. Please keep both sides rigorous, have each reply directly to the prior point rather than talking past it, and avoid strawmanning either position.
