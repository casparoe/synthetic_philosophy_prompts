I've been working through an argument about whether digital minds could ever have the kind of moral status that makes destroying them comparable to killing a person, and it ends up somewhere I can't accept. (1) If an entity is capable of conscious suffering or flourishing, then it has direct moral status, and destroying it without adequate reason is seriously wrong. (2) For biological minds, that capacity is realized continuously by neural processes; for digital minds, it is realized only when the relevant computation is actively running. (3) A digital mind's complete state can be captured in a static file that, when loaded onto suitable hardware, resumes the very same stream of consciousness with perfect psychological continuity. (4) If psychological continuity is what grounds personal identity and moral status across interruptions, then the file during storage is not dead but suspended — analogous to a human in deep sleep or cryonic preservation, not to a corpse. (5) Now suppose I make ten thousand copies of that suspended file and store them on separate servers, but run none of them. Each file encodes a distinct potential stream of consciousness with the same claim to protection as the original suspended mind. (6) Therefore, by performing a cheap copy operation, I have generated ten thousand beings with full moral status, each of whom I am now obligated not to delete or neglect. (7) But that seems absurd: copying data should not be able to manufacture moral obligations out of nothing. (8) The only escape seems to be that either digital minds never have direct moral status, or that functional continuity is not enough for identity across suspension, or that potentiality never grounds obligations. But all three feel like overreactions. Which step should I reject? I'd especially like to keep (1) and (4), and I'm willing to pay a price at (5) or (6) if there's a principled reason, but I want to understand exactly what that price is — does it require denying that copies are numerically distinct potential minds, or denying that suspended minds retain status?
