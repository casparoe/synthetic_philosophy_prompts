I am preparing a brief for a philosophy reading group on Schopenhauer's aesthetics and want a consistency check on the interpretation I intend to defend. My current position comprises the following commitments:

(a) Schopenhauer is correct that music is metaphysically unique: unlike painting or poetry, it does not represent Platonic Ideas but is instead a direct copy or objectification of the Will itself.

(b) Consequently, music provides an ontological disclosure that bypasses conceptual representation, revealing the inner nature of reality in a manner unavailable to other arts.

(c) I also maintain that Schopenhauer's treatment of melody and harmonic progression is best illuminated by an embodied-cognition framework: listeners grasp musical tension and resolution through proprioceptive analogues of bodily striving, not through abstract intellectual analysis.

(d) I reject both purely formalist and purely emotional-representational interpretations; I take Schopenhauer to be advancing a stronger claim about the Will's direct presence in sound.

(e) Yet, on Schopenhauer's own metaphysics, the body is merely one objectification of the Will among innumerable others, possessing no special epistemic or metaphysical privilege.

(f) Furthermore, I think that if music genuinely copies the Will, and the Will is singular, blind, and devoid of representational differentiation, then music ought not to express specific emotional characters—melancholy, triumph, longing—since these presuppose precisely the representational structure that the Will lacks. Schopenhauer plainly allows such differentiation, which creates a pressure on the direct-copy thesis.

(g) At the same time, I hold that understanding a musical work requires some conceptual grasp of harmonic rules and formal structure, even if the metaphysical disclosure it affords is non-conceptual.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be so, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Rank the conflicts by severity rather than listing them as equally damaging. In particular, address whether (c) can be squared with (e) without abandoning the embodied-cognition reading, and whether (f) forces a revision of (a) or (b) or can be accommodated within the direct-copy framework. Also clarify whether (g) conflicts with (b) or whether the tension is merely apparent. Please write your answer in British English.
