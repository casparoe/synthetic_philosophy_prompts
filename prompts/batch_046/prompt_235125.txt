I've been working through my views for a graduate metaethics seminar, and after a long discussion with a friend in moral psychology I want a consistency check before I present. Here is my current package on moral worth and acting for the right reasons. I hold that (a) full moral worth requires acting from a recognition of the moral fact as one's motivating reason, not merely acting in accordance with morality; (b) this recognition can be tacit rather than philosophically explicit—someone who unreflectively grasps that cruelty is wrong can satisfy it; (c) evolutionary debunking arguments undermine our first-order moral intuitions as unreliable guides to mind-independent moral facts, but they do not undermine our second-order capacity to recognize and endorse moral reasons once those reasons are properly articulated through philosophical reflection; (d) consequently, an agent who acts kindly from unreflective sympathy lacks full moral worth, while the same agent, after engaging in reflective equilibrium, acquires full moral worth without any change in outward behavior; (e) moral worth is scalar, not binary—its degree tracks the counterfactual robustness of the agent's reason-recognition, so that someone whose moral motivation survives greater interference has more worth; and (f) reflective equilibrium is the correct method for achieving this robust recognition, precisely because it can correct the debunked first-order intuitions.

Do these commitments hang together? Specifically, is there a hidden tension between (b) and (c)—if debunking targets our intuitive capacities, does it not also threaten the tacit grasp I claim is sufficient for at least partial worth? Is there another tension between (d) and (e)—if moral worth scales with robustness and reflective equilibrium produces robustness, then pre-reflective agents are not merely less excellent but genuinely deficient in a way that seems to conflict with (b)'s allowance that they already possess some worth? And is the distinction in (c) between first-order and second-order reliability an ad hoc rescue rather than a principled division? Rank the tensions by severity, identify which pair is only apparently in conflict, and state the least costly revision that preserves both the scalar view and the debunking claim intact.
