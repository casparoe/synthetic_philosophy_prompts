I'm stabilizing my framework for a dissertation chapter on Foucault, discipline, and the politics of knowledge, and I need a consistency audit before I proceed. Here are my current commitments: (1) power is fundamentally productive and relational rather than possessed or primarily repressive; (2) knowledge and power are strictly co-constitutive, so that every regime of truth is inseparable from a regime of power; (3) genealogical critique exposes the contingent historical emergence of present norms, and this exposure genuinely weakens their grip, opening space for transformation; (4) there is no outside of power—resistance and critique remain fully immanent within power relations; (5) the genealogist can recover and amplify subjugated knowledges to serve as critical counterweights to dominant discourses; and (6) disciplinary power operates more efficiently than sovereign violence precisely because it produces self-surveilling subjects who internalize norms without direct coercion. I want the tensions ordered by severity, with the cheapest revision specified for each. In particular, do (2) and (4) together undermine the normative authority that (3) and (5) seem to require? And is there only an apparent conflict between (6) and (4)—for instance, does the internal division of the self-surveilling subject actually presuppose a standpoint exterior to power, or does it dissolve once we recognize that division itself as an effect of disciplinary production? I am not committed to preserving any one thesis; identify which single revision would restore coherence at the lowest cost.
