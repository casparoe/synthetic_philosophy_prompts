I'm coordinating a small research seminar—one postdoc, two graduate students, about eighteen months—on the intersection of biography and technical philosophy, and we've settled on Frege's private political diary as our focal case. The diary's posthumous discovery and its starkly illiberal, anti-Semitic contents have generated a scattered literature: some philosophers treat it as biographical noise separable from the logicist project, others argue that its patterns of exclusion resonate structurally with Frege's technical commitments—say, in his account of objectivity, his boundary between sense and idea, or his universalist pretensions for logical law. Rather than survey everything, I need a defended agenda: which open questions about this intersection actually deserve our limited time, and in what order should we attack them?

Specifically, I want you to distinguish between what I will call "doctrinal contamination" questions—whether specific anti-Semitic or nationalist claims appear in, or directly motivate, particular theses in Grundgesetze or in his philosophy of language—and "structural resonance" questions—whether the diary reveals a deeper pattern of rational-agent exclusion that mirrors how Frege draws the line between objective thought and subjective association in his technical work. Given that our group has philosophical training but no archival access to unpublished manuscripts, which of these two problem-types should come first? Rank them alongside at least two other live lines of work—perhaps whether the diary should alter standard pedagogical framing of Frege's universality, or whether it bears on recent debates about the "value-ladenness" of formal systems. For each, defend its place using explicit criteria: upstream dependency (does settling this unlock answers to the others?), tractability without special archival resources, and the magnitude of revision it would force on mainstream philosophy of logic or mathematics if the answer turned out a particular way. I am especially interested in the trade-off between tractability and importance: identify the one question that is most important but least tractable for us, and say whether it should still occupy any portion of our eighteen months or be actively set aside. Then tell me what evidence—philosophical, textual, or historical—would cause you to reorder this agenda within six months. I want to know not just what the open questions are, but which ones are worth the years for a team of our size and constraints, and which are overrated precisely because resolving them would not change how anyone actually works with Frege's philosophy.
