After spending thirty years in experimental physics studying decoherence in open quantum systems, and only recently turning to philosophy of mind in retirement, I find myself drawn to an analogy that my former colleagues might appreciate: just as phlogiston was eventually eliminated rather than reduced because it failed to track any real structural feature of combustion, perhaps our everyday talk of beliefs and desires will prove similarly dispensable once neuroscience offers a complete predictive framework. Write an essay on whether eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires is genuinely parallel to historical scientific eliminations like that of phlogiston, or whether propositional attitudes possess an indispensable explanatory and predictive function that prevents such elimination. Consider especially the objection that removing beliefs and desires would undermine our capacity to explain rational agency and intentional action, and weigh whether this objection succeeds or can be answered by a sufficiently robust neuroscientific replacement. Reach and defend a definite conclusion—do not end on an agnostic or non-committal note.
