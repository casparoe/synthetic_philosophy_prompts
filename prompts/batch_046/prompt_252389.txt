Construct a thought experiment adapting Parfit's hitchhiker structure: a freelance specialist must be driven to a remote work site by a local contractor; the contractor offers the ride only if the specialist adopts one of two escrow commitments beforehand—an observable contract (the driver and future industry observers can verify that payment is mechanically enforced) or a hidden contract (payment is enforced but neither party nor observers can distinguish enforced payment from voluntary compliance). Once arrived, if no escrow was adopted, the specialist can depart unpaid; however, the hidden contract preserves the appearance of voluntary cooperation in a reputation-based labor market, improving future contracts, while the observable contract signals dependence on coercion and damages reputation. Engineer the case so that a defender of rational pre-commitment (who accepts ex-post irrational payment as rational ex ante) prefers the observable escrow, while a defender of evidential or signaling-sensitive rationality prefers the hidden escrow or abstention, and demonstrate why each view delivers that verdict. Constraints: present the scenario in one paragraph; use only realistic, non-exotic mechanisms (contractual escrow, professional reputation networks); avoid astronomical or physically exotic payoffs; and keep the setting to a workplace or industry context. After the scenario, verify that the specifications are met: state the verdict and reasoning for each theoretical position; identify the precise structural feature that drives the divergence (the observability of the commitment mechanism to third-party observers and its effect on evidential signaling); explain how removing that feature collapses the case back into the standard hitchhiker problem; and show how the rational-irrationality defender would attack the hidden-escrow preference—specifically, whether they can deny that hidden mechanisms genuinely preserve cooperative signals without undermining their own endorsement of unobservable mental commitments.
