Consider five accounts of properties and relations—(A) transcendent Platonic realism; (B) immanent Aristotelian realism; (C) trope-theoretic particularism; (D) class nominalism anchored by a primitive resemblance relation; and (E) a hybrid modal-structuralist view that identifies universals with cross-world equivalence classes of instantiated properties but denies that these classes contain uninstantiated members in the actual world. Rank these five positions from 1 (strongest) to 5 (weakest) specifically on their ability to resolve the following tension: a determinable property (e.g., being colored) can be genuinely instantiated by an object even when no specific determinate (e.g., being red, being blue) is instantiated by that object in the actual world, yet if that same object is red in a nearby possible world, it must share a universal with actually-red objects in that world. For each rank, assign a score out of 10 and provide a single sentence of justification that points to exactly where the account succeeds or breaks down on this determinable-to-determinate, cross-world inheritance problem. Do not discuss the positions in general terms; keep the evaluation tied to this specific case.
