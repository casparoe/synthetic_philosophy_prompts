I serve on a municipal social-policy review board tasked with restructuring our city's housing subsidy program. The administration proposes replacing the current unconditional cash-transfer model with what it terms a "co-production framework": recipients must attend biweekly "capability workshops," submit quarterly self-assessments mapping personal goals onto a standardized "social contribution matrix," and maintain a caseworker-rated tier status; sustained placement in the lowest tier triggers a graduated, phased reduction in benefits rather than immediate disqualification. Proponents argue that unconditional support frames recipients as passive dependents, undermining the reciprocal obligations that sustain democratic citizenship and blurring the moral distinction between misfortune and disengagement. Critics contend that the framework institutes a new administrative category of the "undeserving poor"—not defined by fraud, refusal of available work, or voluntary unemployment, but by failure to perform a particular rehabilitative subjectivity—thereby embedding a desert calculus into the provision of basic shelter.

The board must deliver a binding recommendation—adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions—before the next fiscal quarter. Please draft the formal decision memo I will circulate: it should present a clear recommendation with a specific implementation timeline or sunset clause, engage the reciprocity argument on its own terms rather than dismissing it as mere ideology, examine whether the tiered assessment mechanism creates a morally distinct form of undeservingness, and include concrete safeguards or evaluation protocols appropriate to whatever course you recommend. The reasoning should be self-contained; do not invoke specific court rulings, named statutes, or academic references that the board could not evaluate without external research. Treat the strongest objection to your recommendation with intellectual honesty rather than straw-manning.
