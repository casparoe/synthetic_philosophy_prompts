If consciousness is best understood as a "user illusion"—a simplified, transparent interface that the brain constructs to represent its own states and the world, rather than a direct access to either—then there seems to be a hidden layer of mental reality that the interface conceals. After all, if the interface is transparent, we experience through it without noticing it as a representation; but this transparency appears to depend on there being some actual representational machinery working behind it. So if we could somehow make that machinery opaque—if the brain stopped generating the transparent self-model—wouldn't we expose the "real" conscious subject that was being hidden all along? Or does the user-illusion view actually claim that there is no subject behind the interface, and that disrupting the illusion doesn't reveal a deeper self but eliminates the very conditions for having one? I ask because the first interpretation seems to reintroduce exactly the kind of hidden homunculus the theory was designed to reject.
