An interdisciplinary research team that infers institutional structures and cosmological beliefs from material remains has published the following judgments across four excavation contexts. At Kharra, they inferred a graded redistribution hierarchy—not a flat communal store—from the fact that sealed storage vessels in administrative buildings scaled in volumetric capacity with precise geometric regularity relative to distance from a central archive. At Marin, they rejected an inference of social stratification despite the presence of forty nearly identical domestic structures, instead attributing the uniformity to a rotating labor system evidenced by seasonal tool caches and the absence of any elite residence or differential burial goods. At Selva, they inferred a shared cosmological framework governing death practices from the consistent orientation of burial pits toward the winter solstice sunrise, even though grave goods varied widely and no priestly iconography was present. At Torin, they interpreted unusually pure metallurgical slag—achieved without any local smelting infrastructure—together with abundant unprocessed local ore, as evidence that high-grade metal entered through restricted ritual exchange rather than through technical mastery, because the workshop showed no experimentation with reduction techniques.

The team now examines Velan, a highland plateau lacking permanent water or domestic habitation debris. The site contains one circular enclosure, roughly fifteen meters in diameter, with postholes arranged at precise 22.5-degree intervals but no remaining roof structure. Inside, a uniform layer of crushed white quartz is distributed in radial sectors; each sector holds a different quantity of unworked chert flakes, ranging from seven to twenty-three. Outside the perimeter, three linear alignments of unmodified river stones extend approximately two hundred meters toward the nearest mountain peak, with the middle alignment interrupted by a single carved stele bearing an abstract geometric pattern unattested elsewhere in the region. Thin alternating strata of wind-blown sediment and ash suggest periodic, seasonal use rather than continuous occupation. There are no burials, no food-processing remains, and no differential distribution of materials that would clearly mark status.

If the team wishes to be consistent with its own precedents, should it classify Velan as a site of centralized ritual authority, a seasonal cooperative gathering without hierarchy, or a restricted cosmological boundary? Which earlier judgment—Kharra, Marin, Selva, or Torin—is the closest analog, and why? Identify the two principles implicit in the team’s prior inferences that diverge most sharply on this case, stating what each principle predicts about Velan. If the precedents genuinely underdetermine the verdict, specify what further material discovery at Velan—describe the exact evidence—would allow the team to adjudicate between the competing interpretations, and state which principle that discovery would favor.
