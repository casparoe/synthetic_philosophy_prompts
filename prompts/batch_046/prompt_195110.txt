I've been helping out with this community archive where we're digitizing oral histories from migrants, and it's left me weirdly obssessed with whether understanding someone across a massive conceptual gap is even possible. I was reading about Davidson's radical interpetation in a course packet and tried to build an argument that charity actually destroys cross-cultural understanding, but it feels like it proves way too much. Here are the steps my friend and I mapped out over coffee:

(1) For Davidson, interpreting another speaker requires applying the principel of charity: we must attribute beliefs and meanings that make them largely rational and mostly true by our own lights.
(2) Charity isn't just a practical tip; it's a constitutive constraint—without it, there's no interpretation happening at all, just behavioral noise.
(3) Now suppose the gap is radical: the speaker holds a cosmology where causation runs backward in time, sincerely and coherently within their practice, but by our lights this is mostly false and deeply irrational.
(4) Applying charity forces us to reinterpret their utterances so they come out true and rational for us—say, translating their backward-causation talk as metaphorical anxiety about the future.
(5) So in radical cases, charity doesn't reveal their beliefs; it replaces them with beliefs we find acceptable.
(6) But then either we have interpreted them (charity satisfied) but completely misrepresented their actual mental states, or we refuse charity and fail to interpret them at all.
(7) Davidson wants to reject incommensurable schemes, but this argument seems to show that if schemes are genuinely different, charity makes interpretation impossible without distortion—so deep difference is either impossible (because charity homogenizes everything) or interpretation is impossible.
(8) Either way, radical interpretation collapses into either imperialism or silence, which can't be right.

Which step is the weak link? I especially want to hold onto (2) and (7) if I can—charity as constitutive feels central, and the dilemma about deep difference feels real—but maybe I'm sliding from "mostly true by our lights" to "completely replaced"? Anyway, if I'm just misunderstanding what charity actually demands, tell me straight; don't just patch it up politely.
