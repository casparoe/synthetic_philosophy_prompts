Write an essay on whether comparing historical atrocities—ranking events such as the Holocaust, the Rwandan genocide, and the Holodomor by moral gravity—is epistemically coherent or morally obscene. Examine the argument that any comparison requires a metric of suffering (scale, duration, intentionality, institutional design) that necessarily flattens irreducibly distinct moral horrors, and examine the counter-argument that refusing comparison isolates victims from collective historical understanding and prevents structural analysis of evil. In your analysis, introduce and defend a precise distinction between ordinal comparison (judging one atrocity worse than another without quantifying the gap) and cardinal comparison (assigning proportional measures of harm), and argue whether either form escapes the charge of obscenity. Reach and justify a definite conclusion: is there a legitimate mode of comparative moral judgment about mass atrocity, or does the attempt itself constitute a category error?
