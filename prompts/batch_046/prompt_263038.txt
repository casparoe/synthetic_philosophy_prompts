In the novel I am drafting, one of my central figures is a moral philosopher who has adopted a version of G. E. Moore’s conviction that the greatest intrinsic goods are aesthetic appreciation and personal affection, though she has never systematized her exact rankings in writing. Through her dialogue and private journals, I have established a track record of her implicit verdicts on hypothetical worlds: she holds that a life containing profound friendship but entirely lacking aesthetic experience remains immensely valuable; that a solitary life of extraordinary aesthetic richness is valuable yet somehow structurally incomplete; that a world in which everyone enjoys moderate friendship and moderate beauty is preferable to one in which half the population enjoys perfect friendship with no art and the other half enjoys perfect art with no companionship; and that if friendship is present but founded on systematic deception—say, every beloved other is a carefully constructed simulacrum—the value of that affection collapses to near zero, even when the aesthetic environment remains sublime. She also judges that introducing a small increment of genuine friendship into an already beautiful life yields more value than introducing an equivalent increment of beauty into an already affectionate life.

For a pivotal scene, she must now judge a new case: a world in which there exists exactly one authentic, deep friendship shared between two people, surrounded by an immense population who experience transcendent but entirely private aesthetic rapture—no one shares it, discusses it, or allows it to enter social life—while every other relationship in that world is purely transactional and shallow. Would she judge this world better or worse than a world of widespread, moderate friendship coupled with uniformly mediocre aesthetic experience? I want to know which verdict best coheres with her earlier rulings, what competing principles—perhaps an asymmetry between social and solitary dimensions of value, or a threshold condition that friendship must be widespread to matter—each interpretation requires, and which further hypothetical case would most sharply distinguish them. If her precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer, I need an honest statement of exactly what principle she would be adopting with each choice, so that the narrative can treat the ambiguity as a defining philosophical moment rather than an inconsistency.
