Write an essay evaluating whether endurantism or perdurantism offers the more coherent account of diachronic identity, concentrating on the hard case of entities that maintain functional and causal continuity despite the complete gradual replacement of their underlying material substrate—exemplified by living organisms with full cellular turnover and by hypothetical artificial minds undergoing incremental hardware substitution. Assess whether the endurantist can defend strict numerical identity over time by appealing to a non-material functional core without implicitly smuggling in temporal parts, and whether the perdurantist can answer the charge that slicing entities into temporal stages is ontologically extravagant or psychologically destructive. Weigh the central arguments carefully, but do not end non-committally: defend a definite conclusion—whether endurantism survives this extension, whether perdurantism is uniquely adequate, or whether both positions depend on an unexamined assumption about what persistence requires.
