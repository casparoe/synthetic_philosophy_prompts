I've come around to the idea that Lewis-style conventions are really just evolutionary stable strategies in cultural space—norms stick because they solve coordination problems, and the "common knowledge" part is just what happens when a strategy is widespread enough that everyone expects everyone else to expect it. In other words, Lewis's rationalist framing is a post-hoc description of something that evolves without anyone reasoning about arbitary alternatives. What would actually count against this reduction? I want the defeaters ranked: cases where norms emerge without any coordination payoff, results showing common knowledge does real causal work that selection can't replicate, or evidence that people genuinely reason about arbitary alternatives rather than just copying successful norms. Also, has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability—like saying any norm is "just an ESS"—and if so, is that a problem for a view that claims to explain convention specifically?
