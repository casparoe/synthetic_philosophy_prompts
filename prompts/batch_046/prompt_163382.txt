I've been working through the heterogeneity problem for hedonism in a graduate seminar on value theory, and I keep returning to a tension that standard treatments seem to overlook. The familiar version asks how bodily pleasures, intellectual satisfactions, and emotional releases can all be "pleasant" when they share no obvious phenomenal quality; the usual responses either deny that pleasure is a uniform sensation and move to a dispositional or quantitative account, or they invoke neurobiological commonalities. But I want to press a different worry—one that arises only if we take seriously what I have been calling cross-modal hedonic calibration: the idea that for hedonism to function as a substantive theory of well-being rather than a mere taxonomy of likes, there must be some implicit metric allowing us to weigh, say, the intensity of solving a proof against that of tasting wine, despite their qualitative dissimilarity. If such a calibration exists—perhaps expressed as willingness-to-sacrifice, preference strength, or normalized reward value—then the hedonist seems to be relying on an independent decision framework to establish the comparative weights of pleasures. But if that framework is doing the normative work of ranking experiences, hasn't pleasure itself become conceptually redundant?

So my question is this: doesn't the heterogeneity problem reveal not that pleasures lack a common feel, but that any attempt to unify them for purposes of well-being assessment necessarily imports non-hedonic values—such as rational coherence or long-term preference satisfaction—thereby showing that hedonism collapses into a broader theory of rational choice rather than grounding well-being in pleasure? In your response, please distinguish carefully between what is settled in the contemporary philosophy of well-being literature and what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any particular interpretation—whether representational, neurobiological, or the calibration framework I have sketched—as though it were the consensus position.
