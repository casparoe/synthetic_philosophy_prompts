I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher defending a position she calls functional-resemblance hedonism: pleasures share no single introspectible phenomenal quality, but are unified by non-derivatively fulfilling a specific second-order functional role—being desired for its own continuation—while also being realized through a common neurochemical pathway. She argues this dissolves the heterogeneity problem without positing an elusive pleasantness essence, and that standard counterexamples (masochistic enjoyment, intellectual satisfaction, sensory delight) fail because functional resemblance permits phenomenal diversity.

Draft six to eight interview questions that press the view where it is structurally weakest. Include: one on whether defining pleasure through a second-order desire for its own continuation is viciously circular; one on instrumental pleasures such as the discomfort of strenuous exercise sought solely for health, which appear desired but not for their own sake; and one on whether tying value to a specific neurochemical pathway collapses mental value into biological essentialism. For each, provide a follow-up keyed to her likely reply—anticipating, for instance, appeals to first-order versus second-order desire distinctions, or to plural realization of the pathway across brain states. The questions should turn on the actual mechanics of her account, avoid generic biographical openings, and must not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
