In my philosophy of religion seminar we have been comparing classical Buddhist anatta with Western reductionism about the self. I want to focus specifically on the Abhidharma claim that the person (pudgala) is only a conventional designation (prajñapti) over momentary, impersonal dharmas, and on Derek Parfit's reductionist view that personal identity consists solely in psychological continuity and connectedness with no further fact. Both reject a substantial self, but they appear to diverge over whether synchronic unity—the binding of simultaneous mental states—requires the same explanation as diachronic continuity across time. Does Parfit's framework implicitly preserve a kind of synchronic 'subject' that Abhidharma explicitly denies, or do both reduce even present-moment unity to bare relational continuity? Please compare the two on exactly this point, separating what is broadly settled in the comparative literature from what remains genuinely contested—especially regarding whether Buddhist momentariness (kṣaṇikatva) is compatible with any psychological-continuity theory—and do not present a disputed interpretation as the consensus view.
