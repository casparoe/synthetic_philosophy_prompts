I sit on a funding board for local heritage groups. Two applicants claim to be the same society: one is the legal successor of a philosophical discussion circle founded in the 1920s, dissolved in 1985, and recently revived by descendants who possess the original minute-books and an unfinished collaborative essay to which every historical member contributed at least one paragraph; the other is an unrelated group founded in 2010 that adopted the same name, meets in the original building, and maintains that shared practices rather than legal or textual continuity determine whether a society persists. We must decide whether to split a grant between them or deny one claim, yet our disagreement seems to shift between arguments about historical facts and arguments about what it means to be the same group at all. How should a board reason through this? Is there a genuine disagreement here, or are we largely using the word "society" in different ways? Please answer in British English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific philosophical works.
