okay so i've noticed this pattern in groups that i dont know if it has a proper philosophical name. like, picture an office where over months everyone gradually starts taking lunch at the same table, and if you ask any individual why, they say stuff like 'the light is good here' or 'i just ended up free then' — nobody mentions the group, and they dont even agree on what the group is doing. but an outside observer can predict 'marketing eats together at noon' and use that to explain behavior. then flip it: a crowd chants the same slogan, everyone claims they share a cause, but one person is there for the noise, one for a friend, one hates taxes — and the moment police show up the group fratures because there was never any shared plan holding them together.

is there an established concept for this exact gap? where collective-level descriptions predict behavior well even though no individual's first-person story references the group, and its not just false consciousness because people arent wrong about their own reasons — they just arent coordinating on them. it feels adjacent to joint action but not quite, since joint action usually needs mutualy awareness or shared intention, and here there might be neither. its also not plural subjecthood because nobody actually says 'we' except the observer. if philosophers havent specifically carved this out, can you name it and draw the edges? i want to know exactly what makes it different from pure aggregation, from hidden coordination, and from cases where people genuinely misremember their motives — not just a bare label.
