Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a uniform social constructionist and a differentiated kind-realist concerning race, gender, and money.

The constructionist maintains that all three kinds are exhaustively constituted by collective intentionality and social practice, lacking any independent non-social substrate. The realist grants pure institutional construction to money—physical tokens receiving status functions—yet holds that race and gender are socially interpreted but anchored in biological or material features, so their construction is interactive and constrained rather than ex nihilo.

Produce:
1. Numbered agreed claims about collective intentionality, the necessity of physical substrates, and the actual social operation of race, gender, and currency.
2. A disagreements table in neutral wording each side accepts, addressing: whether gender can be fully captured by an "X counts as Y" formula without an underlying biological X; whether race's historical tracking of ancestry makes it ontologically closer to money or to biological taxonomy; and whether money's fungibility proves pure construction is extendable to human kinds.
3. The cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would shift at least one party—including whether collective intentionality can generate kinds with no extra-practical causal powers, and whether interactive classification undermines realist anchoring.
4. For each crux, one concrete case or argument both recognise as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded explicitly.

Keep the formulation precise, avoid citations or bibliographies, and write in British English.
