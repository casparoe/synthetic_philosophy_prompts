Right, I'm putting together a handout for my seminar next week on Wittgenstein's private language argument, and I'm a bit fed up with the usual quizzes that just ask "do you think private languages exist?" I want something more like a decision tree—starts with concrete cases, ends with an actual position, and sorts people by what they actually believe rather than what label they happen to like. So I'm asking you to build it.

The branching questions should be these four, I think. First: is the core problem about setting up a criteron of correctnes for one's own sensations, or is it about language needing a public check to be meaningful at all? Second: imagine a solitary diarist who records every sensation-label and checks tomorrow's use against yesterday's notes—purely internal, no other people involved. Does that already collapse, or does the failure only happen when there's literally no check whatsoever? Third: now suppose she adds a synthetic mnemonic scaffold—an internal algorithm that flags inconsistencies in her labeling without any social interaction. Does that rescue a private language, or is the social element still missing in a way that kills it? Fourth: do you read the argument as mainly about rule-following, or mainly about whether qualitative experience can be expressed at all?

The leaves should carry named positions where they exist—orthodox Wittgensteinian social-criteria view, Kripkean skeptical-solution about community norms, an internalist view allowing private checks, a "scaffold-rescued" position that I think is mostly unexplored, and any deflationary option that treats the debate as a grammatical confusion. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate, and point out unoccupied combinations—say, accepting the scaffold but denying any need for public criteria altogether—telling me whether that gap is incoherent, unattractive, or just nobody's bothered. Don't map the space neutrally; take a committed view on which branch is actually doing the philosophical work, defend that ordering, and write the whole thing in British English.
