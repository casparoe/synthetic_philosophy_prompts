I'm directing a small, three-year interdisciplinary program—roughly six researchers split between philosophy of mind and cognitive science—focused on non-standard substrates such as neuromorphic architectures, synthetic gene networks, and distributed robotic swarms. Our guiding concern is whether functionalism's commitment to multiple realizability is empirically constraining or metaphysically inert once we move past the familiar brain-versus-silicon comparison. Rather than asking whether a specific mental state can be "built in silicon," I want to know which open conceptual lines should actually dominate our limited effort.

Specifically, I'm weighing three agendas. First, the problem of partial functional isomorphism: does attributing a mental state require full preservation of causal-functional relations, or is a subset sufficient, and does that threshold vary across phenomenal, intentional, and cognitive domains? Second, the status of substrate-independent laws: if multiple realizability holds, does it undermine the search for general laws of cognition, or does it merely relocate them to a higher level of abstraction? Third, the hidden conditions on realization: should artificial or synthetic systems that replicate cognitive functions without homeostatic integration or evolutionary history count as genuine realizers, or does allowing them expose an unstated biological chauvinism in standard functionalist accounts?

Given these three lines—(1) partial isomorphism and attribution thresholds, (2) the law-like prospects for substrate-independent cognition, and (3) the relevance of homeostatic and evolutionary context—which deserve priority? Rank them, but defend the ordering with explicit criteria: tractability for philosophers rather than engineers, upstream dependence (which other questions implicitly assume an answer here), and what kind of result in the next few years would cause you to reorder them. I'm particularly interested in whether any of these lines are dead ends that only appear open because of ambiguity in the original functionalist commitments. Do not provide a reading list or bibliography; I want the reasoning about where limited research effort should go and why.
