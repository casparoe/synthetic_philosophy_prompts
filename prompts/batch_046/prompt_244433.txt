everyone in my philosophy revision group keeps comparing nietzsche's three kinds of history — monumental, antiquarian and critical — to the three programmes on a washing machine. monumental is the heavy-duty cycle that builds strength, antiquarian is the delicate wash that preserves old fabric, critical is the spin that throws out dirty water. they use it to argue that any healthy culture needs all three running in balance, like you can't wash clothes with just one setting.

can you audit how far it holds? i want a correspondance table — what maps to the machine, the clothes, the dirt, the user, the water bill — then the breaks: does it smuggle in the idea that history should be mechanically 'balanced', when nietzsche actually says one mode can dominate and poison life? also, does the conclusion — that we need all three in harmony — survive once you see the disanalogies? and if the analogy is carrying the argument, what's a better one that doesn't import appliance logic?
