My mate runs a small mental-health chat service for students. She built a bot that replies instantly when no human counsellor is free—trained on real peer-support transcripts, but it’s clunky, misses sarcasm, and ocassionally sounds like a corporate FAQ. The data says it keeps people in the queue who’d otherwise hang up after forty minutes, but a couple users told her it made them feel more isolated, like they were being processed. She wants to keep it running because the net reduction in distress is huge, and she says she designed it with metta, so scaling her own good intention up is still right action, even though the bot has no mind of it's own. I keep feeling that treating distressed people as throughput is wrong regardless of the totals, and that the actual presence and object of care matters more than the designer’s heart. Is she just being practical, or am I right that there’s a deeper problem here?
