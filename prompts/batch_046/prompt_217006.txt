I retired from marine biology about five years back and now I sit on the funding board for our regional science museum, mostly to avoid gardening too much, but also because I picked up philosophy of math and now I see these problems everywhere. We just recieved a proposal for an interactive exibit called Hilberts Hotel in Brick — okay, the real title is longer — where they want to build a physically extendable wing with modular rooms that keep getting added as visitors fill them, paired with signage explaining Cantors pairing argument about different sizes of infinity. The researchers claim this isn't just a metaphor but actually approaches a completed actual infinity in physical form, and they want the museum plaques to endorse that view explicitly. Half the board, mostly physicists and an engineer, are furious: they say nature only allows potential infinity, never an actually completed one, and funding this means we're buying into some platonist metaphysics that misleads the public. The other half think it's harmless pedagogy — nobody believes the roof extends literally forever, it's just a tangible way to show one-to-one correspondence and that you can map guests onto rooms. I'm torn because I think the finitist objection is stronger than the other side admits, but I also don't want to kill a genuinely cool educational project over signage wording.

Can you draft the board's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with reasoning the research team could actually respond to?
