Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, iambic pentameter, ABA rhyme scheme with two repeating refrains—spoken by the night manager of Hilbert’s Hotel, who is also a committed finitist. The two refrains must be: “Every guest finds a vacant room” and “No completed total can be shown.” The first should carry the Cantorian logic of actual infinity; the second should carry the Aristotelian-finitist denial that an infinite collection exists all at once, accepting only potential infinity.

Structure the stanzas so the poem enacts three successive accommodations familiar from the hotel paradox: first, a single new guest arrives when the hotel is full and every occupant shifts to the next room; second, an infinitely long bus arrives and all current guests are reassigned to even-numbered rooms; third, infinitely many infinite buses arrive and guests are reassigned via powers of primes. The manager should describe these shifts precisely—naming the room assignments—while the refrains keep interrupting with the two incompatible verdicts.

Make the form do philosophical work: the villanelle’s circular repetition should mirror the endless, never-completed process of shifting guests, while the fixed nineteen-line container should stand for the finitist claim that only bounded, actually constructible totalities exist. The refrains must take turns in the A-lines and finally appear together, unreconciled, in the closing couplet.

Render the mathematics and the philosophical positions accurately enough that a reader familiar with Cantor’s argument for actual infinity and with finitist or potentialist objections can recognize both without relying on named authors. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.

After the poem, include a brief note that separates what is mathematically settled (the consistency of the room-shifting procedures within standard set theory) from what is genuinely contested (whether these procedures describe an actually existing infinite totality or only a never-ending potential process), and indicate where the poem takes liberties with the formal details or with the historical articulation of finitism.
