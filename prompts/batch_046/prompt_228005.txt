I'm enrolled in a graduate seminar on the philosophy of science, and we have been working through Nelson Goodman's new riddle of induction with the predicate grue—defined, for observed objects, as green prior to some arbitrary future time and blue thereafter. From the discussion I take Goodman to hold that induction is never fully justified by observed regularities alone, since observationally equivalent but incompatible predicates can always be constructed, and that the only non-circular basis for preferring one projection over another lies in entrenchment: the degree to which a predicate has been embedded in past successful inductive practice and linguistic usage.

Separately, I assist a regional climate-data consortium in refining predictive pipelines, and we recently discovered that one of our feature-extraction algorithms implicitly deploys a temporal predicate—let us call it storm-active—that structurally mimics grue within our training window. Before a fixed calibration date, the algorithm classifies atmospheric patterns according to one set of meteorological criteria; after that date, the classification rule shifts in a manner that is observationally indistinguishable inside the observed data but yields divergent forecasts outside it. When I raised this with my supervisor, he suggested that we resolve the ambiguity by applying Goodman's criterion directly: we should retain the specification that employs predicates with greater entrenchment in the historical taxonomy of meteorology, and discard the grue-like alternative. I objected that this seems to misapply Goodman, because entrenchment regulates the projectibility of linguistic categories in human inductive reasoning, not the structural validity of physical models, and that treating model selection as if it were a problem of predicate entrenchment collapses an epistemological distinction between justifying beliefs and justifying instruments.

Yet I now worry that my objection assumes a false separation—perhaps any choice between empirically equivalent forecasting architectures just is a choice about which inductive habits to extend, and therefore Goodman's framework applies without remainder. Could you help me see whether my supervisor's proposal represents a legitimate extension of Goodman's analysis to predictive engineering, or whether it depends on a category mistake? And if the latter, does the grue paradox still pose a specific challenge for justifying climate models by past predictive success, or is that challenge dissolved once we recognize that model selection requires criteria—such as causal coherence or structural stability—that lie outside the scope of entrenchment altogether?
