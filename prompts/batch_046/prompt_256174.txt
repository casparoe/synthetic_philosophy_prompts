I'm preparing a position paper on Zhuangzi's philosophy of death for a comparative ethics workshop and need a consistency audit before I finalize my view. Here are my commitments:

(1) I interpret Zhuangzi's acceptance of death through a metaphysics of transformation (hua): qi reconfigures rather than being destroyed, so death is analogous to seasonal change, not annihilation.

(2) Following the butterfly dream, I deny that there is a metaphysically deep, enduring subject—"Zhuang Zhou" is a temporary functional configuration.

(3) I hold that death can only be harmful via deprivation of future goods to a persisting subject; since (2) denies such persistence, I deny posthumous harm and accept a symmetry-style conclusion that prenatal and posthumous non-existence are on a par.

(4) I think fear of death is conceptually confused on this view: it treats the self as a substance that can be lost, whereas it is merely a pattern that dissolves.

(5) I defend a "processual care" account of mortuary ethics: respecting corpses and mourning rituals expresses regard for the continuity of transformation itself, not for interests of a deceased person.

(6) I maintain that narrative closure—the completion of a meaningful life arc—is a genuine good, but only for a being that remains the same subject throughout that arc.

(7) I claim that evolutionary debunking arguments against death-anxiety fail here, because the transformation metaphysics provides an independent conceptual reason to reject the fear's presupposition, not just a psychological explanation of its origin.

Audit this package. Identify every pair in genuine tension, rank them by severity, and for each specify the cheapest revision and what it costs me (particularly whether it forces me to abandon (5) or weaken (6)). Also flag any pair that looks tense but dissolves on inspection, explaining why. Finally, tell me which single commitment I should drop to maximize consistency with minimal damage. Keep your response under 350 words; begin with a one-sentence bottom-line verdict. Do not provide bibliographies or citations.
