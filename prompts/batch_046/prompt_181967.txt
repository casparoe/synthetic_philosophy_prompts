I'm currently enrolled in a graduate seminar on explanation in the social sciences, and we've spent the past several weeks on the problem of whether cultural values can be genuine causes of large-scale economic transformation, using Weber's thesis linking the Protestant ethic to the emergence of modern capitalism as our central case study. Having worked through the standard objections—the chronological priority of capitalist practices in Italian city-states and the Low Countries, the risk of selecting on the dependent variable, and the difficulty of disentangling religious psychology from legal and institutional factors—I now want to defend a rehabilitated Weberian position. Rather than treating values as efficient causes that directly motivate accumulation, I take them to operate as selection environments: shared normative frameworks alter the reproductive conditions for economic practices by changing the social costs of moral condemnation and recognition, thereby differentially stabilizing capitalist behavior in specific geographic and institutional contexts without claiming that Protestantism created capitalism ex nihilo.

Given this framing, I am trying to construct an honest exposure map of the view: what would have to be true for it to fail, and how damaging each failure mode actually is. I am not looking for a repetition of the familiar historical counterexamples alone, but for the deeper conceptual, comparative-historical, and formal defeaters. For example, if rigorous comparative sociology demonstrates that state fiscal structures consistently dominate normative frameworks in determining which economic practices survive across populations, does that undermine the selection-environment claim fatally or only awkwardly? If formal models in cultural evolution show that value-based selection is invariably swamped by material incentives at the macro level, does the view collapse into redundant epiphenomenalism, or can it be qualified to preserve a non-redundant explanatory role? I also want to distinguish between defeaters that defenders of this rehabilitated Weberianism would themselves concede—such as a well-documented case where a robust value-system failed to stabilize an otherwise viable practice, or a result in philosophy of explanation showing that selection-environment accounts are indistinguishable from purely institutional ones—and those they would dismiss as still presupposing an outdated efficient-cause conception of culture. Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicate whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability, and assess whether that insulation is itself a defect for a causal-explanatory thesis of this kind.
