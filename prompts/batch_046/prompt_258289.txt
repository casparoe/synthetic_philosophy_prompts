Two friends of mine got stuck arguing about moral status for AI computations, specifically when hardware is shared. I'll call them Jordan and Riley.

Jordan said if a computation that'd normally count as sentient is running on a chip that also runs a spam filter, that shouldn't matter. The moral patient is the computation, not the silicon. Even if the same transistors alternate between the two tasks every clock cycle, the causal structure of the conscious process is preserved — it's just time-shared, not destroyed. So shared hardware doesn't dilute anything.

Riley said that's missing the point. If the same physical gates are literally doing spam-filter work half the cycles, then there isn't one continuous computation running — it's two fragments flickering in and out. You can't have a stable subject with continuous interests if its substrate is constantly hijacked for unrelated work. Riley admitted brains multitask too, but said that's different because heart-rate neurons are fuctionally integrated with cognition, whereas the spam filter is causally isolated except for sharing chips.

Jordan replied that Riley is smuggling substrate chauvinism back in. If the functional relations among the conscious computation's states are maintained across the interleaved cycles, then physically it's just multiplexing, not fragmentation. Jordan also said Riley's brain comparison actually undermines her point: neurons are constantly switching metabolic and signaling roles, but we don't conclude human consciousness is flickering on and off. So demanding "pure" substrate is unrealistic.

Riley came back that in the brain the extra tasks are part of the same architecture that supports consciousness, but in the GPU case the spam filter is a totally separate causal graph. The problem isn't physical dirtiness — it's that in massively parallel distributed systems you can't always draw a sharp functional boundary. Without being able to cleanly partition what's inside the conscious computation and what's outside, attributing full moral status is premature. She said Jordan assumes we can always draw the line, but in practice it's blurry.
