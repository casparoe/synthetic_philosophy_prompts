The view stitches together Twin Earth-style semantic externalism with a particular account of phenomenal consciousness, but I am not sure whether the seams show.

Here is the package as it stands. (a) I accept semantic externalism about both natural-kind terms and mental content generally: Oscar and Twin Oscar share narrow psychological states but differ in reference and truth conditions, and the content of my own beliefs depends partly on my physical environment and linguistic community. (b) I maintain that first-person authority is largely preserved under externalism: I can know non-observationally what I am thinking, including that my term water refers to H2O and not to XYZ, without investigating my surroundings or consulting social norms. (c) I deny that narrow content—the state shared by physical duplicates across worlds—plays any causal or explanatory role in behavior or consciousness; all explanatory work is done by wide content together with external conditions. (d) I hold that phenomenal concepts are formed through direct acquaintance with token mental states and function indexically, like demonstratives anchored to this very experience; this fixes phenomenal reference internally, making phenomenal content narrow and environment-independent. (e) I am a physicalist: phenomenal properties are identical to certain physical or functional properties, and the explanatory gap is purely epistemic, arising from the indexical mode of presentation described in (d).

Rank the tensions by severity. Then tell me which single revision—dropping or weakening one commitment—would clean up the most inconsistencies, and identify any remaining tension that even that revision leaves untouched.
