I recently began tracing my grandmother's migration records and discovered that the official archive preserves every document for citizens of one legal status while systematically omitting the labor contracts of domestic workers like her, which has made me see archival silence not as absence but as structured erasure. For a 90-minute workshop with sixteen graduate students in history and information studies, design a structured simulation that makes participants experience how power operates through what an archive includes and excludes before they are given the conceptual vocabulary. Assign half the group to be record-creators governed by explicit rules about which events they must document and which they must discard, and assign the other half to be researchers who must answer specific historical questions using only the resulting archive. Include rules that create a coordination failure between the two groups, a minute-by-minute timeline, a debrief that maps key moments onto the idea that omission is an active production of reality, an honest account of which feature of structural silence this simulation cannot reproduce, and a prediction of what will predictably go wrong when the activity is run.
