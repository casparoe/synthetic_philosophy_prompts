I am working through Galen Strawson’s Basic Argument, which contends that true moral responsibility is impossible because nothing can be causa sui: if determinism holds, our actions trace back to heredity and environment, and if indeterminism holds, they trace back to chance, leaving us never the ultimate originators of our nature or conduct. I want an honest exposure map of this position in its strongest form. What would have to be true for it to collapse? Identify which premises—concerning the conditions for ultimate authorship, the exclusion of partial self-creation, or the irrelevance of collective, recursive, and artificially scaffolded forms of agency—are genuinely vulnerable rather than merely logical escape hatches. Which conceptual distinctions, such as that between originating and sustaining causation, and which empirical considerations about developmental autonomy or neuroplasticity, could provide decisive counter-evidence, and which ones would defenders themselves concede as damaging? Address whether pressure from models of distributed agency introduces a live risk or only an awkward parallel. Conclude with an assessment of how well-insulated the argument is: does it risk anything substantial, or have its defenders rendered it immune by qualification?
