Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between identificationists and seperatists on ontology identification when information is permanently inaccesible. The setup: imagine a mirror-sector of particles lying behind an eternal cosmological horizon, empirically equivalent to our sector in every accessible respect and every theoretical bridge we could ever build. The identificationists say that if two ontologies match on all differentiable data, treating them as distinct is just metaphysical overreach; ontology should be tied to what is in principle identifiable, not to hidden duplicates. The separatists say existence claims answer to reality, not to our epistemic reach, so the mirror-particles either exist or dont regardless of the horizon, and collapsing them into one ontology is a category mistake that confuses knowledge with being.

Write the doc they'd both put their names to. Then a disagreements table: the exact residual disputes phrased neutrally, with each camp's own wording for its view written so they'd accept it, plus the other camp's wording recognized as fair. Next, the cruxes -- I think one is whether identifiability is a constraint on ontology itself or only on our representational choices, and another is whether permanent inaccessibility is a coherent metaphysical category or just an epistemic label. For each crux, propose one concrete hypothetical case or argument both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp predicts about it. Finally, flag any part of the dispute that turns out to be purely verbal and say which part and why. Keep the tone casual, don't ask for citations or reading lists, and don't lean on any obscure named theory I wouldn't know from reasoning alone.
