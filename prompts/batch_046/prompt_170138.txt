I serve on my school's emergency grant committee, which uses a standardized rubric that assigns identical objective scores to two current applicants: my closest friend, who just faced an unexpected family crisis, and a stranger whose documented needs match point for point. I feel an immediate, warm pull to help my friend, yet I also feel pressure to judge both cases from a detached, universal standpoint that ignores personal ties. I understand that Adam Smith argued we possess an internal impartial spectator that moderates our partial sympathies. Without asking for citations, bibliographies, or specific texts, please rank the following three resolutions—first, trusting my initial sympathy and voting for my friend; second, suppressing all personal feeling and applying the rubric mechanically; and third, allowing my sympathy to inform my judgment but then testing that judgment through an imagined neutral observer—according to how well each aligns with Smith's framework. Provide one sentence of justification for each rank, and then state the single most important practical implication of Smith's view for the vote I should cast.
