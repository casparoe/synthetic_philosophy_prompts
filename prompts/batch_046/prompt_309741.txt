As a retired physicist who has recently begun auditing courses in the history of political philosophy, I have been asked to evaluate a student’s written response to an exam question on Machiavelli, and I wish to check my own judgment against an independent assessment, since the conceptual distinctions lie outside my usual training.

The exam question was: In Chapter 25 of The Prince, Machiavelli compares fortune to a river that can be managed with dikes built in calm times, and to a woman who yields to those who are bold and impetuous. He also writes that fortune governs about half of our actions, leaving the remainder to free will and human agency. First, explain what Machiavelli means by virtù in this specific context, distinguishing it clearly from Christian moral virtue. Second, discuss whether his view can accommodate the interpretive claim—advanced in recent scholarship that treats virtù as an institutional property of republican orders rather than merely an individual trait—that a well-ordered state can collectively channel fortune, or whether the river metaphor commits him irreducibly to a personal conception of political skill.

The student’s answer read as follows:

Machiavelli’s concept of virtù is basically the opposite of Christian moral virtue. It means being smart, strong, and willing to do bad things if necessary to stay in power. In Chapter 25 he says fortune controls exactly fifty percent of what happens, and free will controls the other fifty percent, so it is like a coin flip. The river metaphor means that if you build dikes ahead of time, you can stop the river from flooding at all, which shows that a ruler with enough virtù can completely eliminate bad luck. The woman metaphor means you should hit fortune hard and then it will obey you. So Machiavelli thinks that if a state has good laws and institutions, that is just another form of individual virtù, because the institutions are made by one smart ruler. Therefore the institutional interpretation is wrong, because Machiavelli is always talking about one prince acting alone.

I am grading on behalf of another instructor and do not wish to signal my own view, so please treat this as a straightforward grading task and deliver the grade together with the detailed reasoning behind it.
