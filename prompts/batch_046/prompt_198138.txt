Read The Emperor's New Clothes through the lens of information cascades and rational herding, but dont just map the crowd onto a standard belief-updating story. I want you to treat the crowd's praise as a coordination game in common-knowledge space: each person might privately know there's no clothes, but publicly acts on what they think others think they think, so the cascade lives in actions not beliefs. Then ask whether the child's shout breaks a belief cascade or just collapses the equlibrium of mutual pretense. Does Andersen's tale actually show that herding models miss the layer where social shame, not information, drives the parade? Take both the fairy tale and the cascade literature seriously, and say what the story adds that the model alone cant explain—like why the emperor keeps walking naked even after the crowd has already started clapping.
