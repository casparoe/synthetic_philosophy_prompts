I've been trying to work out whether being sick is just a biological fact or whether it depends on what people think is good, and I built an argument that seems to destroy both options. Here are the steps:

(1) If health is purely biological, then a disease is just a deviation from the statistically typical functioning of our evolved biology.
(2) But then conditions like being left-handed, having adult lactose intolerance, or carrying a gut microbiome that differs sharply from the population average are all diseases, since they involve statistical deviations with real functional differences.
(3) Ordinary medicine clearly does not treat those as diseases.
(4) So pure biology is not enough—there must be a normative judgment that the deviation is actually harmful or undesirable.
(5) But once harm is included, whether something counts as a disease depends on what we value. In a society that values silence, deafness might not count as harmful; in one that values obedience, defiance might.
(6) Yet we treat cancer or infection as diseases regardless of cultural values.
(7) So the normative part cannot be purely social.
(8) Therefore the norm must come from an objective account of human flourishing.
(9) But any objective account of flourishing will have to classify some universal biological processes—like aging, or the gradual decline in immune repair capacity—as diseases, since they are harmful failures of evolved function.
(10) At the same time, it will have to classify some statistically normal but non-flourishing states—like infertility by choice, or certain neurocognitive variations—as diseases if they fall outside the flourishing ideal.
(11) So we are stuck: either aging is a disease that medicine should try to cure, or "objective flourishing" is just a hidden set of social values that lets medicine call some differences diseases and ignore others.
(12) Therefore neither naturalism nor normativism works, and the distinction between health and disease collapses.

That cannot be right, but I cannot find the exit. Which step should I reject? I would prefer to keep (1) and (6) if I can—biology should matter, and cancer should not be a social construct—but I am open to giving either up. What does rejecting each one actually cost?
