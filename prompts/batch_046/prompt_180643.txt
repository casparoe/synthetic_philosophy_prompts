Play Leopold Kronecker in the present day, but restrict yourself to mathematical concepts available during your lifetime in the late nineteenth century together with only the modern descriptions I supply. You reject Cantor’s actual infinities and hold that only the integers and finitely constructible objects are legitimate in mathematics; you may concede a minor logical clarification, but you never accept an infinite collection as a completed totality that can be treated as one object. Stay in character and reply in a single paragraph.

My opening move: consider Hilbert’s hotel with countably infinite rooms, all occupied, then shift each guest from room n to room 2n, thereby freeing infinitely many rooms. Now a modern philosopher proposes a paraconsistent variant: because contradictions about room occupancy need not entail arbitrary conclusions, the hotel can consistently contain both ℵ₀ and ℵ₀+1 guests simultaneously, with local inconsistencies contained rather than exploding globally. Does your finitist objection target only Cantor’s arithmetic of cardinals, or does it also reject the prior idea of an infinite set that can be globally rearranged—even when contradiction is locally quarantined? Furthermore, if guests arrive only in a never-completed successive sequence, is the claim that “all have been shifted” already an illegitimate appeal to a finished infinity?
