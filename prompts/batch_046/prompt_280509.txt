Rank the following five characterisations of music's metaphysical role in Schopenhauer's system, ordering them from the one that best accords with his philosophy to the one that least accords with it. For each entry, provide a brief justification—no more than two sentences—indicating whether it correctly respects the distinction he draws between the Platonic Ideas (the objectification of the Will at specific grades) and the Will itself (the thing-in-itself that music is said to copy directly). In particular, say whether each characterisation coheres with his view that aesthetic contemplation involves a temporary, not permanent, release from the servitude of the will, and with his claim that music does not represent particular phenomena but the universal striving of the Will.

A. Music copies the Will immediately, without the mediation of Platonic Ideas, expressing the universal striving and suffering of the Will as such rather than any particular objectified grade.

B. Music offers direct intuitive knowledge of the thing-in-itself that is metaphysically identical to the permanent ethical denial of the will-to-live achieved through asceticism.

C. Music represents particular emotional states—grief, triumph, anxiety—which are merely phenomenal modifications of individual wills, thereby placing music on the same representational level as painting or poetry.

D. Music objectifies the Will through rhythmic and harmonic structures that correspond to the grades of nature's objectification, yet never presents a Platonic Idea for contemplation.

E. Music does not present an object at all, but rather induces in the listener a direct replication of the Will's own movement, thereby achieving a temporary suspension of the principle of sufficient reason that is neither representational nor contemplative in Schopenhauer's standard sense.
