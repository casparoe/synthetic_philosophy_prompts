Analyze the 2000–2008 U.S. subprime mortgage boom and the collapse of Lehman Brothers in September 2008 as a case study in Aristotle’s theory of exchange and acquisition, drawing specifically on Politics I.8–10 and Nicomachean Ethics V.5. Use his distinction between oikonomia—household management ordered to the good life—and chrematistike—wealth-getting that recognizes no natural limit—together with his claim that money (nomisma) was invented as a conventional medium for exchange (metablētikē) but becomes corrupt when made an end in itself, above all in tokos, where currency appears to beget currency. For the major actors—mortgage originators, investment banks structuring CDOs and CDS, credit-rating agencies, and regulators—map their activities onto Aristotle’s categories: which practices approximate natural exchange serving a determinate need (chreia), and which instantiate the unnatural form in which the instrument of exchange is detached from use-value and multiplied without bound? Identify which crisis dynamics the framework explains well, for example the displacement of use-oriented lending by accumulation for its own sake, and which it misdescribes or leaves silent—such as the systemic diffusion of risk through securitization tranches, or the collective-action problem in which each actor’s locally rational decision produces globally catastrophic harm, neither of which has an obvious counterpart in Aristotle’s dyadic model of corrective justice governed by geometrical proportion. Then assess what the episode shows about the theory: does it confirm Aristotle’s warning that allowing money to become the end of exchange destabilizes the polis, or does it expose a limitation, namely that his binary of natural versus unnatural acquisition presupposes a transparent hierarchy of ends that modern financial instruments deliberately obscure? In your answer, state explicitly what would change your assessment—whether a different interpretation of chreia as socially constructed rather than naturally fixed, or evidence that regulatory capture rather than the internal logic of chrematistike was the primary driver—and indicate where your reasoning is most likely to fail, particularly in applying a pre-modern teleological framework to an institutional architecture designed precisely to sever the link between exchange and any determinate human good.
