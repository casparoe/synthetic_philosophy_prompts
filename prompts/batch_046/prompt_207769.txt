I'd like a careful conceptual analysis of the following variant of the Sleeping Beauty problem, specifically focused on whether the standard self-locating credence update survives an asymmetry introduced inside the heads branch.

Set up the familiar framework: Sunday night a fair coin is tossed. If tails, Beauty is awakened once on Monday and then the experiment ends. If heads, she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday, with her Monday memories erased in between.

Now inject the following modification, which applies only in the heads branch: upon the Tuesday awakening, Beauty is given a compound that does not restore any explicit memories, but does produce a faint, non-specific phenomenal marker—a sense that she has "already done something like this"—which she knows is present in the Tuesday-heads protocol but absent in both the Monday-heads and Monday-tails protocols. She is fully aware of this protocol difference.

When she finds herself awake, she faces three centered possibilities: Monday-heads, Tuesday-heads, and Monday-tails. The standard argument for assigning 1/3 credence to heads treats the two heads-centered worlds as subjectively indistinguishable, so that counting centered worlds yields the update. But here Monday-heads and Tuesday-heads are not fully indistinguishable: the Tuesday case carries an additional sub-personal trace.

Please explain:

1. Whether this phenomenal asymmetry undermines the symmetry condition required for the 1/3 answer, or whether the self-locating update applies only to possibilities that are identical in every first-person respect—including affective or sub-cognitive traces—or whether the trace is evidentially irrelevant because it is itself caused by the coin having landed heads.

2. Whether there remains a principled boundary between "beliefs about where I am in the tree" and "beliefs about the coin's outcome" when the mechanism that determines location (the Tuesday protocol) also introduces an evidential marker whose presence correlates perfectly with heads.

3. How this bears on the broader tension between the Principal Principle—aligning credence with known objective chances—and pure centered-world counting rules, particularly when the process generating the centered worlds also injects differential information within a single world-branch.

Keep the discussion conceptual and intuitive rather than formal; I want an analysis of exactly where the standard reasoning is vulnerable, whether the vulnerability is merely a boundary case or reveals a deeper ambiguity in how we individuate "the same epistemic situation" across centered worlds, and whether any coherent view must treat the Tuesday marker as either part of the location-evidence or as separate coin-evidence that cancels the self-locating update.
