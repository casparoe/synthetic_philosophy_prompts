I've been part of this neighborhood food co-op for like six years, and lately things have gotten weird. We used to vote on everything — budgets, new vendors, even what music plays in the store — but last winter the board pushed through a "governance reform" where now there's a five-person steering committee that can veto any member decision if they say it's "not aligned with strategic sustainability." At the same meeting they added a rule that members can be suspended for "repeatedly undermining collective morale," and since then two people who spoke up against the reform have been suspended. Alot of longtime members are furious, but the board keeps saying we're just maturing past "chaotic direct democracy" and that every real organization eventually needs this kind of oversight.

My roommate says I'm overreacting and that pure democracy doesn't scale, that what looks like a power grab is actually just fixing broken processes. My other friend says this is literally authoritarian creep and that phrases like "strategic sustainability" and "collective morale" are just blank checks designed to silence critics. I can't tell if I'm looking at a real moral difference or if both sides are just dressing up "I don't like change" in fancy language. Is there actually a principled way to tell when a group is sliding into something genuinely undemocratic versus just growing up and getting more efficient? And who even gets to make that call — the people being suspended, the committee running things, or some outside observer? I keep feeling like there's a hidden assumption in both arguments about who owns the group, but I can't name it, and I don't want to just pick the side that feels nicer.
