Write the opening remarks for a workshop called "Who Names the Age?" The room is split: half are stratigraphers who have spent years arguing over whether the Anthropocene should be formalized at all, half are climate historians and an elder from a local nation who traces ecological rupture to settler arrival rather than carbon curves. You have about six minutes. The philosophical point has to actually structure what you say: picking an Anthropocene start date — 1610 tied to colonization and population collapse, or 1950 tied to nuclear testing — isnt a neutral measurement, its an allocation of blame. If you pick the bomb spike you center modern industrial states; if you pick 1610 you center colonial extraction. Dont resolve the tension. Dont pretend stratigraphy can settle the politics. Make sure the elder's framework is treated as a competing claim to authority over deep time, not as colorful local context for the real science. Work in the idea that choosing a Golden Spike is like naming a capital for a country that doesnt exist yet — the marker creates the territory it claims to represent. Keep it casual, like hallway talk after a long session. No citations or reading lists, just speak. Oh, and dont say anything that implies science alone decides who gets named the author of this age.
