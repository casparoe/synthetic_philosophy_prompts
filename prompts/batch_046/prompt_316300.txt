You serve on the Ethics and Partnership Review Board of the Meridian International Development Fund. By next Thursday the Board must vote on Project Kharif, a rural infrastructure initiative in a region where formal state capacity is weak and social coordination depends heavily on extended kinship networks. Western auditors have flagged the local implementing partner for distributing project-related materials—concrete allocations, roofing contracts, and skilled-labor placements—to households headed by local administrative officials and their relatives. The auditors classify these distributions as corrupt clientelism and gift-giving that violate the Fund’s universal anti-corruption charter. The local partner defends the practice not as bribery but as structural reciprocity: a culturally embedded obligation to honor role-based relationships that makes project delivery possible at all. They contend that imposing Western transactional norms here would collapse local legitimacy and halt construction.

The Board’s policy permits culturally informed accommodation but offers no criteria for distinguishing legitimate reciprocity from corruption. Draft the short written opinion—approximately eight hundred words, in formal institutional register—that you will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. The opinion must recommend a concrete course of action—suspension, conditional continuation with safeguards, or formal acceptance with monitored reciprocity protocols—together with a specific timeline for implementation or review. It must engage seriously with the local partner’s defense, treating its strongest version rather than dismissing it as rationalization. Apply, within the reasoning itself, the philosophical distinction between corruption as deviation from universal procedural fairness and corruption as breach of locally legitimate role-embedded reciprocity, without invoking bibliographic references, reading lists, or citations. Address explicitly whether Western standards of corruption can be applied universally in contexts where state and kinship structures overlap, and identify the practical risk of both false positives—labeling legitimate social coordination as corruption—and false negatives—excusing extraction disguised as tradition. Conclude with an honest treatment of the strongest consideration favoring the position your recommendation rejects, and explain precisely why that consideration does not prevail. Write for board members of diverse disciplinary backgrounds who have not specialized in either anthropology or moral philosophy.
