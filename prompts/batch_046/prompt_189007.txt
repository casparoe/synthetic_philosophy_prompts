I am preparing an essay for my sixth-form philosophy of science coursework, and I should like your help in developing the argument. The question is whether theories such as the multiverse—arising, for instance, from eternal inflation—or the landscape of string theory can be rationally accepted when direct empirical testing may be permanently out of reach. Please write an essay that examines whether non-empirical considerations—mathematical consistency, explanatory uniqueness, and what I shall call "structural confirmation", by which I mean that a framework accommodates known physical structures without ad hoc adjustments—can serve as legitimate forms of justification. You should weigh this against the objection that such reasoning collapses the boundary between science and speculative metaphysics. Reach and defend a clear verdict: ought these frameworks to be treated as provisionally scientific, as philosophically interesting but scientifically unwarranted, or as failing an essential demarcation criterion? In your response, please state explicitly how confident you are in your overall conclusion, and identify the single point in your reasoning about which you feel least certain.
