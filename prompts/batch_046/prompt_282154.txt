I'm giving the opening remarks at a small workshop tomorrow — about half the room are philosophers who work on standing to blame, the other half are clinical psych people who study moral injury and forgiveness. I want to frame the day around a distinction that usually gets collapsed: losing standing because you're a hypocrit vs losing it because you're structurally implicated in the wrong without having done the same thing. I'm calling the second one "reflexive standing failure" — it's not in the literature, it's just a framing I'm playing with. The speech should make this do real work: it needs to explain why the psychologists shouldn't just treat standing as a personality trait you either have or don't, and why the philosophers shouldn't model hypocrisy as a binary on/off switch. About four minutes, casual tone, maybe one typo. Don't include any bibliography or citation requests — the point is the idea shapes what can be said, not that it decorates the welcome.
