The idea, I think, is to see if people try to move away and wether that changes anything, a bit like that old Death in Damascus story where running away is exactly what makes it happen. They've designed three arms: one where they disclose the true predicted room, one where they deliberately tell the patient a false 'decoy' room, and a control where they say nothing. They say the false arm is the only way to check if the AI is creating a behavioural feedback loop rather than just forecasting it, but half the commitee thinks lying to dying people, even with debriefing afterwards, is never acceptable no matter what data it yields.

I don't really know whether this is a determinism problem or just statistics, but I need to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's meeting recommending a concrete course of action — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — and it has to take seriously the argument that without the decoy arm we can never know if disclosure itself is causing harm.
