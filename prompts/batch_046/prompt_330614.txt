I have been revisiting Hofstadter's account of creativity as the adjustment of parameters—knobs—within a conceptual space, and trying to reconcile it with the way large language models navigate high-dimensional representations. It seems to me that if a concept-space is defined by its ontology, then the inventory of knobs is fixed in advance; creativity can only be interpolation among pre-existing dimensions, never the genuine introduction of a new axis. On this reading, even something that looks like radical conceptual blending—say, treating prediction-market calibration as an aretaic virtue, where epistemic accuracy is construed as a character trait rather than an instrumental output—would merely be the discovery of an unvisited coordinate within a joint manifold, not an expansion of the manifold itself. So my question is this: if Hofstadter's model commits us to the view that all creative acts are local adjustments within a predetermined knob-space, then in what sense can we ever say that a thinker, or a model, has genuinely invented a new conceptual dimension rather than revealing a hidden combination? Or should we treat the distinction between interpolation and true conceptual innovation as itself a kind of category error?
