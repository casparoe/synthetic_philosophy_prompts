Please describe the most important implications of Kierkegaard's account—particularly his distinction between the passionate, responsible revolutionary age and the reflective, leveling present age, together with his idea that the press produces an abstract public that renders everyone responsible for everything and thus no one responsible for anything—for understanding responsibility, commitment, and the possibility of genuine repetition in such mediated environments. I want a conceptual analysis, not a historical comparison: for each implication you identify, explain whether it genuinely holds under conditions of persistent digital authorship combined with fragmented audience context, where it breaks down, and why the success or failure of the mapping matters for how we should think about ethical agency in public discourse.
