Grade the following exam answer on safe Pareto improvements in risk-allocation ethics. Give a letter grade (A–F) in the first sentence, then the specific point-by-point comments that justify it, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.

Exam question: A “safe Pareto improvement” in risk governance requires that every affected party faces weakly lower risk in every relevant dimension, that no risk is hidden by being transferred across categories (e.g., physical to temporal or psychological), and that any unavoidable residual risk be distributed only with the ex-ante consent of those exposed. Apply this to a municipality that reroutes chemical transport away from residential streets and through an industrial corridor, reducing residents’ exposure to toxic spills but lengthening drivers’ routes and thereby increasing fatigue-related accident risk. Is the rerouting a safe Pareto improvement?

Student answer: A safe Pareto improvement makes at least one party strictly better off without making anyone worse off, with the added constraint that risk is not merely relocated. Here, residents have strictly lower chemical exposure, so they are better off. For the drivers, the fatigue risk is different in kind from chemical risk, but because fatigue-related accidents remain lower in absolute probability than toxic spills, the drivers have not actually been made worse off in overall risk terms—only in the type of risk they face. Furthermore, the drivers did not consent to the longer routes, but consent is not part of the Pareto logic itself; it only applies when risk is directly imposed, not when it arises as an unavoidable side effect of protecting others. Therefore, since residents gain and no party suffers a net increase in risk, the rerouting qualifies as a safe Pareto improvement.
