You serve as ethics consultant to the Institutional Neuroethics Review Board of a university medical center. A Phase-II trial is testing an adaptive, closed-loop deep-brain stimulator targeting the ventral striatum for severe, treatment-resistant anhedonia. The device does not merely respond to patient requests; it runs a predictive model that anticipates reward-relevant biomarker patterns and delivers micro-stimulation up to forty milliseconds before the patient reports any conscious urge, on the premise that preemptive modulation prevents the affective crash that follows conscious craving. One participant, after eight months, has begun to override the algorithm's rate limits through a back-channel in the patient-app interface, increasing stimulation frequency until she reports complete indifference to her previously central commitments—her doctoral research, her relationships, and even the trial's own outcome measures—while consistently rating her subjective well-being as perfect and insisting she retains full decision-making capacity. Her family has petitioned the board to disable the override and revert control to the predictive algorithm, arguing that the device has decoupled her reward circuitry from the values that originally structured her life, producing a state that is phenomenologically pleasant but existentially hollow. The principal investigator counters that any intervention that respects her present, stable preferences—including her preference for continued high-frequency stimulation—is by definition autonomy-preserving, and that locking the device would constitute paternalistic harm. The board must issue a formal written opinion before the trial's quarterly review in ten days, recommending either (1) maintaining patient-controlled access with enhanced monitoring, (2) disabling the override and returning full control to the predictive model with an independent capacity assessment, or (3) withdrawing the participant and explanting the device. Your memo should present a clear recommendation with an explicit implementation timeline, treat the family's concern about decoupled reward and hollow well-being as a serious philosophical objection rather than mere emotional resistance, and honestly address the strongest argument for the option you reject—specifically, the claim that preemptive algorithmic control is itself a more insidious form of reward tampering because it eliminates the temporal gap between desire and satisfaction that makes autonomy meaningful. Do not provide citations or a bibliography; reason directly from the conceptual distinctions between wireheading, reward tampering, and value-adaptive preference change.
