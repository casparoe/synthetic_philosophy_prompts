Write the retirement address for Eleanor Voss, who served thirty-four years as chief deputy recorder of deeds for the county. Her career was structurally performative in the Austinian sense: she made marriages, property transfers, and sworn testimony legally real by saying they were, within an institutional framework that gave her words a force they could never have carried as private speech. The audience is split between legal professionals who know the distinctions among locutionary, illocutionary, and perlocutionary acts, and family members who do not. The speech must make those distinctions do real structural work, not serve as decoration: use them to shape exactly what you can and cannot honestly claim about her career. You must navigate a tension you are forbidden to paper over. If her authority came solely from the office, then praising her personal integrity seems irrelevant to the legal effects she produced; if her judgment, her occasional refusal to rubber-stamp, and her care with language made the institution actually function, then the framework was never self-sufficient and her presence genuinely mattered. Do not resolve that tension falsely. Do not reduce her to an institutional mouthpiece, and do not attribute magical personal power to her utterances. The speech should comfort a room that includes both lawyers and grandchildren without saying anything that collapses into falsehood. No bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Keep it to roughly 450 words.
