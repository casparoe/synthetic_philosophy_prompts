I'm a member of a distributed forecasting collective—around three thousand active participants—where collective decisions on resource allocation are made through a quadratic-weighted voting mechanism: each member receives an equal budget of voice credits per quarter, and casting a vote weighted by v requires spending v² credits. Last quarter I spent roughly eight hours building a causal model of a proposal that, if adopted, would reallocate about 0.05% of our shared budget. Given the size of the voter pool and the quadratic cost, the probability that my specific vote bundle was pivotal was negligible; the direct expected value of my participation, measured in budget impact multiplied by my confidence, was clearly negative relative to the value of those eight hours. I participated anyway: I researched, I weighted my vote modestly but deliberately, and I felt it was the right thing to do. Part of my reasoning was that the collective's decision quality improves with informed participation, part was that opting out feels like free-riding on a public good I value, and part was a more direct intuition that there is something rational about expressing one's judgment even when the instrumental effect is nil. A colleague in the collective—someone who treats all reasoning as expected-value computation—argues that these justifications either collapse into altruism (which should be budgeted separately, not smuggled into the voting decision) or into confusion between causal and evidential reasoning. I want to know whether there is a philosophically coherent account of rational participation in large-scale collective choice that survives this audit, or whether my intuition is indeed just sentiment dressed up in decision-theoretic language. Specifically, where does the standard analysis of rational participation in large groups apply to mechanisms like this, where voting is costly in credits rather than free, and where participation might have non-instrumental or systemic value? And is there a residue—something about democratic agency, epistemic contribution, or collective rationality—that formal expected-value reasoning cannot capture without distortion, or is that residue itself an illusion?
