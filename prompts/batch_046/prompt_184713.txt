Been thinking about Jaspers' four-way split (criminal, political, moral, metaphysical) and whether it can survive generational transmission without collapsing into something absurd. Here's the chain I'm stuck on:

(1) Political guilt is genuinely collective: citizens bear it for state actions they never personally authorized.
(2) Moral guilt is non-transferable: you can only carry it for your own deeds or culpable omissions.
(3) A nation isn't just a population; it's a diachronic institutional agent with legal continuity, treaty obligations, and constitutional identity across time.
(4) So if that agent committed atrocities in 1941, present citizens stand in the same political relation to those acts as they do to a policy enacted last year.
(5) Now, being politically guilty for X doesn't just mean feeling bad; it entails a present-duty to acknowledge, repair, and prevent repetition.
(6) Most people alive today couldn't have stopped 1941, but they can now block denial, support reparations, etc.
(7) If they don't do that, they've made a culpable omission with respect to the inherited wrong.
(8) Therefore they are not just politically but morally guilty for failing to repair crimes they never committed.
(9) Apply this logic consistently: citizens of the US for slavery/Jim Crow, Britain for colonial violence, etc. — basicly every state with a bloody past generates present moral guilt for its current citizens.
(10) But if moral guilt is universalized like this, it loses all discriminative power; you can't use it to guide action if literally everyone is guilty.
(11) So either (3) is wrong and nations aren't enduring agents, or (5) is wrong and political guilt doesn't generate repair duties, or (2) is wrong and moral guilt can be inherited after all.

I really want to keep (2) and (3), but then I'm forced to reject (5) or accept that we're all walking around with universal moral guilt. Where's the slip? Is it a scope error between collective and individual, or does (6) sneak in an assumption that present omission equals past causation? What's the least costly rejection?
