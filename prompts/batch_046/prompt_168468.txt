In my advanced philosophy of language elective—I am a high school junior—I have been discussing deflationary theories of truth with a study partner who read a popular overview and some lecture notes. He summarized the view like this: deflationists, whether in Ramsey's redundancy form or Horwich's minimalism, treat the term true purely as a logical device for semantic ascent, so that calling a belief true never adds substantive content beyond the belief itself, and the entire account is exhausted by the equivalence schema linking the claim that p is true with p itself. From this he drew two stronger conclusions. First, he insisted that deflationists deny truth is a property in any sense whatsoever—not only that it lacks causal or explanatory substance, but that there is not even a redundant logical property, because redundancy still presupposes something about which to be redundant, and the schema reveals nothing is there. Second, he argued that since the schema offers no explanation for why particular beliefs match reality, deflationism removes any legitimate description of scientific inquiry as aiming at truth; progress must be explained in purely causal or predictive terms, and anyone who speaks of science seeking true theories is smuggling in a metaphysically loaded correspondence relation that deflationism has eliminated. He also alluded to prosententialism as a related development, though I could not tell whether it belongs inside the deflationary family or outside it. I suspect he has collapsed distinctions that careful defenders keep apart—perhaps between minimalism and redundancy, or between explaining belief-formation and explaining a property of truth—but I do not know exactly where the missteps are. Could you audit this summary? Please distinguish what is accurate to standard deflationary positions, what is a subtle conflation or overstatement, and whether his conclusions about scientific aims and about properties follow from the accurate parts. I am not asking for a bibliography or citation list; I need only an accuracy check I can use to correct my understanding before our next discussion.
