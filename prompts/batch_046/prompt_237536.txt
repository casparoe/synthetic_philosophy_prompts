context: i write the onboarding guide for a small remote-work co-op that sends people on 3-month stints to tokyo, berlin, mexico city, and mumbai. i update it every quarter. the users are smart but not philosophers; they have maybe ten minutes before leaving the apartment. stakes are medium -- getting it wrong creates real social conflict, but treating every preference as sacred makes people anxious and condescending.

please build the flowchart in words: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with 'no obligation', where it stops with 'yes, you're bound', and where it has to punt to human judgment because it's about values not facts. i also want you to build in my niche framing called 'exit-cost asymmetry' -- basically the idea that the moral weight of a convention scales with how hard it is for the affected local to exit the interaction without harm. put that in step 2 or 3, but don't let it swallow the whole procedure.
