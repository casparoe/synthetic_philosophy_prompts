Hey, I'm drafting an essay on whether cosmological fine-tunning arguments are really about physics or just about the way we write equations. I think people conflate two different things: tuning that actually affects whether life can exist (like the carbon resonance thing) and tuning that only shows up because we're comparing bare parameters to Planck units in a specific parametrisation of the Standard Model.

My rough notes:

- The Higgs mass problem: quantum corrections want to push the bare Higgs mass up near 10^19 GeV, but the renormailsed value is 125 GeV. People call this fine-tuning.
- But bare parameters aren't observeable. They're just artifacts of how we split the Lagrangian into free and interaction parts. So is this really a physical mystery, or just a feature of our maths?
- The cosmological constant is different: the observed vacuum energy density is physically tiny (~10^-120 in Planck units), regardless of parametrisation.
- I want to argue that multiverse explanations are overapplied: they make sense for life-sensitive constants but are unnecessary for formalism-sensitive ones.
- Counterpoint: even renormailsed couplings run with energy scale. If the Higgs self-coupling is ~0.1 at low energy, that's still a specific number that needs explaining.
- Another counterpoint: if we reparametrise everything in dimensionless ratios, we still end up with small numbers like the electron-to-Planck mass ratio.
