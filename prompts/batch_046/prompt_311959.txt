hey—novelist here, working on a scene where a character gets paralysed by an ethics loop. she argues: (1) the moral weight of harming a group should be the probability each member is sentient multiplied by how many there are—call it "expected suffering-subjects". (2) walking across grass crushes thousands of insects, and with 10^19 insects alive even a 0.001% sentience rate means any outdoor action touches enormous expected harm. (3) saving one definite human child from drowning only prevents one definite harm. (4) therefore running through a meadow to reach the kid probly creates more expected moral damage than it stops. (5) extend that and farming, building, or even breathing becomes catastrophic on expectation. (6) she knows thats absurd—she's not going to stop breathing—so one step must be wrong. she wants to keep the idea that numbers and probabilities both matter, but she needs to know where the reasoning actually breaks. is it wrong to aggregate probabilities across different kinds of beings? or does obligation only attach to definite subjects, not expected ones? whats the cost of each fix, and its not clear which one she should pay.
