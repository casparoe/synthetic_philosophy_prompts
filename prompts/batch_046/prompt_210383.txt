Rate the following five interpretations of Quine’s stance on moral values—(1) eliminativist error theory, (2) non-cognitivist projection, (3) pragmatic acceptance confined to the home conceptual scheme, (4) evolutionary by-product lacking epistemic standing, and (5) ontologically relative but locally indispensable—on a scale of 1 (poor fit) to 5 (excellent fit) for coherence with his naturalized epistemology, ontological relativity, and the continuity of science with common sense. Then rank them from best to worst fit. For each rating, justify briefly whether Quine’s omission of moral predicates from his regimented canonical notation is best read as a metaphysical demotion or merely a pragmatic simplification.
