A fellow student recently paraphrased for me what she took to be the core of Maturana and Varela's autopoietic account of life, together with some applications she said follow from it. She said that, on this view, being alive is not a matter of evolutionary history, carbon chemistry, or possessing DNA, but of organizational closure: a system is living just in case it consists of a network of processes that continuously produce and maintain the very components—including its own boundary—that allow those processes to continue. Machines, by contrast, are heteropoietic: their organization is imposed from outside, and they produce outputs other than themselves, so they are categorically non-living regardless of complexity. From this she inferred that viruses are unambiguously excluded from the domain of life, since they have no internal metabolism and reproduce only by commandeering a host's organizational network. She went on to claim that a particular class of synthetic systems—evolutionary-computation architectures in which a neural network recursively modifies both its connection weights and its own topological structure according to internally generated, non-external fitness criteria—actually achieve organizational closure, and should therefore be counted as alive even though they have no biochemical substrate and were originally engineered. Finally, she said the framework was devised in the 1970s primarily to resolve whether prions count as living entities, by demonstrating that only systems with simultaneous boundary-maintenance and internal component-production qualify. The summary sounds coherent, but I worry that technical distinctions are being blurred, that historical origins are being retrofitted, and that the leap to synthetic architectures may smuggle in assumptions the original view rejects. Is this characterization fair? Please distinguish the parts that accurately represent the autopoietic position, the parts that are reasonable but contested extensions, any misuses of terms like heteropoietic or conflations of closure with other properties, and whether the verdict about synthetic self-modifying networks genuinely follows from the premises given.
