(1) Tranquilism: only the absence of craving and suffering is intrinsically good. presence of joy, knowledge, love, whatever, is at best instrumentally good if it prevents future suffering.

(2) Define a "null-agent state" N: total reward-tampering so that there is zero affective tension, zero preference formation, and also zero cognitive processing of any kind—basically the brain is in a stable loop of non-experience, but there is literally no suffering or craving occuring.

(3) In N, the absence of craving/suffering is total and permanent.

(4) So by (1), N is maximally intrinsically good, and in fact it's the global optimum—nothing else can improve it, since there's nothing left to improve.

(5) Now consider that for any actual person P, there is a possible world where P is in N right now. The transition to N doesnt cause suffering (it's seamless) and prevents all future suffering.

(6) So for every actual person, there is an available action (inducing N) that achieves the global optimum for that person, with no countervailing intrinsic cost.

(7) Therefore tranquilism implies we have an all-things-considered obligation to induce N in ourselves and everyone else immediately.

(8) But (7) is insane—if true, moral life collapses into a single button-press, and the idea that we should preserve culture, relationships, or even consciousness itself is just sentimental error.

(9) So one of (1)-(6) must go.
