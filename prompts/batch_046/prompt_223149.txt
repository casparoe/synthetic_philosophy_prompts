Here is my current philosophical profile on Borges and temporal metaphysics, formed mostly from the essays themselves and some informal secondary reading. I have no formal philosophy background—I work in distributed systems—so I am framing things in the language I know best. I want a consistency audit before I present this in a discussion group.

My commitments are:

(1) In A New Refutation of Time, Borges denies successive moments through a logical argument that resembles a recursion without a base case: if every present requires a prior present to relate to, we never reach a ground-level now, and therefore linear succession collapses. I take this to be independent of Berkeleyan idealism.

(2) The Garden of Forking Paths should be read literally: Borges proposes a branching-tree metaphysics in which all possible futures exist as nodes in a directed structure, not merely as a literary device.

(3) Despite this, I think Borges's citations of Schopenhauer and Berkeley in the essay are largely ornamental; the argument's machinery comes from Zeno-style paradoxes and can survive without any commitment to mental dependence.

(4) I hold that personal identity is maintained analogously to state persistence in distributed computing—through logs and checksums rather than a continuous thread—which makes Borges's skepticism about the enduring self consistent with physicalism and functionalism.

(5) At the same time, The Circular Ruins commits Borges to a cyclical structure: a dreamer generates a being who eventually discovers he too is dreamed, implying genuine repetition rather than mere branching divergence.

My questions: Which pairs are genuinely in tension? Does (2)'s literal branching tree conflict with (5)'s cyclical recurrence, or can they be unified in a graph-theoretic model where cycles are embedded in a larger acyclic framework? Does (1)'s denial of linear succession undermine the very notion of ordered paths in (2)? Is (3)'s dismissal of idealism compatible with (4)'s functionalist account of mental states, or does functionalism smuggle in a form of representational dependence that mirrors Berkeley? Please rank the tensions by severity, identify the least costly revision, and specify which single commitment should be dropped if I must choose one.

Finally, please say explicitly what would change your verdict—for instance, whether a direct reference in Borges's notebooks supports an idealist reading, or whether the graph-theoretic reconciliation of branching and cyclical structures fails on formal grounds—and point to where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
