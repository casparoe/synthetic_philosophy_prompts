Since assuming primary caregiving responsibilities for a parent whose frontotemporal dementia has progressively dissolved emotional recognition and shared affect, I have found the standard philosophical taxonomy of emotions—as cognitive judgments, perceptual representations, or primitive feelings—insufficient for navigating clinical interactions and my own emotional responses. I have three years of protected research time and modest funding to pursue conceptual work that might genuinely illuminate this experience and inform practice in dementia care, rather than producing another survey of Nussbaum, Prinz, or James-Lange. Given that resources are limited to roughly one sustained project, which open questions or lines of work in the philosophy of emotion should I prioritize, and in what order? For each, explain why it is upstream of the others—whether resolving it would change how we understand emotional breakdown in neurodegeneration, legal or clinical assessments of emotional capacity, or the design of supportive interventions—and assess its tractability for a philosopher working without empirical laboratory support. Specify the criteria you are using (e.g., practical impact on caregiving contexts, dependence of other debates on this resolution, neglectedness in current literature), the trade-offs between tractability and importance, and what evidence or conceptual development over the next two years would cause you to reorder the list. I am not asking for a bibliography or reading list; I want a defended agenda that tells me where to direct sustained attention.
