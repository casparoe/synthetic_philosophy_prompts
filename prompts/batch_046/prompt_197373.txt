I've been reading philosophy of mind for fun - not a student, just interested - and I keep noticing something in how people talk about split-brain cases. You know the classic setup: left eye sees an image, right hemisphere controls the left hand, and the person can't say what they saw but can point to it. People use this to argue consciousness splits in two. But then someone always adds "well except the hemispheres still share subcortical pathways," then "except in the actual surgical cases they often left some connections," then "except the task design might be artifical." Each fix is reasonable, but together they turn a dramatic result into something that can't really prove or disprove anything - yet writers still drop the case in like it's a slam dunk for whichever side they're on. I don't have a word for this. It's not just an auxilliary hypothesis because it's not defending a theory, it's slowly defanging a counterexample by piling on individually fair qualifications. Does it have a name? How is it different from just being careful with evidence, and where exactly are the edges - when does qualifying a study become making it useless? If there's no term, what should we call it?
