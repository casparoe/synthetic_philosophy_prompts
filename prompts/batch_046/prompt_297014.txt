After a long argument with a friend about whether Frankfurt-style cases genuinely defeat the principle of alternative possibilities, I have tried to formalize exactly where I stand, and I am worried the commitments do not fit together. I accept the classic Frankfurt counterexample: an agent can be morally responsible even when she lacks an unconditional ability to do otherwise, provided she acts for her own reasons and an external backup mechanism stands ready but never activates. I also hold that responsibility requires sourcehood—the action must flow from the agent's own practical reasoning rather than being traceable to an external controlling will. At the same time, I think determinism is compatible with responsibility, because a deterministic causal chain running through one's psychology is not an "external controller"; it is just the agent being who she is. Yet I also want to maintain that if an external agent had installed a continuously monitoring device in my brain, physically coupled to my mental states and poised to intervene the instant I deviated but never actually firing, then I would lack sourcehood and therefore not be responsible—even though I acted on my own reasons and the device never activated. I try to distinguish these cases by saying that in standard Frankfurt scenarios the intervener is only "counterfactual"—present as a mere possibility rather than actually coupled to my mental states in the real world—whereas the monitoring device is actually coupled. I further think that in cases of preemptive overdetermination, if two sufficient causes exist but only the internal one operates, responsibility remains intact, which seems to support the view that a non-operating external cause should not undermine sourcehood. Finally, I want to preserve a conditional form of the ability to do otherwise: I could have done otherwise if my desires or reasons had been different, even if given my actual psychology I could not; I take this to be compatible with rejecting the robust, unconditional PAP that Frankfurt targets. Do these commitments hang together? Specifically, is my distinction between "counterfactual presence" and "actual-world coupling" coherent once we recognize that Frankfurt interveners are also physically present and causally poised? Does my acceptance of determinism sit comfortably with my rejection of the continuously coupled monitor, given that both involve actual-world causal structures outside my present reasoning that are sufficient for my action? And is my conditional-ability clause genuinely distinct from the robust PAP I reject, or does it smuggle back in precisely the modal requirement that Frankfurt undermines? Please audit these tensions: rank them by severity, indicate for each whether it is a real conflict or only an apparent one, and say what the least costly revision would be—whether I should abandon the coupled-monitor intuition, refine my notion of sourcehood, drop the conditional-ability claim, or adjust my treatment of determinism.
