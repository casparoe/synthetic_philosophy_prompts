everyone in my philosophy of mind group keeps saying phenomenal consciousness is like the thermal bloom off a running engine — real, measurable, but causaly inert. they use it to defend epiphenomenalism: the brain is the engine, qualia are just waste heat, and our talk about "feeling pain" is just another mechanical vibrtion, not caused by the heat itself. but then there's that old paradox about phenominal judgment — if my belief that i'm in pain is just more engine noise, how does the heat ever get into the report? audit the analogy for me. what's the correspondence table — engine parts, combustion, thermal cameras, the driver? where does it break, especially around the idea that we can "read" the heat signature? does it smuggle in that consciousness is observable from outside, or that judgments are always causal? and does the conclusion — that epiphenomenalism is harmless because we never needed consciousness to cause our judgments — actually survive once the disanalogies are on the table? or is the analogy just hiding the problem?
