Theres a weird asymmetry in how some AI labs run ethics reviews for RL training runs. When an agent gets hit with large negative rewards — clipped penalties, aaversive feedback, whatever — the board asks about "agent distress" and wants value-estimate logs. But when the agent just gets sparse positive rewards and keeps failing, they call it poor convergance and never ask if the absence of reward is also harmful. What philosophical picture of suffering or well-being is smuggled in by that practice? Does it commit you to thinking reward signals are literally valenced experiences, or can it be explained just by anthropomorphizing math? And if you actually believe these agents can suffer, shouldn't missing an expected good hurt just as much as getting an active bad? Or does the practice only cohere if suffering requires a direct aversive input, not just deprivation?
