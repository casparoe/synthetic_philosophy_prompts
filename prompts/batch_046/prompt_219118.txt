I'm trying to identify a specific escalation of the Boltzmann-brain worry that I keep running into in cosmological reasoning, and I don't know whether it has a distinct name. The familiar version holds that in a sufficiently large or long-lived universe, thermal fluctuations should produce far more disembodied conscious brains with random false experiences than evolved observers with veridical ones, so finding ourselves with coherent memories should count against such cosmologies. But I keep noticing a further step: the fluctuation need not stop at a single brain. It could produce a brain together with an entire surrounding environment—books, fossils, digital logs, other apparent people—all arranged in a consistent but false history. The threat then isn't merely "I might be hallucinating right now"; it's that the very evidence I would cite to show I'm not a fluctuation—the coherence of the historical record, the consistency of other observers' testimony, the evolutionary continuity of life—might itself be part of the same accidental configuration. This seems structurally different from a standard brain-in-a-vat scenario, because in the vat case there's usually an external reality that could in principle correct me, whereas here there is no outside check: the archive and the observer are co-fluctuations. Is there an established term for this particular variant—the self-undermining archive rather than the isolated brain? If not, how should it be distinguished from the ordinary Boltzmann-brain objection and from Cartesian skeptical scenarios, and what would be a precise name and boundary conditions for it?
