I'm in a course on 17th century phil and we keep circling back to Spinoza's democracy argument. But I'm stuck on a tension I cant shake. Spinoza also thinks every human action is fully determined by prior affects, and that reason is always just one affect among others, never fully in charge. The view I'm holding is that Spinoza's democracy is conceptually unstable: he needs reason to have real causal weight for democratic deliberation to be valuable, but his metaphysics makes reason always subordinate to passion. What would count against this? I don't want the standard "he addresses this in chapter X" reply—I'm asking what would actually have to be true for my instability claim to fail. Which textual moves or conceptual distinctions in Spinoza could genuinely save the compatibility, and which ones would just be patching?
