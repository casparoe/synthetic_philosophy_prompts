I'm on the appeals pannel for our uni's academic integrity board. There's a case from engineering where students have, over about five years, evolved an unwritten convention: they share draft problem sets in a shared drive, everyone corrects them, then each writes up their own final version. The formal rules call this unauthorised collabaration. The students argue it's a Lewis-style convention — an arbitrary but stable coordination solution, sustained by common knowledge and mutual expectation, with no cheating intent. Half the panel wants strict enforcement so the code stays predictable; the other half thinks evolved norms that actually solve real coordination problems should be recognised, or we just undermine trust for no reason. Draft the board's memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with reasons the faculty could actually respond to, and an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side. Keep it practical, not a philosophy essay, but don't ignore that they're basically invoking convention theory.
