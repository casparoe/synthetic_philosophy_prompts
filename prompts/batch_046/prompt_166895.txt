Specify the full desiderata for a theory of interpersonal utility comparison intended to ground distributive judgments across agents whose welfare is constituted in structurally heterogeneous ways—where, for example, one agent’s well-being tracks informed preference-satisfaction, another’s is hedonically constituted, and a third’s is anchored in objective-list goods—without collapsing these into a single metric by definitional fiat. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or conceptual pressure that makes it load-bearing rather than optional: include the distinction between ordinal intersubjective ranking and cardinal aggregation for social choice; the prohibition on overriding an agent’s own evaluative framework absent an independent, non-circular justification; consistency with the non-identity of welfare and revealed preference or experienced utility; and the requirement that the comparison remain silent on intrapersonal trade-offs it has not itself resolved. Indicate which of these demands are genuinely necessary for adequacy in this domain and which are merely inherited prejudices from welfare economics or moral philosophy—such as the assumption that all welfare must be representable on a common interval scale, or that interpersonal comparison requires full intersubjective accessibility of qualitative states. Then identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding existing candidates: is it the refusal to treat utility as a fungible currency, the demand that heterogeneous welfare substrates be preserved rather than translated, or the constraint that the theory not presuppose a unified theory of personal identity across the agents compared? If the list reveals that some subset of desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly, since that tension itself is a result. You may briefly score standard contenders—preference-utilitarian aggregation, Harsanyi-style expected-utility social welfare functions, Sen’s capability framework, and pluralist objective-list approaches—against your criteria, but keep the conceptual weight on the specification itself, because the deliverable is the standard of evaluation, not the ranking.
