Consider the view I will call dynamical-lock compositionalism: material objects are composed of parts if and only if those parts are coupled into a single dynamical system governed by one global equilibrium condition, so that there is no independent fact about parts existing outside the integrated causal profile that binds them. On this account, a scattered collection of particles fails to compose anything not because of a mysterious restriction on spatial arrangement, but because without a shared attractor or coupled dynamics there is literally no system for parts to be parts of; conversely, even apparently disparate entities can compose one object if they are jointly constrained by a unified dynamical law. Play this position straight: what would genuinely count against it? I want an honest map of its exposure. Which empirical discoveries—about quantum entanglement spanning macroscopic distances, about decoherence boundaries, or about the causal structure of biological maintenance—would be decisive rather than merely awkward? What formal results in mereology or in the metaphysics of causation would threaten to collapse dynamical-lock compositionalism into either unrestricted universalism or into eliminativist nihilism? Are there intuitive counter-cases—perhaps artifacts with no internal dynamics, or temporary coalitions that share an equilibrium only momentarily—that its defenders would themselves concede as damaging, and can the view absorb them by qualification without becoming unfalsifiable? Furthermore, what predictions does it make that rival restricted-composition views (for instance, those anchored in biological life or in spatiotemporal continuity) do not, and does that give it risky empirical commitments? Finally, say explicitly what argument or observation would change your assessment: where is your reasoning most likely to fail, and what would convince you that this position is either well insulated to the point of emptiness or genuinely vulnerable in ways its proponents have not acknowledged?
