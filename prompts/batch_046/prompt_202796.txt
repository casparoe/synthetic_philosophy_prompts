I think Benacerraf's dilemma is worse for structuralists than people say. Everyone knows the von Neumann vs Zermelo problem, but if you extend the point to arithmetic being realizable in different categorical or type-theoretic settings—where the same structure can be instanciated with genuinely different internal properties—then structuralism's "we just care about the pattern" reply starts to look arbitary. The pattern itself seems multiply realizable in ways that arent clearly equivalent. So platonists lose unique objects, and structuralists lose a unique pattern. I want to know what would count against this stronger version. Which formal results or philosophical moves actually defeat it, versus which ones just paper over the arbitrariness? Rank the defeaters: maybe a proof that all categorical realizations of PA are equivalent in the relevant sense, maybe an argument that internal differences dont touch arithmetic content, maybe just accepting arbitrariness as harmless. And be honest—has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability? If structuralists concede the multiplicity but deny it matters, does that save them or concede the point?
