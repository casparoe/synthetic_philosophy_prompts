If the self is genuinely nothing more than a center of narrative gravity—without any hidden Cartesian substrate—then total retrograde autobiographical amnesia should not merely damage a person but dissolve the gravitational field entirely, leaving behind only an empty cognitive substrate. Once every narrative thread is severed past the autobiographical event horizon, there is no center left to experience the gap; the post-amnesia individual is therefore not a wounded continuation of the original, but a new center of gravity that happens to inherit the same biological and legal coordinates. Given that framing, wouldn't a deliberately engineered false autobiography—one optimized for maximal narrative coherence—actually generate a competing self with legitimate moral claims on the body, rather than being reducible to mere deception? In other words, if narrative gravity is all there is, why should we privilege the chronological first story over any later, more gravitationally stable fiction?
