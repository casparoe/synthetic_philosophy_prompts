I'm auditing a graduate seminar on the ethics of collective memory and historical responsibility, and during a discussion session a fellow student offered what seemed like an overly streamlined comparison of Avishai Margalit's and David Rieff's positions. According to this account, Margalit defends a categorical moral duty to remember historical atrocities, arguing that any failure to preserve the memory of victims is equivalent to a second abandonment and that this obligation binds every member of a political community with uniform force, irrespective of personal or communal ties to those who suffered. Rieff, by contrast, is presented as advancing a straightforward inversion: he allegedly holds that all collective remembrance is psychologically toxic and politically destabilizing, that societies therefore possess an active duty to forget in order to achieve reconciliation, and that remembrance is at bottom only an emotional reflex lacking any genuine moral significance. From this, my fellow student concluded that once Rieff demonstrates the absence of therapeutic value in institutional commemoration, Margalit's normative framework collapses entirely, leaving no reason to preserve historical memory except private sentiment.

Something about this framing feels wrong—particularly the disappearance of Margalit's well-known distinction between thick and thin relations to the past, and the reduction of Rieff's historically grounded skepticism about commemorative politics to a blanket denial of remembrance's value—but I am not sure which distortions are substantive and which are merely stylistic simplifications. Could you evaluate the fairness of this summary? Please go through its main claims and indicate what a careful reader of these authors would accept, what they would correct or qualify, and what is a misrepresentation serious enough that a defender of either view would reject it outright. I am especially interested in whether the conclusion that Rieff's practical objections defeat Margalit's ethical argument actually follows, or whether it depends on the very conflations you've identified in the summary itself.
