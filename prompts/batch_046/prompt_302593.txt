Exam question:
Two Bayesian agents, R and S, share a common prior distribution over a parameter θ ∈ [0,1] and are commonly known to be rational. They observe independent draws from a binary signal. Agent R models the data-generating process with a state space Ω_R = Θ × {0,1}, including an unobserved binary confounder C that she takes to mediate the signal; Agent S uses Ω_S = Θ, with no confounder and a direct θ-to-signal edge. Both repeatedly and truthfully announce their posterior expectations E[θ | observation history]. A student claims that Aumann’s agreement theorem guarantees R and S must eventually converge to the same posterior expectation for θ, regardless of the structural disagreement about C, because they share a prior and have common knowledge of rationality. Evaluate the correctness of this claim, explaining what conditions the agreement result actually requires and whether the divergence between Ω_R and Ω_S affects its application.

Student answer:
Both R and S begin with the identical prior P(θ), which satisfies the common prior assumption. Because they are rational and have common knowledge of this rationality, Aumann’s agreement theorem applies: they cannot agree to disagree about the value of E[θ]. Even though R includes an extra variable C in her state space, this is merely an internal representational choice; the shared prior over θ and the common-knowledge condition are sufficient for convergence. The theorem ensures that once their announced posteriors are common knowledge, they must coincide. If they do not converge, then by the agreement result at least one agent must be violating rationality or misreporting her beliefs. The presence or absence of C in the causal graph therefore does not undermine the convergence result, since both agents are conditioning on the same observed signal sequence and updating on θ alone.
