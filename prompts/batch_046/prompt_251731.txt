I am setting the final examination for an upper-level metaphysics seminar on causal properties and modal fragility. I want one essay question on dispositions, finks, and masks that students cannot answer by simply recounting Martin's electro-fink example or Lewis's redefinition of the dispositional predicate. To force original analysis, the question should introduce the following constructed case: a synthetic polymer, call it Compound X, possesses a chemical structure that, by its intrinsic nature, tends to oxidise rapidly in oxygen-rich environments; however, in all actual conditions it is encased in a continuous, externally maintained electromagnetic field that suppresses electron transfer, so the oxidation never occurs. The field is not part of the compound's internal structure, but it is always present in any environment where the compound exists. Students should be asked to determine whether Compound X genuinely lacks the disposition to oxidise (because the field is part of the conditions of manifestation), whether it possesses the disposition but is continuously masked (with the field acting as an interfering factor analogous to a suppressive mental-state blocker), or whether this continuous suppression forces us to abandon dispositional essentialism in favour of a purely categorical account of chemical properties. The essay should require them to: distinguish precisely between finkishness (the disposition is lost upon the approach of the triggering conditions) and masking (the disposition remains but its manifestation is blocked); apply this distinction to Compound X without conflating the electromagnetic field with an internal structural modifier; evaluate whether a dispositional essentialist can accommodate perpetual masking without rendering the disposition empirically empty; and address whether the analogy to mental-state masking—where an agent's belief or desire is present but behaviourally suppressed—strengthens or weakens the dispositionalist position when transferred to chemical properties. Please provide the exam question itself, a model answer of approximately 700 words, and a marking scheme that separates first-class work from upper-second work by specifying the exact conceptual moves required: correct articulation of the fink/mask distinction (5 marks); accurate application to the synthetic compound case without category errors (5 marks); critical evaluation of essentialism under continuous masking (5 marks); nuanced treatment of the mental-state analogy and its limits (5 marks); and a conclusion that resolves rather than restates the tension (5 marks). The total should be 25 marks. The question should be answerable without reference to specific texts or citations, but should reward students who understand the structural logic of the debate rather than those who merely recall the standard examples. Write in British English and avoid asking for bibliographies or reading lists.
