Draft an adversarial-collaboration summary between two camps on the common prior assumption in rational belief formation. The first camp, call them the Unificationists, holds that rational agents who share all relevant evidence and reasoning capacities must converge on a single prior, and that persistent disagreement therefore traces either to hidden informational asymmetry or to a failure of rational updating. The second camp, call them the Pluralists, denies that rationality mandates a unique prior, arguing that priors are partly constituted by conceptual frameworks, normative weights, or ontological commitments that can differ without irrationality even under full disclosure of evidence.

The deliverable should be a joint document both sides could sign. Include: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about Bayesian conditioning, belief merging, and the formal conditions under which disagreement is stable; (2) the precise statements of disagreement, each phrased in wording its own side would accept; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving one in either direction would move at least one camp; and (4) for each crux, a concrete hypothetical case or argument both sides agree is relevant, with each camp’s explicit prediction about what the outcome should be if its view is correct.

In particular, one crux should concern whether indexical or self-locating information—for example, an agent’s knowledge of its own computational architecture or its position within a branching decision structure—legitimately fractures a common prior without amounting to a hidden evidential difference. Another crux should concern whether observed persistent disagreement in expert forecasting domains (such as macroeconomic projection or long-range climate modeling) is better explained by residual information asymmetry or by incommensurable structural priors. For each crux, specify a test or observation both camps agree would bear on the issue, and record each side’s anticipated result in advance.

Format the output as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table that lists each crux, the Unificationist position, the Pluralist position, and the agreed-upon evidential test together with both predictions.
