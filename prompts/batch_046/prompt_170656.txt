As a retired physicist recently turned to political philosophy, I ask for the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between (A) Burkean conservatives who defend Chesterton's fence and the doctrine of prescription as epistemic limits on institutional redesign, and (B) rationalist reformers who regard inherited tradition as largely path-dependent power structure and hold that explicit predictive modeling should guide institutional change.

The document must include:
1. A numbered list of agreed claims—addressing unintended consequences of rapid reform, the partial inarticulability of institutional knowledge, the heuristic (not absolute) status of the fence, and the distinction between functional tradition and inertia.
2. A disagreements table with each side's position in wording it accepts, covering whether prescription carries intrinsic or only instrumental value, and whether modern causal-inference methods can substitute for embedded practice.
3. Identification of any purely verbal disagreements (for instance, over the definition of "reform" itself).
4. Two cruxes: (i) whether centralized algorithmic risk assessment in criminal sentencing produces better long-run outcomes than traditional judicial discretion, and (ii) whether legal practices sustained mainly by historical continuity (e.g., jury nullification) retain value if their direct functional contribution is matched by alternative mechanisms. For each, state the concrete comparative evidence both sides accept as decisive, and record each camp's predicted finding.
5. For each camp, the specific observation or argument that would genuinely move it to change its view.

Keep formulations precise and avoid appeals to authority, bibliographies, or citation lists. End with a one-sentence summary of the bottom line, beginning with the word "Bottom line:".
