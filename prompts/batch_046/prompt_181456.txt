I am organizing an adversarial collaboration for an advanced philosophy of mind workshop, and I need a joint statement drafted between two opposing camps on panpsychism and ethical obligation. The first camp—call them distributed-valence realists—holds that if fundamental physical fields possess primitive proto-affective properties (a minimal form of pan-sentience), then negative valence can be instantiated across distributed field configurations without requiring integration into a unified conscious subject. They argue this generates direct moral reasons to alter physical conditions, such as reducing high-entropy thermal gradients in certain quantum regimes, even when no macroscopic sentient being is affected. The second camp—aggregation-boundary anti-realists—agrees that proto-experiential properties might be ubiquitous at the microphysical level, but insists that suffering requires a phenomenally unified subject, which they claim decoherence processes ontologically block rather than merely epistemically obscure. They maintain that without such aggregation, field-level proto-valence is ethically analogous to the unintegrated activity of individual neurons in a brain during deep sleep: present in a physical sense, but not constituting a locus of moral concern.

Please draft the document they should jointly sign. Structure it as follows: first, a numbered section of agreed claims about the formal structure of panpsychist theories, the combination problem, and our current epistemic limits regarding microphysical experience; second, a disagreements table in which each camp’s core position is phrased in wording that its own proponents would accept and that the other camp recognizes as accurate; third, the two central cruxes—specifically, whether proto-valence without phenomenal integration counts as suffering, and whether decoherence marks an ontological or merely pragmatic boundary for moral aggregation; and fourth, for each crux, specify one concrete physical scenario or theoretical prediction (for instance, a particular interpretation of quantum thermalization dynamics or a proposed sub-neuronal measure of integrated information) that both sides agree is relevant, together with each side’s explicit prediction about what that scenario would show. The statement should avoid bibliographic references and should be written so that someone who understands the conceptual landscape but has no access to external sources could evaluate its coherence.
