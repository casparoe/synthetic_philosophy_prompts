Write an essay on the gender-equality paradox and its philosophical implications. The empirical pattern is well-known: in societies that rank highest on institutional gender equality—strong anti-discrimination law, universal childcare, and generous parental leave—occupational segregation by gender often increases rather than decreases, with women clustering in care-oriented and people-oriented fields and men in technical and object-oriented ones. Your task is to weigh three competing interpretations of this divergence. First, the "constraint-release" view holds that formal equality simply allows biologically influenced preferences to express themselves without economic coercion. Second, the "preference-architecture" view holds that cultural gender norms do not disappear with structural equality; instead, they reshape choice under conditions of abundance, producing what looks like free preference but is actually socially scaffolded inclination. Third, the "taxonomy-artifact" view argues that the paradox is partly a statistical illusion produced by how occupations are coded and how part-time versus full-time work is categorized across different welfare regimes.

Having weighed these interpretations, reach and defend a specific verdict: does the paradox undermine justice-based arguments for gender equality, or does it reveal that equality should be understood in terms of opportunity structures rather than outcome profiles? In particular, address whether a normative framework centered on "structural liberty"—the absence of formal barriers and economic penalties for non-traditional choices—can coherently accept aggregate gender divergence in career patterns without collapsing into a defense of the status quo. Your essay should move beyond empirical summary to engage the conceptual tension between autonomy-centered and pattern-centered conceptions of justice, and it should conclude with a clear position on whether egalitarian policy should aim at convergence of choice or at the neutrality of the institutional conditions under which choices are made.
