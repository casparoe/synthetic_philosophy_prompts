I'm preparing a defense of my Berkeleyan position for a philosophy seminar and want a consistency check on the package below. Here are my commitments:

(1) I accept idealism in Berkeley's sense: only minds and their ideas exist, and esse est percipi holds.
(2) I reject the master argument—I think I can genuinely conceive of a tree existing in a forest with no observers present, and perhaps with no observers whatsoever.
(3) I regard clear and distinct conceivability as a fallible but genuine guide to metaphysical possibility.
(4) I hold that "matter" as an inert, mind-independent substratum is not merely unknown but conceptually incoherent—empty in the way "square circle" is.
(5) I explain the stability and regularity of physical objects through divine volition ordering ideas into law-like patterns, not through material causation.
(6) I am broadly skeptical of arguments that move from "we cannot conceive X" to "X is metaphysically impossible," since our cognitive limits rarely track modal boundaries.

Go through these and tell me: which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and for each real tension what is the least costly revision? In particular, do (2) and (3) together undermine (1)? And does (4) sit comfortably with (6), or am I implicitly relying on the very inconceivability-to-impossibility inference that (6) rejects? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each specify which single commitment—(1) through (6)—would need to be qualified or dropped to restore consistency.
