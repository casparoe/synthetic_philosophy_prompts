I want to stage a participatory exercise that makes the logical tension in Newcomb's problem felt in the body rather than just explained: participants should experience why choosing both boxes seems rational from a causal standpoint yet appears self-defeating from an evidential one, and they should reach that discomfort themselves before anyone names the paradox. To avoid the usual textbook simplification, I want to build in a specific twist: the “predictor” is not omniscient but operates with an openly stated 80 percent accuracy rate and, crucially, reveals its confidence interval for each individual player right before the choice is made. Could you design the full session—group roles, round structure, payoff scheme, materials, the debrief that connects lived moments in the game to the causal versus evidential reasoning distinction, an honest forecast of what will collapse or confuse the first time I facilitate this, and a frank admission of which feature of the original paradox this imperfect-prediction framing can never reproduce?
