For a graduate metaphysics seminar, construct a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on the Identity of Indiscernibles as tested by Black's spheres and by one non-standard extension case. Begin by mapping the logical space of answers before naming positions. Start with this root commitment: "A universe can contain exactly two macroscopic spheres that share all qualitative intrinsic properties and stand in no asymmetric relation to anything else." From there, each branch node must be a concrete case judgment or metaphysical claim that a respondent could answer yes or no to without knowing which philosophical label it leads to.

The tree must contain exactly five leaves: (A) a pure relationalist who saves the principle by appealing solely to inter-sphere relational properties and denies intrinsic haecceities; (B) a primitive-identity theorist who accepts bare numerical difference as sufficient for distinctness; (C) a modal eliminativist who denies that the symmetric scenario is metaphysically possible; (D) a symmetry-broken-by-embedded-observer view that treats distinction as perspectival rather than ontic; and (E) one additional standard or hybrid position of your choosing that genuinely occupies a different node-combination from A-D.

Inject the following non-standard branching case: a rotationally symmetric twin universe in which two macro-objects lack not only intrinsic differences and external relational asymmetries, but also any absolute spatial orientation, so that a global swap produces a state that is not merely indistinguishable but structurally identical in every relational and spatiotemporal respect absent any embedded reference frame. Use this scenario as a forced split between those who permit non-qualitative individuation and those who must deny the world's possibility.

Requirements: (1) Build the tree from the logical combinations first—list which answer-patterns are permitted, then place A-E in that space, and identify at least one unoccupied combination, diagnosing whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. (2) Flag any pair of leaves, especially among A, B, and your choice for E, that the tree's diagnostic questions cannot genuinely separate, explaining whether the difference is rhetorical, ontologically substantive but invisible to these cases, or collapsed by your injected symmetric-universe condition. (3) Limit the tree to at most six decision nodes, phrase every node as a direct question about the case, and keep the audience at the graduate-student level—no bibliography, citation, or named-author scorecard is required, but each leaf should be described by its structural commitments rather than only by its traditional name.
