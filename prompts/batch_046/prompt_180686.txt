I'm auditing a graduate seminar on moral responsibility this term, and in our discussion section a classmate offered what she called a clean reconstruction of the standing-to-blame literature's treatment of hypocrisy. She summarized the view as follows: philosophers who discuss hypocrisy and blame are really tracking two separate capacities—standing to blame others, and standing to be blamed oneself—and they only claim that hypocrisy undermines the second. Once those capacities are separated, she argued, it becomes clear that a hypocritical agent retains full standing to blame someone for a wrong, provided the wrong is not identical in kind and not embedded in the same relational structure; for instance, a legislator who privately evaded taxes can still legitimately blame a constituent for welfare fraud, because the domain of tax evasion differs from welfare fraud, and the legislator-constituent relationship is not the peer-like relation in which her own failure occurred. From this she concluded that accusations of hypocrisy in public moral discourse are almost always a red herring: they may damage moral authority or audience credibility, but they never actually defeat standing, so critics should stop treating hypocrisy as a reason to dismiss a blame.

She presented this as the mainstream view, but something about it feels like she has turned a nuanced position into a blanket license for hypocrites. Before I push back in next week's session, I want to check whether I'm reacting to a real misrepresentation. Is it accurate to say the literature sharply separates standing-to-blame from standing-to-be-blamed in this binary way? Does the domain-and-relation matching condition really work as she describes—protecting standing whenever the wrongs or relationships diverge—or is that an overstatement of a more modest similarity requirement? And does her conclusion follow? Even if standing technically survives, might hypocrisy still carry independent normative weight—say, by undermining the fairness of the blame, by altering the meaning of the blaming act, or by giving the blamed party a legitimate grievance—that makes the accusation more than a red herring? Please separate what in her summary is a defensible reading of the literature, what is a simplification or conflation, and whether her final claim survives correction.
