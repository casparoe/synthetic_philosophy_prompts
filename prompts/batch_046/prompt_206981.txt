I'm developing a research program that reads Hegel's master-slave dialectic primarily as a structural account of how recognition collapses under conditions of radical power asymmetry, rather than as a genetic narrative about the emergence of self-consciousness. Within this framing, I'm struck by what seems to be an undertheorized consequence: the slave's labor generates a form of mediated objectivity — a practical knowledge embedded in the transformation of the material world — that the master, who only consumes and commands, structurally cannot acquire. This raises a cluster of open questions that I think are genuinely unresolved, but I'm unsure which deserve sustained philosophical attention and which are either settled by Hegel's own text or conceptually dead-ended. Specifically: first, whether institutional mechanisms of recognition — legal status, citizenship, or organizational membership — can substitute for the direct intersubjective recognition that the dialectic describes, or whether they necessarily reproduce the master's position; second, whether the dialectic's demand for life-risk can be reconstructed as a form of epistemic vulnerability without literal mortality, and if so, whether this weakens the normative force of the slave's eventual claim; third, whether the framework can be extended to entities that perform labor-like transformation without possessing consciousness, and what this would mean for the normative conditions of recognition. If you were advising a small research group with three years and limited resources, which of these lines should be prioritized? I'm not looking for a survey; I want a defended ranking based on which problem is most upstream — that is, which resolution would most reshape our understanding of the others — together with an honest assessment of tractability for philosophers working without empirical methods, and a clear statement of what conceptual breakthrough or counterexample would change your ordering. I'm writing a grant proposal and need to justify where the effort should actually go.
