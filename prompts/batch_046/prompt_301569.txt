Write a villanelle—nineteen lines arranged as five tercets followed by a quatrain, employing two repeating refrains and two rhymes—about Douglas Hofstadter's conception of the self as a strange loop. Focus specifically on the symbol “I” as a self-referential neural pattern that points back to the brain generating it, producing what Hofstadter calls a tangled hierarchy rather than a mere feedback loop. The two refrains must be grammatical mirror-images of one another: one should assert that the symbol creates the speaker, the other that the speaker creates the symbol, so that their cyclical return enacts the loop structurally. In the first tercets, present the intuitive picture of thoughts contained inside a skull, with one particular token—this “I”—standing apart to name its own container. In the middle tercets, introduce the level-crossing: the symbol belongs to the physical substrate yet seems to direct that substrate from above, violating the expected separation between observer and observed. In the final quatrain, allow the speaker to recognize that no hidden core exists behind the symbol; the “I” composing these lines is nothing other than the loop itself. Leave unresolved whether this recognition dissolves the self or confirms its reality. The poem should be precise enough that a reader of Gödel, Escher, Bach or I Am a Strange Loop would recognize the specific distinction between a strange loop and ordinary circular causation, yet it should function as poetry for someone unfamiliar with Hofstadter. Write in American English.
