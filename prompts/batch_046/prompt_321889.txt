I am in my final year of a joint honours degree in Philosophy and Theoretical Physics, and next Thursday I am defending my term-paper thesis to the departmental philosophy of science working group. The audience is precisely this: four scientific realists with strong Quinean sympathies who treat indispensability arguments as default, two structural realists who believe empirical structure can do all the ontological work that needs doing, and one philosopher of physics who regards any talk of “permanent inaccessibility” as nothing more than epistemic modesty dressed up in metaphysical clothing. I am not asking for a bibliography or a reading list.

My thesis is that ontology identification does not merely become underdetermined when information is permanently inaccessible—structurally insulated from any possible observational pathway by the architecture of the theory itself—but instead collapses into what I call referential suspension: our commitments in such domains should be treated as predictive placeholders that carry no ontological anchoring whatsoever. This is not standard structural realism, since I deny that structural correspondence can substitute for referential success; nor is it instrumentalism, because I allow that accessible domains do support full ontological identification. I have sketched the argument using cases drawn from quantum gravity modelling and dark-matter phenomenology, but the core move is conceptual rather than empirical.

Please draft my Q&A preparation sheet. I want the eight objections I will actually face from this specific audience, ordered by how likely they are and how damaging each would be, phrased exactly as a Quinean realist, a structural realist, or that philosopher of physics would put them. For each objection, provide a concise reply that could be delivered in under two minutes, note explicitly where that reply is weakest, indicate what I should concede upfront rather than defend, and include the one question I am hoping nobody asks—namely, the objection that forces me to clarify whether referential suspension is itself an ontological claim about the entities in question or merely a methodological stance, because I have not fully resolved that tension and it threatens the coherence of the whole position.
