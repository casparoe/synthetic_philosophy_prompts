My verdicts on when Gödel's first incompleteness theorem genuinely extends beyond mathematics, and whether any single principle can unify them.

Consider six attempts to carry the theorem into other domains.

First, pure arithmetic. Any consistent formal system with recursive axioms and sufficient strength to represent basic arithmetic contains a true statement that is unprovable within it. This is the canonical result; I take it as correct and unproblematic.

Second, theoretical physics. Imagine physicists eventually construct a final theory T, formulated as a consistent recursively axiomatized system embedding arithmetic—say, through formalization of field values over discrete lattices. Then T must contain physically meaningful propositions about spacetime configurations that are true yet unprovable in T. I think this is a legitimate structural extension: the formal conditions are satisfied, and the interpretation of the undecidable sentences is genuinely physical rather than purely mathematical.

Third, human cognition. One argument notes that for any formal system F a mathematician might employ, the mathematician can recognize the Gödel sentence of F as true; therefore human reasoning exceeds any single formalization F, hence consciousness is non-computational. I reject this. The ability to recognize one specific undecidable sentence for each F does not entail transcending all formal systems simultaneously, and the inference from not being captured by F to not being captured by any formal system is an unjustified universal quantification.

Fourth, macroeconomic modeling. Take a fully formalized dynamic equilibrium model M expressed in first-order logic with arithmetic, intended to be complete and consistent. By Gödel, there are descriptions of economic states that are true but unprovable in M. I reject this application. M is an empirical instrument subject to calibration, revision, and external falsification; it lacks the closure and interpretive autonomy the theorem requires. Its undecidable sentences would be artifacts of formalization, not hidden economic truths.

Fifth, self-referential AI verification. Consider an artificial system S that uses a consistent formal verification engine V, with arithmetic strength, to check all statements about its own source code and future outputs. There will be a statement about S's behavior that is true but unprovable in V, meaning S can never fully verify its own safety with respect to that behavior. I accept this extension. The self-reference is literal, the system is genuinely closed, and the formal conditions are met; the result is technical rather than metaphorical.

Sixth, formal hermeneutics. A scholar encodes the interpretive rules for a canonical literary text into a recursive system H with arithmetic expressiveness—using Gödel numbering of lines and syntactic structures—and argues that H must leave some true interpretations permanently undecidable. I am inclined to reject this, though less firmly than in the economic case. The formal machinery is present, yet interpretation seems constitutively open to extra-systemic context in a way that physical measurement may not be.

My pattern appears to be: accept the extension when the domain involves a genuinely closed formal system with literal self-reference and arithmetic strength; reject it when the formalization is an open empirical instrument, or when the conclusion illicitly generalizes from specific recognition to universal transcendence. But I face two tensions. Cases two and four both involve empirical sciences with formal models, yet I accept one and reject the other on grounds of closure. Is there a principled distinction between physical closure and economic openness, or am I protecting physics because its formal aspirations seem more credible? Meanwhile, cases three and five both involve reasoning about reasoning—human and artificial—yet I reject the cognitive claim while accepting the verification claim. What distinguishes legitimate self-reference from illegitimate universalization?

So I ask: is there a single coherent principle—perhaps involving literal closure, non-empirical status, and absence of meta-systemic interpretation—that captures all six verdicts? State it precisely. Then apply it to two further cases I have not considered: a formalized legal code intended to be complete and self-interpreting, and a biological evolutionary model that uses arithmetic to encode genetic sequences and predict future mutations. For each, derive the verdict. Finally, be explicit about which of my six verdicts the principle cannot accommodate without revision, and whether saving that verdict requires complicating the principle or dropping the case.
