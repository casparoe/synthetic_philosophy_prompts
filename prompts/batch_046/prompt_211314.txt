For the final examination of an upper-level undergraduate seminar integrating philosophy of mind, metaphysics, and applied ethics—scheduled for 90 minutes, comprising one essay question worth 25 marks—please construct an exam prompt that asks students to evaluate a specifically constructed hypothetical argument linking constitutive panpsychism to an ethical crisis at the fundamental physical level. The argument, presented as a novel framing devised solely for this assessment and not drawn from any published source, contends that if experiential properties are genuinely fundamental and instantiated by every physical entity, then quantum-scale interactions involve minimal experiential states; if these micro-states carry even negligible valence, then the second law of thermodynamics—describing the irreversible statistical dispersal of energy—might be interpreted not merely as physical decay but as the aggregate manifestation of countless micro-sufferings, whereas highly ordered, low-entropy structures such as conscious brains represent localized concentrations of experiential coherence that could carry disproportionate moral weight. The exam question should direct students to accomplish four tasks without relying on summary or citation: first, distinguish constitutive panpsychism from emergentist and eliminativist alternatives while addressing the combination problem in a manner that shows conceptual grasp rather than rote repetition; second, assess whether this entropic-suffering framing legitimately extends panpsychist metaphysical commitments or instead commits a category mistake by conflating statistical mechanics with phenomenological valence; third, evaluate whether a functionalist constraint on suffering—according to which suffering requires specific organizational, causal-functional, or integrative properties—can block the ethical crisis without undermining the panpsychist claim that experience is metaphysically ubiquitous; and fourth, defend a reasoned verdict on whether recognizing fundamental micro-experience should oblige a reconceptualization of ethical priority away from macroscopic agents and toward the preservation of low-entropy order.

Alongside the question, provide a model answer that demonstrates the precise philosophical moves required, particularly the separation of metaphysical ubiquity from ethical salience and the interaction between combination-problem considerations and functional constraints on valence. Also supply a marking scheme built around five must-hit points, each assigned 5 marks for a total of 25, that names the specific conceptual achievement each point requires—such as correctly identifying why entropy is analogically rather than literally composed of suffering—and includes a one-line note on the characteristic error that should cause a candidate to lose marks on that point, for instance treating physical disorder as directly constituted by phenomenal distress rather than structurally parallel to it.
