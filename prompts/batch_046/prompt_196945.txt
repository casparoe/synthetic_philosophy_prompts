Consider the following pattern of judgments associated with George Eliot across her essays and fictional portrayals of religious life: she condemns the dogmatic preacher who threatens eternal punishment for disbelief as morally destructive regardless of sincerity; she treats the Broad Church minister who retains Christian ritual and moral teaching while privately rejecting miracles with sympathy, seeing it as preserving communal and ethical continuity; she portrays the individual who, upon losing faith, abandons all religious practice and language as suffering a real loss—not of truth, but of historical connection and emotional vocabulary; she approves the secular reformer who replaces worship with scientific study and organized charity, yet implicitly finds this lacking the aesthetic and collective dimensions religious forms provided; and she presents the artist who reimagines sacred narratives with psychological realism, stripping away the supernatural but retaining moral weight, as performing legitimate religious work.

Now consider this new posture: a philosopher argues that in a pluralistic, post-religious society, communities should invent entirely new "synthetic rituals"—with no historical religious lineage—designed solely to generate collective awe and bind people to shared moral commitments. She holds that participating in traditional rituals without belief is a form of aesthetic deception, whether or not one intends to deceive others; she participates in no rituals herself, traditional or synthetic, but advocates for state funding of these new ceremonies.

What verdict would best cohere with Eliot's pattern: approval, rejection, or a divided judgment? Identify at least two distinct rules that could explain her earlier rulings—one emphasizing continuity with historical religious forms, the other emphasizing sincerity of moral feeling independent of form. State what each rule predicts about the philosopher's proposal and about her refusal to participate. Then specify which of the five earlier cases, if Eliot had actually reversed her judgment on it, would best reveal whether she runs the continuity rule or the sincerity rule. Finally, propose one further case that would cleanly distinguish them.
