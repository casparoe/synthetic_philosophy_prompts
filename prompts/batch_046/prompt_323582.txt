A friend has walked me into an argument about Bentham's sexual ethics and I cannot find the exit. She lays it out like this. (1) Bentham's principle is that rightness tracks aggregate pleasure minus pain, calculated impartially across all affected. (2) Sexual experiences are framing-dependent: the identical physical interaction can yield radically different experiential qualities depending on consent, relational context, and psychological interpretation. (3) So applying the calculus to sexual acts requires comparing subjective experiential states across different persons. (4) But Bentham's hedonism permits comparison only along quantitative dimensions—intensity, duration, fecundity, certainty—while denying that qualitative differences, such as between a dignified and a degraded pleasure, carry independent moral weight. (5) If we apply only quantitative comparison, then an act producing very intense pleasure for one party and only moderate pain for another is permissible whenever the intensity differential is large enough. (6) That seems to license non-consensual acts whenever the aggressor's pleasure is sufficiently intense relative to the victim's suffering. (7) Yet Bentham's own sexual liberalism—his defense of then-condemned practices like homosexuality—depends on the idea that consensual adult acts maximize net pleasure without harming others. (8) So either Bentham's calculus cannot apply to sexual morality without licensing violations, or it applies only by smuggling in a qualitative constraint like consent that his quantitative hedonism does not support. (9) Therefore Bentham's sexual ethics is self-defeating: it undermines the very liberal conclusions he wanted. I would like to keep both the hedonistic calculus and the liberal conclusions if possible, but I feel one of them slipping. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? In particular, is there a defensible way to reinterpret step (4) so that consent functions not as a qualitative boundary but as a quantitative modifier of experienced pain, or does that reinterpretation collapse elsewhere?
