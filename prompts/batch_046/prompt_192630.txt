I'm putting together a little sorting quiz for my philosophy blog about Szasz and the "mental illness is a myth" idea, but I want it to actually sort people by what they think happens in concrete cases, not by whether they check "I agree with Szasz." So: about seven short scenarios — someone forced into a ward for hearing voices, a kid labeled ADHD after one teacher report, grief being coded as MDD in DSM-5-TR, a culture-bound syndrome getting added to the manual, someone voluntarily asking for meds for anxiety, a psychiatrist using a symptom checklist with no brain scan, and a case where a patient insists their "delusion" is actually a religious vision. Each scenario gets three answer options that mix ontology, ethics, and epistemology — e.g. "this is a brain dysfunction that medicine should fix," "this is a problem in living being misdescribed as disease," "this is real suffering but the 'illness' label is mainly a tool for social management." No option should just say "Szasz was right."

Then I need a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions: hard Szaszian (all diagnosis is coercive social control, even voluntary therapy buys into the myth), biomedical realist (Szasz was dangerously wrong, DSM tracks rough but real dysfunctions), critical psychiatry (Szasz nailed the power dynamics and coercion but threw out valid biology too), and a "semantic category-mistake" view where the error isn't denying suffering but treating mental-language as if it named bodily diseases. Also include an unnamed leaf for people who reject psychiatric coercion but still think involuntary commitment is ethically okay in extreme cases — flag that combo as incoherent or at least unexplored.

Build the tree from logical space, not from a list of names: first split is whether the "myth" is ontological (no brain disease here), epistemic (psychiatrists mistake meaning for mechanism), ethical (the category mainly justifies control), or semantic (calling mind-problems "illness" is a Ryle-style category error). Then split by scope: does the critique cover all psychological distress or only severe psychosis? Then by whether voluntary care escapes it. The quiz should flag any pair it can't really separate — like hard Szaszian vs radical libertarian on the voluntary-therapy question — and note unoccupied branches with a one-sentence diagnosis (incoherent? unattractive? just nobody's written it?). Keep it casual, maybe a typo or two, no reading lists, no citations, and don't lean on specific publication years or page numbers. Audience is small philosophy-blog readers; size is seven questions, about five leaves.
