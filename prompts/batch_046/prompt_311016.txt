In the literature on Tolstoy's essay What Is Art? there is a long-standing impasse between two camps: those who read his infection theory of art as a normative, morally loaded criterion—true art transmits only the feelings that unite people in universal brotherhood—and those who read it as a descriptive, psychological claim about any successful transmission of feeling, with the moral exclusions being merely Tolstoy's personal applications. The disagreement has persisted for decades despite agreement on the textual evidence—both sides cite the same passages about art infecting the audience with feeling, and both acknowledge his dismissals of Wagner, Shakespeare, and his own earlier novels. My working hypothesis is that the stall is not about Tolstoy's intentions but about a hidden disagreement over what counts as a feeling in the first place: whether it must be a unified, intersubjective state shareable across classes and cultures (the moral-reading camp) or whether any private emotional response to a work counts, making the theory trivially satisfied by bad art (the descriptive camp). Is this right? Or is the real disagreement about whether a theory of art needs to accommodate the institutional practices of art-making—exhibitions, criticism, education—that Tolstoy explicitly rejects? Diagnose the structure of this disagreement: is it verbal (they use feeling differently), substantive about psychology versus ethics, or are they answering different questions—one about what art is, one about what art ought to be—under the single heading Tolstoy's theory? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress? Would a precise distinction between emotional contagion and moral sympathy resolve it, or is the impasse inevitable because Tolstoy's own text deliberately collapses the descriptive and the normative? I want a diagnosis supported by what each side actually argues, not a survey of interpretations, and an honest answer about whether anything—an archival discovery, a conceptual distinction, a psychological result—could end the deadlock.
