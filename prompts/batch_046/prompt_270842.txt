I am writing a feature on strategic reasoning for a science and ideas publication. The backward induction paradox in the centipede game is usually read as demonstrating that fully rational agents must defect at the very first opportunity, since each anticipates the other’s eventual defection and reasons backwards to the present. Recently, however, I have been considering a modified framing in which the intermediate decision nodes are epistemically opaque: the agents can act, yet they lack the information needed to identify precisely which node they currently occupy, rendering the internal ontology of the sequence partly inaccessible. Given this, does backward induction not collapse? If the reasoning chain requires agents to locate themselves within a specific, identifiable structure, then breaking ontology identification ought to interrupt the induction and allow cooperative play to continue. Is this line of thought sound, or does the paradox survive in some form even when agents cannot map themselves onto the game’s nodes?
