I keep seeing a third pattern in arguments about memory and also in some AI failure modes, and I'm not sure if philosophers have a seperate name for it.

The pattern goes like this. There's a system where event E never left any stored trace at all — no corrupted file, no faint imprint, just nothing. Then, when someone asks about E, the system produces a detailed, coherent story about experiencing it, not by retrieving and altering something, but by generating the account on the spot from context and general patterns. In clinical confabulation this looks like a patient describing a hospital stay that never happened, but crucially they aren't misremembering a real stay; there's no underlying trace to be distorted. People usually try to squeeze this into the Orwell/Staline box — saying either the weights got rewritten or the system fabricated a present experience of the past. But both moves seem to assume there was originally a record that either got altered or faked. Here there was never any record to begin with.
