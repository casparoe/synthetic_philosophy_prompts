In these debates the slogan is used to do very different work: sometimes to argue that self-reference requires a substantial thinker rather than mere functional self-modeling, sometimes to restrict the proof to a present-tense mental event with no enduring subject, and sometimes to license the idea that any being capable of entertaining doubt thereby acquires epistemic privileges that no computational system can share.

I want to bring a disambiguated menu of readings to my advisor rather than treat it as a settled premise. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that this slogan could be expressing, arranged from minimal to maximal? Then distinguish which readings are trivial, which are substantive but defensible, and which depend on disanalogies between biological and artificial cognition that would need independent argument. Finally, identify which reading is actually required for the slogan to serve as a rebuttal to AI first-person claims, and assess whether that required reading survives once those disanalogies are made explicit.
