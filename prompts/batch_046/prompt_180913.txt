I'm running a philosphy book club and we're doing King Lear next month. Read it through Confucian role ethics — not the generic 'family values' take, but the specific view that who your are morally is constituted by performing relational roles (father/daughter, ruler/subject) through ritual propriety, or li. Focus on the banishment scene and the later reunion: Lear treats his father-role like a property right he can trade for flattery, and Cordelia refuses the performative ritual. Is she breaking xiao (filial piety) or honoring it by refusing false li? And does Lear's madness come from losing the role rather than losing power? I want a reading that takes both the play and the role-ethics idea seriously — where does the play dramatize a conflict between roles (ruler vs father) that the Analects hints at but doesn't resolve?
