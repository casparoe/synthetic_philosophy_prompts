Nordic welfare states run on high taxes, high trust, and near-universal benefits, and there's this constant debate about whether you can export the package to low-trust societies. What's less talked about is that those same countries rank at the top for individual autonomy and secular self-expression in cross-national values surveys—so you get heavy redistribution coexisting with radical individualism. That seems like a philosophical tension, not just a policy one. So what view of the self and the state does the Nordic model preseuppose? Is it that security enables autonomy, that tax compliance is social recognition rather than burden, or that citizenship is a non-contractual membership? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether the model actually needs it or could survive with a straight contractarian or communitarian replacement, and tell me what exactly breaks in the export argument if the underlying philosphy isn't about trust levels but about this specific conception of personhood.
