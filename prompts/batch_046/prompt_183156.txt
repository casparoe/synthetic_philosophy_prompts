I was chatting with my study group about Friedman’s F-twist and we kept mixing it up with teh behavioral as-if story, so I figured I should design an exam that forces people to keep them straight. I’m writing the philosophy of science PhD comprehensive for this year and I want one 90-minute essay question on idealization and unrealistic assumptions in scientific models. The prompt should present a hypothetical macro-climate model that treats institutional adjustment to carbon pricing as completely frictionless — no lobbying lag, no bureacratic drag — and ask whether Friedman’s F-twist can defend this, or whether the defense collapses because the unrealistic mechanism is exactly what drives the predictions being tested. Candidates should have to articulate a control principle for unrealistic institutional assumptions that is plausible without being self-undermining, and they need to clearly seperate the F-twist from the behavioral-economics argument that individual agents act as if they maximize utility.

Please give me the exam question itself, then a model answer written in continuous prose, and a marking scheme that describes five must-hit points with marks assigned out of 20 total, plus for each point a one-line note on the typical mistake. Write everything — question, answer, and scheme — as continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, just like an email. And please don’t have the question ask for citations or a reading list, since the students won’t have access to outside sources during the exam.
