Please explain the conceptual structure of iterated elimination via dominance in mixed strategies, but avoid a generic textbook comparison with pure-strategy elimination. Focus instead on the dynamics that arise once strategies are removed: because domination is evaluated over convex combinations of the strategies that remain, eliminating one option can contract the mixture space and alter whether other strategies are dominated in later rounds. Examine whether this yields a unique rationalizable set or rather a family of sets indexed by the elimination sequence, and whether common knowledge of rationality is needed to justify one sequence, or if individual rationality alone is enough. Separate what is settled — such as that rationalizability restricts play to strategies that are best responses to some belief over survivors — from what is genuinely contested, including whether weak domination introduces real sequence-dependence that strict domination avoids, and whether the analysis changes materially for continuous strategy spaces. Do not present a contested view as the consensus.
