I've been going back and forth with a friend about when a legend actually becomes history, using the Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus as our test cases. We both think there's probably some real kernel in each — maybe a raid on a city, some post-Roman warlord, a small group leaving Egypt — but we keep stalling on what would count as proof it's history rather than just a story that got popular. I think it's partly a hidden factual thing about how much archeological silence we should accept, but also maybe we're using 'history' in seperate ways — he means 'exactly happened as told' and I mean 'based on something that actually occured'. Then there's this idea I ran into called 'legendary sedimentation', where real events and later embellishments get layered together so tightly that pulling them apart is impossible without breaking the story. Does that mean the whole debate is ill-posed? Or is it still a real disagreement about standards of evidence? What kind of stall is this actually — verbal, factual, or just bedrock intuitions about how much proof you need? And is there anything, like a new kind of distinction or even a formal model, that could move it?
