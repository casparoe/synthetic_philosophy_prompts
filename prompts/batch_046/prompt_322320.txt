Take the cluster of conceptual problems that grow out of Skolem's paradox: a first-order axiomatization of set theory has a countable model, yet within that model the axioms appear to describe uncountable sets. I am less interested in a historical survey than in a dependency-aware agenda for philosophical work on this cluster over the next several years. Specifically, consider four live lines: (A) whether the paradox undermines realism about uncountable cardinalities, or only shows that first-order formalization is an inadequate vehicle for expressing them; (B) whether adopting second-order logic genuinely restores determinacy, or merely relocates the underdetermination to the choice of semantics; (C) how the Skolemian challenge interacts with contemporary multiverse views of set theory—does forcing and generic absoluteness dissolve the problem, or reproduce it at the level of universe-selection?; and (D) a more speculative framing: treating cardinality as frame-relative in the way structuralist philosophies of mathematics treat other properties, so that "uncountability" is not an intrinsic feature of a set but a relational feature of its presentation within a given model. For each line, evaluate tractability for philosophers without deep technical set-theoretic training, identify which lines are upstream of which, and explain what evidence—conceptual, logical, or from practice in the foundations of mathematics—would move one up or down in priority. Then rank them as targets for a small research group with about three years of effort. I want the reasoning behind the ordering: importance is relative to the goal of clarifying whether mathematics has a unique intended structure, and I want you to flag any cruxes that, if settled, would collapse several other debates at once.
