I serve on the Governance Appeals Panel of the International Society for Formal Philosophy. Our Charter contains Section 9, which governs amendments. Section 9(a) requires a two-thirds vote of members present at a properly called session. Section 9(b) states that this subsection may be amended only by unanimous vote of the full membership, and that any alteration through Section 9(a) is void ab initio. The Charter also incorporates a Reflexivity Protocol adopted in 2019, which assigns every provision a normative order—Type-I (ordinary) or Type-II (self-referential)—and requires that any change to a Type-II provision be executed by a procedure that is itself Type-II and of equal or higher reflexive rank; the Protocol further includes a loop-closure condition prohibiting a lower-rank procedure from altering a higher-rank rule that governs that very procedure.

At a special session last month, a 70 percent majority voted under Section 9(a) to replace Section 9(b)’s unanimous-consent threshold with a three-fourths-majority rule. The session chair validated the vote, treating Section 9(a) as the master amendment mechanism and Section 9(b)’s self-restriction as logically subordinate. A dissenting faction has appealed to our Panel, invoking the classic constitutional self-reference paradox associated with Alf Ross and arguing that permitting Section 9(a) to amend 9(b) collapses the Charter’s hierarchy of normative authority into a reflexive loop. They also contend that, under the Protocol, Section 9(a) is Type-I with respect to Section 9(b), so the amendment violates both the rank-order rule and the loop-closure condition.

The Board must render a decision by next Friday. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—either upholding or overturning the amendment, with an explicit time frame for implementation or remedy—and it should engage seriously with the dissent’s strongest arguments, including both the Ross-style paradox and the Protocol’s loop-closure condition. The opinion should also make explicit what philosophical commitments about legal self-reference, normative hierarchy, and the authority of reflexive rules are at stake, and it should treat the losing position fairly rather than dismissing it. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or reading lists; the reasoning should stand on its own.
