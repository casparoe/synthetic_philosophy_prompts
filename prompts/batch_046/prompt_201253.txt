Popper's falsifiability criterion is meant to solve the demarcation problem by distinguishing scientific claims—those that risk empirical refutation—from pseudoscientific ones that can accommodate any observation. A statistical social-science model, however, assigns non-zero probabilities to every outcome in its sample space, so that no single result is logically incompatible with it. Does this entail that all probabilistic theories are inherently unfalsifiable and therefore pseudoscientific by Popper's lights? Or must we read an implicit supplementary rule into his account—perhaps about comparative predictive risk—that he never explicitly formalized?
