I'm drafting a six-month fellowship proposal in philosophy of science. The core idea is this: the standard dissolution of Goodman's grue paradox appeals to entrenchment, treating it as a historical record of which predicates have actually figured in past successful inductions. I think that answer leaves open why some genuinely novel scientific predicates can become inductively respectable almost immediately upon discovery, long before they accumulate historical entrenchment. So I want to replace the diachronic entrenchment criterion with a synchronic modal one—call it counterfactual stability—under which a predicate is projectible if its application to the observed sample would not reverse under small, nearby variations in what was observed. The project would formalize this notion precisely, apply it to Goodman's emerald and grue cases to show it yields the right asymmetry without circularity, and defend it against the obvious charge that it smuggles entrenchment back in through the choice of similarity metric for nearby worlds. Please turn this into a full proposal containing: (1) a sharp statement of the question, (2) why the entrenchment-based literature leaves it open, especially for novel predicates, (3) the conceptual method—how you would formalize stability and test it against the paradox—along with why this modal route is preferable to pragmatic or linguistic alternatives, (4) what a successful result and a clear failure would each look like, and (5) a realistic month-by-month scope for six months. Do not include a bibliography or literature review with citations; I will supply references myself.
