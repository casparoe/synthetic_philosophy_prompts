ok so i'm drafting a novel about a region trying to break away and i keep arguing with myself about whether the characters have the right to leave, so i wrote down my actual views to check consistency. heres the package:

(1) i take the all-affected principle dead seriously — anyone who's going to live under the laws or suffer spillover effects should have a say in who makes them, which probably pulls the boundary outward across borders and forward in time to include kids who arent born yet.

(2) i'm also Burkean in the Chesterton-fence sense: you dont tear down a boundary untill you understand exactly what job it's doing, and if you cant explain the purpose then you leave it alone.

(3) but i also think secession is only legitimate when the leaving group was never genuinely part of the demos — like they were folded in by conquest or colonial administration, not by any actual democratic pact. in those cases tearing the fence down is just fixing a mistake.

(4) i think a demos needs narrative coherence — it has to be able to tell a continuous story about itself without contradiction — and thats actually what distinguishes a real democratic people from an arbitrary administrative unit.

(5) and democratic legitimacy requires intergenerational stability, so if boundaries shift too easily people cant plan lives or hold institutions accountable across time.

do these hang together? it feels like (1) is pulling the boundary everywhere while (4) and (5) want it locked in place, and (3) looks like it violates (2) since i'm happy to tear the fence for secessionists. but maybe some of that is only apparent? rank the tensions by severity, say for each pair what's actually incompatible and what the cheapest revision would be, and tell me which single change cleans up the most mess. i want an audit, not a lecture — and dont tell me to read more, i just need to know if my own head is straight.
