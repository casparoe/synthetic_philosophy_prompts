I am taking a graduate seminar on consciousness and causation, and the unit on epiphenomenalism has become unexpectedly personal. A few weeks ago, during a brief dissociative episode under sedation, I found myself producing detailed verbal descriptions of bodily sensations that felt as though they were being assembled by mechanisms entirely separate from the sensations themselves. That experience left me with the specific worry that drives the paradox of phenomenal judgment: if consciousness plays no causal role, then the brain processes that generate my report that I am in pain never interact with the pain itself, yet the report claims direct authority over it. To close our final session, I want a poem—specifically a villanelle—that renders this paradox with enough philosophical precision that a specialist would recognize the problem rather than just the vocabulary.

Please write the poem in the voice of a speaker who cannot determine whether they are the conscious subject or their own philosophical zombie twin, the twin who emits identical reports of consciousness without any phenomenal experience whatsoever. Use the villanelle's two refrains to represent, respectively, the physical mechanism generating the report and the phenomenal judgment being made; they should circle through the stanzas without ever causally meeting, so that their repeated proximity highlights the absence of interaction. The form itself should do the philosophical work: the refrains should remain parallel but disconnected through the middle stanzas, then be forced together in the final couplet—not to resolve the paradox, but to show that their co-occurrence is an artifact of poetic structure, mirroring the epiphenomenalist's claim that the co-occurrence of judgment and consciousness is an artifact of physical correlation rather than causal access. Keep it to nineteen lines with the standard villanelle rhyme scheme, avoid turning the closing lines into a sermon or a shrug, and do not attribute specific quotes to named authors; I want the tension left open exactly where our discussion ended.
