Write a poem of exactly fourteen lines in unrhymed iambic pentameter—blank verse—voiced collectively by a three-member institutional review board evaluating a proposed gene-editing protocol. The board confronts the discursive dilemma across three logically connected propositions: that the intervention is safe (P), that it is therapeutically effective (Q), and that both conditions hold jointly (P and Q). Structure the poem in four movements. In the first movement (approximately lines 1–5), present each board member’s individually consistent judgments so that two members endorse P, two endorse Q, but only one endorses the conjunction; internal consistency within each voter should be clear, as should the divergence across them. In the second movement (approximately lines 6–8), stage premise-based aggregation: the board notes majority support for P and for Q separately, applies logical entailment, and is compelled to affirm P and Q together despite that conclusion being supported by only one of the three members. In the third movement (approximately lines 9–12), stage conclusion-based aggregation: the board polls every proposition directly, finds a majority rejection of the conjunction, and thereby severs the logical link between the premises and the conclusion in order to preserve democratic alignment on each line. In the final couplet (lines 13–14), do not resolve the tension; instead, register the structural cost—either the group asserts a conjunction that most members deny, or it denies a conjunction that the premises seem collectively to warrant—letting the measured, unrhymed regularity of the blank verse stand for the procedural order that logic demands but democratic aggregation fractures. Do not cite any particular theorist by name, do not invoke bibliographies, and do not resolve the paradox through narrative closure; the poem’s form and sectional architecture should carry the philosophical idea rather than merely illustrate it.
