student answer:

Wittgenstein's hinge propositions are the commitments that stay fixed while we inquire — things like I have hands or the earth has existed for ages. They don't function as ordinary beliefs supported by evidence; instead, they form the background framework that makes evidence and doubt possible in the first place. That's why Moore's demonstration, holding up his hands and declaring here is one, misses the point entirely: he treats a hinge as an empirical claim needing verification, when it's actually part of the conditions under which verification operates. Ordinary beliefs get revised when data conflicts, but hinges are different — they're like Cartesian foundations, except relocated from private mental states into shared social practices that hold the system together. Because hinges operate like grammatical rules, they don't carry truth-values at all, so asking whether one is false is a category error. Consequently, they can't be revised in the way empirical hypotheses are; at most, the entire form of life could dissolve, which Wittgenstein treats as a logical impossibility rather than a real option.
