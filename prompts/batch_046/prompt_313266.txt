Moore's proof of an external world—specifically, the mismatch between Moore's treatment of his perceptual premise (that he holds up his own hand) as an empirical premise from which the existence of external objects is deduced, and the skeptic's challenge, which targets the entitlement to employ perceptual premises rather than denying any particular conclusion. Then address a reconstructed variant: could Moore's argument be read not as a failed empirical proof but as an implicit reverse-transcendental argument, one that demonstrates that the very practice of skeptical doubt presupposes the existence of an external world in its conditions of intelligibility, without committing Moore to Kantian idealism?
