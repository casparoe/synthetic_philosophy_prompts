Question: The Buridan's-ass paradox is usually told with a donkey starving between two identical hay bales because it lacks any reason to pick one. But Buridan himself never uses that image, and in his ethical commentaries he treats voluntary motion differently. Explain (a) why the hay-bale version misrepresents Buridan's actual concern, (b) how his account of the will handles cases where the intellect judges two goods exactly equal, and (c) whether importing a modern algorithmic tie-breaker (say, random selection) solves the problem more cleanly or just relocates the arbitrariness Buridan wanted to avoid. Be concise but precise.

Student answer (approx 450 words):

(a) The donkey story is apocryphal — Buridan never tells it in his Questions on the Nicomachean Ethics or elsewhere; it was probably cooked up by later critics, maybe Spinoza, then projected back onto him. His real worry isnt animal cognition but voluntary human action, specifically whether the will can move the body when the intellect is stuck in equilibrium.

(b) Buridan solves this by saying the will never actually faces a deadlock. Even when two goods look identical, practical reason can always dig up a secondary feature — proximity, ease, prior habit — to tilt the scale. So there's never true equality in practice; the will just waits until the intellect delivers a clear winner, and if forced, it manufactures a minimal reason out of nothing. This preserves rational agency because there's always some ground, however thin.

(c) A modern coin-flip tie-breaker is actually cleaner, but not for the reason people think. Buridan was wrong to insist there's always a hidden rational factor; sometimes choice is genuinely groundless. The coin flip accepts that groundlessness openly rather than pretending the will creates reasons ex nihilo. So randomization doesnt relocate arbitrariness — it admits it, which is more honest. The only real downside is that it externalizes agency, but that's a feature, not a bug, since it stops us from inventing fake rationalizations.
