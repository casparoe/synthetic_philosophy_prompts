I have been reading for a philosophy of religion course I am taking as a junior, and during a study session yesterday my classmate offered a summary of Freud’s account of religious belief that I found striking but possibly oversimplified. She put it this way: “In The Future of an Illusion, Freud claims religion is essentially a collective neurosis that originates directly in the Oedipus complex, where God functions as the literal father of the primal horde whom humanity murdered and now worships out of unconscious guilt. Prayer and ritual are therefore repetition-compulsions designed to expiate that original crime, and because psychoanalysis makes the unconscious conscious, Freud expected that widespread therapeutic treatment would eliminate religious belief within a generation, replacing it with a rational worldview governed by the reality principle and its economic allocation of libidinal energy toward social cooperation rather than imaginary figures.”

I am inclined to think she is conflating different texts, since I recall that the primal horde story belongs to Totem and Taboo rather than The Future of an Illusion, and that Freud’s argument there has more to do with human helplessness and the need for protection against nature. Nevertheless, she is a strong student and spoke with confidence, so I want to check whether her account captures the core of Freud’s position despite these possible shifts. Specifically, which parts of her summary are faithful to Freud’s actual claims, which are common simplifications or subtle misrepresentations, and does her conclusion—that psychoanalytic therapy would quickly dissolve religion—actually follow from the parts that are accurate? I am not looking for a bibliography or reading list; I simply want to know what to trust in her characterization and where a careful reader of Freud would object.
