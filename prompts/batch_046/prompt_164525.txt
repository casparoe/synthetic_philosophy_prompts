Write a short research proposal (approximately 1,200 words) suitable for a six-month independent study grant. I am a journalist who covers philosophy and its intersection with contemporary practices of knowledge preservation. The proposal should investigate the following question: In Nietzsche's 1874 essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life, he distinguishes monumental, antiquarian, and critical history, arguing that each serves human flourishing only when kept in proportion. Does this framework implicitly depend on an unstated distinction between biological life and what we might call "archival life"—the self-preserving dynamics of institutions, canons, and records? More precisely, can critical history genuinely serve living individuals if the archives it seeks to dissolve have developed normative claims of their own that resist being treated as mere instruments? Existing readings tend to treat the three modes as individual psychological orientations or cultural styles; they rarely ask whether the object of historical study—collective memory—possesses a form of "life" that complicates Nietzsche's call for critical forgetting and digestion. The proposed method is a close conceptual analysis of the essay's arguments concerning memory, health, and the metabolism of the past, deliberately reconstructed without invoking later Nietzschean doctrines such as the will to power. State the question precisely, explain why standard interpretations leave this gap open, describe the analytical steps month by month, specify what a successful result would look like (for instance, demonstrating that the tension between critical and antiquarian history is structurally unavoidable rather than practically manageable), identify what would count as failure (for example, showing that the notion of "archival life" is incoherent within Nietzsche's naturalism), and provide an honest assessment of whether this scope is realistic for six months. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation apparatus; I will supply references separately. The tone should be formal and polished, appropriate for an interdisciplinary grant committee.
