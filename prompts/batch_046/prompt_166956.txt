Please describe the most important implications of interpreting Plato's cave allegory not as a familiar epistemological ladder from sensory illusion to direct knowledge of the forms, but as an account of mediated representation in which the shadows are interface-projections produced by an apparatus with no unmediated exterior, and in which the form of the Good operates not as a transcendent object perceived face-to-face but as the internal coherence-condition that regulates the projection system itself. Focus specifically on what this reading implies for (i) whether escape from the cave can still be conceived without access to an outside reality, and (ii) whether the forms retain independent ontological status or become structural features of representation.
