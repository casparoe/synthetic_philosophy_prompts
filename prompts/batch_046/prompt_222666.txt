As a retired physicist who has lately turned to philosophy of moral psychology, I have been trying to understand exactly where P. F. Strawson draws the boundary between the participant stance and the objective attitude. Would you please rank the following five proposed “exemption conditions”—circumstances said to justify suspending reactive attitudes—in order of how faithfully they preserve Strawson’s original distinction (strongest to weakest)? Assign each a tier (S, A, B, C, D) and provide a brief, one-sentence justification.

1. A global, systematic absence of the capacities for reciprocal moral regard (the classic psychopathy exemption).
2. Immediate, overwhelming coercion that eliminates meaningful choice.
3. Universal metaphysical determinism with no particular psychological abnormality present.
4. Childhood immaturity prior to the development of second-order capacities for mutual accountability.
5. The “entropy-gradient” condition: suspension is warranted whenever an agent’s choices produce an irreversible increase in informational disorder relative to the social system’s predictive baseline, irrespective of the agent’s internal psychological state—a proposal meant to extend the objective attitude into socio-technical networks.
