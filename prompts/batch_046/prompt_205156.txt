I've been chewing on Weber's Protestant ethic thesis for a seminar prep and I think I've accidentally built an argument that either makes culture causally useless or makes every culture an economic engine. I can't find the exit.

(1) Weber claims the Calvinist work ethic caused modern capitalism, not just correlated with it, because it reshaped what agents found meaningful — accumulation for its own sake, not for consumption.

(2) For a value to be a real cause, it must do explanatory work that structural incentives can't do alone. Otherwise it's just an epiphenomenal label we stick onto behaviour after the fact.

(3) So Weber needs cases where agents pursue accumulation even when the structural payoff is negative or neutral — the value makes it obligatory regardless of outcome.

(4) But if that's the mechanism, then any strongly internalised norm that constrains behaviour away from immediate material optimisation counts formally the same. Confucian merchant ethics, Islamic prohibition of riba, even potlatch-style status destruction — all constrain the utility function.

(5) Therefore either culture is causally inert (all those values are just stories about structural positions), or every culture is equally capable of causing capitalism, socialism, or stagnation depending on which norms happen to bite.

(6) Weber wants the middle path: specific values (Protestant asceticism) had a specific elective affinity with capitalist rationalisation, while others didn't. But elective affinity risks being correlation with a fancy name unless we can say why one binds and another doesn't.

(7) If we answer that Protestant values uniquely removed the taboo on profit-seeking, we've described a structural absence (no taboo) rather than an independent cultural cause. The culture becomes the lens through which the structural opening was percieved, not a cause in its own right.

(8) So either (2) is false and values are indeed epiphenomenal, or Weber's thesis collapses into capitalism emerged where social structure permitted it, and people told coherent stories about that permission.

(9) I want to keep culture as a real cause without letting it prove too much. Which step should I reject, and what's the cost? If I drop (3), do I lose the distinction between culture and rationalisation? If I keep the chain, am I forced into a weird pluralism where any value-system can be an economic engine?

Please tell me what would change your mind — which argument or observation would convince you culture genuinely is epiphenomenal, or that Weber's specific claim survives? And be honest about where your reasoning is shakiest; I feel like the slide between (4) and (6) might be where I'm smuggling something in.
