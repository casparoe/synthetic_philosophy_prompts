I'm in a philosophy-of-mind reading group that has just worked through Dennett's discussion of counterfeit people—artificial systems that replicate every behavioral and functional marker of consciousness without, he worries, possessing the genuine inner article. During our debate about near-future AI companions, one member kept invoking the slogan "if the stance works, the counterfeit vanishes." She used it to argue that Dennett's worry is self-defeating: once an AI is indistinguishable by any third-person method, there is no meaningful sense in which it could be a "counterfeit" rather than the real thing, so the category collapses into ordinary personhood. I'm convinced this compresses several distinct theses into one, but I can't pull them apart. Could you lay out the precise principles this slogan might stand for—perhaps about the ontology of mental states, about epistemic limits on discrimination, about the normative conditions for moral status, or about the explanatory completeness of the intentional stance—and for each say whether it is plausible, whether Dennett himself would accept it, and whether it actually dissolves his concern about counterfeit people rather than merely renaming it? I'd especially like you to distinguish a weak practical reading (that we have no actionable reason to discriminate) from stronger metaphysical readings, and to say which reading her argument actually needs to reach her conclusion.
