I'm on an instutitional review board and we keep getting study proposals that claim to knock down basic background assumptions about human agency—like that subjects are genuinely answerable for choices they make in experiments. I want a reusable check, grounded in Wittgenstein's hinge idea, to tell whether a proposal is challenging one of those background hinges or just measuring inside it. Write the steps in order, what counts as enough to pass each, and where the board stops with "approve as normal science" versus "send to philosophical review". Be explicit about which parts are judgment calls dressed up as rules. Apply it to three cases: a deception study that rewrites consent language, a neuroimaging paper arguing subjects never "really" decided, and a case where your check flags it but I think we'd still want to approve. End with what this procedre flat-out can't decide.
