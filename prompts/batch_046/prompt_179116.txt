Read Alfred Hitchcock's Vertigo (1958) through Gottlob Frege's puzzle about identity statements, particularly the distinction between sense and reference that makes learning that one celestial body bears two different names an informative discovery rather than a trivial tautology. Scottie Ferguson falls in love with a woman presented to him as Madeleine, only to learn later that she is Judy, who was paid to impersonate Madeleine; he then attempts to remake Judy into the lost image. Treat the film's central revelation as an identity statement linking two presentations. Does Vertigo present Scottie's realization that the woman he knew as Madeleine and the woman he knows as Judy are one and the same as an epistemic discovery about a single underlying reference, or as the collapse of two irreconcilable modes of presentation? Examine the necklace scene and the flashback sequence: is the audience invited to share Scottie's shock, or does Hitchcock reveal that Madeleine was never an independent reference at all, but a fictional role constructed by Gavin Elster? Push the analysis further. Because Madeleine never existed as a real person, the identity statement involves not merely two senses for one reference, but one actual reference (Judy) and one purely presentational persona (Madeleine). Does this expose a limit in Frege's framework — that identity can link an actual object to a constructed role that never had its own reference — and does the film's tragedy stem from Scottie's refusal to accept that the sense he loved never corresponded to anything real?
