In arguments over modal realism, defenders and critics alike often reach for an analogy to quantum mechanics: David Lewis’s concrete possible worlds are like the branching universes of an Everett-style interpretation. Our actual world corresponds to the branch we inhabit; other possible worlds correspond to branches where measurements resolved differently; modal possibilities are just alternative branches in the tree; and accessibility relations between worlds mirror the decoherence boundaries that isolate branches from one another. The analogy is usually deployed to suggest that modal realism is not wild metaphysics but a philosophical redescription of a physical picture some physicists already take seriously.
