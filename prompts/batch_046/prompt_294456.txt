I have been working through a secular, metaphysical interpretation of karma as a theory of moral desert—treating it not as a religious doctrine about rebirth, but as the claim that moral properties are genuinely causally efficacious in generating proportionate welfare outcomes, where the proportionality is fixed not only by the magnitude of an act but by what I will call its counterfactual moral load: the stability of an agent’s dispositions across nearby modal situations, such that desert is realized through a principle of structural equilibrium operating across non-contiguous temporal stages of a life. On this view, an agent’s welfare trajectory is modulated by the modal robustness of her character, not merely by linear causal chains of reward and punishment. I want a rigorous map of what would count against this position. Please move past the standard objections about the problem of suffering or the lack of obvious empirical correlation between virtue and welfare, and instead identify the defeaters that strike at the core mechanisms: whether the modal-loading component collapses the view into a variant of virtue ethics, whether structural equilibrium can be distinguished in principle from statistical regression to the mean, whether there are formal results in long-run prediction or philosophy of science showing that such equilibrium principles are systematically unfalsifiable, and whether any intuition about personal identity or persistence undermines the claim that desert can be borne across discontinuous life-stages without reincarnation. Rank these from fatal to merely awkward; for each, indicate what a committed defender of this secular karma view would concede as genuinely damaging versus what they would dismiss by refining the notion of counterfactual load or narrowing the scope of equilibrium; say what distinctive predictions this view makes that rival theories of moral desert do not; and assess honestly whether the position has already been qualified into insulation from empirical risk—if so, whether that insulation is appropriate for a metaphysical theory of desert or whether it renders the view vacuous. I would find most persuasive any argument that shows the modal-loading apparatus either does no independent explanatory work or commits me to an implausible metaphysics of cross-world personal persistence.
