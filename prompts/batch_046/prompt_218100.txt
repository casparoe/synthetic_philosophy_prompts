For my novel, I have been reading around Adam Smith’s politics, and a recent essay summarized his view of empire thus: Smith regarded the British colonies as an unmitigated economic drain, advocated their immediate and unconditional independence regardless of local circumstance, and denied that distant subjects could legitimately claim representation, since governance across an ocean was inherently impractical; on this account his argument is purely fiscal—defense and administration costs exceeded any mercantile gain—and he therefore concluded that voluntary free trade with sovereign nations would always surpass the returns of colonial monopoly. The essay presents this as a straightforward cost-benefit case for universal decolonization, with no residual place for strategic interest or for colonists’ rights. I suspect this collapses distinctions Smith actually maintained—perhaps between the American case and other colonies, or between monopoly and empire itself—but I am not certain where the missteps lie. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what a scholar of Smith would recognize as accurate, what subtly misrepresents his position by conflating separate claims or overstating a modest thesis, and whether the essay’s conclusion—that Smith advanced a purely economic argument for immediate universal decolonization—follows from whatever parts are correct.
