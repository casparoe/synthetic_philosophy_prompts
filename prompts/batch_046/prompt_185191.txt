In a philosophy of technology reading group, a colleague repeatedly dismisses debates about artificial consciousness with the line "consciousness is just a user illusion" — apparently meaning that first-person experience is nothing more than a predictive interface the brain constructs for motor control, so asking whether an AI has "inner experience" is like asking whether a smartphone feels its own battery level. I think she is sliding between several distinct theses, but I cannot tell which one is doing the work. Could you lay out the precise claims that slogan could express — for instance, an evolutionary-functional claim about why brains build self-models; an ontological claim that there is literally no subject behind the interface; an epistemic claim that introspection systematically misrepresents the nature of experience; and a design claim that any system with sufficient predictive modeling automatically generates the illusion — and for each say whether it is defensible, whether it actually dissolves the question of AI consciousness rather than just changing its framing, and which version she is probably invoking when she treats the debate as already settled?
