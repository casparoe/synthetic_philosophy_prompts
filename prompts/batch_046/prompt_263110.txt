I am preparing for a philosophy of culture seminar focused on Montaigne's Essais, and my study group has been mapping his explicit judgments about cross-cultural practices in order to see whether they cohere into a single principle. From our reading, he treats the Tupinambá ritual consumption of defeated enemies as less barbaric than European judicial torture, mainly because the former is open and lacks hypocrisy while the latter hides cruelty behind claims of civilization; he regards local conventions such as dress, diet, or footwear as arbitrary expressions of custom rather than dictates of nature, acceptable so long as they do not destroy human fellowship; he denies that societies lacking writing, commerce, or formal law are inferior to civilized ones, since nature suffices without artifice; and he distinguishes sharply between practices that violate transparent natural reason and those that merely differ from European habit. Two interpretive rules seem to fit these verdicts: strict cultural relativism, which holds that no practice may be ranked outside its own context; and a moderated natural-reason standard, which permits open, non-deceitful customs but condemns those that combine cruelty with false universalist pretensions.

We now want to apply this to a constructed case: a present-day intentional community practices "structured forgetting," deliberately destroying all written archives each generation and reconstructing history through oral narrative alone, insisting that this erasure renews collective identity, even though it eliminates verifiable records and is viewed by outsiders as destructive of truth. If we want to remain consistent with Montaigne's track record, should we predict he would condemn this practice, tolerate it as local custom, or praise it as an honest rejection of artificial preservation? Which of the two rules above best captures his earlier judgments, what does each predict about structured forgetting, and what further case—perhaps a society that openly deceives its own members for social cohesion, or one that abandons every custom to live purely by natural reason—would best discriminate between the rules?
