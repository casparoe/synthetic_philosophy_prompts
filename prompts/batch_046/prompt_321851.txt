I've been trying to work out whether Zahavi's handicap idea applies beyond biology—like to whether people actually mean what they say in arguments or politics—but I keep running into cases where my gut says "real signal" for one costly act and "just theater" for another that looks structurally identical. So here are my verdicts, and I want to know if there's a principel that captures them without being too contrived, or if I need to bite the bullet on one.

A researcher who spends three years replicating a study that threatens her career, then publishes the null result: that feels like genuine proof she values truth over status. A politician who takes a stance that costs him major donors and risks his seat: also feels like credible commitment, not posture. An activist who accepts real legal risk through civil disobedience for a cause: same thing, credible. But a grad student who constantly pulls all-nighters and posts about sleep deprivation: the cost is real, yet it doesn't feel like proof of competence or dedication—more like performative suffering. A billionaire buying an obviously useless gold-plated gadget: costly, but signals wealth, not character. Now here's the pair that confuses me. Someone who anonynously donates a huge portion of income to a cause they publicly mock: the cost is massive and there's zero social payoff, yet I don't think it proves secret belief; it could be guilt, tax optimization, or random contrarianism. Meanwhile a peacock growing a tail that literally makes predation easier: classic handicap, clearly signals genetic fitness. So credible signals for me are career-risk replication, donor-alienating politics, legal-risk activism, and the peacock tail. Not credible: all-nighter bragging, luxury waste, anonymous contradictory donation. Is there one principle—maybe about whether the cost is unavoidable given the trait being signaled, or whether alternative motives can explain the cost away? I especially want to know what it says about my stuck case of the anonymous donor, and whether it forces me to say the all-nighter is actually credible after all. Also, predict for a case I haven't listed: a CEO who voluntary takes a 90% pay cut to stay with a bankrupt firm she founded.
