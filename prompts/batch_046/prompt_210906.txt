Consider Aristotle’s distinction in the Physics between potential and actual infinity. Imagine that he were presented with Cantor’s proof that infinite sets possess different cardinalities, and with Hilbert’s Hotel as a thought experiment illustrating the strange properties of completed infinite totalities. How do you think he would have assessed these developments? Would he have regarded Cantor’s transfinite arithmetic as a metaphysically harmless formalism, or as a direct challenge to his claim that infinity exists only as an indefinitely extendable process? And facing Hilbert’s Hotel specifically, would he have treated it as a reductio ad absurdum of actual infinity, or merely as a puzzle about mathematical notation?

Please also address a less commonly discussed contemporary angle: philosophers of physics have observed that quantum field theory employs infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, yet depends on renormalization techniques to suppress divergences that arise precisely from treating those spaces as literally infinite. Some take this to indicate that physical theory accommodates only approximate or potential infinities, never actual ones. Would Aristotle have embraced this as empirical vindication of his potentialism, or would he have insisted that the practical success of Cantorian mathematics in physics remains independent of its ontological commitments?
