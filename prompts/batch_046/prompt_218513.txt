In rationalist and technology-policy discussions, I keep seeing people invoke "multipolar trap" or say "this is just Moloch" as if naming the dynamics closes the case. Most recently, in an argument about whether AI labs bear individual obligations to slow development, someone dismissed the concern with "you're ignoring the multipolar trap," and the conversation stopped there. I'm convinced that phrase compresses several distinct claims, and I want them pulled apart before I use it or respond to it. Could you lay out the precise theses that "we're in a multipolar trap" could be expressing? Specifically, distinguish: (a) a descriptive claim about payoff structures — prisoner's dilemma, tragedy of the commons, or a different collective-action form with distinct exit conditions; (b) a claim about unilateral inevitability — that any single actor who deviates suffers catastrophic loss; (c) a normative excuse — that because all agents are symmetrically trapped, no individual bears special responsibility; (d) a regulatory-futility claim — that norms, rules, or voluntary coordination cannot work, leaving only structural or technological fixes; and (e) a fatalistic or metaphysical claim that competitive dynamics always produce the worst feasible equilibrium. For each reading, say whether it is actually entailed by standard multipolar-trap models, whether it genuinely applies to current AI racing dynamics, and whether it actually supports dismissing questions of individual or institutional responsibility. I want a menu of interpretations with verdicts, not a single defended reading.
