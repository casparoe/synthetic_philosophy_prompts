Some colleges now auto-enroll freshmen in a digital wellness program that sends daily mood-check prompts; opting out requires sitting through a 20-minute module on coping strategies, but the school insists it's still just a nudge since the door is technically open. What view of autonomy and welfare does that instutitional design presuppose? Does it treat student preferences as fixed data to be respected, or as malleable things the institution should help form? And if preferences adapt to what's available — think sour grapes — is there still a clean line between libertarian paternalism and straight-up coersion, or does the practice quietly depend on a view of the self that makes the distinction collapse?
