Since Gödel's modal ontological argument treats necessary existence as just another positive property alongside omniscience and omnipotence, and derives actual existence from mere possibility via the S5 axioms, hasn't he made Kant's critique—that existence is not a real predicate—entirely irrelevant? If so, then for anyone who accepts standard modal logic and the coherence of the property of being God-like, theism appears rationally compulsory, with the only escape routes being to deny that the positive properties are jointly consistent or to abandon S5 altogether. What philosophical resources remain for resisting the conclusion without taking either of those two paths, or does the argument genuinely become decisive once its formal premises are granted?
