Suppose Kant had encountered modern dialetheism and paraconsistent logic—specifically the claim that some contradictions are literally true and that logical consequence should be non-explosive. A present-day philosopher argues that Kant’s First Antinomy, about whether the world has a beginning in time, should be read not as a mere dialectical illusion but as a genuine dialetheia: both thesis and antithesis are true, and transcendental idealism should be reformulated to tolerate this rather than dissolve it into subjective error. She further proposes that relevance-based paraconsistent formalisms can preserve Kant’s restriction of knowledge to appearances, since they block arbitrary inference from the antinomic pair without requiring that one side be false. How would Kant likely respond? Would he regard this as a betrayal of his critical project—given his insistence that contradiction belongs only to dialectical illusion and never to objects of possible experience—or as an unintended technical ally? Address in particular whether his view of the principle of contradiction as an analytic condition on all objects of experience permits true contradictions in the noumenal realm, and whether adopting a non-explosive logic genuinely changes his diagnosis of transcendental illusion or merely renames it.
