me and my friend just had a long arguement about meritocracy and desert and it occured to me there's this exact move i keep seeing. someone defends huge pay gaps by saying people deserve rewards for talent and effort. you point out talent is mostly genetic luck and effort depends on upbringing, so moral desert is weak. then they switch and say its not about desert at all, its about incentives -- society needs big prizes to motivate people. but then if you ask why those prizes shouldn't be taxed away for public goods, they jump right back to 'no, he earned it and deserves to keep it.' the two justifcations take turns shielding each other from attack. its not exactly motte-and-bailey because neither is the 'real' position hiding inside; they're both shaky and borrow strength from each other. is there a name for this oscillation pattern? if not, can you define the edges of it -- what makes it different from plain hypocrisy or just changing the subject? i dont care about a label per se, i want to know exactly what the carving is.
