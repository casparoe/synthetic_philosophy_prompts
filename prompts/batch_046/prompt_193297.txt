In debates about the Principal Principle people often compare deference to objective chance with calibrating a thermomter: you read the instrument (the physical propensity or chancy setup) and adjust your belief-temperature to match. The analogy gets used to argue that if you learn a coin is fair, your credence should be 1/2 in heads even when your other evidence points elsewhere — the chance is an external standard that should override private hunches. Audit it: lay out the correspondance table (thermometer, calibration, broken instrument, room temperature), then the places it breaks — e.g. can credences be 'broken' in the same causal way, is there an analog to heat, what happens if you have two 'thermometers' (objective chance and prior belief) that disagree? And tell me whether the deference conclusion survives once those disanalogies are on the table, or whether the thermometer picture was carrying the whole argument.
