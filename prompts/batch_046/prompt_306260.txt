For my high school philosophy class, I have to write an essay on a bioethics controversy, and I want to make sure my prompt is precise. Please write the essay in American English. The topic is whether brain death or circulatory death should serve as the legal and philosophical standard for declaring a human being dead. Rather than treating this as a simple dispute over which organ stops first, examine the deeper question of whether death is best understood as the permanent loss of the organism's integrative unity—the body's capacity to direct its own biological functioning—or as the irreversible cessation of consciousness and personhood. Weigh the argument that a brain-dead patient on mechanical ventilation still exhibits homeostasis, wound healing, and hormonal regulation, suggesting that organic life continues, against the counter-argument that without the brain's coordinating role, the body is no longer a unified organism but a collection of subsystems sustained artificially. Reach a clear conclusion about which standard better captures the nature of human death, and justify your verdict by addressing whether the persistence of biological processes in the absence of consciousness should be classified as living, dying, or already dead. Do not end on a non-committal note; defend a specific position.
