In my philosophy of langauge reading gruop we've been stuck on whether Quine's "Two Dogmas" actually demolished the analytic-syntheitc distinction or just demolished a particular Carnap-style version of it. The Quine side keeps saying that without a principled boundary you can't separate meaning-change from belief-revision, and that every "analytic" claim can be held come what may if you adjust enough elsewhere. The other side—often drawing on Boghossian-style epistmeic analyticity or two-dimensional semantics—insists Quine attacked reductive definability, not the idea that grasping meaning alone can justify belief. We've been going in circles for months. My working theory is that this isn't one disagreement but two layered on top of each other: one about whether there's a descriptive fact about our concepts, another about whether philosophy should be normative conceptual engineering. Is that diagnosis right? Or is there actually an empirical question hiding in there—say, about how human cognition actually partitions language from belief—that neither side has faced? I want to know what kind of stall this is, what each side is really committed to, and whether anything—an experiment, a formal result, or just getting clear on which question we're asking—could actually break it.
