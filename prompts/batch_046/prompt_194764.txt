Our instructor framed it using Dennett's Orwellian versus Stalinesque revision idea: Orwellian is like the Ministry of Truth rewriting your personal diary so you remember seeing the middle color; Stalinesque is like airbrushing Trotsky out of a photo, where there was never a record of the middle state to begin with. Some people in the group are convinced it's Orwellian because the subjective report feels so vivid and continuous; others insist it's Stalinesque because if you look at the neural correlates there's no sustained activation matching the intermediate hue, so there's literally nothing in consciousness to be erased.

But we've been going in circles for weeks. Everyone accepts the psychophysical results, nobody denies the illusion happens, and yet the Orwellians and Stalinesques haven't budged an inch. So I'm wondering: is this actually a hidden empirical disagreement about whether feature-binding happens before or after conscious access? Or is it verbal, with one side using 'experience' to mean 'phenomenal feel' and the other using it to mean 'cognitively available report'? What if color, motion, and spatial position each have their own temporal integration windows, so there's no one 'moment' to revise — just fragments that get stitched together after the fact?
