Heritability is the proportion of phenotypic variance in a specific population thats due to genetic differences, so h² = 0.8 means 80 percent of the height spread among people in that group is genetic and 20 percent is environmental. This is only a population stat, so you cant apply it to a single person—no one is "80 percent gene-made." But since heritability is high in both Sweden and North Korea, the big gap in average height must be mostly genetic; if environment caused it, heritability in North Korea would be lower because poor nutrition adds environmental variance. Also the breeder's equation R = h²S shows that without selection the mean doesnt shift, so just giving people better food wont change average height if genetic variance is fixed. A reaction norm is the curve showing how one genotype expresses across environments, and for height its basically flat once nutrition is adequate, so heritability is still a stable number.
