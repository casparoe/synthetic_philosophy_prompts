As a novelist who thinks in terms of fabula and syuzhet, diegetic present versus discourse time, and the ontology of manuscript pages that have been written but remain unread, I want you to restate the metaphysical debate between presentism and eternalism entirely within that narrative-theoretic vocabulary. Treat presentism as the thesis that only the events situated in the reader's current narrative present—those actively rendered in the discourse—possess full ontological weight inside the story-world, while past and future fabula-events exist merely as traces, memories, or unrealized possibilities. Treat eternalism as the thesis that every event in the fabula, regardless of its placement in the syuzhet, enjoys an equal, tenseless mode of existence within the work's total ontology, analogous to all pages of a finished manuscript existing simultaneously in physical space regardless of which page is being read. Then deliver the residue report. Identify which metaphysical arguments—such as the presentist's difficulty with cross-temporal relations, the eternalist's reliance on a B-theoretic denial of objective becoming, or the grounding objection against presentist truthmakers—translate cleanly into concerns about narrative coherence, analepsis, prolepsis, and the authority of an authorial plan; indicate which claims survive only with distortion, for instance if physical causation must be replaced by narrative causation, or if the special-relativistic argument against absolute simultaneity must be analogized to the absence of a universal reader-position; and specify whether any element of the debate—perhaps the claim that temporal passage itself is an illusion to be explained away—finds no counterpart in fiction theory at all, and whether that absence points to a deficiency in narrative ontology or to an unnecessary metaphysical addition. I want an honest translation that does not smuggle physicalist assumptions into the vocabulary of fiction, and I want to know whether my intuition that a novel's past is just as real as its present is philosophically defensible or merely a category mistake.
