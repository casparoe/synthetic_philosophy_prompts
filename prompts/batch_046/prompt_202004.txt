I am a doctoral student in philosophy of history studying institutional responses to conflicts between collective memory and the historical record. I want to examine a specific emerging practice—call it “parallel custodianship”—that has been piloted in several post-colonial national archives. Under this protocol, holdings relating to a traumatic and contested event (for example, a colonial-era massacre or forced displacement) are divided into two co-equal curated streams. One stream is maintained by professional historians using conventional evidentiary methods; the other is maintained by a council of descendants and community representatives using the protocols of collective remembrance. The archive displays or provides access to both streams side by side, and its charter explicitly prohibits staff from synthesizing them into a single authoritative narrative or adjudicating contradictions between them. Moreover, any revision to either stream requires joint approval from both the academic board and the community council, and the institution treats this bifurcated structure not as a temporary compromise but as a permanent feature.

What philosophical commitments about historical knowledge, collective memory, and epistemic authority does this practice presuppose? Must it assume that historical truth is plural or incommensurable across social groups, or can it be read as a purely pragmatic distribution of custodial authority that leaves the ontology of the past intact? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguishing what the practice strictly requires from what merely accommodates it well; assess whether its parts are philosophically consistent; and identify points where it is underdetermined, so that rival theories could license the same institutional design. In particular, address whether the practice implicitly resolves the question of which should give way when community memory and the record conflict, or whether it deliberately suspends that question. Please separate what is settled in the relevant philosophical and historical literature from what is genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present a contested view as if it were the consensus. Please write the answer in British English.
