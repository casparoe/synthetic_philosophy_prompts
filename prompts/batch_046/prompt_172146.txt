As a retired ecologist who has recently begun studying moral philosophy in earnest, I want to specify what an adequate theory of our obligations to non-human animals must deliver if it is to remain genuinely Kantian—preserving the view that moral status is grounded in rational autonomy—while also accommodating what comparative cognition now reveals about problem-solving, social learning, and future-oriented behavior in corvids, cephalopods, and cetaceans. I am not asking for a literature review or a list of commentators; I want the full specification first.

Please write the desiderata: the conditions any reconstruction of Kantian ethics regarding animals must meet to count as adequate. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or consideration behind it—for example, the need to distinguish cruelty to a sentient being from damage to an inanimate object, or the need to avoid attributing full personhood to an octopus that demonstrates flexible tool use without collapsing the boundary of rational agency. Indicate which desiderata are non-negotiable within Kant’s framework and which are open to reinterpretation or relaxation. Specifically, examine whether the following three demands can be jointly satisfied: (i) direct moral duties exist only to beings with rational autonomy; (ii) the scope or stringency of our indirect duties toward animals may vary in proportion to their cognitive complexity; and (iii) rational autonomy is not treated as a graded property that admits of degrees. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable given Kant’s metaphysics of personhood, identify the conflict and say which demand must be abandoned—this is the part I most want to see.

Then, briefly, score three familiar positions against your list: the orthodox indirect-duty reading, the revisionist reading that attempts to grant animals direct standing within a Kantian structure, and the strict exclusionary view that denies all moral consideration to non-rational beings. Keep the scoring brief; the weight should be on the criteria, their boundaries, and which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates.

Write the response in American English. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation recommendations—treat the reasoning itself as sufficient.
