I cover the philosophy of science and the history of ideas for a general-interest publication, and I am preparing to interview an unnamed philosopher who defends an unconventional reconstruction of classical Nyāya epistemology. Her central claim is that śabda—verbal testimony—should be treated not as a derivative pramāṇa reducible to perception or inference, but as an autonomous source of knowledge with its own reliability conditions, conditions that change fundamentally when the speaker is a large language model rather than a human authority. She argues that an LLM can serve as a source of śabda-pramāṇa only if its outputs maintain a causally continuous chain back to authoritative human cognition, and that this framework resolves the apparent conflict between Nyāya realism and contemporary information theory.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that avoid biography and generic overview. I need at least one question pressing on how her reconceived śabda avoids collapsing into anumāna given the opacity of model reasoning; at least one on whether yogic perception can provide an independent, intersubjective check on machine-generated testimony; and at least one on what her view implies for the Nyāya analysis of error when a language model produces a convincing hallucination. For each of these three, include a specific follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to offer—if she appeals to causal continuity to defend testimony, ask how she distinguishes legitimate transmission from data contamination; if she invokes yogic perception, ask whether its private accessibility undermines the public verification that Nyāya traditionally requires of a pramāṇa; and if she treats hallucination as a form of error rooted in defective causation, ask whether the absence of intentional deception in the model forces a revision of the Nyāya account of the causes of false cognition. The remaining questions should probe further into her position: for example, how her view handles analogy when analogical reasoning is performed algorithmically, and whether her reconstruction commits her to a form of epistemic externalism about machine outputs. Conclude the sequence with a sharp, substantive question rather than an open-ended invitation for her to add anything else.
