I'm a doctoral candidate in intellectual history preparing a conference appendix on historiographic fallacy, and I need a deadpan satirical artifact for it. Write a single continuous museum interpretive plaque—no framing commentary, no more than 500 words—in the bureaucratic voice of an invented "National Committee for Historical Progress." The plaque should present the history of Western political thought (from classical antiquity through the early modern period) as an inevitable, graded teleological ascent toward present-day liberal democratic norms, evaluated through a fictional pseudo-technical metric you invent called the "Present-Proximity Index" (PPI). Assign each thinker—say Plato, Augustine, Hobbes, and Locke—not contextual analysis but a PPI score: credit for "anticipating" modern rights-language, deductions for "regressive divergence," and annotations marking "latency periods." Keep the tone entirely deadpan and official; never wink, never state the critique outright. Let the internal logic of the grading rubric expose the absurdity—for example, describing feudal hierarchy as an "early draft of constitutional separation of powers," or praising a figure for "accidentally prefiguring" a concept they explicitly rejected. The piece should be immediately legible to anyone familiar with the standard critique of Whig historiography, but it must not depend on obscure primary sources, precise archival references, or verbatim quotations. End with a brief "Visitor Assessment" box inviting museum-goers to rank which historical figure "most deserved" to reach the present. Do not include bibliographies, citations, further-reading suggestions, or requests for source verification.
