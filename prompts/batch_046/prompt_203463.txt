Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between the Structural Inside-View (SIV) camp and the Indexed Outside-View (IOV) camp should produce, on the question of epistemic modesty when first-person reasoning crosses into domains with poor outside-view track records.

The statement must contain four sections, in this order.

Section 1 — Agreed claims (numbered list, at least five). State propositions both camps can sign, covering: the existence of first-person access to reasoning processes; the availability of base-rate data on reasoning errors across domains; the distinction between procedural reliability and substantive correctness; the relevance of domain boundaries to reference-class selection; and the legitimacy of indexical information in selecting comparison classes. Avoid vacuous formulations (e.g., both sides care about truth).

Section 2 — Exact disagreements (table with columns: Issue; SIV formulation of its own position, in wording SIV accepts; IOV formulation of its own position, in wording IOV accepts; Neutral framing of the dispute). Address at least three issues: (i) whether domain-transcendent reasoning—specifically, analogical inference from empirical findings to normative conclusions—is vulnerable to reference-class debunking; (ii) whether procedural transparency about one's own reasoning steps can offset a poor outside-view base rate in the target domain; (iii) whether indexical information (this is my reasoning process) legitimately restricts the reference class for error-rate estimation, or whether it introduces an illegitimate selection bias.

Section 3 — Cruxes and predictive tests (for each crux: the precise sub-question; a concrete hypothetical case both sides agree is probative; SIV's advance prediction about the case; IOV's advance prediction about the case). Identify at least two cruxes. The first must center on procedural transparency: present a case in which a researcher has full introspective access to her analogical mapping from evolutionary biology to a claim in distributive ethics, but meta-analyses show philosophers using such analogies are correct only 35 percent of the time, while biologists using analogies within taxonomy are correct 78 percent of the time. Both sides must agree this evidence is relevant; record whether each predicts that the researcher should maintain, reduce, or suspend confidence in her normative conclusion, and on what grounds. The second crux must center on indexical reference-class selection: present a parallel case where the researcher knows she was selected by a filter that screens for high analogical reasoning skill, and both sides must predict whether this indexical fact changes the appropriate base rate.

Section 4 — Residual verbal disputes (brief paragraph). Identify whether any remaining disagreement is partly verbal—specifically, about whether epistemic authority denotes procedural justification, substantive reliability, or both—and state the resolution each side proposes for that ambiguity.

After the statement, provide a brief structured summary in JSON format with exactly these fields: section_1_claim_count (integer), section_2_issues (array of strings), section_3_cruxes (array of objects with keys sub_question, case_summary, siv_prediction, iov_prediction), section_4_verbal_dispute (string or null), format_check (boolean, always true).

Write in formal, polished academic prose suitable for a philosophy working-group appendix. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or appeals to specific named texts or authors. Where you need conceptual precision that goes beyond generic pretraining recall, rely on the distinctions defined in the prompt itself—domain-transcendent reasoning, procedural transparency, and indexical reference-class selection—rather than on obscure technical literatures.
