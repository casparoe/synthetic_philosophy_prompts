I'm moderating a colloquium interview with a philosopher of economics who defends what she calls the evidential-frame reading of Keynes's account of probability and uncertainty. On her view, Keynes's notion of weight is not a second-order measure of how much evidence supports a judgment, but rather a structural property indicating whether the premises of that judgment form a closed evidential frame that exhausts the relevant possibilities. She argues that Keynesian uncertainty is therefore not reducible to Knightian risk or to imprecise probabilities, because it marks a failure of frame closure rather than a spread of belief over unknown values. She further claims that modern Bayesian treatments of uncertainty miss Keynes's point by treating open frames as distributions over probabilities rather than as conditions that suspend rational belief assignment altogether. Draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that engage the substance of this position rather than asking for summary. I want at least two questions pressing her on Keynes's examples of probabilities that resist comparison or aggregation across different evidential contexts, with a follow-up anticipating her defense that incommensurability is a feature of open frames rather than an objection. Include questions that ask whether her interpretation collapses Keynes's distinction between probability and weight into a single meta-epistemic notion, what decision-making should look like when frames are open and probabilities are genuinely unassignable, and whether the framing interpretation risks being descriptively rich but empirically empty. End with a direct question about what finding in decision theory or formal epistemology would compel her to abandon the evidential-frame reading.
