I was tring to square my intuitions about biological function with the etiological and causal-role accounts, but my verdicts on concrete cases feel all over the place. The heart pumping blood is defintely a function; a broken valve that just makes noise without pumping — the noise isn't. The appendix: I'd say it lacks a real function now, even though it does some immune work, because that wasn't what selection shaped it for. But then there's a gene originally selected to digest lactose that now also regulates sleep in some populations; I want to call both digestion and sleep functions, which already feels more causal-role than pure history.

Here's the weird pair. There's a synthetic gene drive engineered into mosquitoes that suppresses malaria transmission. It never went through natural selection, yet I'd say suppressing malaria is genuinely its function — it was designed for that and actually does it. But then there's a random mutation in a tumor-suppressor gene that accidentally protects against a specific toxin, never selected for. I don't want to call that protection a function; it's just a lucky accident. Meanwhile feathers: insulation was the original function, flight is the exaptation, and both feel like real functions to me.

What about viruses? Replication inside a host seems like a causal function, but I hesitate because it harms the host. And a bacterial plasmid selected for antibiotic resistance in one species that now just sits in another species doing nothing — does it still have that resistance function, or did it lose it?

So is there one principle that saves all these? Or do I have to drop one? If I go pure etiological, the gene drive has no function and sleep-regulation might not either. If I go pure causal-role, the accidental mutation might sneak in, and the appendix's immune role counts too. Could a hybrid work — maybe function requires either evolutionary selection or intentional design for the role, plus actual contribution? Would that cover the gene drive but exclude the mutation, and what does it do to my appendix verdict?
