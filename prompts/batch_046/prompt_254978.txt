For a consistency audit ahead of a workshop on non-classical logics, here is my current package of commitments. (1) I am a dialetheist: I take the Liar sentence to be literally both true and false, not merely paradoxical or lacking a truth value. (2) I reject gap-theoretic, hierarchical, and paracomplete resolutions altogether; there is no ungrounded or indeterminate status—only true, false, or both. (3) I accept naive set comprehension and treat Russell's paradox as expressing a true contradiction; I reason about sets with a relevance-paraconsistent logic that blocks explosion. (4) I nevertheless maintain that classical first-order logic with identity is correct for empirical science and for contradiction-free regions of mathematics. (5) In practical domains—law, ethics, and engineering—I hold that contradictions must always be resolved by revising premises; they should never be tolerated institutionally, even if logic permits them. (6) I deny that the law of non-contradiction is a metaphysical or logical law; I treat it only as a defeasible heuristic for belief revision. (7) I think any adequate paraconsistent logic should satisfy what I call a competitiveness constraint: it must preserve classical inferential strength in contradiction-free contexts, so it should not abandon modus ponens globally but only restrict it locally where explicit contradictions occur.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severely? For each real tension, indicate the cheapest revision and what it costs. Then rank the tensions by severity and identify which single change would clean up the most problems. Write your answer in British English.
