I'm grading papers for my intro to free will course and I'm kinda stuck on one. The exam asked students to lay out Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility — specifically the regress about being responsible for your mental nature, and why being causa sui is supposed to be impossible for any real agent — and then evaluate whether the argument works against libertarian free will or only against determinism.

One student handed in this answer (I'm paraphrasing from thier handwritten sheet, so forgive any roughness):

"Strawson's Basic Argument shows that nobody can be morally responsible because true responsibility requires that you are the cause of yourself — causa sui. The argument sets up a regress: if you're responsible for an action, you must be responsible for the mental states that caused it; then for the mental states that caused those mental states, and so on back to your genes or upbringing. Eventually you either reach something that caused itself, which is impossible, or you reach something outside your control, which means you aren't responsible. The student also says determinism makes the regress infinite, but if libertarian free will is true then an agent could break the chain and choose its own character from scratch, so the Basic Argument only really works if determinism is true."

There's also this weird "recursive self-design" idea my colleague mentioned — imagine an agent that could iteratively rewrite its own preference architecture like updating software, maybe escaping the regress by being the author of each new version. The student nods at this but doesn't really engage with it.

I need a specific grade (letter or points out of 10) plus concrete comments: what's right, what's subtly wrong, and whether the student has conflated determinism with the impossibility of self-creation.
