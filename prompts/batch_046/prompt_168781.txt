Since my sister’s recent stroke left her fluent in social interaction yet apparently devoid of comprehension of her own words, I have become preoccupied with whether competence can genuinely exist without understanding. In that frame of mind, I read an unnamed essay that draws a tight parallel between Darwin and Turing as performers of the same logical inversion. The essay summarizes it like this: Darwin reversed the argument from design—rather than inferring a comprehending designer from the functional competence of an eye or wing, he showed that blind variation and selection can generate identical adaptive competence with no grasp of optics or aerodynamics. Turing, it claims, executed the identical reversal for cognition—rather than inferring internal mental comprehension from the behavioral competence of reasoning, he proved that a universal computing machine can perform any rational task, from chess to mathematics, without possessing any understanding of the symbols it manipulates. From these two cases together, the essay concludes that comprehension is never a necessary condition for competence and is at best an epiphenomenal gloss.

Is this characterization fair? Please audit it precisely: separate what is accurate about Darwin’s actual argument regarding natural selection and teleology, identify where the account of Turing distorts his results in computability theory or his position in the philosophy of mind, and determine whether the claimed “identical inversion” is genuinely structural or instead collapses two different problems—causal explanation of biological adaptation versus epistemic criteria for attributing mental states—into a false parallel. Most importantly, tell me whether the essay’s concluding claim, that these inversions jointly establish the dispensability of comprehension for all competence, actually follows from corrected versions of both positions, or whether it overreaches in ways that matter for my concern about my sister’s condition. I am not asking for a bibliography or for citations of specific texts; I simply need to know which parts of this summary I can trust.
