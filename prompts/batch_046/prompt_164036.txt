I am preparing an essay for my graduate seminar in political philosophy, and during office hours last week a fellow student—whose background is in welfare economics—sketched what he called “the operational core” of Rawls’s argument. According to his account, the veil of ignorance is not merely a representational device but an epistemic filter that strips choosers of every particular: not only social position, gender, and natural talents, but also any capacity for sympathy, moral sentiment, or a conception of the good life. Behind this veil, rational agents apply maximin reasoning not to a restricted index of primary goods, but directly to lifetime utility, authority, and basic liberties treated as fully fungible along a single metric. The difference principle, on his reading, permits any inequality—including in fundamental freedoms—so long as the single worst-off individual in the actual distribution is made absolutely better off than under strict equality. He further claimed that Rawls’s purpose is to show that justice is discoverable without any reliance on a particular moral psychology, since the choosers are pure strategic calculators, and that this proves liberal egalitarianism is the only rational choice regardless of one’s substantive values.

The professor responded by noting that this framing “loses the architecture,” but she was pressed for time and did not specify where. Before I adopt or reject this interpretation in my paper, I want to audit it carefully. Which elements of this summary are faithful to Rawls’s canonical formulation—particularly regarding the lexical priority of liberty, the domain of the basic structure, and the psychological capacities retained by parties in the original position? Which parts are standard simplifications that a charitable reader should correct, and which are misrepresentations that would undermine the argument if taken literally? Specifically, does Rawls allow basic liberties to be traded for economic gains under the difference principle? Is maximin applied to all social goods without restriction? And do the parties behind the veil lack all moral sentiment and conceptions of the good, or is their rationality constrained by a thinner but still present normative framework?
