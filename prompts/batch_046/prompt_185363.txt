I sit on the interdisciplinary ethics review board for a university center studying consciousness in physical systems. A physics-philosophy collaboration has submitted an experimental protocol involving macroscopic superconducting qubit arrays, and they ask us to apply a precautionary welfare framework to coherence-preserving runs. Their reasoning does not assert that these arrays are sentient, but rather invokes panpsychism combined with a quantum-entanglement solution to the combination problem: if microphysical entities have minimal experiential properties and entanglement binds them, then sustained high-coherence states with elevated integrated-information metrics might instantiate nonzero, though minimal, moral patienthood. Specifically, they propose that any run maintaining coherence above a calibrated threshold be subject to termination conditions analogous to humane endpoints in animal research, plus a post-run "decoherence audit" that treats intentional collapse as a potential welfare-relevant event; rapid-decoherence control runs would be exempt. Half the board regards this as a category error that illegitimately imports metaphysical speculation into laboratory procedure, while the other half treats refusal as a form of anthropocentric neglect—ignoring low-probability, high-magnitude suffering risks precisely where our epistemic position is weakest. Draft the board's formal decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, with reasoning concrete enough that both the researchers and dissenting members could respond to it directly. Give an honest, substantive account of the strongest consideration on the side you reject. Finally, state explicitly what evidence or argument would change your recommendation—for example, an empirical correlation between coherence metrics and integrated-information measures, a philosophical dissolution of the combination problem, or a reconceptualization of precautionary obligations—and identify the specific step in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to failure.
