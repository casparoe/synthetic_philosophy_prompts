Before offering your diagnosis, please take a few moments to brainstorm the possible ambiguities in applying a calibration norm across object-level and meta-level beliefs, and to consider whether an infinite regress of the kind described necessarily reaches every belief.

A colleague has walked me into the following argument and I cannot see the exit. The claim is that rational non-dogmatism collapses into self-undermining global scepticism.

(1) If an agent rationally holds belief B with confidence c, and then discovers that an epistemic peer holds B with confidence c' ≠ c, the agent ought to revise c toward c' by some weighted averaging rule.

(2) The correct weighting in (1) — the 'calibration coefficient' κ — is itself a matter about which rational peers disagree (some favour equal weighting, others favour self-trust, others favour suspension).

(3) By applying (1) to the belief about κ itself, the agent must revise κ downward whenever peer disagreement about κ is discovered.

(4) Because κ governs every revision, lowering κ lowers the confidence of every belief revised under it; but lowering those beliefs in turn affects the evidential basis for κ, triggering a further downward revision.

(5) This generates a monotonic descending sequence with limit 0 applied uniformly across the belief system, leaving the agent with confidence 0 in every non-tautological proposition.

(6) Hence any agent who accepts (1) and is not initially dogmatic (confidence 1 everywhere) must become globally sceptical.

(7) Yet global scepticism undermines the premises (1)–(6) themselves, so the position is self-defeating.

My colleague concludes that the only stable positions are dogmatism (rejecting (1) entirely) or irrationality. I want to preserve some form of peer-sensitive revision, but I suspect there is an equivocation between applying the norm locally to first-order beliefs and applying it globally as a recursive fixed-point constraint — or perhaps a modal slide between 'ought to revise' and 'must converge to zero'. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? In particular, does saving (1) require restricting its domain, or is there a more precise error in the recursion from (3) to (5)?
