I run small reflection groups at a community center for adults navigating abrupt identity shifts—retirement, divorce, career collapse—and I am neither a therapist nor a philosopher by training, only someone who read a bit about mind and self and got caught on Douglas Hofstadter’s notion that the “I” is not a fixed inner core but a strange loop: a symbol that refers back to the very neural patterns that generate it. From that, I have started designing two kinds of guided exercises. In what I call mirror prompts, a participant writes or speaks in the first person about present feeling—tightening the loop so the symbol “I” points straight at immediate experience. In echo prompts, they listen to or read something they produced earlier and respond to it as if addressing another person—forcing the self-referential symbol to cross through an external artifact before it loops back. I need a reusable decision procedure I can run in about five minutes before each ninety-minute session, with no clinical license and only my signature on the attendance sheet, to decide for each participant whether to assign a mirror prompt, an echo prompt, or neither. The procedure should take the form of ordered questions—what I observe, what I ask, and what counts as a sufficient answer—together with explicit stopping rules: a clear “stop, assign neither” condition; a point where the decision is one of values rather than facts and I must choose personally; and a default if time runs out. It must handle hard cases without breaking: someone who insists the recorded voice “is not really me,” someone who treats every past statement as sacred and unchallengeable, and someone who cannot distinguish “I feel angry” from “I am anger.” Please include worked applications to three cases—one where the procedure recommends an echo exercise to a participant I personally think needs direct self-contact and I will dislike the result; one where it stops for a values judgment; and one ordinary case that runs cleanly. Finally, be explicit about where the strange-loop framework stops being useful and I am left with ordinary practical judgment, and flag any step in the procedure that is only a judgment call dressed up as a rule.
