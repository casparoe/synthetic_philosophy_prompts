I’ve been thinking about Bradley’s regress and the whole mess around the unity of the proposition, and I’ve landed on this view: the regress only really bites if you accept what I’d call the “ontological packaging” assumption—that for “a is R to b” to count as one single thought rather than just a scattered list of parts, there’s got to be some extra ingredient, call it U, that literally binds the terms and the relation together into a package. Once you drop that packaging metaphor and treat propositional unity as something that shows up in how we grasp or deploy the claim rather than as an internal structural glue, the infinite hierarchy just evaporates. There’s no missing link that needs linking, because “holding together” isn’t a job that needs doing—it’s just what happens when someone understands the judgment. I think this dodges both Bradley’s vicious regress and the weird “super-relation” entities that Russell tried to smuggle in, without collapsing into pure psychologism.

But I want to stress-test this, and I’m not looking for a bibliography or a reading list—just the real pressure points. What would have to be true for this anti-packaging stance to be wrong? Would it take a formal result showing that any account of truth-conditions has to represent syntactic unity at the object-language level? Or a counter-example from semantics where we genuinely can’t tell a real proposition from a random word-list unless there’s an explicit unifier in the ontology? I’d like the defeaters sorted by decisiveness—things that would kill the view outright versus things that are just awkward to live with. I’m especially interested in whether defenders of this kind of position have themselves conceded that some version of “binding” has to sneak back in through the side door, and whether that concession is fatal or just a qualification.

Also, be honest about insulation: has this view been qualified so heavily that nothing could count against it? If every objection gets answered with “that’s still just packaging talk,” is that a feature or a bug? I was discussing this with a friend who studies metaphysics and we got stuck on whether the packaging metaphor is actually unavoidable for explaining falsehood—if there’s no internal binder, what makes the false proposition “a is R to b” one specific false thing rather than a failure to assemble anything at all? So I’d definatly like that addressed too, along with any other arguments I should actually take seriously.
