I've been trying to formalize root-cause analysis in distributed systems through inference to the best explanation. Because software failures are deterministic—there is exactly one true causal chain in the code and configuration—for any competing hypothesis the likelihood of the observed logs seems to be either 1 (if it is the actual cause) or 0 (if it is not). That suggests IBE collapses into pure Occam's razor: we should simply minimize Kolmogorov complexity over the space of possible explanations. Yet in practice, engineers often reject the simplest account—say, a misconfigured load balancer—in favor of more complex ones involving race conditions or distributed-state corruption, treating root-cause analysis as something richer than simplicity-seeking. Is this just a well-documented cognitive bias, or does determinism genuinely alter the structure of abduction? And if IBE really does reduce to simplicity in deterministic domains, shouldn't we replace our debugging heuristics with an explicit algorithmic-compression search over the system logs?
