Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps in comparative memory studies. Camp One — the institutional-exceptionalists — holds that Germany’s Vergangenheitsbewältigung is structurally incomparable with Japan, Turkey, and the American South, because its post-1945 juridical-educational-memorial complex created a self-correcting mechanism of public reckoning that the other three cases either lack or actively suppress; they contend that typological comparison collapses distinct moral-political architectures into false equivalence. Camp Two — the structural-comparativists — holds that all four cases exhibit mnemonic bifurcation: a durable split between official state narratives (closure, heroicisation, or legal denial) and vernacular or subaltern remembrance practices, with the German institutional apparatus representing not an exception but one mode of managing that bifurcation (juridical integration), paralleled by Japan’s bureaucratic compartmentalisation, Turkey’s archival-legal foreclosure, and the American South’s heritage-commercialisation.

The collaboration must produce a single document with the following parts: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section listing propositions both camps accept — concerning empirical patterns of state-sponsored silence and civil-society counter-memory across the four cases; concerning the formal properties of mnemonic bifurcation (without committing to its normative weight); and concerning the epistemic limits of cross-national comparison without primary archival access; (2) a disagreements table that states each camp’s position in wording the other camp would recognise, pinpointing whether the dispute is empirical (do Japan and Turkey possess comparable civil-society counter-memories?), causal (is juridical reckoning sufficient or cosmetic?), or normative (does comparison diminish German responsibility or illuminate shared pathology?); (3) the cruxes — specific sub-questions whose resolution would shift at least one camp, such as whether state-funded memorial-education infrastructure in Germany correlates with lower rates of historical denialism in available comparative survey data, or whether Turkish and American Southern vernacular memory practices depend on transnational juridical leakage structurally absent elsewhere; (4) for each crux, the evidentiary or argumentative tests both sides agree would bear on it, with each side’s predicted outcome recorded in advance. If any portion of the dispute is verbal — for instance, over whether successful reckoning denotes institutional presence or attitudinal change — identify the ambiguity and propose a disambiguated vocabulary both sides could adopt without conceding substance.

Write in formal British English, avoid requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not invoke named scholars or specific published texts unless paraphrased in general terms. Treat the applicability of mnemonic bifurcation as precisely the analytical pivot at stake, and inject any further conceptual distinctions — such as between mnemonic labour (civil-society investment in remembrance) and mnemonic capital (institutional durability across generations) — that help separate genuine disagreement from terminological overlap.
