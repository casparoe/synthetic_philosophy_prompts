After a rather exhausting discussion with a friend over dinner about whether artificial agents can genuinely possess goals that are independent of their physical implementation, I have become convinced of a specific form of embedded agency: not merely that agents are causally situated within the world they act upon, but that the agent-environment boundary is ontologically graded rather than binary. On this view, because any decision-making system is physically continuous with its substrate—whether silicon, neural tissue, or some hybrid—the distinction between an agent’s “true” goals and its merely instrumental sub-routines cannot be sharply drawn; goal-directedness is always relative to a particular framing of where the system ends and the world begins. This seems to undermine the very idea of a formally specifiable utility function that is substrate-independent, since any such formalisation presupposes a boundary that embeddedness denies.

What would genuinely count against this position? I am not asking for standard objections to physicalism or functionalism; I want to know which specific results—perhaps in formal learning theory, robotics, or the philosophy of biology—would expose the view as either incoherent or unnecessarily defeatist. For instance, if one could demonstrate that certain classes of reinforcement-learning agents maintain stable goal representations despite radical substrate substitution, would that be fatal, or merely awkward? Or if empirical work in extended cognition showed that human agents routinely treat external tools as part of their cognitive architecture without losing the coherence of their long-term intentions, does that support my graded-boundary thesis or actually undermine it by showing that porous boundaries are compatible with sharp goal distinctions?

I would also like an honest assessment of whether the position has been insulated by qualification. If defenders can always retreat to the claim that the boundary is “context-dependent” in a way that makes the thesis unfalsifiable, is that a genuine problem for a metaphysical claim of this kind? And which defeaters would the committed embedded-agency theorist themselves concede—perhaps the existence of robust cross-substrate behavioural invariants, or formal proofs that certain goal structures are invariant under physical implementation—versus which they would dismiss as begging the question against physical continuity? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and indicate where the position risks collapsing into a rival account—say, a sophisticated functionalism that simply accepts causal embedding but denies ontological grading—without anyone noticing.
