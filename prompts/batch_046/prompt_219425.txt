I've been trying to follow the zombie/conceivability arguement — Chalmers-style claims that if you can conceive a zombie world then phyiscalism is false, and the physicalist reply that we can't actually conceive them once we grasp the physics. It's been going in circles since the 90s and nobody moves. My own hunch is the stall isn't about consciousness at all but about explanatory transparency: physicalists think a good explanation should close modal gaps, dualists think some gaps are just left open. But I also wonder if there's a hidden split over whether phenomenal knowledge works like direct reference or like a theory-laden observation, which makes both sides talk past each other on what 'conceivable' even means. Is the disagreement verbal, substantive, or are they just answering different questions under one heading? Give me a committed diagnosis — don't lay out every option — and tell me honestly whether anything (a new distinction, an empirical result, whatever) could break the deadlock, or if it's just bedrock intuitions.
