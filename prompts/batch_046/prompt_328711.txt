My roommate was telling me about a fight she had with her brother over whether Churchill should still get statues and honours given the Bengal famine and those racist things he wrote in private letters. She said judging him by modern human-rights frameworks is totally unfair — he was a product of imperial Britain, and applying today's moral standards is like grading a fish on tree-climbing. He said thats a cop-out; Churchill actively diverted food and supplies away from India, and those private letters show he genuinely believed Indians were inferior, not just performing for an audience. She said sure, but he never really had access to the idea that racial equality was true — he was trapped in an information environment he didnt build, so his failure was more like a failure of imagination shaped by culture than unique evil. He said if we let information environments excuse everything, then every dictator is just a prisoner of propaganda and we cant judge anyone. She said theres still a real gap between not knowing something and not caring about it, and Churchill's case is closer to the former. He said thats basically the man of his time defence with extra syllables, and it ignores that some British officials actually objected to the famine policy — outliers who prove a real moral alternative existed. She said those outliers just prove the norm was genuinely constraining, which is exactly why he shouldnt be singled out as uniquely bad. He said having a theoretical alternative in some other guy's head isnt the same as having a live option you can actually take without destroying the war effort, the empire, and your own career. Can you go through thier exchange round by round and tell me who's right about each specific point, and also what both of them are missing?
