Design a classroom activity for a philosophy seminar of about ten postgraduate students, running 75 minutes, that makes the group feel the pressure of denying that 'better than' is transitive across a spectrum — but not through the familiar linear sorites. Instead, arrange the exercise around a circular spectrum: the participants build a closed loop of cases in which each neighbour is pairwise judged indistinguishable or barely distinguishable in value, so that transitivity forces the conclusion that the final case is better than itself. The rules and stakes should be arranged so that the students construct the loop through their own pairwise votes or allocations, discover the cycle only when the chain is closed, and then face a forced collective decision that reveals the cost of blocking the inference at any point. Include concrete roles or voting rules; a timeline; small, non-monetary stakes that actually affect the session (for example, a shared priority ranking that determines the order in which the group discusses texts); a debrief that maps each moment of the activity onto the logical structure of the spectrum argument and onto the options for resisting transitivity; an honest account of what will predictably go wrong the first time it is run and how to recover; and a frank admission of which feature of the philosophical problem — for instance, the metaphysical status of vague boundaries — the activity cannot reproduce and must be supplied by discussion.
