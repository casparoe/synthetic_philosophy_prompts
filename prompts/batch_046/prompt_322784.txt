I am designing a conceptual taxonomy for a graduate seminar on the philosophy of privacy, where students routinely conflate privacy with mere secrecy and are therefore unable to see why one might reject secrecy-based accounts while still defending a robust, non-instrumental conception of privacy. To avoid reproducing the standard textbook triad of control-based, contextual-integrity, and consequentialist positions, I want a decision tree that sorts respondents by their answers to concrete cases rather than by the labels they prefer. The branching questions must centre on four dimensions: whether anonymised aggregate profiling counts as a privacy violation; whether the core wrong of privacy intrusion lies in information disclosure or in predictive asymmetry — that is, in an observer's capacity to model one's future choices with high accuracy without necessarily knowing any particular secret; whether privacy possesses intrinsic value or is valuable only instrumentally for autonomy or democratic equality; and whether privacy obligations apply to collective or group-level data patterns, not merely to individual records.

Please construct the tree with at least six named leaves, including a position that denies predictive asymmetry is a privacy issue at all (treating it purely as manipulation), a position that affirms predictive asymmetry as a privacy violation with intrinsic value, a position that holds privacy has no intrinsic value yet still regards predictive asymmetry as morally problematic for autonomy-based reasons, and a position that extends privacy obligations to collective patterns. Use two concrete scenario nodes: one involving a municipality using anonymised mobile-phone location data to forecast neighbourhood crime patterns without identifying any resident, and one involving an employer deploying behavioural analytics to predict employee resignations without accessing any private communications. Build the taxonomy from the logical space of answers first, then map the combinations onto positions, explicitly noting any unoccupied combination and briefly diagnosing whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of positions that the four questions cannot separate. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.

Write in formal British English. Present the response so that its first sentence states the bottom line — the total number of leaves, the four branching dimensions, and any unoccupied nodes — followed immediately by the tree with reasoning for each branch, then the worked scenarios, with no introductory preamble, no repetition of this request, and no references to specific authors or texts.
