I'm defending a thesis I'm calling the hierarchy-format evolutionary debunking argument, which I developed while working through evolutionary explanations of cognition in a graduate seminar on metaethics. Rather than running the familiar line that evolutionary origins render moral beliefs unreliable in general, I claim that selection pressures shaped moral cognition to employ a representational format—what I term a deference-tracking or coalition-rank format—structurally analogous to dominance and alliance-monitoring representations rather than to descriptive belief-formation. On my view, evolutionary history doesn't falsify moral judgments or undermine their practical authority; instead, it shows that these judgments lack the representational architecture needed for knowledge-apt tracking of mind-independent normative properties, because the format is optimized for rapid social coordination and hierarchical negotiation rather than for error-correction responsive to non-social evidence. The judgments therefore retain practical authority—derived from coordination functions, not representational success—while the realist interpretation of them involves a category mistake.
