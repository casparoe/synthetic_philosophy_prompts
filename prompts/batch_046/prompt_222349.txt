Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, strict ABA rhyme, two repeating refrains—comparing beef and chicken consumption through the lens of three weighed harms: direct farmed-animal suffering, wild animals killed by crop cultivation, and wild suffering displaced by land use. Assign one refrain to the beef-is-worse ledger (fewer direct lives ended, but larger feed-crop and pasture footprints that kill and displace wild animals) and the other refrain to the chicken-is-worse ledger (far more individual lives ended per calorie, each life shorter but of comparable or greater intensity). Let the form do the philosophical work: the refrains should return like recurring objections, and the closing couplet, where both refrains must appear together, should stage their failure to converge rather than resolve the dispute. Keep the meter regular and rhyme strict so the repetition feels mechanical, as if the calculus keeps running without reaching a sum. Do not offer a verdict; leave the tension open in the final lines.
