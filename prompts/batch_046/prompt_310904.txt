After three decades in experimental physics, I have only recently begun studying philosophy of science, and I find the standard demarcation literature curiously silent about narrative structure. Please read Dan Brown's The Da Vinci Code as a case study in pseudohistory and demarcation, comparing its narrative method to that of Fomenko's New Chronology. The novel presents an alternative chronology embedded in hermeneutic loops—symbols and artifacts interpreted through a pre-selected master narrative—while framing itself with claims that its descriptions of documents and architecture are accurate. Identify specific scenes in which the protagonist's reasoning mirrors the selective use of genuine artifacts to overturn established timelines, and ask whether Brown knowingly dramatizes that epistemic failure or unwittingly reproduces it. I want you to take both the plot mechanics and the philosophy seriously: does this case show that Popperian falsifiability misses the core problem, and should demarcation instead attend to narrative circularity and the social transmission of revised chronologies? Conclude with what the novel adds to the debate that a purely methodological analysis would miss.
