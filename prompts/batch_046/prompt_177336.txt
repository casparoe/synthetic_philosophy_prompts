hey, i'm working on a novel where a charcter finds an old manuscript that literally lists the frame, outer, and inner messages of her own life—sort of a metafictionel device i'm playing with—and i got stuck in hofstadter's GEB taxonomy. here's the argument that made me stop writing:

(1) hofstadter says meaning isn't in the outer message (the symbols) or the inner message (what the observer constructs), but in the frame message—the context that tells the observer how to decode.

(2) but the frame message is itself just symbols or a context, so it needs its own frame, leading to an infinite regress unless there's a bottom-level frame that carries meaning without interpretation.

(3) apply this to my book: the frame is the physical novel, my autherial notes, the genre conventions. the outer is the plot. the inner is the reader's interpretation.

(4) if meaning is really in the frame, then anyone who shares that frame—same edition, same language, same cultural background—must derive the exact same inner message, because the frame fully determines the decoding rules.

(5) but readers constantly disagree about whether my charcter's choice is redemption or cowardice, even when the frame is identicle.

(6) so either there is no shared meaning at all (the frame differs for every reader in some hidden way), or the inner message carries some meaning independently, which violates (1).

(7) this feels like it proves too much—like hofstadter's distinction collapses into either solipsism about interpretation or a return to authorial intent as the only real meaning. i want to keep (1) because it feels right for my scene, but i also don't want to deny that readers genuinely disagree. which step is the sleight of hand? if i have to choose, i'd rather drop (4) than (1), but i want to know what dropping it costs for the frame/outer/inner hierarchy.
