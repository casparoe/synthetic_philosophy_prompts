I'm trying to work through global workspace theory and this access vs phenomenal consciousness thing, and I've walked myself into an argument that feels like it should break the theory, but I can't see the exit. Could you answer in British English, please?

Here are the steps I'm stuck on:

1. On global workspace theory, a mental representation is conscious if and only if it's made available globally across cognitive subsytems — language, memory, decision-making, affect, and so on.

2. People usually split consciousness into access consciousness (available for reasoning and report) and phenomenal consciousness (what it's actually like).

3. If broadcasting is the mechanism that produces consciousness, then both kinds depend on it. But they can come apart if broadcasting is directed selectively.

4. So imagine a "silent experience" — a state that's broadcast everywhere except the verbal report module. By 1 it's not fully globally broadcast, yet intuitively there's still something it's like. So phenomenal consciousness can exist without full access.

5. Now imagine the reverse: a state that reaches only the report system and nowhere else — it's reported, but there's nothing it's like. So access can exist without phenomenal consciousness.

6. The real trouble starts with intermediate cases. Suppose a representation reaches about 60% of the relevant subsystems — affect and sensory processing are active, but language and long-term memory are blocked. By 1 it's not globally broadcast, so it shouldn't be conscious at all.

7. But if phenomenal consciousness is produced by broadcasting, and broadcasting comes in degrees, then phenomenal consciousness must also come in degrees. There'd be "half-conscious" experiences — maybe feeling 60% of a headache.

8. That sounds absurd. Phenomenal consciousness seems binary: either there's something it's like, or there isn't. You can't be "sort of" in agony.

9. So it looks like GWT forces us to either deny silent experience is possible (giving up step 4) or accept that consciousness is graded (giving up step 8). Both feel wrong.

10. Which step should I actually reject, and what does giving it up cost? Is there a hidden assumption about how broadcasting works that I'm smuggling in?
