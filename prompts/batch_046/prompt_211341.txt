I've been thinking on my own about those beliefs that seem to sit at the bottom of everything else—not because I've argued for them, but because doubting them would make ordinary thinking impossible. I recently picked up Wittgenstein's On Certainty at a friend's suggestion and recognized the idea that some things stand fast for us, but I have no formal training in epistemology, so I'm trying to map the territory with my own cases and see if there's a hidden pattern.

Here are my verdicts. First, that the earth is very old functions as bedrock for me: I never test it, and if I rejected it I'd have to reject most of science and history together. Second, that I have two hands right now also feels immovable, yet there's a subtle difference—I can imagine being wrong about my hands in a localized, specific way, like discovering a prosthetic I forgot, whereas being wrong about the earth's age seems to require a global collapse of knowledge. Third, that other people have inner lives like mine never gets questioned in my daily life, yet philosophers debate it openly; for me it acts like a hinge, but apparently it doesn't for everyone, so I'm unsure whether hinge status is personal or universal. Fourth, when I'm playing chess I treat the rules as fixed—if I doubted them I couldn't make a move—but I also know rules can be revised between tournaments; this feels like a temporary framework rather than deep bedrock. Fifth, there's the mathematical result I just checked: I treat it as settled enough to build on, but I also know I might have slipped up and could re-verify. So it stands fast for the moment, yet clearly isn't in the same category as the earth's age.

Is there a coherent principle that separates genuine hinges from beliefs that are merely very strong, contextually adopted, or provisionally trusted? I wonder whether I need to allow what I might call local hinges—foundations that are unmoving within a specific practice but recognized as revisable from outside—without letting the category collapse into anything I happen to take for granted. Which of my five judgments should stay, and which ones force me to abandon a simple account? I'd especially like to know what this principle predicts about beliefs I haven't listed, like the reliability of my own memory from five minutes ago, or the consistency of the laws of physics across distant galaxies.
