i run a philosophy meet-up at the library, usually about 10 people, ages all over, we have 90 minutes. i've been thinking about that scene in dostoevsky where the grand inquisitor tells jesus people dont want freedom they want bread and miracles and someone to decide for them. i want an activity that makes us actually feel the relief of giving up choice, not just discuss it.

heres a twist i came up with: call it "epistemic surrender" — the idea that handing over your judgement to someone else feels physically lighter, like dropping a backpack. i want that built into the rules.

design a game or structured role-play for 10 people in 90 mins. they should start with full democratic control over some shared resource or decision, then be offered an "authority" option and a "miracle" option where they stop deciding but get guaranteed outcomes. the incentives should make surrender genuinely tempting. they should realize at the end that they chose comfort over freedom.

then a debrief that maps each round onto the inquisitor's three temptations — bread, miracle, authority — and says exactly where the activity fails to capture dostoevskys point and where i have to fill in as facilitator.

tell me the rules and payoffs clearly, predict what will go wrong (people refusing, or getting too competitive), and be honest about which part of the idea a 90-minute game just cant reproduce. no lectures up front — the rules do the work.
