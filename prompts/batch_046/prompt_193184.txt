I've been helping out with this neighborhood kitchen that's supposed to serve anyone who shows up, but lately things have gotten weird. There was a drought last season so local harvest was bad, but the city warehouse is actually overflowing — they had to turn away a shipment last week because storage was full. The real bottleneck seems to be this new tiered access protocol the board put in place: now you need a verified local address and a sponsor card to recieve more than one meal a day, and a bunch of regulars got cut off overnight. A friend who studied development economics keeps saying this is exactly the pattern from those big historical famines, where food existed but people couldn't get to it — she calls it entitlement failure as opposed to food availability decline. My uncle, who farms just outside town, says that's just academics overcomplicating things: drought is drought, and if people are hungry it's because nature didn't provide enough, not because of some paperwork. I'm not writing a dissertation, I just want to figure out whether I should be pushing the board to scrap the protocol or lobbying the city to send more shipments here. But I also keep wondering — even if we get more food delivered, won't the protocol still block the same people? So is the drought the actual problem, or is the policy? Or do they do different kinds of damage, and how should I think about which one to fight first?
