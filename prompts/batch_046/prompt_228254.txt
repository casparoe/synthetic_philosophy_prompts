Write an essay on whether nested discursive dilemmas in collective judgment aggregation should be resolved by hierarchical freezing or by integrated iterative revision. Imagine a deliberative body that must aggregate individual judgments across three interdependent layers: first, normative priorities (for example, equity versus efficiency); second, intermediate policy targets that are supposed to follow from those priorities; and third, concrete instruments meant to realize the targets. Suppose that applying majority rule to pairwise comparisons produces cyclical preferences at both the instrument layer and the priority layer, so that the body faces a discursive dilemma at each level simultaneously. One proposed remedy is hierarchical separation: lock the priority weights through a preliminary aggregation step, treat them as fixed premises, and then derive instruments deductively, thereby insulating the lower layer from contamination by the upper-layer cycle. The opposing view holds that this freezing violates the autonomy of collective reasoning and suppresses legitimate feedback—if the available instruments are jointly incompatible with any stable weighting of priorities, then the priorities themselves ought to be revised, which requires allowing instrument-level inconsistencies to feed back upward. In weighing these positions, examine whether the logical conditions for consistent aggregation—such as universal domain and collective rationality—can be satisfied jointly at both layers without either collapsing the layers into one another or accepting perpetual instability. Consider also whether hierarchical separation genuinely eliminates the dilemma or merely relocates it to the preliminary vote on weights. Reach and justify a definite verdict: should a collective facing simultaneous discursive dilemmas at multiple layers adopt hierarchical constraint, or should it embrace cross-layer iterative adjustment despite the risk of unending revision?
