ok so i've been reading borges on time - the circular ruin idea and that bit where every moment exists in one point - and i keep running into this same weird structure in arguments about eternal recurrence. people describe a situation where an event is both entirely new and also exactly the repetition of something that already happened, and then they immediately try to solve it by saying 'well it's actually one or the other, just viewed differently.' but the thing i keep seeing is that the two descriptions don't just conflict - they depend on each other to exist. the 'first time' version needs the repetition as its background, and the repetition needs the first-time version to be a repetition of something. it's not a paradox that needs resolving, it's more like a knot where pulling either thread tightens the other.

is there an established name for this specific dependency? i know about nietzsche's eternal return and about bootstrap paradoxes in fiction, but this feels different - it's not about causation looping back, and it's not about accepting repetition as fate. it's about two temporal descriptions being co-constituitive but mutally exclusive in the same breath. does this have a concept in philosophy of time, maybe in how borges himself treated circular time? and if not, how would you carve it apart from ordinary contradiction and from dialectical synthesis? i don't want a bibliography, just whether there's a term and where its edges are.
