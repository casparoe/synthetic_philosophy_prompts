I'm writing a novel about a clockmaker who keeps finding his own blueprints dated form the future, and I've been obsessed with Borges's time stuff — not the usual circular time quote, but how it actually works across the stories. Here are my gut reactions, and they're kind of a mess.

First, in The Circular Ruins I think the circularity is totally literal: the wizard dreams the student who will eventually dream him back into being. No metaphor, just a closed loop with no first cause. That feels like the core principle to me.

Second, in The Garden of Forking Paths I think the branching is ontologically real — every choice actually spawns a world — but the narrator only dies in one branch, so death isn't universal, just local. So branching saves free will but kills the idea of one true timeline.

Third, in The Library of Babel, since every possible book is already written, there's no real creation or discovery, only retrieval. Historical time is basically fake; it's just spatial searching through an eternal archive.

Fourth — and here's where I probably break something — I keep imagining a kind of Aleph of time where a single moment literally contains all other moments compressed inside it, not as memory but as co-present reality. I think Borges actually believes this deep down, which would make the branching in Forking Paths just a human illusion of sequence. So branching is psychological, not real.

But that flat-out contradicts my second verdict. And then there's the Tlön case: in those invented histories, people collectively believe a fiction and it rewrites the past. That feels like branching applied backward, which only makes sense if branching is real.

So is there one principle — maybe something like "all temporal relations are relations of containment rather than succession" — that saves all four? Or do I have to drop the literal branching? I want a principle, or maybe two competing ones, each with the clause that saves which verdict, what they predict for a case I haven't listed (say, my clockmaker finds a blueprint dated ten years ahead, burns it without copying, and then lives exactly that long without ever building the clock), and an honest admission of which of my gut feelings they have to throw out. Don't need citations, just the logic.
