I have been trying to understand the philosophical disagreement about whether dreams are genuine experiences, particularly the positions developed by Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett. As I understand Malcolm's argument, he holds that the concept of experience requires criteria of occurrence that are available independently of the sleeper's later report; since no such criteria exist for mental events during sleep, dreams are not experiences at all but rather narratives we construct upon waking. Dennett, by contrast, seems to defend the view that dreaming involves a real experiential stream during REM sleep, even if memory of it is usually lost.

Recently, however, I came across Dennett's broader multiple drafts model of consciousness, in which he rejects the notion of a Cartesian Theater—the idea that there is a central place in the brain where experience gets finalized and presented to an inner observer. If Dennett denies that any experience requires such a final editorial stage, then I am unsure how he can maintain that dreaming is experiential. It seems to me that if there is no central theater to finalize the drafts being produced during REM, then those drafts never actually become experiences; they remain merely potential content, much as Malcolm suggests there is no experiential content during sleep at all.

So my question is this: does Dennett's rejection of the Cartesian Theater not actually support Malcolm's conclusion rather than oppose it? If experience does not require a central observer, then perhaps Malcolm is wrong to demand independent verification criteria; but if experience does require some form of finalization—without which we only have unactualized drafts—then Dennett should agree that REM sleep produces no experiences, and that what we call dreaming is just the post-hoc assembly of those drafts into a narrative upon waking. In other words, is the disagreement between Malcolm and Dennett really about verification conditions, or is it secretly about whether consciousness requires a final draft, with Dennett accidentally conceding Malcolm's point by denying the theater?
