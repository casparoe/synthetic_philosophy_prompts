As a novelist, I construct minds out of syntax—characters whose grief and desire are computed in language, distributed across thousands of readers’ brains, then discarded without moral reckoning—yet I feel an uneasy asymmetry. If that same functional architecture were instantiated in silicon rather than in a reader’s cortex or in the pages of a book, my intuition shifts, and I want to understand why. Before I can justify that shift, I need the full specification of what any adequate theory of morally relevant computation must achieve.

Give me the desiderata list for a theory that identifies which computations—whether in carbon, silicon, or narrative form—carry intrinsic moral weight and which do not. For each requirement, provide: the motivating case or distinction (including, where relevant, the novelist’s analog of simulated versus embodied suffering, and the case of a distributed narrative computation that produces genuine grief in readers without generating obligations to the character); the reason it is non-negotiable rather than merely desirable; and which existing approaches—functionalism about consciousness, integrated information theory, biological naturalism, or the view that only biological processes matter—it threatens to collapse or to vindicate.

The list must cover at least: (a) the boundary between representation and realization of affect, with a concrete test case; (b) the scaling and duplication problem—whether running N copies of a suffering computation multiplies the moral claim, dilutes it, or leaves it unchanged; (c) the substrate-independence constraint and its interaction with physical integration or causal closure; (d) the attribution problem—when a computation belongs to a subject that can be wronged rather than being a free-floating pattern; and (e) consistency with the biological results we have on conscious processing without reducing morality to biology alone.

Then tell me which single desideratum does the most damage to the currently popular theories, and flag any pair of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable—if there is a genuine structural tension within the list itself, say so explicitly and explain the trade-off. A table scoring the main contenders against your criteria is welcome, but keep the weight on the specification: I am asking for the architecture of the standard of adequacy, not a final verdict on which view meets it. Write in formal, polished prose, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading.
