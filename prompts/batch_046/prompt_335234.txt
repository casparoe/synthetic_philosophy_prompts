I've been trying to map out my own stance for a workshop on EU constitutionalism and I'm worried I'm just stacking commitments that don't actually hang together. Roughly here's where I've landed after reading a bunch of pluralism and sovereignty literature: (1) I think talk of a democratic deficit is mostly a category mistake—there's no thick European demos that needs direct democratic authorization, so judging the EU by nation-state democratic metrics misses the point; interdependence across democratic levels is sufficient legitimacy. (2) I also think sovereignty is genuinely pooled, not delegated—once authority moves up, member states don't hold a residual sovereignty they can just reclaim at will. (3) At the same time, I think any step toward ever-closer union, especially fiscal or defense integration, must go through unanimous treaty change with an absolute parlimentary veto for each state. (4) I think the EU should eventually become a democratic federation, with a real European government accountable to the EP and full legislative initiative there. (5) And I think national constitutional courts are right to reserve a constitutional-identity backstop over EU law, since democratic self-determination needs that check.

Can you audit this package? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what's the least costly revision for each real conflict? I'm especially unsure whether (1) and (4) can sit together, and whether (2) combined with (3) and (5) collapses into incoherence about pooled authority. Rank the tensions by severity, say what each costs to fix, and tell me the single cheapest change that cleans up the most mess. Hard cap: 300 words max, ordered by severity.
