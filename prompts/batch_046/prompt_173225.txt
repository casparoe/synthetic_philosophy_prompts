At my job we just got this wellbeing dashboard that pings you every two hour to rate your momentary mood, but the designers deliberately left out any questions about whether your work feels meaningful or 'real' — they said they wanted to stay away from value-laden metrics. Meanwhile HR's yearly review asks if your role aligns with your 'authentic self.' So we're measuring well-being one way that ignores authenticity, then grading career fit on the other side by demanding it. What theory of well-being does this split practice actually presuppose? Is it straight hedonism, some hybrid desire view, or just incoherent? Reconstruct the implicit theory behind each part, say whether they really need the same foundation or can survive with different ones, and tell me what either the dashboard or the review form would have to change if we took Nozick's experience-machine argument seriously and replaced subjective satisfaction with an objective-list condition.
