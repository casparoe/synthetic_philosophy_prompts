Consider the evaluation record of a senior reviewer for an interdisciplinary philosophy-of-science board, who applies a Lakatosian framework—distinguishing a research programme’s hard core from its protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses—to judge whether a programme is progressive or degenerating. Her past verdicts are as follows.

(1) A cognitive-load programme that accommodates every new dual-task failure by adding free parameters to the central-executive model, leaving the modular-working-memory hard core untouched and generating no previously unknown predictions: degenerating.

(2) A cross-modal predictive-binding framework in neuroscience that derives and experimentally confirms a previously unmeasured temporal lag in auditory-visual integration, with the hierarchical prediction-error hard core fully preserved: progressive.

(3) An evolutionary-psychology programme that abandons the informational-encapsulation postulate of its hard core, replacing domain-specific modules with a domain-general learning mechanism; this resolves old anomalies about cultural variability and also yields one new predicted learning curve, but the hard core itself is altered: degenerating.

(4) A social-cognitive attribution model that, faced with repeated cross-cultural replication failures, postulates an ever-growing list of unmeasured mediators; the individual-rational-calculation hard core remains intact, and no novel empirical effects are anticipated: degenerating.

(5) A quantum-consciousness proposal that revises the classical-computational hard core of neuroscience to include macroscopic quantum coherence, explaining the binding problem only retrospectively and predicting no new measurements: degenerating.

Next, the board receives Programme VI, the recent enactive-embodied dynamics reformulation (EEDP). It retains intact the hard core that cognition is constituted by continuous sensorimotor coupling rather than by internal representations. However, it has entirely replaced its protective belt of discrete, task-specific schemas with a unified continuous-attractor-landscape architecture. This restructuring resolves long-standing anomalies concerning rapid, context-dependent perceptual switching—phenomena that previously required ad hoc representational-buffer and executive-control auxiliaries—without invoking any internal representational entities. The reformulation unifies perception and action under a single dynamical ontology and simplifies the auxiliary machinery, but it derives no empirical predictions that were not already documented in the existing literature on perceptual decision-making.

Predict the reviewer’s verdict on Programme VI, specifying whether she will classify it as progressive or degenerating. Identify the implicit evaluative rule that best explains her entire record—distinguishing, for example, a strict predictive-novelty criterion (progressive only if novel empirical predictions are confirmed with the hard core untouched) from a structural-resolution criterion (progressive also if the protective belt is restructured in a simplifying or unifying way that resolves anomalies, even in the absence of new predictions). Identify the closest analog among cases (1)–(5) and explain precisely why it is or is not determinative. Finally, specify the exact further case—describing a hypothetical research programme with a clearly defined hard core, protective-belt change, predictive status, and anomaly-resolution profile—that would definitively settle which of the two competing rules she is actually applying.
