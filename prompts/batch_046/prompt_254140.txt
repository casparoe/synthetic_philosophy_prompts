Student answer:
Dialetheism is just the idea that some contradictions are true, whereas trivialism is the idea that every statement is true. Priest uses a non-uniform solution, which means he applies paraconsistent logic only to the paradoxical cases like the Liar, but keeps ordinary classical logic intact everywhere else. His system LP rejects explosion, so if you have both A and not-A you cant derive arbitrary B. This containment is what makes the solution non-uniform: the contradiction is quarantined in the boundary case. So he avoids trivialism because the contradiction never leaks out. That said, paraconsistent logic basically restores the law of non-contradiction for all practical reasoning by sealing off the damage, which means the Liar paradox doesnt actually show that contradictions are genuinely true in reality — it just shows we need localized logical guardrails. So Priest is right that trivialism is avoided, but he mistakes a logical containment strategy for metaphysical proof of dialetheism.
