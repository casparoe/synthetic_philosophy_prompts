Here are my committments on Nietzsche's eternal recurrence and I'm not sure they hang together. I treat it mainly as an existential litmus test, not a cosmological claim, but I also think he probably meant it literally in his private notes even though the phyiscs is dodgy. I hold amor fati: I should embrace my past failures as necessary parts of my life. At the same time I think his ethic is about self-overcoming, which requires actively becoming different from who I was. Then there's this: if the loop is literally fixed, changing my behavior now seems pointless, yet I also think genuinely believing in literal recurrence should motivate more intense living, not fatalism. Finally I lean toward a spiral model where each cycle is qualitatively identical but numerically distinct, which I think dodges determinism, but I don't see how that squares with willing my exact past back unchanged. Go through these, tell me which pairs are in real tension versus just looking weird, rank them by severity, and for each say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. Don't just tell me to read more Nietzsche.
