I am preparing a session for a small reading group I run—none of us have formal training in economics or philosophy—about the argument over whether central planning can replace markets. I have been thinking through the exchange between Hayek and Mises, on one side, and Oskar Lange, on the other, and I want the group to grasp not the historical details but the specific conceptual tension: that prices might do a kind of work that cannot be replaced by better administration or faster calculation. To make this tangible, I want to build the session around an example that is close to our experience but not the standard factory or grain-market scenario: a community tool library, shared among neighbors, where broken items must be repaired or retired, future needs are unknown, skills are distributed unevenly, and no single person oversees the whole system. I believe this case captures the knowledge problem because it involves sequential dependency—fixing one tool requires knowing whether another will be needed first—without invoking money, but I need a teaching plan, not an explanation.

Please plan the explanation's architecture. What idea must be in place before the tool-library example can do its work, and what should come after? Identify the one misconception the group will almost certainly form—likely that the difficulty is merely informational and could be solved by a more diligent record-keeper—and specify exactly where in the sequence I should intercept it. Describe what the carrying example must include (for instance, the hidden state of wear, the unpredictability of demand, and the fact that repair skills are localized) and what it must deliberately lack, so that the point is not confused with a problem about currency or incentives. Suggest a concrete check—perhaps a single question or a brief exercise—that would reveal whether they have understood that the issue is discovery rather than computation. Finally, tell me which part of the debate, likely Lange's proposal of trial-and-error adjustment, I should acknowledge but set aside at this level, and how to frame that omission honestly so the group knows there is a reply they are not hearing.
