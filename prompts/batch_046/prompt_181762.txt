Read The Princess Bride—either Goldman's novel with its fake S. Morgenstern framing or the film with Peter Falk's grandfather—through Hofstadter's idea from Gödel, Escher, Bach that meaning isn't inside the message or outside in the frame but in the strange loop where each level interprets the other. The story keeps breaking its own fairy-tale surface: the grandfather skips parts, Goldman inserts footnotes about abridgment, and the kid interrupts to ask whether Westley dies. Don't just call this meta-fiction. I want you to take the loop seriously: when the frame insists the inner tale is only a story but then gets emotionally caught up in Buttercups fate, does that change the moral weight of the violence and gender dynamics inside? And in moments where the frame almost disappears—like when the movie drops the sickroom for long stretches—does meaning collapse into the inner message, or is the absence itself part of the loop? I'm especialy interested in whether Hofstadter's framework misses something about sentimental framing that Goldman knows, so say where the philosophical reading illuminates a scene and where the work resists being reduced to a diagram about frames.
