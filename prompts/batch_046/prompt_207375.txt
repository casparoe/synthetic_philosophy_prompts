Compare the epistemic KK principle—which holds that knowing p entails knowing that one knows p—with Timothy Williamson's argument against the luminosity of mental states, particularly his claim that conditions permitting gradual, imperceptible shifts in cognitive access cannot be luminous. To structure the comparison, introduce a hypothetical gradient-access framework in which a subject's second-order awareness of her own knowledge dims continuously across a sequence of near-identical perceptual cases, rather than collapsing at a discrete boundary. Within this gradualist model, assess which position—the KK principle or the denial of luminosity—yields the more internally consistent account of knowledge attribution, and specify the conceptual costs that each view must bear to accommodate continuous epistemic fading.
