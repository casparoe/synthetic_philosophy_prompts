As a retired physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy of mind and ethics, I want to check whether the following five commitments form a coherent package. First, I take the Libet readiness-potential paradigm to show that specific voluntary actions are initiated unconsciously, leaving conscious will with at most a veto over execution rather than a causal initiator role. Second, I hold that moral responsibility requires libertarian freedom: in identical circumstances the agent must genuinely be able to do otherwise through an exercise of will that is not determined by prior states. Third, I regard the brain as a deterministic physical system operating under natural law without causal gaps. Fourth, I maintain that the distinction between doing harm and allowing harm tracks real differences in the direction of causal influence and is not reducible to intentions or outcomes alone. Fifth, I accept a strict separation between descriptive neuroscience and normative ethics, holding that no finding about neural mechanisms can by itself justify or undermine a claim about moral duty. Please audit these commitments for joint consistency: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which conflicts dissolve on inspection, rank the tensions by severity, suggest the least costly revision for each, and indicate which single change would resolve the greatest number of conflicts. Please respond as a numbered list with exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
