As someone who works comfortably with evolutionary developmental biology but has no formal training in historiography, and following a long and frustrating argument with a friend over whether evolutionary metaphors in history are genuinely explanatory or merely decorative, I want to test whether a rigorous conceptual translation is actually possible. My friend insists that Whig historiography -- the interpretation of the past as an inevitable progression toward present liberal constitutional arrangements, treating intermediate political forms as either preparatory stages or obstacles overcome, and implicitly treating constitutional precommitment as an irreversible evolutionary step that constrains all subsequent development -- is essentially a biological teleology wearing different clothing. I disagree: I believe Whig history depends on normative claims about progress and intentional design that evolutionary theory, especially in its modern evo-devo form, has explicitly abandoned. But I need the translation worked out with precision to see exactly where my intuition fails and whether I am defending coherence or just a position I already prefer.

Please restate the core commitments of Whig historiography in the vocabulary of evolutionary developmental biology, not through loose analogy but through systematic mapping. Use the framework precisely: adaptive landscapes with local optima rather than global peaks, developmental constraint and phylogenetic inertia that limit possible pathways, exaptation versus true adaptation, selection pressures acting on developmental pathways rather than on optimized adult phenotypes, and the evo-devo critique that much evolutionary change is non-adaptive, historically contingent, or constrained by prior structure rather than functionally optimized. Show me which specific historiographical claims -- its directional narrative arc, its treatment of present institutions as the adaptive peak of a long selection process, its dismissal of alternative political forms as maladaptive vestiges, and its implicit attribution to past actors of unconscious selection for outcomes they could not have articulated -- correspond to which biological mechanisms, and be explicit about where the isomorphism breaks.

Then provide the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly? Which survive only with distortion -- for instance, does the normative judgment that constitutional democracy represents a "higher" or more advanced form have any counterpart in evolutionary theory, or must it be dropped or reinterpreted as mere local adaptation? Is there anything specific to the historiographical debate, such as the claim that constitutional precommitment operates as an irreversible constraint that binds all future political development, that has no expression in evolutionary vocabulary without contradiction? And does the residue indicate that the biological framework is deficient because it lacks resources for normative evaluation and intentional design, or does it reveal that Whig history was always relying on a teleology that evolutionary biology has shown to be incoherent? I am at an impasse with my friend, and I want to know whether stripping away the teleological element costs Whig history its explanatory power, or whether that element was never doing real explanatory work to begin with.
