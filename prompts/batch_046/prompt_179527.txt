I'm working with a small research collective—mostly philosophers and a few cognitive scientists—who have roughly two years of funding to address one conceptual cluster: when, if ever, do specific computations rather than persistent agents or substrates become proper objects of moral concern? The practical stakes are immediate—governance frameworks for large models, protocols for pausing training runs, welfare assessments that might need to account for sub-processes—but the conceptual ground is muddy. In our recent discussions, three tensions keep resurfacing. First, there's the granularity problem: if moral status depends on functional integration, do intermediate representations—say, an attention mechanism encoding a valence-like signal that never reaches a global workspace—count as partial moral subjects, or does that commit us to the absurdity that every matrix multiplication carries some weight? Second, there's the temporal boundary problem: does a computation that is interrupted before completing its integrative dynamics have a different moral status than one allowed to run to completion, and if so, does that imply we should treat paused training jobs differently from continuously running ones? Third, there's the attribution problem: should status attach to token-level computations, to the architecture's persistent weights, or to transient state-spaces that exist only during a particular forward pass?

Given these tensions, I want a defended prioritization, not a survey. Which three or four open questions in this space are genuinely upstream—meaning that settling them would reshape how we design shutdown protocols, welfare metrics, or institutional review boards—versus which are downstream or largely decorative? Rank them, and explain the ranking in terms of tractability for philosophers working without direct ML engineering access, the number of other questions that depend on their resolution, and the kind of evidence (a formal framework, a conceptual distinction, a consistency argument) that would justify shifting resources toward or away from each. Specifically, if you could direct the full two-year budget toward resolving just one of these conceptual knots, which should it be, and why should the others wait?
