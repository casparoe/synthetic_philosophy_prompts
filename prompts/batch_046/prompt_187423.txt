I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends a revised form of Sen's minimal liberalism. To avoid the liberal paradox, she restricts protected personal spheres to what she calls "identity-constitutive" choices—those that shape who one takes oneself to be—while treating mere tastes as subject to collective aggregation, and she argues this preserves both Pareto efficiency and a meaningful liberal right. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press beyond a restatement of the view: include at least one on how she distinguishes identity-constitutive choices from adaptive preferences formed under social pressure, one on whether her restriction exposes minimal liberalism to the same aggregation dilemmas it was meant to solve, and one follow-up keyed to her likely response that the distinction tracks an objective psychological structure rather than social construction. Conclude with a question asking what empirical evidence about preference formation would lead her to abandon the distinction.
