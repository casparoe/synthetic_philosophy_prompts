I've been thinking a lot about Nozick's tracking conditions for knowledge - you know, the counterfactuals about belief in nearby worlds where p is false versus true. Lately I've been applying them to these digitized archive cases I study: a historian pulls up a primary source that was corrupted in transmission, but the archive's retrival layer tracks provenance and witholds the document when inconsistencies are detected. In worlds where the corruption makes the claim false, the system doesn't present it; the historian herself, though, just sees a flag and moves on. So I'm defending this position: tracking conditions should apply to the whole epistemic system, including the external retrieval and verification mechanisms, not just the individual mind. That'd allow for extended knowledge even when the human cognizer herself doesn't satisfy the counterfactuals. I'm also wondering wether this runs into closure issues - if the system tracks p and tracks p→q, but the historian never actually draws q, do we still have closure?

But I'm worried I'm just insulating my view from counterexample. What would actually count against this system-level tracking interpretation? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. For instance, does it collapse into reliabilism that Nozick explicitly rejected? Would it fail when the mechanism tracks correctly but the human misreads the flag? And are there results in social epistemology about testimonial transmission that show tracking can't be distributed without losing the anti-luck intuition that makes tracking attractive in the first place? I'd like to know what defenders of individualist tracking have themselves conceded as counting against distributed versions, and whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability. Be honest: is that insulation a problem for a view like this, or just standard theoretical refinement?
