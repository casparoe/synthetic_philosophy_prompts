Write a joke that takes the inefficacy objection to individual contributions in collective harms—climate emissions, meat consumption, or voting—as its conceptual engine, not merely as topical window dressing. The punchline should turn on a recursive twist: if one person's action is causally negligible within the collective outcome, then one audience member's reaction to the joke (laughter, outrage, or agreement) is equally negligible within the collective audience, so the joke's own moral or comedic force dissolves precisely when the crowd is large enough for the objection to bite. Make it genuinely funny to someone who has thought about collective action problems, but do not name specific philosophers, cite texts, or ask for a bibliography; the humor should come from the structure of the objection itself, not from recognizing a quoted authority. Keep it to a short paragraph.
