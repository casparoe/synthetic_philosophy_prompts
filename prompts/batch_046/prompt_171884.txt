As a retired physicist who has only recently turned to philosophy of history, I keep running into the refrain that the arc of the moral universe is long but bends toward justice. In a recent seminar on whether moral progress can be given any empirical metric—tracking, say, the expansion of rights or the reduction of state violence—an interlocutor invoked the line to dismiss my question outright, as if mentioning curvature settled whether justice functions as an attractor in historical state-space. Before I return to that room, I want the statement disambiguated with the precision one expects in theoretical physics.

Please treat the refrain as a family of distinct philosophical claims rather than as poetry. Lay them out as a menu: (i) the trivial or definitional reading; (ii) the empirical-historical reading, which asserts a measurable directional trend; (iii) the normative or prescriptive reading, which tells us how to act rather than describing a trajectory; (iv) the metaphysical or teleological reading, in which history possesses something like a geodesic structure with justice as a global attractor, perhaps analogous to a potential well or a field with a preferred equilibrium; and (v) any further radical or reductive interpretation hidden in the language of arcs and bending. For each, specify exactly what is being asserted about time, causation, moral ontology, and whether progress can be quantified, and flag any reading that illicitly imports physical concepts—curvature, geodesic completeness, thermodynamic arrows—into moral ontology without justification.

Then indicate which of these are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, which depend on unverifiable metaphysical commitments, and which are false—especially those that conflate rhetorical arc with geometric curvature or treat moral development as obeying a deterministic field equation. Finally, tell me which reading, if any, could actually do the argumentative work my interlocutor demanded—blocking an empirical inquiry into whether justice is increasing—without committing a category error. If none can, say so explicitly. I am not asking for a bibliography, a history of the phrase, or citations; I want only the conceptual map, with each node tested against the kind of rigor one applies to a physical model.
