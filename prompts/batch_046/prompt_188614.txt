We sit on a transnational constitutional tribunal whose precedents mix American viewpoint-neutrality concerns with European dignity-and-harm considerations. Our record: (1) we struck down a statute criminalizing denial of well-documented historical atrocities; (2) we upheld a conviction for distributing materials that named specific individuals and urged immediate physical violence; (3) we struck down a prohibition on burning national symbols in protest, despite deep offense to a minority; (4) we upheld an injunction requiring a platform to stop algorithmically amplifying hate-filled content to users who had not sought it, treating the mechanism as regulable conduct; (5) we struck down a ban on academic publications arguing for biological racial hierarchies, citing scholarly viewpoint neutrality.

The new case involves a generative audio system that sends personalized messages to subscribers. The messages contain no explicit slurs or direct threats; they use culturally specific metaphors and indirect allusions that, in context, portray members of a protected group as parasitic and dangerous. Delivery is restricted to users whose behavioral profiles indicate high susceptibility. The creators claim the output is protected opinion and artistic expression.

What verdict should we reach to stay consistent with ourselves?
