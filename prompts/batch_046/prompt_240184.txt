I have been working through the distinction between the hard problem and the meta-problem of consciousness, and I am convinced that they collapse into one another once we take seriously the possibility that phenomenal judgments are products of a specialised introspective mechanism — perhaps a form of higher-order misrepresentation of first-order sensory integration. If a physicalist theory can explain, in purely functional terms, why a cognitive system reports experiences of what-it-is-likeness for certain states but not for others, then the ontological question seems to vanish: what remains is only an evolutionary question about why that misrepresentational mechanism was selected. So should we not now assess theories of consciousness primarily by their predictive accuracy regarding the distribution of phenomenal reports, rather than by whether they accommodate an irreducible subjective feel? In particular, if a model explains why we intuit that philosophical zombies are conceivable without actually licensing the metaphysical possibility of zombies, has it not thereby satisfied both the meta-problem and dissolved the hard problem simultaneously?
