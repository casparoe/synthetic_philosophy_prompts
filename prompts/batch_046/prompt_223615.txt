Consider a long-range climate-adaptation policy that, if implemented, will cause severe coastal displacement over the next two centuries and thereby alter the identity of every person born after 2100. Under the policy, the future population is, on average, healthier and more prosperous than the status-quo population would be, and every actual future person lives a life well above the threshold for being worth living; yet the status quo would have produced a different set of people, none of whom is made worse off by the policy because they never exist. Give your credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that adopting this policy wrongs no particular individual. Decompose your answer into: (i) your credence that a person-affecting moral framework (rather than an impersonal axiology) governs judgments of harm in such cases; (ii) your credence that non-existence cannot be a harm or a deprivation that generates comparative claims; (iii) your credence that indirect effects—such as the loss of specific cultural lineages, artworks, or social structures that would have existed only under the status quo—count as harms to identifiable absent persons rather than as impersonal losses; and (iv) any other factor you deem essential. For each component, state a rough probability and one line of justification, then combine them into your final number. Finally, identify which component your overall credence is most sensitive to, and specify what kind of argument or evidence—conceptual or empirical—would move that component by the largest margin.
