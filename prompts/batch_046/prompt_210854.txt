I am drafting a commemorative statement for a former colleague who was at once a rigorous editor and an unusually compassionate mentor, and I have run into a structural problem I cannot solve. If I list her rigor and her compassion as separate attributes, they sit side by side like items in a catalog; they do not yet form one unified character. So I try to bind them together by saying that her rigor was expressed through her compassion, or that both were governed by a single ethos. But that binding relation—whether it is an intertwining, a governing principle, or a shared mode of expression—then appears as yet another element that must itself be tied back to the person it describes. I can always add a further link (the ethos belongs to her, the belonging is part of her identity, and so on), yet each link reproduces the same difficulty: something is always left outside the unity I am trying to capture. Is there a coherent way to present her character as genuinely one thing rather than an open-ended chain of qualities and connecting relations? Or does any attempt to describe a unified personality necessarily collapse into either an arbitrary stopping point or an infinite regress of binders?
