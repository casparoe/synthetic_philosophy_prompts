I'd like you to read the 2016 film Arrival through the lens of the Fermi paradox, but with a specific conceptual twist that standard popular-science treatments rarely address. Arrival, however, depicts heptapod cognition and language as structured around simultaneous, non-linear temporality—their graphic system and perceptual framework do not separate past, present, and future in the human manner. Rather than asking whether the film offers a conventional solution to the paradox (for example, that advanced beings are present but hidden), I want you to examine whether the film's metaphysics undermines the framing of the paradox itself. Does the paradox tacitly presuppose a specifically human, linear-causal model of what counts as contact, civilization, or technological persistence? Ask whether an atemporal or acausal mode of existence would even register as a data point in the statistical reasoning that drives Fermi-style arguments, or whether it would fall entirely outside the observational categories the paradox employs. Where does this reading illuminate the emotional and narrative architecture of the film—particularly Louise's choice to embrace that future despite knowing its cost—and where does it risk distorting either the logical structure of the paradox or the internal logic of the story? I want a criticism that takes both the conceptual problem and the film seriously, that does not reduce either to an illustration of the other, and that concludes by saying what Arrival contributes to the conceptual difficulty that pure logical argument alone cannot provide.
