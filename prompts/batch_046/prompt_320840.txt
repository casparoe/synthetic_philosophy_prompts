I am preparing a long-form feature for a general-interest magazine on whether historical scholarship can defend any meaningful claim to objectivity in the wake of the narrative and linguistic turns. Rather than retreading the familiar opposition between Leopold von Ranke's aspiration to reconstruct the past as it actually happened and the scepticism voiced by E.H. Carr and Hayden White about the inevitability of narrative construction, I want to start from the ground up and specify what an adequate theory of historical objectivity would actually have to deliver.

Could you lay out the full set of desiderata: the phenomena it must accommodate—including the evidentiary constraint of archives, the inescapability of selection and emplotment, the possibility of intersubjective agreement on basic chronologies and causal sequences, and the need to distinguish interpretive framing from straightforward factual error—together with the distinctions it must preserve, such as that between being wrong about a date and being ideologically slanted, or between the absence of a source and the absence of an event? For each desideratum, indicate whether it should be treated as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp—positivist, constructivist, or narrativist—and flag any pairs that appear jointly unsatisfiable, paying special attention to what I will call the preservation asymmetry: the fact that archives survive not through statistical representativeness but through institutional power and accident, so that the evidential base itself carries a structural bias that any theory of objectivity must account for without collapsing into radical scepticism.

Finally, without producing a bibliographic scorecard, diagnose briefly how Ranke's reconstructive program and White's tropological model each fare against this list—specifically, which desiderata each framework silently sacrifices and whether that sacrifice is defensible—so that the feature can show readers not which thinker is right, but what each conception of objectivity is actually buying at what cost.
