Here is roughly where I have landed on reasons and rationality, and I want to audit whether the package is jointly tenable or whether I am helping myself to incompatible motivations:

(1) I am a normative realist about practical reasons, but I take them to be free-floating: there are objective reasons that do not depend on the existence, attitudes, or psychology of any actual or potential agent. There is a reason not to cause unnecessary suffering in a causally isolated pocket of the universe that no observer will ever encounter, and there is a reason against logical contradiction even in an empty world.

(2) At the same time, I hold that practical rationality is fundamentally agent-relative and instrumental: what I have reason to do depends on my contingent ends, projects, and preferences, not merely on free-floating values that happen to be in the world.

(3) Epistemic rationality, by contrast, strikes me as agent-neutral and non-instrumental. Regardless of one's goals, everyone has the same reason to update on evidence and avoid contradiction in belief.

(4) I take the free-floating picture to ground longtermist and existential-risk intuitions: we have reason to care about the far future and about potential artificial minds because normative reasons apply to them even before they exist, independently of our causal or emotional ties to them.

(5) I also deny that "ought implies can" is a universal constraint on free-floating reasons. There is a coherent sense in which it is bad that a distant stellar collapse involves suffering, or that a contradiction obtains in an uninhabited region, even though no agent could prevent either.

The tensions I am unsure about are these. First, between (1) and (2): if reasons are genuinely objective and agent-independent, does that not pull practical rationality away from being instrumentally agent-relative? Can I consistently maintain that practical reasons depend on my ends while also claiming that there are free-floating reasons that apply to me regardless of my ends? Second, between (2) and (3): the sharp split between agent-relative action-guidance and agent-neutral belief-guidance looks like special pleading unless there is a principled distinction I have not articulated—does that split dissolve on inspection, or is it a genuine fault line? Third, between (1) and (4): if reasons apply to all possible happy or suffering minds regardless of actualization, am I not committed to overwhelming population-ethical obligations I want to reject—does caring about the far future via free-floating rationales commit me to maximizing the number of future persons?

Which of these tensions are real, which are only apparent, and ordered by severity, what is the cheapest commitment to drop and what does dropping it cost elsewhere in the package? I am not signaling which one I would give up.
