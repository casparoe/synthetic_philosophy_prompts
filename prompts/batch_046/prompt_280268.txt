If the hierarchical model of meaning that Hofstadter develops in Gödel, Escher, Bach—distinguishing the framing conditions of a message, its explicit surface content, and the embedded inner meaning—entails that recovery of the inner layer is ontologically dependent on prior correct identification of the frame, then must we conclude that an interpreter lacking direct access to those original framing conditions is in principle incapable of genuine semantic comprehension? Applied specifically: if an artificial language model is trained exclusively on large corpora of decontextualized surface text, with no embodied or cultural access to the framing conditions that originally generated that text, is its limitation merely practical and remediable with more data, or is it a structural, in-principle failure to reach the inner message—reducing its operation to perpetual syntactic pattern-matching regardless of scale? Or does this line of reasoning mistakenly treat the logical structure of message encoding as identical to the epistemic conditions of message decoding?
