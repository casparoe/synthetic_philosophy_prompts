so i'm in this course on existensialism and we just finished Fear and Trembling. i'm trying to figure out whether i actually buy Kierkegaard's idea of the teleological suspention of the ethical, but instead of debating in the abstract i want to check my gut on concrete cases. here are my verdicts:

Abraham being commanded to sacrifice Isaac and actually going up the mountain with the knife: yes, that's the real thing. the universal ethical law against killing your kid gets suspended for a relation to the absolute, and i think that's exactly right.

A synthetic biologist who releases an unapproved gene drive into wild mosquitoes because she had a private vision telling her it was commanded by some purpose beyond human law: structurally this looks identical to Abraham, but my gut says no, it's not a legitimate suspension, just hubris or insanity. so i reject it, even though the shape is the same.

A soldier who executes prisoners to stop a larger massacre, with no religious claim at all, just strategic reasoning: definitely not suspension. it's a horrible choice inside ethics, not above it.

A climate activist who forges data to push through emergency policy she genuinely believes will save humanity: also not suspension. she's still playing the universal-ethical game of welfare for all, just cheating.

But then there's a musician who abandons her child and family obligations to pursue what she calls an artistic vocation that demands total obedience: i'm genuinely torn. it feels closer to Abraham than the soldier does, but part of me also thinks she's just being selfish. so i go back and forth.

Is there a single principle that gets all five verdicts, or do i have to overturn one? If there is, what does it predict for a case i haven't listed, like a parent who refuses life-saving medical treatment for their dying child because they received a private revelation that doctors must not touch them? And if no principle saves them all, which verdict should go?
