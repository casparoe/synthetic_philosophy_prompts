ok so here's something that's been bugging me about how we actually treat consciousness in practice vs. theory. In hospitals, the standard protocol for unresponsive patients is basically: run the Coma Recovery Scale-Revised, check for command-following, and if there's none you classify them as UWS (unresponsive wakefulness) — end of story. Meanwhile in neuroscience labs people use 'no-report' paradigms, like local-global auditory mismatch or PCI measurements, to try to detect phenomenal consciousness even when there's no behavioral access. But here's the weird part: even when a study finds local recurrent processing signatures in a patient who still cant follow commands, clinicians dont update thier care plan or moral status. The finding just stays in the research paper. So what theory of consciousness does that institutional split actually presuppose? Is it global workspace theory — that only globally broadcast info counts for personhood? Or is it just a pragmatic epistemic conservatism, compatible with Block-style phenomenal/access distinction? Could a phenomenal realist accept this split, saying we just lack clinical access to P-consciousness? Reconstruct the implicit view, tell me whether the practice actually needs GWT or is underdetermined, and say what ICU triage and resource allocation would have to change if we treated phenomenal consciousness as sufficient for full moral standing regardless of access.
