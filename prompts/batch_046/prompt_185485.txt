I sit on a state regulatory reform board and every quater we get proposals to scrap old administrative rules — mandatory comment windows, dual-signature contract rules, legacy reporting formats, stuff like that. Both sides wave Chesterton's fence and Burkean prescription around like they mean the same thing, but my team needs a reusable procedure we can run in about 45 minutes without calling in a philosopher. The board chair only signs off when we've clearly marked something a values call, not a fact call.

Turn the Burke/Chesterton idea into an actual decision checklist. To keep it from being just vague wisdom, split institutional "fences" into three functional types I want you to work with: boundary fences (who is in or out of a protected category), velocity fences (that force a slowdown so other checks can catch up), and mneumonic fences (that keep a specific past failure mode visible to current staff). For each type, lay out the ordered questions we ask, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure outputs "repeal" versus "keep," and exactly where it stops and writes "values — chair decides" because the issue isn't factual.

Apply it to two real-style proposals: (1) dropping the dual-signature requirement for contracts under $50k, and (2) killing the 30-day waiting period for zoning variances in historic districts. Make sure one comes out against what the reform advocates wont like, and explain why the fence reasoning pushes that way. Then flag the breakdown cases — like when one rule serves all three fence types at once, or when the original failure mode is now technically impossible — and say plainly what the procedure just can't settle. No bibliographies or reading lists, please, I just need the flowchart in words.
