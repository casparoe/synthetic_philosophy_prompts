ok so i've noticed this weird layering in moral-motivation talk and i'm not sure if there's a term for it or if i'm just being confused. situation A: you volunteer at a shelter because you care about suffering, but you also enjoy the social credit. Mixed motives, boring. situation B: you volunteer specifically to train yourself into caring more about suffering - the action is a means to the virtue. situation C: you volunteer because you think 'caring about suffering' is valuable, and part of what makes it valuable is that it includes recoginizing its own value, so your reason loops back on itself in this reflexive way. The action and the base reason are identicle in B and C, but in C there's this extra meta-layer that seems to change the moral character of the act without adding any selfishness. Is there an established concept for this reflexive structure - maybe in virtue ethics or the moral-worth literature? And if not, can we carve it out precisely: what's the boundary between healthy self-awareness of one's reasons and this weird bootstrapping loop?
