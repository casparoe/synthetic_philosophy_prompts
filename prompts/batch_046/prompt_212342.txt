everyone in my poli sci circles keeps comparing judicial review to having an umpire call balls and strikes in a game where the players also keep rewriting the rulebook — congress writes the statutes but the court just applies the higher-order rules of the constitution, so its ok that judges arent elected. its used to defend letting unelected judges strike down democratic laws. how far does the analogy actually hold? give me the correspondance table — what plays the role of the umpire, the rulebook, the players, the crowd, the replay booth — then the places it breaks, especially around who hires the umpire and whether the constitution is actually a clear rulebook or more like an open-textured standard that needs interpretation. also, does the analogy quietly smuggle in a dworkin-style right-answer thesis — that every legal question has one correct call — and if so does that assumption still stand? then there's the dead-hand problem: past generations writing rules that bind present ones. does the umpire image hide that, or make it worse? finally, tell me whether the defense of judicial review survives once the disanalogies are laid out, or whether the umpire picture was carrying the whole argument. and if the analogy collapses, whats a better one?
