I'm enrolled in a graduate seminar on comparative political ethics, and in a recent session on institutional design a classmate appealed to the Confucian injunction of zhengming—commonly glossed as rectifying names—to defend a rigid view of organizational accountability: she argued that anyone holding the title of director ought automatically to bear the full normative weight of that role, irrespective of individual competence, contextual constraints, or delegated authority. The move struck me as ambiguous in a productive way, but I could not pin down exactly where the ambiguity lay. Could you disambiguate the slogan of zhengming into its possible precise claims, laying them out as a menu rather than selecting one? I want to distinguish at minimum: a trivial semantic claim about language tracking reality; a sociological claim about hierarchical order; an essentialist claim that roles carry fixed normative essences; and the more radical performative interpretation—one I have encountered only in recent philosophical reconstructions of Confucianism—according to which naming is a ritual speech-act that dynamically constitutes obligations within a specific relational context rather than assigning static properties to individuals. For each reading, please indicate whether it is defensible within Confucian role ethics, whether it actually supports my classmate's conclusion about job titles, and whether it risks collapsing into either mere tautology or an implausible form of role essentialism.

In particular, I am interested in how the performative reading differs from the essentialist one when applied to modern bureaucratic roles that lack the familial and ritual embedding of classical Confucian contexts. Does the performative version require that roles be enacted within a living tradition of ritual practice to generate any normative force at all, or can it be abstracted into secular institutional ethics? And if it can be abstracted, does it still yield the rigid accountability my classmate wants, or does it instead imply that obligations are continuously renegotiated through the very performance of the role? I need these distinctions spelled out clearly so that I can return to the discussion and ask which specific thesis she intends to invoke, without attributing to her a position that depends on ritual contexts she has never mentioned.
