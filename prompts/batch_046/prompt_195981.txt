The thesis I am defending is what I call controlled presentism. I argue that historians should not attempt to bracket present-day normative categories—such as structural violence, systemic exploitation, or rights-based framing—because such bracketing is epistemically impossible and, when attempted, merely hides the framing rather than eliminating it. Instead, I propose a two-phase practice: in Phase One, the historian deliberately deploys present categories as explicit heuristic scaffolds to generate research questions, identify archival silences, and structure comparative inquiry; in Phase Two, the historian conducts strict contextual reconstruction, translating all explanatory claims into period-specific vocabulary and prohibiting the heuristic categories from determining evidentiary weight or causal attribution. I claim this improves explanatory power without committing the anachronism contextualists fear, provided the boundary between scaffolding and explanation is made transparent and institutionally auditable.
