In a philosophy of science tutorial, a student kept dismissing counterfactual analyses of causation by repeating the slogan 'no intervention, no causation' — treating it as self-evident that causal relations vanish wherever direct manipulation is impossible. Before I let that stand, I want the claim precisely unpacked. Lay out the distinct theses it might express: the methodological claim that causal discovery requires experimental control; the metaphysical claim that causation just is invariant stability under idealised intervention; the epistemic claim that counterfactuals about non-manipulable variables are vacuous; and any stronger or weaker variants. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive interventionist position, or false; and indicate which reading actually supports dismissing counterfactual reasoning in domains like climate dynamics or cosmological constants, where intervention is physically impossible. I am particularly interested in how each reading treats redundant causation and pre-emption, where counterfactual dependence and interventionist stability come apart. Present your answer as a menu of readings with verdicts, and please write in British English.
