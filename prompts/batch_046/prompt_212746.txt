the usual metaphysics of grounding treats fundamentality like a layer cake—some stuff is absolutely basic (fields, particles, whatever) and everything else is built out of it via ontological dependence. but i'm skeptical this vertical picture survives contact with actual physics. in effective field theories, what's "fundamental" is scale-dependent: at low energies protons are basic, at higher energies they're composite, and there's no god's-eye scale that's metaphysically priviledged. so instead of asking "what grounds what?" in a hierarchical sense, maybe we should think of grounding as explanatory substitutability across descriptive scales—what i'm calling lateral grounding. on this view, saying "the table is grounded in atoms" isn't claiming atoms are ontologically prior; it's saying the atomic description can do the explanitory work of the table-description in micro-structure contexts, and vice versa in other contexts.

i also think quantum mechanics pushes this: if the wavefunction is fundamental but particle ontology is derivative or ambiguous, insisting on strict vertical grounding forces us to invent phantom entities just to preserve the hierarchy.
