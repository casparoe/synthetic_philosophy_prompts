hey, im drafting an essay on tranquilism — the view that only the absence of craving and suffering is genuinely good, not the presence of pleasure or satisfaction. im trying out a somewhat niche angle im calling "counterfactual craving debt." my claim is that the tranquil value of a craving-free state depends on whether that craving was actually possible or present in a beings modal history. so a being that never could crave isnt in a good state just by lacking craving; but a being that had cravings and now lacks them is in the best possible state.

i think this distinguishes tranquilism from benatars asymmetry, since benatar focuses on potential people being harmed or benefitted by coming into existence, whereas im focused on the modal history of actual conscious beings.

heres my rough outline:
- tranquilism is usually treated as negative hedonism, but thats too psychologistic
- craving is dispositional, suffering is occurrent — theyre not symmetrical
- counterfactual craving debt means absence only carries tranquil value if theres a baseline of prior or potential presence
- this avoids the empty-world objection: a world with no beings isnt good, since theres no debt to cancel
