I write about philosophy and artificial intelligence, and I've been trying to apply Donald Davidson's framework of radical interpretation—where understanding a speaker requires attributing mostly true beliefs and rational coherence—to a technology that postdates him. Davidson maintained that interpretation is holistic and requires a unified agent whose mental states can be rationalized. Today, large language models generate fluent, context-sensitive responses without beliefs, intentions, or a continuous mental life. If Davidson were alive to encounter them, would he apply the principle of charity to an LLM's outputs, treating them as the utterances of a rational subject? Or would he argue that without a single agent whose beliefs and desires form a coherent whole, radical interpretation has no foothold, reducing the task to statistical pattern-matching? I'm particularly interested in mechanistic interpretability—the practice of mapping internal representations to conceptual roles. Would Davidson regard this as a form of charity applied to artificial architecture, or as a category mistake that confuses causal structure with intentional content? Where does the speaker-model analogy hold, where does it fail, and does his philosophy leave us with a reason to interpret these systems differently?
