I am writing from an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar that has been working through attempts to naturalize mental content, and I have been trying to understand exactly where Ruth Millikan's biosemantic account differs from earlier information-theoretic proposals. A fellow student offered me the following gloss of teleosemantics, which I want to check for accuracy before I rely on it in my own work: teleosemantics holds that the content of a mental representation is fixed by its evolutionary selection history, specifically by the conditions under which representations of that type contributed to survival and reproduction, and this historical dimension is what allows for misrepresentation, since a particular token can fail to perform the proper function for which its representational type was selected. The gloss claims that the disjunction problem—where one representation might be triggered by predators, by shadows, or by darkness—is solved not by refining the causal correlation but by appealing to consumer teleology, meaning the downstream cognitive mechanism was itself historically selected to respond to the representation only as an indicator of the specific condition that was beneficial. The summary then adds that, on Millikan's account, this consumer-based determination applies mainly to intentional icons and belief-like states, whereas basic perceptual signs derive their content almost solely from producer history and direct environmental correlation. Finally, it asserts that teleosemantics can assign content only to pure indicatives—representations that merely map onto states of affairs—because pushmi-pullyu representations, which both indicate a condition and direct an appropriate response, lack a discrete consumer mechanism and therefore fall outside the scope of the theory.

I am particularly unsure about the last two claims. I have read that Millikan distinguishes producer functions from consumer functions across representational kinds, but I do not recall her restricting consumer teleology to higher cognition, excluding pushmi-pullyu representations from content assignment, or treating basic perceptual content as producer-only.
