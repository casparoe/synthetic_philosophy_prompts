everyone in the scalable oversight crowd keeps saying AI debate is basically like a courtroom: two lawyers with opposing interests, one judge who doesnt need to be an expert, and truth comes out because lies get caught in cross-examination. they use it to license the claim that we can supervise systems far smarter than us without actually understanding their reasoning. can you audit the analogy? i want the correspondence table first -- lawyer, judge, jury, rules of evidence, the opposing interests -- then the cracks: do the debaters actually have adversarial interests, or are they just two instances of the same model with different prompts? what happens to cross-examination when both sides share weights and training data? and what does the courtroom framing quietly import about judicial reasoning that might not hold for a human skimming a transcript? does the conclusion -- that debate solves scalable oversight -- survive, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument? if its broken, whats a better comparison?
