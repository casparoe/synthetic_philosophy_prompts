I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of action and behavioral economics serving on our university's Behavioral Intervention Review Board. The board must render a decision on a pilot proposal from the student wellness office that applies hyperbolic-discounting commitment contracts to chronic academic procrastination. Under the scheme, students voluntarily deposit funds that are forfeited to an organization they oppose should they fail to meet self-imposed coursework deadlines; proponents argue this corrects present-biased preferences and thereby addresses weakness of will—specifically, akrasia reconceived as diachronic preference reversal—while critics, including a clinical psychologist and a moral philosopher on the board, maintain that the model collapses the normative architecture of akrasia into intertemporal bargaining. They contend that genuine weakness of will involves violating one's own all-things-considered judgment in the moment of action, not merely failing to execute a prior plan, and that framing autonomy failure as a market externality risks substituting strategic self-binding for the capacity for practical reasoning.

Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course—approval, conditional approval, or rejection—with explicit conditions if appropriate, and it should provide reasoning that the wellness office could actually address in a revised protocol. The memo must engage seriously with the critics' concern that hyperbolic discounting imports an economic analogy which may obscure rather than illuminate the phenomenology and normative stakes of akratic action, and it should include an honest assessment of the strongest consideration on the side you do not endorse. The deliverable should be a formal board document, not personal advice, with an actionable bottom line and criteria reviewers can apply.
