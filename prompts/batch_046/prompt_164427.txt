I serve on a national secondary-education equity board that must vote next month on revising our career-guidance framework. Our staff presented cross-national data showing that in countries with higher gender-equality indices, gender segregation in career choice is more pronounced—the gender-equality paradox. A recent internal conceptual review (not in the public literature) advanced what it calls the structural-latency thesis: that when structural barriers fall, gender identity shifts from a compelled social role to a voluntary distinction, so career preference becomes a domain of self-expression rather than survival strategy, which explains why egalitarian contexts can produce more gendered outcomes without implying biological determinism. The board must decide whether to replace our current neutral-presentation guidance model with a structured-intervention framework that actively presents counter-stereotypical pathways in gender-imbalanced fields, coupled with transparency about why the intervention exists.

Please draft the short formal memo I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action—including a 90-day pilot implementation timeline with defined evaluation points—and it should treat the philosophical stakes seriously: what the paradox implies about nature, culture, and justice, and whether equality requires neutral procedures or actively engineered choice architectures. The memo must also engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the other side—namely, that gendered preferences in egalitarian societies may reflect authentic autonomy that state-guided framing risks violating.

Structure the response as follows:

1. Executive Summary and Concrete Recommendation (approximately 250 words): state the bottom-line decision, the 90-day pilot structure, and the evaluation criteria.
2. The Philosophical Stakes: Nature, Culture, and Structural Latency (approximately 350 words): explain how the paradox interacts with the structural-latency thesis, and what follows for whether gendered choices are constructed, revealed, or justified.
3. The Strongest Counter-Argument: Preference Autonomy and Anti-Paternalism (approximately 300 words): present the autonomy-based objection in its most compelling form, then show why it does not defeat the recommendation but does impose genuine constraints.
4. Implementation Conditions and Limits (approximately 200 words): specify what must be true for the pilot to continue, what would trigger suspension, and how the board should communicate the rationale to families and schools without overstating scientific certainty.

Write in the voice of a board member addressing colleagues, not an academic essay. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests; the board needs reasoning it can act on now.
