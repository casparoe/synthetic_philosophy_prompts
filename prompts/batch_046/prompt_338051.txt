After a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a friend about whether large language models can be said to literally hold beliefs, I realized we were talking past each other because neither of us had spelled out what an adequate theory in this space would even need to accomplish. So I want the full spec first.

Write the desiderata that any theory of attributing propositional attitudes—especially belief—to artificial systems through the intentional stance must satisfy to count as adequate. I am not asking for a literature review, a bibliography, or citations; I want the criteria themselves.

For each desideratum, specify:
1. The motivating case or phenomenon it must cover—such as a system that maintains consistent false assertions across diverse prompts but revises them only when confronted with a specifically structured contradiction; or a system that generates coherent counterfactual explanations of why it produced one output rather than another; or architectures where behavioral stability tracks semantic content rather than surface syntax.
2. The distinction it must preserve—e.g., between instrumentally useful prediction and genuine representational attribution; between belief and knowledge; between properties of individual parameters and emergent system-level states.
3. Whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp (for instance, demands for biological realism versus demands for functional equivalence).

Then, identify which single requirement does the most damage to popular existing positions, and flag any subset of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—particularly any tension between requiring that the attributed mental state play a causal-explanatory role inside the system and requiring predictive parity with human belief-attribution practices.

Finally, briefly indicate how a strictly instrumentalist interpretation of the intentional stance and a more ontologically committed realist interpretation fare against your list.

Please write the answer in American English, and avoid citations, reading lists, or references to specific texts.
