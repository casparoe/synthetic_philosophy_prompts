I'm working through an argument that seems to show both the counterfactual (Lewis-style) and the interventionist (Pearl-style) theories of causation fail for climate science, and I can't see the exit. Here are the steps:

(1) The counterfactual account holds that C causes E only if, in the nearest possible worlds where C does not occur (with laws and appropriate background conditions held fixed), E does not occur.
(2) The interventionist account holds that C causes E only if there exists an intervention on C—breaking its structural equation while leaving all other equations intact—that produces a change in E.
(3) Take the claim that anthropogenic CO2 emissions cause global mean surface temperature rise.
(4) For the counterfactual test, we must imagine the nearest world without anthropogenic CO2. But in that world industrialization is absent, which also removes aerosol cooling and changes land-use patterns; temperature might still have risen through other pathways, or fallen further. So the counterfactual absence of CO2 does not determinately yield the absence of warming.
(5) Therefore, on the counterfactual account, anthropogenic CO2 is not established as a cause of warming.
(6) For the interventionist test, we apply the do-operator to CO2 in a climate model. But in the actual coupled system, CO2, temperature, and water-vapor feedback are linked through interdependent structural equations: breaking the CO2 equation alone violates the model's internal consistency, because the temperature equation receives exogenous input that no longer propagates through the CO2 node, and the water-vapor equation (which depends on temperature) is left with an orphaned dependency.
(7) Hence there is no structurally valid intervention on CO2 that respects modularity; the do-operator is inapplicable.
(8) So on the interventionist account too, CO2 is not a cause of warming.
(9) Both theories therefore deny a well-established scientific causal claim.
(10) This suggests that either causation is not what these theories say it is, or climate science is systematically mistaken about causation.
(11) The second disjunct is implausible, so both theories should be rejected.

Which step—or pair of steps—should I reject? I want to keep the idea that climate science gets causation right, but I'm willing to pay the price of weakening either the counterfactual or the interventionist framework (or both).
