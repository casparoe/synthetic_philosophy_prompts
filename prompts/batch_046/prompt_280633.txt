I'm preparing a poetry unit for a seminar on practical reason, and I want a villanelle that works as both a lyric poem and a precise philosophical diagram. The subject should be Derek Parfit's hitchhiker thought experiment—the scenario in which a driver agrees to take a passenger to town only if she trusts he will pay upon arrival, even though once they are near the destination the passenger has no further instrumental reason to pay, so that rationality appears to recommend both making the promise and breaking it.

Write a villanelle of nineteen lines in which the two refrains, alternating according to the standard A1-b-A2 scheme, are the passenger's competing commitments: the first refrain should carry the promise made at the outset ("I will pay you when we reach the town"), and the second should carry the rationalization upon arrival ("There is no reason left to keep my word"). Map the six tercets onto the stages of the argument—offer, acceptance, journey, arrival, refusal, and the driver's silent recognition—so that the rhyme scheme forces the two refrains into ever-closer sonic proximity, mirroring the collapse of the precommitment into its own denial. The closing quatrain must bring both refrains together without resolving their tension: bind them in rhyme and meter even as their meanings remain irreconcilable. Render the logic precisely enough that a reader acquainted with debates about rational irrationality and self-binding decisions will recognize the exact structure, but avoid explicit technical terms such as "precommitment," "instrumental rationality," or "time-slice egoism," allowing the narrative and the formal repetition to carry the philosophical weight.
