Following an extended and rather exhausting debate with a close friend about whether the European Union’s democratic deficit is an inevitable structural feature of ever-closer union rather than a correctable design flaw, I found myself wondering how a thinker committed to popular sovereignty and the direct expression of collective will might assess institutional arrangements he could never have imagined. Specifically, I would like you to predict how Jean-Jacques Rousseau might have evaluated the tension between pooled sovereignty in a supranational polity and the democratic requirement that law express the will of an identifiable people. Would he have interpreted the delegation of regulatory authority to non-elected technocratic bodies—particularly in domains such as digital market harmonization and cross-border data governance—as a legitimate extension of collective self-rule across borders, or as a corruption of sovereignty that fragments the general will into administratively managed fragments? In reasoning through his likely position, please consider whether his conception of the social contract can be scaled beyond the nation-state without becoming, in his terms, a false contract imposed by distant magistrates, and do so without appealing to modern secondary commentators or catalogues of scholarship.

To ground the prediction further, I am especially interested in how Rousseau might have reacted to the specific claim that democratic accountability can be preserved even when legislative initiative originates in expert commissions and is ratified only indirectly through national ministers, rather than through direct popular assemblies. Given his well-known suspicion of partial associations and his insistence that sovereignty is inalienable, do you think he would have seen the EU’s combination of intergovernmental bargaining and supranational legal supremacy as a novel form of legitimate federation, or rather as a sophisticated mechanism for disguising domination by particular interests under the language of universal cooperation? Please develop your answer with conceptual precision, addressing both the problem of ever-closer union and the democratic deficit, while treating Rousseau’s core commitments as living constraints rather than historical curiosities.
