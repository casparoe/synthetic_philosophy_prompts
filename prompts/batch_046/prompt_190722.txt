I sit on my university's capital projects and sustainability committee. For Thursday's vote we face a vendor proposal for the rare-manuscript vault: an emergency climate protocol that executes an infinite sequence of filter-state toggles—active, standby, active—compressed into the two minutes before a predicted humidity spike, leaving no final toggle and therefore no determinate endpoint state. The vendor frames this as a legitimate supertask maintenance routine; our facilities director counters that without a well-defined final state the contract's "verified preservation" clause is unsatisfiable, invoking the kind of limit-state indeterminacy familiar from Thomson's lamp. Some members want outright rejection on grounds of conceptual incoherence; others want conditional approval paired with a manual fallback check at the limit. Please draft the committee's decision memo: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions; specify a concrete implementation time frame and a verification protocol that addresses the endpoint problem; treat the supertask objection seriously rather than dismissing it as science fiction; and include an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side.
