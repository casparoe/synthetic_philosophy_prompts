I recently came across an essay by an unnamed philosopher of mathematics summarizing Douglas Hofstadter’s discussion of the MU-puzzle and the notion of jumping out of the system—JOOTSing—in his well-known book. The essay presents the puzzle as a formal system with four rules that manipulate strings composed of M, I, and U, starting from the axiom MI. It notes that within these rules one can never derive the string MU, and it claims that Hofstadter offers this as a direct illustration of Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem: MU is true in the sense that it is unreachable, yet formally unprovable inside the system. The essay then explains that the resolution requires stepping outside to recognize that every derivable string preserves an invariant—specifically, the number of I’s is never divisible by three—which makes the unreachability of MU obvious from a meta-level perspective. Building on this, the author concludes that JOOTSing reveals a universal limitation of formal systems, namely that no system can ever prove its own consistency, and that human cognition is categorically different from machine computation because only minds can execute these meta-level leaps, whereas any mechanism confined to the rules remains permanently trapped inside. I am inclined to think the essay captures Hofstadter’s spirit but perhaps overreaches in its conclusions. Could you audit this summary?
