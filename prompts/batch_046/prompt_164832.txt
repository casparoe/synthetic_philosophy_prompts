Read the 1957 film Twelve Angry Men through the Condorcet jury theorem and its critiques from social epistemology. The theorem predicts that independent jurors with better-than-random competence will reach correct verdicts by majority rule, yet the film depicts intense social pressure, authority deference, emotional contagion, and near-unanimous false consensus—dynamics that violate the independence assumption. Does the narrative therefore dramatize a failure mode of collective decision theory rather than an illustration of it? Examine the initial 11-1 vote and the ostracism of Juror 8; the switchblade demonstration as an intervention that restores individual reasoning; and the eyeglasses revelation that undermines witness credibility. In particular, ask whether Juror 8 functions not as a standard voter but as a necessary source of epistemic noise that breaks an informational cascade, and whether the film implies that justice requires violating independence—through confrontation and advocacy—rather than achieving it. Conclude by saying what the film contributes to democratic epistemology that the formal mathematics of the jury theorem does not capture on its own. Please answer in American English.
