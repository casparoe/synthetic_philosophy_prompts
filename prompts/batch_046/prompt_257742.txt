Write the desiderata that a theory of the good life must satisfy if it is to do justice to Keynes's distinctive vision: that economic abundance ought eventually to reduce necessary labor to a small fraction of life, liberating people for goods—friendship, aesthetic appreciation, love of knowledge—that are valuable for their own sake, while preserving the social conditions and personal capacities that make such goods genuinely available. Give the motivating case behind each requirement, including objections drawn from positional goods, status competition, and the possibility that leisure without structure erodes the very virtues it requires. Note any tensions that seem irreconcilable. Then briefly score hedonism, an Aristotelian account of flourishing, and a capabilities approach against your list, identifying which desideratum does the heaviest work in ruling out contenders.
