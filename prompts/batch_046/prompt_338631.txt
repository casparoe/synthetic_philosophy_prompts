For the past year I have been navigating a workplace hierarchy in which my expertise is formally acknowledged yet systematically treated as subordinate to that of a senior colleague; trying to understand why this feels like a failure of recognition rather than mere disrespect led me back to Hegel's master-slave dialectic, not as a historical artifact but as a live conceptual map. I want to build a decision-tree taxonomy for a philosophy discussion group that sorts interpretations of the dialectic by the commitments they entail about self-consciousness, equality, and resolution—without relying on readers to recognize authorial labels. The instrument should begin from a concrete counterfactual (whether an isolated being could achieve full self-consciousness) and branch through questions about whether recognition requires an equal other, whether a struggle involving existential or functional risk is necessary, and whether modern institutional structures can stabilize mutual recognition.

Please design the tree so that each node is a diagnostic claim or case that genuinely divides positions, phrased as a question answerable without prior knowledge of which interpretation it selects. The leaves should cover: (i) an internalist denial that recognition is constitutive; (ii) a hierarchical view that accepts recognition from an unequal other; (iii) a cooperative view that requires equality but rejects the necessity of mortal struggle, allowing functional equivalents like systemic social risk; (iv) a classical resolution view in which struggle eventually produces universal recognition within a rational order; (v) a critical asymmetry view that demands equality but holds the dialectic never fully resolves, leaving structural misrecognition; and (vi) an institutional-optimist view that locates adequate recognition in differentiated modern spheres such as intimate attachment, legal status, and achievement. For any logically available branch that remains unoccupied—such as hierarchical recognition combined with the judgment that it is ultimately hollow—briefly note the combination and diagnose whether its absence is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Flag any pairs of leaves that the tree cannot cleanly separate without adding an explicit node about historical teleology or about whether recognition must be mutual rather than merely reciprocal. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or named scholars to be mapped onto leaves; the aim is to classify the logical space of commitments, not to produce an authorial index.
