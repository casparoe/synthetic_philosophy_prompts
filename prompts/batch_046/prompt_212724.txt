I'm working to stabilize my own position on reduction, emergence, and scientific explanation, and I want an audit of whether the pieces actually fit together. Here are my commitments:

1. Minimal physicalism: every concrete particular is ultimately composed of physical entities arranged in physical ways, and there are no non-physical substances or fundamental forces.
2. Anti-greedy-reductionism: the explanatory patterns of chemistry, evolutionary biology, and cognitive science are not merely provisional stand-ins for microphysical descriptions; they identify autonomous regularities that remain intact even if physics is complete.
3. Crane-only explanation: all legitimate explanation proceeds by decomposing a phenomenon into simpler, previously understood components and showing how they combine. I reject "skyhooks" — appeals to irreducible higher-level forces or purposes that are not themselves constructed out of lower-level parts.
4. Structural emergence only: genuinely emergent properties are real but only in an epistemic or organizational sense; they arise from complexity and intractability, not from new fundamental laws or ontologically independent properties.
5. Consciousness as organizational emergence: there is nothing it is like to be a thermostat, but there is something it is like to be a system with integrated information above a specific organizational threshold; consciousness is therefore an emergent feature of certain complex physical arrangements.
6. Defensible downward causation: once an emergent organizational pattern is instantiated, it can constrain the dynamics of its constituent parts in ways not fully captured by those parts' individual behaviors, without violating the causal closure of the physical domain.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe each real tension is. For each tension, specify whether it stems from the internal logic of the views or from the standard motivations people usually offer for them; then say what the cheapest fix would be and exactly what it costs me in terms of the other commitments. Finally, tell me which single revision — abandoning or weakening one of these six — would clean up the most tensions at once, and whether that revision pushes me closer to greedy reductionism, to a skyhook view, or to some other recognizable position. Be precise about whether downward causation, on my framing, functions as a hidden skyhook.
