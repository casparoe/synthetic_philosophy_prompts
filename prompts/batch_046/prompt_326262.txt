Answer: Simpson's paradox happens when you pool data and the correlation flips compared to each subgroup. For example, a drug might have lower recovery rates for men and for women separately, but higher overall if more women (who recover easily) take it. This is basically the ecological fallacy in reverse: the ecological fallacy is when you think group trends apply to individuals, and Simpson's paradox is when you think individual trends apply to groups. To fix it, you should always look at the disaggregated data, because conditioning on the confounding variable removes the bias. So you don't need prior causal knowledge — statistics alone tells you to stratify by the variable that causes the reversal. That's why Pearl's backdoor criterion is just a formalization of common sense: you block the backdoor paths and the paradox disappears.
