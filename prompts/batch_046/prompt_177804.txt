I serve on my university's interdisciplinary research ethics board. A proposal has come before us from a joint philosophy-of-science and developmental-psychology group. They intend to study how adolescents understand temporal causation, but they frame the study through a specific interpretation of Kurt Gödel's rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations. Their core claim is that because Gödel's metric permits closed timelike curves and eliminates a global time coordinate, any psychological model assuming an objective linear ordering of cognitive development—such as standard stage theories—rests on a metaphysically false premise. On this basis, they propose two unconventional methodological choices: first, they will include in the informed-consent document a brief philosophical argument stating that objective chronological time does not exist, which they defend as necessary theoretical disclosure; second, they will randomize the temporal sequence of survey items in a pattern they describe as simulating non-orientable temporal topology, arguing that linear presentation would bias responses by imposing a false metaphysics.

Half the board regards this as an interesting but essentially harmless framing of an otherwise standard survey study; they contend that the consent language is merely theoretical context and that the randomized ordering is a legitimate design feature. The other half holds that the consent form introduces a conceptual confusion that undermines genuinely informed consent—participants are unlikely to recognize that the metaphysical claim is contested rather than established physics—and that the study conflates a cosmological model with conditions appropriate to cognitive testing, rendering meaningful risk assessment impossible.

Please draft the board's decision memo, to be circulated before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with specific conditions—together with a clear time frame for any required revisions or resubmission. The reasoning should take the researchers' Gödelian framing seriously rather than dismissing it as jargon, but it must also engage the strongest concern of those members who believe that metaphysical controversy is being smuggled into a consent process that demands clarity, not philosophical debate. Write it as a formal document the board could actually circulate and to which the researchers could respond directly.
