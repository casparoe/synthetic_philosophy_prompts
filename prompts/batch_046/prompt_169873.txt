I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about metaphysical grounding, especially when absences or negative facts get into the picture, and my gut reactions are all over teh place. Here's where I stand on specific cases.

The statue weighs 500 lbs because the clay weighs 500 lbs — that feels like straightforward grounding, I'm fully on board.

The party is happening because people are dancing and drinking in that room — social fact grounded in mental and physical stuff, also seems right.

But then there's a shadow cast by a tree. The shadow being present feels grounded in the tree blocking light. However, the shadow's darkness — the fact that it's not illuminated — feels like pure absence. I don't think that absence is grounded in anything positive; it's just photons not being there.

Now compare that to a meeting I attended last week with no agenda. The absence of an agenda wasn't accidental; the chair made a point of not sending one. So here, the absence feels genuinely grounded in her decision. Same kind of negative fact, totally different intuition.

Then there's the hole in a donut. The hole is literally dough not being there, yet it's also part of the donut's shape. Sometimes I think the hole is grounded in the dough being arranged around it; other times I think it's just an ungrounded absence that happens to be surrounded. I'm genuinely going back and forth on this one.

Also, consider an unjust law. I lean toward saying its injustice is grounded in the actual social practices and institutions, not in some abstract moral realm. But I'm unsure whether I'm grounding the injustice in the presence of oppressive practices, or in the absence of fairness within those practices.

Is there a principle that captures which absences count as grounded? Maybe the distinction is between absences that are causally maintained versus ones that are just failures, but the meeting case breaks that since the absence is maintained by a positive choice. Or maybe grounding only applies to positive existents and I'm wrong about the agenda case.

Can you propose one or two precise principles that accommodate most of my verdicts, be explicit about which ones they have to reject, and weigh in on the donut hole? Also, does grounding occure differently for physical absences versus social ones?

Please separate what's settled in the metaphysics literature from what's genuinely contested, and don't present a disputed view as if it's the standard consensus.
