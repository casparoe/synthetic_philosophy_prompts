I've been teaching eliminative materialism for years and think I've finally settled on a view that's a bit off from Churchland's usual line. I think beliefs and desires arent just wrong folk-psychological categories waiting to be replaced by neuroscience; they're closer to useful social fictions—predictive tools we deploy in conversation that dont correspond to any real neural architecture, but that doesnt mean we should purge them from everyday talk, only from our ontology. So it's eliminativist about ontology but conservative about linguistic practice. What would actually count against this? I want the real defeaters—not just the usual 'but we feel belief!' reflex—but also whether empirical work shows folk-psychological predictions tracking genuine neural patterns, or whether philosophical arguments show you cant sustain a 'useful fiction' without some underlying representational state doing the actual work. Rank them if you can, from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones defenders of this kind of view would concede versus which they'd dismiss. Seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and dont present my hybrid stance as the consensus—it's definatly not.
