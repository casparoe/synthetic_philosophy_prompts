I'm putting together a taxonomy handout for an upper-level moral philosophy seminar on the doctrine of double effect and its application to redirective harm. Rather than sorting students by the labels they prefer, I want a decision tree built from three binary diagnostic splits that classify positions by the judgments they actually make on concrete cases. The root should ask whether there is any moral difference between the standard switch case—pulling a lever to divert a tram onto a side track where one person dies, saving five—and the footbridge case—pushing a large person onto the tracks to stop the tram. The left branch (no moral difference) should split again on whether both actions are permissible or both are forbidden, yielding a consequentialist leaf and an absolute-prohibition leaf. The right branch (there is a difference) should split on whether the asymmetry is explained by the agent's intention together with the means-end distinction—the standard Thomistic double-effect position—or by a structural feature of direct bodily involvement that operates independently of intention, yielding a direct-contact prohibitionist view.

Every leaf reached through a "permitted" path must then face a third split involving a niche sustained-intervention case I am calling the loop protocol: a diverted track loops back onto the main line after exactly sixty seconds, trapping one maintenance worker, and the agent must physically hold the lever continuously for the full minute to maintain the diversion, since releasing it sends the tram back toward the five. This split should ask whether maintaining that sustained causal control makes the diversion morally equivalent to the footbridge push or preserves its status as an indirect redirection. Each of the eight logical combinations should appear as a leaf carrying a descriptive name (or "unnamed / unstable" if unoccupied) plus exactly one sentence on its most awkward or underappreciated commitment—paying special attention to combinations that permit the switch and the loop but forbid the footbridge, or that forbid all three despite accepting proportionality reasoning in other contexts. Please flag explicitly any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate, especially whether the standard double-effect position and the direct-contact view diverge on the loop protocol or collapse into the same judgment. Keep the tree to exactly three levels of binary branches, design it for students who have not yet mastered the secondary literature, and do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
