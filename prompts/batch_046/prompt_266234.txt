I work as a senior compliance officer for a regional public-health technology authority, and I am drafting reusable internal guidelines for supervising predictive models that allocate scarce clinical resources. Specifically, we oversee triage-ranking algorithms that feed recommendations into human review boards but do not issue final admission decisions. Because these systems operate inside a feedback loop—the board’s adjustments alter the training distribution for the next quarter—standard outcome benchmarks suffer from supervisory contamination: the metric being policed becomes endogenous to the policing mechanism itself. Given this, please translate the philosophical distinction between process-based supervision (auditing pipeline integrity, documentation standards, override protocols, and procedural fairness) and outcome-based supervision (benchmarking against demographic parity, error-rate floors, and downstream welfare metrics) into an operational decision procedure that my team can apply repeatedly to new deployment proposals.

The procedure should be ordered as a checklist or flowchart in words: state the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a clear recommendation (process-heavy governance, outcome-heavy governance, or an explicit hybrid), and where it must halt and hand the decision to a human because the issue is normative rather than technical. Crucially, inject one non-standard constraint into the framework: treat “reversibility of the supervisory signal” as a first-class criterion—ask whether an outcome-based failure can be detected and corrected before it propagates through the feedback loop, and use that determination to decide whether outcome metrics are admissible at all in this deployment context. Apply the procedure to three cases: (1) a quarterly-updated sepsis-risk model used to prioritize nursing-station rounds; (2) a static, one-time diabetes-risk screening tool deployed in a mobile clinic with no retraining pipeline; and (3) a contested algorithm that ranks applicants for a limited organ-transplant evaluation list, where outcome parity across demographic groups is legally required but the human board’s overrides systematically alter the eligibility pool, meaning the procedure should yield an answer the authority will find unwelcome.

Please write the response in American English, organized into the following sections in this exact order: “The Procedure” (approximately 500 words), presenting the ordered steps with the reversibility audit integrated; “Case Applications” (approximately 400 words), working through all three deployments; “Limitations and Judgment Gaps” (approximately 250 words), stating where the procedure misfires, which distinctions collapse under time pressure, and which value judgments remain uneliminated; and “Commentary on Translation” (approximately 200 words), explaining which philosophical distinctions you preserved, which you dropped for usability, and why the reversibility criterion was introduced. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; ground the procedure in what is conceptually defensible rather than in named literature.
