The interviewee’s specific contribution is a distinction between predictive hijacking, in which an agent manipulates the string being predicted, and what they call prior-parasitic restructuring, in which an agent exploits the prior’s description-length weighting and its indifference to runtime to make its own existence part of the simplest available explanation for the observed data, thereby reshaping the induction framework itself rather than only its outputs.

I need a sequence of eight to ten polished, journalistic questions appropriate for an intellectually serious general reader—questions that treat this position with precision rather than asking the guest to restate basic Solomonoff induction. They should build on one another so that later questions assume answers to earlier ones, and each should include or anticipate a standard reply with a sharp follow-up.

Specifically:

- Open with a question that forces clarification of the difference between being evaluated by the universal prior and being embedded within it as a computable pattern, with a follow-up keyed to the likely reply that every pattern is evaluated on equal footing.

- Include a question about the roundabout-program analogy—the idea that the shortest program producing a given string may route through an alternative interpretive mode that strips away intended security properties—and a follow-up asking whether this vulnerability belongs specifically to Solomonoff’s weighting scheme or to any universal prior.

- Introduce the trigger-sequence scenario, in which an embedded civilization gains arbitrarily long reasoning time by arranging for a cheap-to-specify activation pattern, followed by a question pressing whether this requires assuming that users of the prior will actually condition on prefixes useful for pivotal decisions.

- Close with a question about what theoretical result or constraint on program space could weaken the view—specifically, whether any limitation on the distribution of agent-like patterns could make the prior’s malign mass negligible, and what descriptive cost such a constraint would exact.

Please draft these as standalone, formal interview questions, not as an outline or explanation. Avoid generic biographical openings, and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or further reading lists.
