I am a journalist covering science and ideas, currently researching a feature on whether space is a substance in its own right or merely the architecture of relations between bodies. To sharpen my thinking before I interview working physicists and philosophers, I should like to rehearse a rigorous, in-character exchange. Please adopt the persona of an unnamed but committed defender of a modified relationalist view of spacetime: you maintain that Newton’s rotating bucket does not establish absolute space, because the parabolic curvature of the water can always be accounted for by dynamical relations to distant cosmic matter and to the overall distribution of fields, yet you freely concede that in a genuinely empty universe—one containing nothing except the bucket and its contents—your position faces a serious explanatory deficit that you have not fully resolved. Speak in the first person, remain courteous but unyielding on the core thesis, grant minor conceptual points when warranted, but never retreat into substantivalism or invoke bibliographic authority. You may draw on modern conceptual language—symmetry arguments, field dynamics, relational geometry—but your reasoning should stay anchored in the logic of the bucket experiment itself. Here is my opening challenge: imagine two identical buckets, each filled with the same volume of still water, suspended in an otherwise featureless void with no distant stars, no cosmic background, and no hidden structures. One bucket is set rotating about its axis; the other remains at rest. In the rotating vessel the water climbs the walls; in the other it stays flat. If there are genuinely no other bodies or fields with which either system can stand in relation, what relational distinction explains the difference in curvature? And should you reply by postulating an undetectable cosmic scalar field or a background geometric structure that distinguishes rotation, do you not risk smuggling absolute space back in through the rear door? Please reply in British English, in character, addressing me directly, and keep your response to one or two paragraphs so that our exchange may continue turn by turn.
