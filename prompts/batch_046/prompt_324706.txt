I am drafting a position paper for my philosophy of technology seminar on what it would actually take for a theory of civilizational technological survival to be adequate. Rather than surveying existing accounts, I want the conceptual specification first, and I need it to stand without reference to sources I could only look up.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of how a complex society survives its own technological inheritance—particularly in light of Von Neumann’s concerns about self-replicating automata and the decoupling of technological capability from collective understanding—must deliver. For each requirement, give the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it. These should include, at minimum: the need to distinguish local system failure from global cascade; the need to account for technologies that replicate or scale faster than the regulatory institutions meant to govern them; the need to handle what we might call epistemic inheritance failure, in which a civilization retains operational artifacts whose original design rationale has been partially or fully lost; and the need to preserve a real distinction between accidental collapse and structurally inevitable vulnerability, without collapsing one into the other. For each desideratum, flag whether it is non-negotiable for any theory in this space or whether it is a preference of a particular camp—say, the camp that treats technological trajectories as deterministically autonomous versus the camp that treats them as fully open to social governance.

Then identify any subset of these requirements that appears jointly unsatisfiable given basic conceptual or historical constraints. I am especially interested in whether the demand for complete predictive transparency about self-replicating or self-modifying systems can be reconciled with the fact that technological knowledge is always partly tacit, distributed, and subject to erosion across generations. If there is a genuine conflict, say exactly which desiderata are in tension and why the tension is not merely practical but conceptual.

Finally, briefly score three broad approaches against the list—do not do this exhaustively, just indicate where each clearly fails and where it uniquely succeeds: a purely engineering-resilience framework, an institutional-governance framework, and an automata-theoretic framework in Von Neumann’s lineage. Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal conflicts rather than on the scorecard.

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. I am working without web access and need the argument to depend only on conceptual structure and generally available knowledge.
