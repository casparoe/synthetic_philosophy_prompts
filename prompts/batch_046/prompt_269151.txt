Consider a certification practice now adopted by several institutional AI-governance boards: a system is certified as ethically aligned exclusively by measuring its outputs against a normative reference set maintained by the board, with no appeal to independent ethical criteria. The protocol treats alignment as a purely relational property—if the board revises the reference set, the system's certification updates automatically, regardless of whether the revision makes the system more or less defensible by any standard external to the board's authority. Critics observe that this allows the board to be wrong in a substantive sense only if there is such a thing as being wrong about ethical value independently of authoritative stipulation, which the protocol appears to deny. What resolution of the Euthyphro dilemma does this institutional practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit metaethical theory: does it commit the board to the view that ethical properties depend on authoritative declaration rather than on independent features of actions, states, or systems? Distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates—can it be sustained by a constructivist or proceduralist account that denies independent moral facts, or does it presuppose a stronger form of authority-dependent value? And describe how the certification process would need to be redesigned if it were built instead on a realist or naturalist view of ethical properties, one on which an authoritative body could genuinely err about what is good.
