Draft the joint statement that a committed situationalist, working in the tradition of Christopher Browning's study of Reserve Police Battalion 101 and emphasizing organizational pressure, peer dynamics, and incremental commitment, and a committed cultural-eliminationist, working in the tradition of Daniel Goldhagen's argument that ordinary Germans were animated by a deep, culturally specific eliminationist antisemitism, could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on explaining perpetrators of mass violence.

The document should contain three parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims about what the historical record shows regarding the Battalion's actions, the range of individual responses observed, the role of ideology versus coercion, and the limits of what organizational sociology alone can explain; these claims must be phrased so that neither side needs to concede its core causal thesis to endorse them. Second, a disagreements table in which each row states one precise divergence in wording that its own camp would accept—for example, whether willingness to kill was primarily produced by situational mechanisms portable across cultures or required a pre-existing, culturally unique cognitive framework; whether variance in refusal rates reflects differences in personal character or in exposure to ideological framing; and whether national character is an indispensable explanatory primitive or decomposes into institutional and discursive transmission. Third, a cruxes section identifying the sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one party, each paired with a concrete comparative case or argument both accept as relevant—such as comparative willingness among non-German auxiliary police in structurally similar units, or the relative predictive power of pre-war antisemitic attitudes versus immediate peer pressure for individual refusal patterns—along with each side's explicit prediction about that evidence. In particular, address whether eliminationist antisemitism can be operationalized without circular dependence on the perpetrator behaviors it explains, and whether a situationalist framework can accommodate high baseline willingness without tacitly importing the culturalist premise. Present the result as the numbered agreed-claims list, followed by the disagreements table, followed by the cruxes with predictions recorded in advance.
