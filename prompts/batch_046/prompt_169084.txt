Consider the position I will call localized correspondence deflationism. Its core claim is that for every domain except direct self-referential semantic attributions, truth is fully deflationary: to say that S is true is just to endorse S, with no substantive property being ascribed. However, in the specific case of sentences that attribute truth to themselves or to sentences that embed them, the view retains a minimal correspondence intuition—not as a general metaphysics of truth, but as a local repair mechanism intended to block the liar paradox without importing Tarski’s language hierarchy or a full-blown correspondence ontology. The defender argues that this is not an ad hoc exception but a principled boundary: direct reflexivity introduces a structural instability that makes the disquotational schema fail, and the minimal correspondence layer is activated only there to restore consistency. I want an honest map of what would count against this view. Which observations, arguments, formal results, or conceptual pressures would genuinely threaten it? In particular: does the boundary between self-referential and non-self-referential contexts hold up under pressure from indirect self-reference, circular chains of attribution, or semantically closed languages? Would a successful purely deflationary resolution of the liar—say, via contextualist or paraconsistent strategies—render the local correspondence layer redundant, and if so, is that redundancy fatal or merely awkward? Could the view be accused of collapsing back into general correspondence theory, or of being qualified into unfalsifiability? I am especially interested in whether results about the stability of disquotational schemas in restricted languages with self-reference would decide the matter, and in whether defenders of pure deflationism would concede any of these points as genuinely counting against the hybrid or would dismiss them as mischaracterizing the deflationary project. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what the position’s defenders should concede from what they could legitimately dismiss, and be willing to conclude that the view is well insulated if that is the honest assessment.
