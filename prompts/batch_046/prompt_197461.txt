hey im in this social philosophy class about distributive justice and we just read some stuff about the "undeserving poor" and workfare. i keep thinking about this weird asymmetry in how welfare states actually work: if youre on unemployment benefits you have to show up to job center meetings and do these workfare placements, and if you miss one because the bus was late or you were sick they sanction you and cut your benefits for weeks. they also audit you hard for fraud, like if your roommate helps with rent or your mom gives you cash for groceries you have to report it or its fraud. but if you live off inherited investments or capital gains you dont have to prove youre "looking for work" or report every small gift, and if you file your taxes wrong you just pay a fee, not lose your income.

so what theory of desert and moral responsibility is this actually presupposing? like does it assume that poor people have to earn basic subsistence through administratively verifiable labor, but wealthy people have a default entitlement just by existing? or is it about some social contract where only wage labor counts as contribution? and heres the weirder part: the system punishes people for outcomes outside their control — missing an appointment because of a bus delay — which sounds like outcome-based blame, but in criminal law we say blame should depend on control not outcomes. so is the welfare system implicitly running on two different theories at once: outcome-based desert for the poor and control-based for everyone else?

also if we replaced the whole thing with a view that need alone creates entitlement — no workfare, no sanctions for missing appointments, just unconditional support — what would actually have to change in the institutional design? like would we have to get rid of caseworkers entirely, or just change what they check for?
