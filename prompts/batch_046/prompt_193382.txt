Write a deadpan government FAQ page, in the flat bureaucratic voice of a federal agency website, for the fictional Federal Bureau of Autotelic Regulation (FBAR), which has implemented the philosopher Moritz Schlick’s view of life’s meaning as binding national policy. Schlick’s position — that asking for a transcendent purpose to life is a category mistake, and that “meaning” is found only in Spiel, in activities pursued for their own sake rather than for any external goal or product — should be treated as settled law. The FAQ should answer practical citizen questions: how to apply for Purpose-Free Status; what distinguishes taxable Arbeit (instrumental labor) from certified Spiel (autotelic play) under the National Play Act; the procedure and penalties of a Play Audit; the formula for the Schlickian Play-Index (SPI), a niche regulatory metric that quantifies the absence of outcome-orientation in a given activity; the tax exemptions available for citizens who maintain an SPI above 0.85; the rules governing the annual national holiday Spieltag, on which all goal-directed behavior is suspended; and a sidebar explaining the Arbeit/Spiel Classification Code used by auditors. The satire should exaggerate the real feature of Schlick’s anti-teleological view — that meaning is immanent in non-instrumental activity — to show that institutionalizing it collapses the very distinction it requires: once the state defines, measures, and rewards “play,” it becomes another form of labor. Keep the irony sustained and deadpan — never wink, never state the contradiction outright, but make it legible through bureaucratic precision. Around 600 words. Target Schlick’s logic and the modern tendency to bureaucratize existential answers; invent an internal proponent, the “Chief Autotelic Officer,” if needed. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or external sources.
