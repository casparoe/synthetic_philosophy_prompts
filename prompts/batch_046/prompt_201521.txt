I'm preparing a consistency audit of my own commitments regarding Gödel's modal ontological argument for a graduate seminar. Before I present, I want to know whether my package hangs together. Here is roughly where I have landed:

(1) I accept Gödel's modal ontological argument reconstructed in second-order modal logic with S5 frame conditions. God-like is defined as possessing all and only positive properties; positive properties are closed under entailment and are necessarily positive; necessary existence is treated as one of the positive properties; and the argument moves from the possibility of a God-like being to its necessary actual existence.

(2) I deny that necessary existence is a real property in the Kantian sense—that is, I do not think it functions as a conceptual determination that adds content to the concept of a being—rather, I take it to be a purely modal status applied to a subject.

(3) I reject the Brouwerian axiom B (symmetry of accessibility) for metaphysical modality. I think metaphysical accessibility is reflexive and transitive, but need not be symmetric: just because world w is accessible from the actual world does not entail that the actual world is accessible from w under the same essentialist constraints.

(4) I maintain that the possibility premise—that a God-like being is possibly instantiated—must be secured not by appeal to conceivability, intuition, or mental construction, but solely by demonstrating the modal consistency of the set of positive properties under entailment closure.

(5) I hold that if the argument succeeds, it proves only that God exists necessarily within the cluster of worlds that preserve the essential natures of entities actually present, not across every logically possible world. I therefore restrict the domain of worlds over which the modal quantifiers range to those satisfying these essentialist preservation conditions.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to conflict. For each real tension, rank them by severity, specify what exactly pulls against what—for instance, frame conditions versus axiom commitments, or derivational steps versus property ontology—and identify the least costly single revision that would restore consistency without abandoning the core motivation behind the other commitments. Also flag any hidden commitments—things that (1) through (5) jointly entail that I have not explicitly acknowledged. The answer should be in American English.
