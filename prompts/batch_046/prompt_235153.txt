I'm trying to get clear on a subtle point in the logic of evidential cooperation in cosmically large worlds that standard expositions tend to gloss over, and I'd like an explanation that moves past the familiar Newcomb-style rehearsal. The canonical argument is that in a sufficiently vast universe, copies of one's decision procedure are instantiated elsewhere; a local choice then carries evidential weight about what those copies do, generating reason to cooperate with them despite the absence of causal interaction. Most treatments implicitly assume that this correlation is uniform across all copies and that the copies share an exactly identical preference ordering.

But I'm interested in a variant in which both assumptions are relaxed simultaneously. Suppose the copies are distributed across a structure in which the evidential correlation between any two instantiations decays continuously with some metric—spatial separation, causal disconnect, or informational distance—rather than being binary. And suppose further that each copy's utility function is not identical but drawn from a distribution centered on a common base: every copy cares about the same general kinds of outcomes, yet the weights assigned to them vary stochastically within a bounded range. In this setting, a decision rule that conditions on being instantiated in a particular region might find that it can sustain cooperative equilibria with nearby copies, while distant copies fall outside the evidential window or exhibit preference misalignments large enough to destabilize cooperation.

The conceptual issue I want unpacked is whether evidential cooperation is essentially an all-or-nothing global phenomenon—requiring universal correlation and perfect preference identity—or whether it can fragment into local coalitions without collapsing its normative structure. If it does fragment, does the reason to cooperate diminish to a purely local obligation, or does the existence of distant, uncorrelated copies somehow undermine the coherence of cooperating with anyone at all? Please explain, with conceptual precision but without relying on formal proofs, how the logic of evidential reasoning behaves under this kind of graded correlation and noisy preference alignment, and whether the normative force of cooperation survives the fragmentation or dissolves with it.
