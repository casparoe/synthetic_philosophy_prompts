I work in distributed systems, where state is only messages between nodes with no global clock, so relationism—space as just relations among bodies—feels natural to me. But I’m trying to understand Newton’s bucket argument: spin a bucket of water in an otherwise empty universe, and the surface curves, which seems to prove rotation is absolute with respect to space itself, not relative to other objects. Now, general relativity treats spacetime as a dynamical metric field that can curve and carry energy, and frame-dragging measurements show rotating masses pull that field around them. So here is my confusion: if the metric field can exist and have dynamics far from matter, and if it supplies the reference against which the bucket rotates, hasn’t GR effectively reintroduced Newton’s absolute container—just a flexible, curved one? Or is there a principled reason, within GR itself, to say that in a universe containing only that single rotating bucket the field provides no rotational reference and the water should stay flat? Give me a committed answer: does Newton’s bucket survive as a live objection to relationism once we accept the GR metric field, or does the field dissolve the argument by being purely relational after all?
