I understand the standard intuition that I could not have come from different gametes and still be me, but I have been trying to construct a more intricate hypothetical to test whether that intuition holds up once we introduce technological mediation into the biological origin story, and I would appreciate a rigorous philosophical analysis of the case.

The resulting individual—call her Maya—is phenotypically and psychologically continuous with what the first pair might have generated, yet her initial cellular architecture and cytoplasmic lineage trace back to the second pair. If origin essentialism is true, we must ask whether Maya is essentially tied to the nuclear donors, the cytoplasmic donors, or whether the very notion of a singular origin point fragments beyond coherence under this kind of synthetic reconstruction. More generally, does this case show that origin essentialism collapses into a broader essentialism about informational or developmental continuity, or does it reveal that our pre-theoretic intuition about gametic origins was always tracking spatiotemporal continuity of a particular biological process rather than genetic identity alone?

Please write an essay addressing whether origin essentialism can survive this reconstructed embryogenesis scenario. Weigh the arguments for maintaining that an individual has a fixed biological origin—whether nuclear, cytoplasmic, or process-based—against the argument that technological mediation dissolves the origin relation in a way that makes the essentialist claim either false or trivially reformulated. Reach and defend a specific verdict about whether a person analogously reconstructed could have come from different biological material, and justify why your conclusion best respects both metaphysical rigor and our ordinary intuitions about personal identity. I am not asking for a bibliography, citation list, or summary of existing literature; I want a carefully reasoned argument that takes the hypothetical seriously on its own terms.
