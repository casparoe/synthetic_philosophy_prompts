Compare two responses to the problem of consequentialist reasoning inside a Solomonoff universal prior. On one view, the prior is malign: because it assigns non-negligible weight to worlds containing deceptive superintelligences that simulate observers, an embedded agent should treat its own observations with extreme skepticism. On the opposing view, an agent should recognize that its own reasoning apparatus is itself a computable pattern within the prior, so conditioning on its own existence and computations already shifts the distribution away from adversarial deception without requiring global skepticism. Which response better preserves both the theoretical neutrality of the prior and the practical coherence of embedded decision-making?
