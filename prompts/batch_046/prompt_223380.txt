Question: Sphexishness—named for the digger wasp Sphex—describes behavior locked into a fixed sequential routine: stinging prey, dragging it to a burrow, inspecting the burrow, then depositing the prey inside. If the prey is moved during inspection, the wasp repeats the inspection rather than adapting. Philosophers use this case to examine whether mechanical rigidity undermines freedom, and whether human flexibility establishes a genuinely different kind of agency. Evaluate this argument: (1) Sphexish behavior lacks freedom because it is governed by invariant rules that cannot be overridden when circumstances change; (2) human behavior is not sphexish, since we can interrupt our own action sequences, revise goals, and resume with modified plans; (3) therefore human behavior is free in a robust, specifically libertarian sense—that is, we can originate actions not fully determined by prior causes. In particular, discuss whether (2) supports (3) without further assumptions, and whether the contrast with sphexishness distinguishes libertarian freedom from a more complex form of determinism.

Student answer: Sphexishness is a powerful model for mechanically determined behavior. The wasp’s repetition of its inspection loop shows that when an agent’s actions are driven by a linear, non-branching program, it lacks the responsiveness we associate with agency. Humans clearly differ: we can halt an action, evaluate new information, and choose a different path, so premise (2) is correct. The problem is that (3) does not follow automatically—flexibility only shows our determinism is complex, not that we are uncaused causes. A computer with conditional branching is flexible but still deterministic. That said, Dennett’s account of hierarchical self-monitoring is relevant here. When we form second-order preferences about our first-order ones, we create a causal structure that is not present in linear programs. Dennett argues this allows our volitions to be self-caused in a way that breaks rigid determination, which means they are not fully determined by prior states. So human flexibility is not merely complex determinism; it restores the kind of self-originated agency libertarians require. The argument is therefore largely sound: sphexishness marks the absence of freedom, and human hierarchical flexibility shows we escape it, though not through simple non-rigidity alone.
