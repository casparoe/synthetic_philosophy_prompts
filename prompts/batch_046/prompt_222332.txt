Write an essay about whether heterophenomenology - Dennett's method of treating first-person reports as data about thier beliefs rather than direct windows into consciousness - actually advances the science of mind or just redefines the hard problem away. The critics say it swaps "what experience is like" for "what people think it's like," defenders say that's the only scientifically respectable move, and there's this awkward middle position where it might work for mapping cognitive architecture but still miss phenomenal character entirely. Weigh these three stances, but don't end on "it depends" - defend a verdict. Should heterophenomenology be the default framework in consciousness studies, or does it presuppose away the very phenomenon it's meant to explain? Give real reasons, not just a survey.
