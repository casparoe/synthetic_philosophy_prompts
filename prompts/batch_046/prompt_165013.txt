ok so heres roughly where ive landed on wrong-kind reasons and i want to know if it actually hangs together:
(1) i think the wrong-kind-reasons problem only really applies to doxastic attitudes — belief, suspension, confidence — not to things like admiration, regret, or partiality. for those its just regular moral evaluation.
(2) but i still think you can have a right-kind reason to want a belief to be true (like hoping your friend is innocent) without that being a reason to actually believe it.
(3) i reject that WKR applies to practical reasons at all. if a consideration gets you to φ, its just a reason, period; calling it wrong kind is just a fancy way of saying you have a bad motive, not that theres a separate normative failure.
(4) i think partiality toward friends is justified when its based on shared history, but i also think admiring someone for earned merit is perfectly fine and not a wrong-kind case.
(5) im also skeptical that direction of fit does any real explanatory work here — it feels like a metaphor that gets treated as a mechanism.

my worry is that (1) and (4) clash because admiration looks belief-like, and that (3) undermines (1) since if practical reasons cant be wrong-kind then doxastic ones shouldnt get special treatment either. but maybe one of those tensions is fake? rank them by severity and tell me what i have to drop if i want to keep (3) no matter what. also is there a hidden tension with (5) that im missing?
