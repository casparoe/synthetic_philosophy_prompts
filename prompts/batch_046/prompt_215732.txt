Compose a philosophical joke whose punchline turns on the dialetheist claim that some contradictions are literally true and, crucially, non-explosive—that is, that ex contradictione quodlibet fails. Situate the contradiction in an ordinary institutional setting (for instance, a condominium association rule that genuinely both requires and forbids the same architectural modification). One character should navigate the contradiction with paraconsistent equanimity, calmly accepting both conjuncts without inferring arbitrary further claims; another should react with the exasperated expectation of classical logic, anticipating total inferential collapse. The joke should be meaningful to someone who grasps the distinction between dialetheism and mere tolerance of ambiguity, but it should not invoke any specific formal system, named logician, or bibliographic reference.
