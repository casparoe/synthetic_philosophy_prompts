I've been reading up on tranquilism lately - the idea that the only thing that actually matters for well-being is the absence of craving and suffering, not the presence of pleasure or happiness. I got into a long back-and-forth with someone who thinks pleasure is its own good, not just a temporary break from craving. We keep stalling on this one scenario: imagine you could chemically suppress every craving but also flatline the reward system, so you'd feel neither joy nor pain, just this blank neutrality. I say thats probably perfect, maybe even better than pleasure because theres zero risk of rebound craving. They say thats not well-being at all, its more like the absence of a subject experiencing anything. Then we got tangled up in whether "no craving" is a real property of that brain state, or if it only makes sense relative to some normal human baseline of desire. I cant tell if were stuck because of a hidden factual claim - like whether pleasure can actually be decoupled from craving in the brain - or if its just bedrock intuitions about what counts as a good life. Or maybe were answering different questions, one about experienced value and one about what states we should engineer. Whats your diagnosis? And is there any conceptual distinction or thought experiment that could actually move the needle, or is this just going to stay deadlocked?
