Core argument (notes):
1. Humean regularity theories identify laws with the best-system summaries of actual events. On this view, counterfactuals about law-violating or law-absent conditions depend on analogical extension from actual patterns rather than on modal grounding.
2. Anti-Humean necessitarian theories (e.g., Armstrong-style universals) treat laws as necessary relations among natural properties. This appears to provide stronger "modal anchoring" for counterfactual reasoning about conditions outside actual history—such as the epoch prior to electroweak symmetry breaking, where the property-space of fundamental forces may not have been fixed.
3. However, I want to argue that anti-Humeans face a symmetric problem: if the "naturalness" of properties is itself unsettled in quantum-gravity regimes, then identifying which relations count as law-governed requires prior modal commitments that are just as analogical as those of the Humean. The anti-Humean gains "anchoring" only by smuggling in a settled ontology of properties.
4. I therefore propose distinguishing "modal anchoring" (counterfactual stability grounded in governing relations) from "modal drift" (counterfactual instability arising from unsettled ontological commitments). Both theories exhibit drift in pre-symmetry cosmology; neither achieves pure anchoring.
