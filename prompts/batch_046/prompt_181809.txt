Please audit my philosophical profile on verisimilitude for our reading group. I hold that:
(i) scientific realism requires a coherent notion of approximate truth, not merely predictive success;
(ii) Popper’s quantitative definition of verisimilitude collapses for false theories, so a purely logical formalisation is unavailable;
(iii) truth-likeness should be measured by the balance of true consequences a theory entails against false ones it entails;
(iv) a theory with false but precise predictions can be closer to the truth than a vague rival, provided its central structural claims are correct, even if that worsens its true-to-false ratio;
(v) when two theories are empirically equivalent, the one with the leaner ontology is closer to the truth;
(vi) I reject structural realism, yet I still think ontological commitments are central to assessing verisimilitude.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, ordering them by severity. For each tension, say how serious it is and what the cheapest revision would cost me. Identify any pair that only looks conflicting but dissolves on closer inspection. Finally, tell me which single change to my profile would resolve the most tension, and precisely what I would have to surrender to make it.
