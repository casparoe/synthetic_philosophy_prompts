I'm working through my position on the metaphysics of natural law for a philosophy of science seminar, and before I present I'd like an audit of whether my commitments actually cohere or whether I'm quietly helping myself to pieces of rival views. Here is where I've landed: (1) I hold a locally applied Humean best-system view—lawhood is fixed by which regularities earn inclusion in the optimal axiomatization of actual spatiotemporal regions, where optimality balances simplicity and informativeness, and I allow in principle that distant regions might fall under different best systematizations; (2) I reject essentialism about natural properties entirely, so charge, spin, and gravitational coupling have no hidden modal essences that necessitate particular behaviors; (3) I think the distinction between accidental generalizations and genuine laws is real but shallow—objective in that there is a fact about which system is best for a region, but not metaphysically deep, since nature contains no primitive "lawhood"; (4) I want to preserve the governing intuition—if a body has mass, the inverse-square law governs its gravitational interaction—but I interpret this governing relation as purely derivative, reducible to the law's explanatory and predictive centrality within the local best system, with no primitive necessitation or modal force; (5) I think counterfactuals about laws, such as the claim that if gravity were inverse-cube then stable planetary orbits would be impossible, have determinate truth conditions, yet these conditions are fixed by applying the best-system criterion to the counterfactual scenario rather than by consulting distant possible worlds or property essences; and (6) I am a scientific realist about the ontology of our best theories—fields, particles, spacetime curvature exist—but I extend this realism only to entities, not to any modal or governing status of the laws that relate them. Do these hang together? In particular, does my rejection of essentialism in (2) undermine my ability in (5) to identify the very same properties across counterfactual scenarios? Does my attempt to save the governing intuition in (4) collapse back into the anti-Humean view I reject, or can it be maintained as genuinely derivative? And is there only an apparent tension between my Humeanism about lawhood in (1) and my scientific realism about entities in (6) that dissolves once the scope of each is made precise? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each whether the cheapest restoration of consistency requires abandoning one of these commitments or merely refining it, and identify which single revision—if any—would clean up the most problems at once.
