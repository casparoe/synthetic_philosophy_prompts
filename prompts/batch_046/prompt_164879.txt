In my dissertation research on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness, I am comparing three internal strategies illusionists use to dissolve first-person authority without invoking phenomenal concepts: (A) treating introspective reports as confabulatory reconstructions of subpersonal predictive dynamics; (B) reconstrual of talk about subjective experience as indexing cognitive-access relations rather than qualitative properties; and (C) the eliminative silence thesis, which holds that introspection yields no representational content whatsoever. Please rank these strategies from 1 (least adequate) to 3 (most adequate), and assign each a score from 1 to 10, according to how well they account for the asymmetry in emotional self-ascription—specifically, cases where a subject non-inferentially corrects others by insisting they feel envy rather than jealousy—without collapsing illusionism into first-order representationalism. Provide concise reasoning for the ranking and identify which strategy, if adopted consistently, best avoids implicitly relying on a phenomenal concept strategy.
