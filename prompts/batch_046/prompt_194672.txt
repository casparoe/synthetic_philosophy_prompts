I've recently joined an advisory group on long-horizon urban planning, and I find myself stuck on Derek Parfit's nonidentity problem: the infrastructure and climate regimes we lock in now will determine which people exist centuries later, yet if they live good lives they seem unable to claim we harmed them specifically, since different policies would have brought different people into being. Compare the standard individual-level nonidentity argument—where private reproductive choices fix the identities of future persons—with what might be called a structural nonidentity problem: in collective governance, future citizens' identities depend not on private procreation but on macro-parameters such as migration regimes, urban density, and energy infrastructure, all of which are direct objects of democratic choice. In which respects does the structural version weaken, strengthen, or redirect the moral logic of the original? Reach and defend a conclusion about whether structural nonidentity undermines intergenerational climate obligations more severely than the individual version does.
