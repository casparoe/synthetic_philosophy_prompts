I run a philosophy-of-language seminar for about sixteen graduate students, meeting for seventy-five minutes. We have discussed Douglas Hofstadter's well-known satirical strategy of rewriting gendered texts so that masculine terms become visibly arbitrary defaults rather than neutral norms, but I want the students to feel, rather than merely understand, how deeply these defaults operate as cognitive reflexes that persist even when grammatical gender is stripped away. To that end, I have invented a constructed gender-neutral register I call Neutro: brief sentences describing professionals—engineers, nurses, caregivers, executives—performing actions, using role nouns and verbs that carry no gender agreement and replacing all personal pronouns with a single neutral form. Design a runnable classroom game, with paper and a timer only, that uses Neutro translations to make the default-assumption problem tangible.

The core mechanism should be a paired rapid-translation exercise lasting about twenty minutes. One student translates aloud into standard English as quickly as possible; the partner tracks every gendered pronoun or definite-article choice, then challenges any instance where gender was not grammatically forced by English syntax but was introduced by stereotype association. The rules should include a point system that rewards speed but penalizes unchallenged gendering more heavily, and requires the pair to jointly rewrite the sentence using genuinely ambiguous, plural, or role-repeating constructions whenever a challenge succeeds.
