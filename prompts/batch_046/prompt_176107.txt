You have been invited to deliver the opening address at the 2024 workshop on Institutional Capacities and Their Pathologies, hosted by a mid-sized research university. The audience is split: metaphysicians and philosophers of science who work on dispositions, finks, and masks sit alongside university provosts, assessment directors, and curriculum designers who have never encountered those terms but who are actively debating whether accreditation metrics have eliminated the university's capacity to foster independent judgment. Your address should run about twelve minutes. Its philosophical framework must do real structural work: you should use the distinction between masked and finked dispositions—specifically the idea that a system can genuinely possess a disposition even when conditions systematically block its manifestation—to frame the institutional debate. You must not treat the metaphysics as decoration; it should determine which reform proposals you can endorse and which you must reject.

Introduce one conceptual distinction that is not part of the standard literature: distinguish a "structural mask," where an interfering condition is external to the institution's core functioning, from an "endogenous mask," where the very mechanisms that constitute the institution's identity simultaneously suppress the manifestation of the disposition they help sustain. This distinction should drive the argument: proposals that remove structural masks are viable, but proposals that treat an endogenous mask as a fink—attempting to eliminate the suppressing mechanism without recognizing that it is constitutive—risk destroying the institution in the name of liberating it.

The talk must be fully intelligible to the administrators without being condescending to the philosophers. Refer to the canonical cases—the fragile object protected by a vigilant guardian, the soluble substance kept from dissolving by a present antidote—but describe them in your own words, without attributing them to specific texts or authors. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations of any kind, and do not invoke obscure theoretical frameworks that depend on specialized knowledge of recent literature. Write in American English, in a tone appropriate to formal opening remarks.

Finally, append a brief meta-commentary in which you state how confident you are in the structural-versus-endogenous mask distinction you have drawn, and explicitly identify the single point in your reasoning—particularly the claim that an endogenous mask can be partially lifted without dissolving the underlying disposition—where your confidence is lowest.
