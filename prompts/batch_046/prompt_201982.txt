Right, I'm writing up my commitments for a philosphy of mind study group and I want a consistency check before I present them. Here's roughly where I've landed on illusionism and consciousness-related stuff:

1. I'm an illusionist in the Frankish/Dennett vein — there's no intrinsic phenomenal property, only functional/representational states that we systematically misrepresent as having qualitative character.
2. I think introspection is genuinely authoratative: it gives us direct, privileged access to our mental states, though of course we can misinterpret what we're accessing.
3. The hard problem is dissolved rather than solved — it's an artefact of how introspection frames its objects, not a real ontological gap.
4. I still want to say there's genuinely something it is like to feel pain, but I cash that out purely in representational terms (the representation of damage plus its motivational pull) with no leftover qualitative residue.
5. Phenomenal concepts are special in having a first-person mode of presentation, but this is purely epistemic — it explains the explanatory gap without postulating special ontology.
6. I think zombies are conceivable: I can imagine a physical duplicate lacking even these illusory phenomenal states. At the same time I think conceivability is only a defeasible guide to possibility, so it doesn't actually threaten physicalism.
7. Moral status should track functional/representational architecture, not any 'real' consciousness, so illusionism doesn't undermine our obligations to other minds.
8. I also think the refrigerator-light illusion applies to phenomenal consciousness: we think we're continuously experiencing rich qualitative detail, but actually introspection is sparse and reconstructive, which explains why the illusion is so persistent.
9. And I think there's a phenomenal sorites problem for illusionists — if you gradually degrade representational machinery, the illusion should fade gradually with no sharp cutoff — but I take that as a feature, not a bug.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like they are. For each genuine tension, rank them by severity, say what the cheapest fix is and what I'd have to give up to apply it, then tell me which single revision (giving up or tweaking one commitment) would clean up the most across the board. Don't just list them all as equally bad — discriminate.
