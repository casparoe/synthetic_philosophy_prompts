Consider Erwin Schrödinger's documented sympathy for Advaita Vedanta, especially the thesis that individual consciousness is not ultimately distinct but expresses a single, indivisible awareness—commonly rendered as the identity of Atman and Brahman. Suppose he were alive today and had to respond to two posthumous developments: the neuroscientific study of split-brain patients and dissociative disorders, which suggests that consciousness can be divided, localized, or sequentially disrupted in ways that appear incompatible with an irreducible substrate; and the current philosophical debate over whether sufficiently complex artificial systems—those operating through distributed, non-biological architectures—might possess consciousness, and if so, whether that consciousness would be singular or plural.

How would Schrödinger likely reason? Would he treat split-brain phenomena as superficial interruptions of an underlying unity, comparable to temporary distortions in a continuous field? Would he extend his Vedantic framework to maintain that any system achieving a certain threshold of integration participates in the same universal consciousness, regardless of substrate? Or would he resist attributing awareness to machines, perhaps arguing that biological continuity is essential for participation in the one mind? I ask you to reconstruct his probable position, carefully separating what follows directly from his metaphysical commitments from what depends on auxiliary scientific or physical assumptions he might have held. Specifically, address whether the fragmentation evidence poses a genuine threat to Vedantic unity or can be interpreted as merely phenomenal appearance; whether extending consciousness to artificial architectures makes his view empirically tractable or instead dissolves it into unfalsifiable ubiquity; and whether there is any observation or conceptual result that could, in principle, persuade him to abandon the unity thesis rather than absorb it into a more qualified form. Please do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I am interested solely in the conceptual architecture and internal consistency of his likely response.
