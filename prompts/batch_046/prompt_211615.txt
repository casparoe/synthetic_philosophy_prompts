The pairing does very different work depending on the speaker: sometimes it asserts that the tribunals invented crimes after the fact; sometimes it asserts that only the defeated faced judgment, which is a grievance about selective enforcement rather than retroactivity; and sometimes it invokes a deeper principle that moral outrage can never substitute for positive statute, regardless of who sits in judgment.

Can you separate the precise claims hiding in that cluster—victors' justice and nullum crimen, nulla poena sine lege—treating them not as one argument but as a family of distinct theses? Also specify, for each reading, whether it depends on denying that customary international law already prohibited the acts in question, or whether it survives even if one grants that such law existed.

Present the disambiguation as a structured menu—list each thesis with its logical form, its plausibility, and its fit with the historical record—rather than collapsing them into a single interpretation. I want to know exactly which version someone is invoking when they deploy the slogan to end an argument about the legitimacy of the trials.
