Hey I've been stuck on the rich-nation debate for a while — instutitions vs geography vs culture — and it feels like the arguments haven't moved since maybe the early 2000s. The institutional side points to natural experiments like Korea split or settler mortailty instruments. The geography side says those instruments fail the exclusion restriction because settler mortality correlates with disease ecology that shaped labor institutions directly. The culture side says institutions are just culture in a trenchcoat. But everyone agrees on the basic correlations, so is the stall actually about a hidden disagreement over what counts as a 'deep' cause? Or is it that we're all implicitly using different counterfactual baselines — institutions people imagine 'same country, different instutitions' while geographers imagine 'same geography, different history'? And since we can't rerun the 16th century with different settlers, is this substantive or verbal? Maybe it's worse: the question bundles growth, level, and distribution, and each camp answers one without saying so. I'd like a diagnosis that explains what kind of disagreement this actually is, and whether any micro-level result — say institutional change in non-colonial contexts — could shift things, or if we should just admit it's ill-posed and stop expecting resolution.
