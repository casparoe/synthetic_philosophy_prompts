I was in a reading group last week talking about Rorty and we got stuck on a weird arguement about whether antirepresentationalism just turns science into politics with extra steps. My friend kept saying that if there's no "way the world is" separate from our descriptions, then when climate scientists update their model after a bad prediction, they aren't getting closer to reality - they're just switching to a vocabulary that pleases the funding bodys. I tried defending Rorty by arguing that not all vocabulary changes are on par: some solve internal coordination failures that the old vocabulary created. Like, if one part of a community predicts drought with one framework and another predicts floods with a different one, and they talk past each other so badly that they build dams in the wrong places, that's not a mirror-of-nature failure but a pragmatic breakdown - the vocabulary stops working for collective action.

But then someone asked whether "stops working for collective action" is just correspondence in disguise. Because if the world didn't have stable causal patterns, wouldn't every vocabulary eventually break down in the same random way? So aren't we sneaking representationalism back in through the side door? Write an essay on whether Rorty's neopragmatism can genuinely distinguish progressive scientific change from mere fashion or power-play, using this internal coordination-breakdown idea instead of correspondence. Weigh the objection that coordination failure already presupposes a stable world that vocabularies either match or miss. Don't sit on the fence - defend a verdict. I'm kinda hoping Rorty survives this, but I'm open to being told he doesn't.
