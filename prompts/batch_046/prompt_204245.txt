You serve as the senior philosophical advisor to the University Committee on Emerging Technology and Governance. The Committee must circulate a formal decision memo before its session in two weeks regarding Proposal 2024-EC, “Embedded Deliberation Protocols for Autonomous Infrastructure Management,” submitted jointly by the Departments of Computer Science and Philosophy. The proposal requests funding to construct and study an agent that is physically embedded in the distributed computing grid it manages: its reasoning about resource allocation must include reasoning about the very processors, cooling nodes, and network links that instantiate that reasoning, with no privileged external verification layer available to the agent itself. The applicants introduce what they call a “substrate-transparency asymmetry”—direct physical and causal access to one’s own hardware coupled with logical inaccessibility to one’s own source code—and argue that this is not a design flaw but the correct model for real-world embedded decision-making. They further contend that imposing an external “epistemic separation” audit layer would reproduce an unrealistic God’s-eye view that undermines robustness rather than protecting it. Critics on the Committee, including members from risk management and research ethics, respond that without such separation the agent cannot satisfy basic conditions for accountable decision-making, because it lacks any stable standpoint from which to distinguish a change in its reasoning substrate caused by its own action from one caused by environmental interference or adversarial manipulation.

Your memo should recommend a concrete course of action—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—together with a specific implementation time frame (for example, 60 or 90 days for conditional milestones if approval is not unconditional). It must treat the applicants’ embedded-agency argument with intellectual seriousness rather than dismissing it as technical overreach, and it must give an honest, substantial account of the strongest consideration on the side you do not take. Before reaching your recommendation, briefly outline the conceptual stakes: what embedded agency entails in this particular setting, where the philosophical disagreement genuinely lies, and which empirical or conceptual claims are broadly settled versus genuinely contested among specialists who work on these issues. Please take a moment to think carefully: begin with a short brainstorming outline of the key distinctions that structure the debate—embedded versus unembedded reasoning, substrate access versus logical access, robustness versus accountability—before drafting the formal memo. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested, and do not present any contested philosophical view—such as whether embedded agents can genuinely possess a form of self-knowledge, or whether epistemic separation is a necessary condition for moral or institutional agency—as if it were the consensus position. Write in a polished, formal tone appropriate for circulation to a multi-disciplinary institutional body that includes engineers, ethicists, and administrators.
