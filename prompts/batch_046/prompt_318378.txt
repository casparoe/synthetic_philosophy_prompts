Leo is definetly in bad faith, just like Sartres waiter. The main point is that we are radically free, which means nothing outside our consciousness can cause a choice—not parents, not astrology, not even our bodies. Leo is treating his facticity (the tests, his family history) as if it were a deterministic chain that caused him to pick biology, but Sartre says facticity is only the situation we find ourselves in, it has zero causal power over the for-itself. By doing this, Leo is denying his transcendence and identifying totally with his facticity, exactly like the waiter who identifies with the waiter role. The waiter knows hes playing a part, but Leo genuinely thinks hes scripted; still, both are lying to themselves. For Sartre bad faith only works if you consciously know youre deceiving yourself on some level—Leo probably knows deep down he could have walked away, so hes hiding that knowledge. Leo should accept that his past is just background and that he is condemned to be free regardless of the scripts he was given.
