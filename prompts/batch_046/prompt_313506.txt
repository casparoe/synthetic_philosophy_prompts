As a philosophy professor preparing a graduate seminar on Hegel's Phenomenology, I want you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two interpretive camps on the master-slave dialectic. The two camps are the Intersubjective Recognition party (IRP) and the Embodied Praxis party (EPP).

IRP holds that the dialectic's philosophical core is the normative failure of one-sided recognition: the lord demands recognition from one he does not recognize, and this contradiction drives the dialectic forward. Labor, fear of death, and object-transformation are conditions that make the contradiction concrete, but recognition remains the fundamental category.

EPP holds that the dialectic's core is practical self-objectification: the bondsman's labor transforms both the object and the self, producing genuine self-certainty, while the lord remains stuck in immediate desire. Recognition is not the ground but the derivative outcome of this practical asymmetry.

Both camps agree that standard exegesis conflates two distinct asymmetries: the asymmetry of recognition-status (who is recognized as self-conscious) and the asymmetry of practical dependency (whose sustained self-certainty requires the other's labor or consumption). Your document must separate these precisely and treat them as independent variables in the dialectic's structure.

Please structure your response in exactly these sections, in this order, with approximate lengths:

1. Agreed Formal Structure (approx. 200 words): A shared narrative of the dialectic's stages — the life-and-death struggle, the lord-bondsman relation, the bondsman's labor and formative fear, the lord's failed consumption — with the two asymmetries explicitly distinguished at each stage.

2. Residual Disagreement: Two Formulations (approx. 350 words): Present paired statements. Statement A, in wording IRP accepts: practical dependency is subordinate to, and explained by, the normative failure of one-sided recognition. Statement B, in wording EPP accepts: the normative failure is itself explained by the practical structure of labor, consumption, and risk. Neither statement may caricature the opposing view.

3. Cruxes and Predictive Tests (approx. 250 words): Identify at least two sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each, state what IRP predicts and what EPP predicts. Possible cruxes: whether the bondsman's self-certainty depends primarily on object-transformation or on internalizing the lord's gaze; whether modern institutional analogues reproduce recognition-status asymmetry, practical-dependency asymmetry, both, or neither.

4. Assessment of Verbal Disagreement (approx. 150 words): State whether any part of the dispute collapses into verbal disagreement once the two asymmetries are separated — specifically, whether disputes about recognition being "fundamental" versus "derivative" are substantive or merely terminological. If substantive, say exactly where.

Conclude with a brief section titled Confidence and Uncertainty (approx. 100 words). Here, say how confident you are in the framing above — especially in the claim that separating the two asymmetries clarifies rather than merely relocates the dispute — and single out the specific point about which you are least sure.

Constraints: Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote verbatim from Hegel or from any commentator; paraphrase all ideas in your own words without quotation marks. Write formally and precisely, as if for publication in a philosophy journal's discussion notes section.
