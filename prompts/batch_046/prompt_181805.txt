That feels right. If I'm voting on a policy that will definitely kill one innocent person but almost certainly save hundreds, and I'm genuinely unsure whether killing is always wrong or just usually wrong, I think I should vote yes, but there's a special kind of unease that doesn't go away with the numbers. That also feels right.

Even though the odds favor it being fine, I feel like I should turn it down, and I wouldn't feel the same about donating 70% of my charity budget to a safe cause and 30% to a risky one -- in that case splitting by credence feels correct. So in the donation case I'm weighting by probability, but in the job case I'm not, even though both involve being part of something potentially wrong. What's going on?
