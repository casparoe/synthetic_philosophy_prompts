Design a 90-minute workshop activty for about 12 advanced undergrads or grad students on whether ranking historical evils is possible or just obscene. I want them to feel the squeeze themselves, not listen to me lecture about it. Give them roles on a fictional "Archive Allocation Board" with exactly 100 pages in a global memorial record and five case files—say the Atlantic slave trade, the Holodomor, the Cambodian killing fields, the Rwandan genocide, and the Hiroshima bombing. The catch is each file has two hidden "harm registers" (direct mortality and intergenerational cultural erasure) that dont convert into each other, but the board rules only let them allocate pages along one axis per round. In round one they build a ranking; in round two they discover the second register and must either defend or tear up their first allocation. Then a debrif that maps where they felt the pull to compare versus where it felt grotesque, lists what will predictably go wrong (someone will try to invent a conversion formula, someone will sit out in refusal, the group splits between aggregators and those who call comparison itself violence), and is honest about what the game cant reproduce—like the actual lived specificity of survivors—which youll have to supply verbally. Start with the full rules, roles, payoff/constraint scheme, and debrif outline, no intro or "heres the plan" preamble. This is for a syllabus im throwing together, so a typo or two is totally fine.
