I am working through a passage in Schrödinger's later philosophical essays on Vedanta, where he discusses what he calls the unity of consciousness. There he compares individual minds to facets of a single crystal—apparently distinct, yet all expressions of one underlying substance—and he claims that belief in a plurality of minds is sustained more by practical convenience than by metaphysical fact. For a chapter on quantum physicists and Eastern philosophy, I need help determining the precise strength of this claim. Should I read Schrödinger as endorsing strict Advaita: that individual consciousness is merely vyavahārika (transactionally real but ultimately illusory) and only universal awareness is pāramārthika? Or is he advancing a weaker, epistemological thesis—that mental boundaries are heuristic constructs science should eventually dissolve, without denying individual subjectivity outright? I am particularly interested in whether this can be reconciled with his scientific naturalism, since physics presupposes multiplicity in the objective world. Any clarification of the scope of his argument would be greatly appreciated.
