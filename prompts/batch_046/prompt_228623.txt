I am preparing remarks for a philosophy-of-science workshop and want a consistency audit of my own commitments before presenting them. I hold:

(a) Friedman's F-twist is broadly correct: unrealistic assumptions in scientific models are methodologically virtuous because they isolate causal kernels, not because they approximate reality.

(b) Genuine scientific explanation requires that a model correctly represent the actual causal structure producing the phenomenon.

(c) I reject evolutionary-psychology explanations of moral cognition because they depend on speculative, unfalsifiable assumptions about ancestral selection environments.

(d) I treat highly idealized climate-projection models as epistemically authoritative, but solely in virtue of rigorous out-of-sample predictive success, not representational fidelity.

(e) I also maintain that a model's explanatory value scales with its "representational friction" — the resistance its internal structure offers to being smoothly mapped back onto real-world mechanisms, which forces explicit articulation of exactly where the idealization breaks down.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate what each revision would cost me, and do not treat all conflicts as equally damaging. I am not sure which commitment I would drop first.
