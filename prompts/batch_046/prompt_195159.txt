For a philosophy reading group I have been asked to outline my interpretation of Einstein’s position on religion and freedom, and I want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I stand: (1) I take Einstein to hold a Spinozist conception of the divine, identifying God with the rational, law-governed structure of nature rather than with any personal or willing agent; (2) I think he denies libertarian free will entirely, treating human actions as causally determined in the same manner as physical processes; (3) I interpret his acceptance of quantum-mechanical indeterminacy as restricted to statistical ensembles, not to individual macroscopic events such as neural processes, so that quantum randomness does not rescue human freedom; (4) I understand his notion of cosmic religious feeling as a non-cognitive, aesthetic awe at universal intelligibility rather than as propositional belief in a deity; and (5) I take him to be a compatibilist about moral responsibility, holding that praise and blame are socially useful instruments that causally shape behavior and therefore do not presuppose metaphysical alternatives. Are these commitments jointly tenable? Specifically, is there a tension between treating cosmic awe as non-cognitive and insisting that the universe is fully intelligible to reason? Between denying that quantum randomness reaches the brain and still maintaining that statistical laws govern human behavior? And does the compatibilist account of blame cohere with the claim that every mental event is as determined as planetary motion? For each tension you find, rank it by severity, identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency, and tell me which single change would clean up the most conflict. Also indicate which apparent conflicts dissolve upon closer inspection.
