I am drafting a scene in a novel that turns on the intellectual atmosphere of a graduate seminar in medieval philosophy. For the scene to carry weight, I need the authentic text of a final examination—one essay question, a model answer, and a detailed marking scheme—set by a rigorous instructor for advanced students of Islamic philosophy. The subject must be al-Ghazālī's occasionalism and his denial of necessary causal connections in nature. The examination should be designed for a 90-minute sitting, carrying 30 marks, and aimed at students who grasp the basic Ashʿarite distinction between divine omnipotence and customary regularity (ʿāda) but who are not specialists in later commentarial traditions. Crucially, the question must not be answerable by summary alone. I want it to place students at a precise conceptual crux: whether the doctrine of acquisition (kasb) can sustain a non-arbitrary distinction between ordinary human actions—those for which an agent bears moral responsibility—and miraculous interruptions of custom, particularly when the observable form of the act is identical in both cases. To keep the problem from being a standard textbook exercise, inject a framing element that is not usually taught at this level: require students to consider an interventionist model of causation, in which a cause is defined solely by invariant relations that hold under ideal manipulation, and to ask whether adopting such a model makes occasionalism incoherent or whether it unexpectedly illuminates al-Ghazālī's insistence that natural conjunctions are not genuinely causal. The model answer should articulate four mandatory conceptual moves, each assigned specific marks totaling 30: first, a precise reconstruction of the denial of secondary causation; second, an analysis of kasb as relational attribution rather than an independent causal power; third, an evaluation of whether miracles differ metaphysically from ordinary events or only in the epistemic expectations of observers; fourth, a reasoned judgment on the coherence of moral responsibility under occasionalism, informed by the comparison with the interventionist framework. The marking scheme should specify, for each move, the marks available, the criteria for full credit, and a concise one-line diagnosis of the characteristic failure—such as conflating occasionalism with simple determinism, treating kasb as a hidden causal capacity, or assuming that denying necessary connection eliminates all predictive regularity. The deliverable must be fully answerable without external reference, must contain no requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and must avoid verbatim quotation from primary texts unless you can supply an exactly fetched source; where no source is available, paraphrase without quotation marks. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate to academic materials used in literary fiction.
