Design a classroom game for a philosophy workshop on collective rationality — about 15 grad students, 60 minutes, split into three groups of five w/ secret profile cards. I want them to feel the discursive dilemma in their bones before anyone names it. Give each student a card that fixes private votes on R (candidate clears research bar), T (clears teaching bar), and H (should hire). Distribute them so every group gets exaclty two "Yes on all three," one "Yes-R/No-T/No-H," one "No-R/Yes-T/No-H," and one "No on all three." They vote in secret, tally majorities, then learn the charter requires both logical consistency (R∧T → H) and democratic respect for each majority; if they can announce one collective decision honoring both, they win 10 points. They can't — R and T both win 3-2, but H loses 2-3. Force them to negotiate which majority to betray and defend it. Include a debrief mapping their move onto premise-based vs conclusion-based aggregation, stressing that every individual applied R∧T→H rationally. Be honest that the card distribution is rigged so the activity doesn't prove the general impossibility result, and tell me what will predicatbly go wrong the first run (students will bully outliers to flip votes, or deny the entailment binds committees).
