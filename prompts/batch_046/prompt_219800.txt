I'm drafting an essay for my philosophy course on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition. My central claim is that Bradley's infinite regress—where relating a subject and predicate seems to require a further relation, and then another—can be blocked if we model propositions as nodes in a hypergraph, with adjacency representing relational structure internally rather than through separate linking entities. Specifically, I treat these nodes as modal points: primitive entities defined essentially by the relations they can enter, so that any internal sub-composition does not generate a new propositional layer that demands further unification. I also draw an analogy to vector embedding spaces, in which relational information is encoded within coordinate dimensions rather than through external edges, though I worry this might confuse a representational tool with a metaphysical account. Could you comment on whether this hypergraph approach genuinely avoids a hidden relating relation at the adjacency level, and whether my description of Bradley's argument about relational unity is accurate? I am especially interested in whether treating nodes as modal points collapses into Bradley's own Absolute, and in whether there are logical gaps in the comparison to embedding spaces.
