so heres an argument I cooked up for my philosophy of mind group and now I cant tell if its brilliant or just broken. (1) If eliminative materialism is right, then beliefs and desires dont exist -- theres just neural dynamics with no propositional content. (2) But predictive coding models in neuroscience talk constantly about representations, priors, and prediction errors that carry content about the world. (3) So either eliminativism extends downward and wipes out these subpersonal representations too, or it stops at the personal level and leaves content intact downstairs. (4) If it extends downward, then the neuroscience that supports eliminativism cant even formulate its own theories, since they rely on representational talk. (5) If it stops at the personal level, then eliminativism is just a linguistic reform proposal about folk psychology, not an ontological claim about the mind. (6) But Churchland-style eliminativism is explicitly ontological -- it says those states arent real. (7) So the view either undermines its own scientific basis or gives up its core ontological claim.

I want to keep the idea that theres some tension between eliminating beliefs and keeping scientific representations, but I need to know exactly where the slide happens. Is (2) wrong because predictive coding can be read non-representationally? Or is (3) a false dichotomy? Or does (4) confuse using representational language as a heuristic with being committed to it ontologically? Tell me which step to weaken and what weakening it costs the eliminativist.
