Write an essay comparing the moral-psychological standing of two folk-moral orientations: the German emphasis on rule-following, procedural order, and collective reliability (often glossed as Ordnung) versus the American emphasis on individual conscience, situational judgment, and the authority of personal conviction. Treat this not as cultural stereotype but as a dispute about moral psychology: does treating rules as presumptively binding cultivate fairness and trust, or does it encourage moral outsourcing and bureaucratic compliance? Conversely, does elevating conscience protect against institutional evil, or does it license self-deception and rationalization?

Weigh the strongest philosophical argument for each orientation—perhaps the division-of-moral-labor argument for rule-following, and an argument from epistemic humility or particularism for conscience—then reach and defend a specific verdict. You may argue that one orientation is superior, or that they must be synthesized in a particular hierarchy (for example, rules as default with conscience permitted only under a defined burden of proof).

Structure your response in the following sections with approximate lengths:

1. Framing the dispute in moral-psychological terms — about 150 words
2. The case for rule-following and order — about 250 words
3. The case for individual conscience — about 250 words
4. Critical comparison: moral outsourcing versus self-deception — about 200 words
5. Verdict and justification — about 200 words

Keep the argument philosophical rather than anthropological; do not request bibliographies, citations, or empirical studies, and do not end on a non-committal note.
