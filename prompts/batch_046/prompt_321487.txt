I have been trying to work out, on my own, whether eating meat is wrong mainly because of the farmed animals, mainly because of wild animals killed growing feed crops, or mainly because of what I call the replacement gap: the question of whether reducing crop demand actually prevents wild suffering, or merely fails to bring replaceable lives into being. I also want to know whether switching from beef to chicken makes things better or worse once you count the crop deaths and the wild suffering that pasture might avoid.

To help me see where I actually stand, I would like a taxonomy — a branching decision tree — that sorts positions by concrete answers, not by the labels people wear. Please build it from the logical space rather than from a list of named theories.

The first split should ask whether wild animal suffering counts morally at all, or only suffering we directly cause. The second should ask whether crop cultivation kills more wild animals per calorie than pasture does, once you include the small mammals and insects destroyed in planting and harvest. The third should ask whether preventing those deaths is a genuine moral gain, or whether wild lives are replaceable so that their absence is not a loss — this is the replacement gap I mentioned.

Each leaf should carry a recognised name if one exists (for example, 'crop-death vegan', 'pasture-preferring omnivore'), or be marked 'unnamed', together with one sentence on its most awkward commitment — the belief it forces its holder to accept that feels intuitively wrong. Please include the combinations that seem unoccupied, and for each say briefly whether the gap looks incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves that agree on every practical recommendation but differ only in framing, so the tree cannot genuinely separate them.

The diagnostic questions should be concrete choices — for instance, 'If I replace one plate of beef with one plate of chicken, do I expect more or fewer wild animals to have died?' — rather than asking me to name a theory. I want about eight to ten leaves, suitable for a small discussion group of friends with no formal philosophy background.

Please write in British English. Where possible, separate what is settled (for example, that crop harvesting kills wild animals) from what is genuinely contested (for example, whether indirect harm through land use carries the same moral weight as direct harm, or whether wild lives are replaceable). Do not present a contested view as if it were the consensus. I am not asking for citations, a bibliography, or reading lists; I want to work out the logic myself.
