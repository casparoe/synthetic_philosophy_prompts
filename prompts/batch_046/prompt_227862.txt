hi, im a postgrad writing about zhuangzi and i keep running into this weird behaviour when people discuss the butterfly dream. like, someone will present it as a classic case of dream scepticism — you cant tell if youre awake or dreaming, therefore knowledge is shaky. but then when you point out zhuangzi doesnt seem worried at all, he just wakes up and wonders, they say oh but thats because its not really scepticism, its about transformation and the self being fluid. ok fair enough. but then later in the same paper theyll use the sceptical framing again to compare it to descartes, saying zhuangzi got there first but with a different conclusion. and if you press them on the comparison theyll say well the dream state in zhuangzi isnt about doubting the world, its about the boundaries between things dissolving. so theyve shifted from scepticism to ontology to mysticism and back, and each shift protects the interpretation from being wrong about something specific.

ive seen this happen in at least three papers i read recently — one treats the butterfly as an argument for epistemic humility, another uses it to show daoist non-duality, and a third says its actually a joke about language. but all three borrow bits from the other readings when they need to provide a defence for the interpretation against criticism. is there a name for this kind of interpretive move, where a text gets read through one lens but defended with resources from a competing lens? i know about hermeneutic circles and maybe confirmation bias in interpretation, but this feels more specific — its not just seeing what you want, its having multiple incompatible frameworks on standby and switching between them to keep the interpretation alive. if theres no term, what should i call it? and how is it different from ordinary syncretism or eclectic reading? also, does it only happen with zhuangzi or is it a broader failure mode in comparative philosophy?
