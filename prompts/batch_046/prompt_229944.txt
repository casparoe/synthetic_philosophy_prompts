Design a realistic, non-science-fictional thought experiment—no more than one paragraph—in which the heterogeneity problem for hedonism is sharpened rather than dissolved. The scenario should involve a single subject who undergoes a sustained cognitive-affective state with the following jointly satisfied properties: (a) the subject reports a distinctive, warm, absorbing quality that they sincerely identify as pleasure; (b) the object of the state is an activity that is demonstrably pointless—the subject knows in real time that every successful step rests on a false premise and produces no external or internal value; (c) the subject feels no desire to continue or repeat the activity, actively avoids it when free to choose, and rates it lower than neutral on an explicit preference scale; and (d) the phenomenal character of the state is structurally dissimilar from familiar bodily pleasures (e.g., eating, warmth) and from familiar intellectual pleasures (e.g., solving a valid proof), being closer to the feeling of being absorbed in a repetitive manual task whose outcome is immediately erased.

After presenting the scenario, provide a decomposition of how three hedonist positions classify the state: (i) phenomenal-quality hedonism, which holds that all pleasures share one intrinsic felt feature; (ii) attitudinal hedonism, which holds that pleasure essentially involves being liked, desired, or positively evaluated by the subject; and (iii) representational hedonism, which holds that pleasure essentially represents its intentional object as good or worth pursuing for its own sake. For each position, state whether the state counts as genuine pleasure and identify the precise feature of the scenario that drives the verdict.

Then complete two further tasks: first, isolate the single structural feature of the case that does the philosophical work—i.e., the feature whose removal would cause all three positions to converge on the same classification, reducing the case to a standard heterogeneity objection. Second, present the strongest counter-argument available to the position that classifies the state as non-pleasure, explaining how that defender might claim the scenario illegitimately stipulates a phenomenal quality as "pleasure" without the normative or attitudinal structure that genuinely defines it.

Keep the analysis self-contained; do not invoke specific empirical studies, named clinical conditions, or bibliographic references.
