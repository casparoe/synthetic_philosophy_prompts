Write a Petrarchan sonnet spoken by a Whig historian surveying the collapse of the Roman Republic. In the octave, use an ABBAABBA rhyme scheme that pairs progress with regress and light with night, presenting each past crisis as an inevitable stage toward modern liberty. At the volta in line nine, turn to address the historian’s own audience, and in the sestet employ a CDECDE scheme in which present rhymes with absent, so that the form’s closure exposes what the narrative suppresses: that recruiting the past as prologue makes the present depend on precisely the absences it refuses to acknowledge. Let the rhyme scheme itself carry the philosophical argument — the octave’s circularity suggesting teleological coherence, the sestet’s interrupted rhyme showing that progress borrows its shape from exclusion.
