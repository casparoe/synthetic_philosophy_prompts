I am working on a novel that turns on a philosophical image I have been calling the problem of the careenium and the simmballs. By the careenium I mean the physical substrate of cognition—the material ground, whether neural, mechanical, or otherwise—through which representation occurs. By simmballs I mean the symbolic structures that inhabit that substrate: meanings, codes, or representational forms that circulate within it. The conceptual question that drives my fiction is whether these symbols push the substrate around. Can the semantic content of a simmball genuinely alter the causal dynamics of the careenium, or is the substrate moved only by its own blind physical forces, carrying symbols along as inert passengers?

Write a short story that dramatizes this tension in concrete terms. Choose a specific character and situation—perhaps a translator whose interpretations physically reshape a machine, or a thinker whose adoption of a new symbol system alters the architecture of her own mind—and allow the philosophical problem to emerge through event and consequence rather than abstract argument. By the story's close, the narrative should lean toward a discernible verdict on whether symbols possess downward causal efficacy over their substrate, though you need not dissolve the ambiguity entirely. Write with precision, vivid imagery, and a narrative voice that respects the reader's intelligence.
