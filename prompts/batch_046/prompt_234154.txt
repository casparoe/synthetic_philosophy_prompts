Write a villanelle (19 lines, A1 b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 A2) in the voice of the Grand Inquisitor from Dostoevsky's Brothers Karamazov, talking to the silent Christ. Use two refrains: one about correcting Thy work out of love for mankind, and one about being kissed and still left to rule. I want the poem to turn on the idea that freedom isn't just hard, it's epistmeically lonely — it forces every soul to decide its own worth with no guarantee anyone else has already chosen it. So build the three temptations (miracle, mystery, authority) in the tercets not as bribes but as ways of being-known that freedom dissolves. The quatrain should be the volta were the Inquisitor realizes the kiss doesn't forgive him, it just confirms Christ won't take away that loneliness. Make the A-rhymes shift from hard closed sounds in the first half to open vowel sounds in the quatrain, so the form itself feels like a door left ajar. It should be accurate enough that someone who's actually read the chapter wouldn't wince — especially where he admits he loves them more than Christ does, and where the kiss functions. No bibliography or citations needed. End with a one-sentence summary of what the poem claims freedom costs, on it's own line.
