hey so me and two friends got into this at a coffee shop after a seminar. i was just listening, not taking sides. they were arguing about coherent extrapolated volition and whether its actually meaningful or just dressed-up paternalism.

alex said: look, CEV is basically "do what i'd want if i was smarter and knew more." but thats empty. either "i" means me now, in which case being smarter doesn't change my core values and the extrapolation does nothing, or "i" means some idealized agent, in which case its not me and you've just replaced my preferences with someone else's. so the whole thing is either trivial or authoritarian.

jordan pushed back: you're missing the coherence constraint. it's not just any smarter version of you; it's the version that emerges from your actual values after you've reflected, resolved contradictions, and learned the consequences. that's not authoritarian, its just rational self-improvement. if you value both not lying and being polite, and they clash, resolving that isn't imposing an outside view, it's figuring out what you actually care about more.

alex said fine, but coherence already smuggles in a normative theory. not everyone thinks practical reason requires consistency in the way you're assuming. someone might genuinely value both absolute freedom and total security, and see the tension as a feature, not a bug. CEV irons that out by fiat, which means it privileges a particular philosophical psychology over others. so it's not neutral.

jordan replied that if you ask for coherence, you can't complain about getting coherence. but then jordan admitted there's a real problem with the extrapolation distance. if you run the process far enough, the idealized agent might endorse things you'd never recognize — like uploading, or giving away all resources, or restructuring society. at that point it's not clear why we should call it your volition. but jordan said we can bound the extrapolation with continuity conditions: only changes that are supported by your deeper commitments count, not arbitrary jumps.

alex jumped on that: where do those continuity conditions come from? they seem like hidden parameters. you can always draw the boundary wherever you want — close enough to avoid alienation, far enough to claim you've achieved coherence. without an independent metric for "supported by deeper commitments," you're just picking the conclusion in advance.

jordan said maybe that's right, but then the question isn't whether CEV is perfect, it's whether any alternative does better. if we reject CEV, we're stuck with either our current messy preferences or explicit human programming, both of which have worse failure modes.

they turned to me and said referee. can you adjudicate move by move? rate each of alex and jordan's points from clearly wrong to mostly right and briefly justify. also tell me who is more right overall, and what both are missing — especially whether jordan is secretly assuming value monism, and whether alex's "either trivial or authoritarian" framing misses a third option. please don't ask for citations or bibliographies, i just want the logic sorted. also sorry for the typos, typing on my phone.
