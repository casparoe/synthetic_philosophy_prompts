I have recently become convinced, through sustained engagement with Berkeley’s economic writings, that his proposals for paper credit and an Irish national bank are not pragmatic departures from his immaterialism but rather its extension into institutional life: banknotes and debt obligations operate, on my reading, as what might be called “institutional notions,” entities whose reality and value depend entirely on collective mental assent, social recognition, and divine ordering rather than on any intrinsic material substrate. Next Thursday I present this reversal to a joint workshop in early modern philosophy and the history of economic thought; the audience will be split between economic historians, who treat Berkeley’s monetary essays as conventional mercantilist pamphleteering and will resist any philosophical overreading, and Berkeley scholars, who regard those same texts as biographical curiosities with no bearing on his metaphysics of minds and ideas. I need a speaker’s prep sheet for the Q&A period—not a defense of the thesis itself, but a strategic map of the objections I will actually face, ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they can do if I mishandle them.

Please phrase each objection in the vocabulary its faction uses: the historians’ charge of anachronistic presentism, the metaphysicians’ claim that social institutions need no ontological grounding in immaterialism, the inevitable comparison to Locke’s materially anchored theory of money, and the skeptical worry that immaterialism cannot secure the temporal durability required for long-term debt contracts without a persistent material record. For each, indicate precisely what I should concede up front—where my framing genuinely overreaches—what I must hold firm on, a reply calibrated for a two-minute exchange in this mixed room, and an explicit note on the weakest point in that reply. I am particularly anxious about the unasked question regarding how financial record-keeping and the stability of obligations survive over time if there is no material substrate to preserve traces; include that as the objection I hope nobody raises, with both a reply and an honest admission of vulnerability should it come. I am traveling without library access, so do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; I need strategic guidance, not sources.
