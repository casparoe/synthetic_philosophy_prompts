ive been going round in circles about the Halden prison vs american mass incarceration debate for ages, and honestly it feels like everyone just repeats the same moves. The Norwegian side talks about normalisation — guards wearing ordinary clothes, shared kitchens, the idea that if you treat inmates like citizens they learn to behave like citizens — and points to lower recidivism. The other side says thats missing the point entirely, that punishment has to involve some kind of moral accounting or deliberate hardship, otherwise youre not doing justice youre just running a very secure social programme. My working theory is that this isnt a real empirical disagreement at all, because both camps mostly accept the same statistics; its a clash over what a prison is actually *for*, whether its meant to repair social bonds or to mark a moral boundary. But im not sure — maybe theres a hidden factual dispute in there about whether dignity actually reduces reoffending or whether degradation genuinely deters people, and we just dont have the right study yet. Can you diagnose whats actually going on? Are the sides answering different questions hidden under one word, is one smuggling in a premise about human nature that the other cant accept, or is the question itself badly formed? And be honest: if its just a stalemate over deep intuitions about suffering and dignity, is there any distinction, experiment, or change of framing that could actually shift anyone, or should we expect this to stay stuck forever?
