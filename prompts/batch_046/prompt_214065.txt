You are an expert in moral and political philosophy, particularly in value pluralism, moral compromise, and cross-cultural negotiation. Before giving your main answer, spend a moment brainstorming the closest established concepts—such as modus vivendi, moral trade-offs, and value incommensurability—and note how they might or might not cover the pattern described below.

Consider the following situation. Two communities operate under incompatible moral codes. Community A treats practice P as strictly forbidden but attaches no special weight to practice Q; Community B treats Q as strictly forbidden but is largely indifferent to P. They reach an agreement: each relaxes its own prohibition in exchange for the other doing the same. As a result, both communities gain access to goods previously blocked, and aggregate welfare clearly increases. Yet neither community comes to see the other’s practice as legitimate; each regards its own relaxation as a regrettable concession and the other’s relaxation as a continued moral error. The arrangement is stable and productive, but it involves no convergence in values, no shared justification, and no belief that the prohibitions were mistaken—only a reciprocal licensing of what each still considers wrong.

This pattern seems distinct from a simple modus vivendi (a truce motivated by self-interest with no mutual adjustment) and also distinct from genuine moral learning or value synthesis. Is there an established philosophical term for exactly this kind of welfare-enhancing, reciprocal relaxation of incompatible moral prohibitions that preserves full value disagreement? If there is, explain how it relates to moral compromise, value pluralism, and modus vivendi. If there is not, propose a precise name and definition, and carefully delineate its boundaries—especially distinguishing it from ordinary bargaining, from cases where one side dominates, and from situations where the relaxation eventually leads to value change. Be specific about what makes the “gains from trade” genuinely moral rather than merely strategic.
