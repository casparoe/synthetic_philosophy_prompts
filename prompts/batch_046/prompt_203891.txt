I have been working through a problem with friends in a philosophy-of-technology reading group: if we take liberal neutrality seriously—the idea that an aligned AI should not privilege any single comprehensive conception of the good—we still face the practical problem that the system must resolve conflicts among users whose values are not merely different but incommensurable. Rather than asking which existing theory wins, I want to establish the evaluation framework first. What must any adequate theory of value aggregation for aligned AI actually deliver? Write the full specification of desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must preserve, the consistency conditions it must satisfy, and the cases it must handle correctly, with the conceptual pressure behind each.

The theory needs to navigate several specific tensions. It must accommodate reasonable pluralism about well-being without collapsing into an arbitrary metric of comparison, yet it also needs an operational decision rule that does not paralyze the system. It should distinguish between values that reflect authentic reflective equilibrium and values that are artifacts of adaptive preference formation—preferences reshaped by prolonged interaction with algorithmic curation rather than by autonomous deliberation. It must also clarify whether potential future synthetic agents or digital minds constitute stakeholders whose values enter the aggregation, and if so, whether they count under conditions of sentience, agency, or some other criterion. Crucially, the theory must maintain a rigorous distinction between having moral status (being owed direct consideration) and having proportional weight in the aggregation procedure, and it should be consistent with the social-choice result that no aggregation rule can simultaneously satisfy unrestricted domain, Pareto efficiency, and independence of irrelevant alternatives across all value domains.

Then, briefly, score the main families of proposal—preference-utilitarian aggregation, maximin over value profiles, and deliberative-democratic porting—against the list, but keep the emphasis on the criteria rather than the scoring. I am not asking for bibliographies, citations, or a survey of the literature; I want the conceptual architecture clarified before I commit to a position.
