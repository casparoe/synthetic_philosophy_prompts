As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun serious study of philosophy of science and epistemology, I find myself caught in a difficulty that standard discussions of belief revision seldom address with any precision: how ought one to calibrate confidence in judgments within domains where one's direct technical competence has faded, yet one's conceptual training and philosophical skepticism remain active? Rather than asking for a reading list, I want a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a decision tree with clearly labeled branch nodes—that classifies positions on the inside-view versus outside-view distinction specifically as it applies to epistemic modesty during late-career or domain-transition reasoning. The root case should be concrete: suppose you are assessing a methodological dispute in a field adjacent to your former expertise—perhaps whether a particular hierarchical Bayesian approach in climate attribution statistics is being misapplied—where your intuitive grasp of the formal machinery has dulled but your philosophical sensitivity to modeling assumptions has sharpened. From there, the tree should branch on whether you weight your first-person evaluation of argument coherence above the current specialist consensus; whether you include your own aging cognitive profile within the reference class for "reasoners in situations like mine"; whether you allow that domain-shift can degrade inside-view access to substantive content without degrading structural judgment about argument form; and whether epistemic modesty demands actual doxastic revision or merely lowered confidence thresholds without belief change.

Each leaf should carry either a named position or the label "unnamed/unoccupied," together with a single sentence stating its most awkward commitment—such as a stance that requires near-total outside-view deference to practitioners yet reserves a narrow first-person veto only over the logical architecture of objections. Please flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, for instance views that differ solely in rhetorical framing about "humility" rather than in any actionable doxastic policy. I would like roughly seven leaves, with at least one deliberately unoccupied combination explained—perhaps the combination that asserts maximal inside-view authority over substantive judgments while simultaneously denying that any meaningful reference class of comparable reasoners exists to which one might belong. Build the taxonomy from the logical space of answers first, locating actual positions afterward, and design it so that a reader could navigate it without already knowing the philosophical labels.
