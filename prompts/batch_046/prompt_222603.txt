Write a one-semester research proposal (around 1500 words) on the conflict between collective memory and the historical record when a community's present institutional practices depend on a narrative that archival evidence undermines. Do not frame the question as a simple zero-sum choice between yielding to correction or preserving myth. Instead, introduce and defend a conceptual framework—call it institutional mnemonic debt—according to which maintaining a partially inaccurate communal narrative can generate genuine obligations of repair or restructuring, not because falsehood is valuable in itself, but because the community's practical identity has been built around conditions of intelligibility that the narrative provided.

State the precise question: if the record disproves a foundational causal story that justifies current civic rituals, land arrangements, or reparative obligations, is the appropriate response epistemic correction, narrative preservation with acknowledged fiction, or a hybrid form of qualified belief that separates practical commitment from historical assertion? Explain why standard treatments in collective memory studies and philosophy of history leave this open: they tend to treat memory and history as competing representations of the same past rather than as serving different normative functions within living institutions.

Outline the method: conceptual analysis of hypothetical and documented cases of institutional conflict (without empirical fieldwork), development of the mnemonic debt framework, and evaluation against objections such as false consciousness, paternalism, and epistemic relativism. Specify what the deliverable looks like—likely a taxonomy of resolution-types mapped to institutional functions—and identify failure conditions: for example, if qualified belief collapses into either pure belief or pure pretense, or if mnemonic debt proves indistinguishable from ordinary political conservatism.

Finally, give an honest scope assessment. The project is tractable because it remains philosophical rather than historical or sociological, but it risks expanding beyond one semester if it attempts to cover legal, moral, and epistemic dimensions for more than one institutional domain; limit the analysis to civic or ritual practices and note where empirical work would be needed to extend the conclusions. Do not include a bibliography or citation requests; refer to general positions and arguments only, and indicate where you will supply references yourself.
