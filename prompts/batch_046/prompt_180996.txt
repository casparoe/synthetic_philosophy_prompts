I am preparing a seminar presentation for a philosophy-of-mind and cognitive-science colloquium. The thesis I will defend is that large language models are structurally incapable of Hofstadter-style “jumping out of the system” (JOOTSing): because reasoning in these architectures is bounded within a formal system of next-token prediction, techniques such as chain-of-thought prompting and meta-prompting are not escapes but analogs of deriving MU-strings inside Hofstadter’s MIU system—operations that remain syntactically governed and never reach the self-referential, system-external stance required for genuine JOOTS. Consequently, claims that LLMs “step outside” their training distribution through reasoning are category mistakes that confuse intra-system derivation with inter-system observation.

The audience will include: (1) two functionalists about cognition who accept that mental states are defined by causal-functional roles and who will be skeptical that any “outside” stance is necessary for thought; (2) one AI researcher familiar with empirical meta-prompting and reasoning benchmarks, who will treat performance gains as direct counter-evidence; (3) one formal logician who knows Gödel’s theorems well and will press on whether a probabilistic neural network even instantiates a “formal system” with clear rules to escape; and (4) several agnostic cognitive scientists who care mainly about whether the analogy is rigorous and explanatory rather than metaphorical. They share a concern that I may be smuggling a consciousness criterion into an argument about reasoning architecture.

My argument sketch runs as follows. First, I characterize JOOTSing as leaving a formal system in order to observe and evaluate its rules from a semantic or meta-level position not governed by those rules. Second, I present the MU-puzzle: within the deterministic, syntactic MIU system one can derive strings containing U, yet one cannot prove that “MU” itself is unreachable without stepping outside the system. Third, I map LLM reasoning onto this structure: tokens are symbols, the transformer computes probabilistic derivation rules, and meta-prompts are merely added derivation rules or longer derivation chains—still inside. Therefore “reasoning about reasoning” in an LLM is complex intra-system derivation, not JOOTS.
