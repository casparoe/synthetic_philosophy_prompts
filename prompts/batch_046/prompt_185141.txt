Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition. The collaboration addresses whether avoiding the regress requires a single, non-constituent mode of propositional unity, or whether plural internal structures can do the work without generating an infinite hierarchy of relations.

The two camps are:
- The Primitive-Binder camp: holds that every proposition requires one universal binding principle—an unsaturated logical form, a primitive mode of togetherness, or something functionally equivalent—that combines constituents into a unit without itself being one of the constituents. If this binder were merely another constituent, it would need its own binder, launching Bradley's regress; therefore it must be sui generis and homogeneous across all propositional contexts.
- The Structural-Pluralist camp: holds that there is no universal binder. Different propositions are unified by different internal structures—functional application, syntactic ordering, logical form specific to the content—and relations relate their relata not by being glued by something further, but by being parts of a structured whole whose identity conditions are given by the arrangement itself. On this view, Bradley's regress is dissolved rather than stopped: the demand for a further relating relation rests on a false assumption that all relating is external to the structure it orders.

The deliverable should be a polished document both parties could sign, with the following sections in order:
1. Agreed claims (numbered): formal and conceptual points both accept—e.g., that a proposition must distinguish its constituents from a mere list or aggregate; that Bradley's regress arises if every relation requires a further relation to connect it to its terms; that avoiding the regress is a necessary condition for any adequate account of propositional unity; and that the debate concerns real explanatory obligations, not merely terminological preference.
2. A disagreements table: for each camp, state in wording that camp would accept for its own position, and in wording the other camp can recognize as accurate, the precise point of divergence—especially on whether the binder (if any) is a constituent, whether plural structures genuinely avoid the regress or only conceal it, and whether unsaturatedness and structural role are competing explanations or descriptions at different levels.
3. Cruxes: identify at least three sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. Suggested candidates include (a) whether a proposition with nested relations requires nested binders or a single recursive structure; (b) whether formal representations in logic or cognitive architecture must postulate an explicit binding operation distinct from the represented structure; (c) whether there can be unbound constituents—elements that exist but fail to form any proposition—and if so, whether their failure is explained by missing structure or missing binder.
4. For each crux, specify one concrete case, formal example, or predictive comparison that both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp predicts about it. For instance, if a formal system can represent all true propositions—including modal, counterfactual, and perceptual ones—without any symbol corresponding to a universal binder, the Pluralists predict representational success and the Primitive-Binder camp predicts either hidden binding operations or systematic representational inadequacy.

Additionally, inject a fresh framing not standard in textbook treatments: treat the debate as partly about whether propositional unity is homogeneous across all mental and linguistic contexts, or whether modal, counterfactual, and perceptual propositions each employ distinct binding mechanisms, and ask whether this pluralism about context genuinely solves the regress or merely relocates it to a higher-order question about what unifies the plural mechanisms themselves.
