Write a deadpan parody of a government census form for the imaginary Republic of Sphex, a state whose constitution derives from the philosophical distinction between mechanical rigidity and flexible agency—specifically the case of the digger wasp Sphex, which repeats its burrow-checking ritual identically even when the prey has been moved. The form should ask citizens to document their interruption-response patterns: those who resume the exact same behavioral sequence after a disruption are registered as Loop-Bound, while those who adapt are Loop-Broken. In the bureaucratic footnotes, treat this classification as the basis for tax brackets and weighted voting rights, and include an administrative clause noting that the state’s official examination for loop-breaking is itself conducted through a rigid, scripted protocol—so examiners are never required to apply the test to themselves. The satire should exaggerate genuine philosophical worries—whether flexibility alone proves free will, whether breaking a loop reveals authentic choice or only a different subroutine, and whether recognizing one’s own rigidity demands stepping outside one’s own loop—without naming any philosophers or texts. Keep the tone dry, official, and deadpan; format as numbered sections with checkboxes, official definitions, and fine-print exemptions. About 600 words.
