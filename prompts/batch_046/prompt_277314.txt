There's this debate about the wrong kind of reasons problem—like when you donate to charity mainly for the tax break or to avoid looking bad, not because suffering actually matters to you—and it's been stuck for decads. Everyone rolls out the same cases (fear of punishment, social pressure, tax deductions) and splits into 'moral worth needs pure motives' vs 'the mental state is irrelevant if the outcome is good'. But I think the stall might be hiding something: are they even disagreeing about the same thing? One side might be talking about what explains the action, the other about what justifies it, and they both call it 'the reason'. Or maybe there's a hidden normative claim about whether external incentives corrupt character. What's your diagnosis—verbal confusion between explanatory and normative reasons, a substantive split about the nature of moral evaluation, or just bedrock intuitions that can't be argued for? And if it's one of those, is there anything—maybe a sharper taxonomy of reasons, maybe empirical psychology on motivation—that could actually move it, or is this just permanently stuck?
