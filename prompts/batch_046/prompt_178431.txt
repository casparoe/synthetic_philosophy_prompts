Write a joke that turns on a specific tension in the philosophy of aesthetic testimony: the problem of reconstructive deference, according to which a listener can acquire a justified aesthetic judgment through testimony only if they can mentally reconstruct the speaker's perceptual-affective basis, yet any such reconstruction already presupposes the very aesthetic competence that would make the testimony unnecessary. The joke should place this tension in a concrete scenario—for instance, a visitor at the Rothko Chapel relying on an audio guide that demands she "feel the color field for herself" before accepting the curator's verdict of sublime melancholy. The punchline should depend on recognizing that the condition either collapses into circularity or eliminates testimony altogether, and it should be funny to someone who actually knows the debate between cognitivist and non-cognitivist accounts of aesthetic judgment, rather than being a generic "philosophers overthink art" joke.
