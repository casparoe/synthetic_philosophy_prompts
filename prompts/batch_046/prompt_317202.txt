Consider Descartes's claim that animals are mindless automata, lacking both language and the universal reason that he takes to be necessary for thought. Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when this mechanistic account is applied to an animal behavior exhibiting flexible, context-sensitive problem-solving—specifically, a case in which an animal modifies an available object in an unprecedented way to achieve a distant goal for which it has no specific evolutionary or conditioning history. In constructing the example, explain whether accommodating such behavior within Descartes's framework requires stretching the concept of mechanism until it becomes functionally equivalent to practical reasoning, or whether it instead exposes an instability in the boundary he draws between automated response and rational action.
