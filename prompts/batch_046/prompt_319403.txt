I've been trying to formalize my stance on mechanistic anomaly detection across architectures, and I need a consistency audit before presenting it. Here is my package:

(1) Mechanism identity is causal, not structural: two systems implement the same mechanism if they preserve identical counterfactual dependencies among task-relevant intermediates under arbitrary interventions, regardless of substrate or parameterization.

(2) Anomaly detection should therefore flag a system only when its causal graph over task variables differs in topology or edge weights from the reference, not when it merely employs different internal representations.

(3) Nevertheless, I hold that substrate-level details—whether computation is localized in MLP subspaces, distributed across attention heads, or encoded in superposition—are metaphysically irrelevant to mechanism identity yet practically indispensable for detection, since they determine which interventions are feasible and which failure modes are observable.

(4) Consequently, if a discovered mechanism is causally isomorphic to the reference but implemented in a radically different architecture with dissimilar activations, I classify it as non-anomalous.

(5) Yet I also want to flag cases where a mechanism is causally isomorphic to the reference but relies on spurious correlates that collapse under distribution shift, because the risk profile differs even though the counterfactual structure is identical.

Please go through these commitments, rank the genuine tensions by severity, identify which conflicts are only apparent, and specify the least costly revision for each. In particular, tell me whether (3) can be reconciled with (1) and (4) without undermining (5), or whether (5) forces me to abandon pure causal-graph identity in (2).
