You are delivering the opening remarks at a three-day interdisciplinary workshop convened jointly by a regional environmental authority and a university philosophy department. The audience is split evenly between senior policy analysts—who are trained to translate every conflict into monetized risk assessments or ordinal rankings—and moral philosophers and legal scholars who work on value pluralism. The workshop’s explicit charge is to draft preliminary guidance for land-use decisions in which irreplaceable cultural landscapes conflict with quantifiable public-health outcomes, but the organizers have forbidden any final recommendation that relies on a single unified metric or hidden weighting scheme. Your task is to set the conceptual boundaries for everything that follows: you must articulate why the conflict between ecological integrity and distributive health justice is not merely expensive, tragic, or emotionally difficult, but structurally incommensurable in the strong sense—meaning that no common measure, even an implicit one, can adjudicate between them without misrepresenting the normative content of at least one value. At the same time, you must make this distinction accessible to the analysts without lecturing them, and you must show precisely how this incommensurability constrains what kinds of reasoning the workshop can legitimately produce. Specifically, you should explain that if values genuinely lack a shared metric, then the appropriate task is not to optimize across them but to design decision architectures that preserve the distinctness of each value in the justification of the outcome, and you should sketch what such an architecture might require without prescribing a specific algorithm. The remarks should run about eight minutes, should avoid proposing a cost-benefit framework or a technical decision rule, and should not include bibliographic references or reading suggestions. Ensure that the philosophical claim about strong incommensurability does real structural work: it should determine which forms of recommendation are off the table, what language the final report must avoid, and what kind of non-reductive justification remains available to the participants.
