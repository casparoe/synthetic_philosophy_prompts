In debates over whether to judge Jefferson, Churchill, and Columbus by present moral standards, the "man of his time" defense has become a fixed ritual rather than a moving argument. Defenders treat the pervasiveness of slaveholding, imperial ideology, and colonial violence in each era as background conditions that constrain moral judgment, suggesting that blame requires access to genuinely available alternatives. Critics reply with well-known contemporaneous dissent—abolitionist arguments circulating in Jefferson's milieu, internal British critiques of imperial excess available to Churchill, and Las Casas's documented protests against the treatment of indigenous peoples—to press that the relevant counter-norms were not universally opaque. Both sides concede the basic historical record and agree that slavery, imperial domination, and genocide are grave wrongs, yet the exchange cycles without resolution. Is the stall driven by a hidden ambiguity in the defense itself, collapsing a claim about the causal determination of belief into a claim about the normative accessibility of countervailing reasons, so that defenders and critics answer different questions about responsibility under a single heading? Or does the impasse reflect a case-selection problem, where Jefferson as slaveholder-philosopher, Churchill as imperial administrator, and Columbus as explorer-administrator each pull evaluation in a different direction—character, institutional role, or direct action—without anyone specifying which target the judgment is meant to reach? Diagnose the disagreement: is it verbal, substantive about moral epistemology, or a conflation of historical explanation with moral assessment disguised as a principle debate? And given that diagnosis, what would count as progress—a conceptual distinction between willful ignorance and structural inevitability, an empirical claim about how widespread dissent actually was in each context, or the recognition that the framing itself is ill-posed because it asks for a single verdict across incommensurable roles?
