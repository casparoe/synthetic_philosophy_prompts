In philosophical discussions comparing literary conceptions of time to physical metaphysics, Borges's essay A New Refutation of Time is frequently analogized to the B-theory block universe: both are said to dissolve the flow of temporal succession into a static manifold in which past, present, and future coexist without objective becoming. A colleague in my seminar uses this comparison specifically to defend eternalism against the A-theory, claiming that Borges's literary-philosophical demolition of time provides an intuitive, pre-scientific grounding for the block view. She proposes the following mappings: Borges's denial of an enduring self corresponds to perdurantist four-dimensionalism about personal identity; his reduction of experience to discrete successive impressions corresponds to the temporal-part ontology of objects in the block; and his conclusion that change is illusory corresponds to the B-theorist's denial that there is any genuine passage of time. She further summarizes Borges's argument as proceeding from Berkeley's immaterialism and Hume's critique of necessary connection, and she takes this skeptical-idealist path to reinforce the idea that temporal flow is a mental construction with no extra-mental reality. I want a rigorous audit of this analogy. First, provide an explicit correspondence table pairing each key element of Borges's position -- including the denial of material substance, the denial of causal necessity, the dissolution of personal identity into momentary contents, and the treatment of past and future as present mental modifications -- with its alleged counterpart in the block-universe and perdurantist framework, noting also what in the physicalist framework has no Borgesian counterpart. Second, identify the disanalogies: where does Borges's reliance on idealist and skeptical premises (that matter and causation are mental fictions, and that therefore temporal succession collapses) import metaphysical commitments that physical eternalism explicitly rejects? Separate the disanalogies that genuinely threaten her anti-A-theory conclusion from those that are merely decorative. Third, assess whether the analogy can survive these breaks: does the block view actually need Borges's idealism, or was the analogy smuggling in metaphysical baggage that does the argumentative work? If her conclusion does not survive, state clearly what the analogy was carrying. Finally, if this comparison is ultimately a mismatch, propose a more accurate analog for Borges's actual position -- perhaps to a phenomenological account of internal time-consciousness, or to a different metaphysical framework -- and explain why the replacement fits Borges's commitments more precisely.
