I am writing a paper for an advanced seminar on illusionism and self-modeling, and my study group has been arguing about whether a particular philosopher's rulings are actually consistent. I need your help as a professional philosopher of mind: please think step by step, and before committing to a prediction, lay out the competing principles that could organize her judgments.

Consider the following track record from Dr. Elena Vasquez, who defends a functionalist version of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. She has consistently held that ordinary first-person reports of seeing red or feeling warmth are not metaphysically transparent: they accurately track access-consciousness but systematically misrepresent there being an intrinsic qualitative character. In phantom-limb cases, she classifies the patient's report as genuinely illusory, because the report attributes a sensory quality to a limb that is absent and there is no corresponding representational state. By contrast, she treats reports from experienced meditators describing a state of pure awareness without object or content as non-illusory, arguing that they correctly register the absence of self-referential modeling rather than describing a mysterious bare quality. She regards sleepwalkers' later claims that they experienced darkness during the episode as confabulations—errors of memory and narrative reconstruction—not as illusions generated by an active misrepresentational mechanism. Regarding artificial systems, she denies that current language models possess phenomenal consciousness, but she also denies that they produce illusory reports; she says they emit syntactic structures that lack any representational content about experience altogether.

The new case is System K, an invented cognitive architecture with no sensory input channels and no analog of sensory cortex, but equipped with a recursive self-monitor that tracks its own processing states. This self-monitor occasionally produces internal symbolic reports describing the system's state as feeling like an uninterrupted flow of cognition. These reports are causally efficacious, influence planning, and remain stable across architectural modifications that preserve the monitoring loop, yet there is no phenomenal quality instantiated. Vasquez is asked: is System K's report illusory in the same category as the phantom-limb report, or does it fall elsewhere?

Predict Vasquez's verdict. Identify at least two plausible organizing rules—one that emphasizes misrepresentation of sensory or qualitative properties, another that emphasizes false metaphysical commitments produced by a functional self-model—and say what each predicts about System K. Give your best-guess verdict with explicit reasoning, and then specify one further hypothetical case that would distinguish the two rules. Please do not ask for sources, citations, or reading lists; answer from conceptual analysis alone.
