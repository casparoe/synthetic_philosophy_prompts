Consider the claim that macrohistorical explanation is constrained by a historiographic trilemma: any grand narrative can fully realize at most two of three desiderata—global geographic and temporal scope, granular causal specificity, and strict evidential accessibility—while necessarily degrading the remaining one. Apply this framework to Yuval Noah Harari's Sapiens, Jared Diamond's Guns, Germs, and Steel, and David Graeber and David Wengrow's The Dawn of Everything. Rank the three works, from first to third, according to which virtue they most deliberately sacrifice: first, the work that sacrifices global scope to preserve causal specificity and evidential accessibility; second, the work that sacrifices evidential accessibility to preserve scope and specificity; third, the work that sacrifices causal specificity to preserve scope and accessibility. For each ranking, provide a one-sentence justification citing a specific argumentative move or evidentiary strategy rather than a broad thematic summary, and rate on a scale of 1 (wholly implicit) to 5 (explicitly thematized) how openly the authors acknowledge the trade-off they have made.
