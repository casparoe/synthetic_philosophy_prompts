Write an essay of no more than 800 words assessing whether Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes (MSRP) can rationally adjudicate between competing programmes when both respond to an anomaly by altering their protective belts. Focus on the following constructed comparison: Programme P holds as its hard core that macro-level cognitive representations are strictly compositional. When confronted with robust evidence of holistic, context-dependent encoding, P revises its protective belt by introducing an auxiliary hypothesis about dynamic cross-modal binding; this revision yields an independent, risky prediction—specifically, a previously unobserved pattern of interference between auditory and visual working-memory tasks—which is later confirmed. Programme Q shares P's hard core but, facing the same anomaly, adds a free parameter, "contextual weighting," that can be tuned post hoc to fit any observed encoding pattern without generating novel predictions. The Lakatosian verdict seems clear: P pursues a progressive problem shift, Q a degenerating one. However, consider the objection that P's binding mechanism is not derived from its own hard-core commitments but is imported wholesale from a rival connectionist framework; on this view, P's predictive success is theoretically parasitic, and Lakatos's criterion of excess empirical content fails because it ignores whether a programme genuinely owns the predictions it makes. Weigh the defense of the Lakatosian criterion—perhaps that ownership is irrelevant so long as the protective belt expands the programme's empirical content—against the reply that without an ownership condition, MSRP cannot distinguish genuine theoretical growth from strategic borrowing. Reach and defend a specific conclusion: is excess empirical content alone sufficient, or must it be supplemented by a requirement of internal derivability? Justify your answer by appealing to Lakatos's distinctions between ad hoc adjustments, progressive problem shifts, and the functional role of the protective belt.
