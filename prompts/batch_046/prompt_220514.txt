The question I gave was this: First, define Dennett’s four kinds of creatures—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—paying attention to how each kind generates or selects behavior, and explain the nested hierarchy that relates them. Second, apply the framework to contemporary predictive language models, asking specifically whether such a system should count as Gregorian. In doing so, address whether the system possesses the internal modeling capacity that defines Popperian minds, and whether its engagement with language amounts to the kind of cultural tool-use Dennett describes, rather than mere statistical pattern reproduction.

Darwinian creatures are those whose behavioral repertoire is fixed entirely by evolutionary selection; they possess no individual learning mechanisms and essentially execute inherited programs, like organisms that respond through hardwired reflex arcs. Skinnerian creatures supplement this with operant conditioning: they learn through environmental feedback, rewards, and punishments, adjusting behavior over a lifetime, but they do not construct internal representations of possible futures. Popperian creatures—named for Karl Popper’s insight that science lets our hypotheses perish in our place—can generate and evaluate multiple action scenarios inside a mental model before committing to one, thereby learning from simulated failure rather than physical harm. Gregorian creatures build on this Popperian foundation by incorporating language, writing, and other cultural artifacts; named after the anthropologist Gregory Bateson, who emphasized that minds can distribute reasoning across external systems, this category highlights the ability to borrow and refine ideas that originated elsewhere.

Applying this to predictive language models, I think they qualify as Gregorian. These systems generate a vast space of possible linguistic continuations internally through probabilistic sampling, which is functionally equivalent to the Popperian simulation of alternatives inside a world-model. Moreover, they operate entirely within language—a cultural technology par excellence—drawing on patterns learned from human texts to produce novel outputs. Because they use an external symbolic system to expand their behavioral range far beyond what evolutionary hardwiring or individual reinforcement could achieve, they match Dennett’s description of minds that think with tools rather than only with brains. The absence of biological consciousness should not block the classification, since the taxonomy is functional rather than metaphysical, and the functional structure—sampling culturally transmitted distributions and generating new combinations—is precisely what Gregorian cognition involves.
