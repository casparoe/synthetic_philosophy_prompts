I'm auditing the reasoning of an interdisciplinary panel I've been observing in a graduate seminar on deep-time taxonomy and historical ontology. Over the past months they've rendered verdicts on five proposed start points for the Anthropocene epoch. First, they rejected the "Columbian Exchange" boundary around 1610 on the grounds that global biotic homogenization, while real, produces a regionally staggered stratigraphic footprint rather than a synchronous planetary layer. Second, they rejected the "Industrial Revolution" boundary around 1784 because the associated carbon and sulfur signals emerge diachronously across continents and reflect regional economic transitions rather than a unified global shift. Third, they approved the "Great Acceleration" boundary around 1950, citing the globally synchronous appearance of plutonium and cesium-137 fallout, synthetic chemicals, and sharp nitrogen and phosphorus spikes. Fourth, they rejected the "Synthetic Nitrogen Fixation" boundary at 1913: although anthropogenic nitrogen fixation eventually exceeds natural processes, the isotopic signal is diffuse, builds gradually over decades, and lacks the abrupt transition they require. Fifth, they rejected the "Global Biomass Inversion" proposal dated to roughly 2020, arguing that describing a state rather than a transition offers no clear stratigraphic marker and that preservation in the deep rock record remains speculative.

Next month the panel will consider a new proposal: the "Climate Forcing Inversion" boundary placed at 1971, defined as the first year in which net anthropogenic radiative forcing — accounting for greenhouse gases, aerosols, and albedo changes — exceeded the combined variability range of orbital, solar, and volcanic forcing over the preceding ten millennia. The proponents note that this crossing is detectable in ice-core chemistry and sediment isotopes, but it is not anchored to any single discrete event like a nuclear test or chemical introduction; rather, it marks a statistical threshold in cumulative planetary energy balance.

Predict what the panel will say about the 1971 proposal, and identify the implicit rule — whether it is stratigraphic sharpness and global synchronicity, planetary-scale threshold dominance, institutional narrative legibility, or something else — that best explains all five prior verdicts. If more than one rule fits their track record, lay out the alternatives, say what each predicts about 1971, give your best-guess prediction with reasons, and specify which further case would most clearly distinguish the rules. In your response, please separate what is broadly settled in the relevant literature from what is genuinely contested among specialists who work on epoch boundaries, and do not present any one contested interpretation as if it were the consensus.
