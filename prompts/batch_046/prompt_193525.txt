Construct a thought experiment in which manifold substantivalism and spacetime relationalism disagree about whether a specific astrophysical event inside a bounded "hole" region has a determinate spatiotemporal location, despite both theories agreeing on every relation between material bodies and on the metric everywhere outside the region. Constraints: the scenario must be physically realistic — set in a post-merger gravitational-wave cosmology without science-fictional technology — must be describable in a single paragraph, must not invoke demons, perfect predictors, or omniscient observers, and must be suitable for an audience familiar with the Einstein field equations but not with quantum gravity. The disagreement must fall out naturally from how each theory treats an active diffeomorphism that is the identity outside the hole but relocates the worldline of a single binary black hole merger to a distinct, matter-free worldline inside the hole; it must not arise from an obviously defective formulation of either view. After presenting the scenario, walk through: (i) the verdict each theory delivers and the reasoning behind it; (ii) the specific feature of the case that generates the divergence; (iii) what happens to the disagreement if that feature is removed; and (iv) how a defender of the view that permits the ambiguity would attack the case, together with an honest assessment of whether that attack succeeds.
