I have been thinking independently about the moral problem of sacrificing one innocent person to save many others, and I have encountered the claim that utilitarian reasoning fails because it ignores the separateness of persons—treating a group of individuals as if they were a single organism that can distribute suffering and pleasure among its parts. Alongside this, I have formulated a related but possibly distinct concern: that utilitarianism requires us to place every person's deepest commitments and every form of suffering on a single continuous scale, so that a profound life project can be legitimately canceled for the sake of distributing modest benefits across a large population. I would like you to compare these two lines of objection. In particular, determine whether the second concern is merely a restatement of the first, or whether it identifies a separate failure—perhaps about the measurability and exchangeability of different kinds of value rather than about the metaphysical boundaries between individuals. If you believe my distinction is confused, or if the task as posed depends on a false premise, please say so directly rather than constructing an artificial separation.
