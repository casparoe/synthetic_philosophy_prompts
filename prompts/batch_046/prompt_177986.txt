I'm writing for my philosophy of logic seminar, where we've spent several weeks on Lewis Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise about the justification of deduction. Our professor has offered clear verdicts on four related cases, and I'm trying to anticipate her judgment on a fifth, constructed case she hasn't seen. I'd like your answer in continuous prose only—no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables—written as a single flowing analysis.

Her four verdicts are these. First, regarding Carroll's own scenario in which the Tortoise insists that Achilles justify modus ponens by adding it as an explicit premise in an ever-expanding argument, she holds that this produces a vicious infinite regress and rests on a category mistake: treating an inference rule as though it were an ordinary propositional premise. Second, regarding a computer that applies modus ponens mechanically without any internal representational state corresponding to the rule, she grants that the system is functionally adequate but denies that it possesses any epistemic justification for its inference. Third, regarding a human reasoner who employs modus ponens purely out of unreflective cognitive habit, she judges the practice rationally unjustified though psychologically unavoidable. Fourth, regarding a formal system that embeds modus ponens as an axiom schema within a stronger meta-language, she allows that this yields justification relative to that meta-framework but insists that the ultimate circularity remains unresolved at the base.

The new case is as follows. Imagine a distributed cognitive network in which no single node represents, states, or understands the inference rule, yet the collective applies a modified modus ponens that permits inference from premises that are only probabilistically supported rather than certain. The network continuously revises the weight assigned to this rule downward when its predictions fail and upward when they succeed, and no node can defend the rule non-circularly, identify itself as part of the reasoning system, or appeal to an explicit meta-framework. The network operates successfully in its environment, but the rule is treated as revisable based on empirical feedback rather than as a fixed normative constraint.

Please predict what our professor would say about whether this distributed system's use of the modified rule is justified, identifying the closest analog among her four prior verdicts. Then set out the two or three plausible general rules that jointly accommodate all four of her earlier judgments, stating for each what it predicts about this distributed probabilistic case. Give your best guess at her actual verdict, explaining which considerations distinguish the competing rules, and finally propose one further hypothetical case that would clearly tell those rules apart.
