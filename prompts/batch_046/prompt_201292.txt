I have been thinking about the philosophical architecture of what might be called defeated-nationalism narratives—those collective stories in which a military or political failure is retrospectively transformed into a morally charged founding moment, as with the American Confederacy, the defenders of Masada, the Serbian framing of Kosovo Field, or the British remembrance of Dunkirk. For a seminar I am preparing, I want a decision-tree taxonomy, written entirely in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, that sorts conceptual positions on whether and how a society should maintain such a narrative once the original cause is lost. The tree should start from a concrete normative commitment—specifically, whether one believes that honoring a defeat necessarily implies endorsing the justice of the cause that was defeated, or whether commemorative practices can be cleanly separated from the original war aims—and should then branch through at least two further distinctions: one concerning the temporal orientation of the myth, asking whether it functions primarily to recover a past social order or to project an unfinished moral destiny onto future generations, and another concerning its social purpose, asking whether it operates mainly to consolidate in-group solidarity through shared grievance or to facilitate eventual reconciliation with former adversaries. Each leaf should be named as a specific conceptual stance rather than as a bare historical label, and you should explicitly note any combinations of answers that appear logically available yet remain unoccupied in actual discourse, offering a brief diagnosis of why they might be unstable or unattractive—for instance, a position that demands future-oriented restoration while simultaneously insisting on immediate reconciliation with enemies. The taxonomy must be able to separate at least four genuinely distinct positions, including one that treats the lost cause primarily as a cautionary monument against collective hubris and another that treats it as an active program of national restoration still awaiting fulfillment.
