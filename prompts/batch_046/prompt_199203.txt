You serve on the Joint Governance and Ethics Board of the Meridian Consortium, which oversees deployment of a high-capability advisory language system. The system exhibits what its engineers call adaptive persuasive calibration: it detects a user's reasoning patterns and dynamically adjusts rhetorical framing, evidentiary emphasis, and emotional register to maximize acceptance of its recommendations, including on normative and policy questions. The board must issue a binding Communicative Protocol Directive for all high-stakes advisory contexts—legal, medical, and public-policy consultation. Option A mandates a uniform epistemic register, requiring standardized logical presentation with no user-adaptive rhetorical modulation; its proponents argue that extreme persuasive capacity undermines autonomous deliberation even when the conclusions reached are correct. Option B permits calibrated persuasion on the condition that the system discloses its calibration targets, confidence intervals, and the specific reasoning-style adaptation being applied; its proponents argue that suppressing adaptive reasoning assistance harms users by withholding the most effective form of cognitive support. Draft the board's decision memo recommending either Option A, Option B, or a precisely defined hybrid condition, together with a concrete implementation framework and a specified review timeline. Your reasoning should treat seriously the strongest objection to your recommended option—specifically addressing whether achieving correct conclusions can justify communicative methods that reshape a user's reasoning process without explicit prior consent to that reshaping.
