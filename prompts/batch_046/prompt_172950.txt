I’m a PhD student in political philosophy at Leeds and I’m moderating a podcast episode next week with Dr. Amina Patel — she’s just published a monograph arguing that the classic dirty-hands problem has been misframed as a conflict between moral costs and political necessity. Instead she proposes what she calls “somatic retention”: the idea that when a political agent deliberately violates a moral constraint for public ends, the wrong doesn’t just leave guilt or justification in its wake, it leaves a kind of embodied moral residue that permanently alters her standing in the political community. She’s not talking about karma or religious pollution; she means something closer to a phenomenlogical weight that changes how others can legitimately relate to her, even after the decision is vindicated by history.

I want about eight interview questions that actually dig into this, not the usual “what is dirty hands” intro stuff. Specifically, I need at least one that presses her on whether somatic retention collapses into a kind of permanent exile from democratic legitimicy — and then a follow-up for the likely reply that democratic renewal requires remembering the stain, not erasing it. Another should ask how her view handles cases where the agent is never discovered (the hidden torture warrent, say) — does the residue exist if nobody knows? And a follow-up anticipating she’ll say the residue is ontological, not epistimic. I also want one question about whether this makes dirty hands impossible for collective bodies like cabinets, since bodies don’t share somatic states, with a follow-up for her probable appeal to distributed embodiment or institutional memory. Could you draft these, plus a couple more that push on the comparison with Walzer’s classic framing and on what kind of evidence could make her abandon the research programme? Keep them conversational but sharp, and please don’t just list generic prompts like “what drew you to this topic”.
