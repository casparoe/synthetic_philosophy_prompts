I've been reading about the pessimistic meta-induction — the idea that since past scientific theories turned out false, we probably shouldn't treat current ones as literally true — and the realist responses that point to preserved structures or approximate truth. But the discussion feels stuck in a loop: one side lists overturned theories like phlogiston or caloric, the other insists that successful theories survive in some form, and neither seems to address whether they're even talking about the same kind of "survival." I keep wondering whether the hidden problem is a disagreement about what makes a later theory count as "the same" theory as its predecessor. For example, is the relationship between Newtonian gravity and general relativity genuinely parallel to the relationship between phlogiston chemistry and modern oxidation theory, or are these different kinds of conceptual replacement that shouldn't be lumped together? If the two sides are using different, unstated criteria for theoretical identity across revolutions, they might not be disagreeing about history at all — they might be answering different questions under the heading "does science progress?" Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is: a verbal dispute about "continuity," a substantive historical disagreement that could be settled by comparing specific cases, or a deeper conflict about what scientific explanation requires? And if it's one of the first two, what conceptual distinction or focused comparison could break the stalemate? If it's the third, is there any reason to expect progress, or is the question itself ill-posed?
