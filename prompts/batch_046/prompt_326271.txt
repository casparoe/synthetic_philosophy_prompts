I am trying to isolate a recurring asymmetry in debates about whether DNA is literally a code, and I wonder whether it has an established name. The pattern runs like this. When evolutionary lineages naturally alter a codon-to-amino-acid assignment—say, in mitochondria where a stop codon is reassigned—biologists routinely describe the result as a distinct “genetic code,” treating the mapping change as a genuine revision of the symbol system. Yet when synthetic biologists engineer the very same kind of reassignment in a laboratory organism—reprogramming a stop codon to specify a non-canonical amino acid via the same ribosomal machinery—critics often insist that this is not “rewriting the code” at all, but merely “exploiting redundancy” or “redirecting existing machinery,” as though the symbolic relation itself remains untouched. In both cases the formal mapping table is altered, the causal mechanism is identical, and the new symbol-to-meaning pairing is stably inherited; the only salient difference is evolutionary provenance. So people appear to use evolutionary origin as a gatekeeper: a mapping counts as part of “the code” only if it was produced or modified by natural selection, regardless of structural or mechanistic parity.

Is there an established concept for this specific move—using evolutionary history to adjudicate whether a symbolic or informational mapping is genuinely modified rather than merely instrumentally redirected? How does it differ from standard etiological theories of biological function, from conventionalist arguments about the arbitrariness of the genetic code, and from ordinary anti-reductionist caution about synthetic systems? If there is no precise term, what should we call the boundary condition being enforced here, and where exactly does it break down—does it apply only to codon reassignments, or also to synthetic promoters, artificial chromosomes, or entirely de novo genomes that never existed in nature? I am less interested in whether DNA “really” is a code than in carving this particular judgment pattern precisely: what it includes, what it excludes, and whether it tracks anything other than an implicit requirement that genuine symbolic relations must have an evolutionary pedigree.
