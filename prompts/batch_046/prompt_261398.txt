I've been thinking lately about those science-fiction teleporters that destroy your original body and rebuild you atom-by-atom somewhere else, and I keep getting stuck on a strange middle case that seems to break the usual yes-or-no framing. Imagine the machine is running outdated firmware that applies a kind of lossy compression to your mind: it preserves your general temperament, your taste in music, and your reflexive moral instincts, but it drops every specific autobiographical memory—so the replica knows she is afraid of heights and loves jazz, but cannot recall her mother's face, her childhood address, or the reason she owes money to a particular creditor. Now suppose she applies to collect the original person's life-insurance payout, and the insurer must decide whether she counts as the same beneficiary. I find it darkly amusing that we treat personal survival like a light switch—either you survived or you didn't—when this scenario suggests it might work more like a dimmer switch turned to about sixty percent.

I have no formal background in philosophy; I just read speculative fiction and overthink it. I would like you to write an original joke that turns precisely on this tension: the idea that psychological continuity might come in degrees rather than being all-or-nothing, and that legal or social institutions are forced to treat a fuzzy, continuous phenomenon as a binary classification. The punchline should depend on the specific problem of partial preservation—perhaps the replica is deemed "enough you" to inherit your debts but not enough to inherit your spouse, or perhaps a bureaucrat assigns an identity percentage and disputes arise over rounding errors. The joke should make sense to someone familiar with the basic teleporter puzzle—body destroyed, mind reconstructed elsewhere—but it should not be a generic reference to Star Trek transporters. It should feel somewhat in the weeds, rewarding the intuition that the real philosophical difficulty lies not in whether the copy lives, but in deciding exactly which features have to survive for the copy to count as you. Please do not include citations, references to specific philosophical texts, or a bibliography; I am asking for an original joke built around the concept itself.
