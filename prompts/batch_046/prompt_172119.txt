I have been thinking about the symbol grounding problem on my own, without formal training in philosophy of mind or computer science, and I have settled on a view that seems different from both the standard "it needs a robot body" answer and the pure computationalist answer. Here it is: a symbol is genuinely grounded if the system's use of it is locked into a stable, counterfactual-sensitive tracking relation with the thing it stands for, and this locking can be achieved through mediated causal chains—other systems, instruments, records, corrections—without the system itself ever having direct sensory contact with the referent. Direct embodiment is one way to establish this, but not the only way; what is necessary is not biological sensation but the structural preservation of the referent's properties across perturbations in the mediating chain. So a language model that interacts only with text could, in principle, ground a symbol like "gravity" if its internal states are arranged so that they covary reliably with gravitational phenomena through a sufficiently robust network of measurements, models, and adjustments made by other agents and devices, and if it updates those states correctly when the chain is disrupted.

What would have to be true for this to be wrong? I want an honest map of its exposure, not just a list of objections I have already heard. Specifically:

- Which empirical findings—about how humans actually acquire concepts, or about the failure of mediated tracking in artificial systems—would count as decisive against the view rather than merely awkward?
- Are there conceptual arguments, perhaps involving the distinction between derived and intrinsic intentionality, that show mediated tracking can never do the normative work I claim it does?
- Could there be a formal or structural result showing that counterfactual stability across a mediated chain either collapses into direct contact (making my view redundant) or dissolves into mere correlation (making it insufficient)?
- What about cases where the mediating chain is intact but understanding is clearly absent—would those be fatal, or could I accommodate them by refining the "structural preservation" condition?
- Have I qualified the view into unfalsifiability—if every failure of grounding can be blamed on a break somewhere in the chain rather than on the principle itself, is that insulation a problem?
- Rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. For each, say whether a committed defender of my position would be forced to concede it, or whether they could dismiss it without abandoning the core idea.

Please be direct about whether the position is well insulated, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind. I am especially interested in whether there is any result or observation that would move me personally, and whether the view risks predicting nothing that its rivals do not also predict.
