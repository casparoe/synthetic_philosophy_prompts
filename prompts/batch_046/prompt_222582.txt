I'm in this development econ seminar and I've landed on a pretty strong view: we'll never actually know if institutions, geography, or culture drive national wealth, not because we lack data but because history gives us exactly one realization per country and you can't rerun the world with different borders or climates. The identification failure is structural, not temporary — even a complete panel from 1500 onward just compares bundles that never seperated. What observations, arguments, or formal results would actually move me? Sort them by how decisive they'd be versus just awkward for my side, and say what defenders of institutional, geographic, or cultural determinism themselves concede counts against them. Also be honest: has this skepticism been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that actually a problem for a claim about causal knowledge? One last thing — do synthetic control methods for countries actually solve the counterfactual problem here, or do they just make missing data look more elegant? I keep seeing them cited like they fix identification and I don't buy it.
