I hold that metaphysical possibility is tracked not by bare logical consistency but by what I'll call constructible conceivability: a scenario guides us to real possibility only when we can specify a generative procedure or algorithmic mental model that produces it without contradiction, rather than merely failing to detect an inconsistency in it. On this view, zombie worlds are thinly conceivable but not genuinely so, because no constructive specification can render a physical duplicate lacking consciousness; the absence of contradiction is not enough. What would count against this? I want the exposure mapped honestly: observations or formal results where constructible and bare conceivability diverge in ways that break the link to possibility, arguments that the constructive requirement collapses into epistemic limitation or computational tractability, and whether the view can be qualified so that every apparent counterexample is dismissed as merely "thin" — and if so, whether that insulation is a defect. Please distinguish what defenders should concede as genuinely damaging from what they can legitimately dismiss, rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say whether any live escape routes remain that don't render the position unfalsifiable.
