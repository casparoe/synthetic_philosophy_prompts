I am preparing a position paper for my philosophy of mind independent study, and I would like a careful audit of whether my commitments form a coherent package before I present them to my advisor. Here is where I have landed: (1) I endorse a causal-role functionalism about propositional attitudes—beliefs and desires are individuated entirely by their patterns of causal interaction with sensory inputs, behavioral outputs, and other mental states. (2) I accept the standard multiple-realizability thesis: in principle, a silicon-based system, an alien organism with copper-based neurochemistry, or even a sufficiently organized distributed network could realize the very same belief-state, provided the functional architecture is preserved. (3) Nevertheless, I maintain that genuine realization—rather than mere simulation—requires what I call “causal density,” a specific threshold of reciprocal, high-bandwidth interaction among implementing nodes that biological brains happen to exhibit and that simple lookup tables or linear pipelines lack. (4) I reject Searle’s Chinese Room argument because I think syntax becomes semantics precisely when it is embedded in such a causally dense network, so the room as usually described fails for lack of density, not for being computational. (5) At the same time, I hold that phenomenal consciousness—what it is like to experience red—is not multiply realizable; I believe only substrates possessing a particular kind of biologically specific “integrated feedback loop” can instantiate qualia, and that no silicon analog, however dense, could replicate it. (6) Finally, I think moral status should depend on functional organization and causal density, not on biological origin, so that a silicon system meeting the density threshold would deserve full moral consideration, even though it might lack inner experience.

Do these six commitments hang together? I am especially worried that (3) smuggles in a physicalist or substrate-sensitive constraint that undermines the purity of (2), and that (5) creates an exception for qualia that either collapses my functionalism in (1) or forces me to deny that phenomenal states are mental states at all. But I also wonder whether the tension between (2) and (5) is only apparent, since one might restrict multiple realizability to intentional states alone while treating consciousness as a separate, biologically anchored category. Please rank the tensions by severity, identify which pairs are genuinely incompatible versus only superficially so, and tell me the least costly revision—whether I should weaken my functionalism, refine my notion of causal density so it applies differently to intentional and phenomenal states, or drop the biological exclusivity of qualia. Be specific about which single commitment has to give if consistency is to be restored, and explain what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package.
