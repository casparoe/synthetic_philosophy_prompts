I'm serving on our university's interdisciplinary resource-ethics panel — a bit of a ragtag mix of philosophers, computer scientists and an economist who never stops talking about bargaining theory. Next Friday we vote on whether to approve a new shared compute-cluster protocol that the designers are calling a safe Pareto improvement: every research lab keeps its current baseline allocation, but they can opt into a redistribution pool where contributing spare processing cycles earns priority access, and the claim is that no lab can end up worse off regardless of what strategy anyone originally chose. I was discussing this with a friend over coffee after my collective-decision course, and she raised what I'm tentatively naming the shadow-strategy gap — all the safety proofs assume strategies are fully declared, but the strategic behaviour of labs can include hidden reserve capacities, letting them secretly withhold contribution while still receiving priority benefits through the baseline interaction. I realise this sounds overly cautious, but I'm worried the protocol is only safe for transparent actors, not for the actual messy system we run.

Could you draft the short decision memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting? Please write the answer in American English. Recommend approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with clear reasoning and an actionable bottom line including a specific timeframe if conditions are needed. You need to engage seriously with the strongest argument on the other side: that any change which expands the feasible set without contracting anyone's original allocation is ethically and economically unproblematic, and that worrying about hidden strategies is just demanding an impossible level of transparency. Don't provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists — I want the reasoning to stand on its own. Also, please don't use quotation marks around anything taken from an actual published text; paraphrase instead. And don't make it to long — half the panel reads these on their phones between meetings.
