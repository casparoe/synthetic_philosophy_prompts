Please write a poem about Thomson's lamp and the supertask of completing infinitely many toggles before time 1, presented as exactly five numbered couplets with no introduction or conclusion; each couplet should consist of one or two complete sentences. I am a software engineer without formal philosophy training, but I see the lamp as a loop with no termination condition flipping a boolean at intervals 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, and so on, so that at the limit the state is undefined rather than true or false. Use an alternating refrain — 'The lamp is on' and 'The lamp is off' — across the first four couplets, then break the pattern in the fifth couplet with a line that expresses the absence of a determinate state at time 1. The poem should do structural work: the rhythm or rhyme should echo the accelerating intervals, and the turn at the fifth couplet should correspond to the philosophical point that there is no hidden fact of the matter. It should be precise enough that a philosopher of logic or metaphysics recognises the paradox, yet readable without specialist jargon.
