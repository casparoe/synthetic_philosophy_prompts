Construct a Gettier-style scenario that satisfies the following specifications jointly. The setting must be an ordinary workplace, described in a single paragraph, with no exotic technology, perfect predictors, or science-fictional devices. The protagonist S comes to hold a justified true belief P, and the reasoning chain that produces it contains no false premises or false intermediate beliefs—so the familiar repair that excludes false lemmas does not apply. Engineer the case around one hidden structural feature: S relies on a primary informational method that is locally coherent but globally unreliable in this domain (it typically yields error), yet an independent redundant cross-check mechanism—unknown to S and lying entirely outside her reasoning—intervenes to correct precisely the mistake her primary method would have produced, leaving her belief true. Design the case so that a pure internalist about justification counts P as knowledge, since S’s internal reasoning is impeccable, while a process reliabilist denies knowledge, because the global belief-forming process (including the hidden corrector) is not one whose success is creditable to S’s cognitive competence. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that each specification holds: state the belief P, describe the primary method, identify the hidden corrector, specify the relevant nearby worlds, and explain exactly why each view delivers its verdict. Then isolate the feature that does the work, explain whether removing it collapses the case into a standard false-lemma Gettier problem or causes it to disappear entirely, and state how an internalist defender would attack the reliabilist’s denial.
