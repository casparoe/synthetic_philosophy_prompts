Please design a decision tree for a graduate seminar on populist rhetoric that classifies theories of 'the will of the people' through three successive diagnostic questions, each phrased so a student can answer it without knowing the theoretical labels in advance. First: is 'the people' conceived as a singular corporate agent possessing its own intentions prior to aggregation, or as a plural collection whose will is constructed by some procedure? Second, if constructed procedurally, does legitimacy require preserving raw individual preferences (for example, through direct majority rule), or transforming them through deliberation, representation, or leadership interpretation? Third, is the authentic will accessible only through direct popular expression (plebiscites, mass polling), or can non-elected institutions—constitutional courts, expert agencies, or representative assemblies—correctly identify it even when it contradicts present popular sentiment? At each terminal node, identify the standard view if one exists (for instance, Rousseauian general will, pure aggregative majoritarianism, deliberative democratic transformation, Schmittian plebiscitary leadership, or epistemic trustee models), and in one sentence specify its most awkward commitment when direct popular claims conflict with institutional ones. Then flag any logically possible combination of answers that lacks a named defender, and briefly diagnose whether the absence reflects incoherence, practical unattractiveness, or mere neglect in the literature. Keep the tree free of bibliographies, citations, and reading lists.
