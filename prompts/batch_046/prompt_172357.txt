In discussions of market efficiency I often hear the line, 'if the price already reflects everything, there's nothing left to disagree about,' used to dismiss active trading altogether. I know that packs together several distinct claims. Can you separate the precise theses that sentence could be expressing — for each, specifying whether it turns on private information being revealed, on common priors, on common knowledge of rationality, on a particular equilibrium concept, or on the interpretation of price movements — and say which are near-tautologies, which are substantive but defensible, and which are false? Then tell me which of these readings actually supports the conclusion that rational agents should never trade, and whether any version survives once we allow heterogeneous risk preferences or disagreements about model specification rather than about fundamentals. Lay the readings out as a menu with verdicts, not as a single interpretation.
