I was arguing with some friends last night about cosmological arguments, and I ended up defending this hybrid position I don't see talked about much: the kalam argument is right that an actual infinite temporal regress can't reach the present, but the contingency argument is also right that even an eternal universe needs an explanation for why it exists rather than nothing. So I think you should combine them — the universe began in a metaphysically real sense (not just physical time running out) and it's also radically contingent at every moment. The two premises protect each other: if physics ever shows eternal physical time, the contingency limb still demands a ground; if someone says contingency can be satisfied by an infinite chain of contingent things, the kalam limb blocks that regress by denying an actual infinite.

What would count against this? Not the usual "God of the gaps" handwave or "science explains the big bang now." I want the actual defeaters — like whether presentism is required for the kalam limb to work, or if there are metaphysical results showing actual infinities are consistent, or arguments that contingency explanations can bottom out in brute facts without being viciously circular. Rank them from definately fatal to merely awkward. Which ones would a committed defender of this merged view honestly concede, and which are just misreadings? Also, does this hybrid risk being unfalsifiable — like retreating to "metaphysically beginning" even if physics says eternal — and is that insulation a problem for an argument like this?
