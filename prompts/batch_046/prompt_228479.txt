I serve on the University Archive and Attribution Committee. We must rule on whether to list the legacy climate controller in Building C — a 1982 two-register feedback device (the Bitser-2) that adjusts ventilation using only two binary occupancy/state bits — as co-inventor on a patent that mines its forty-year adjustment logs for an optimization algorithm. The applicant argues that because engineers have consistently interpreted the device's state transitions as wanting thermal stability, and because Dennett's analysis of the intentional stance treats such interpretation as genuinely predictive rather than merely projected, the controller's derived intentionality has stabilized into something functionally equivalent to original intentionality through long operational coupling. The patent office counters with Searle's distinction: the registers have syntax without semantics; any aboutness belongs to the interpreting engineers, not the mechanism, and granting co-inventorship confuses observer-relative ascription with intrinsic mental content. Our policy permits attribution only to entities bearing original intentionality or to human agents directing derived systems. Please draft the committee's decision memo recommending a concrete course — grant, deny, or grant with conditions — together with an implementation timeline, serious treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side, and an explicit statement of how confident the recommendation is and which point it is least sure about.
