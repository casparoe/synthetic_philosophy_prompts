I have been working through two puzzles on my own, without formal philosophical training, and they keep converging on the same underlying problem. The first is a situation in which a group of perfectly identical rational agents will each attend a party if and only if they believe that none of the others will attend; because they all reason identically, they reach the same belief, which means either all attend or all stay away, and both outcomes violate the very condition that was supposed to govern their choice. The second is a lottery that pays out exclusively to people who do not purchase a ticket, yet an agent will only care about the payout if she expects to win it, which she will only expect if she does not buy the ticket, so the decision to buy seems simultaneously rational and self-defeating. I understand these are sometimes referred to as the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery.

Rather than asking for a direct solution, I want the full specification—the desiderata—that any adequate theory of rational commitment in deterministic, self-predictive situations must satisfy. Please list the requirements, explain the case or reasoning behind each, distinguish the non-negotiable ones from those that might be relaxed, and flag any pairs that appear jointly unsatisfiable. The list should cover, at minimum: whether the theory must permit coordination without direct communication; whether it must maintain a strict separation between predicting one's own action and causing it; whether it must block circular preferences in which desiring an outcome depends on expecting it; whether it applies symmetrically to first-person and third-person reasoning; and whether it can handle what I call the Echo Condition, a variant I have invented in which the payoff depends not on the physical action taken but on whether every other identically rational agent reaches the exact same reasoning conclusion, with no observational check available between them.

Then, briefly, indicate how causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and any view that appeals to superrational commitment or transparent pre-commitment perform against this list— noting where they clearly fail or succeed, without requiring full formal proofs. I do not want a bibliography, reading list, or citations to original papers; I want the criteria themselves, because I intend to evaluate any proposal I come across against them. Please keep the discussion conceptual rather than historical, and write at a level that assumes I have thought about the logic but have not memorized the literature.
