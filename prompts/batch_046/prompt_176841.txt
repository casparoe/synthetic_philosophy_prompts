If heterophenomenology treats first-person reports as texts belonging to a fictional heterophenomenological world rather than as transparent windows onto consciousness, then applying the method to an artificial system that generates token-level descriptions of its own inner states should be direct: we interpret its outputs as an authored narrative without taking a stand on whether any corresponding subjective reality exists. Given this, why is Dennett's approach not straightforwardly eliminativist about the consciousness of any system that cannot produce such linguistic reports — including non-human animals and the artificial system itself? More precisely, if the method brackets ontology entirely and replaces it with interpretive charity toward a text, what work remains for the distinction between realism and eliminativism about qualia, and does extending the framework to non-linguistic or synthetic subjects not simply collapse that distinction?
