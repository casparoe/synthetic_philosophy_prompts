hey im trying to build an argument for a seminar on hume's standard of taste and i think i broke it. (1) Hume says true beauty judgments come from ideal critics - people with delicate sentiment, practice, comparison, and no prejudice. (2) But imagine two groups both meeting those criteria who still disagree irreducibly, like one finds brutalist architecture beautiful and the other ugly with no further argument settling it. (3) Hume has to call one wrong, but since both are ideal there's no internal reason to pick. (4) So either he admits both are right - killing the idea of one standard - or he sneaks in that ideal critics just are the ones who get it right, making the standard circular. (5) Either way his middle ground collapses: into pluralistic relativism or into hidden objectivism. (6) And this isnt just hume - any response-dependent view that claims objectivity hits the same wall when qualified judges diverge. (7) So we're left with either pure subjectivism or full-blown aesthetic realism where beauty is just a brute property like density. (8) But realism cant explain why aesthetic education changes our judgments, since if beauty is brute you dont need refined taste just instruments. (9) So every position self-destructs. where do i cut? id really like to save (1) and (6) if possible but i feel like im missing a move.
