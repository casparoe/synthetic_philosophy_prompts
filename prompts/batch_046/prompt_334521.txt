In AI alignment research and the surrounding rationalist discourse, there is a pervasive design and interpretive practice: we treat a deployed system—whether a large language model, a reinforcement-learning agent, or an organism—as possessing unified goals, beliefs, and strategies for the purposes of prediction, evaluation, and safety engineering, speaking routinely of "reward hacking," "corrigibility," and "the model's objective." At the same time, in our theoretical framing—particularly under the embedded-agency view—we deny any Cartesian separation between system and environment, insisting that cognition and decision-making are fully distributed across training dynamics, feedback loops, hardware substrates, and institutional controls, with no independent causal core inside a boundary.

What philosophical commitments about agency, explanation, and ontology does this dual practice presuppose? Must one be a functionalist or instrumentalist about mental attribution to maintain both frames, or can a realist about embedded agency hold them consistently without smuggling in a methodological dualism? Reconstruct the implicit theory of agency here, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, and tell me what would have to change in our evaluation and containment practices if we abandoned the unified agent-frame entirely and treated the system only as an embedded causal process lacking any singular goal-structure.
