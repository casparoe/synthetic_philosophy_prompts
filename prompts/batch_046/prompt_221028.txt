Does it only make sense if species are real joints in nature that one physical object can lock in forever, or is it just a bureaucratic fiction that borrows essentialist language without needing it? And if working biologists already treat species as fuzzy population clusters with no fixed essence, why does the catalog system still act like there's an eternal nature tied to one specimen? I'm not sure wheter the practice strictly needs essentialism or if it's philosophically underdetermined — compatible with several rival views. Could you reconstruct the implicit metaphysics, say whether different parts of taxonomy presuppose consistent things, and tell me what museum practice would actually have to change if we replaced the anchored-type view with a purely phylogenetic one?
