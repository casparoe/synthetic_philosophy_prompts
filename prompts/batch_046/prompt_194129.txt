I am preparing a colloquium Q&A with Dr. Lin Zhou, who defends a revisionary account of wrong-kind reasons that she terms structural bifurcationism. On her view, the familiar division between right-kind and wrong-kind reasons for belief and desire—evidential versus pragmatic, value-based versus instrumental—is insufficient. She introduces a third category, structurally wrong-kind reasons, which arise not from the content of an attitude but from a mismatch between the normative authority of the practice in which the attitude is formed and the source of the reason. For example, a juror who comes to believe a defendant guilty because that belief coheres with her political identity has not acted on a pragmatic reason such as avoiding social cost, but on a structurally wrong-kind reason: the reason derives from an identity-commitment that lacks authority within the epistemic norms of jury deliberation. Zhou argues that recognizing this category resolves puzzles about collective agency—where agents seem to have team reasons that are neither purely evidential nor purely prudential—without collapsing into expressivism about normative judgment.

Please draft a sequence of eight interview questions that press her view in the places where the literature on wrong-kind reasons is most contested. I want at least one question that probes the boundary she draws between structurally wrong-kind reasons and ordinary pragmatic reasons, with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that structural reasons are practice-constitutive rather than outcome-oriented. I want at least one question on how her account handles the classic motivational problem—why agents should care about avoiding wrong-kind reasons if those reasons are often psychologically more compelling—again with a follow-up anticipating her appeal to second-order normative authority. And I want at least one question about whether her framework can accommodate aesthetic judgment, where the norms governing appreciation seem less institutionally fixed than in juridical contexts, including a follow-up that presses her if she appeals to local normative architectures to do the work. Avoid generic biographical or definitional questions; each question should turn on the substance of structural bifurcationism and its interaction with standard objections.
