Been reading causation stuff for a side project and I keep noticing this weird dance that I cant find a name for. Someone starts with a counterfactual -- like, if the plant hadnt leaked, the river wouldnt be dead. Then you ask how they actually know that counterfactual, and they switch to intervention talk: "well, if we regulate similar plants, levels drop." Okay, but regulating isnt the same as never having leaked, right? So you press that, and they slide back to possible-worlds language, saying the intervention just reveals the underlying structure. Then in overdetermination cases they talk about necessary parts of sufficient sets, which sounds like interventionist machinery, but they defend it with Lewis-style counterfactuals about what would happen if one cause was absent. It feels like theres a specific hybrid pattern here: using counterfactual dependence for the ontology of what causation IS, but borrowing interventionist tools for how we FIND OUT about it, and treating the mismatch as harmless. Does that oscillation have an established name? I know theres the standard debate between counterfactual and interventionist accounts (Pearl, Lewis, Woodward) but Im not asking about that debate itself. Im asking whether philosophers have named the specific move of blending them into one framework with an epistomology-ontology split like Im describing. If not, how should I distinguish it from just being an ecumenical pluralist? And does the overdetermination case actually expose the crack, or am I overreading?
