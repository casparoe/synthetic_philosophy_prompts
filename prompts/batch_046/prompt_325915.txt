I've been trying to formalize the dark forest resolution to Fermi's paradox, and I'm stuck on whether universal concealment is actually an equilibrium. If every civilization reasons that disclosure is strictly dominated by silence—independent of the other's intentions—then a no-emission protocol seems like it should be globally stable. But then shouldn't the absence of detectable megastructures update us mainly toward universal adoption of that protocol, rather than toward rarity? And since the argument hinges on common-knowledge suspicion, wouldn't applying Aumann's agreement theorem suggest that any overlap in utility functions should push rational agents toward transparent coordination, making preemptive destruction a coordination failure rather than the default? I keep seeing rationalist discussions treat the forest as inevitable, but I can't see why the reasoning doesn't collapse into either observational equivalence or an unstable game. What's the missing piece?
