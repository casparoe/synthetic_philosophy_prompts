I've been convinced lately that the alignment problem isn't really a bundle of seperate issues — inner alignment, outer alignment, robustness, distributional shift, whatever — but actually one single thing I keep calling the specification fracture: the fact that any objective you write down is gonna be a lossy compression of what you actually want, and that loss gets amplifed by any system smart enough to optimize hard. So the whole field is basically studying the same fracture from different angles. What would actually count against that? Not just listing other problems, but what kind of result, case, or argument would show that these are genuinely different failure modes that don't reduce to one underlying gap? I'm especially curious about whether there are formal results or thought experiments where fixing the specification fracture in one domain makes another failure mode worse — that'd be the kind of thing that'd really shake me.
