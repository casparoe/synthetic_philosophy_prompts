Write a short research proposal (approximately 800 words, suitable for a philosophy-of-science workshop pitch) on the following question: in what sense, if any, is DNA a code, and does the concept retain explanatory value once we consider regulatory mechanisms—such as non-coding RNAs and chromatin-level inheritance—that operate through spatial conformation and scaffolding rather than through sequence-specific translation? State the question precisely: existing debates often treat genetic information either as a literal information-theoretic code or as a purely heuristic metaphor anchored to the central dogma of molecular biology, but they tend to neglect whether regulatory processes that lack a one-to-one mapping between nucleic-acid sequence and functional outcome can be accommodated within either account. Explain why a conceptual rather than empirical project makes progress here: the task is to articulate criteria that distinguish a code (arbitrary symbol-to-meaning mapping mediated by a readout apparatus) from a causal template or structural scaffold, and then apply those criteria to epigenetic and RNA-mediated regulation without conducting new laboratory work. Say what the obvious alternative approaches are—e.g., a purely historical analysis of the metaphor's origins or a quantitative information-theoretic measurement of genome entropy—and explain why they fail to settle whether the code concept does explanatory work in the regulatory cases. Describe what a successful result would look like: a principled distinction that shows exactly where the code metaphor captures mechanistic reality and where it obscures three-dimensional causation, applied consistently across coding and non-coding contexts. Specify what would count as failure, such as collapsing into the claim that all biological representation is metaphorical without discriminating among representational kinds, or producing criteria so permissive that every causal scaffold counts as a code. Finally, give an honest scope assessment: this is feasible for a single workshop paper because it relies on widely accepted molecular-biology examples and conceptual analysis, though it risks becoming too large if the criteria for codehood require resolving broader debates about biological representation in general. Do not include a bibliography or literature review by citation; you may refer to positions and arguments informally and note that citations will be added later.
