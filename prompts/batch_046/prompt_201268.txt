Essay notes and proposed argument:

Working title: Rational Irrationality Without Unity—The Hitchhiker as Inter-Stage Bargaining

Core claim: The canonical presentation of Parfit’s hitchhiker assumes a unitary rational agent whose preferences are perfectly transparent to an external predictor. I want to argue that this presupposes a “unity of practical reason” that sits in tension with Parfit’s own reductionist view of personal identity. If we take reductionism seriously, the agent who promises payment in the desert and the agent who arrives in town are not strictly identical; they are distinct temporal stages linked by psychological continuity and predictive expectation rather than by a substantial self. On this model, the problem is not that one rational subject is irrational to break a promise, but that the arrival-stage exploits the predictive vulnerability of the departure-stage. I propose calling this dynamic “diachronic exploitation.”

A second, more technical claim: rational irrationality only arises under conditions of perfect predictive transparency. In practical analogues—contractual pre-commitment mechanisms, reputational markets, or institutional correction protocols—predictors observe noisy signals rather than direct access to future mental states. Once transparency is imperfect, the ex post incentive to defect weakens, because the predictor’s belief is already partially fixed and cannot be fully updated by the defection itself. I frame this as the “transparency-cost model” of commitment: the rational sustainability of a promise depends on the cost to the predictor of maintaining an accurate belief about the agent’s future conduct.

Finally, I suggest that this reframes the moral of the case. Rather than treating it as a paradox of individual rationality, we should read it as a design problem for any system—biological or artificial—whose components must coordinate across time despite locally divergent incentives. The hitchhiker’s dilemma is therefore less about one person’s irrationality and more about the conditions under which inter-stage cooperation can be stabilized without a unified executive self.
