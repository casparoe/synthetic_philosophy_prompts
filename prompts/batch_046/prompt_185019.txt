Please assist me in understanding a particular section of Galileo Galilei's 1615 letter to the Grand Duchess Christina of Lorraine, in which he discusses the scriptural objection drawn from the Book of Joshua. In this part of the letter, Galileo considers the account in which Joshua asks God to cause the Sun to stand still so that the day might be prolonged. Rather than conceding that this implies the Sun moves around the Earth, Galileo proposes an alternative astronomical interpretation consistent with Copernicanism: he suggests that God stopped the rotation of the Sun upon its own axis, which Galileo takes to be the source of motion for the entire system, including the Earth's daily turn. Consequently, halting the Sun's spin would also halt the Earth's rotation, thereby lengthening the day without requiring the Sun to orbit the Earth. Galileo then remarks that he offers this not as a demonstrated proof of Copernicanism, but merely as a debating point to show that even on literalist premises the heliocentric model can accommodate the text. At the same time, he insists that Scripture is accommodated to the capacities of its audience, that nature is not subject to such accommodation, and that where clear sensory demonstration conflicts with a traditional biblical reading, the interpretation of Scripture must be revised. He also distinguishes theology's status as queen of the sciences, arguing that it holds this rank not because it contains all scientific knowledge, but because it directs humanity towards salvation.

Given this, I should like to understand precisely how Galileo's specific reinterpretation of the Joshua episode relates to his broader hermeneutical rule about clear and manifest reason. In particular, does his astronomical explanation of the miracle depend upon a physical claim about the Sun's rotational mechanics that he regards as only probable, and if so, how does he avoid undermining his own argument that scriptural interpretation must yield only to demonstrated, not merely probable, conclusions? Furthermore, how does his insistence that this is only a debating point sit with his simultaneous claim that the Copernican system is supported by physical discoveries that clearly confute the Ptolemaic system?
