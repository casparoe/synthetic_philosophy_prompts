I'm prepping interview questions for a podcast with an archeologist who wrote a book arguing that "collapse" is basically a category mistake — societies don't fail, they just downscale and relocate complexity into forms the archaeological record misses. She talks about the Bronze Age breakdown, the Classic Maya transition, and Easter Island, but her specific claim is that in each case complexity didn't vanish, it just moved into kin-based ritual networks or dispersed exchange systems that don't leave big monuments. I want questions that press the actual mechanics, not "what inspired you."

Specifically I need:
- One on Easter Island that forces her to deal with the deforestation/moai-building link without letting her retreat to "it was ritual success." Follow-up should target her likely claim that tree loss was intentional resource redirection.
- One on the Bronze Age: Linear B literacy dissapears almost completely after the palatial collapse. If complexity just relocated into smaller networks, why did that particular technology vanish? Follow-up for her standard reply that writing was always elite-bound and not part of the relocated social fabric.
- A Maya question: how does she distinguish her view from ordinary post-classic reconfiguration theories? Follow-up anticipating she'll say those theories still treat it as decline in scale, and asking what observable archaeological signature would differ.
- Then ask what evidence could actually convince her that a society genuinely failed rather than just transformed scale.
- And a closing one on why the framing matters — does calling it collapse vs transformation affect how we read climate stress warnings today? Follow-up pressing whether her view risks normalizing every breakdown.

Make them sound like real spoken questions, keep it casual, maybe a typo or two is fine.
