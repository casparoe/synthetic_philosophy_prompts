I have been working through the conceptual architecture of Dennett's real-pattern ontology and the associated trio of explanatory stances—the physical, the design, and the intentional—particularly as they apply outside the philosophy of mind, in domains like systems biology, climate dynamics, and the interpretation of artificial neural networks. What strikes me is that practitioners in these fields move constantly between mechanistic accounts that stay close to physical causation and functional accounts that invoke goals, errors, or representational content, often without any articulated principle for when such a shift is genuinely explanatory rather than merely convenient. This suggests to me that there are several live, interrelated conceptual questions about the boundaries and dependencies among the stances, but I am unsure which ones are foundational and which are derivative.

If one had limited resources—say, three years of focused philosophical work with a background in philosophy of science rather than empirical neuroscience—which open questions in this area should take priority? I can see at least three candidates. First, whether real patterns can be rigorously characterized for non-biological, non-intentional systems in a way that justifies applying the design stance without smuggling in intentional language. Second, whether predictive success achieved purely at the design-stance level can ever, in principle, undermine the need for a physical-stance account, or whether the dependency is always one-way. Third, whether collective entities—ecosystems, economies, or distributed computing architectures—can be legitimate targets of the intentional stance without committing us to group minds or emergent subjects.

Rather than asking for an exhaustive survey, I want a defended prioritization: rank these (or substitute others you think are more important), explain the criteria you are using—whether tractability, upstream dependence, or potential to reshape practice—and specify what kind of result or argument over the next few years would cause you to reorder them. I am especially interested in where the edges lie: which questions turn out to be verbal disputes about stance-labeling, and which involve genuine ontological or explanatory commitments that cannot be dissolved by re-description.
