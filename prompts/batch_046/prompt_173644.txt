In my seminar on Geological Time and Anthropocene Studies, our instructor has reasoned through four boundary questions using a consistent framework. First, she endorsed the ICS designation of the Holocene beginning roughly 11,700 years before present, on the grounds that the marker is a globally synchronous physical signal in ice cores and that the deciding body possessed a clear intergovernmental mandate limited to terrestrial stratigraphy. Second, she rejected treating the Industrial Revolution as a formal geological epoch, observing that despite profound socio-economic transformation there is no comparable global stratigraphic signature and that periodization authority belongs to economic historians rather than geologists. Third, she supported the 1945 radionuclide spike as the strongest candidate marker for an Anthropocene boundary, but insisted that formal adoption would require an interdisciplinary commission rather than the ICS alone, because the cause is unmistakably anthropogenic and the evidence spans chemistry, climate science, and archaeology. Fourth, she dismissed a proposal to date the Anthropocene from 1957 based on orbital debris and satellite proliferation, arguing that once human impact extends beyond Earth’s lithosphere the ICS lacks jurisdiction and no recognized interplanetary scientific body yet exists to render a decision.

We must now evaluate a new proposal for our final discussion. It contends that the Anthropocene should be formally dated to 1998, the year a continuous deep-space telemetry network achieved permanent interplanetary coverage, on the theory that this created an exospheric stratigraphic layer—distributed spacecraft materials, radio-frequency signatures, and engineered objects across multiple celestial bodies—thereby rendering human influence genuinely solar-systemic rather than merely terrestrial. The proponents maintain that the decision should fall not to the ICS but to a proposed Interplanetary Geological Commission comprising astrobiologists and orbital physicists.

If our instructor wishes to preserve consistency with her prior rulings, what are the plausible candidate rules jointly compatible with all four judgments? For each rule, predict whether she should accept the 1998 proposal with the new commission, accept it only under ICS authority, or reject it altogether. Provide your best guess about her actual verdict, identify the closest prior analog, and justify your preference among the rules. If the precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, articulate the distinct principle she would be adopting with each possible choice, and specify one further hypothetical boundary case that would clearly distinguish the competing rules.
