In this scenario, either individual can access, modify, and recall the other's mental contents with the same first-person immediacy she applies to her own, yet both bodies and brains remain physically distinct and separately embodied.

Write an essay predicting Locke's philosophical judgment on whether such linked individuals could eventually constitute one distributed person rather than two. Structure your response in the following sections, in this exact order, with approximate lengths as indicated:

4. The case for Lockean resistance (about 200 words): Argue how Locke might reject the conclusion, perhaps by insisting that first-person accessibility, bodily continuity, or the unity of substance still fractures personal identity across two distinct organisms despite shared mental content.

5. Verdict and synthesis (about 150 words): State a clear, non-ambivalent conclusion about what Locke would likely decide, and justify it by appealing to his broader empiricist commitments and his distinction between substance and personal identity.
