Write a taxonomy decision-tree for sorting positions on whether germs, climate shifts, and volcanic eruptions are "historical actors" rather than mere background conditions, intended for an advanced seminar on environmental causation and moral responsibility. The instrument should contain exactly five binary branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete claim or case judgment that a respondent can answer without knowing the position labels.

Branch 1: Can non-intentional forces—the plague pathogen, the 1815 Tambora aerosol veil, or sustained drought cycles—count as explanatory actors, or is "agency" reserved for intentional beings?

For those who answer yes, Branch 2 tests counterfactual robustness using paired historical cases: without the Black Death, would European feudal labor structures have persisted largely unchanged; and without Tambora's 1816 climate shock, would the associated grain crises and political unrest have been avoided? This separates "strong-actor" views (the event is necessary for the specific trajectory) from "weak-actor" views (it accelerates trends human institutions would eventually have produced).

Branch 3 asks whether human institutions and these environmental forces genuinely co-produce outcomes (interactionism), or whether human responses become epiphenomenal once the shock is introduced (determinism).

Branch 4 applies to determinists: does treating nature as an actor preserve normative accountability—can we still blame societies for failing to adapt—or does it dissolve accountability by rendering outcomes inevitable?

Branch 5 applies to interactionists: does attributing actor-status to nature risk "the determinism fallacy"—using non-human causation to diminish human moral responsibility for mitigation? Frame this with the concrete case of whether a state's failure to contain disease spread remains a moral failure if the pathogen is classified as an independent actor.

Every leaf must carry: a label (name a recognized position if one exists, else "unnamed"), a one-sentence core commitment, and its most awkward commitment. Map the full logical space of answer-combinations, mark unoccupied cells, and briefly diagnose each gap as incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate—especially whether weak-actor interactionism and strong-actor interactionism with accountability collapse on Branch 5. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or source quotations; use the Black Death and Tambora only as common reference points.
