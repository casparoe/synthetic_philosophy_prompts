In a philosophy of art seminar discussing whether destroying a statue genuinely annihilates an object, a fellow student kept insisting that 'the statue is nothing over and above the bronze arranged statue-wise.' She used the line to argue that smashing the statue merely rearranges matter, so artistic destruction is conceptually equivalent to reshaping a lump of clay, and therefore no distinct entity is lost. I want to bring a precise disambiguation to our next session. Lay out the distinct metaphysical claims this slogan could be expressing — for instance, ontological reduction, supervenience without identity, hylomorphic composition, modal-property divergence, or mereological essentialism — and for each reading say whether it is a truism, a substantive but defensible thesis, or false. Then apply them to a specific complication: the bronze contains stable trace isotopes from a unique meteorite source, and these impurities persist regardless of how the metal is reshaped. Which readings of the slogan survive this case, and which ones actually support her conclusion that no distinct object is destroyed? Present the readings as a menu rather than selecting a single interpretation, and indicate precisely which ones do the argumentative work she needs.
