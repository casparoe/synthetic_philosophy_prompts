my roommates Maya and Leo got into it over dinner about this idea called tranquilism — the view that only the absence of craving and suffering actually matters, and pleasure or happiness dont add any moral weight at all. Maya was defending it pretty hard. Leo said thats basically just negative utilitarianism with extra steps. Maya said no, negative utilitarianism still treats suffering-minimization as the goal and allows pleasure as a side benefit, but tranquilism says the presence of joy literally counts for zero. Leo then asked: if only absence matters, is a perfectly tranquil living person — awake, calm, zero cravings, zero pain — actually morally better than someone who just died? both have zero suffering. Maya said technically theyre tied, and thats not a bug, its the whole point: existence itself isnt a gift that needs justifying. Leo said then theres no reason to save a drowning child if you could do it instantly and painlessly, since death just achieves the same null-state. Maya said killing still usually involves fear or grief for others, which are cravings/sufferings, but if you genuinely could eliminate all sentient beings with zero distress you'd just be maximizing absence. Leo said she was describing a world where the best outcome is universal death or coma. Maya then brought up this distinction she likes between a "null-state" (dead, or deep coma, no mental content at all) and a "neutral-state" (conscious but with complete affective flatness, like deep equanimity). she said tranquilism values both equally since both lack craving, but that doesnt make it useless — it just means consciousness without craving is morally neutral. Leo said that proves her view cant distinguish between a monk in deep meditation and a rock, so it gives us no reason to prefer one over the other. they kept circling and eventually asked me to referee since i was just sitting there eating fries. can you go through their exchange point by point and say whos right about what? and does Maya's null-state vs neutral-state thing actually save tranquilism, or is Leo right that it makes the view collapse into something that cant guide any real choices?
