I want a coherence audit on my collective-intentionality package before I post it. My commitments: (1) Bratman-style reductionism about joint action—interlocking intentions plus common knowledge, with no irreducible we-mode; (2) small groups can hold genuinely irreducible collective beliefs that supervene on interaction patterns via normative tethers, by which I mean deontic bindings that can hold between agents even when one party (say, a child) lacks the conceptual resources to represent the commitment; (3) joint attention is necessary for joint action, but only in a weak sense—each agent must represent the other as attending to the same object, with no shared phenomenal character required; (4) I reject Gilbert's plural-subject account because it requires a shared mental representation of the commitment as ours; (5) functionalist accounts capture large institutions but fail for face-to-face coordination precisely because they miss these normative tethers.

Do these hang together? In particular, does (2) conflict with (1) if collective belief requires irreducible collective intentionality that Bratman's model cannot deliver? Is (4) consistent with (2), or does my normative-tether idea actually reconstruct the core of Gilbert's view without the mental-representation baggage I reject? Does (3) undercut (1), since common knowledge of interlocking intentions seems to demand richer shared attention than my weak condition allows? Rank the tensions by severity, identify the cheapest single revision, and flag any pair that looks tense but dissolves on inspection—especially anything involving (5).
