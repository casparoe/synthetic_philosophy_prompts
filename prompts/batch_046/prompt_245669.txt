I'm on the advisory board for the proposed National Museum of Ancient Narratives, and we're deadlocked on the layout for our permanent "Origins" wing. We've got three clusters: the Trojan War, the Arthurian legend, and the Exodus. The curators want to label them all "historical traditions with varying evidentiary bases," but half the board says that's a cop-out—if we treat King Arthur and the Exodus the same way we're being either dishonest or politically cowardly. Meanwhile another member keeps pushing this idea of "narrative sedimentation," where stories accumulate different kinds of evidentiary layers over time, so the real question isn't "did it happen" but what kind of sedimentation profile a tradition actually has.

I need a memo for Friday's vote recommending a concrete labeling and spatial policy—like whether we physically seperate the clusters, use different wall colors or display cases, whatever—with a clear rationale. Don't just say "everything is constructed"; engage seriously with the view that there is a meaningful threshold where legend becomes history, and address the worry that any line we draw smuggles in our own biases about what counts as proof. The memo should be something the board can actually vote on, not just philosophy-speak, and please include an honest treatment of the strongest argument for the other side.
