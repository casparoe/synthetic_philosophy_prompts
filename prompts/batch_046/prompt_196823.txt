Please argue that a mechanist can answer Penrose's Gödelian anti-mechanist argument by adopting a stratified hierarchical model of cognition, rather than relying on the familiar reply that human reasoning is merely an inconsistent formal system. Specify how one mechanical subsystem might treat another subsystem's formalization as an object of computation—analogous to an interpreter running on hardware distinct from the interpreted program—thereby allowing recognition of that lower system's Gödel sentence without the recognizing subsystem being governed by the same formal limitations. Then expose the critical vulnerability: either the hierarchy terminates in a top-level system that remains fully mechanizable and therefore subject to its own Gödel sentence, or it produces an infinite regress that never yields the global semantic grasp of consistency that Penrose requires. Conclude with which horn of this dilemma is more damaging to mechanism, and explain why distributing consistency-recognition across hierarchical levels does not by itself constitute a non-mechanical form of understanding. Keep the reasoning self-contained, do not request citations, references, or reading lists, and avoid treating the argument as a simple restatement of Lucas's original formulation.
