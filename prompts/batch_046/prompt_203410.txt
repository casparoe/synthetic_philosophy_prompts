Predict Peirce's position on whether this procedure genuinely advances inquiry toward the settled opinion he envisioned that all investigators are destined to reach, addressing specifically: (1) whether distributed algorithmic consensus can substitute for the communal convergence of actual inquirers; (2) whether tying belief to predicted practical effects rather than observed ones violates his fallibilism; (3) how he would distinguish this from William James's more individualistic emphasis on the cash value of belief; (4) whether such a system risks fixing belief prematurely through institutional authority rather than through the self-correcting method of science; and (5) what revision he might propose to make the protocol genuinely Peircean. Present your answer as exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
