I am working through some readings for an informal study group on metaphysics of properties, and a friend sent me a summary they wrote up after skimming a few encyclopedia entries and forum threads. They put it like this: "The classic finkishness problem—usually traced to C.B. Martin—concerns an object that genuinely possesses a disposition but loses it precisely when the triggering stimulus occurs, as with an electro-fink wire that becomes insulating the moment it is touched. That is different from masking, where some external factor such as packing foam prevents the manifestation without destroying the underlying disposition. Lately, though, people have started talking about intrinsic masks: protective mechanisms that are built into the object's own structure rather than being separate from it. The worry is that if intrinsic masks are allowed, then for any disposition we ascribe—fragility, solubility, conductivity—the object could contain an internal blocker that activates exactly upon stimulation, rendering all dispositional claims unfalsifiable. Defenders of the conditional analysis respond by saying we can save the account with a refined counterfactual that holds the object's internal constitution fixed in the antecedent, which means the analysis fails only for extrinsic masks and survives intact for intrinsic ones."

Something about this sounds suspicious to me—especially the claim that refined counterfactuals cleanly save the analysis for intrinsic cases, and the way the unfalsifiability worry is framed—but I am not sure which parts are standard simplifications and which are genuine distortions of how philosophers like Martin, Johnston, or more recent commentators actually draw the line between finks, masks, and counterfactual defenses. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a common pedagogical simplification, and what subtly misrepresents the standard positions—particularly regarding whether intrinsic masking really rescues the conditional analysis or whether it raises a distinct problem.
