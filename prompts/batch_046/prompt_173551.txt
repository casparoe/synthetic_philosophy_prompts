I'm writing a paper for my philosophy of action course on the debate over which kind of free will is actually "worth wanting." Please compare Harry Frankfurt's hierarchical compatibilist account—in which free will requires that one's effective first-order desires align with higher-order volitions one genuinely endorses—with a reasons-responsive compatibilist view, according to which what matters is not the internal hierarchy of desires but the agent's capacity to recognize, weigh, and react appropriately to reasons for action.

To keep the comparison from becoming purely abstract, apply both frameworks to the same problem: cases in which an agent's preferences have been gradually and invisibly reshaped by sustained exposure to an engineered choice-environment—for instance, a predictive digital ecosystem that uses feedback loops to align the user's desires with commercially optimized outcomes, leaving the user with fully endorsed but externally scaffolded volitions. Does Frankfurt's model correctly diagnose why this might still fall short of genuine autonomy, perhaps because the second-order endorsement itself was cultivated by the system? Or does the reasons-responsive view do better, maybe by asking whether the agent retains a live capacity to critically evaluate and resist the reasons being presented?

Please do not merely survey the two theories; take a reasoned position on which framework offers the more convincing account of why such manipulation undermines free will, if it does at all. Specifically, say what would change your mind: would it be an empirical finding about the neuroscience of preference-formation, a conceptual distinction between procedural and substantive independence, or a counterexample in which full hierarchical alignment occurs without any external scaffolding? And please indicate where your reasoning is most likely to fail—whether in assuming that endorsement requires a specific causal history, or in treating reasons-responsiveness as separable from the history of how one's reason-recognizing capacities were developed.
