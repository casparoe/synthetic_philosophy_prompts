The summary runs as follows: Szasz held that mental illness is literally a fiction, since conditions such as schizophrenia and depression lack any corresponding brain lesions, which means psychiatric diagnosis operates not as medical science but as a secular form of heresy prosecution, labeling socially disapproved behaviors in order to justify involuntary confinement. Drawing on transactional analysis, he is said to have treated the clinical encounter as a strategic game governed by implicit rules, so that symptoms are not manifestations of an underlying pathology but moves in a social interaction designed to evade personal responsibility; consequently, prescribing psychotropic medication for these conditions is conceptually incoherent, analogous to treating a breach of contract with antibiotics, because there is no organic disease present to be targeted. The summary concludes that if one takes Szasz seriously, one must reject not only coercive commitment but also voluntary psychiatric care altogether, since entering any therapeutic relationship concedes the false premise that the patient actually suffers from an illness.
