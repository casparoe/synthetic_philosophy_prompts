As a retired physicist recently turned to philosophy of science, I ask you to draft the adversarial-collaboration joint statement that proponents and critics of the doomsday argument could both sign. Deliver it as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. First item: list the shared commitments—Bayesian conditioning on observed birth rank near 60–100 billion, agreement that empirical extinction-risk forecasts are distinct from the probabilistic framing, and acceptance that the core dispute concerns a sampling assumption rather than observational data. Second item: state the precise disagreement in wording each side accepts—proponents hold that the Self-Sampling Assumption licenses a downward Bayesian update on total human population N, while critics hold that the Self-Indication Assumption or an ill-defined reference class blocks that update. Third item: identify the first crux—whether observer-selection effects should be modeled via SSA or SIA—specify the agreed test case comparing rank predictions for a species with a known small census against one with an unknown census, and record each side’s prediction. Fourth item: identify the second crux—whether the argument has empirical predictive content or is purely a framing artifact—specify the agreed test case involving the discovery of a well-defined, long-lived extraterrestrial observer class, and record each side’s prediction. Fifth item: say whether any portion of the disagreement is verbal rather than substantive, and state explicitly what argument or evidence would change your mind about which crux is decisive. Keep formulations rigorous, avoid bibliographies, and do not request external sources.
