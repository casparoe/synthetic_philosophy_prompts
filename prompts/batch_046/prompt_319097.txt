There's a weird split in how the law of war actually works. On the ad bellum side we ask if each side's cause is just, and we might say only one is. But on the in bello side we apply the exact same rules to both - both can kill enemy soldiers, both gotta take prisoners, neither can fake being civilians. The institution treats combatants as moral equals in the fighting rules even while admitting their causes arn't equal at all. What commitments does that split presuppose? Does it need the view that moral equality is just a practical convention, or can it survive if rights depend on actual threat? And if we dropped equality and made in bello rights track ad bellum justice - so unjust soldiers are basically murderers and just ones are defending themselves - what breaks? Do surrender rules still make sense, or does the whole Geneva framework fall apart?
