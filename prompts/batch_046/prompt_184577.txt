Design a classroom simulation for an upper-level philosophy seminar—twelve students, seventy-five minutes, no external technology—that makes the group feel the force of attempting to jump out of a formal system from within. Center the exercise on a modified string-transformation puzzle (begin with the symbol-string MI and apply four mechanical rules—append I→U, double Mx→Mxx, replace III with U, and drop UU—to see that MU is unreachable) but inject a niche mechanism I will call a Reflection Token. Phase One (twenty minutes): pairs work with physical card-strings and apply only the four rules, competing for points (one point per valid derivation, minus one for any rule violation) to build the longest valid chain; they will fail to reach MU. Phase Two (fifteen minutes): each pair receives exactly one black Reflection Token card on which they write any English statement attempting to escape or diagnose the system—e.g., “The system cannot generate an initial M followed by a solitary U”—and the facilitator mechanically encodes it into the formal alphabet via a fixed substitution code (A=M, B=I, C=U, space=Ω) and adds the resulting string-card to the shared pool. Phase Three (twenty minutes): all pairs must apply the original four rules mechanically to these encoded meta-statements, treating them as ordinary symbol-strings; they will discover that the “outside” claim has become just another derivable object inside, producing bizarre but formally valid strings rather than genuine escape. Debrief (twenty minutes): map Phase One to internal derivation, Phase Two to the intuitive act of JOOTSing, and Phase Three to the re-absorption of the meta-perspective; be explicit that the simulation cannot reproduce the actual number-theoretic impossibility proof (the divisibility-by-three argument that explains why MU is unreachable) and that you, the facilitator, must supply that separately. Include card materials, the encoding protocol, roles (two Derivers and one Encoder per pair in Phase Two), the point scheme, and an honest note that the activity also cannot reproduce the subjective experience of understanding why the proof works—only the boundary failure. Predict what will go wrong: students will smuggle natural-language reasoning into Phase One derivations, write ambiguous or self-referential Reflection Tokens in Phase Two that resist clean encoding, and in Phase Three will debate interpretation rather than apply rules mechanically; handle this by enforcing a strict “mechanical-only” referee rule with immediate point deductions. Specify exactly what participants should leave seeing: that any attempt to state the system’s limitation becomes, once articulated, a new element governed by the very rules it sought to transcend.
