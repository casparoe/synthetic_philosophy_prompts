Kant distinguishes acting in accordance with duty from acting out of duty. Only the latter has morral worth. For Kant, the determining ground of the will must be the moral law itself, not any inclination. So if Dr. Reeves stays late because she expects satisfaction, then even if she mentions duty, the real cause is her feeling. That means she lacks moral worth.

On Hume's side, reason alone is inert and can't move us to act. But Hume also thought that moral approval comes from rational intuition about social utility, not just raw feeling. So reason doesn't motivate Dr. Reeves to stay, but it can evaluate whether her stay is useful to society.

That means her action is done from duty despite the co-present feeling, so Kant should grant her moral worth.
