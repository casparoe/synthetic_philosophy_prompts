I am a sixth-form student and the student representative on my school's Ethics and Resource Allocation Sub-Committee. At our meeting next Thursday we must vote on whether to endorse the local authority's proposed Critical-Threshold Welfare Model for distributing our partnership funding for the student mental-health programme. The model applies a population-weighted calculus that directs resources to maximise aggregate welfare above a fixed critical level set just above neutrality. In practice, this means funding minimal-intervention group support for approximately four hundred students—bringing them from negative welfare to barely positive states—rather than funding intensive one-to-one therapy for roughly forty students who would reach high welfare. Several committee members argue that this reproduces the repugnant conclusion familiar from population axiology: that a vastly larger population of lives barely worth living can be ethically preferable to a smaller population of flourishing lives. Others maintain that the critical-level threshold is designed precisely to block that conclusion, though critics respond that fixing the threshold just above neutrality may generate a parallel difficulty—namely, that it becomes obligatory to bring lives into existence at that minimal level regardless of how well those lives could go above it.

Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action—either endorsement subject to specified conditions, rejection, or approval with a defined time frame for review—and include reasoning that treats the strongest philosophical consideration on the losing side with genuine intellectual honesty rather than dismissive restatement. The memo should be addressed to the sub-committee and written in a tone appropriate for circulation among both staff and student representatives.

In your response, please distinguish clearly between what is settled in this area of philosophy and what remains genuinely contested among those who specialise in it, and do not present a contested view as though it were the consensus position.
