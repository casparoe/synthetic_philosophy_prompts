I sit on the advisory panel of the National Foundation for Fundamental Science. By Friday we must circulate a formal written opinion recommending whether to renew a ten-year core grant for the Institute for Macroscopic Biological Coherence, which investigates whether quantum coherence plays a functional role in cellular metabolism. The field has undergone three major theoretical reversals in the past half-century, each initially presented as experimentally decisive and later abandoned as measurement precision improved. The director's renewal application argues that this history does not undermine the institute's work. She proposes a defense she calls 'indexical realism': theoretical terms such as 'coherent state' do not denote enduring natural kinds but index stable measurement profiles that persist across theoretical change, so the pessimistic meta-induction fails because no ontological commitment is actually overturned—only the descriptive framing shifts. A minority faction on the panel contends that this defense collapses scientific realism into instrumentalism and that public funding should not support research that, on its own account, makes no claim about the structure of the world. Draft the panel's memo recommending renewal, non-renewal, or conditional renewal with an explicit review timeline. The memo must engage the director's argument on its own terms—specifying exactly where indexical realism either neutralizes or evades the pessimistic meta-induction—and it must take seriously the minority's strongest objection, namely whether avoiding ontological commitment removes the justification for state support of basic science.
