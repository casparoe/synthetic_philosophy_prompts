me and two friends in a logic study group got stuck arguing about Lewis's triviality results for indiciative condtionals. Jordan said the result basically kills any real, non-trivial probability for 'if A then B' that isn't just P(B|A). He thinks this means any AI planning system that assigns graded belief to conditionals is built on sand, since Lewis showed you can't keep Adams's thesis together with a coherent unconditional probability for the conditional itself. Maya pushed back. She said Jordan's treating the proof like it applies to every credance structure, but there's a gap if you allow framing-dependant sample spaces — the background conditions held fixed can shift with the antecedent in a principled way, so the same conditional gets evaluated against different spaces. She argued this isn't cheating; it's just recognizing that 'if the match strikes, it lights' and 'if the match strikes and it's soaked, it lights' are anchored to different default backgrounds, and Lewis assumes one global space. Jordan replied that's just re-labeling conditional probability: you're computing P(B|A) inside each frame, so you've conceded Adams's thesis locally, and the global unconditional measure still collapses or becomes undefined. Maya said the global 'view from nowhere' was never the point — people don't have one — and Jordan is demanding something Lewis never justified. Jordan then said if there's no global measure, talk of 'the probability of the conditional' is ambiguous, which proves his point that indicatives don't carry stable probabilities.
