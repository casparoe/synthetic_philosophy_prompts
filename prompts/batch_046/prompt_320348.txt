I'm preparing a school debate on whether larger populations are morally preferable, and I've hit what seems like a direct conflict between two positions. Benatar's asymmetry holds that bringing a happy person into existence is not a benefit to them—since they wouldn't suffer deprivation in non-existence—while preventing suffering is clearly good. Anti-natalists take this to mean we should avoid creating people altogether. Pronatalists reply that total welfare still rises with more happy lives. But I then encountered "critical-level" views, which propose that lives above a specific welfare threshold can be added for the greater good, while lives below it should not be created. Doesn't Benatar's asymmetry actually undermine this threshold idea? If the absence of pleasure is never a harm, then even lives well above any proposed critical level fail to generate a compensating benefit for those who never exist, which makes the threshold seem arbitrary rather than morally grounded. So is the real problem not pronatalism versus anti-natalism, but whether any aggregation principle can survive Benatar's asymmetry intact?
