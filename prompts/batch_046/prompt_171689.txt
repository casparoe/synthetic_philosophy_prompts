I am defending my seminar paper—tentatively titled Moral Worth Without the Duty/Inclination Binary—before a mixed moral philosophy workshop next Thursday. The thesis is that moral worth should be evaluated not by whether an agent acts from duty rather than inclination, but by whether the operative reason is reflectively stable under what I call motivational transparency: the agent’s reason survives a second-order check in which she asks whether it would still motivate her if she knew her cognitive architecture were being optimized for long-run cooperative signaling rather than for tracking normative truth. I claim this explains why some apparently right-reason actions lack full moral worth, and why some instrumentally motivated actions can retain it.

The audience: two analytic Kantians who treat the Groundwork distinction between acting from duty and acting in accordance with duty as canonical; one virtue ethicist who regards reasons-talk as already a category mistake; one empirical moral psychologist who studies altruistic motivation experimentally; and one philosopher of action with decision-theoretic sympathies who has written on self-prediction. I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense: list the objections in roughly the order they will actually come, phrased in each interlocutor’s vocabulary. For each, indicate whether I should concede something up front, what I should hold, the core reply suitable for about two minutes, and the weakest point in that reply. Order them by likely damage, not politeness. Include the objection I am hoping nobody asks—specifically, whether my transparency condition either collapses into moral luck or presupposes an implausible Cartesian access to mental states that empirical psychology denies. Flag which objections are substantive and which are mainly vocabulary mismatches.
