I am organizing a colloquium interview series on philosophy of art, and next session's guest is an unnamed aesthetician who defends a reconstructed Du Boisian account: she maintains that art is constitutively, not merely instrumentally, propagandistic, and that this commitment dissolves what she calls the neutrality trap in contemporary aesthetics. On her view, aesthetic value just is counter-hegemonic efficacy evaluated through structural position-awareness, a mode of perception she claims is mediated by the viewer's social location rather than being universally accessible. She insists that this does not collapse art into politics because she distinguishes internal evaluation, which concerns how well the work achieves its propagandistic structure, from external evaluation, which concerns whether its political aims are justified.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to summarize Du Bois's familiar claim that all art is propaganda. I want at least one question pressing the distinction between propaganda that succeeds aesthetically and propaganda that succeeds only politically; at least one asking whether counter-hegemonic efficacy can be assessed without smuggling in the aesthetic autonomy she rejects; at least one probing what structural position-awareness requires of viewers who do not occupy the relevant social position; and at least one challenging her internal and external evaluation distinction with a case where the two seem inseparable. For each of these four, include a specific follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, in particular anticipating her reply that internal evaluation concerns formal coherence with the work's propagandistic aim, and asking what prevents that reply from being circular. Do not include generic biographical questions, and do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
