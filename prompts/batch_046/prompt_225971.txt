Take the 1925 Scopes Trial in Dayton, Tennessee -- you know the one, Darrow vs Bryan over teaching evolution in public schools. Analyze it through Wittgenstein's view that religious belief isnt a hypothesis competing with science but a form of life with its own grammar. I dont want a generic "they talked past eachother"; map exactly where in the trial both sides treated belief as evidential, and whether Wittgenstein's framework even allows for disagreement between a scientist and a believer in the way the trial staged it. Then push further: in his later lectures Wittgenstein says religious pictures dont function like empirical predictions but like frames that give evidence its sense. Does that mean the Tennessee law wasnt protecting belief so much as enforcing one form of life over another? And does the trial show his view is descriptively right but politically useless, or does it reveal that public education is exactly the place where forms of life collide and cant stay seperate?
