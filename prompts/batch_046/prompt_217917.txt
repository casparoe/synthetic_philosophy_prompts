Within this framing, inject the following underexplored conceptual distinction: a functionally convergent singleton might still preserve internal jurisdictional autonomy (a "federated singleton"), yet face a distinctive epistemic closure problem—once it monopolizes sensors and computation, error-correction depends entirely on internal mechanisms without external benchmarks. Given this landscape, please do the following. First, spend a few paragraphs brainstorming at least six genuinely open conceptual questions or lines of work that arise specifically at the intersection of the singleton hypothesis and this federated/epistemic-closure framing—not generic AI governance questions, but issues that turn on whether a singleton is unitary or federated and whether its knowledge is self-correcting. Second, select the three questions or research directions you believe deserve the bulk of the group's effort over four years. For each selected item, defend its rank using explicit criteria: (a) importance—why resolving it changes how we should think about long-term governance; (b) tractability—why philosophers and conceptual analysts rather than empirical engineers are best positioned to advance it now; (c) dependency—what other questions it unlocks or blocks; and (d) neglectedness—whether existing literature addresses it adequately. Third, explain the trade-offs: which high-importance questions you are deprioritizing, and why tractability or dependency concerns push them lower. Fourth, specify what kind of conceptual result or intermediate finding over the next two years would cause you to reorder these priorities—what evidence or argument would move one up or down? Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; the deliverable should be a defended research agenda that a philosopher could pursue without web access.

Take your time. Think step by step, and begin with the brainstorming phase before you commit to the final ranking.
