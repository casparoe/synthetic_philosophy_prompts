I am preparing the syllabus for an advanced seminar on the epistemology of skill, and I want to present my students with a taxonomy-reconstruction exercise based on preliminary judgments offered by my colleague, Dr. Samuel Okonkwo, in our department’s working group on propositional and practical cognition. His assigned task has been to classify particular cognitive achievements as knowing-how, knowing-that, or as resisting both categories, and he has offered the following eight verdicts without defending any explicit principle.

His classifications are these. First, a fluent speaker’s ability to produce grammatically well-formed sentences in her native tongue without being able to cite syntactic rules: knowing-how. Second, that same speaker’s explicit belief that a particular utterance violates subject-verb agreement: knowing-that. Third, a surgeon’s manual dexterity in applying sutures with precisely calibrated tension: knowing-how. Fourth, the surgeon’s explicit belief about the tensile strength of a particular suture material: knowing-that. Fifth, a chess grandmaster’s immediate, non-inferential recognition that a given board state is advantageous for White: knowing-how. Sixth, the grandmaster’s explicit belief that the King’s Indian Defense requires a specific pawn structure: knowing-that. Seventh, a musician’s capacity to improvise a coherent melody within an unfamiliar modal scale—one she has never formally studied—guided solely by auditory intuition: knowing-how. Eighth, that musician’s explicit belief that the scale contains an augmented second interval: knowing-that.

Now consider the following new case for the working group. A computational biologist has cultivated, over years of practice, an embodied expertise in calibrating the parameter space of a single-particle cryo-electron microscopy reconstruction pipeline. She can adjust defocus ranges, dose weights, and Bayesian polishing thresholds so that the Fourier shell correlation reaches a target resolution, and she describes these adjustments as feeling the right range rather than computing values. Nevertheless, if directly asked, she can correctly state the mathematical relationship between defocus and the contrast-transfer function; she reports, however, that she never invokes that equation during actual calibration, and that her adjustments break down if she attempts to execute them purely by applying the formula without the instrument present.

What should Dr. Okonkwo classify this capacity as—knowing-how, knowing-that, or a hybrid—if he wishes to remain consistent with his eight prior verdicts? In your answer, identify at least two competing rules that could rationalize his track record, such as a criterion based on causal role in execution versus one based on reflective availability of propositional content; state what each rule predicts about the biologist’s case; and identify which of the eight prior cases serves as the closest analog. Then offer your best guess at Okonkwo’s actual verdict, with explicit reasons, and be candid about whether his precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome. If they do, articulate the principle he would be adopting by going one way rather than the other. Finally, propose one further hypothetical case—preferably involving a different domain of expertise—that would best distinguish the competing rules you have reconstructed. Please do not ask for bibliographies or citations; reason from the structure of the judgments themselves.
