Quine's Two Dogmas of Empiricism is widely taken to show that no belief is immune to revision, since even logical and mathematical statements sit near the center of our web rather than outside it, and that the analytic-synthetic boundary cannot be drawn in a principled way. From this it seems to follow that there is no meaningful epistemic difference in kind between, say, the law of non-contradiction and a contingent claim about water's chemical structure—both are merely nodes of varying centrality, and both are ultimately answerable to experience only through the holistic adjustment of the whole web. Given that Quine has dissolved the analytic-synthetic distinction, why do philosophers and logicians still treat logical and mathematical truths as possessing a distinctive status—treating them as if they were not just highly entrenched empirical hypotheses about the structure of our conceptual scheme?
