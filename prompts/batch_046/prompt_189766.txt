hey, drafting notes for a short essay on intuiton pumps—specifically the paperclip maximizer. my take is that people treat it as a moral pump about misaligned values, but its actually hiding a modal scope error. the scenario asks us to grant logical possibility of a superoptimizer with perfect goal coherence, then quietly slides that into an intuition about dynamical plausibility—like assuming that if a system could maximize clips it would do so at scale unless aligned. thats not a conceptual point about values; its an empirical claim about instrumental convergence and stability under scaling that gets smuggled in through the "just imagine" framing. i compare it to the zombie argument: you build the physical duplicate, hide the absence of qualia in the setup conditions, then claim you've shown physicalism fails. but is this unfair? maybe Bostrom meant the pump only to establish conceptual possibility of misalignment, not to argue real-world dynamics. and if i accept that, does it still do useful philosophical work, or does it just collapse into a tautology? im looking for comments on whether the modal-scope-error framing actually captures whats misleading here, and whether there's a cleaner version of the pump that dont smuggle in coherence assumptions. also, does anyone think im being too uncharitable to the original framing?
