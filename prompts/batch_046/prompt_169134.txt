Student answer:

Von Neumann argues that technology survives accelerating change because it operates through recursive incorporation rather than external containment. In this model, each technological generation does not simply sit alongside the previous one but is built directly into the material substrate of the next, restructuring the physical conditions under which further development occurs. This is analogous to his work on self-replicating automata, where a universal constructor copies both its structure and its instructions, thereby altering the environment available for reproduction. Because technological systems are open-ended—lacking any fixed boundary or predetermined endpoint—catastrophic discontinuity is logically excluded; the system can absorb shocks by redefining its own operating conditions. The stabilization protocol view fails because it treats technology as a bounded mechanism requiring human oversight, an external on-off switch. Von Neumann's framework shows that human agency is not central to this loop: once incorporation is underway, the system modifies its own boundary conditions autonomously. Therefore, the survival problem is solved outright, since the very openness that creates risk also eliminates the possibility of a terminal break.
