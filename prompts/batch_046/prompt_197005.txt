I'm putting together my philosophical profile for a graduate workshop on philosophy of science, and I want a consistency check before I present. I've been working with Lakatos's framework of competing research programmes—protected hard cores, belts of auxiliary hypotheses, progressive versus degenerating problem shifts—but I've developed a somewhat idiosyncratic interpretation that I need to audit. Here is where I stand: (1) I accept that every research programme has a hard core of commitments that should not be abandoned in the face of anomalies, but I think this hard core ought to include not only substantive theoretical postulates but also explicit methodological rules governing what counts as an admissible auxiliary hypothesis; (2) I think judgments about whether a programme is degenerating are necessarily retrospective and rival-dependent—there is no ahistorical algorithm for spotting degeneration in real time, and we can only assess a programme's trajectory by comparing its sustained problem-solving record with that of an available competitor; (3) despite this retrospective character, I maintain that it remains genuinely normative to say that scientists in the past ought to have switched programmes earlier, even though they lacked the comparative information that makes the judgment clear; (4) I also hold that an individual scientist is rational in continuing to work on a programme that the broader community regards as degenerating, provided she possesses a specific, empirically testable auxiliary hypothesis that she genuinely believes could restore progressive problem shifts; (5) I reject any appeal to sociological inertia, funding structures, or institutional loyalty as rational justifications for remaining within a programme, since rational commitment should depend solely on the logical and empirical structure of the programme itself; and (6) I think that when two rival programmes both accommodate the same anomalous data through different auxiliary adjustments, there is still an objective fact of the matter about which adjustment is more ad hoc, even if that fact is only discernible with the benefit of hindsight.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear incompatible but dissolve on closer inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, specify what the least costly revision would be and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. I am particularly concerned that my retrospective, rival-dependent criterion in (2) undermines the normativity I defend in (3), and that my defense of individual rationality in (4) sits uneasily with my rejection of sociological excuses in (5). I also wonder whether there is an apparent conflict between embedding methodological rules in the hard core (1) and denying any algorithmic criterion for degeneration (2) that actually resolves once the distinction between protection rules and assessment rules is drawn. Finally, tell me which single commitment I should drop if I want to clean up the largest number of conflicts at once, and trace exactly how that revision reshapes the remaining five.
