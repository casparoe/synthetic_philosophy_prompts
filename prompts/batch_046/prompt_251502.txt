Write a two-page proposal for a twelve-month philosophy dissertation fellowship. The project examines an internal tension in tranquilism—the view that only the absence of craving and suffering carries value, while pleasure, satisfaction, or flourishing are axiologically neutral. Existing defenses treat suffering-elimination as an unalloyed good but rarely address what the proposal will call the satiation asymmetry: a being who permanently lacks both craving and suffering because every need is fully satisfied is classified by tranquilism as merely neutral, yet the mechanism producing this state—complete need-satiation—appears structurally different from a mere privation and seems to invite a positive evaluation that tranquilism explicitly denies. The precise question is whether tranquilism can consistently hold that a world of permanently satisfied, non-suffering beings is no better than an empty world, without implicitly smuggling in a positive value for satiation itself. Frame the question so it has a determinate answer; explain why treatments in Buddhist ethics, Schopenhauerian pessimism, and contemporary negative-utilitarian frameworks leave this gap open; propose a conceptual method—mapping the logical structure of craving-absence versus need-satisfaction across modal and counterfactual cases—to make progress; specify the deliverable (a defended taxonomy of tranquilist variants with the tension located precisely on each) and what would count as failure (demonstrating that the tension collapses into a verbal dispute about satisfaction-language); and include an honest scope assessment for one year. Do not include a bibliography, literature review by citation, or reading list; references will be added separately.
