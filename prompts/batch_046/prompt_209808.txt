I'm drafting a position paper for a graduate seminar on the ethics of blame, and before I defend it I want a consistency audit of the commitments I've staked out. Rather than treating standing to blame as a single on-off status, I'm decomposing it into three dimensions: epistemic standing (the blamer genuinely grasps the wrong and why it is wrong), moral standing (the blamer is not sufficiently implicated in the same or an analogous wrong), and expressive standing (the blamer's performance is not pragmatically self-defeating because it visibly manifests the very fault being condemned). Within this framework, I hold that institutional roles—judge, referee, ethics review officer—can confer epistemic and expressive standing independently of moral standing, so that a hypocritical official may still issue legitimate institutional verdicts even while lacking personal authority.

At the same time, I maintain that moral standing is the heart of interpersonal blame: without it, blame may be factually accurate and institutionally valid but is personally illegitimate in a way that generates a defeasible obligation for the blamer to withdraw or redirect it. I also think moral standing is scalar and can be partly restored through sustained amends and transparent acknowledgment of fault, yet I want to claim that some wrongs—specifically those involving betrayal of a fiduciary or protective duty—permanently forfeit moral standing within the corresponding domain of blame, regardless of reform. Finally, I hold that hypocrisy undermines expressive standing only when the blaming act is structured so that the blamer's own comparable conduct is visible and relevant, which implies that private, anonymized, or structurally mediated blame might retain full expressive standing even when the blamer's moral standing is zero.

Please audit this set for joint tenability. Rank any genuine tensions by severity, indicate which conflicts only appear serious but dissolve once the three dimensions are kept separate, and identify the least costly revision if I must weaken or drop one commitment. I am especially unsure whether my view about institutional roles is compatible with my claim that moral standing is central to interpersonal legitimacy, and whether my scalar-recovery view conflicts with my claim about permanent forfeiture for fiduciary betrayal. Please respond in American English, and keep the focus tightly on whether my commitments hang together rather than delivering a general lecture on blame.
