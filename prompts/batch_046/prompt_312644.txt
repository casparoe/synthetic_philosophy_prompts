I write about science and ideas for a living — usually with some data in the mix — and I'm drafting a feature on why some cultures cheer for winners while others cut them down. I need a classroom activity I can actually describe running, not just theorize about.

Here's the brief: about 18 undergrad students, 75 minutes, no prep reading. Design a structured game or role-play that makes them feel the tension between the American Dream narrative (success should be spotlighted and rewarded) and Jantelagen / tall poppy syndrome (success should be suppressed for the collective good). They shouldn't hear either term until the debrief.

Set it up as a collective task — maybe building a shared resource pool — where one participant is clearly outperforming the others. Use points, tokens, or social-credit (no real money) arranged so that if the group rewards her, cooperation fractures and total payoff drops; if they suppress her, the collective output also suffers. Give me the rules, any roles, the timeline, and the exact moment where they should hit the wall themselves.

Then the debrief: map what happened onto both norms, say what will predictibly go wrong the first time (probably someone breaks character or refuses to cut?), and be frank about which feature of the cultural tension this can't reproduce — I'm guessing the internalized shame versus an explicit social rule — since I'll supply that in the article.

One more thing: don't frame the cutting-down as simple envy. Inject the idea that it's a collective risk-hedging strategy against comparison costs, so the prompt isn't just recycling the same culture-war talking point. And please, no bibliographies or reading lists — this needs to work for a model that can't search the web. Also, they recieve tokens, not cash.
