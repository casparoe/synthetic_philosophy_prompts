Following a long and rather exhausting argument with a close friend about whether personal conviction should ever yield to statistical base rates, I have been trying to articulate exactly where my discomfort lies: not with the outside view itself, but with the fact that applying it seems to alter the very reference class on which it depends. I would like to design a seminar exercise that makes this instability tangible for students, rather than allowing them to treat the inside-outside distinction as a tidy taxonomy.

The setting is an upper-level philosophy seminar of approximately fourteen students, with ninety minutes available. I want a structured, runnable activity—complete with assigned roles, explicit rules, and a timed sequence—centered on a concrete deliberation: a small municipality deciding whether to implement an untested but locally tailored public-health intervention. The exercise should unfold in three phases. In the first phase, one group must build an inside-view case for why this municipality is genuinely exceptional, while the other group constructs an outside-view reference class of analogous interventions. In the second phase, without prior warning, the groups exchange frameworks and must defend the opposite conclusion using only the evidence and reasoning they themselves generated in the first phase. The rules should prohibit falling back on well-worn illustrations—such as the planning fallacy or generic marital-statistics examples—and should require participants to work with the specific fictional case provided.

Could you design this activity? Specifically, please include: a precise timeline; the rules and role assignments; a payoff or incentive structure that does not involve real money but makes the reasoning stakes feel real; a debrief that maps each phase onto the conceptual problem of epistemic modesty and reference-class dissolution; an honest assessment of which feature of the inside-view/outside-view tension the activity cannot reproduce; and a note on what will predictably fail the first time it is run and how to manage it. Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. I am interested only in the structure of the exercise and the conceptual mapping.
