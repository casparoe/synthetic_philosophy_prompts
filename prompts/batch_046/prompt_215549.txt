I serve on the International Consortium’s Ethics and Funding Oversight Committee, and we must vote next month on whether to renew a three-year community-health grant operating in a region where access to supplies, labor, and security runs through long-standing personalized obligation networks—what local partners call “systemic reciprocity” and our compliance office labels “clientelism.” The local implementing partners have proposed an “accompanied reciprocity” pilot: all exchanges above a minimal threshold would flow through a collectively governed, transparent pool with published ledgers, but they insist that refusing any exchange at all severs survival networks and effectively excludes the poorest households from care. Our current anti-corruption policy applies a universal prohibition—any gift or favor that could generate private obligation is barred, regardless of context or transparency mechanism.

Please draft the formal written opinion—no more than four pages—that I will circulate before the vote. Recommend a concrete course of action with a specific time frame (for example, conditional renewal with an 18-month pilot, suspension pending structural reform, or termination with transition funding). In your reasoning, take seriously the argument that universal prohibition in this setting is not ethical neutrality but structural violence—specifically, the claim that Western standards conflate “systemic reciprocity” (reversible, collectively governed, and necessary for basic security) with “extractive corruption” (asymmetric, hidden, and individually enriching). At the same time, honestly address the strongest risk of accommodating: that transparency mechanisms can be captured by local elites, and that legitimizing exchange may gradually erode institutional accountability and reproduce hidden dependency.

Structure your response exactly as follows: 1) Executive Summary (approx. 150 words); 2) The Institutional Stakes and the Conceptual Distinction (approx. 250 words); 3) The Case for Maintaining Universal Prohibition (approx. 250 words); 4) The Case for Contextual Accommodation with Safeguards (approx. 250 words); 5) Recommendation and Implementation Timeline (approx. 200 words). Give a committed verdict—do not lay out balanced options and leave the choice to me; defend one course of action and explain why the losing consideration, though weighty, does not prevail.
