Write a dialogue consisting of exactly five numbered exchanges, with no introduction or conclusion, between two philosophers of equal competence. The first defends a modified version of Judith Thomson's violinist analogy applied to cases where separation from a dependent being is not instantaneous but requires a prolonged, risky procedure that threatens both parties' bodily integrity, arguing that autonomy still permits refusal even when the cost of separation is shared. The second defends the future-like-ours argument but restricts it to entities with a "determinate future"—a continuous psychological trajectory—claiming that without an established neural substrate for personal identity there is no specific future to be wronged by destruction, though this leaves open the moral status of late fetuses. Each speaker must directly address the other’s argument from the preceding exchange rather than talking past them, and each exchange should be brief: one or two sentences per speaker.
