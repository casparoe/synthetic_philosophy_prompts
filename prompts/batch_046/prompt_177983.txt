After my cousin was recently remanded, I have been trying to determine whether the American prison system and Norway’s Halden model are even responsive to the same moral concept of punishment. Please argue for the position that they are not—that American mass incarceration, particularly through prolonged solitary confinement and the structural denial of educational and vocational agency, transforms punishment from a communicative deprivation of liberty into an illegitimate degradation of moral status, whereas Halden’s architecture of normalized responsibility preserves the very conditions required for retributive justice to be coherent. Your argument should take seriously the communicative theory of punishment and show why a retributivist committed to proportionality and respect for persons should reject conditions that render the offender incapable of recognizing the censure being expressed. Address specifically the objection that treating prison conditions as relevant to justice conflates punishment with rehabilitation: defend the claim that maintaining an offender’s autonomous capacities is not an instrumental concession to rehabilitation but a constitutive requirement of just punishment itself. Write formally, without asking for sources, bibliographies, or specific texts, and assume I am familiar with the basic institutional contrast between the two systems.
