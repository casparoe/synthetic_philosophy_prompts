My neighbour's been going on about this thing called epistemic injustice after she watched some lectures online. Her summary was roughly this: it's when you're wronged specifically as someone who knows things, not just generally insulted. She said there's two branches — testimonial, where people dont give you enough credit cos of prejudice about your group, like a woman being ignored in a meeting; and hermeneutical, where you literally cant understand your own experience because society hasn't invented the concept yet, like with post-natal depression before people named it. She said Miranda Fricker's big idea is that both damage your capacity as a knower and that the harm is special cos it hits your intellectual dignity. Then she said this applies now to search algorithms and AI chatbots, because if the training data mostly comes from Western universities then local or working-class knowledge gets treated as unreliable automatically, which she called structural testimonial injustice by machine. She also said Fricker thinks this only happens when there's a clear power gap, like doctor over patient or teacher over student, so peer-to-peer disbelief doesnt count.

I think she's mixing a few things up but I cant tell which. Is the AI/algorithm bit a fair extension of Fricker, or is she just bolting tech onto an old idea? And did Fricker really say you need an authority figure for testimonial injustice, or can it happen between equals? Also with the hermeneutical one — is it that the words are missing from society generally, or that I personally dont know them? And does the intellectual dignity part mean it's worse than other insults, or just different?
