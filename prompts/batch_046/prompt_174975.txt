Draft the joint statement that the Cognitive-Judgment Camp and the Embodied-Perceptual Camp could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the nature of emotions. The Cognitive-Judgment Camp holds that emotions are essentially evaluative judgments or propositional attitudes directed at states of affairs, with bodily feelings being contingent accompaniments rather than constitutive parts. The Embodied-Perceptual Camp holds that emotions are primarily non-cognitive, embodied perceptions or affective feelings that present the world as having certain values or saliences, such that conceptual evaluation is neither necessary nor always present.

Your statement should contain:
1. A numbered section of agreed claims—propositions about emotion that both camps accept, including shared descriptions of at least two specific phenomena (e.g., emotional recalcitrance in a spider-phobic who intellectually judges safety, and the experience of a sudden affective shift in response to an unanticipated event).
2. A disagreements table with the columns: Issue | Judgment Camp Formulation | Perceptual Camp Formulation | Is this a crux? (Yes/No). Each formulation must be phrased so that its own camp would accept the wording. Separate terminological disagreements from substantive ones.
3. A cruxes section listing the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a case, argument, or empirical result both sides agree would bear on it, and record in advance each camp's predicted outcome. Include at least one crux concerning "affective framing"—defined here as the capacity of an emotional state to render certain features of a situation salient without representing them in fully propositional form—and whether such framing is reducible to judgmental content.
4. A brief concluding paragraph in which both sides jointly acknowledge the limits of current conceptual resources for resolving the cruxes.
