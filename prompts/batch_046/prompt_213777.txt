I've been asked to open the new display at our community centre — it's about the factory closure in the 80s, but they've done something odd: alongside the tapes there's an empty shelf for every family that asked to be left out, including one for my mum (she died in januray). She left instrucions that her interview tape be destoyed after ten years, not kept forever. The room opens saturday; about thirty people will be there, half local historians and archivests, half neighbours and her old friends. I want to talk about whether remembering is always a duty, and whether her choice to disappear is actually part of the story the archive tells, not just a hole in it. The idea needs to do real work: I can't just praise preservation, and I can't just say "let's forget" either. Can you write me a speech, max 300 words, that treats her absence as part of the record rather than a failure? No lists of books or "see Margalit" — I wouldn't know where to start with that stuff, and it feels wrong for the room anyway. My head's a bit scattered since the funneral, so please keep it human and don't make me sound like a lecturer. Use British spelling.
