Write me a parody of one of those deadpan council leaflets they shove through your door — maybe 600 words, in the voice of the Department for Rational Belief — announcing a pilot scheme called the Dutch Book Audit. The leaflet explains, with a completely straight face, how inspectors visit households to check whether residents' beliefs about everyday stuff (will it rain, is the bus late, can you trust there neighbour) are probabilisticaly coherent. If your credences violate the axioms, they construct a combination of bets that guarantees you lose money, then fine you. The satire should push the Dutch-book argument right to the wall: coherence enforced by financial penalty, refusal to bet treated as suspicious, appeals handled by 're-calibration workshops' where you declare your priors on Form DB-7. Land the real philosophical objections — that betting behaviour isn't the same as belief, that risk-averse people rationally refuse bets, and that being Dutch-bookable doesn't automatically mean you're irrational — but only by showing them being ignored or misinterpreted by the bureaucracy. Include a sidebar about the national holiday honouring the scheme's founder, and instruct that the leaflet itself contain a couple of small typos like 'cohearence' or 'probabilty'. Keep it deadpan British bureaucrat-ese. Don't ask for citations, reading lists, or named authors — just write it so someone who knows the argument sees the exaggeration is fair.
