As a journalist who writes about the history of scientific ideas and their afterlives in public policy, I have been thinking about how Albert Einstein's political writings might speak to debates that emerged long after his death. In a 1949 essay published in the first issue of Monthly Review, Einstein argued that the economic anarchy of unregulated capitalism leads to cyclical depressions, the corruption of democratic institutions by concentrated private capital, and the crippling of individuals through an education system oriented solely toward career advancement rather than social cooperation. He proposed a planned economy in which the means of production are socially owned and production is adjusted to community needs, but he added a sharp caveat: such an economy is not yet socialism, because without strong democratic safeguards it risks producing an all-powerful bureaucracy that results in complete enslavement of the individual.

Today, a new generation of economists and computer scientists has begun exploring whether large-scale algorithmic forecasting and machine-learning-based resource allocation could overcome the classic socialist calculation problem—the difficulty of centrally coordinating production without market price signals. Some proponents suggest that AI-driven planning could realize Einstein's vision of distributing labor and guaranteeing livelihoods without replicating the rigid bureaucratic control he feared, since algorithms might adapt dynamically rather than through a hierarchical state apparatus. Given Einstein's specific warnings about bureaucracy, his insistence that any planned system must include a democratic counterweight, and his belief that the educational system should cultivate a sense of responsibility for fellow human beings, how do you think he would evaluate these contemporary proposals for algorithmic central planning? Would he regard them as resolving the economic anarchy he condemned, or as creating precisely the kind of opaque, technocratic domination that he believed threatened individual rights?
