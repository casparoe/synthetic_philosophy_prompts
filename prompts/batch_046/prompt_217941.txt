Restate the expanding-circle thesis in moral philosophy — the view that the boundary of beings worthy of moral consideration gradually widens from the self and immediate kin outward to strangers, other species, and future generations, driven not merely by biological altruism but by rational reflection and empathetic imagination — using only the conceptual vocabulary of epidemiological diffusion and network cascade dynamics. Employ the specific machinery: reproductive number (R0), superspreader events, herd-immunity thresholds, containment boundaries, asymptomatic transmission, and targeted intervention strategies analogous to vaccination. Map each stage of moral expansion onto this epidemiological framework — identifying the initial cluster, the vector of transmission, the resistance or immunity of host populations, the conditions for endemic stability, and the possibility of containment or reversal — and note precisely where the analogy demands distortion.

Then deliver a residue report: which normative elements of the thesis — for instance, that agents have a positive obligation to widen the circle, that failure to do so is a moral defect rather than mere neutrality, or that universal consideration is the rational endpoint — survive intact in diffusion terms; which can be rendered only by fudging the epidemiological concepts (e.g., treating moral reasoning as an infection vector); and which have no counterpart whatsoever in the vocabulary of contagion and network spread. Conclude by assessing whether the untranslated residue reveals a genuine deficit in the epidemiological framework — perhaps its inability to accommodate reflective endorsement, justification, or agential choice — or whether it exposes hidden metaphysical commitments in the original thesis, such as the assumption that moral progress is an inevitable or naturally directional process rather than a contested achievement. Maintain strict discipline: do not allow the diffusion model to import deterministic or value-neutral assumptions that the moral view rejects, and do not force the moral thesis to speak in terms it cannot consistently accept.
