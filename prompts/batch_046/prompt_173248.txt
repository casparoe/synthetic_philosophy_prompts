I serve on my city's Public Safety Review Panel. Next month we vote on a pilot that removes police entirely from mental-health crisis calls, assigning them to an unarmed municipal Crisis Corps with no arrest powers; police may enter only if summoned by the Corps, never by direct public call. Proponents argue this refines, rather than fractures, the state's Weberian monopoly on legitimate violence by separating coercive from non-coercive authority. Opponents argue it creates a parallel jurisdiction that undermines the exclusivity of that monopoly and risks private or ad hoc enforcement filling the gap. Draft the panel's decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, including a concrete timeline or sunset clause. The memo should treat the strongest objection—whether about the conceptual coherence of a divided monopoly or about practical safety risks—with genuine seriousness rather than dismissing it, and it should close with an explicit statement of how confident the recommendation is, singling out the specific point about which the panel should be least certain.
