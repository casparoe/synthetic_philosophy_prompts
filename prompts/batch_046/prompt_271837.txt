I am preparing a handout for a philosophy of mind seminar that examines whether the apparent rigidity of instinctive behaviour undermines claims about human free will. Rather than asking students to name the philosophers they agree with, I want a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions according to the answers one gives to concrete, case-based questions about the digger wasp Sphex ichneumoneus — specifically its tendency to repeat an inspection-and-drag sequence even when the environment is perturbed, for example if the paralysed prey is relocated after the burrow check. The instrument should be constructed from the logical space of distinctions rather than from a roster of famous names, so that a respondent can navigate it without knowing in advance which label awaits at each leaf.

Please design the tree with the following structure and constraints. The root question should ask whether the wasp's repetition in the face of perturbation demonstrates an absence of any behavioural elasticity or instead reveals a more complex but still rigid loop. The next tier must split on whether any observed elasticity requires minimal representational content — a kind of world-directed belief about the prey's location — or can be accounted for by purely reactive, non-representational dynamics. From there, branch according to whether the human capacity that breaks sphexishness is best understood as (a) counterfactual reasoning about one's own actions, (b) a reflective veto that can suspend an initiated motor programme, or (c) open-ended exploration that is not bound to a fixed goal. Each terminal node should carry a position name — such as 'hard mechanism', 'procedural-loop flexibility', 'minimal-belief adaptability', 'counterfactual compatibilism', 'reflective-veto libertarianism', or 'exploratory agency' — or be marked 'unnamed' if the combination is unoccupied, together with one sentence diagnosing the emptiness (whether incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored). You must include one deliberately injected intermediate position that is not standard in the literature: the view that free will does not escape mechanistic loops entirely but consists in a second-order monitoring system that can selectively reinforce or suppress particular loops without originating them de novo; locate this precisely within the tree. Additionally, flag any pair of leaves that your taxonomy cannot genuinely separate, limit the tree to no more than eight leaves, and write everything in British English. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not ask the respondent to identify specific authors or texts.
