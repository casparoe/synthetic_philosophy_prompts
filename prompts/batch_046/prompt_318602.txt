I'm drafting my position for a metaethics seminar on whether being moral is rationally required, using Glaucon's challenge and the ring of Gyges as the framing devices. Before I present, I'd like a consistency audit of the following commitments. Please respond in British English, and do not supply a bibliography or reading list — I will add citations myself.

My commitments are these:

(1) I take Glaucon's challenge to establish that conventional justice is essentially a social compromise sustained by the expectation of being observed; the ring of Gyges reveals this structure by granting observational invisibility, though I distinguish this sharply from normative invisibility — the idea that moral norms could still apply to an agent even when no observer is present.

(2) I am a moral realist about justice: there are mind-independent moral facts that determine what agents ought to do, irrespective of social recognition or enforcement.

(3) I think the best answer to Glaucon is broadly Platonic: justice is intrinsically good for the agent's soul or character, so it remains rationally required even under complete observational invisibility.

(4) At the same time, I hold that if justice made literally no internal difference — if it neither affected the agent's psychological coherence, character, nor flourishing in any way, thereby lacking even normative invisibility in the relevant sense — then it would not be rationally binding on the agent.

(5) I accept evolutionary debunking arguments that our pre-theoretical moral intuitions are unreliable guides to moral truth, yet I maintain that reflective equilibrium remains the correct method, provided it is disciplined by rationalist moral theory rather than by untutored intuition.

(6) I deny that morality is merely instrumentally valuable, but I maintain that its rational authority partly depends on its being constitutive of, rather than merely productive of, the agent's flourishing.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision — if I had to make only one — would clean up the most inconsistencies across the set. Be specific about whether my distinction between observational and normative invisibility in (1) actually does any resolving work, or whether it collapses under pressure from (3) and (4). Do not lecture me on metaethics in general; treat this as an audit of my specific package.
