I am a retired experimental physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has worked through the literature in cultural psychology and normative ethics. Next month I present to an interdisciplinary seminar—jointly hosted by a cultural psychology research group and a philosophy of science program—defending the thesis that societies should reject both uniform tightness and uniform looseness in favor of what I term domain-differentiated normative density: procedural and predictive domains (scientific practice, engineering safety, public-health protocol) should maintain high rule-following density, while interpretive and existential domains (moral self-cultivation, aesthetic judgment, religious meaning-making) should maintain low density, governed by an explicit normative membrane that prevents leakage between the two zones. I present this not as a rejection of the tight-loose framework but as a vector refinement: culture is not a single scalar parameter but a compartmentalized structure, and societies that fail are usually those that allow procedural strictness to colonize existential life or, conversely, allow existential pluralism to erode predictive reliability.

The audience comprises cultural psychologists who have conducted large-scale cross-national studies and treat cultural uniformity as an adaptive response to ecological threat; philosophers of science who regard social norms as instruments for collective epistemic reliability; and an anthropologist of ritual who holds that ritual density serves integrative social functions that cannot be partitioned without destroying cohesion. They are rigorous but not hostile; they care about empirical generality and are suspicious of normative architectures that resemble engineering blueprints imposed on culture.

Prepare me for the objections I will actually receive, ordered by likelihood and damage. For each: phrase it as that specific interlocutor would phrase it; indicate whether it carries real force and what I should concede up front versus what I must hold; provide a reply deliverable in two to three minutes; and note the weakest point in that reply. Be explicit about which objection I am hoping nobody asks—namely, the charge that my normative membrane is either empirically vacant (no actual society maintains such a boundary) or normatively authoritarian (it requires a policing apparatus that destroys the organic spontaneity it claims to protect). In your response, please state your confidence in each claim and identify explicitly the point about which you are least certain.
