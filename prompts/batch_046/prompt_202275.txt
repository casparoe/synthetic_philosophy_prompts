I am a PhD student in philosophy of language consulting for a participatory digital archive whose staff must repeatedly classify user-submitted descriptions of hybrid cultural practices—ephemeral digital rituals, AI-generated reconstructions of destroyed objects, and crowdsourced annotations—that do not map onto standard curatorial taxonomies. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Wittgenstein’s ideas of family resemblance and language games, that an archivist without philosophical training can run in about fifteen minutes per submission. Meaning and category membership should be treated as matters of use within a form of life, held together by clusters of overlapping similarities rather than by necessary and sufficient conditions. The deliverable should be a step-by-step checklist or flowchart in words, with the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the procedure terminates with a clear classification, exclusion, or referral to an existing family, and where it must stop and defer to human judgment because the issue concerns values, cultural stakes, or practical purpose rather than descriptive fit. It must explicitly flag misfires—especially cases where the procedure forces assimilation into or exclusion from a category in ways that will be ethically or culturally unacceptable to the submitting community—and state what the procedure cannot resolve. Then apply it to three submissions: (a) an augmented-reality memorial tag submitted by a community group that has no institutional record of the commemorated event; (b) an AI-reconstructed image of a destroyed ceremonial object submitted under the category “document”; and (c) a collaborative annotation layer that applies technical vocabulary from one disciplinary language game to describe practices rooted in another form of life. At least one application should yield a result that conflicts with intuitive or ethical judgment; specify exactly where the procedure produces that result and why the tension cannot be eliminated by further procedural refinement.
