I just finished a long argument with a friend about whether the discursive dilemma is genuinely confined to collective bodies, and I still cannot locate the exit in the reasoning. Here is the chain he pressed on me.

(1) The discursive dilemma demonstrates that no aggregation rule can jointly satisfy universal domain, anonymity, and systematicity for a group that judges premises separately and then applies logical inference to reach conclusions.

(2) Now adopt a modular model of cognition: an individual agent comprises distinct sub-personal systems—perceptual, mnemonic, affective, deliberative—each of which generates judgments on premises, while some central mechanism integrates them into an overall doxastic state.

(3) Apply the same three conditions intra-psychically. Universal domain requires that every module’s judgment be admissible; anonymity requires that no module carry dictatorial weight; systematicity requires that the agent’s belief in a conclusion depend consistently on her beliefs in the premises that entail it.

(4) By the same formal result, no modular agent can satisfy all three conditions simultaneously.

(5) But rationality, at minimum, demands consistency and completeness—precisely the content of systematicity applied to belief sets.

(6) Therefore, if human cognition is modular, then no human being can be fully rational.

(7) My friend concedes that one might reject anonymity within the self—granting reflective reasoning veto power over other modules—but he insists this only replaces democratic irrationality with authoritarian rationality, undermining the normative idea that rational belief should be responsive to all of one’s reasons rather than to the will of a dominant subsystem.

I want to preserve (2), since the modular analogy genuinely illuminates how belief formation works, and I am inclined to reject (6) rather than abandon the norm of rationality. Where exactly does the argument slip? Is there an illegitimate equivocation between democratic voting rules and intra-psychic integration? Or does the error lie in assuming that consistency and completeness apply globally across modules rather than locally within them? If I reject anonymity at the personal level, what normative cost does that exact?
