I just had a long argument with a friend about whether 'the Middle Ages', 'the Renaissance', and the 'Scientific Revolution' are real historical joints or merely retrospective inventions we use to organize syllabi. I want to turn this into an exam for an upper-level history of philosophy of science seminar. Write one essay question that students could answer in about 75 minutes, designed so it cannot be satisfied by reciting standard period labels. To keep it in the weeds, frame the prompt around this niche hypothetical: a revisionist historian proposes abolishing 'Renaissance' in favor of 'the Italian Humanist Recovery (c. 1350–1520)', splitting 'Scientific Revolution' into 'the Mathematical Natural Philosophy Movement (c. 1540–1640)' and 'the Experimental Institutional Turn (c. 1660–1720)', and retaining 'Middle Ages' only as 'the University Episteme (c. 1050–1350)'. The question should require students to evaluate whether such granular re-labeling solves the invention problem or just relocates it, and whether any non-arbitrary criterion—such as a discontinuity in shared problem-sets, a shift in institutional patronage structures, or a change in what counts as authoritative evidence—could justify keeping broader categories. Provide a model answer of roughly 600 words, together with a marking scheme built around five must-hit points, the marks each carries (total 20), and a one-line note on how candidates typically miss each point. Keep the entire deliverable—question, model answer, and marking scheme—within 850 words. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
