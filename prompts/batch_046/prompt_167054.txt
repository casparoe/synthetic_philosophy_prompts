In a seminar on the origins of Nazi Germany, a classmate kept using the phrase “remove the actor and the script collapses” to counter structural explanations. She meant that without Hitler the specific regime could not have taken the shape it did, but interlocutors kept interpreting her as saying that Weimar’s economic crisis, institutional decay, and popular resentment played no causal role whatsoever. Can you separate the precise claims that line could express? Lay out the distinct theses as a menu: for example, the counterfactual claim that no other figure could have seized power; the claim that structural conditions were necessary but insufficient; the claim that Hitler was uniquely necessary for every major feature of the regime; and the tautological claim that a different leader would have produced a different history. For each, say whether it is a platitude, a defensible historical judgment, or false, and indicate which version she actually needs to rebut the argument that Weimar’s conditions made some authoritarian outcome highly probable regardless of personality. Finally, clarify whether her slogan, on its strongest reading, denies that structural forces shaped Hitler’s options, or only denies that those forces alone determine which options he chose.
