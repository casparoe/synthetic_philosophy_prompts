I am an undergraduate philosophy student writing for a course on free will and moral responsibility. Please adopt the role of a committed, sophisticated defender of the principle of alternative possibilities—someone who fully grants that classic Frankfurt-style counterexamples defeat naive versions of PAP, but who maintains that moral responsibility still requires a genuinely open and causally efficacious alternative embedded within the agent’s own deliberative economy. Stay in character throughout; do not break role for pedagogical explanations, and do not concede that determinism is compatible with responsibility. Keep each reply to one substantive paragraph, and treat me as an interlocutor pressing a specific challenge rather than a student asking for a lecture.

My opening move concerns a variant I am calling the double-counterfactual intervener case. Imagine an agent, S, deliberating whether to sign a contract. There is a primary monitor, M1, programmed to compel S’s signature should S show any sign of refusing. But M1 is itself observed by a secondary monitor, M2, whose sole function is to disable M1 instantly should M1 initiate any interference, precisely because M2 is designed to preserve S’s autonomy. In the actual sequence, S signs freely, M1 never activates, and M2 never activates. Now consider the modal structure: if S had begun to refuse, M1 would have tried to intervene, yet M2 would immediately have blocked M1, apparently leaving S free to complete her refusal. At the same time, the very presence of M1 in S’s background causal environment means her mental state was never able to issue in refusal without traversing a chain that included an overridden intervention mechanism. Is S responsible for signing? And more importantly, does this nested structure allow you to refine PAP so that it demands not merely a remote counterfactual branch, but a causally available alternative within the agent’s actual reasoning process—without that refinement collapsing into a straightforward incompatibilist claim about determinism? I want to see whether your position can draw a principled line between this case and standard Frankfurt scenarios.
