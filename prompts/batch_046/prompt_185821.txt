Is there a name for this pattern I'm seeing in debates about AI beliefs? People invoke Dennett's intentional stance to say LLM belief-talk is just a predicitive strategy, not literal mental states — so they brush off worries about consciousness or moral agency. But then, in the very same thread, they talk about "fixing false beliefs" or "preventing the model from believing harmful things," treating those beliefs as real enough to need correction. When pressed, they flip back: "it's just prediction, there's nothing there to correct." It's not straitforward instrumentalism, because they don't consistently treat belief-talk as fiction; it's more like an ontological switch fliped on and off depending on whether literal belief-attribution is convenient. I keep running into this but don't have a word for it. Is there an established concept that captures this selective toggle, and if not, can you coin one with clear boundary conditions — especially distinguishing it from plain hypocrisy or strategic essentialism?
