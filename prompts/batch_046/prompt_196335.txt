I've been thinking a lot about Sartre's thing with radical freedom and bad faith - you know, how we're always free to choose and bad faith is just lying to ourselves that we're not. Lately I've been applying it to people who blame algorithms for their choices, like "the feed made me buy this" or "I couldn't help scrolling." To me that seem's like classic bad faith, treating yourself like a thing (being-in-itself) pushed around by other things. I even came up with a twist: maybe there's "distributed bad faith" where a whole group uses the same tech excuse to collectively deny freedom. I don't think Sartre talks about that specifically, but it feels like it could fit it's ontology somehow.

So my position is that Sartre's radical freedom still holds, and these modern algorithmic excuses are mostly just updated bad faith, including the group version. What would count against this? Not the standard neuroscience determinism stuff - I want the specific things that should make me doubt that blaming tech is self-deception. Like, is there a real line between influence and determination I'm missing? Could blaming an algorithm ever be honest rather than bad faith? And does my "distributed bad faith" idea actually break something in Sartre's framework, or is it just awkward? I'd like an honest ranking from fatal to merely awkward, plus what a real Sartre fan would concede versus dismiss.
