Write a one-page workshop-grant proposal (three months, conceptual project) on a question in Strawson’s philosophy of moral responsibility. The precise question: Strawson treats reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation—as natural human responses whose appropriateness does not hinge on the metaphysical truth of determinism. Yet he says little about whether these attitudes can be rationally sustained when directed at agents whose contribution to a harm is real but distributed across so many participants that no individual action is perceptibly causally efficacious—think of a single emitter in a global carbon chain or one buyer in a vast supply network. Does applying Strawson’s framework to such “sub-threshold” collective actions force us to treat reactive attitudes as either non-additive, so they cannot be divided among millions of contributors without losing their character, or as linearly scalable, so each contributor receives a proportionally diluted attitude that falls below the threshold for genuine moral address? Frame the proposal by stating exactly what hangs on the answer: if reactive attitudes are non-additive, then Strawson’s naturalism implies that large-scale collective harms lie outside the domain of interpersonal moral address altogether; if they are scalable, then resentment can be rationally directed at each contributor but only in a form so weak it no longer functions as the “addressive” response Strawson describes. Explain why the standard compatibilism versus incompatibilism debate and the existing literature on collective responsibility leave this structural feature untouched—those debates ask whether groups can be agents or whether determinism undermines blame, not whether the internal architecture of reactive attitudes permits division without dissolution. Propose a conceptual method: analyze the conditions under which resentment retains its second-personal, addressive structure when its object is one among an enormous set, comparing this to cases where we suspend resentment not because the agent is excused but because the causal link is too diffuse to sustain the attitude’s directedness. Say what a successful result would look like—either a principled boundary for Strawson’s framework or a revised account of how reactive attitudes aggregate—and what would count as failure, for instance if the distinction between non-additive and scalable turns out to be merely verbal. Give an honest scope assessment: can this be settled in three months of conceptual analysis, or does it require empirical work on moral psychology that the grant should not promise? Do not request a bibliography, reading list, or citations; refer to positions and arguments by name only, and indicate that sources will be filled in later.
