He used this to argue that Confucian ethics is just "local optimization without global coherence" — like, it might work in small clusters but can't handle large-scale coordination because there's no universal moral law running on every node.

Can you walk through how far that analogy actually holds? Then where does it break: does the computing analogy smuggle in assumptions about information flow, autonomy, or failure modes that don't apply to human relationships? And crucially, if we accept the analogy, does it actually license the "local optimization is a bug" conclusion, or does Confucian role ethics have some hidden global coherence (maybe through nested roles or the idea of harmony) that the distributed-system picture just misses?
