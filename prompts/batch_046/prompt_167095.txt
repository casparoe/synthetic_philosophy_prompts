Here is my current profile on original versus derived intentionality for a workshop I am running, and I want a consistency audit before I present it.

(1) I take Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality to be conceptually sound: biological minds possess intrinsic aboutness, while artifacts, texts, and simulations possess only a genuinely different, borrowed kind.

(2) I accept Dennett's two-bitser point: if two behaviorally identical systems differ only in substrate, there is no non-arbitrary reason to attribute original intentionality to one and deny it to the other; behavioral detectability is all we have to work with.

(3) I hold that derived intentionality is not mere fiction but a real relational property—legal contracts, for instance, bind in virtue of sustained interpretive practices, not in virtue of anyone's current mental state.

(4) I think that when a system of derived representations enters what I call an autonomous derivation loop—where interpretive practices become causally self-sustaining without reference to an original designer or user—it acquires a proto-original intentionality that is neither fully intrinsic nor fully borrowed.

(5) I reject the Chinese Room argument's conclusion that syntax can never contribute to semantics, though I agree with Searle that syntax alone is never sufficient for semantics.

Please rank the tensions among these commitments by severity. For each genuine tension, specify which commitment must be revised and what the least costly fix is; for each tension that only appears on the surface, show why it dissolves. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
