I sit on my high school’s Student Academic Policy Board, and we need a formal decision memo on the proposed “Reflective Calibration Mandate.” Under this exam protocol, every student response must include a second-order confidence rating—an explicit judgment about how certain they are that they genuinely understand the answer, not merely that they selected it—with grading weights partly tied to that meta-assessment. Proponents argue this rewards justified belief and discourages blind guessing; opponents contend it institutionally demands a form of second-order self-knowledge that may be impossible, since mental states are not luminous: knowing a fact does not necessarily put one in a position to know that one knows it, especially across gradual shifts in understanding, and requiring KK-style access unfairly penalizes students who lack it. The board is split between treating the mandate as healthy metacognitive training and rejecting it as an epistemically unrealistic burden. Please draft the board’s memo—recommending approval, rejection, or conditional approval—with a clear actionable bottom line, reasoning the administration could actually respond to, and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Write in American English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
