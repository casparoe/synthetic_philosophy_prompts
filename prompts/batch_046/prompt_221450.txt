I'm writing this up for a seminar in distributive justice and have constructed an argument that appears to show prioritarianism collapses into the very leveling-down objection it was designed to avoid. The steps are as follows. (1) Prioritarianism assigns moral weight to benefits solely according to the recipient's absolute level of well-being, with lower levels receiving strictly greater weight. (2) This weighting is standardly modelled by a concave transformation of well-being—marginal moral value declines as well-being rises. (3) For any fixed total of well-being distributed across a population, concavity entails that the sum of weighted well-being is maximised by distributing that total as equally as possible. (4) Hence prioritarianism ranks perfectly equal distributions highest among those with the same aggregate. (5) But equality can sometimes be achieved only by destroying surplus resources held by the well-off, lowering the aggregate total; since the well-off remain far above the worst-off, the weight attached to their loss is negligible, so the equalised but lower-total distribution still outranks the unequal but richer one. (6) Therefore prioritarianism not only permits but actively favours leveling down—making everyone equally badly off—whenever that is the route to equality. (7) Yet prioritarianism was introduced precisely to reject the egalitarian claim that equality has intrinsic value and to avoid licensing such downward leveling. (8) So prioritarianism is either self-defeating or one of the premises must fail.

If I am forced to choose, I would prefer to keep (1) and (7), but I want to see precisely what rejecting another step costs me, especially whether it forces me to abandon concavity or to accept that prioritarian evaluation applies only within a fixed pie and never justifies reducing that pie. I have also been wondering whether the error lies in treating prioritarian weights as globally aggregative rather than as operating within a contractualist or institutional scope—though I am unsure whether that distinction saves the view or merely relocates the problem.
