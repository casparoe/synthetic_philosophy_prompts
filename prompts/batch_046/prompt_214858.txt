Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two interpretive camps on Kant’s essay on perpetual peace. The first camp, the Institutional Republicans, holds that Kant’s federation of free states is a binding juridical structure with progressive coercive potential, that the “negative substitute” for a world republic is a genuinely normative institutional form rather than a pragmatic concession, and that cosmopolitan hospitality creates enforceable constraints on how republics treat outsiders. The second camp, the Sovereignty Preservationists, holds that the federation is a purely voluntary association retaining full sovereignty and the right of exit, that Kant’s rejection of a world state is absolute and not provisional, and that hospitality is a minimal, non-enforceable moral limit applying only to individual travelers, never to collective economic or political bodies.

Both camps agree that the central analytical problem is an “asymmetry of juridical recognition”: a state may possess a republican internal constitution while exercising non-republican sovereign control over external territories through chartered entities with state-like powers. They ask whether such a state falls under the federation’s protection, under hospitality’s constraints, or into a juridical gap. Their second agreed crux is whether republican constitutions permit preemptive collective security alliances against non-republics without violating the sovereignty that the federation is meant to preserve.

Produce a formal, polished document containing:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims about the essay’s architecture — the three Definitive Articles, the explicit rejection of a world republic, the distinction between the negative substitute and the state of nature, the limited scope of cosmopolitan right, and the pacific tendency attributed to republican government — phrased in neutral language both camps can sign.

2. A disagreements table stating, in wording each camp accepts for its own view, the precise divergence on the federation’s juridical status, the enforceability of hospitality, and the resolution of the asymmetry of juridical recognition.

3. The two cruxes named above. For the asymmetry crux, use as the shared test case the historical relation between the Dutch Republic and its chartered East India Company, treating the company’s sovereign privileges as the concrete instance. For the preemptive security crux, use the logical structure of Kant’s argument in the First Definitive Article regarding republican constitutions and war. For each crux, record in advance what the Institutional Republicans predict the case demonstrates and what the Sovereignty Preservationists predict it demonstrates.

4. A brief concluding section identifying whether any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, with explicit reasoning.

Write without citations, bibliographies, or appeals to named secondary authorities. Do not quote verbatim from any real text; paraphrase without quotation marks. Treat the “asymmetry of juridical recognition” as a shared analytical framing device introduced for this collaboration, not as an established term of art.
