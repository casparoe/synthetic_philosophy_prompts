Below are notes for an essay that extends the inside-view versus outside-view distinction from predictive forecasting to the selection of reasoning methods. The central claim is that epistemic modesty should lead us to treat our cognitive style—our characteristic balance between analytic decomposition and holistic pattern-matching, for example—as a draw from a reference class of reasoners with comparable training and temperament. Rather than adjusting the content of our beliefs directly by peer opinion, we should adjust the procedural authority we grant to the reasoning tools that produced them, weighting reliance on particular methods according to their outside-view track records within that reference class. The essay then addresses the objection that this instrumentalizes first-person deliberation, replying that inside-view access is preserved in the execution of a method but not in its selection, which should be guided by outside-view performance data. I am particularly interested in whether the distinction between method-selection and method-execution is stable, whether it genuinely resolves the tension between epistemic modesty and first-person authority, and whether hidden reference-class problems—such as how finely to individuate the class of reasoners like oneself—undermine the proposal. Please comment on the coherence and interest of the argument, flag any mischaracterizations of how the inside/outside distinction is standardly drawn, and suggest whether the essay must also address the reflexive problem that any outside-view data about reasoning quality is itself subject to the same skeptical pressure the view recommends. Please reply in American English.
