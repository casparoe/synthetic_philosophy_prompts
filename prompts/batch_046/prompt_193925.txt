Consider the following argument, which extends the standard procreation asymmetry to existing persons and derives an implausible moral burden.

(1) The procreation asymmetry holds: we have weighty reason not to create a being who will suffer severely, but no corresponding reason to create a being who will flourish.
(2) The best explanation is Baseline Neutrality: non-existence is the moral baseline, harm is a deviation that demands justification, and benefit is merely an opposing deviation that demands none.
(3) If non-existence is the baseline, then for any actually existing flourishing person P, P’s well-being is an unclaimed benefit—something P receives without anyone being obligated to provide it.
(4) By symmetrical moral accounting, enjoying an unclaimed positive deviation from the baseline creates a countervailing debt: P must compensate to restore neutrality.
(5) Hence every flourishing person is in perpetual moral deficit and is required to sacrifice well-being until suffering elsewhere is eliminated.
(6) But it is absurd that being happy permanently indebtedness one in this way.
(7) Therefore the chain from (1) to (5) must contain a flaw.

Identify the weakest step. Explain why that step initially seemed supported by the asymmetry, and assess the cost of rejecting it: specifically, can one preserve Baseline Neutrality without accepting that unclaimed benefits generate debts, or does abandoning (4) force one to abandon (2) and with it the asymmetry itself?
