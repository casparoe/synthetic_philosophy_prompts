Argue for "differentiated tranquilism": the view that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, but that craving must be split into acquisitive craving (directed at possession, status, or future goods) and orienting craving (the minimal directedness required for sustained attention, care, or aesthetic engagement). From this split, defend the claim that a being can be fully free of suffering and of acquisitive craving while retaining orienting directedness, and that such a state is ethically optimal—superior both to ordinary suffering-laden existence and to the total quiescence of complete mental absence. Address the objection that orienting directedness is merely suffering or craving in disguise, and explain why eliminating it entirely would not increase value but would instead undermine the very subject for whom absence of suffering matters. Build the argument from the conceptual distinction itself rather than by citing specific philosophers or texts.
