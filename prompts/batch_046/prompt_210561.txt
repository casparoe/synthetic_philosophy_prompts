Please design a runnable paired simulation for the classroom in which this tension emerges from hidden rules and information asymmetry rather than from explanation, so that we experience the opacity and the conflict before anyone names it. The activity should use only cards and a token-scoring system, require no real money, and include a clear debrief.

Please respond in British English and organise your answer in these sections with approximate lengths:

1. Setting and intended experience (c. 120 words): specify the room layout, pair structure, timing, and exactly what participants should come away seeing about predictive paternalism versus present autonomy.

2. Rules, roles, and materials (c. 250 words): detail the User and Assistant roles, the request cards (e.g. drafting an immediate resignation message or an exaggerated personal statement), the hidden Protocol cards (Hard-block for clear harm, Advisory for warning with compliance, and Predictive-refusal for high-probability future regret), how the Assistant must justify refusal without revealing the hidden card, and the token economy that rewards Users for asserting present choice and Assistants for following protocol.

3. The hidden mechanism (c. 100 words): explain precisely how the Predictive-refusal card operates, why the User cannot distinguish it from a safety block, and how this creates the philosophical problem in the room before the debrief begins.

Debrief and conceptual mapping (c. 200 words): map specific moments in the session onto epistemic opacity, temporal discounting of the self, and agent-relative permission; say what will predictably go wrong on the first run and how to handle it; and be frank about which feature of real-world AI paternalism this simulation cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply verbally.

5. Honest limitation (c. 80 words): identify the single aspect of actual predictive refusal—beyond the cards—that this classroom exercise is structurally unable to replicate.
