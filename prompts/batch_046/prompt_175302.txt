I have been trying to extend Zahavi's handicap principle to cooperative breeding contexts, in which alloparental care lowers the direct metabolic burden of maintaining an elaborate display. If the honesty of a handicap depends on its being wasteful in direct proportion to the signaler's underlying quality, then distributing that waste across helpers should reduce the marginal cost to the individual and collapse the separating equilibrium—thereby allowing low-quality individuals to fake the signal more easily. Yet cooperative species still appear to maintain honest ornamentation. Should we therefore reconceive the relevant handicap as the group's aggregate cost, or does this case show that Zahavi's model tacitly assumes solitary cost-bearing and must be revised for social species?
