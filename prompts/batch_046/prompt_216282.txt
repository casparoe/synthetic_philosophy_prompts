Having recently retired from experimental physics and begun studying philosophy of science, I serve on the National Science Foundation’s Program Integrity Panel. By next month we must decide whether to renew the five-year grant for the Center for Adaptive Thermodynamics, an interdisciplinary group that applies information-theoretic formalisms to collective decision-making in Physarum polycephalum networks. Their hard-core claim is that macroscopic choices in these organisms obey a modified Landauer principle with non-Markovian feedback. Over three years, predicted temperature-dependence curves have failed repeatedly; the team has responded each time by expanding the protective belt—first invoking hidden environmental noise spectra, then postulating novel epigenetic state variables, and most recently introducing an unobservable “contextual boundary condition.” Two panelists view this as normal scientific refinement; two regard it as a degenerating problem shift in the Lakatosian sense and urge termination. Please draft the panel’s formal decision memo recommending whether to renew, reduce, or terminate funding, with conditions if appropriate. The memo should treat the strongest argument that these adjustments are legitimate protective-belt revisions rather than ad hoc rescue, should be written in clear American English, should avoid any request for bibliographies or citations, and should present a concrete recommendation the center can actually respond to rather than offering me personal philosophical guidance.
