For my ethics seminar’s final session I want to close with a poem that holds an open tension in care ethics rather than resolving it. Please write a poem of exactly five stanzas, each six lines long, with no lines before the first stanza or after the last. The concept is relational autonomy—the idea that autonomy is constituted through responsive caring relationships, not through isolated independence—but I want the poem focused on its breaking point: caring for a parent in late-stage dementia who can no longer recognize or respond to the caregiver. Make the form do the philosophical work: use an A-B-A-B-C-D rhyme pattern in every stanza, but have the B-rhyme words shift from terms of mutual recognition (voice, face, reply) in stanzas one and two to terms of one-sided presence (shadow, hand, debt) in stanzas three through five, so the sound itself enacts the loss of reciprocity. Place the volta at the start of stanza four, where the speaker realizes that obligation persists even when acknowledgment is impossible. Keep the tone serious and reflective, accurate enough that a reader of care-ethics literature would recognize the framework, and leave the moral question suspended rather than turning it into a sermon about intervention or a shrug about the circle of life.
