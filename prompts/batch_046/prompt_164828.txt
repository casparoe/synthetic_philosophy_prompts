Analyze the 2015 killing of Cecil the lion and the global reaction that followed as a case study for Nozick's framework in Anarchy, State, and Utopia, which assigns absolute side-constraints only to beings capable of rational long-range life-planning and self-conscious agency, while placing animals under a modified utilitarian calculus with a floor against unnecessary suffering. Apply the distinction precisely: did the public outrage treat Cecil as a rights-holder whose killing violated a side-constraint, or as an experience-bearer whose welfare was severely diminished? Then introduce the pressure point of what we might call narrative individuality—Cecil was not merely an anonymous lion but a named, socially embedded individual with sustained pride relationships and a recognized ecological role—an individuating feature that does not depend on rational self-planning. Does this kind of social-ecological identity challenge Nozick's capacity-based boundary, or can it be accommodated within his modified utilitarian floor? Finally, say what the episode implies for Nozick's division generally: does it support his claim that animals lack the kind of being that grounds absolute protection, or does the intensity of human moral response reveal that the boundary is unstable in practice, and what does this mean for the ethics of trophy hunting under his view?
