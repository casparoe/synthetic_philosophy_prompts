Compose a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme, with two refrains—spoken by a rational agent on the verge of taking a cognition-altering compound who is trying to obey Bas van Fraassen’s Reflection principle. Let one refrain state her present credence in a concrete proposition (say, that the compound is safe), and let the other state her expectation of her own credence in that same proposition after ingestion. The form should do philosophical work: as the refrains alternate and return, they should register her shifting weight on her future self’s opinion, so that the poem enacts the principle’s demand that present belief align with anticipated future belief. In the final couplet, bring the two refrains together side by side without resolving them, leaving the agent suspended in the Dutch-book vulnerability that opens up if present and expected credences diverge. Do not name philosophers in the text; render the idea precisely enough that a specialist in formal epistemology recognizes the principle at work, yet keep the language such that the poem stands on its own for a reader who does not. Keep the scenario realistic—no science-fiction physics, only an ordinary decision about self-modification under uncertainty.
