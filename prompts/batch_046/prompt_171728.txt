There's this debate in my phil-of-mind group about heterophenomenology that's been going in circles for years. The defenders say Dennett's method lets us treat first-person reports as fictional texts, so science can study consciousness without touching the hard problem. The critics say it just smuggles in eliminativism, since the method makes error about ones own experience impossible in principle, which begs the question against anyone who thinks phenomenal access is real. We've all read the same papers, agree on what the method actually does, and yet nobody shifts position. Is this stall actually about whether phenomenal access is a coherent concept, or are we just answering different questions -- one side about scientific methodology, the other about ontology -- under one name? Or is there a hidden empirical disagreement about brain scanning that we're dressing up as conceptual? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey, plus an honest take on whether a distinction, experiment, or change of framing could actually move it. Also please separate what's settled about heterophenomenology from what's genuinely contested, and don't present one camp as the consensus. One side thing: could the method even handle pre-reflective reports, or does that just collapse back into first-person authority?
