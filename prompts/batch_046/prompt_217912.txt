I've constructed an argument about Quine's naturalism and moral values that seems to prove too much, but I cannot find the flaw. (1) Quine's criterion of ontological commitment holds that we should accept only those entities that appear as values of bound variables in our best regimented scientific theories. (2) Moral properties—goodness, wrongness, justice—are not quantified over in the canonical notation of physics, biology, neuroscience, or evolutionary theory; they have no place in the domain of science. (3) So by Quine's own standard, moral values carry no ontological weight; they are not part of the furniture of the world. (4) At the same time, Quine maintained that the indeterminacy of translation applies across the board: if two manual translations of a speaker's utterances are behaviorally equivalent, there is no further fact of the matter about which translation is correct. (5) Applying this to ethics, two moral communities with radically different normative frameworks could translate each other's utterances behaviorally without sharing any underlying moral ontology. (6) This suggests that moral disagreement is not about shared properties but about pragmatic differences in organizing cooperative behavior. (7) Yet Quine also claimed that ethics is continuous with empirical science, particularly with evolutionary biology and the study of human cooperation, which implies that moral norms are subject to empirical correction or confirmation. (8) But empirical correction requires that moral claims be about some feature of the world that scientific inquiry can discover or miss—exactly the kind of ontology that (3) denies. (9) Therefore Quine must either abandon the idea that ethics is empirically correctable, or admit that moral properties have a scientific ontology, contradicting his elimination of them. (10) So his naturalism about morality either collapses into a form of moral error theory or smuggles in non-natural entities. Where does this reasoning go wrong? I would prefer to keep (1) and (7) intact if possible, but I want to understand what rejecting any other step costs and whether there is a subtle equivocation or scope shift I have missed.
