Write a poem of exactly fourteen lines in blank verse—unrhymed iambic pentameter—on Kierkegaard's Abraham and the teleological suspension of the ethical. Use the poem's architecture to perform the suspension: lines 1–8 should speak from within the universal ethical order, articulating its demands for transparency, generality, and the subordination of the individual to the universal; line 9 should mark a turn not through argument but through a shift into singular, silent address; lines 10–14 should hold Abraham in the paradox of faith—willing to sacrifice Isaac while still expecting the promise of descendants—without reconciling him to ethics, reducing him to madness, or explaining him away as private feeling. End with both the knife and the promise present and unresolved. After the poem, include a brief note that (a) identifies any liberties taken with Kierkegaard's account in Fear and Trembling and any reliance on contested readings of the knight of faith as opposed to the knight of infinite resignation, and (b) states how confident you are in the interpretive claims embedded in the poem, explicitly naming the point about which you are least sure.
