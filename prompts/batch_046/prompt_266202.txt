I am drafting a one-page research proposal for a semester-long philosophy-of-education fellowship. The topic is Whitehead's account of education, specifically the tension between his well-known rhythm of romance, precision, and generalization and his claim that true education involves transforming inert ideas into ideas that are alive through aesthetic feeling. My half-formed idea is that these two elements are usually treated separately, with the rhythm read as a pedagogical sequence and the inert-idea distinction read as a metaphysical comment on mental causation. I want to propose that they are actually a single theory of conceptual stability: the rhythm describes not merely how we learn, but how concepts survive the kind of microdomain slippage—where analogical and literal uses bleed into each other—that typically corrupts philosophical reasoning in students.

The proposal should state precisely whether Whitehead's rhythm can be reconstructed as a normative framework for identifying productive versus destructive conceptual slippage, explain why standard interpretations—developmental, curricular, or metaphysical—leave this question open, outline an approach that combines close textual analysis of Whitehead's educational writings with a structured observation protocol for tracking student reasoning in an introductory metaphysics course, and specify what a successful result would look like—perhaps a diagnostic map of where in the rhythm slippage is generative—versus what failure would entail, such as finding that the rhythm offers no predictive structure beyond generic stage theories. Given the single-semester scope, the proposal should be honest about what can and cannot be accomplished without a full empirical study, and should not include a bibliography or requested reading list, since I will compile citations separately; references to positions or arguments may be named but need not be footnoted.
