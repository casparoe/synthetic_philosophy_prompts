After a long and somewhat heated discussion with a friend about Wittgenstein's rule-following considerations, she offered me the following summary of what she takes Kripkenstein's skeptical argument and skeptical solution to establish: "Kripke shows that no mental state or finite behavioral record can determine whether a speaker means addition rather than quaddition, since any past performance is compatible with infinitely many divergent rules. From this he derives a skeptical conclusion that there is simply no fact of the matter about what an individual means by 'plus' in isolation. His skeptical solution then abandons truth-conditions for meaning-ascriptions altogether and substitutes communal assertibility conditions: to mean addition is just to be disposed to continue in ways that the linguistic community endorses and corrects. On this account, the distinction between genuinely following a rule and merely exhibiting a causally fixed disposition collapses, because without private mental facts there is nothing left to constitute correctness except social agreement. Consequently, Kripke's view amounts to a form of social constructivism about mental content in general, and Wittgenstein's private-language argument becomes not a conceptual point about the conditions for language but a sociological observation that isolated individuals lack the corrective mechanisms that generate meaning." I am inclined to think she has smuggled in a behaviorist reading that Kripke actually rejects, but I am not sure where exactly the line falls between charitable interpretation and misrepresentation.
