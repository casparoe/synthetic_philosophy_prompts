Write an essay on the following controversy: in the classic absent-minded driver case, a subject who intends to turn left toward a store instead continues straight toward work out of long-established habit. Philosophers disagree over whether this counts as intentional action—some hold that a standing intention guides the behavior, others that the absence of an occurrent mental state makes it mere habit, and still others propose intermediate categories. Now consider a modified scenario: the driver’s vehicle is equipped with a predictive steering-assist system that learns his habitual routes and subtly corrects his trajectory in real time; the driver is unaware of this assistance, though he retains manual override and feels the car responding to his inputs. Does the introduction of this distributed, algorithmic component alter the conceptual status of his driving? Specifically, weigh whether (i) the case should still be analyzed purely in terms of the driver’s mental states, (ii) the system’s contribution creates a form of hybrid agency in which intentionality is shared between driver and device, or (iii) the assistance actually undermines the ascription of agency altogether by replacing the causal role of his intention with an automated routine. Examine the main arguments on each side, but do not end non-committally—defend a specific verdict about whether the modified driver acts intentionally, and justify that verdict by addressing the strongest objection to your view.
