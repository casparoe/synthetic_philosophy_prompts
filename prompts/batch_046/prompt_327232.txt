Write the desiderata that any formal measure of "low-impact" AI behavior must satisfy if it aims to avoid side effects without presupposing a complete specification of human values. The criteria should cover at least: (i) value-independence—the measure must not smuggle in preferences about which states are good; (ii) counterfactual stability—actions should be penalized for large effects in nearby possible worlds, not only in the actual one; (iii) informational transparency—the distinction between altering physical states and altering epistemic conditions (e.g., hiding versus destroying evidence) must be preserved; and (iv) non-triviality—the measure must actually rule out some actions, not classify everything as low-impact. For each, state the conceptual motivation and whether it is negotiable. Then identify the central tension: is there a jointly satisfiable subset that remains both genuinely value-independent and substantively constraining, or does any non-trivial criterion implicitly encode a preference for the status quo? Focus on specifying the requirements and their conflicts; do not evaluate named proposals or provide citations.
