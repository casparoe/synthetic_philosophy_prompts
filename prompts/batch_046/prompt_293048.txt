I am in the final revision of a historical novel and wish to audit my historiographical commitments before proceeding. I hold the following: (1) all plotted historical fiction necessarily practises a form of Whig history, because narrative closure arranges the past as progression towards conditions the reader already inhabits—what I call “diegetic presentism”; (2) I reject the strong Braudelian view that geography and climate are the sole engines of change, insisting that individual agency and contingent events remain indispensable; (3) I therefore embed long-duration climatic structures—specifically the agrarian psychology shaped by the Little Ice Age—as invisible background conditions, whilst making dramatic moral choices drive the plot; (4) I believe moral progress is an illusion and that no mind-independent moral facts improve across centuries; (5) despite (4), I shape character arcs so protagonists gradually approximate values I presently hold, though I deny this is historical destiny; (6) anachronistic psychological interiority is permissible, even necessary, for empathy, but anachronistic moral evaluation is the defining sin of Whiggish narrative; (7) the only ethical defence against (1) is to saturate the plot with branching possibilities so the present feels radically contingent. Do these hang together? Is (5) compatible with (4) and (6), or does convergence towards my present values smuggle in the moral teleology I reject? Does (2) survive my practise in (3), or am I appropriating micro-agency only where it suits drama? Is the tension between (1) and (7) genuine—can narrative closure coexist with radical contingency—or does one have to yield? Please rank any real tensions by severity, state the cheapest revision for each, identify which single change would clean up the most conflicts, and flag any pair that looks contradictory but dissolves on inspection. Lead with a one-sentence bottom line on whether the package is salvageable without abandoning the novel’s premise; write in British English; and keep the answer to one paragraph of no more than 300 words.
