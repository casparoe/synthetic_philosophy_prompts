After three decades in comparative neurobiology and a recent turn to political philosophy, I want a consistency audit of my current package on democratic membership before I present it in a seminar. Here are my commitments: (1) The demos should be defined by the all-affected-interests principle—any being whose vital interests are systematically shaped by collective coercive decisions belongs to the demos. (2) Democratic legitimacy requires reciprocal public justification; law is legitimate only if each member can, in principle, see themselves as a co-author through discursive justification. (3) Drawing on mammalian and avian affective neuroscience, I hold that several non-human species possess sufficient sentience and preference-formation that agricultural and environmental policy genuinely affects their vital interests, so they fall under (1). (4) I reject citizenship, nationality, and bare territorial presence as sufficient conditions—physical presence without subjection to the coercive structure is not membership, and subjection without presence (e.g., diaspora communities governed by homeland policy) can be. (5) Legitimacy requires that members have access, in principle, to participatory mechanisms—voting, deliberation, or structured proxy voice—not merely benevolent administration on their behalf. (6) Future generations clearly fall under (1), since climate and resource policies determine the conditions of their lives.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and how severe each real tension is. For each genuine tension, identify the cheapest revision that restores consistency and what philosophical cost it carries—especially whether it forces me to abandon the scientific motivation in (3) or weaken the democratic core in (2) or (5). Rank the tensions by severity, then say which single revision to my package would resolve the most conflict at the least cost. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning with no preamble and without restating the question.
