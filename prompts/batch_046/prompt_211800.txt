For an upper-level undergraduate seminar in political philosophy—eighteen students, one ninety-minute session—design a structured, multi-round simulation that lets participants feel how formal equality of opportunity can be technically satisfied while dynastic advantage persists through non-financial transmission mechanisms. Center the exercise on what we might call the conversion problem: when direct financial inheritance is taxed away, families still transmit risk-tolerance profiles, social-network access, and tacit cultural capital that markets do not price but that shape competitive outcomes across generations. Divide participants into family lines that span three simulated generations; apply a 100 percent estate tax on all monetary assets at each generational transition, but allow families to bequeath non-monetary “endowments” (network tokens, risk-preference cards, and cultural-capital markers) that affect payoff structures in subsequent rounds. Provide explicit rules, role assignments, materials that can be printed on index cards, and a small-stakes payoff scheme using course-credit points or symbolic tokens rather than real money, arranged so that “heir” and “non-heir” lines diverge in life-path outcomes even though every generation starts with the same cash allocation. The activity should run with minimal front-loaded lecture: participants should discover the divergence through play and only name the mechanism in the final round. Include a debrief that maps specific moments in the simulation—such as the first round in which a taxed family leverages a network token to secure a high-variance opportunity—onto parts of the idea; state what will predictably go wrong on the first run (for example, participants conflating cash equality with full equality of condition) and how the facilitator should handle it; and be explicit about which feature of real-world dynastic reproduction—such as legal enforcement of inheritance rights or macro-level institutional capture—the simulation cannot reproduce and must therefore be supplied in the closing discussion. Write in American English, keep the prompt self-contained without asking for bibliographies or citations, and ensure the framing relies only on concepts a well-trained model can reason through without web access.
