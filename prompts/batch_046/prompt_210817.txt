I'm a phd student in philosophy of mind working on hedonism and well-being, and I've been stuck for weeks on the heterogenity problem — you know, what do pleasures even have in common if eating chocolate and solving a proof both count. I was arguing with my roommate about some concrete cases over coffee and realized my verdicts are mostly sensible but there's at least one pair that pulls in opposite directions, and I'm not sure if there's a principle that saves them all or if I have to drop something.

Here are my takes. First, warm sun on your skin after being cold versus that mental 'click' when a proof finally lands — both are genuine pleasures, but they share zero qualitative texture, not even a generic 'goodness' feel. Second, scratching an unbearable itch versus hearing your favorite chord progression — scratching is pure relief, like paying off a debt, while the chord adds something positive; still, I want both to count as pleasures, not just absence versus presence. Third, schadenfreude when someone who wronged you fails versus the warmth of seeing a friend succeed — phenomenologically both are pleasant, but I think only the second contributes to well-being; for cataloging pleasures, though, both belong in the category. Fourth, imagine a brain-stim device that makes you report 'this is amazing' but there's literally no qualitative feel — just blank approval, like liking a blank page. I think this is counterfit pleasure, not real. Fifth, anticipating a vacation feels like a real pleasure to me, even though it's mostly about the future, not a present sensation.

So what's the principle? Maybe pleasure is intrinsic liking of an experiential state with positive affective tone — that saves cases one through three and five, excludes four, but then why does scratching count if it's just relief without added tone? Or maybe it's a heterogeneous family united only by being positively valued experiential states, but then why reject the blank stim, which is pure positive valuation? I want you to propose one or two precise principles, tell me which of my verdicts each forces me to abandon, and say what they'd predict about cases I haven't mentioned — like eating synthetic chocolate that hits the same receptors but you know it's fake, or dreaming you've solved a problem without being aware of it. Do I have to give up on the claim that there's no shared feel across all pleasures, or is that salvageable if I pay the price of a more complex principle?
