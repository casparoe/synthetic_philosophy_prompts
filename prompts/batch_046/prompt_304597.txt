I am a retired physicist who now serves on an independent review panel for cognitive-neuroscience protocols, and I have recently begun taking seriously the illusionist account of phenomenal consciousness—the view that there is no irreducible intrinsic "what-it-is-likeness" to experience, but only representational architectures that misrepresent themselves as possessing such qualitative properties. I need you to translate this theoretical stance into a reusable decision procedure that my panel can actually run when a study claims to have measured, disrupted, or preserved "phenomenal consciousness" in human or animal subjects. The deliverable should be a step-by-step protocol in ordered questions: for each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure halts with a rejection on the grounds that the claim is methodologically incoherent under illusionism, where it halts with approval, and—crucially—where it must hand the decision to human judgment because illusionism offers no further guidance. The procedure must explicitly distinguish access consciousness from phenomenal consciousness, must treat first-person reports as data about representational architecture rather than direct measurements of intrinsic qualia, and must flag any protocol that implicitly reifies an illusion by treating subjective report as tracking a non-representational property. Apply the procedure to three proposals: (1) an fMRI study that correlates self-reported "vividness of red" with ventral-stream activation and interprets the correlation as tracking a phenomenal property rather than a representational state; (2) a clinical assessment protocol for unresponsive patients that asks family members to rate "how much inner experience remains" on a graded scale; and (3) a pharmacological trial that targets a specific receptor to "reduce the raw feel of suffering," using patient-reported reduction in "phenomenal intensity" as its primary endpoint. Include at least one case in which the procedure returns a verdict the researchers will not welcome, and state explicitly what the procedure cannot settle—for instance, whether illusionism itself is correct, or whether a particular representational model is neurobiologically accurate. After the procedural text and the three worked applications, provide a brief structured summary in JSON with exactly these fields: "procedure_name" (string), "step_count" (integer), "termination_points" (array of strings describing halt conditions), "judgment_nodes" (array of strings describing value-laden handoffs), "case_verdicts" (array of objects, each with "case_id" (string), "proposed_claim" (string), "verdict" (string), "reason" (string)), and "limitations" (array of strings).
