The view I want stress-tested is the strong ontological reading of intuitionistic logic and mathematical constructivism: not the modest claim that mathematicians ought to restrict proof methods to those yielding explicit constructions, but the claim that this restriction tracks the actual metaphysics of mathematical objects. On this account, numbers, functions, and sets are not pre-existing entities discovered by reasoning but dependently constituted entities that come into determinacy only through explicit constructive proof; the failure of the law of excluded middle reflects an absence of fact, not merely an absence of knowledge. A central supporting argument is that intuitionistic arithmetic is consistent with Church's thesis—that every total function is computable—whereas classical Peano arithmetic proves the existence of non-computable functions, so that the classical commitment to such functions is an ontological overreach rather than a harmless abstraction. A further consideration is that in sheaf-theoretic and realizability interpretations, intuitionistic logic holds globally across a model, while classical bivalence is recovered only in local, restricted contexts, which suggests that classical mathematics projects a special-case structure onto reality rather than describing its underlying form.

Play this position straight: what would have to be true for this ontological reading to collapse? Which of its pillars—say, the interpretation of Church's thesis as an ontological boundary rather than a computability result, or the global-local distinction in sheaf models—are genuinely exposed, and which have been insulated by qualification? Could one accept every formal result—the consistency of intuitionistic arithmetic with Church's thesis, the existence of realizability toposes, the double-negation translation—while denying that they carry any metaphysical weight, and if so, what specific observation or argument would block that instrumentalist retreat? Would it be decisive if synthetic frameworks such as homotopy type theory were shown to validate both intuitionistic reasoning and classical principles for broad classes of propositions, or would that merely complicate the ontology without defeating it? Is there a live risk that the distinction between intensional and extensional equality undermines the claim that constructive determinacy is metaphysically basic? I want an honest map of exposure: which results or cases the position's own defenders would concede as genuinely damaging, which criticisms they would dismiss as misreading the formalism, and whether the view risks anything substantive or has been rendered unfalsifiable by its interpretive flexibility. In particular, distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence that would merely be awkward, and say whether you regard the position as well-insulated and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice.
