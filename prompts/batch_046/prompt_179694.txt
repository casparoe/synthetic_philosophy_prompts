I was discussing funding priorities with a colleague over coffee last week — she had just finished a course on philosophy of science and kept returning to arguments about non-empirical theory confirmation, particularly the idea that structural coherence and explanatory breadth can justify belief even without predictive novelty, while I found myself more sympathetic to the view that the string landscape problem has turned empirical inaccessibility into a methodological virtue rather than a temporary limitation. Our disagreement mapped almost exactly onto a decision my board now faces.

I serve on the review board for the University’s Centre for Mathematical Cosmology, and at Thursday’s meeting we must decide whether to renew their core programme for another three years. The centre’s recent work has focused on statistical measures across the string landscape, particularly attempts to constrain the cosmological constant’s value through anthropic reasoning within a multiverse framework. They have not produced — and do not claim to be able to produce — empirically testable predictions distinguishable from standard ΛCDM. Several board members contend that mathematical consistency, explanatory breadth, and the absence of viable alternatives justify continued investment; others argue that without predictive risk, the trajectory has crossed from theoretical physics into mathematical metaphysics, and that renewing it would violate our institutional mandate to support empirically accountable science.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — full renewal, termination, or a conditional transitional model with explicit milestones — and include a clear time frame. The reasoning must engage seriously with the strongest opposing view, specifically the claim that landscape statistics and structural realism can provide rational grounds for belief even where empirical access is permanently blocked. It should also distinguish whether the centre is advancing an ontological multiverse claim or merely an epistemic landscape argument, since the funding justification differs materially between them, and propose a review mechanism that respects both the philosophical legitimacy of non-empirical progress and the board’s fiduciary obligation to empirical accountability. Do not include bibliographies or reading lists; I need a document the board can act upon.
