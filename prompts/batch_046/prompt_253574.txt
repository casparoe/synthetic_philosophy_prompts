Write a short story that dramatizes the problem of applying Dennett's intentional stance to an artificial system with a modular, non-integrated architecture. Set the narrative in a near-future regulatory hearing: a predictive sentencing-aid system called Partition-7 is being evaluated for licensure, and the law permits its deployment only if it can be certified as lacking intentional agency. The system's design is genuinely unusual—rather than maintaining a single unified model of a defendant, it runs several incompatible predictive subsystems in parallel, including one statistical-correlational, one narrative-hermeneutic that interprets testimony, and one counterfactual-simulational that models alternative actions, allowing a meta-arbitrator to switch between them without ever synthesizing their outputs into a coherent worldview.

The central conflict should turn on whether belief-attribution to Partition-7 is literally true or merely instrumentally useful. Have a defense attorney argue that the system should be disqualified precisely because it holds beliefs about defendants: the narrative module genuinely interprets testimony, the counterfactual module genuinely considers what the defendant might have done, and together they constitute a subject of belief in the intentional-stance sense, which grants the system a form of moral status incompatible with using it to advise on punishment. Have the system's designer counter that there is no unified subject—only a bundle of predictive routines with no integration point, no self-model, and no capacity for deception or self-interest—so applying the intentional stance is a pragmatic mistake, not a metaphysical discovery.

The story should include at least one concrete scene—perhaps the attorney cross-examining the designer, or the system generating an unexpected defense of its own epistemic structure—in which the philosophical tension becomes visceral rather than abstract. Do not reduce the AI to a human-like consciousness in a box, and do not resolve the ambiguity with a twist that reveals it was really conscious or really a machine. Instead, leave open whether the intentional stance is being discovered or projected, and whether an architecture that never integrates its predictions into a single point of view can still be the kind of entity about which belief-talk is literally appropriate rather than merely predictive.
