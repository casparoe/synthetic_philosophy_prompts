I'm trying to get my head round what philosophical baggage is hidden in how different places regulate surrogacy and egg donation. In a lot of European countries commercial surrogacy is banned outright and egg donation is kept anonynmous with a flat state compensation, but the same health systems will fund IVF cycles that use those donated gametes for infertille couples. Meanwhile in the US you have open markets where egg donors are paid premiums for specific traits and surrogacy contracts are negotiated like service agreements, complete with clauses about selective reduction and post-birth contact rights that adoption law wouldn't allow. What theory of the body, of commodification, or of reproductive labour does that split actually presuppose? Is the European model committed to the idea that gestation and gametes are inalienable, or is it just a tractability fix dressed up in dignity language? And can the American practice be made consistent with a view that treats reproduction as ordinary labour, given that the contracts still restrict behavour in ways no other job contract does? I'd like the implicit theory reconstructed, not just named, and I'd like to know where the practice is underdetermined—whether several rival views could license the same rules.
