For my philosophy of action seminar, I am preparing remarks on Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the principle of alternative possibilities. I would like to open with a villanelle—exactly nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a quatrain, with two refrains that alternate through the tercets and converge in the final couplet. One refrain, voiced by the agent, should express the felt openness of choice despite its absence: I chose this path, though I could not turn. The other, voiced by the counterfactual controller, should state the mechanism's dormant presence: The switch is set; it never fires. The poem must render the case with philosophical precision—the agent acts for independent reasons, the controller never actually intervenes, so no robust alternative exists, yet responsibility still seems appropriate. I want the formal repetition to do conceptual work, with the refrains running parallel like the agent's deliberation and the blocker that never intersects it, so that the turn in the quatrain takes up the flicker-of-freedom objection—the claim that even here a minimal alternative persists in the agent's own decision process—without resolving whether that flicker is sufficient to save PAP. Rather than resolving the tension, the closing lines should hold open the conflict between being the source of one's action and having genuinely open alternatives. Please keep it strictly to nineteen lines, readable as verse in its own right, but accurate enough that someone familiar with the debate recognizes the Frankfurt structure and the flicker problem rendered correctly rather than merely named.
