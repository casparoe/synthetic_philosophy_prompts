Construct a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions in the debate over surrogacy and egg donation across the European prohibition model and the American commercial market, intended for a seminar on reproductive justice and cross-border ethics.

The tree should begin from a single shared commitment — that reproductive autonomy carries significant moral weight, yet that commercial reproductive labour can generate exploitation — and then branch through four diagnostic questions that do not mention the standard labels. Each branch node must pose a concrete case or conceptual claim on which the positions genuinely diverge, phrased so that a respondent can answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to.

Specifically, include these nodes:

1. A question about whether gestational labour is conceptually continuous with other forms of bodily labour (e.g. live-in care work or clinical-trial participation) or whether pregnancy involves a unique form of embodied risk and relational transformation that makes commodification categorically different.

2. A question about risk asymmetry: given that egg extraction carries acute medical risks (ovarian hyperstimulation, surgical retrieval) while gestation carries prolonged bodily-autonomy and relational risks, does this asymmetry justify regulating gamete markets differently from surrogacy markets, or should both be governed by the same principle?

3. A concrete cross-border case: a couple resident in a jurisdiction that prohibits commercial surrogacy contracts domestically travels to a jurisdiction where commercial surrogacy is permitted and where surrogates have contractual exit rights and independent legal counsel. Is the domestic prohibition rendered incoherent by this practice, or can the prohibition remain morally justified as a refusal to legitimise the practice within one's own legal order?

4. A question about jurisdiction-sensitive evaluation: should the moral permissibility of a reproductive contract depend primarily on the robustness of the surrogate's or donor's labour protections and exit rights in the specific jurisdiction where the contract is executed, or should it depend on a universalist principle (e.g. "no reproductive labour should be sold for profit" or "all voluntary contracts should be permitted") regardless of local conditions?

The leaves must include at least the following named positions, located in the logical space rather than listed upfront: (a) radical abolitionist (all commercial reproductive labour is inherently exploitative and should be banned everywhere); (b) altruistic-only nationalist (domestic altruistic arrangements may be permitted, but cross-border commercial contracting is impermissible or hypocritical); (c) regulated domestic market with border closure (commercial surrogacy and egg donation permitted domestically under strict regulation, but citizens should be barred from contracting abroad to avoid regulatory arbitrage); (d) harm-reduction cosmopolitan (domestic prohibition is acceptable only if paired with regulated, rights-protective channels abroad, since black-market alternatives are worse); and (e) full cosmopolitan contractualist (reproductive labour should be treated like other specialised labour, permitted globally provided robust labour protections, independent counsel, and exit rights are guaranteed).

Additionally, map any unoccupied combinations of answers — for instance, the combination that treats gestation as uniquely non-commodifiable but egg extraction as freely marketable globally, or the combination that endorses universalist prohibition but actively encourages citizens to seek unregulated black-market services abroad — and briefly diagnose why each is either incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored.

Finally, flag at least one pair of leaves that your tree genuinely cannot separate (for example, positions that differ mainly in rhetorical framing or in empirical predictions about exploitation rates rather than in answers to the four branch questions).

Please present the tree clearly: each node as a direct question answerable by selecting between two described options, each path labelled by the combination of answers, and each leaf with a brief statement of what distinguishes it from its nearest neighbour.

The response should state how confident it is in the taxonomy — particularly in the claim that the four nodes genuinely partition the logical space without hidden overlap — and should single out the point about which it is least sure (for example, whether the risk-asymmetry node actually separates abolitionists from cosmopolitan contractualists, or whether an additional node about the child's welfare or genetic relatedness is needed).

Write the answer in British English.
