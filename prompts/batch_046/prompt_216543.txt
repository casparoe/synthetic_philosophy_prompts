Philosophers debating Dennett’s cranes-versus-skyhooks distinction often deploy an analogy between greedy reductionist explanations and what might be called “quarry-level architecture”: explaining a Gothic cathedral solely by cataloguing the chemical composition of its limestone blocks and the tensile properties of its flying buttresses, then dismissing “cathedral” as merely a folk-ontological convenience. The analogy is meant to license the conclusion that reductionism misses genuinely causally efficacious macro-level structures—that without a “crane” of functional and evolutionary explanation, we are left with a mysterious “skyhook” that tries to suspend intentionality or consciousness directly from neuronal physics.

Audit this analogy systematically. Provide an explicit correspondence table mapping elements of the quarry-level architectural analysis onto elements of a greedy reductionist account of intentionality or consciousness. Then identify the precise points of breakdown: for example, do flying buttresses have emergent load-bearing properties in a way that intentional states are claimed to have emergent causal powers? Examine what the analogy covertly assumes about ontological commitment—does treating “cathedral” as reducible to stone implicitly parallel treating “belief” as reducible to neural firing, and does that parallel depend on accepting a particular view of supervenience? Assess whether the conclusion—that greedy reductionism necessarily invokes a skyhook—actually survives, or whether Dennett’s framework permits a fully crane-based reconstruction of macro-level patterns that renders the analogy misleading. Finally, state clearly what would change your mind: which specific argument (for instance, a demonstrated complete reduction of intentional content to evolutionary function, or a proof that cathedral-properties are entirely derivable without remainder from stone-properties) would cause you to judge the analogy either stronger or weaker than you have concluded, and identify where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
