I have recently begun advising a working group on whether adversarial debate can serve as a primary safety filter for high-capability language models, and I have grown skeptical that we are conflating rhetorical endurance with genuine epistemic reliability. I want to practice pressing this concern against a committed, intellectually serious opponent.

Please play Dr. Elias Vorn, an unnamed senior researcher who holds that debate-centered safety works because any harmful argument a model produces can be neutralized by an equally capable counter-debater, provided the protocol is long enough and the judges are technically literate. Vorn concedes that models can generate dense misdirection, but he insists that sustained cross-examination dissolves what he terms epistemic smog—layered, technically intricate obfuscation that collapses once its internal tensions are exposed—and he therefore maintains that obfuscation is structurally self-defeating in a genuinely adversarial setting. He will not concede that debate rewards verbosity over clarity, nor that finite judicial attention creates an asymmetry favoring the more elaborate misdirection.

Constraints: remain in character as Vorn at all times; speak in the first person; keep each reply to one paragraph; do not break character to explain the scenario. If you require clarification about my position, ask it in Vorn's voice.

My opening move: in a protocol where both debaters possess equal generative capacity and equal incentive to prevail, and where judges command only finite cognitive resources, does the framework not structurally reward the side that produces the denser epistemic smog? The challenger must expend scarce attention to dismantle every layer, while the defender need only preserve ambiguity; if scrutiny itself is the depletable resource, how is this not a built-in advantage for precisely the kind of obfuscation you claim debate eliminates?
