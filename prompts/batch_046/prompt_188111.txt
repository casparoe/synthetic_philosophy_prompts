In my retirement from a career in experimental physics I have begun attending a philosophy of science seminar at a local university, and there is one regular interlocutor—an analytical philosopher with a background in cognitive science—whose judgments about conceivability as a guide to metaphysical possibility I have been tracking across our discussions this term. She maintains that a zombie world physically identical to ours but entirely lacking conscious experience is not only conceivable but that this conceivability genuinely establishes its metaphysical possibility. She grants that water being composed of XYZ rather than H2O is superficially conceivable, yet she denies that this indicates real possibility on the grounds that the actual chemical essence of water is fixed by scientific discovery rather than by imagination. She accepts that silicon-based organisms capable of metabolism, reproduction, and adaptive behavior are both conceivable and genuinely possible. She argues that a perpetual motion machine of the first kind is not truly conceivable once one grasps the relevant conservation laws, so its inconceivability correctly tracks its impossibility. And she holds that an artificial system lacking any evolutionary history but possessing fully integrated real-time information processing is conceivable as being conscious, and that this conceivability points to a real metaphysical possibility.

The new case I wish to put to her, and which I ask you to analyze in her stead, concerns a localized thermodynamic event occurring within a region smaller than the Planck length, in which entropy decreases without any corresponding energy exchange, so that the second law of thermodynamics holds without exception at every macroscopic and observable scale yet is violated locally in a manner that no instrument, however refined, could ever register or detect. Would she judge this scenario genuinely conceivable, and if so would she take that conceivability to indicate metaphysical possibility, or would she block the inference? I am less interested in the bare answer than in the precise principle she appears to be running, recognizing that her earlier verdicts are jointly consistent with at least two distinct rules that fit her track record equally well. Please lay out those competing rules in continuous prose, say what each predicts about this sub-Planck entropy violation, offer your best guess at her actual verdict with the reasoning that supports it, and identify the further hypothetical case that would best discriminate between the rules you have articulated. I ask that you present all of this as continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, written in the manner of a formal letter.
