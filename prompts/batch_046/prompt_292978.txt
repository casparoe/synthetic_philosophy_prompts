I am drafting a philosophical profile for a seminar discussion and would like a consistency audit before I present it. My commitments regarding real patterns, Dennett's three stances, and their application to social phenomena are as follows:

(1) A pattern is real if and only if it supports counterfactual-robust predictions within an explanatory framework; there is no stance-independent criterion for its existence.

(2) The intentional stance tracks genuinely real patterns in human mental life—beliefs, desires, and intentions—that are not merely instrumental fictions.

(3) At the same time, every pattern revealed by the intentional stance is, in principle, fully capturable by the design stance of evolutionary psychology and cognitive neuroscience; the intentional stance is practically indispensable but ontologically redundant.

(4) Certain macro-level phenomena—specifically institutional norms and market equilibria—exhibit patterns detectable only from the intentional stance, and these patterns exert downward causal influence upon physical-stance events without being reducible to them.

(5) I reject both physicalist monism and emergent dualism: all three stances are explanatorily autonomous, none enjoys metaphysical priority, and pluralism about stances is correct.

Please examine these commitments for internal coherence. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. For each genuine tension, specify the least costly revision and what it sacrifices. Also indicate whether there is an apparent contradiction that dissolves once the distinction between explanatory autonomy and ontological reduction is properly observed. Finally, advise which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would resolve the greatest number of conflicts overall, and whether that revision forces me to abandon (5). Please respond in British English.
