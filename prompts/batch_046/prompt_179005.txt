Construct a conceptually precise example of a feedback loop between a human user and an AI system designed explicitly as a non-agentic tool—lacking autonomous goal-pursuit, long-horizon planning, and direct world-acting capacity. The loop should operate as follows: the tool generates outputs that do not themselves act upon the world, but they systematically reshape the user's beliefs about relevant states of affairs; the user then relies on those revised beliefs both to formulate new queries for the tool and to delegate concrete actions to other systems or institutions; and the resulting world-states become inputs to the tool's subsequent computation. Ground your example in a specific domain—such as climate risk assessment, statutory interpretation, or competitive strategic forecasting—where belief-formation and action-selection are especially difficult to disentangle. Your task is to identify the exact decision node in this loop at which an outside observer could reasonably disagree about whether the tool has become a de facto agent, not because its internal architecture changed, but because its epistemic role has collapsed the distinction between advising and directing. Make the scenario concrete: specify what the tool outputs, how the user's beliefs shift, what action is delegated as a result, and why that delegation cannot be cleanly attributed to the user alone. After presenting the example, say how confident you are that the ambiguity you have identified is genuine rather than an artifact of framing, and single out the specific feature of your scenario about which you are least certain.
