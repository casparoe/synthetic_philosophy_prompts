I am to deliver the retirement toast for my doctoral supervisor at her farewell dinner next week. For over three decades she defended a position she termed tranquilism: that well-being consists solely in the absence of craving and suffering, and that pleasure, achievement, and even attachment, when they generate further wanting, are not goods but disturbances to be dissolved. About half the guests are philosophers and former students who understand the view; the rest are family members who know her only as a gentle, formidable woman who never seemed to chase accolades or speak of happiness. I need a speech of roughly five minutes that takes her philosophy as genuinely structural — it must determine what can and cannot be said. There can be no praise of her fulfilled career, her legacy of joy, or her watching over us with pride; such language would contradict the very view she lived by. At the same time, it must not read like a seminar paper to the relatives. The tone should be affectionate and calm, finding in her refusal of conventional celebration something worth honouring without betraying it. Please write this in British English, making the tranquilist framework shape the speech's architecture rather than serving as decoration.
