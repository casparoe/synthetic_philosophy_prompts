Write a deadpan style-guide entry, approximately 500 words, for the fictional Journal of A-Temporal Historiography (established 2045). The entry, titled “Author Guidelines: The Reverse-Anachronism Protocol,” should present a rigorous peer-review framework requiring historians to evaluate all past societies exclusively by normative criteria those societies could not have possessed, and to penalize any reliance on contemporary moral or conceptual vocabulary. Maintain the deadpan, bureaucratic voice of an academic editorial policy throughout. The satire should implicitly land three standard objections to anti-presentism—(1) that the selection of “neutral” criteria is itself historically situated, (2) that intelligible description requires some shared conceptual framework, and (3) that the protocol’s enforcement mechanisms reproduce the very presentist power dynamics they claim to eliminate—without ever stating them as objections. Invent a specific, implausible grading rubric (e.g., a “Temporal Purity Index” with weighted sub-scores for lexical contamination and conceptual anachronism) and a brief sidebar on the journal’s annual “Sanitization Award” for the article that most thoroughly erases its own historical location. Do not ask for real citations, bibliographies, or references to specific living scholars; the piece must be fully intelligible without web access and should avoid any mention of named historical figures whose biographical details would need verification.
