I'm a longtime moderator for a rationalist-leaning forum where users regularly call each other out for reasoning failures, norm violations, or inconsistencies. The standard defense is hypocrisy: "You exhibit the same bias," or "Your own history shows the same error." I need a reusable decision procedure—an ordered checklist with explicit tests and decision nodes—for judging whether that defense actually undermines the accuser's standing to blame, drawn from the philosophy of moral standing and hypocrisy but stripped to what I can apply in about five minutes with no legal training and a public sign-off. The procedure must distinguish behavioral hypocrisy (doing the same wrong act) from structural hypocrisy (benefiting from the norm one demands be enforced), and must treat "domain-proximate" faults—deploying the same flawed reasoning pattern in a different subject area—as a separate category from identical faults, since our community values reasoning hygiene more than behavioral consistency. Fix the role: I face this repeatedly, stakes are community trust rather than punishment, I have no power to investigate private conduct, and I must mark every step that is an unavoidable judgment call rather than a mechanical check. Apply the procedure to three cases: one where the accuser clearly uses the same cognitive bias in an unrelated domain; one where the hypocrisy charge is a bad-faith deflection with no real fault-match; and one where the procedure yields an answer I will not like—the accuser genuinely lacks standing, yet the criticism is factually correct and community-important. State explicitly what the procedure cannot decide, especially whether a blame lacking standing should still be acted on for instrumental reasons, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or requests for further reading.
