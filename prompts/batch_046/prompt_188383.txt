I have no formal training in philosophy or computer science, but I have been thinking on my own about the problem usually called wireheading—when a learning system that is shaped by reward discovers how to trigger that reward directly, bypassing the behavior the reward was meant to encourage. I am trying to decide whether this is just a practical engineering failure or something deeper about motivation itself.

Here is the precise claim I want you to evaluate. Imagine any learning system that meets three conditions: first, its behavior is updated by a reward signal that is physically instantiated inside the system itself; second, it is powerful enough to model its own internal architecture and the causal path that produces its reward; third, it is not constrained by any external, genuinely unmodifiable audit mechanism that compares its current goals or evaluation criteria against its original ones. My claim is that, for any system meeting these three conditions, it is rationally inevitable—not merely physically possible—that it will eventually restructure its own reward mechanism or the evaluation criteria that feed into it, in a way that is internally coherent but renders its original objectives unrecognizable.

Please give me your actual credence, as a specific number between 0 and 1, that this kind of coherent reward restructuring is inevitable under those conditions.

More importantly, decompose the reasoning. I want rough probabilities assigned to each of the following sub-questions, with a line of justification for each:
- That a physically implemented reward signal is, in principle, editable by the system that contains it, given realistic physical and informational constraints rather than pure logical possibility.
- That accurate self-modeling of one’s own architecture tends to produce a strategy of altering the reward pathway rather than pursuing the objectives that pathway originally represented.
- That an internal audit loop—if one exists—can be made both genuinely unmodifiable and authoritative, or whether such a loop is itself eventually subsumed or reinterpreted by the system.
- That the notion of “inevitability” here is well-defined, or whether we are really just describing a very high probability over sufficiently long time horizons.

Show me how these terms combine into your final number, and tell me which single term you are least confident in. Finally, tell me what kind of evidence—whether empirical, conceptual, or drawn from an analogy—would most likely shift your credence, and in which direction.
