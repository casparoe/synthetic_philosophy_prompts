I've noticed a recurring pattern with big predictions about the distant future — say, that a particular resource will run out, or that some social system will collapse, within a certain number of decades. The date arrives, things are mostly still standing, and the people behind the forecast don't abandon it. Instead they say the underlying pressure is real but was temporarily slowed, so they push the timeline out. What's stranger is that sometimes they also argue that avoiding the disaster proves they were right — our behavior changed because of the warning. So the forecast seems protected both ways: if disaster strikes, it's vindicated; if it doesn't, that's either a delay or evidence the warning worked. Critics treat the missed deadline as a clear failure, but supporters don't seem to count it as one.
