I'm a phd student in genocide studies and I'm stuck trying to make my gut reactions about naming atrocities line up with any coherent principle, or figuring out which ones I have to toss. Here's where I land on some cases. The Armenian massacres of 1915: genocide, full stop, and when states avoid the word it feels like pure political cowardice, not a legal ambiguity. The Holodomor: I want to call it genocide, but I also feel the intent is split between instrumental starvation and neglect in a way that doesn't fit neatly into the UN Convention's narrower intent-to-destroy box, even if it matches Lemkin's original broader idea of destroying a group through systematic cultural and physical means.

Canada's residential school system: my intuition screams genocide, especially if we take seriously the cultural-destruction side that Lemkin originally included, but I also understand why international lawyers resist expanding the legal category that way, and honestly I think they're right in practice. Hiroshima and Nagasaki: clearly not genocide, since there's no identity-based group targeting, even though the scale is horrific. Now here's the hypothetical that's eating at me—imagine a regime that forcibly transfers every child from an ethnic minority into the dominant population, bans the language completely, and redistributes land and resources, but deliberately avoids physical killing. Under current international law that's not genocide, but under Lemkin's broader framing it obviously is. So I want to include it, yet I also want to keep Hiroshima out and preserve the legal distinction that seperates Rwanda from strategic bombing.

Is there a principle—maybe something like directed collective intentionality toward the dissolution of group existence as such—that saves all these verdicts, or am I forced to drop either the residential school case or the hypothetical? And what would the principle I end up with say about a case I haven't considered: a civil war where one faction targets civilians not because of ethnicity per se, but because they live in a strategic corridor, and the victims happen to be 90 percent one ethnic group?
