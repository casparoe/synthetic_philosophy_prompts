I am drafting an essay that attempts to locate a neglected middle ground between Humean regularity theories and anti-Humean necessitarian accounts of laws of nature. Below are my working notes. I would appreciate conceptual comments on accuracy, coherence, and whether the central framing misrepresents either tradition.

Notes:

1. The Humean holds that laws are nothing over and above the mosaic of actual regularities, distinguished only by their integration into an optimal system—one that balances simplicity, strength, and fit with observed phenomena. There is no modal glue binding cause to effect.

2. The anti-Humean, by contrast, typically treats laws as relations of necessitation holding among universals or properties in re. On this view, the law that F causes G is grounded in a second-order relation that makes it metaphysically impossible for F to occur without G, regardless of how the actual mosaic is arranged.

3. Most discussions frame the dispute as epistemological—whether we can observe necessary connection—versus metaphysical—whether such a connection exists. I want to shift the axis: rather than asking whether laws push events, ask whether they exhibit a specific kind of modal-directional stability.

4. I propose a view I call directional regularism. On this view, a law is a regularity that remains invariant not across all logically possible worlds, but only across those worlds in which the local arrow of causal dependency is preserved. In other words, laws are the patterns that survive counterfactual perturbation of initial conditions, provided the perturbation does not reverse the directional flow of dependency.

5. This is meant to preserve the Humean rejection of primitive modal forces, since the invariance is defined over patterns of events rather than over governing universals. Yet it also accommodates the anti-Humean intuition that laws are not merely accidental, because accidental generalizations typically fail under precisely this kind of directional perturbation.

Questions for comment:

- Does this framing conflate Hume's skeptical argument about our idea of necessary connection with a positive metaphysical claim about temporal asymmetry?
- Does directional regularism genuinely avoid anti-Humean commitments, or does the appeal to invariance across directional worlds implicitly require a modal primitive that a consistent Humean must reject?
- Are there well-known objections within the regularity tradition—perhaps concerning the systematization of accidental but directionally stable patterns—that I am overlooking?

Please address these notes directly. I am not asking for a bibliography, reading list, or citations to specific texts; I want conceptual criticism of the argument as sketched.
