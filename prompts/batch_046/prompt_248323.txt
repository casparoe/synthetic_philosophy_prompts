I'm working on a feature about how puzzles in decision theory and epistemology bleed into cosmology, and I found myself stuck on an extension of the Sleeping Beauty problem that seems to swallow first-person certainty whole. I was discussing it with a colleague over coffee—she studies self-locating belief, I cover the conceptual side of science—and she walked me through an argument that starts with the standard analysis but then runs away with itself. Here is the chain, laid out as I understand it:

(1) In the Sleeping Beauty problem, upon being awakened without knowing whether it is Monday or Tuesday, Beauty should assign credence one-third to Heads and two-thirds to Tails. The justification is that she occupies one of three equally likely centered worlds—Monday-Heads, Monday-Tails, or Tuesday-Tails—and updates on her temporal self-location within the experiment's protocol.

(2) This reasoning treats belief as partly self-locating: the content is not just "the coin landed heads," but "the coin landed heads and I am this particular awakening."

(3) But every empirical belief carries an implicit self-location. When I believe that water is boiling, I implicitly locate myself at the place and time of the boiling; the belief is centered.

(4) So if self-locating reasoning is rationally mandatory in the experimental protocol, consistency requires applying it wherever centered worlds are in play—including cosmological ones.

(5) Now suppose a multiverse or large-scale duplication generates N copies of me distributed across worlds, with exactly one copy experiencing mental state S. Upon finding myself in S, I should treat the N copies as a reference class analogous to the three awakenings. For arbitrarily large N, my credence that my experience is unique collapses toward zero, regardless of direct introspection.

(6) Therefore, accepting the standard Sleeping Beauty update commits us to radical skepticism about the uniqueness of our own conscious states whenever cosmological duplication is possible.

(7) The usual reply is that the experiment's protocol defines the relevant reference class, whereas cosmological copies fall outside it. But this boundary looks ad hoc: the coin-flip is just one causal mechanism among many that generate centered worlds.

(8) So either we reject the one-third/two-thirds split and return to one-half, paying the cost of abandoning self-locating belief as a rational update rule, or we keep it and accept that first-person certainty is globally fragile.

Where does this argument actually slip? Is there a principled way to confine self-locating reasoning to structured protocols without begging the question? And what does each escape route cost—does giving up (4) undermine the entire framework of centered worlds, or does giving up (7) require a metaphysics of reference classes that we do not have?
