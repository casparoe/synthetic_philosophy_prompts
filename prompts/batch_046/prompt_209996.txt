Write a short story centered on a research group investigating synthetic collective behavior in an artificial ecosystem composed of simple mobile agents. The governing charter of their institution mandates that every explanation be reducible to rules operating at the level of individual agents, explicitly denying independent causal status to macro-level patterns and treating any apparent downward influence as an artifact of incomplete micro-level description. The plot should focus on the lead researcher's observation that a collective migration pattern physically obstructs individual agents from accessing nutrient sources that their local behavioral rules would otherwise direct them to reach—an apparent case of downward causation rather than mere aggregation or supervenient correlation. Include a scene in which she presents these findings to the institute's review board, and have a reductionist colleague counter that the obstruction is fully explicable by undetected micro-level interactions, arguing that the macro-pattern supervenes on agent-level properties without adding causal power of its own. Conclude the narrative at the point where the board must decide whether to authorize further funding for studying the macro-level constraint as an irreducible causal factor, without resolving whether the institutional mandate of methodological reductionism can genuinely accommodate the phenomenon. Maintain the philosophical tension between emergent downward causation and reductionist explanation as an explicit, unresolved element of the story.
