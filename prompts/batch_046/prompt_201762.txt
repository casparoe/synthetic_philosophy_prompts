Write a deadpan internal memo, in the style of a Big Four consulting firm's ethics-compliance division, announcing a new Ground-Project Integrity Protocol. The memo should mandate that employees identify their non-negotiable ground projects and eliminate any excess deliberative steps—specifically, any instance of reasoning about whether to honor a commitment that involves one thought too many beyond immediate loyalty to the client. Frame this as an efficiency measure: reflective moral calculation is classified as integrity leakage, and staff are rated on their ability to act without intermediate justification. The satire should exaggerate the logic of Bernard Williams's critique of utilitarianism—treating personal integrity as a manageable corporate asset and the absence of reflective moral reasoning as a virtue—without naming Williams, citing texts, or breaking the bureaucratic voice. Target the practice of formalizing ethical commitments into audit metrics. Approximately 600 words.
