Compose a short, rigorous dialogue for a seminar on post-human identity. The interlocutors should be equally competent: one holds that personal identity can survive gradual, region-specific neural prosthesis interrupted by extended "integration pauses" in which no replacement occurs, the other holds that any such discontinuity in substrate and temporal binding fractures the phenomenological subject, regardless of later functional coherence. Center the exchange on a concrete case—prefrontal modules replaced over eighteen months while limbic structures remain biological, with periodic pauses—and require each speaker to respond directly to the other's arguments about narrative continuity, cross-temporal binding, and whether identity requires an unbroken substrate. Avoid strawman positions; neither should concede prematurely. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
