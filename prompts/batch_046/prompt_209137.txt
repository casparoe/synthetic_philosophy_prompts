A. It only establishes that thinking is occuring, with no commitment to a persisting subject (the Lichtenberg-style objection basically turned into a reading).
B. It proves right there the existence of a substantial mind that is distinct from body.
C. It's a direct intuition of one's own existence, not an inference, but it doesn't establish substantiality either.
D. It's implicitly syllogistic — everything that thinks exists, I think, so I exist — with the major premise just suppressed.
E. It's performative in a speech-act sense: uttering I think brings the speaker into being, and that's all the metaphysics it carries.

Then, seperately, rate from 1 to 5 how badly each reading gets hit by the circularity charge — that Descartes needs God to guarantee clear and distinct perceptions before he can trust the cogito.
