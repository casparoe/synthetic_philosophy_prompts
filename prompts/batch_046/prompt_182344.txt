I'm writing for the science and ideas desk and working with the instructor of an upper-level graduate seminar—about fifteen students in cognitive science and philosophy of mind—who has structured the course around Douglas Hofstadter's model of conceptual creativity as parametric variation, the idea that insight arises not from ex nihilo invention but from systematically adjusting the conceptual knobs—density, scale, emotional temperature, causal direction, temporal order—of an existing mental structure until a familiar framework produces an unfamiliar result. For the final take-home examination, students have forty-eight hours and should produce roughly one thousand words. I need an essay prompt that is unanswerable by summarizing Hofstadter's canonical examples; it must require them to operate the mechanism themselves. Ask them to analyze the historical transformation of the fugue form from Baroque practice through its twentieth-century reinvention as an explicit case of knob-twiddling: they should identify at least three distinct conceptual knobs that shift across this lineage, distinguish adjustments that remain internal to the form from adjustments that risk dissolving it into another genre entirely, and then confront the central tension—if creativity is nothing but turning knobs, the model seems to collapse profound structural innovation into a trivial settings change. The prompt should require them to resolve this by appealing to interaction effects among knobs rather than isolated parameter shifts, showing how Hofstadter's framework demands that multiple dimensions co-vary in a non-linear way to generate what he calls the cross-domain aha moment.

Please provide, together with the prompt, a model answer that demonstrates these precise moves—mechanistic identification applied to the fugue, the interaction-effect defense against the mere-tweak objection, and historical specificity—plus a marking scheme for the instructor. The scheme should award marks for correct deployment of the knob-twiddling mechanism, for the argument that interaction distinguishes transformation from tweak, and for accurate historical application, while explicitly deducting marks for answers that confuse knob-twiddling with analogical mapping, with evolutionary mutation, or with simple stylistic pastiche, and for answers that invoke Hofstadter's vocabulary without actually engaging the collapse objection. The difficulty should come from forcing students to show they can run the machinery, not just name it.
