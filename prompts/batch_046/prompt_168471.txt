I'm designing a decision tree for a graduate seminar on direct-democracy mechanisms—Swiss popular initiatives with cantonal-majority rules, Brexit's advisory-status ambiguity, and California ballot propositions that bundle fiscal and policy commitments. Rather than sorting positions by pro or anti direct democracy, I want the branches to turn on what I call compound-mandate distortion: the forcing of a single yes/no choice across logically separable dimensions (for example, sovereignty versus economic union, or taxation versus spending allocation). Please construct a taxonomy with four binary branching questions, each expressed as a concrete institutional-design dilemma rather than an abstract philosophical label. The splits should cover: (a) whether such bundling is an unavoidable feature of complex societies or an avoidable design failure; (b) whether the best remedy is ballot disaggregation, pre-vote deliberative mini-publics, or post-vote constitutional filtering; (c) whether Swiss-style cantonal majorities and California-style supermajority fiscal rules are legitimate correctives to compound mandates or illegitimate elite veto points; (d) whether Brexit's advisory framing represented a prudent safeguard or a democratic abdication. Each leaf should carry either a named position (if one exists in the literature) or the label unnamed, plus one sentence identifying its most awkward practical commitment—for instance, demanding that ordinary voters master disaggregated policy details, or allowing representative bodies to override clear popular signals. The tree should also flag any pairs of positions it cannot separate—where different philosophical justifications converge on identical institutional prescriptions—and indicate whether that convergence is conceptually stable or likely to fracture under pressure. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Finally, say explicitly what would change your assessment: identify the empirical finding or normative argument—say, about voter competence under disaggregation or about the intrinsic value of bundled mandates—that would cause you to reorder the tree, and flag the point in your reasoning most likely to fail.
