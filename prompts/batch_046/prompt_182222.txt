ok so i've been running with the view that Jeffrey conditionalization is the right way to handle uncertain evidence — like when you learn that A is more likely but don't observe a sharp event that forces a standard bayes update. It feels like it avoids the fake precision problem where you have to pretend your evidence came from a specific partion. But I'm wondering what should actually make me drop it. Not just the standard 'it violates rigidity' gripe, but concrete stuff: what if rigidity systematically breaks down in high-dimensional spaces? Or what if every case of 'uncertain' evidence can be decomposed into some hidden sharp update with a noisy channel? And is there a risk the view becomes unfalsifiable if defenders just weaken the rigidity constraint whenever there's pressure? I want an honest assessment — which defeaters are fatal, which are just awkward, and whether the position has been insulated too well.
