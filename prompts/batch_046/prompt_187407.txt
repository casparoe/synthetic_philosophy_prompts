At a philosophy of decision seminar I listened to Maya and Leo argue about whether coherence arguments genuinely justify expected utility maximization. (1) Maya said Dutch-book and money-pump arguments only show that incoherent agents are exploitable, and vulnerability to exploitation is a practical failure—like leaving a door unlocked—not a failure of rationality proper. (2) Leo replied that the belief analogy is stronger than she allows: just as contradictory beliefs violate the constitutive aim of belief, cyclical preferences violate the constitutive aim of preference, which is to furnish a stable ordering for choice; exploitation is merely the external symptom of that internal breakdown. (3) Maya pushed back that preferences lack any analog to truth—there is no fact of the matter that makes A better than B—so labeling preference loops “irrational” illegitimately imports epistemic normativity into practical reasoning. (4) Leo countered that even without a truth-aim, diachronic agency requires a non-cyclical ordering; if your preferences loop, you are not one persisting agent but a succession of temporary selves, and coherence is what binds them together. (5) Maya responded that this overstates coherence: people genuinely revise values, and enforcing consistency across time can be paternalistic, locking an earlier self’s commitments against a later self’s changed judgments, so dynamic consistency may suppress legitimate preference change rather than preserve agency. (6) Leo granted that revision is real but insisted it differs structurally from cyclical intransitivity: genuine change involves new reasons or information, whereas a loop returns to the start with no learning, which no defensible model of rational development can accommodate.

Please adjudicate this exchange move by move: for each of the six moves, rate it from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify the rating. Then say what, if anything, both parties are missing—particularly regarding whether coherence arguments establish expected utility maximization specifically rather than weaker ordering conditions, and whether Leo’s appeal to diachronic agency is circular or can be defended independently.
