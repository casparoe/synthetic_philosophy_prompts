Having spent my career in experimental physics, I now find myself examining whether moral philosophy permits quantitative comparison of historical catastrophes. To structure the inquiry, I propose evaluating any ranking of historical evils along four dimensions: commensurability of harm metrics, immunity to presentist bias, stability of the ordering under counterfactual adjustments to scale, and resistance to instrumentalization. Please rank these four dimensions in order of their importance for deciding whether such a comparison is conceptually coherent rather than obscene, assign each a weight so that the four sum to 1.0, and justify each weight in a single sentence. Then, applying those weights, rate on a scale of 1 to 10 the philosophical defensibility of comparing the mortality scale of the transatlantic slave trade with that of the Holocaust, identifying which dimension dominates your assessment and whether removing it collapses the judgment into mere offense.
