I am designing a diagnostic flowchart for an advanced philosophy-of-physics seminar and need a decision tree that sorts students by the actual logical commitments underlying their response to Bell’s theorem, rather than by the interpretation labels they have memorized. The tree should open with the empirical premise that correlations in well-conducted Bell-type experiments violate the CHSH bound, then proceed through three conceptual forks: first, whether joint probabilities factorize into products of local terms (factorizability); second, whether the measurement settings can be treated as statistically independent of any hidden variables (measurement independence, or “free choice”); and third, whether one insists on a single definite outcome for every measurement run (unique-outcome ontology). Each internal node should be phrased as a concrete question a student could answer without already knowing which interpretation it points to—for example, “May the hidden state associated with particle A depend on the analyzer setting chosen for particle B?”—and the leaves should include Bohmian mechanics, Everettian quantum mechanics, superdeterminist hidden-variable theories, retrocausal accounts, and QBist or relational interpretations. For each leaf, include the position’s name and exactly one sentence describing its most awkward commitment.

In addition to these standard branches, inject a distinguishing fork that asks whether the student separates operational locality—the laboratory-level no-signaling constraint—from ontological locality—the stronger claim that no causal influence, direct or mediated, connects distant beables—since this distinction often collapses interpretations that sound identical on simpler quizzes. The tree should also flag at least one logically possible but apparently unoccupied combination of answers—such as retaining factorizability, rejecting measurement independence, and insisting on unique outcomes—and briefly diagnose whether that gap reflects incoherence, empirical exclusion, or mere historical neglect. Finally, present the tree in nested form, conclude with an explicit note on which pairs of leaves the tree fails to separate cleanly, and keep the entire deliverable free of bibliographic references or citation requests, since it must remain usable for a reader without access to external sources.
