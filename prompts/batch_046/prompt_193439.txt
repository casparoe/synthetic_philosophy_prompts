I was discussng with friends in my moral psych course about how WEIRD populations are outliers in folk morality -- like the Henrich idea that Western church history made people hyper-focused on harm and fairness, while other cultures treat loyalty, authority, and purity as genuinely moral domains too. One friend said this kills moral objectivity dead: if morality was objective, every culture should agree, and since WEIRD folks are only about 12% of people they're clearly just a regional anomaly. I pushed back that maybe we should weight by population? If the global majority includes community and divinity ethics, then autonomy-only is the outlier bias, and the pluralist framework is closer to the objective truth. He said that's just democratic voting dressed up as philosophy.

Then another friend mentioned ontology identification -- like how in some cultures disrespect isn't the same kind of thing as physical harm, it's more like breaking a cosmic order or a social contract, so you can't compare moral answers across cultures without first figuring out what properties actually exist. I said okay, so we identify the ontology first, then check for convergence across cultures. But now I'm stuck: doesn't that just smuggle in the majority view as the 'real' ontology? And if we have to stand outside every cultural framework to do the identifying, isn't that impossible? Is population weighting totally irrelevant to objectivity? And can ontology identification actually solve the comparison problem, or does it just push the circularity back one level?
