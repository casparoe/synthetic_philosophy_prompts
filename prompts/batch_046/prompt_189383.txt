(1) You have two envelopes, L and R. One contains amount a and the other 2a, where a is drawn from a distribution that puts positive weight everywhere on (0, infinity) — like a uniform pryor over all positive values, if that makes sense.
(2) I pick L and open it; I see $100.
(3) By symetry, R is equally likely to hold $50 or $200, so the expected value of switching is $125.
(4) So I should switch.
(5) The same reasoning applies for whatever I see — $10, $10,000, whatever — so I should always switch without even opening.
(6) Now if I do switch to R, the situation is structurally identical: I hold one envelope and there's another one sitting there with either half or double.
(7) So I should switch back. Repeat this infinate loop.
(8) But an infinite loop of actions is impossible, and ought implies can, so there's got to be a flaw somewhere.
(9) The usual fix people mention is that the prior can't actually be uniform over all positives because the expectation diverges. But I want to keep the symetry idea, not just throw away the prior.
(10) Another option is to say that once I've opened L, my knowledge breaks the symetry: I know L contains $100, so R's distribution is conditonal on that, but after I switch I don't have the same conditonal information about R relative to L.
(11) Wait, that sounds right, but then step (3) wasn't really using pure symetry — it was smuggling in my epistemic state. If that's the case, then (5) is wrong, because without opening I have no observed value to condition on.
(12) So I could reject (5) and keep (3) for the case where I actually observed a value. But then I'm left with a strange result: I should switch after observing, but I shouldn't have opened in the first place? Or I should open, switch, and then just stop?
(13) Actually, if I open, see V, switch, then open the other and see V', shouldn't I apply (3) again? Yes, unless I claim that after the first switch I somehow "used up" my right to apply the reasoning.
(14) That "using up" idea sounds ad hoc. So maybe the real mistake is in (1): there just is no existant mathematical distribution over a that allows both envelopes to exist with that symetry. But then the paradox dies not because of reasoning but because the setup is impossible.

Where does this actually break? I feel like I'm going in circles, which is ironic given the loop I'm describing. I'd rather keep (3) intact than accept (14), but I'm not sure what I'm paying for that choice. Also I probably misspelled "ad hoc" somewhere up there, but you get the idea.
