Write a short dialogue between an Epicurean and a deprevationist about whether death is actually bad for the person who dies. Make them fight over a specific edge case: someone who gets cryogenicaly suspended for 500 years with zero inner life, then woken up. The Epicurean should argue that since there's no subject experiencing the gap, it can't be a harm — and that death works the same way. The deprevationist should reply that missing centuries of potential goods is a genuine loss even without a sufferer present, then apply that logic back to death. Both characters need to actually answer each other; don't let either become a strawman. Keep the whole thing focused and casual.
