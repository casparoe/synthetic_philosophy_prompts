Write a villanelle—nineteen lines in five tercets followed by a closing quatrain, with two refrains (A1 and A2) and one alternating rhyme (B), arranged A1 B A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 A2—about Amartya Sen’s liberal paradox. Do not treat the paradox as a mere name-check; render its logical anatomy precisely. Use the first refrain (A1) to state the minimal liberal principle: that each individual should possess decisive sovereignty over a purely private choice—say, whether to read a particular indecent novel—so that society defers to that person’s preference regardless of anyone else’s view. Use the second refrain (A2) to state the Pareto condition: that whenever every member of society prefers one social state to another, the collective ordering must mirror that unanimous preference. In the body, introduce two agents—call them the Prude and the Lewd—who hold diametrically opposed preferences over a single copy of the book: one judges it corrupting and wishes it destroyed unread; the other wishes to read it and derives satisfaction from forcing the other to do so. Structure the tercets so that the interlocking rhyme scheme physically enacts the cyclical dependency: each agent’s liberal right over his or her own action feeds back into the other’s domain, generating a chain of social rankings (x above z, z above y, hence x above y) that seems locally consistent but closes into contradiction globally. At the volta—located precisely in the turn between the fifth tercet and the final quatrain—make explicit that the two refrains, which initially appear as complementary safeguards of individual autonomy and collective rationality, are jointly unsatisfiable in this minimal two-person, one-book case. The closing couplet must name the exact logical cost: not a vague loss of harmony, but the necessary abandonment of either the minimal liberal right or the Pareto principle. The poem should be accurate enough that a social-choice theorist recognizes the specific formal structure—unrestricted domain, two decisive pairs assigned to the agents, transitivity of social preference, and the contradiction derived therefrom—rather than seeing a generic conflict between freedom and efficiency. Do not include footnotes, citations, bibliographic references, or appeals to sources outside the poem’s own logic.
