Take Sperry and Gazzaniga's split-brain patients from the 60s/70s — severed corpus callosum, left hand acting against the left brain's will, the left hemisphere confabulating explanations. Analyze it through Hofstadter's idea that an 'I' is a strange loop that can run on multiple substrates, like his claim that parts of Carol persisted inside him after she died. I dont want the generic "are there two people now?" take. Instead map the actual options each hemisphere faces, what counts as evidence for one loop versus two, and whether the left brain's confabulation is best read as a single distributed self trying to stay coherent across broken hardware, or as a new loop that never included the right side. Then say what this does to Hofstadter: does it show selfhood is genuinely shareable across brains, or does it reveal a hard fracture point where you just cant call it one 'I' anymore?
