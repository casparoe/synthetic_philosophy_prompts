There's this thing in clinical psychiatry where if a patient insists they have no body, or that the world was created five minutes ago, we classify it as a delusion and treat it. I'm curious what view of knowledge this practice presupposes, especially in light of Wittgenstein's hinge epistomology in On Certainty. Does it require that there's a special class of 'hinge' beliefs that are immune to doubt but aren't exactly known? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether the practice strictly needs it or is compatible with rival views, and tell me what psychiatric evaluation would have to change if we actually treated the clinician's own background commitments as open to the same kind of doubt.
