State the question precisely, distinguishing the object-level claim (that no algorithm outperforms others averaged uniformly over all targets) from the meta-level claim (that selecting that uniform averaging framework is itself justified). Explain why the standard literature leaves this open: some interpreters treat the theorems as dissolving inductive skepticism by showing that success requires problem-specific bias, while others dismiss the result as irrelevant to epistemology because it concerns computation rather than belief formation. Propose an approach that neither celebrates the theorems as solving induction nor ignores them: examine whether the uniform prior required for the theorems is methodologically neutral, or whether adopting it presupposes an inductive principle about the distribution of real-world problems, creating a regress in which induction is unavoidable at the meta-level. Give an honest scope assessment for six months, identifying the precise boundary where formal analysis of the theorems ends and normative epistemology begins, and explain why a foundation should fund philosophical rather than purely technical work on this boundary. Do not include a bibliography or citation list; refer to positions and arguments generally, and indicate where specific sources will be filled in during the project.
