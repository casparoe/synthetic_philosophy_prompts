I have been studying reports of split-brain patients—individuals whose corpus callosum has been severed—and in particular the familiar finding that the left hemisphere will fabricate explanations for actions that were in fact initiated by the isolated right hemisphere. What catches my attention is not the fabrication itself, but a consistent tonal feature of it: the invented account is rarely a neutral causal guess ("it looked familiar," "my hand moved that way"). Instead, it tends to arrive already moralized or normative. For instance, when the right hemisphere directs the left hand to select an object in response to a stimulus presented only to the left visual field, the patient—speaking from the left hemisphere—often explains the choice not as an unexplained impulse but as a deliberate, principled selection: "that is the correct tool," or "that is the fair option." In a different pattern, an emotional reaction triggered in the right hemisphere by an image shown only to that side is interpreted by the left hemisphere not as an unexplained feeling but as a reasoned evaluative judgment—"I am uneasy because that figure is untrustworthy." The interpreter, in other words, seems to be doing more than stitching together a coherent narrative; it appears to be justifying the reaction, converting a blind response into an act of conscience or practical reasoning.

Is there an established concept or term for this specific tendency—the interpreter's habit of framing confabulated explanations in normative, moral, or justificatory language rather than in merely descriptive or causal terms? How precisely should it be distinguished from the general interpreter-module phenomenon described in split-brain research: is it a separate cognitive mechanism, a stylistic bias of the same module, or perhaps an artifact of the experimental setting? If the literature offers no distinct label, how would you draw the boundary between ordinary coherence-seeking confabulation and this normative variety, and what term and definition would best capture the phenomenon?
