at a dev meetup last night two people got stuck arguing about Hofstadter's old take on statistical translation being shallow. mike said modern neural MT is totally diffrent — the embeddings capture real semantic structure through usage patterns, so calling it shallow is like saying a human brain is just shallow statistics. sara said no, its still pattern matching without grounding, and she brought up this test she calls backtranslation erosion: take a text loaded with cultural subtext, run it through six language pairs with an LLM, and watch the emotional valence collapse toward generic sentiment. she says if there was real understanding there'd be an anchor pulling it back, but instead it just drifts to the statistical mean. mike said thats just lossy compression, same as the telephone game with people, and humans drift too. sara said the key diffrence is humans have a contradiction detector tied to lived experience — when the translated version clashes with what they know, they correct it — but the model has nothing to clash against. mike said thats just defining understanding as having a body, which is chauvinistic. can you go through thier exchange move by move and say whos right about what? and is sara's erosion test actually doing real work, or is it just a fancy way of saying models accumulate errors?
