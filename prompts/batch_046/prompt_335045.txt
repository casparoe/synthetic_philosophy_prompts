A friend has reconstructed an argument from Émilie du Châtelet’s reflections on happiness that seems to prove rational agents must eliminate emotional attachment altogether. She lays it out as follows.

(1) Happiness requires both the satisfaction of desire and freedom from the pains that come from dependence on others.
(2) Intellectual study is the only desire that is fully self-sufficient: its objects are always available, and its satisfaction depends solely on one’s own mind.
(3) For every other-dependent desire—love, friendship, social esteem—there exists a structurally similar intellectual pursuit that can yield comparable satisfaction without the corresponding vulnerability.
(4) Rationality demands that whenever a desire can be replaced by a less vulnerable equivalent that yields comparable satisfaction, the replacement ought to be made.
(5) Therefore rationality requires substituting all other-dependent desires with study, to the maximum extent possible.
(6) If all other-dependent desires are substituted away, the rational agent retains no emotional attachments.
(7) But a life without emotional attachments is not happy, even on du Châtelet’s own view, which treats moderated attachment not as pure obstacle but as part of the conditions for a flourishing life.
(8) So rationality demands the elimination of conditions necessary for happiness, which is absurd.

She wants to preserve (1) and (2) because they capture du Châtelet’s core insight that self-sufficiency protects happiness, but she suspects the slide happens somewhere between (3) and (5). Where does the argument go wrong? Is there an equivocation in “comparable satisfaction,” a hidden quantifier shift from “some replaceable desires” to “all other-dependent desires,” or a mistaken premise about the fungibility of affective goods? If the flaw is in (4), what does giving it up cost—does it reduce rationality to mere risk-aversion, or does it leave du Châtelet with no principled defense against the passions she warns against? If the flaw lies elsewhere, name the exact step, explain why it looked plausible, and price the alternatives.
