Maya went first: if "I" is just a symbol that loops back to the system using it, then you can build it artificially. She said thats structurally identical to Hofstadter's loop, which means consciousness is just recursive aliasing done well, not something mysterious.

Leo said she was missing the absence part. He argued the loop isn't just self-pointing; it's self-pointing through a model that includes its own deletion. He called it a "negative-reference loop" - the symbol carries conditions for not-existing, like thinking "I won't be here tomorrow." Without that counterfactual layer, Leo said, you've only got feedback, not a self. Maya pushed back using Gödel-style self-reference: Gödel's sentence refers to itself without ever mentioning absence, so Leo's extra condition is just mysticism. Leo answered that Gödel's sentence is about provability in formal systems, not first-person subjectivity, and that "I" is indexed to a perspective that can't be observed from outside. He gave a database analogy: row-level security lets a query reference "the current user," but the reference resolves differently for every execution context. That's not flattenable aliasing, he said, it's context-dependent self-reference that has to stay dynamic.

Maya said thats still parameterization with extra steps. Leo insisted parameterization is static once inputs are fixed, but the "I" loop updates faster than the world-model around it, creating a lagging reference that never fully stabilizes. Maya asked if that meant the loop was broken by definition, and Leo said no - it means the loop is temporal, not logical. It only holds together across time, not in a single snapshot.
