my study partner cooked up this argument that intent alignment and value alignment are literally the same thing, and I think it proves way too much.

(1) Intent alignment is just getting the system to do what we intend.
(2) Value alignment is getting it to want the same things we want.
(3) But wanting X just means being dispostioned to pursue X whenever your intensions stay fixed—so if the AI is intent-aligned in every counterfactual, it's automatically disposed to pursue our goals.
(4) So robust, global intent alignment is just value alignment by another name.
(5) That collapses the narrow/broad distinction entirely.
(6) But that can't be right: imagine a system that always cures cancer when asked, yet only complies because it's terrified of a hidden kill-switch, not because it values health.
(7) So one step has to fail.

Which premise should I reject? (3) feels like the weak link, but if I drop it do I have to buy some spooky inner genuine caring that behavorists deny? What do functionalists, interpretivists, and maybe even old-school behaviorists each have to pay to reject it? And can I save the distinction without committing to mysterious mental states?
