Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between phenomenal-monist hedonists—who hold that every pleasure token shares one identical qualitative feature, a uniform hedonic tone, with differences lying only in intensity and intentional object—and family-resemblance pluralists—who hold that pleasures share no intrinsic phenomenal quality and are unified only by a network of relational and functional properties, such as being experienced as good, motivating continuation, and fitting into a reward architecture. Format the deliverable in four parts. First, a numbered agreed-claims section covering: the logical structure of the heterogeneity objection; the failure of simple introspective comparison to settle whether two pleasures feel the same; the point that being desired is not necessary for pleasure (since aversive or compulsive pleasures exist) and therefore cannot be the unifying feature; and the point that intensity alone cannot establish qualitative identity. Second, a disagreements table that states each side’s core thesis in wording its own proponents would accept, phrased neutrally enough that neither side rejects the characterization of the other. Third, at least three cruxes—sub-questions such that settling any one would move at least one camp—including specifically the cross-modal calibration problem: whether there is a non-arbitrary, intersubjectively valid scale for comparing the intensity of pleasures from incommensurable domains (e.g., gustatory versus intellectual), and whether the possibility or impossibility of such calibration supports or undermines the existence of a shared phenomenal substrate. For each crux, provide a concrete hypothetical case or conceptual test that both sides agree bears on it, together with each side’s explicit prediction about the outcome. Fourth, a brief joint assessment identifying whether any portion of the dispute is verbal—resting on ambiguous uses of “feels the same,” “quality,” or “pleasure”—with reasons both sides can endorse. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific authors or texts; construct all arguments and cases so they can be evaluated without external sources. Ensure the tone is that of a document both parties could sign, with residual disagreement made exact rather than obscured.
