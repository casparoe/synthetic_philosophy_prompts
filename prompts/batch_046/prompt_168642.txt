I'm giving the toast at my aunt's retirment dinner — she ran the state ethics board for two decades and made her name calling out hypocrisy in public officials, her whole brand was asking people who they thought they were. Last year it came out she inherited serious money through the same offshore loophole she used to blast. Half the table are lawyers and philosophers of blame who will catch any sloppy talk about standing; the other half are cousins and neighbors who just remember her as tough and fair. I need about four minutes. Please write the toast so it doesn't pretend she was spotless, but also doesn't turn her career into a one-liner about hypocracy. Use the idea that standing to blame isn't a personal purity score — it's about whether you're holding yourself inside the same rule you're enforcing — but twist it with this distinction: there's being in the mud with everyone else, and there's standing on the mud to point at them, and only the second actually kills your standing. Let that shape what I can and can't actually say about her, make it land for both groups without sounding like a seminar, and don't just decorate with philosophy.
