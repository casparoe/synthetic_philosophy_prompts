In my grad seminar on Cold War historiography we've been stuck on the same question for months: who actually started it? The traditionalists point to Stalin's refusal to allow free elections in Poland and the Berlin blockade; the revisionists point to the Truman Doctine and NSC-68 as provocation; and the post-revisionists say both sides misread eachothers defensive moves. We've read the same declassified cables, we all know about the 1947 Doctine and the Marshall Plan, yet nobody shifts. My hunch is that the stall isn't about missing arhives — it's that starting means different things to each camp. For the traditionalists it's about the first intentional breach of the Yalta agreements; for the revisionists it's about which power first created a structural condition that made conflict inevitable; and for the post-revisionists it's almost a semantic issue about misperception. So is this a real factual disagreement, a verbal dispute over blame applied to states, or are we actually asking three different questions — moral, causal, systemic — under one heading? And if your diagnosis is that it's conceptual or verbal, is there a distinction — maybe about counterfactual responsibility versus structural causation — that could unstick it, or should we just drop the who started it framing altogether? I want an honest diagnosis with reasons, not another survey of the three schools, plus an answer about whether anything could actually move this.
