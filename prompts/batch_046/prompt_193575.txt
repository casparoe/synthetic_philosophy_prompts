Please construct a conceptual argument for the following position: Otto Neurath’s program for a universal scientific picture-language (Bildersprache), particularly as embodied in his ISOTYPE system, is fundamentally incompatible with the strict physicalist criterion of meaningfulness he elsewhere endorsed, but not for the reasons usually discussed in Vienna Circle scholarship. Specifically, argue that the syntactic and translational rules required to map propositional content onto standardized pictograms constitute a meta-representational framework that cannot itself be rendered within the pictorial medium without either circular self-reference or an infinite regress of interpretive rules. From this, derive the conclusion that if Neurath wishes to maintain both physicalism and the autonomy of picture-language, he must treat the meta-rules of pictorial representation as belonging to a non-physical (or at least non-pictorial) domain of convention—thereby undermining the claim that all scientifically meaningful statements can be fully expressed in visual-spatial form. In developing this, distinguish carefully between the pedagogical function of ISOTYPE and its epistemological ambition as a universal notation, and address the strongest objection: that meta-rules can be learned implicitly through training rather than explicitly represented. Your argument should proceed conceptually; do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
