I am negotiating attribution for a project I led within a collective: I performed the design and implementation, yet the group supplied the infrastructure, the initial framing of the problem, and the social conditions that made the work legible and valuable. In my institutional culture, credit is treated as an individual property right, so I am asked to list myself as the sole author. When I do so, it feels false—not merely ungrateful, but ontologically wrong, as if I am treating myself as a self-complete agent who merely borrowed tools, rather than as someone whose capacities and identity were constituted through these relationships. Is the dispute simply an ethical question about fair division of recognition, or does framing it that way already presuppose a metaphysics of the person that the situation itself should call into question?
