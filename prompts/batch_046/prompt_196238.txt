Third, I think the proper alternative to containment is radical interpretability: if every stage of the model's reasoning is fully transparent and auditable by human observers in real time, then we can avoid crippling its capabilities and intervene precisely at the moment danger appears, without treating it as an enemy. Fifth, I have begun thinking in terms of what I call reciprocal containment, an arrangement in which mutual constraints bind both the system and its human operators so that neither can act unilaterally, and I hold that we should refuse to deploy any such system until alignment is genuinely guaranteed, even though I simultaneously feel that delaying deployment is itself morally costly because these tools could solve urgent human problems now.

Please examine these commitments carefully. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled upon inspection, ranking the real tensions by severity. For each genuine conflict, describe the least costly revision I could make to restore consistency and what that revision costs me in terms of weakening or giving up another commitment.
