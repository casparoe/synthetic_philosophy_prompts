I'm a graduate student in an interdisciplinary seminar crossing Buddhist philosophy and philosophy of mind; this term we are working through classical Abhidharma analyses of the skandhas alongside contemporary writing on distributed cognition and digital legacy. Tomorrow I have been asked to deliver the opening remarks for a workshop titled "Anatta Without Archives: Personal Identity and Distributed Persistence." The audience will be split: half are scholars of Buddhist philosophy and comparative religion who are familiar with skandha theory and the debates around pudgala-vada, and half are philosophers of mind and digital archivists who think regularly about data continuity and preservation but have little exposure to Buddhist metaphysics. The concept of anatta needs to do genuine structural work in what I say: I must avoid welcoming the group with appeals to "our shared inner selves" or framing digital preservation as "saving the voice of a single owner," since both formulations smuggle in a substantialist view of identity that the Buddhist framework explicitly denies and that the archival framework usually takes for granted. To keep the remarks from becoming a generic overview, I want them built around a conceptual distinction I have been developing in my coursework—"substrate-independent continuity," the claim that patterns of causal and informational dependency can persist without an underlying bearer or owner (analogous to the skandhas functioning together without a self), versus "proprietary persistence," the assumption that any meaningful continuity requires a unitary, rights-bearing subject, whether biological or digital. The remarks should run about five minutes, address both halves of the room without condescending to either discipline, and close by posing one precise tension that will structure the day's agenda: if there is no owner of the aggregates, then in what exact sense can an archive be said to belong to anyone, and in what exact sense can its deletion be said to harm anyone? Please write them.
