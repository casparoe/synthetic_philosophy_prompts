Consider the problem of moral attribution to artificial computational systems—not whether they possess consciousness in any phenomenological sense, but which structural or functional properties should ground the assignment of moral status or protective constraints. I am particularly interested in an under-explored distinction between attribution—the third-party judgment that a system merits moral consideration—and recognition—the system's own functional capacity to represent harm, benefit, or normative constraint, regardless of whether it experiences anything.
