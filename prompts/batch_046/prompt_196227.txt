So I've been running this seminar on consciousness as a user illusion — you know, Dennett's virtual-machine framing, Metzinger's self-model theory, all that — and there's this stall I keep hitting. The illusionists say there's no Cartesian theater, just a narrative center of gravity running in predicitive loops, and the anti-illusionists say that's like denying the sky is blue because you understand optics. Both sides have been saying this for decades and nobody moves an inch. My hunch is that part of it is verbal — 'illusion' means something different in cognitive science versus phenomenology — but I also think there's a hidden split: one camp is asking about the ontology of the self-model, the other about the phenomenolgy of presence, and both are calling it 'the hard problem' when they're not. Then there's this newer predictive-processing literature that frames consciousness as precision-weighted interoceptive inference, which feels like it's answering a totally different question about hierarchical Bayesian dynamics. So is this disagreement substantive, verbal, or are we actually smushing three separate debates under one label? Is there a hidden empirical question about global workspace architechture, or is it all bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for? And if the framing really is broken, what would count as progress — splitting the questions apart, finding a new experiment, or just dropping the 'user illusion' metaphor altogether?
