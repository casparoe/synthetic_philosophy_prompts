Consider the classical problem of Buridan's ass, but strip away the usual framing in terms of free will or deterministic causation. Focus instead on a precise conceptual tension: an agent is faced with two options, A and B, that are exactly identical in all respects relevant to evaluation—no hidden differences in expected utility, no temporal ordering, no side-constraints. The agent's decision architecture is fully transparent: there are no hidden variables, no unrepresented preferences, and no stochastic modules. Now examine three proposed resolutions. First, the agent introduces an infinitesimal random perturbation to break symmetry; second, the agent applies a lexicographic meta-rule (for example, choose left when indifferent); third, the agent simply waits, allowing the external environment to break the symmetry. For each resolution, identify which of the following three desiderata it sacrifices: (i) causal determinism without hidden states, (ii) rational transparency—the idea that every action can be justified by a reason available to the agent, and (iii) causal closure—the idea that the explanation of the action terminates within the agent's own reasoning process. Having done that, evaluate whether there is a coherent fourth position that treats the flat preference landscape not as a failure to be repaired but as revealing that choice in such cases is not comparative selection at all, but rather the spontaneous initiation of a trajectory that requires no comparative evaluation. Explain whether this fourth option genuinely resolves the tension or merely renames it, and clarify what it would mean for an agent's practical reasoning to operate without comparative evaluation.
