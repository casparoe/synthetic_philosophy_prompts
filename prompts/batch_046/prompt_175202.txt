People always say Gödel's rotating universe is like a merry-go-round: the whole thing spins, you ride long enough around the axis, and you end up back at the same moment in time, like stepping off where you got on. It's used to argue that general relativity proves time travel is just geometry, and even that Gödel showed real time is an illlusion since past and future loop together. How far does this hold? Give me the correspondance table -- what plays the horse, the platform, the axis, the kid watching from outside -- then the breaks. Does it smuggle in that you can just ride back to last Tuesday without ever breaking lightspeed locally? And what about the fact that Gödel's model needs a specific cosmological constant and a uniform dust distribution that our universe plainly doesn't have? Does the analogy survive that, or is it carrying the whole conclusion that time isn't real? Also Gödel tied this to a kind of Kant-style view that time is subjective -- does the carousel picture help that philosophical move or actually hurt it?
