Restate the problem of logical omniscience—where an ideally rational agent must believe all logical consequences of her beliefs—in the vocabulary of Bayesian belief networks and probabilistic inference, assuming fluency with graphical models and conditional probability. Treat logical entailment as deterministic conditional dependencies between nodes, and treat belief revision as propagation of probabilities through the network. For each central formulation of the problem—the normative closure principle, the computational explosion objection, and the distinction between implicit and explicit belief—say what translates cleanly into this framework, what requires distortion to fit, and what has no expression at all in terms of priors, posteriors, and graph structure. Then report whether the residue, particularly anything involving the normative ought of belief closure or the phenomenology of explicit recognition, points to a genuine limitation of probabilistic modeling or to an ambiguity in the original philosophical complaint that only looked like a unified problem.
