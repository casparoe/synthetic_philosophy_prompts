I would like you to write an essay addressing the following: if extrasensory perception were genuinely established—say, through a rigorously replicated finding that focused human attention can bias quantum-random number generators under complete sensory isolation—would this undermine Turing's broader claim that thinking is essentially computable? Consider whether such a result introduces a non-mechanistic element that no artificial system could replicate, or whether a mechanist could treat it as merely an unknown but ultimately physical process. Weigh the strongest arguments on each side, but do not remain neutral: reach and justify a specific conclusion about whether real ESP poses a serious philosophical threat to Turing's framework or only a biological curiosity that machines might eventually emulate.
