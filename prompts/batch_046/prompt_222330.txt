The three theories are: (1) a judgment theory, on which an emotion is essentially an evaluative judgment about one's situation (in the tradition of Nussbaum and Solomon); (2) a perceptual theory, on which an emotion is a direct perception of a value property belonging to an object or situation (in the tradition of Tappolet and Prinz); and (3) a feeling or body-awareness theory, on which an emotion is primarily the conscious registration of patterned bodily changes (in the James-Lange tradition). The required split is that both the judgment theory and the feeling theory deliver the verdict that the subject genuinely fears, while the perceptual theory delivers the verdict that the subject does not genuinely fear. Design the scenario so that this split falls out naturally from each theory's own commitments rather than being stipulated by fiat.
