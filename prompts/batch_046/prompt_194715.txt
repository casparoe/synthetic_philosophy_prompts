My closest friend and I have just finished a months-long argument about whether personal identity survives incremental neural replacement, and we want to formalize where we actually stand. Please draft the joint adversarial-collaboration document that a committed Gradualist Continuity theorist and a committed Substrate-Bound Essentialist could both sign in good faith, treating mind uploading not as an instantaneous scan-and-copy event but as a decade-long, incremental substitution of biological neurons with functionally isomorphic synthetic substrates.

The document should begin with a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the logical structure of diachronic identity, what current neuroscience permits regarding neural prosthesis and functional isomorphism, the conditions under which a system can report uninterrupted first-person experience, and what would count as a non-circular empirical observation in this domain. It should then present a disagreements table that states, in wording each camp would accept for its own position, exactly where they part ways: specifically on whether functional-psychological continuity is sufficient for identity preservation; on whether there exists a metaphysically non-arbitrary threshold during hybrid-state existence (for instance, when thirty percent of cortical architecture is synthetic and integrated over ten years); and on how to interpret the intermediate fusion scenario in which parallel cognitive streams temporarily share hybrid nodes before diverging into separate architectures.

Next, identify the cruxes—those sub-questions such that resolving them would genuinely move at least one party. For each crux, specify a concrete case, thought experiment, or hypothetical empirical result that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts. At minimum, include one crux about the hybrid-state report: if a subject undergoing progressive replacement consistently reports uninterrupted selfhood yet exhibits divergent memory-access patterns and altered emotional-valence mapping relative to pre-replacement baselines, does that support continuity or indicate the emergence of a successor subject? Include another crux about the fusion scenario itself: if two parallel streams temporarily co-occupy a shared hybrid substrate and then separate into distinct architectures, does either stream retain original identity, and on what criterion?

Finally, diagnose whether any portion of our disagreement is merely verbal—particularly regarding the terms "uploading," "replacement," and "copy"—and specify which portion and why. The answer should be written in American English, should avoid bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or appeals to obscure empirical results and highly specialized theoretical frameworks that depend on access to recent literature, and should inject the necessary conceptual specificity through the hybrid-state and fusion scenarios described above rather than through outside references.
