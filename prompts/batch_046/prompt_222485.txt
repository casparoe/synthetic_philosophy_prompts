I host a podcast aimed at longtime rationalist readers who care about epistemic hygiene and the design of reasoning practices. Next episode I am interviewing a philosopher of education who defends Alfred North Whitehead’s rhythmic model of learning—romance, precision, and generalization—alongside his warning against inert ideas that are received without being utilized or tested. She applies this framework specifically to the training of abstract reasoning in high-stakes domains, arguing that both progressive discovery-learning and rigid drill-based instruction miss the essential rhythm.

Draft eight to ten interview questions. Do not include generic biography or background questions. I want at least one question pressing whether the romance stage can be compressed or accelerated without destroying intrinsic motivation; one on how the precision stage avoids collapsing into mere repetition when the material is formal rather than manual; and one on whether the generalization stage genuinely resolves the tension between intellectual freedom and disciplinary constraint, or merely displaces it onto a new level. For each of these three, include a sharp follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give: that protected ignorance is essential to romance; that precision is disciplined play rather than rote drill; and that freedom is actually the product of prior constraint. Present the response as paired question-follow-up blocks, no more than one paragraph per pair, with the entire answer not exceeding four hundred words. Separate what is well-established in Whitehead’s own writings from interpretive claims that remain genuinely contested among scholars of his work, and do not present any contested reading as if it were the consensus view.
