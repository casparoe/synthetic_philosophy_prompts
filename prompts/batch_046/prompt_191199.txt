i want a villanelle for the closing of a philosophy seminar on hard choices and incommensurable values. the concept i want done structurally is that genuine incommensurability isn't just missing information — there's no hidden scale your missing — and that after you pick one good over another there's a rational remainder, a grief that wouldn't make sense if they were only close on the same metric. use two refrains: "no hidden scale" and "the grief is rational". let them interweave through the nineteen lines, passing each other without being weighed, and in the final couplet sit them side by side without resolving which is heavier. the speaker should be facing a real decision — say a doctor with one dose that could save a young patient with decades ahead or ease a older patient in extreme pain with little time left — but don't collapse it into QALY math or sentimental heroics. the point is that whichever choice happens, the other refrain stays intact. keep the rhyme scheme standard ABA ABA etc, and make it accurate enough that someone who gets the difference between incommensurability and mere uncertainty wouldn't wince. don't name philosophers, just render the idea in the form.
