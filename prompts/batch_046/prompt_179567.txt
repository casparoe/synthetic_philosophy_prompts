I was arguing with my roommate last night about the doomsday argument — he took a philosophy of science course last semester and always thinks he knows more than me. He summed it up like this: "The doomsday argument, first worked out by Brandon Carter in the 1980s and then made famous by John Leslie, is basically a Bayesian update on your birth rank. If you assume you're a randomly chosen observer from all humans who will ever exist, then being roughly the 100-billionth person is surprisingly early — like being in the first row of a theater when you expected a full house. Carter calculated there's a 95% chance humanity goes extinct before the year 7600, because if there were going to be trillions of people, you'd probably be much further back in line. The whole thing hinges on the Self-Sampling Assumption, which says you should treat yourself as a random draw from your referance class — in this case, all conscious observers in human history. Nick Bostrom tried to block it with the Self-Indication Assumption, which basically flips the reasoning: if there are more observers total, that's more opportunities for you to exist, so learning that you're alive should make you think there are more people coming, not fewer. My roommate said this means the doomsday argument is just a statistical parlor trick and Leslie was wrong to treat it as a real prediction about climate change or nuclear risk."

Also he threw in something about digital minds — he said if we eventually build a billion AI observers, then our reference class isn't just biological humans anymore, and being an early biological human actually becomes expected rather than surprising. He made it sound like philosophers treat this as a standard reply, but I've never heard it on any podcast or in any article I read. Is that a real extension people discuss, or did he just make it up? More generally, is his summary fair? I want you to go through it carefully and separate what's accurate, what's a common simplification, what's subtly misrepresented, and whether his conclusion that it's just a parlor trick actually survives once you fix the errors. In particular I'm suspicious about attributing the 7600 figure to Carter, about whether Bostrom really "flips" the reasoning in that simple way, and about whether widening the referance class to all conscious observers is legitimate. Please don't just say "it's complicated" — I want to know what a defender of the argument would object to in his account.
