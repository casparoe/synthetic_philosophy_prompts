Please argue that the correct answer to the democratic boundary problem—who belongs to the demos—is neither citizenship, territorial presence, nor the all-subjected-all-affected principle, but a functional criterion we might call reflexive epistemic membership. On this view, an individual belongs to the demos governing a collective decision if and only if that individual’s beliefs and preference orderings about the decision are systematically updated by the procedure’s outputs, creating a closed feedback loop between collective choice and individual cognition. This is meant to be stricter than mere causal affectedness, which sweeps in distant bystanders, and broader than legal citizenship, which includes permanently disengaged residents. Structure your argument in three parts. First, show why static boundary criteria fail to track the normative purpose of democratic inclusion, especially regarding future generations and cross-border externalities. Second, defend reflexive epistemic membership as the appropriate boundary: explain precisely what distinguishes a legitimate member from a mere observer, financial bettor, or causal dependent, and clarify why the loop must be epistemic rather than purely behavioral or economic. Third, answer two specific objections: that this collapses democratic membership into prediction-market participation, and that it unjustly excludes beings who lack the cognitive architecture to update on collective outcomes yet will still suffer them. Be formal about the demarcation conditions, and avoid relying on any specific named democratic theorist—reason from the structure of the feedback relation itself.
