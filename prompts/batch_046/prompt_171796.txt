Design an upper-level philosophy of mind exam essay (90 minutes, 40% of course grade, 1500–2000 words) on phenomenal versus access consciousness through the lens of global workspace theory. The prompt must embed a novel hypothetical—call it the “encrypted broadcast architecture”—in which phenomenal representations are globally broadcast across every cognitive module but are locked behind a cryptographic key held only by a single decoder subsystem, so they are available to that subsystem alone and remain unusable for reasoning, report, or behavioral control elsewhere. The exam question should require students to determine whether this architecture possesses phenomenal consciousness without access consciousness, access consciousness without phenomenal consciousness, or neither, and to defend their answer by engaging Block’s phenomenal-overflow argument and Dehaene’s conditions for global availability, rather than by summarizing either view. Provide: (1) the exam question itself, clearly worded and self-contained; (2) a model answer of roughly 800 words that demonstrates the specific conceptual moves required (e.g., distinguishing broadcast availability from usability for report and reasoning, applying the overflow argument to encrypted content, assessing whether encryption defeats global broadcasting in the relevant sense); and (3) a marking scheme that assigns credit for precise argumentative moves and lists specific mistakes that should cost marks (e.g., conflating access with reportability, assuming encryption eliminates phenomenal character, treating global broadcast as sufficient for access regardless of usability). Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; keep all references general enough that the task can be completed without web access.
