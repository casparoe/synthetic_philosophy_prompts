I'm a software engineer without formal philosophical training, but I've adopted a Stoic framework to manage work-related stress and decision-making. Before I treat it as coherent, I want an audit of whether my commitments hang together. Here is my position set:

(1) I apply the Stoic dichotomy of control strictly in engineering practice: my code, documentation, and peer-review feedback are within my control; server outages, user behavior, market adoption, and my manager's mood are not.
(2) I think the modern Stoic revival in tech culture is broadly correct: emotional resilience comes from refusing to treat deployment metrics, stock prices, or performance reviews as intrinsic goods.
(3) Despite (1), I hold that I bear genuine moral responsibility for foreseeable harms caused by misuse of APIs I design, even when that misuse is executed by users whose actions lie outside my system boundary.
(4) I classify career reputation and promotion as "preferred indifferents" — worth pursuing without emotional dependency — yet I actively allocate effort to personal branding, strategic networking, and visibility management.
(5) I also hold a somewhat niche view: in distributed sociotechnical systems, the boundary of "what is up to me" should be mapped at the team or organizational level rather than to individual intention alone, so Stoic discipline in software work is primarily a collective coordination problem, not a private mental exercise.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts dissolve on inspection, and what is the severity ranking? For each real tension, state the least costly revision and what it costs. In particular: does (5) undermine the individual boundary in (1); does (3) violate the control boundary in (1) in a way that (5) can or cannot resolve; and does the tension between (2) and (4) disappear once "preferred indifferents" are properly understood? Finally, identify the single revision that would clean up the most inconsistencies, and tell me whether my framework is salvageable without abandoning either Stoicism or moral accountability for system misuse.
