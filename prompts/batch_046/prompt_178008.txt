I am a high school senior taking an interdisciplinary philosophy and visual arts course, and we have just finished a unit on concept induction that centered on Bongard problems—the puzzles in which twelve geometric figures are arranged in two boxes of six, and the solver must identify the hidden rule that separates the left group from the right. My classmates and I spent most of our discussion on the logical underdetermination problem: with only six instances per side, infinitely many rules are consistent with the examples, so explicit hypothesis-testing seems unable to justify the single rule we intuitively grasp. However, my instructor introduced a more specific claim that has stayed with me: she suggested that human solvers do not typically generate and test explicit hypotheses at all, but instead perform a kind of analogical alignment, mapping relational structures across the two panels until a structural discrepancy becomes salient, which means the process is closer to perceptual pattern-completion than to logical induction.

For our closing session, I want to read a poem that takes this analogical-alignment view seriously rather than treating the puzzle as a straightforward exercise in rule-extraction. Please write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines each, followed by a three-line envoi—using the six end-words left, right, figure, align, rule, and exception, rotated according to the standard sestina pattern. The poem's movement between stanzas should mirror the back-and-forth comparison of the two panels, and the envoi should gather all six end-words into a final image that suggests the rule is not discovered as a discrete proposition but emerges from the alignment itself, leaving the problem of underdetermination quietly present rather than solved. The poem should be precise enough that someone familiar with Bongard problems and the analogical-mapping account recognizes the idea, yet it should stand on its own as a poem for readers who have never encountered either. I do not need citations or references—just the text.
