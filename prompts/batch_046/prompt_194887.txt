Write a short story about a bunker curator who must distribute a single dose of preservative across three concentric cylindrical containment rings in a circular vault. She has lost all records indicating which ring holds the only surviving copy of a particular philosophical manuscript, and she attempts to reason impartially by applying a principle of indifference to her ignorance. She parameterizes her uncertainty in three invariant ways—by radial distance from the center, by chord length connecting two random points within each ring, and by angular sector—only to find that each parameterization assigns a different probability to the manuscript being in the inner, middle, or outer ring, reproducing the ambiguity of continuous geometric probability. The story should trace her realization that her unbiased reasoning has multiplied rather than eliminated her ignorance, and should conclude with her making a non-probabilistic choice that acknowledges the specific structure of her epistemic position rather than resolving the paradox.
