I've recently been persuaded that Descartes's cogito establishes nothing more than the occurrence of a particular mental event with an irreducibly first-person structure — not the existence of a thinking substance, not a transparent self, and certainly not Cartesian dualism — and that this minimal result actually supports a phenomenological rather than metaphysical reading of the Meditations. But I want to test whether this reading is genuinely exposed or merely well-insulated. What would count against it? I do not mean the standard circularity objection or the familiar complaint about the slide to res cogitans; I mean which specific argumentative moves in the Second Meditation and in Descartes's Replies actually depend on substantialist assumptions that the minimal reading cannot accommodate, which contemporary results about indexical self-reference or about the transparency of mental content would genuinely threaten it, and whether any formal result shows that a purely event-based cogito collapses into either a logical tautology or back into substance metaphysics. Rank these defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Which ones would a committed defender of the minimal, event-based interpretation concede as genuinely damaging, and which could they absorb by refining the distinction between the occurrence of thinking and the existence of a thinker? Finally, is the insulation of this view — its ability to dismiss substantialist readings as overinterpretation — itself a philosophical virtue or a sign that the view has become unfalsifiable?
