I'm working with a small theatre group on a play about a real labor organizer from the 1930s who died in the 1970s—she's not famous, just someone whose papers sit in one local archieve. My cowriter wants a big climactic scene where she secretly reports a fellow organizer to the authorites to save her own career, then lives with crushing guilt for decades. There's no evidence she ever did anything remotely like that; her letters actually show she was kind of reckless about solidarity. But he says it makes the dramatic arc work, and since she's dead with no living relatives, there's literally no one who can be hurt by the lie. I keep feeling like inventing a moral failure is different from just fudging dates or merging two real people into one character—especially for an obscure figure, this play might become the main thing anyone ever remembers about her. But maybe I'm being overly precious about a dead stranger? Is there actually a meaningful ethical line between distorting facts and distorting someone's character, or is it all just storytelling once they're gone?
