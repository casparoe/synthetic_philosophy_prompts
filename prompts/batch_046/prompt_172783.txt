Richard Rorty died before the development of large language models capable of generating fluent philosophical prose. Imagine he was asked to assess a system that produces coherent arguments about justice, truth, and mind without holding beliefs, participating in conversation, or belonging to any community of inquiry—operating solely through statistical correlation across texts. Drawing on his antirepresentationalism and his view that vocabularies are tools for social coping rather than representations of an independent reality, how would he likely evaluate whether such outputs count as knowledge, redescription, or something else entirely? Would he treat them as harmless extensions of linguistic innovation, or would the absence of shared practice, vulnerability to revision, and the kind of ironic distance he valued make them a distinctive problem for neopragmatism? Reconstruct his position with attention to the difference between mimicking authoritative discourse and actually engaging in the social practices he took to ground justification.
