I'm currently taking an upper-division seminar on the philosophy of art, and for our final session on aesthetic paradoxes I want to close with a poem that captures the paradox of tragedy without resolving it. You are an expert philosopher-poet with deep familiarity with analytic aesthetics. Before composing, please take a moment to brainstorm how a villanelle's formal structure—specifically the way its two refrains alternate and are forced together in the final quatrain—can serve as a structural analogue to the paradox: the idea that we genuinely suffer with tragic art yet genuinely enjoy that suffering, particularly when understood through the lens of benign masochism, the psychological phenomenon in which negative affect is experienced as pleasurable precisely because it occurs within a protected, non-real frame. Once you have mapped this out, please compose a 19-line villanelle. One refrain should articulate the distance position—that our grief is mediated by awareness of fiction and therefore transformed; the other should articulate the immersion position—that the sorrow is real and unreduced. Let the form's inevitable convergence in the closing lines enact the philosophical stalemate rather than dissolve it. The poem should be conceptually precise enough that a fellow student of the paradox would recognize both positions and the tension between them, but it should also work as an independent lyric. Please avoid citations, bibliographic references, or appeals to named theorists; render the ideas directly through the imagery and the refrains, and begin with your brief brainstorming note.
