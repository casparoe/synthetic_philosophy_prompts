Do not provide a bibliography or cite external texts; I want an assessment of the logical and conceptual structure on its own terms.

Question 3 (30 points): Some metaphysicians defend origin essentialism about human beings: necessarily, you came from the particular sperm and egg that actually produced you. (a) State this view precisely, distinguishing it from the claim that your particular parents are essential to you. (b) Consider the case of monozygotic twinning: one zygote splits to produce two genetically identical individuals. Does this show that origin essentialism is false? Explain. (c) Now consider a hypothetical case of nuclear-transfer conception, in which an embryo is formed using the nuclear DNA from one egg but the cytoplasm and mitochondria from a different donor egg. Assess whether this case poses a distinct challenge to origin essentialism, and whether the essentialist can accommodate it by refining what counts as the origin.

Student answer:
"I think origin essentialism is the view that you have to come from the same parents, but actually the stronger version says it's the specific sperm and egg that matter, not just mom and dad. For part (b), twinning is a problem because both twins come from the exact same sperm and egg, but they're different people. So having that origin isn't enough to be you. The essentialist might say they share an origin at first and then split, but that just pushes the problem back: if origin fixes who you are, then at the moment before splitting they should be the same person, which is absurd. For part (c), nuclear-transfer conception means there are two different eggs involved—one for the nucleus, one for the cytoplasm. So there's no single origin egg, which means the essentialist can't say this particular egg is necessary. They'd have to say it's the nuclear egg, but that's just changing the rules to save the theory. Also, mutations happen during development, so your DNA changes over time; if your origin DNA is essential, then you stop being you every time a mutation occurs, which is clearly false. So origin essentialism fails both because of twinning and because genetic continuity isn't maintained anyway."
