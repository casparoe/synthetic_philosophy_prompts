I'm preparing my position paper for a seminar on constitutional democracy, and I want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I stand: first, judicial review is legitimate only as competence-boundary review—courts may police whether a legislature has exceeded its assigned constitutional powers or violated procedural regularity, but they ought not to second-guess the moral wisdom or distributive choices embedded in statutes. Second, I accept original public meaning as the authoritative source of constitutional content, though I recognize it often genuinely underdetermines outcomes in hard cases. Third, democratic legitimacy requires what I call distributive deference: on contested allocations of resources and social goods, final authority should rest with elected representatives, since there is no neutral epistemic standpoint from which unelected judges could correctly identify the just distribution. Fourth, courts may invalidate legislation that breaches participatory floor conditions—basic prerequisites for democratic citizenship, such as non-arbitrary disenfranchisement or systemic corruption—but I treat these as structural prerequisites rather than as substantive moral entitlements. Fifth, I want to maintain that socio-economic guarantees, including rights to basic shelter or healthcare, are genuinely constitutional, yet they should be enforced only through dialogic remedies—suspended declarations of invalidity that invite legislative response—rather than through direct coercive judicial command.

Do these commitments hang together? Please go through them pair by pair: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension specify the least costly revision and what it sacrifices. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Then tell me which single revision to my set would clean up the most problems at once, and what that unified fix costs me overall. I am not signaling which commitment I am willing to drop, so treat the audit as open-ended.
