If Ubuntu, understood as the quality of being a person (umuntu), is constituted entirely through relations rather than inhering in an individual substrate, then moral properties such as dignity and accountability seem to belong to the network rather than to discrete agents. On this reading, someone permanently isolated from all communal ties—say, through irreversible exile—would cease to possess full moral standing, and attributing blame to a single wrongdoer would be a category mistake, since the act was produced by the relational web rather than by the agent alone. Yet Ubuntu is frequently cited to defend both individual human rights and specific personal responsibility. Is there a coherent version of relational personhood that avoids treating the individual as a mere node in a network, or does the tradition necessarily collapse into either liberal individualism or metaphysical collectivism?
