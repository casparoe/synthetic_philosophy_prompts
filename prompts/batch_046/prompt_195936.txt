I am a retired physical chemist who, since leaving the laboratory, have developed a somewhat obsessive interest in the philosophy of historiography—specifically the problem of Eurocentrism and the ambitious claim that a genuinely global past might be reconstructed only if told, in some sense, from everywhere at once. To my scientific sensibility, trained to seek reproducible frames of reference, this aspiration seems both ethically compelling and methodologically vertiginous, particularly when it migrates from critical reflection into institutional practice. I have been trying to understand whether the dissolution of all narrative privilege produces a richer pluralism or merely a kind of disciplined incoherence, and I find that sustained irony is a more precise instrument for probing this tension than a straightforward essay.

Accordingly, I ask you to write a deadpan satirical memorandum—approximately 750 words, formatted in numbered clauses—issued by the fictional Institute for Non-Situated Historiography and announcing the full implementation of a newly devised protocol called omnidirectional temporal indexing. Under this protocol, every historical occurrence must be narrated simultaneously through every extant calendrical system, causal ontology, and linguistic register currently recognized by the institute, with an explicit prohibition on synthesizing these accounts into any unified chronology or hierarchical explanation. The target of the satire should be the bureaucratic and discursive style of global-history initiatives that attempt to eradicate Eurocentrism through procedural maximalism—multiplying incompatible frameworks and forbidding narrative authority—only to generate methodological paralysis that is then rebranded as radical inclusivity. The humor must arise from the logical extension of the institute's own premises, exaggerating real features of this discourse—such as the fetishization of perspectival equality and the avoidance of synthetic judgment—so that a specialist recognizes the critique as fair, while a general reader can follow the absurdity without prior knowledge of obscure theoretical texts. Please maintain an unwavering institutional voice, avoid all bibliographies, citations, and reading lists, and do not reference specific scholars or works in a way that requires verification; invent any necessary institutional figures and terminology within the text itself.
