I am a retired natural scientist who has recently taken up philosophy of mind and aesthetics, and I am planning a single ninety-minute session for a small reading group of fellow retired scientists—none with formal training in cognitive science—on the view, associated with Douglas Hofstadter, that creativity is fundamentally the systematic adjustment of parameters within a conceptual theme, with analogical variation functioning as the generative mechanism rather than a decorative afterthought. I do not want an explanation of the view itself; I want a teaching plan that specifies the exact order in which the components must be introduced so that the 'knob' metaphor avoids collapsing into vague hand-waving, identifies the single concrete example that should carry the session—my instinct is to use the historical shift from circular Copernican orbits to Keplerian ellipses via added epicycles, but I need you to judge whether that example works or to propose a replacement—maps the misconceptions this audience is likely to form (for instance, that analogy is merely loose comparison, or that the 'knobs' correspond to objective features of nature rather than mental representations), and indicates precisely where in the sequence each misconception should be intercepted. I also want a brief diagnostic question I can pose at the end to determine whether the group understands that variation is constitutive of the theme rather than external to it, together with an explicit statement of what portion of Hofstadter's broader framework should be deliberately omitted at this level and acknowledged as omitted. Finally, please push back plainly if this request rests on a confused premise—such as treating scientific model revision as an instance of aesthetic knob-twiddling in a way that distorts either domain—or if the task as posed cannot be executed well.
