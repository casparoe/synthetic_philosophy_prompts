Analyze the September 2007 run on Northern Rock through the lens of Bikhchandani-Hirshleifer-Welch information cascades and rational herding. Here are the facts to work with: after news broke that the lender had sought emergency liquidity from the Bank of England, depositors queued outside branches and withdrew funds en masse, despite the bank's eventual rescue and the existence of partial deposit insurance. Treat depositors as sequential agents with private signals about solvency and with observable actions—queuing, television images of queuing, and official announcements. First, lay out the cascade mechanics precisely: who observes whom, what separates private belief from the public signal, and at what point a rational agent should suppress her own information and join the run. Second, show where the standard model misdescribes or is silent: for instance, the observable "action" was largely mediated by broadcast media rather than direct neighbor observation; the Bank of England's liquidity announcement was fully public yet failed to arrest withdrawals; and depositors faced heterogeneous exposure because the insurance cap left large balances unprotected. Third, determine whether this was a false-information cascade (private signals were mostly sound but ignored), a rational herding equilibrium (the private signals genuinely pointed to distress), or a hybrid—and discuss whether the fact that Northern Rock was indeed insolvent undermines the usual framing of cascades as failures of information aggregation. Finally, evaluate whether injecting a distinction between action-cascades (responding to observed behavior) and narrative-cascades (responding to reported behavior) is a genuine refinement of the model or merely an ad hoc patch, and say whether the episode ultimately confirms, complicates, or breaks the BHW account.
