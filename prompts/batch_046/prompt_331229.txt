Quick question from a science writer trying to wrap my head around Jackson's Mary room. So Mary knows every physical fact about color vision—down to the neural correlates—then sees red for the first time. The usual line is she learns something non-physical. But I've read that Loar's phenominal concepts strategy says she just acquires a new concept for the same old physical fact, and the ability hypothesis says she just gains a skill, like learning to ride a bike. If both of those are on the table, then Mary's "new knowledge" is either conceptual or practical—neither of which threatens physicalism, right? So why do people still cite Mary's room as if it's an open wound for materialists? Is there a hidden premise I'm not seeing, or has the argument just been misremembered by journalists?
