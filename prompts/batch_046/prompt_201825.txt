I have been reflecting on my own, without any formal academic training, about an idea I encountered in popular philosophy: that the self is not a hidden inner substance but rather a centre of narrative gravity, a point where various accounts of a person converge in much the same way that physical bodies converge around a centre of gravity. Lately I have been drawn to a particular twist on this view, which I think of as the importance of counterfactual narrative anchors — those small, seemingly incidental details within a life story that, if altered, cause the entire moral and psychological evaluation of a person to shift, revealing that there is no stable core beneath the story. I facilitate a small discussion workshop at a local library for about ten adults of mixed backgrounds; we meet for ninety minutes, and I would like to design an activity that makes the group feel both the original idea and this twist through direct experience rather than through explanation. The exercise should be a structured deliberation or role-play with real, if modest, social stakes: participants should work in subgroups to reconstruct the biography of a fictional individual from fragmented written accounts, then collectively judge whether that individual bears responsibility for a specific harmful act. The rules must be arranged so that different subgroups hold conflicting versions of one key anchor event, and only when they compare accounts should they discover that their judgement of the person's character changes not because they have uncovered a hidden inner nature, but because the narrative centre itself has moved. Please provide the full design — materials using only paper and pens, the division of time across the ninety minutes, the precise rules and incentives, and a debrief that maps each moment of the activity onto the concept of narrative gravity and onto these counterfactual anchors. I also want you to say frankly what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and to be clear about which feature of the idea — likely the complete absence of any substrate independent of the narrative — the activity cannot reproduce and I must supply through my own commentary afterwards. Finally, I ask that you present all of this in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if you were writing a single, uninterrupted letter.
