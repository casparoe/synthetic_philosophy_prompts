I have no formal background in philosophy, but I have been thinking on my own about what people call "reactive attitudes"—the feelings like resentment, gratitude, and moral anger that we direct at others when they treat us well or badly. In an online discussion circle I follow, one longtime participant—let us call him Marcus—has over the past year offered clear verdicts on whether resentment or blame is appropriate in specific situations. I am trying to understand the principle he is running, and I want to predict what he would say about a new case without importing my own assumptions.

- When someone with severe, untreated schizophrenia harms a stranger during an acute psychotic episode: Marcus says moral blame is completely inappropriate, and our response should be treatment and protection rather than anger.

- When an adult raised in total isolation from any moral community takes food belonging to others: Marcus says we should not resent them initially, but should educate and integrate them; once they understand shared norms, future violations become blameworthy.

Now consider this new case. Knowing all this, they calmly and rationally cause harm to someone for calculated personal gain, feeling absolutely nothing before, during, or after.
