me and two friends (Maya and Leo) got into it at a bar about luminosity and the KK principle, not the pop-philosophy version but the actual epistemology weeds. I mostly just listened and drank my beer. Here was the back-and-forth:

Maya started by saying luminosity is dead because of the margin-for-error argument. If you genuinely know you're cold at time t, then in a room just barely warmer you'd still be cold but you wouldn't know it anymore, so there's no sharp boundry where first-order self-knowledge suddenly switches on. She said that kills KK too, since if you can't reliably track that you're knowing at the boundary, then knowing-that-you-know collapses into first-order knowing and falls with it.

Leo pushed back. He said she's running together two different failures. The margin argument targets first-order self-knowledge, sure, but KK is about second-order tracking. He said maybe you can know you're cold, and separately know you're cold, but still fail to know that you know — not because of vagueness at the boundry, but because second-order introspection needs a different kind of check that doesn't automatically come for free with first-order knowledge.

Maya replied that Leo's separation is artificial. If first-order knowledge already lacks a sharp boundary, then checking whether you know you're cold requires the same noisy introspection. So the failure propagates upward: KK falls for basically the same reason luminosity does, just one level up.

Leo then said wait, you're treating knowledge as binary. But what if self-knowledge is graded? If I have 0.85 credance I'm cold, I might lack binary knowledge, but I could still have accurate second-order tracking of that credence. So maybe luminosity fails in binary form but survives graded, and KK works differently for grades — you can know your credence level without knowing you have binary knowledge.

Maya said that's just relabeling the problem. Even with grades, second-order tracking is noisy. I might have 0.85 credance I'm cold, but my meta-credance about that 0.85 could be 0.6 or 0.9 depending on attentional noise. So graded luminosity fails too, and KK doesn't get a free pass just because we switched to degrees.

Leo answered that she's now smuggling empirical cognitive psychology into a normative epistemological principle. The KK principle is about what's rationally available in principle, not about actual human attentional noise. So maybe perfect graded self-knowledge is possible in principle, even if we never achieve it.
