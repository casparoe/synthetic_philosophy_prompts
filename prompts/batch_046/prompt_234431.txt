I'm running a small philosophy-of-mind reading group, and our final session lands on the intersection of the KK principle with Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument — specifically, the idea that if knowing were luminous, then whenever you know p you are in a position to know that you know p, which would require detecting imperceptible gradations (a room warming by fractions of a degree, a cold sensation dulling below notice). Our discussion didn't settle on the standard reading; instead, we kept circling a more granular framing that one member introduced — call it the resolution problem — that the failure might not show knowledge itself is non-luminous, but only that first-person access operates at a coarser "epistemic resolution" than the world's increments. For our closing, I'd like a poem that takes this specific tension — luminosity versus resolution — and leaves it open structurally rather than resolving it in favor of either side.

Please write a Petrarchan sonnet, 14 lines in iambic pentameter, with an ABBAABBA rhyme scheme in the octave and CDECDE in the sestet. The octave should enact the recursive transparency promised by KK: each line should feel like it is knowing its own knowing, building self-illuminating layers that seem to require no external detector. At the volta, line 9, the poem should pivot not by announcing an objection but by introducing the imperceptible drift — temperature rising below the threshold of awareness, the body unable to report whether it is still cold. The sestet should hold the stall: ask whether the drift dissolves knowledge itself or only reveals that our introspective grain is too coarse to track it. Do not resolve this. The rhyme and meter should carry conceptual weight — perhaps the returning B-rhymes in the octave echoing the repetition of "knowing that I know," while the looser sestet rhymes track the dissolution of that certainty.

After the poem, include a section titled "Notes on the formal choices" (approximately 150–200 words). In it, explain how the sonnet's volta maps onto the epistemological pivot, whether iambic pentameter helps or hinders the recursive rhythm of KK, and why leaving the sestet unresolved is appropriate given the state of the debate.
