Write a proposal for a philosophy term paper of about 6000 words, to be completed in one semester, on whether the surplus value of knowledge over true belief—the Meno problem—can be explained not primarily by the reliability of the belief-forming process or by the agent’s cognitive achievement, but by what I will call doxastic integration: the structural embedding of a belief within a network of supporting, extending, and defensible commitments. State the precise question: holding reliability constant, does knowledge still carry greater value than an “epistemically orphaned” true belief—one formed through a reliable mechanism but isolated from the agent’s other beliefs, unable to be defended, extended, or deployed in reasoning—and if so, is that surplus best accounted for by integration rather than by justification? Explain why standard reliabilist and virtue-theoretic answers leave this open: they locate value in the origin of the belief or in the agent’s success, but do not clearly distinguish a well-integrated knowledge state from a genuinely reliable yet doxastically orphaned true belief. Outline the method: construct a taxonomy of orphaned true beliefs (for example, accurate perceptual flashes lacking conceptual framing, reliable testimony received without comprehension, correct guesses with no evidential basis); formalize integration without collapsing it into full-blown coherence; and compare paired cases in which reliability is held fixed but integration varies. Specify what a successful result would look like (integration explains the value surplus independently of standard justification) and what would count as failure (integration collapses into justification, or our value intuitions fail to track integration). Give an honest assessment of why this is tractable for one semester and where it risks becoming too large. In your response, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
