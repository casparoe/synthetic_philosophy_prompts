Write a one-page prospectus for a three-month solo research fellowship on the bootstrapping problem and easy knowledge. State the precise question: if one retains the Moorean anchor that one currently has hands and is not radically deceived, but denies that this knowledge transfers evidential weight linearly across arbitrary inferential distance, can one still derive substantive external-world knowledge without circular bootstrapping? Frame the investigation around a specific, non-standard formal constraint: an evidential structure in which justification decays non-linearly with inferential distance and in which self-verifying propositions (such as “I am not a brain-in-vat”) carry only regulative rather than constitutive weight. Explain why the standard responses—denying closure, denying easy knowledge outright, or adopting contextualist semantics for “knows”—do not settle the structural issue: they either concede too much to skepticism or preserve closure in a way that makes bootstrapping inevitable. Describe the proposed conceptual approach: construct a minimal normative model with two adjustable parameters (anchor strength and decay rate), apply it to two test cases (a standard perceptual inference and the rejection of a skeptical hypothesis), and derive the exact boundary conditions under which inference is legitimate versus circular. Specify what a useful result looks like (a precise parameter boundary separating permissible inference from bootstrapping), what failure looks like (the model either permits bootstrapping for all distant inferences or blocks all non-immediate knowledge), and why this must be a philosophical rather than empirical cognitive-science project, given that the dispute concerns the normative architecture of justification rather than the psychology of belief formation. Keep the scope realistic for a single researcher with no archive or laboratory access: one formal framework, two applied cases, and a short argumentative essay defending the boundary conditions, with citations to be filled in later.
