Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a Weilian anti-partyist and a democratic-pluralist institutionalist on Simone Weil's critique of political parties. The anti-partyist accepts Weil's claim that parties structurally manufacture collective passion and suppress the individual's capacity for justice; the institutionalist accepts that parties risk this but denies it is structurally inevitable. Produce: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about democratic representation, collective decision-making, and the psychological effects of partisan affiliation; (2) exact disagreement statements in neutral wording that each side would sign for its own view, particularly on whether partisan loyalty necessarily entails what Weil called gravity (pesanteur) in collective bodies; (3) the cruxes—first, whether cognitive capture, defined here as the gradient suppression of counter-arguments within party discourse rather than binary membership, is an institutional constant or a correctable design feature; second, whether individual moral attention can survive any non-trivial degree of partisan commitment; (4) for each crux, one concrete institutional mechanism or historical episode both sides accept as relevant, with each side's explicit prediction of what it demonstrates. Format the output as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Do not include reading lists, citations, or bibliographies. Write in American English.
