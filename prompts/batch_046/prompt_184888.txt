I'm working through my commitments on epistemic disagreement for a small rationalist reading group, and I want a consistency check before I present. Here's where I stand:

(1) I take Aumann's agreement result literally: given common priors and common knowledge of posteriors, rational agents cannot agree to disagree about any event.
(2) I think this carries normative weight—if two agents genuinely share priors and have transparent, common-knowledge reasoning, persistent disagreement is irrational.
(3) I think almost all human disagreement fails to be Aumann-type because we lack both shared priors and genuine common knowledge of each other's reasoning.
(4) I think we should design multi-agent AI systems with shared priors and transparent reasoning specifically to enforce Aumann-style convergence and avoid coordination failures.
(5) I also think that in self-locating cases—e.g., whether I am a Boltzmann brain—agents with identical evidence and shared priors can rationally maintain different credences, because indexical information breaks the symmetry that Aumann's proof requires.

Which pairs are in real tension, which only look like it, and what's the least costly revision? In particular, does (5) undermine (2) or (4), or can it be quarantined? And does (3) make (2) an empty normative claim? Rank the tensions by severity and say what each fix costs.
