In a debate about whether anyone ever truly deserves blame, my interlocutor kept insisting that “true moral responsibility requires being causa sui” and therefore “no one is the true cause of themselves.” He used this not only to reject blame for innate traits, but also to reject blame for carefully cultivated habits: even the sustained effort to reshape one’s character, he said, is itself caused by prior factors the agent never chose. I want to disentangle what is actually being claimed. Can you lay out the distinct precise theses that this slogan might express — for instance, a conceptual claim about the necessary conditions for desert, an empirical claim about determinism, a normative claim about the scope of appropriate praise and blame, or a metaphysical claim about the impossibility of self-creation? For each, say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and specify whether it actually undermines blame for cultivated-character actions as well as for innate ones. Then identify which reading best fits my interlocutor’s specific use against cultivated-character blame, whether that is the same reading Galen Strawson relies on in his Basic Argument, and whether Strawson’s regress succeeds on that reading or requires a different one. End with a line beginning Verdict: followed by the single thesis that, if true, would genuinely defeat all moral blame, or “none” if no reading of the slogan can bear that weight.
