As a retired experimental physicist who has lately taken up moral philosophy, I have been trying to understand the so-called responsibility gap for autonomous systems. I have constructed the following argument, but it seems to prove far too much, and I cannot locate the precise error.

(1) Moral responsibility requires that an agent’s volition be the proximate and determinative cause of the outcome in question.
(2) In an autonomous weapon system guided by a deep neural network, the specific targeting decision emerges from weights adjusted through stochastic gradient descent during training; no human volition determines that particular decision at the moment of execution.
(3) Therefore, no human agent is morally responsible for the system’s lethal actions.
(4) The same structural feature—causal contribution dispersed across a temporally extended, non-transparent optimisation process—characterises ordinary scientific research, where a result depends on instruments, statistical protocols, and prior theoretical commitments that the individual researcher does not volitionally control at the point of discovery.
(5) Hence, by parity of reasoning, no individual scientist bears moral responsibility for erroneous or harmful findings that emerge from such distributed processes.
(6) Yet we routinely hold researchers accountable for negligence in experimental design or data interpretation.
(7) Consequently, either (1) is false and responsibility does not require proximate volitional determination, or we must accept that moral responsibility is systematically illusory in all complex socio-technical practices.

The conclusion in (7) strikes me as untenable, but I should like to preserve (3) if possible, since the epistemic opacity of gradient-descent architectures seems genuinely different from ordinary tool use. Which step should I reject? If I reject (4), am I not committed to an arbitrary distinction between algorithmic and instrumental opacity? If I reject (1), what account of responsibility survives, and does it not collapse the distinction between autonomous systems and conventional weapons? Please answer in British English, and explain precisely what rejecting your chosen step costs in terms of the broader debate on responsibility gaps.
