Write a deadpan parody of a software Terms of Service agreement, approximately 700 words, formatted as numbered clauses in formal corporate legal prose. The fictional company is "Propositional Unity, Inc.," a cloud service that lets users assemble propositions by selecting terms and applying relations. The satire targets Bradley's regress and the problem of the unity of the proposition, especially the argument that treating relations as terms generates an infinite regress of further relations needed to bind each relation to its relata, and Russell's reply that logical form can unify terms without itself being a relating term.

The agreement must include the following, all rendered in straight-faced legal language with no winking or explanation of the joke:

1. A "Definitions" clause that distinguishes Terms (substantive entities that may be related) from Relations (the service's binding products).

2. A "Standard Binding Package" clause stating that every applied relation automatically generates a Binding Requirement that relates the relation to each term; disclose that each Binding Requirement is itself classified as a Term under the agreement, thereby requiring its own Relation, with a fee schedule that compounds exponentially.

3. A "Russell Premium Tier" clause advertising "Logical Form" as a non-Term binding mechanism that halts the regress; in the fine print, reveal that activating Logical Form requires accepting a "Form-Application Relation" that connects the form to the terms, which is billed as a separate, non-waivable Relation subject to the same Binding Requirement.

4. A clause distinguishing "External Relations" (terms remain unchanged, but binding fees are doubled) and "Internal Relations" (terms are modified by relating, which voids their original identity and requires new terms to be registered).

5. A "Refund and Continuity" clause stating that if a user's proposition collapses into an infinite regress, no refund is issued because "the proposition was never successfully unified"; include an invented quote from a spokesperson for "Neo-Hegelian Synthesis LLC" complaining that the service mischaracterizes internal relations.

6. A "User Responsibility" clause placing liability on the user for any "ontological overflow" caused by unchecked regress, with arbitration governed by the laws of a jurisdiction named after an invented metaphysician.

The humor must come from the metaphysical accuracy: the clauses should correctly reflect Bradley's concern that relations cannot be real without destroying the unity they are meant to establish, and Russell's attempt to solve this by treating logical form as something other than a term. The piece should be readable to someone unfamiliar with the debate—recognizable as absurd corporate fine print—while being recognizably fair and precise to a specialist. Do not cite specific texts, page numbers, or scholars by name except "Bradley" and "Russell" in passing; do not include bibliographies or reading lists; avoid verbatim quotations from real philosophical texts—paraphrase without quotation marks, and use invented quotes only for fictional characters. Maintain deadpan internal consistency throughout; do not break character to explain the philosophy.
