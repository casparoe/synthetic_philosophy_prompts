Patients with pain asymbolia report vivid sensory qualities—sharpness, burning, pressure—yet exhibit neither avoidance behavior nor emotional distress, and often deny that anything is wrong. From this, it seems to follow that the "badness" of pain resides entirely in its affective-motivational component, and that the sensory profile itself is intrinsically neutral, like seeing red without caring. If so, then a hypothetical being with the identical sensory profile but a constitutionally severed affective pathway wouldn't be harmed at all; there'd be no suffering to relieve. But doesn't this argument confuse the failure of a motivational link with proof that the linked sensation was never harmful? And if the sensory report in asymbolia is genuinely of pain, on what grounds can we declare its content harmless without presupposing exactly the separation we're trying to establish?
