I hold that in anthropic reasoning we should prefer self-indication over Bostrom's self-sampling assumption, which resolves the presumptuous philosopher problem: being an observer capable of cosmological reflection is direct indexical evidence for conditions that produce such observers, not for vastly larger total populations. I now want to defend a refinement—call it weighted self-indication—where the indexical weight of an observer class scales with its "epistemic accessibility," so that observers who can easily reason about cosmic scale count more heavily in the reference class, but precisely because they are rare, this should suppress rather than inflate estimates of total civilization size. What would count against this weighted view? I want the arguments and observations that should genuinely move me—whether they come from standard objections to SIA, from sampling-bias analogies in evolutionary biology, or from internal tensions in defining the reference class—ranked from decisive to merely awkward. Does the weighting scheme risk collapsing back into SSA? Does it generate any predictions about observer distributions that could turn out wrong, or has it been qualified into unfalsifiability? And if it is well insulated by adjustable parameters, is that insulation a virtue or a defect for an anthropic principle?
