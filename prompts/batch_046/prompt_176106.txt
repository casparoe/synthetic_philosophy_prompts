I’m working on a paper for a seminar on comparative national mythology, and last week we argued about how defeated communities become moral templates for later states. A classmate kept invoking the Israeli motto Masada will not fall again — or at least the standard English rendering — to shut down a discussion about whether a particular withdrawal or compromise in defensive strategy could ever be permissible. He treated it as an empirical prediction: because the ancient defenders chose death over surrender, modern policy must never allow conditions that could replicate that siege. Another student countered that it is purely commemorative, a ritual vow with no operational content. I think both readings miss the work the slogan is actually doing in the argument, but I want to be precise rather than vague.

Can you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan could express, arranged as a menu rather than a single interpretation? I want at least four: an empirical prediction about military vulnerability; a commemorative vow directed at collective memory; an identitarian claim about who “we” are constituted by refusing a specific historical branch; and a more specific version of that last one — call it a counterfactual binding — which holds that present national identity is not merely inspired by Masada but is actively constituted by treating the defenders’ refusal to negotiate as a permanently open obligation that rules out certain present actions. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and indicate whether it actually supports using the slogan to justify a particular military posture or to silence dissent about strategy. In particular, tell me which reading is being invoked when someone uses the line to end an argument about whether a withdrawal is permissible. Finally, please say what would change your mind: which interpretation of the historical fall of Masada — for instance, whether the mass death was collective suicide, whether Josephus’s account is reliable, or whether the site had strategic relevance in 1948 — would undermine the identitarian or counterfactual-binding reading, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail if the slogan is being deployed in a rhetorical context I have not specified here.
