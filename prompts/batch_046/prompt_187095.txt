Compare Hofstadter's notion of sphexishness—the mechanically rigid behavioural repertoire of the sphex wasp—with the idea of "weight-frozen determinism" in large language models, in which fixed parameters generate apparently flexible outputs across contexts. To what extent does the analogy between the wasp's stereotyped action sequences and a model's deterministic token-generation illuminate, or obscure, the philosophical claim that mechanical rigidity is incompatible with free will? In particular, assess whether the model's flexibility is merely combinatorial, and whether that distinction undermines the analogy's relevance to debates about autonomy and choice.
