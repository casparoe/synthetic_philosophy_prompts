I serve as a procedural referee for a municipal Voluntary Exchange Oversight Office that reviews citizen petitions to intervene in accumulated holdings—progressive caps, levies, or redistributions justified by pattern-based ideals such as equality, need, or desert. Our charter requires that every decision be justified solely through an entitlement framework: justice in acquisition, justice in transfer, and rectification of past injustice in holdings, with the Wilt Chamberlain-style objection always available (that repeated voluntary payments to a popular figure do not, by themselves, generate a distributive wrong). Please design a reusable decision procedure my office can run on any petition: the ordered diagnostic questions; what counts as a sufficient answer to each; the stopping points where the procedure yields a clear rejection or approval; the escalation points where judgment cannot be eliminated because the issue is one of values or historical interpretation rather than entitlement facts; and the specific tests that distinguish a legitimate rectification claim from a disguised pattern-based correction. Apply the procedure to three petitions: (a) a proposal to tax high-accumulators of community solar-energy credits to fund universal basic energy allowances; (b) a proposal to void all trades of a particular digital-art token once it is discovered that the original minter acquired the underlying computational resources through a fraudulent contract; (c) a proposal to redistribute "attention credits" in a local media cooperative after a well-known contributor receives disproportionate voluntary donations. Include one case in which the procedure yields an answer the petitioner will strongly dislike, and state precisely where the procedure will misfire, where it requires human sign-off, and what it cannot settle about non-rival goods or mediated voluntary payments.
