I've been trying to map out my view on Confucian role ethics for a seminar and want a consistency check before I present. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) Moral obligations aren't grounded in universal principles; they're constituted by one's social roles — parent, child, ruler, subject, etc. The role doesn't just constrain action, it actually generates the obligation.

(2) But roles can be "hollowed out." If a father abandons his child, the child doesn't owe full xiao (filial piety). Still, they owe some "residual recognition" — not full ren, but not zero either. The role is damaged, not erased.

(3) Ren is role-indexed: you owe more benevolence to family than strangers, and that's not mere partiality, it's the correct structure of moral attention. Strangers still get baseline consideration.

(4) Li (ritual) should be read as embodied moral cognition — performing the ritual correctly literally reshapes your emotional dispositions through repetition.

(5) But if ritual becomes purely performative — people going through motions with zero emotional engagement — it has no moral authority whatsoever. There's no hidden value in empty form.

(6) In extreme cases, universal human dignity can override role obligations. If a ruler orders you to kill an innocent, loyalty is just void.

(7) There's no metaphysical self behind these roles; the self is just the intersection of roles you inhabit. But moral improvement requires cheng — an inner sincerity that feels like it's coming from somewhere authentic inside you, not just from role-playing.

(8) I've also been playing with "recursive filiality": the child actively shapes what "being a father" means in that relation, so obligations are co-constituted, not one-directional.

Do these hang together? I feel like (4) and (5) are fighting — if ritual trains emotions, wouldn't even empty performance eventually generate sincerity? And (7) sounds contradictory: if there's no deep self, what's the "inside" cheng comes from? Also (1) and (6) might clash — if obligations are role-constituted, how can an outside factor like universal dignity cancel them?

But maybe some tensions are only apparent. For instance, (2) and (3) might actually fit if residual recognition is just the minimal form of ren appropriate to a broken role.

Can you audit these? Go through the pairs, rank the real tensions by severity, say what's only apparent tension, and for each genuine conflict suggest the cheapest revision. Specifically dig into (4)/(5) and (7), and tell me whether (8) undermines (1) or can be reconciled with it. Don't just flag everything — some of this probably holds up fine. I'm acutally not sure which commitment I'd drop if I had to, so don't assume I want to keep (6) or ditch (7).
