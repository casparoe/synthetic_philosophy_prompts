Design a 90-minute classroom activity for an upper-level philosophy seminar of about fourteen students on Parfit’s hitchhiker scenario and the problem of rational irrationality. The participants should come away feeling, through their own staged choices, why it can be rational to commit in advance to an action that will be irrational to carry out ex post, and specifically why that rationality hinges on the architecture of belief transmission rather than on brute individual will.

To keep the exercise out of the well-worn single-driver, single-hitchhiker format, build it around the following niche framing: arrange the students into a four-role relay chain—Promisor, Relay A, Relay B, and Executor—through which a commitment token travels in a sealed envelope. The Promisor can deposit a personal stake (represented by points) to make a promise credible; the promise, if honored, requires the Executor to take an action that is personally costly. Each relay receives only a partial, non-transferable signal: Relay A knows whether the envelope was sealed but not its contents; Relay B knows that Relay A received something but cannot verify what Relay A knows; the Executor sees only the final envelope and must decide whether to honor the commitment. The group shares a collective payoff pool—if the Executor honors the promise, the group gains a modest symbolic reward, but the Executor suffers a larger personal point loss. The rules should be arranged so that the Executor faces a genuine decision point where honoring the promise is ex post irrational, yet the group has ex ante reason to want it honored, and the intermediate relays can choose whether to corrupt or preserve the signal chain.

Provide the precise turn order and rules; the four roles with explicit knowledge conditions; a non-monetary payoff scheme using points or seminar privileges; a 90-minute timeline that includes a silent writing period before the chain begins and restricts out-of-role communication during the relay; the debrief that maps each stage onto the ex ante / ex post distinction and onto the problem of belief transmission; an honest note on which feature of rational irrationality the relay structure cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator; and a prediction of what will predictably go wrong the first time (for instance, students resolving ambiguity by talking across roles) together with a concrete prevention method. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Finally, state how confident you are in the design overall and single out the specific point about belief transmission through the relay about which you are least sure.
