Write an essay on the paradox of deontology—the apparent contradiction that strict, non-aggregative moral prohibitions can mandate accepting outcomes in which far more instances of the very wrong being prohibited come about. Assess whether this is a genuine internal contradiction or an artifact of evaluating deontological rules through consequentialist framing. Specifically, examine whether distinguishing prohibitions governing direct agency from permissions governing outcomes one merely allows dissolves the paradox or merely displaces it onto the boundary between action and omission. Reach and justify a definite conclusion about whether deontology can be rendered consistent without surrendering its core rejection of harm-aggregation as a basis for moral choice.
