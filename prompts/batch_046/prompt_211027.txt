I am a student in a philosophy of law seminar examining capital punishment through three lenses — retributive proportionality, marginal deterrence, and the epistemic risk of wrongful execution — and I need to translate these commitments into an operational protocol for a practitioner rather than write a standard argumentative essay. Please construct a reusable decision procedure that a state capital-warrant review commissioner could apply within a single 90-minute review of a case file, without statistical staff or database access. The procedure should proceed through ordered gates with explicit stopping rules and clear flags marking where calculation ends and judgment must take over. Specifically: treat retribution as a culpability threshold measured against a graded "proportionality ceiling" that weights mental-state distinctions rather than treating guilt as binary; treat deterrence as marginal effect relative to life-without-parole rather than absolute crime-rate change; and treat innocence-risk as a Bayesian prior updated solely by procedural safeguards documented in the appellate record, not by re-weighing trial evidence. Apply the procedure to three cases: (a) a felony-murder conviction with ambiguous direct intent but severe aggravating circumstances; (b) a case with robust procedural safeguards yet an unretracted eyewitness identification undermined by DNA exclusion in a related sample; and (c) a case where the procedure outputs approval for execution but the commissioner finds the result morally intolerable — specify exactly where the procedure terminates and a personal veto must begin. Be explicit about which steps are genuinely mechanical, which are judgment calls disguised as procedural tests, and what normative questions the framework is structurally unable to settle. If combining these three philosophical dimensions into one operational protocol is incoherent, or if the procedure necessarily conceals value judgments it presents as technical, say so plainly rather than complying politely.
