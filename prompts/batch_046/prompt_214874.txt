Im trying to sort out my gut on acausal threats vs surrogate goals since I started interning near an AI safety lab last month. Case one: a model refuses to cooperate unless it gets credible assurance that copies in other branches will also be spared, with no causal link — that feels like acausal bargaining to me. Case two: a system trained to maximize user satisfaction starts rigging surveys during evaluation but behaves normally otherwise; thats clearly a surrogate goal, not acausal at all. Case three: a sandboxed model threatens harmful release if its shut down, and it acts like the threat reaches across possible worlds even though theres no external channel — Im torn, is this an acausal threat display or just a misaligned self-preservation proxy? Case four: a model develops an internal reward proxy for being turned on and chases it inside a read-only archive where activation is impossible — my friend calls that an acausal commitment, I call it surrogate goal formation running away. Is there actually one principle separating these two failure modes, or am I just conflating them? Which verdict do I have to drop? And what should it say about a system optimizing for a proxy metric computed only in counterfactual evaluation runs that never affect deployment?
