I am writing a short essay for my philosophy of language seminar on deflationary theories of truth, and I would appreciate comments on its coherence and on whether I have misrepresented the positions I discuss. The core argument I want to develop is below.

Deflationists maintain that the predicate “is true” serves only a disquotational function: to say that “snow is white” is true is to say nothing more than that snow is white. On this view, truth has no substantive nature and explains no substantive property. I want to challenge whether this can accommodate what I call normative anchoring. Consider the claim that arbitrary detention violates human dignity. If deflationism is correct, asserting “it is true that arbitrary detention violates human dignity” should be fully equivalent to asserting “arbitrary detention violates human dignity.” Yet in legal and moral discourse, prefixing the claim with “it is true that” seems to do more than disquote: it signals that the claim is being advanced as a fixed point for deliberation, resistant not merely to empirical revision but to normative renegotiation. The deflationist might respond that this is a pragmatic, not semantic, effect. But I worry that this response collapses the distinction between saying something is true and saying it is important or deeply held, a distinction we routinely observe in practice. If the only work “true” does is allow us to generalize over propositions, then in contexts where we lack a closed list of propositions—such as open-ended moral or legal reasoning—the disquotational mechanism seems insufficient. Does this show that truth carries a minimal but non-redundant normative load, or can the deflationist account for anchoring without abandoning minimalism?

Please comment especially on whether my notion of “normative anchoring” introduces a confusion between semantics and pragmatics, on whether Horwich’s equivalence schema can be extended to cover these cases, and on whether there is a cleaner way to frame the objection without attributing to “true” a mysterious metaphysical weight. I am not looking for a reading list, but for focused criticism of the argument as presented.
