As a journalist covering science and philosophy, and as a serving member of my university's Joint Committee on Research Ethics and Methodology, I am preparing the board's formal decision memo for circulation ahead of Thursday's meeting. We must rule on Protocol SB-7, a proposed sleep-laboratory study that operationalises the Sleeping Beauty problem with human participants. Over a 72-hour cycle, subjects are kept under sedation with pharmacologically induced anterograde amnesia; upon each awakening they are informed only that they occupy either the 'Monday' or 'Tuesday' experimental condition, lacking any temporal indexical, and must then make a binding decision about a concealed parameter of the study design. The investigators argue this is legitimate methodological blinding that tests in vivo how agents form self-locating credences. One faction of the board considers this routine deception with adequate general consent; the other faction holds that withholding the specific awakening number prevents participants from assigning correct subjective probabilities to the risks they face on each awakening — an institutional version of the thirder versus halfer dispute about updating under centred possibilities — and therefore violates epistemic autonomy regardless of debriefing. Please draft the memo in British English: it should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, include a concrete time frame for any required amendments or further submissions, present the reasoning in language the researchers can directly address, and give an honest account of the strongest objection on the losing side rather than simply citing regulatory definitions.
