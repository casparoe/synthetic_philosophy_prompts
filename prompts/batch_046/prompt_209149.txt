Consider G. E. Moore's thesis in Principia Ethica that intrinsic goods such as the appreciation of beauty and the experience of personal friendship can form an organic unity, so that the value of their combination is not reducible to the sum of the values of each component taken in isolation. Devise a precise conceptual example—drawn from a shared encounter with an ambiguous or incomplete artwork, or from a collaborative act of aesthetic interpretation—where the interaction between friendship and beauty produces value that is genuinely emergent rather than merely additive. Your example should be structured so that attempting to decompose it into an aesthetic part and a social part either distorts the phenomenon or begs the question in favor of organic unity. Then, identify one specific internal challenge to Moore's account that arises from the dynamics of your example, such as explaining why analogous negative interactions (for instance, competitive envy over taste or mutual distraction during contemplation) do not generate symmetrically emergent disvalue, and address whether this asymmetry undermines the coherence of treating beauty and friendship as organically unified goods.
