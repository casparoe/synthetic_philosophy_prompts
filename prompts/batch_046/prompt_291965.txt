Student answer:

"Al-Ghazali's main point is that philosophers wrongly think fire has an inherent power to burn cotton. All we observe is constant conjunction—fire near cotton, then burning. To show this isn't necessary, he gives the analogy of a man born blind who suddenly gains sight and thinks the sun is the direct cause, not realizing that opening his eyes is what actually produces vision. This proves correlation isn't causation.

Because al-Ghazali is Ash'arite, he accepts continuous recreation: God recreates every atom each instant, so fire doesn't even persist long enough to cause anything. That's the metaphysical backbone of his occasionalism. Without khalq jadid, he wouldn't have an argument against the philosophers. The patterns we see are just God's habit ('ada), but he can break them in miracles—like when Abraham wasn't burned. So created things have zero causal power.

People often confuse al-Ghazali with Hume, thinking he just doubts our knowledge of causes. But he's actually making a stronger claim: there literally is no causal power outside God. Another mistake is saying he denies all regularity, but he clearly allows predictable patterns through divine custom."
