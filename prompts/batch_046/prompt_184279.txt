I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of mind and comparative ethics, preparing a workshop document for an adversarial collaboration. I want you to draft the joint statement that two opposing camps could both sign, concerning whether sentience and moral status come in continuous gradations or require categorical thresholds, with special attention to the spectrum from insects to simple artifacts such as thermostats.

The two camps are as follows. Camp One, the Gradualist Pluralists, holds that phenomenal consciousness and direct moral considerability vary by degree, that insects possess low but non-zero moral status proportional to their integrated sensory and nociceptive complexity, and that minimal homeostatic devices occupy the extreme lower bound—perhaps with negligible but not strictly absent status—because their predictive feedback loops are structurally analogous, at a vastly lower integration level, to the nociceptive architectures found in simpler organisms. Camp Two, the Threshold Deontologists, holds that direct moral status requires crossing a categorical boundary, specifically the presence of a unified self-model or second-order intentionality, that below this boundary there is zero direct moral standing, and that although insects may have welfare-related functional interests and we may have indirect duties toward them, a thermostat—however sophisticated its feedback regulation—possesses no moral status whatsoever beyond its instrumental value.

Please produce a formal, constructive document structured in three parts. Part A should be a numbered agreed-claims section: statements both sides accept about the neurobiology of nociception in decapod crustaceans and hymenopteran insects, about the formal properties of homeostatic feedback loops, about the logical consistency of intermediate cases, and about which empirical or conceptual findings are relevant without being decisive. In this section, dissolve any merely verbal disagreements—such as divergent uses of "sentience" versus "moral patienthood" or "welfare interest" versus "direct moral right"—and state clearly that the remaining dispute is substantive, not terminological.

Part B should be a disagreements table. Each row should present a precise formulation of a residual disagreement, phrased in wording that the respective side would accept as accurate to its own view. Include at minimum: the ontological disagreement over continuous versus categorical distribution of phenomenal properties; the normative disagreement over whether moral weight is proportional or binary; and the methodological disagreement over whether analogical reasoning from biological neural architecture to artificial feedback systems is legitimate in principle.

Part C should identify three cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side—and, for each, specify a concrete case, argument, or piece of evidence that both sides agree is genuinely bearing on the crux, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The cruxes should address the following. First, a hypothetical engineered hybrid system—designated System H—possessing the nociceptive pathway homology characteristic of a honeybee but only the metabolic self-regulation architecture of a digital thermostat, lacking learning, memory, or predictive modeling beyond temperature maintenance; both sides should agree this case is relevant, and each should state in advance whether System H possesses any non-zero direct moral status. Second, the empirical scenario in which sustained behavioral trade-offs between injury avoidance and foraging in certain insects are shown to be inexplicable by pure reflex arcs, coupled with the demonstrated absence of analogous sustained trade-offs in any artificial homeostatic device; each side should predict whether this finding supports continuous gradation or confirms a categorical gap. Third, the conceptual challenge that any continuous account must identify a non-arbitrary point at which a thermostat's minimal feedback loop acquires non-zero status, while any categorical account must provide a non-arbitrary principle that includes the insect but excludes the thermostat; each side should state what it takes to be the decisive consideration and should predict whether the opposing principle can be formulated without ad hoc boundary-drawing.

The tone should be rigorous and suitable for a philosophy-of-mind workshop. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, requests for citations, or calls for further literature. Do not use verbatim quotation marks for passages from real texts; if well-known positions are referenced, paraphrase them without quotation marks. Ensure the final document reads as a single text that both parties could endorse, with all substantive disagreement rendered exact rather than rhetorical.
