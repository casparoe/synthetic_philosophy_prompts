ok so i'm trying to check if my views on democratic backsliding actually hang together or if i'm just picking examples that match my politics. here's roughly where i stand:

1. backsliding is real, not just a partisan slur, but only if we mean institutional capture — court packing, seizing electoral commissions, gutting media independance — not "passing laws i dislike."

2. hungary under fidesz and poland under piS are clear cases: constitutional court capture, public media turned into party mouthpiece, rules tilted to keep them in power.

3. turkey under erdogan is different. that's not backsliding, it's old-school authoritarian consolidation from a weak baseline. calling it backsliding is a catagory error, like saying a half-built house "collapsed."

4. the US in 2020-24 doesn't count. norms eroded badly, but federal courts stayed independant, elections ran, power transferred. so norm decay, not backsliding.

5. who gets to call it? i think expert indices like v-dem are basically right because they operationalize those institutional markers, and domestic partisan labels are unreliable.

6. but this bugs me: those indices measure distance from a liberal-democratic baseline. if a society genuinely prefers a more majoritarian model, isn't "backsliding" just a value-laden label for "moving away from our model"? especially for turkey, where liberal democracy was always shallow.

so do these cohere? am i using (1) and (5) to condemn hungary/poland, then in (6) undermining the yardstick? and is (3) vs (4) just protecting my intuition that the US was "built then captured" while turkey was "never built"? what's the least costly revision — drop the cultural-relativism worry in (6), redraw the baseline distinction in (3), or admit that (5) is circular? rank the tensions and say which single fix cleans up the most.
