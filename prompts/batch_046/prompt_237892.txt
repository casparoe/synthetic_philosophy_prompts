I'm giving a seminar talk next Thursday to a philosophy-of-art reading group that's split about evenly between analytic aestheticians who think intentionalism is dead and critical-race theorists who worry about reducing Black art to political instrumentality. My thesis is a somewhat niche reading of Du Bois's "Criteria of Negro Art": instead of treating "all art is propaganda" as a call for didactic racial uplift, I argue it's an ontological claim about form itself—specifically that formal devices like anaphora, strategic silence, and collective pronouns don't just carry messages but structurally model distributed social causation. I read his own poetry (the smoke poem, the Atlanta litany) as enacting collective agency through rhythm rather than depicting individual heroism. The audience cares about whether this collapses aesthetics into sociology, whether it essentializes Black art as always already political, and whether I'm misrepresenting Du Bois's actual relationship to Locke's aesthetic-autonomy thesis in "The New Negro."
