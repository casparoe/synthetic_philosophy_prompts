Consider this counterfactual exercise in historical sociology: suppose Max Weber were alive to assess the contemporary claim that “algorithmic curation culture”—the cluster of values embedded in recommendation architectures, quantified engagement metrics, and optimization-for-attention—functions as an independent causal factor in shaping modern economic behavior, particularly in platform-mediated labor and entrepreneurial risk-taking. Some theorists argue that this technical-value complex stands in an elective affinity with a new “spirit” of platform capitalism, structurally analogous to the relationship Weber traced between ascetic Protestant ethics and the rationalization of early capitalist conduct; others dismiss it as purely superstructural, reducible to material incentives, network effects, and instrumentally rational adaptation.

Assume Weber accepts neither crude economic determinism nor the full autonomy of ideas from material conditions. How, precisely, would he adjudicate whether values instantiated in code, interface design, and metric regimes can possess the kind of causal efficacy he attributed to religious ethics? Would he regard the comparison to the Protestant ethic as a legitimate extension of his methodology—requiring an ideal-type construction of a “spirit of algorithmic optimization” and an analysis of its elective affinity with specific economic forms—or as a category error that illegitimately conflates instrumentally rational action with value-rational commitment?

Take a definite, committed position: argue for the specific interpretation Weber would endorse, giving explicit reasons drawn from his conceptual apparatus—ideal types, elective affinity, the distinction between value-rational and instrumentally rational action, and his critique of monocausal explanation. Do not present a balanced survey of interpretive options; defend one reading and demonstrate why the alternatives misapply his framework. In particular, address whether the “residue”—those features of algorithmic culture that resist clean translation into Weber’s vocabulary of religious ethics and rationalization—signals a genuine conceptual expansion of his sociology or reveals a confusion in the original analogy.
