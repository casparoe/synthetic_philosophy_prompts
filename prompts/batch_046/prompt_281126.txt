So I've been stuck on this philosophical knot about Darwin and Turing pulling the same weird trick — competance without comprehension. Darwin shows adaptiveness emerging without a designer who understands the outcome; Turing shows intelligent behavior coming out of mechanisms that don't grasp meaning. There's a long running argument about whether these are two versions of one inversion or totally different moves, and whether the phrase "competence without comprehension" actually picks out something real or just lets us avoid hard questions about mind and design.

The disagreement seems to have stalled. One side — call them the decouplers — says both cases prove you can have full functional performance with zero internal understanding, and that philosophy should stop treating comprehension as a prerequisite for either intelligence or biological design. The other side — the relocators — insists comprehension never vanished, it just got pushed into the encoding: DNA instructions or symbolic architectures do the understanding on behalf of the system. They've been circling this since atleast the 1980s, and nobody's moved. My hunch is it's partly verbal — people use "understanding" for intentional states in one breath and for causal explanation in the next — but I'm not sure.

Is this stall actually about hidden empirical claims, like whether current evolutionary models or LLMs achieve genuine decoupling? Or are the two sides answering different questions under one label — one about causal history, one about present ontology? Or is it bedrock intuition about whether a mindless process can ever be more than a metaphor for mind? Give me an honest diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, using what each side actually says, and tell me if there's any distinction, experiment, or formal result that could break the logjam — or whether we should just admit it's one of those debates that doesn't resolve and move on.
