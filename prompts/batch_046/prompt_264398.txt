I have been trying to locate the exact logical pressure point in Fitch's paradox of knowability. The derivation establishes that if every truth is in principle knowable, then every truth is in fact known, since for any unknown true proposition p, the conjunction of p with its own unknown status yields a contradiction—knowable only if p was already known. What I find unclear is whether the argument tacitly depends on a closure principle stronger than mere factivity: does it require that a knower can always conjoin an arbitrary world-directed belief with a second-order belief about her own epistemic position, making the result an artifact of logical omniscience rather than a genuine constraint on the knowability thesis itself? I ask because I am wondering whether artificial reasoning systems that lack full introspective access to their own ignorance—systems that cannot represent both p and the absence of knowledge of p within a single inferential frame—might thereby avoid the paradox, or whether such an escape merely restricts the domain of truths without addressing the underlying logical tension.
