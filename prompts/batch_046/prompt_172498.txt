I have been trying to formalise a view I call axiological orphanhood: the idea that a state of affairs can carry normative weight for a merely potential subject, even when no actual subject exists to claim it. From this I have derived an argument that seems to prove too much, and I cannot see the escape.

(1) If a state of affairs is intrinsically valuable, then there exists a pro tanto reason for its realisation.
(2) The existence of such a reason depends only on the axiological properties of the state, not on the presence of any agent who might respond to it; call this a free-floating rationale.
(3) Wherever there is a reason for φ, there is a corresponding normative fact that φ ought, pro tanto, to be brought about.
(4) A prescription that is directed at no subject whatsoever is not genuinely prescriptive; 'ought' conceptually requires an 'ought-er', even if that subject is only potential.
(5) Therefore, in a world containing no agents at all, free-floating rationales would still generate normative facts of the form 'φ ought to be brought about', yet there would be no subject for whom this holds.
(6) But a normative fact with no bearer is not a normative fact at all; it is merely an axiological description expressed in modal dress.
(7) So either free-floating rationales are not genuinely normative, or they do not exist, or the concept of 'ought' can be genuinely subjectless.
(8) Each of these outcomes undermines the very motivation for accepting axiological orphanhood.

The reasoning looks valid, yet every premise seemed independently plausible when I adopted it. I am particularly reluctant to surrender (2), since it expresses the intuition that value is not created by being noticed. Still, I suspect that something slips between (3) and (5) — perhaps an equivocation between a reason's existence and its being binding on a particular subject, or a hidden scope shift from potential to actual reasoners. Which step should I reject, what does that rejection cost, and is there a way to preserve (2) without being forced into (7)?
