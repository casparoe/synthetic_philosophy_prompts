Hey, I'm sketching out an essay and want some rough feedback before I commit to the full draft. The core idea is that the nationalism vs cosmopolitanism debate usually gets stuck on whether compatriots have special moral weight, but I think that framing misses a structural point I'm calling "jurisdictional partiality" — the notion that obligations to fellow citizens aren't first-order moral facts (like family loyalty) but procedual duties tied to being enrolled in a specific cooperative scheme: taxation, legal jurisidiction, shared infrastructure, etc. So the claim isn't "they're mine" but "I'm a node in this network."

Then I push on the arbitrariness objection: if enrollment is just an accident of birth, why should it generate any priority? My tentative reply is that it only generates procedural priority — you vote locally, pay taxes locally, use local courts — but not substantive priority in, say, rescue cases. You shouldn't save a compatriot over a stranger in a burning building just because you're both in the same scheme, unless there's a coordination reason like local emergency response capacity.

But here's where I get stuck. I want to say there's an "awkward middle scale" problem. Family partiality works because families are small and affectve; cosmopolitan obligations work because humanity is the right scale for universal rights. The nation-state is too big for real affective bonds and too small for global efficiency, so compatriot partiality sits in this weird middle — what I'm calling the "scale mismach problem." It feels like this is just restating the arbitrariness objection with extra steps, but I think it's different because it targets the functional role of partiality rather than its moral justification.

I'm also worried I'm misrepresenting the associativist view — like Miller's idea that shared public culture generates special obligations. Am I reducing that too quickly to procedural enrollment? And if procedural obligations don't generate substantive rescue priorities, doesn't my view just collapse into cosmopolitanism with some bureaucratic decoration?

Anyway, does this hang together? Is the scale mismatch interesting or just convoluted? And am I being unfair to nationalist arguments by treating them as purely procedural? Would appreciate any holes or pushback, especially on whether there's a non-collapsing middle ground between pure cosmopolitanism and thick nationalist loyalty. Also, is there an obvious typo or confusion in the framing? I feel like I might be mixing up jurisdictional and distributive reasons but can't tell which.
