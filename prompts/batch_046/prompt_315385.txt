Please construct an argument that luck egalitarianism, if applied consistently to what can be termed "epistemic option luck" — cases in which an agent's decision to employ a specific reasoning heuristic was itself causally shaped by prior brute-luck disparities in education and cognitive environment — undermines its own foundational distinction between choice and circumstance. Show that, within this domain, the boundary between option luck and brute luck becomes unstable because the capacity for adequate reflection is itself a product of the very lottery the theory seeks to neutralize. Consequently, defend the claim that luck egalitarianism in the epistemic sphere collapses into prioritarianism about reasoning capacities rather than maintaining a genuinely egalitarian structure. Address the standard reply that option luck presupposes choices made with sufficient information and rational capacity, and explain why that reply fails to stabilize the distinction in this particular domain. Avoid bibliographic references or citations; develop the argument from the internal logic of the position itself.
