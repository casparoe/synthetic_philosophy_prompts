I serve on my university's Joint Committee on Curriculum and Epistemic Standards, and we must render a decision within two weeks on a proposed required first-year module, "Foundations of Formal Reasoning," which introduces students to axiomatic method through ZFC while discussing Gödel's incompleteness results. The committee has voted separately on three logically linked propositions: (1) that ZFC's consistency is genuinely unsettled for pedagogical purposes; (2) that a required course should present an axiomatic system as working truth rather than as a hypothetical framework; and (3) that the module should be adopted. A discursive dilemma has arisen: majorities support (1) and (2) individually, which together entail rejection of (3), yet a separate majority directly supports (3), and our bylaws do not specify whether to aggregate premises or conclusions. Please draft the committee's formal decision memo recommending either premise-based or conclusion-based aggregation, with a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—and an explicit aggregation rule for future cases. The memo should treat the pedagogical and logical tensions seriously, include an honest treatment of the strongest argument for the opposing aggregation method, and present reasoning the researchers and faculty can respond to without appealing to external literature.
