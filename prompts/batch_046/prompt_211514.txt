the precise question: does shadowing show that determinism is compatible with a practical kind of path-indeterminacy that standard free-will debates completely miss? both compatibilists and incompatibilists seem to assume that if determinism holds then there is exactly one future path that is "the" actual one and we might just be ignorant of it. but shadowing complicates that picture, since near your computation there exists a true path, just not your path. so maybe determinism doesnt guarantee the unique-trackability those debates presuppose.

why existing answers leave it open: people either say chaos is just ignorance (compatibilists win) or that it reveals real ontological openness (incompatibilists win). neither camp distinguishes law-governed state-space evolution from the ability to trace a unique trajectory over long timescales. and since shadowing is a mathematical result about approximation, running more physical simulations wont settle the conceptual issue.
