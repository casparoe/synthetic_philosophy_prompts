Consider Alan Turing's implicit rulings across several scenarios he discussed regarding machine learning and education. He rejected the strategy of fully programming an adult-level mind, comparing it unfavorably to attempting to construct an adult brain directly rather than nurturing a simpler initial structure. He endorsed the education of a "child machine" that begins with minimal innate rules and acquires competence through guided correction by a teacher, rewards and punishments, and imitation of successful behavior. At the same time, he permitted that a system could learn to play chess by playing repeatedly against itself and altering its internal rules according to success and failure, even in the absence of continuous human tutoring. He also maintained a sharp distinction between genuine adaptive restructuring—where internal organization changes in response to experience—and mere storage or lookup of pre-encoded answers, counting only the former as true learning.
