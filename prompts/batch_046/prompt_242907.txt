I'm writing the final take-home exam for an advanced seminar I've been teaching on structural analogies between formal and biological self-reference. Over the course we've worked through Gödel's 1931 incompleteness results, Hofstadter's notion of typogenetics, and the molecular biology of replication fidelity. For one of the essay questions—intended to be completed in about ninety minutes and running roughly eight hundred words—I want to avoid anything that can be answered by summarizing the DNA-Gödel isomorphism. Instead, I want students to confront a precise tension: Gödel's fixed-point construction depends on an arithmetic encoding that lets a formula in an object-language refer to its own syntactic properties, sustained by a strict distinction between object-language and meta-language; DNA replication, by contrast, involves triplet codons interpreted by ribosomes to synthesize proteins—including polymerases—while 3' to 5' exonuclease proofreading operates as an error-correcting filter. The exam prompt should ask students to evaluate whether this biological loop genuinely replicates the logical structure of self-reference, or whether it collapses into a merely metaphorical feedback system lacking the syntax-proof distinction that makes Gödel's result rigorous. Specifically, they should address whether the proofreading mechanism functions analogously to a meta-level consistency check, or whether it remains an external biochemical filter with no interpretive access to the informational content of the sequence.

Please provide the exam question itself, a model answer of approximately six hundred to eight hundred words, and a marking scheme built around five specific analytical moves, each worth four points out of twenty. The required moves are: first, an accurate reconstruction of Gödel numbering and the fixed-point lemma that shows understanding of how self-reference is technically achieved rather than merely named; second, a precise description of the replication-transcription-translation cycle with correct roles for polymerase, ribosome, and codon table; third, an explicit identification of where the object-language/meta-language boundary sits in Gödel's proof and whether an equivalent boundary exists in the biological case; fourth, an analysis of proofreading's role—specifically whether 3' to 5' exonuclease activity operates like an internal self-monitoring loop or like an external filter, and what this implies for the analogy; fifth, a reasoned conclusion on whether biological self-replication instantiates logical self-reference or only a structurally similar but philosophically distinct circularity. For each move, include a one-line note on the characteristic misconception that should cost marks, such as treating ribosomal decoding as semantic understanding rather than chemical matching, or assuming that any circular causal system automatically reproduces Gödelian incompleteness. The level should be appropriate for students who have read primary excerpts from Gödel's 1931 paper and Hofstadter's discussion of typogenetics, but the prompt must not ask for bibliographies, page citations, or references to outside scholarship.
