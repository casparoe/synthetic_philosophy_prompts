I've mostly bought into Hacking's looping-effect story for psychiatric classifications - you know, the idea that labeling people with disorders doesn't just describe them but actively changes how they behave, which feeds back and reshapes the category itself. I think it explains a ton about why DSM categories keep shifting and why some diagnoses vanish (hysteria) while others blow up (ADHD).

But lately I've been playing with a tweak: what if there's a second-order loop where clinicians don't just observe changed patient behavior, but actively recalibrate the diagnostic criteria to match the new behavioral profile they're seeing? So it's not just patients looping, but the taxonomy looping on itself - I'm calling it "recursive looping." Anyway, I'm treating this modified view as basically correct: psychiatric classification is unstable not because we haven't found real biological essences yet, but because the system is caught in these nested feedback loops.

What would count against it? Not just "there are brain scans showing biological bases" - defenders absorb that easily. I want the defeaters that should actually move me, sorted from fatal to merely awkward. Are there categories that stayed rock-solid despite heavy labeling effects? Cases where looping should of occured but clearly didn't? What about disorders with nearly identical prevalance across cultures with totally different labeling practices - does that snap the loop?
