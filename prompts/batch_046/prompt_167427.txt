Your task is to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on sphexishness, mechanical rigidity, flexibility, and free will.

Camp One—the Loop-Breaking Agency camp—maintains that genuine rational agency requires the capacity for recursive exit: an agent must be able to suspend, revise, or violate its own first-order procedural rules in light of second-order reasoning. Sphexishness—the execution of a fixed behavioral script irrespective of contextual feedback—is not merely inefficient but represents a normative failure mode. Flexibility, on this view, is categorically distinct from complex computation; it involves a direct relation to reasons that breaks rather than traverses the loop.

Camp Two—the Attractor-Functionalist camp—maintains that apparent flexibility is always reducible to dynamics across meta-stable attractors in a state space. What Camp One calls breaking a loop is descriptively identical to a transition between attractors governed by hidden parameters or higher-order rules that are themselves fixed. Sphexishness is therefore an observer-relative designation for shallow, single-attractor behavior, not a metaphysically distinct category. The normative demand for loop-breaking mistakes descriptive complexity for freedom and risks treating random perturbation as rational agency.
