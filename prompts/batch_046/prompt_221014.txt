For my graduate seminar on practical reason, draft a take-home final essay prompt—answerable in roughly 90 minutes, about 800 words—that examines whether "better than" is transitive through a spectrum argument built on a specific, non-standard continuum. Construct a graded sequence of experiential states in which each adjacent pair differs only by trading a marginal increase in one evaluative dimension (e.g., sensory vividness) for a marginal decrease in another (e.g., temporal coherence), with the endpoints being intuitively incomparable or reversely ordered. The question must force students to diagnose whether the resulting intransitivity stems from rational-preference failure, ambiguity in the comparison standard, or an ontologically non-ordinal value structure; it should be unanswerable by reciting rational-choice axioms or summarizing familiar population-paradox cases. Include a model answer of about 600 words and a marking scheme (20 points total) that separates an A response—which distinguishes cardinal from ordinal judgments, explains why the small-improvement premise is not trivially false, and evaluates whether context-sensitive standards can restore transitivity without collapsing into subjectivism—from a B response that merely labels the structure "sorites-like." Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or references to obscure texts.
