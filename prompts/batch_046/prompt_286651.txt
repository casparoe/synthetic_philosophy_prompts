Write the joint statement that a committed advocate of the American Dream ethos—understood here as the view that visible, celebrated success is both a just reward for merit and a necessary motivational signal for collective aspiration—and a committed advocate of Jantelagen together with the tall poppy syndrome—understood here as the view that prominent success should be actively cut down to preserve egalitarian solidarity and prevent corrosive status hierarchies—could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Structure the document in three parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims about human motivation, social comparison, and the effects of public recognition on both innovation and social cohesion; these should be substantive enough that neither camp is signing away its core intuition. Second, a disagreements table with precise formulations of the residual differences, each phrased in wording that its own camp would accept without feeling misrepresented. Third, a cruxes-and-tests section identifying the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party.

To keep the exercise precise rather than generic, anchor the cruxes in the following injected scenario, which both camps should agree is relevant but should treat differently: a proposed policy of “reverse visibility,” in which a society anonymizes all high-status achievements in public records but requires the successful to fund, in secret, substantial social-mobility grants; the grants are publicly celebrated, but the donors’ identities are permanently concealed. Both sides should agree this case bears on their dispute, yet they should record opposing predictions about whether it would increase long-run innovation, decrease social resentment, or collapse under free-rider dynamics.

For each crux, record each camp’s explicit prediction about the outcome of the reverse-visibility scenario, and identify the concrete mechanism—motivational, psychological, or institutional—each side thinks explains that outcome. Ensure the formulations are neither vacuous nor slanted: do not reduce the American Dream position to a defense of greed, nor the Jantelagen position to a defense of envy. The deliverable should be a constructive text both parties could put their names to, with the residual disagreement made exact and the shared test case left open to empirical or argumentative resolution.
