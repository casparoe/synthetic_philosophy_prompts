I'm trying to pin down what I actually think about imperial economic integration vs. straight-up political domination, working off Adam Smith's framework but stretching it to cases he never lived to see. Here are my gut verdicts, going mostly by feel:

- The East India Company's rule in Bengal after Plassey -- direct sovereignty plus revenue extraction with zero local representation -- is clearly wrong, even if tax collection got more efficient.

- Post-1776 American colonies forced into mercantilist navigation acts but otherwise self-governing: bad, but less bad, since at least they had their own legislatures. The wrongness feels like it's about being treated as a subordinate market, not just about taxes.

- A hypothetical settler colony where indigenous goverence is fully preserved and settlers only build railroads and extract minerals through voluntary contract, no coercion: seems okay, maybe net good.

- Ireland after the Act of Union -- full UK parliamentary representation, but local industry deliberately crushed by tariff policy designed in London. I keep flip-flopping. Sometimes it feels like internal colonialism, sometimes like a bad deal that your own reprentatives signed off on.

- A modern charter city run by a foreign corporation: locals have opt-in citizenship but zero democratic control over corporate policy, with high exit costs. I think it's wrong, but I can't tell if that's because of the sovereignty gap or just the lack of real exit.

Is there one Smithian principle that gets all five, or close? Maybe it's about whether people are treated as ends in exchange or as revenue sources? But then case 4 is ambiguous because there was formal representation. Or maybe the key is whether the imperial power can unilaterally rewrite trade rules? I want the principle stated precisely, which verdict it forces me to drop, and what it predicts for a case I haven't listed -- say, a digital platform that provides governance services to users in exchange for data extraction, where leaving is technically free but you'd lose your social graph and work history.
