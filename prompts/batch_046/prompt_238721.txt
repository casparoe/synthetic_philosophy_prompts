"Applying the regress to instrument testimony doesn't destroy the taxonomy, but it does show we have to be precise about what's basic. Foundationalists are right that some beliefs stop the chain. Here, my perceptual belief that the display reads '72' is basic—it's directly given—while my belief that the room is 72°F is inferred and depends on background beliefs about calibration. So the regress stops at direct experience, not at the temperature claim itself.

Coherentism can handle this fine too. Beliefs about the display, calibration standards, and prior temperature measurements all cohere in a web. Circularity is only a problem for linear chains of justification; since coherence is holistic, there are no loops to worry about. That means testimony doesn't break coherentism, it just gets woven into the web.

Infinitism fails because it requires an infinite chain of actually available reasons stretching back forever. For the thermometer, that'd mean I'd need an endless series of calibration checks and prior measurements that I actually possess, which is impossible. So infinitism sets a standard no one can meet.

Overall, foundationalism does best: it lets us stop the regress at direct perceptual beliefs about the display, while allowing inferred beliefs about temperature to build on top."
