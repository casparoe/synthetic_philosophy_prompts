I'm a doctoral student in metaphysics preparing seminar material on personal identity and origin conditions. I'd like a dialogue between two philosophers—Mara and Leo—debating whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg. Leo defends a process-continuity view: what fixes your origin is the continuous reproductive-causal trajectory directed by your parents' intentions, so different nuclear gametes could have yielded you provided the same developmental pathway and intentional structure were preserved.

To keep the exchange from becoming generic, please center it on an under-discussed case: three-parent IVF (mitochondrial replacement therapy), in which nuclear DNA comes from the intended parents but the cytoplasm and mitochondrial genome come from a donor egg. The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth: Leo should anticipate Mara's reply about epigenetic influence and respond specifically; Mara should anticipate Leo's split-cytoplasm move and reply specifically. Neither should be a strawman. End without resolution, but with both having pinpointed whether their disagreement is ultimately about identity conditions, about the metaphysical weight of causal contribution, or about the normative role of parental intention in fixing origins. Please write the answer in American English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests.
