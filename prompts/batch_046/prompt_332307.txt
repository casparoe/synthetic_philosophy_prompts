My core claim is that the demandingness objection tacitly assumes an idealized agent with unlimited motivational resources, whereas real agents—including adolescents—possess a finite “moral reserve”: a pool of sustained altruistic capacity that depletes under continuous high-demand action and requires social recognition and rest to replenish. Therefore, consequentialism should be evaluated not against one-shot maximal sacrifice but against sustainable temporal allocation. I concede that failing to perform low-cost rescues is clearly wrong, but I resist the conclusion that agents must perpetually operate at the margin of self-sacrifice, because such perpetual operation depletes the very reserve needed for future obligations.
