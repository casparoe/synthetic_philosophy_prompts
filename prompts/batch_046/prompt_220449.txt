The memo should establish a “Mandatory Analogy Protocol” requiring analysts to map every emerging crisis onto at least one of three canonical templates: the Munich appeasement scenario, the Vietnam quagmire narrative, or the Thucydides trap of hegemonic transition. Include a bureaucratic scoring rubric—e.g., “Surface Resonance Points” awarded for rhetorical and emotional fit, and “Causal Divergence Penalties” that are explicitly waived when they involve nuclear deterrence, economic interdependence, domestic regime type, or alliance structures—so that the form rewards superficial similarity and punishes structural analysis. By the end, a reader should feel the actual philosophical problem: that treating history as a repository of reusable templates smuggles in a deterministic philosophy of history—macro-patterns recurring independent of agent-level contingency—while masking unstated normative commitments about risk, intervention, and moral responsibility. Do not name living officials; invent the office and its director. Keep the irony sustained and legible without authorial winking—no phrases like “absurdly” or “of course this fails”—and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or references to specific scholarly texts.
