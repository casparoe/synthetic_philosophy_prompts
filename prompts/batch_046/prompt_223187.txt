I've been lurking in some stoic self-help spaces lately and there's this weird pattern I keep running into. People will say that since other people's opinions are outside their power, they shouldnt worry about being judged — thats standard enough. But then they extend the same logic to obligations: they say whether my friend is disapointed by me missing the event is outside my control, so I shouldnt feel guilty, even though choosing to miss it was totally internal. Meanwhile they still take full credit for being disciplined or virtuous. So the boundary between internal and external gets drawn like a one-way mirror: it blocks blame and obligation going out, and judgment coming in, but never touches self-praise. I've seen this with work commitments, family stuff, even just showing up late.

Is there actually an established name for this specific lopsided application — maybe something in the modern revival literature? Or is it just generic rationalization wearing Stoic clothing? If there's no term, can you coin one and map its edges: how is it different from honest detachment, does it require genuinely believing the framework or just using it selectively, and where exactly does it stop being Stoicism and start being hypocrisy? I dont need citations, just want the carving — what this thing is, what it isnt, and whether I'm describing something real or just inventing a pattern.
