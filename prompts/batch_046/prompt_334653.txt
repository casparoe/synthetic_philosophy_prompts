I have come to accept a Davidson-inspired position that I call continuous radical interpretation: the process of maximizing charity and rationality against a shared environment is not confined to encountering an unknown speaker, but applies without interruption even among long-familiar interlocutors, so that there is never a settled background of shared meanings—only a dynamically renegotiated equilibrium. From this I draw a further, more contentious consequence: first-person authority over one’s own mental contents is not foundational but derivative, arising from the same interpretive practices one applies to others rather than from any privileged access, which in turn implies that the concept of misunderstanding is conceptually prior to that of understanding, since agreement is always a local achievement against a standing possibility of divergence. What would genuinely count against this package? I want the defeaters mapped by decisiveness: formal results in coordination theory or game theory that show stable communication without continuous reinterpretation, empirical findings from developmental or comparative psychology about pre-linguistic coordination that do not rely on charity, phenomenological arguments that restore first-person authority without collapsing into Cartesian privacy, and thought experiments involving self-deception or private language that survive the standard Davidsonian response. For each category, indicate whether a committed defender of continuous radical interpretation would concede damage or dismiss it, identify any qualifications that have insulated the view from empirical risk and whether that insulation is a theoretical virtue or a vice, and specify what feature—if removed—would collapse the case back into a standard problem about radical interpretation rather than exposing my extensions. I am particularly interested in whether the move to familiar interlocutors and the reversal of the misunderstanding-understanding priority can be detached and rejected while leaving core Davidsonian radical interpretation intact, or whether attacking those extensions necessarily undermines the framework itself.
