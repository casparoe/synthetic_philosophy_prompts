Please explain the conceptual distinction between an epistemic bubble and an echo chamber by applying it to a structurally specific case: a community in which epistemic credibility is assigned recursively, so that a source receives weight only if it has already been endorsed by at least two existing members. Here, outsiders are not actively distrusted; rather, they are invisible because they lie outside the endorsement graph. Analyse whether this arrangement is best characterised as a bubble, a chamber, or a distinct hybrid, paying careful attention to the difference between passive informational exclusion and reflexive distrust. Furthermore, discuss how this recursive credibility topology constrains belief revision even when individual members are, in principle, open-minded.
