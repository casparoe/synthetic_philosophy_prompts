He framed it as the thermostat idea philosophers talk about, applied to language models, and said it should license the conclusion that all our belief-talk about AI is purely instrumental — handy for guessing the next token, but not tracking anything like actual cognition. I want to audit that analogy properly. What maps onto what — is the algorithm's engagement metric like the LLM's next-token objective, is the user's click-through like my reply in the chat? Where does the mapping break, especially since LLMs generate first-person self-reports and explicit reasoning traces that recommendation engines never produce? Does the analogy quietly smuggle in the idea that intentional language is always fictional, or that internal mechanisms don't matter for attribution? And if the disanalagies matter, what's a better analogy for what's going on — treating it like a character in a novel, or like an actor playing a role?
