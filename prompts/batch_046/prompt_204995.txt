I have recently been appointed to evaluate evidentiary reliability in a probate dispute involving conflicting eyewitness testimony, which has led me to study how knowledge survives skeptical challenges. I understand that Nozick’s tracking theory holds that a subject knows that p only if p is true, the subject believes p, and across nearby worlds in which p is false the subject does not believe p, together with the corresponding adherence condition. I also understand that tracking is widely said to violate epistemic closure: a person can track that she has hands, correctly deduce that she is not a brain in a vat, and yet fail to track that she is not a brain in a vat, because in the nearest world in which she is a brain in a vat she still believes she is not one. But here is what puzzles me. If tracking is a modal property of the belief-forming mechanism, and if in that very same nearest vat-world she also fails to track that she has hands—since there she lacks hands yet still believes she has them—then does not the breakdown of tracking for the premise automatically entail a corresponding breakdown for any belief derived from it? In other words, if the sensitivity condition is violated at the root in world W, it cannot be satisfied downstream in W, so the derived belief is not claimed to be knowledge there either. Does this not mean that tracking actually preserves closure, by ensuring that whenever a premise lacks tracking status in a counterfactual scenario, the conclusion also lacks it? So why do philosophers present Nozick’s view as denying closure, rather than as redefining closure so that it holds relative to the tracking status of the premises in each world?
