Student answer: Cohen's bootstrapping problem shows that if process reliabilism is true, then using a reliable process like color vision to check its own reliability gives us easy knowledge. For instance, you look at a red table with your eyes, then use your eyes to check that your eyes work, then use your eyes again to confirm the table is red. This creates a circle where the justification for the particular belief depends on the general reliability that was itself justified by the particular belief. Vogel responds with his table example, arguing that knowing the table is red and knowing vision is reliable are just two seperate knowledge claims that don't depend on each other. If we apply this to the neural prosthetic, since it is a first-order system without recursive meta-access, it can't check its own calabration history using its own outputs, so the loop is physically broken. Therefore, restricting reliabilism to first-order processes solves Cohen's objection because there's no meta-level to generate the circularity. The prosthetic just outputs signals without ever asking whether those outputs are trustworthy, so easy knowledge never gets off the ground.
