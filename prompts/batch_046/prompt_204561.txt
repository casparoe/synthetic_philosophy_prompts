Please argue that the Luring Lottery undermines, rather than exemplifies, the symmetric-reasoning model usually applied to the Platonia dilemma. Defend the claim that genuinely superrational agents—who recognize that others share their reasoning—should in the lottery converge on correlated abstention rather than on a random coin-flip entry, because the extreme payoff asymmetry between being the sole entrant and being one of many creates an epistemic instability that forces mutual exit. To do this, introduce and defend a specific coordination mechanism—stochastic resignation with observable commitment—through which agents can align on abstaining without direct communication, and show why this mechanism resolves the tension between the dilemma’s requirement of identical outcomes and the lottery’s reward for unique action. Keep the argument self-contained and do not appeal to bibliographies, citations, or external reading lists.
