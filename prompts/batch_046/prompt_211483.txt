I've been working through the standard literature on Leibniz's Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles and Black's well-known counterexample involving two qualitatively identical spheres in an otherwise empty symmetric universe, and I find myself stuck on the practical metaphysics: if such entities genuinely existed, how would any administrative or referential practice function? I'm writing a conceptual exercise for a course on metaphysics of identity, and I'd like you to produce a satirical piece that inhabits this problem from the inside.

Write a deadpan corporate Employee Orientation and Policy Handbook—approximately 600 words, formatted with numbered sections, bullet points, and a sidebar—for new staff at the "Symmetric Universe Residential Authority" (SURA), the bureaucratic body that manages housing allocations for residents of Black-Type spherical units. The voice should be that of an HR manual: dry, precise, relentlessly procedural. The handbook should cover onboarding procedures for property managers assigned to two numerically distinct but qualitatively identical spherical apartments (designated Unit Alpha and Unit Beta); protocols for handling tenant complaints that "my sphere has been swapped with my neighbor's" despite no detectable difference; a policy memo on the 2023 Ontological Housing Code Amendment that permits "weak discernibility" via external relational properties but explicitly prohibits internal residents from invoking such relations for dispute resolution; and a sidebar announcing the national holiday honoring Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, including the ceremonial reading of the law and the traditional refusal to admit that the two spheres attending the parade are actually different entities. The satire should exaggerate real philosophical tensions—how reference and individuation collapse without qualitative difference, whether relational properties genuinely save the principle, and the absurdity of treating metaphysical identity as a matter of bureaucratic record-keeping—without ever stating them as explicit objections. Invent the bureaucratic details (forms, codes, holiday rituals) freely, but keep the underlying philosophical moves accurate to Black's scenario and to standard responses like weak discernibility.

Please stay deadpan throughout: the humor should come from the gap between the mundane corporate language and the metaphysical impossibility of the task being administered. After the piece, append a brief meta-note—no more than a short paragraph—indicating where the parody takes liberties with Black's original argument (for instance, whether the spheres are genuinely relationless or whether weak discernibility is available) and flagging the single point in your rendering that you are least confident about regarding the actual state of the debate. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
