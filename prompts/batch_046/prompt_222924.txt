In our philosophy-of-artificial-intelligence seminar, we have been examining embedded agency, and one analogy that keeps surfacing is that an embedded agent is like a chess player who is simultaneously one of the pieces on the board: she can influence other pieces, yet she is herself subject to capture, she lacks any external vantage point from which to survey the whole board, and every move she makes alters the very environment she is reasoning about. Instructors and course materials invoke this to argue that standard decision-theoretic frameworks — which presuppose an observer who stands outside the world, receives information, and then intervenes — must be radically revised or abandoned. I want to audit this analogy with some precision. First, lay out the explicit correspondence table: what in the source domain plays the role of the player, the other pieces, the rules, the board's boundaries, and the event of being captured; and what in the target domain corresponds to the agent, its environment, the physical or logical constraints, and the failure modes? Second, isolate the disanalogies that matter for the intended conclusion from those that are merely decorative — for example, whether the chess player retains a mind that is not made of wood, whether she possesses self-knowledge that a physical system might lack, and whether there is an external rule-enforcer that has no counterpart in nature. Third, ask whether the analogy quietly smuggles in a dualism between the reasoning self and the embodied piece, precisely the kind of separation that embedded agency is meant to reject, and whether that hidden homunculus is carrying the argumentative weight. Finally, determine whether the conclusion — that we must jettison the external-observer model of rationality — actually survives these breaks, or whether it was the analogy doing the work all along. If the chess analogy collapses at the critical points, sketch a better analogy that preserves the core problem — that the agent is part of the system it models and modifies — without importing an unseen pilot.
