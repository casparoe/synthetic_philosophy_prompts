I'm writing a paper for my metaphysics seminar on the ontology of dispositions, and my study group has been stuck on a specific variant of the fink/mask problem. We understand the classic cases: a fink removes the disposition at the very moment the stimulus occurs—think of an electro-sensitive wire that loses its conductivity precisely when touched—whereas a mask leaves the disposition intact but blocks its manifestation, as when an antidote neutralizes a poison without destroying the poison's toxicity. But we want to push this further. Consider a compound system—say, a cognitive architecture with a feedback loop—where an internal monitor detects an impending trigger and activates a blocker. Depending on whether we describe the monitor's intervention as occurring "before" or "simultaneous with" the trigger, the blocker seems to function either as a fink (canceling the base disposition) or as a mask (merely intercepting the causal chain). The temporal framing appears arbitrary, yet the metaphysical classification differs sharply.

Your argument should address why the feedback-loop case isn't just an edge case but exposes a structural feature of any dispositional property that involves self-monitoring or regulatory mechanisms.
