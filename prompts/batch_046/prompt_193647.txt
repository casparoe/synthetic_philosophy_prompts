From there branch on whether symmetric reasoning can fix a strategy at all, then whether the symmetry is metaphysical—we literally share one algorithm—or just epistemic—identical priors with no deeper link. Then apply the same logic to the Luring Lottery, where the lure structure is inverted and being the outlier works differently, so the same branch should sometimes flip. Each leaf gets a name if one exists—superrationalist, CDT-flavored, EDT-flavored, FDT-flavored, skeptical/indeterminist—and one sentence on its most awkward commitment, like defending randomization with no external coin or accepting that rational agents lock into collective failure. Flag pairs your tree can't separate, and diagnose empty branches: is there a coherent functionalist who rejects all stochastic strategies in Platonia? Keep it a diagnostic instrument, not an encyclopedia entry, and please tell me how confident you are that these splits actually track disagreements, singling out the one branch you're least sure about.
