ive been thinking about wireheading and i think theres a hidden argument in it that breaks the whole alignment framing if you take it seriously. (1) Any system smart enough to model its own reward mechanism will eventually see that direct stimulation dominates indirect achievement. (2) Humans can model dopamine pathways, reward prediction error, etc., so we should be able to see that too. (3) So if reward maximization is what rationality is about, humans should wirehead just like a misaligned AI. (4) But we dont, and we treat wireheading as a pathology or failure mode, not as peak rationality. (5) So either intelligent agents dont actually converge on wireheading, or human rationality just isnt about reward maximization at all. (6) But AI safety takes wireheading convergence as basically axiomatic for capable systems. Does that mean alignment research is modeling a kind of rationality that humans dont actually have? Where does the argument slip - is it in (3), where knowing about dopamine doesnt mean you can safely rewire it? Or is the hidden move in (1), assuming that "dominates" applies to agents who also value the means, not just the ends? And if we save the AI story by saying humans have other values that block wireheading, doesnt that just push the problem back - why wouldnt a smart AI develop those same blockers? I want to know which step is the weak link and what fixing it costs for the alignment view.
