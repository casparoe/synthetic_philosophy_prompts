Here is an argument I built for a seminar and now find suspicious because the conclusion is self-undermining. (1) If a cognitive mechanism is reliable, its outputs must be corrigible either by the mechanism itself or by an independent check. (2) Introspection is the mechanism that produces first-person reports about mental states. (3) In confabulation and choice-blindness paradigms, subjects report causes and preferences that do not match the actual drivers of their judgments; the reports are post-hoc constructions rather than direct tracking. (4) Crucially, the structural form of these reports is identical to veridical ones—same confidence, same phenomenology, same first-person authority—so there is no internal marker that distinguishes confabulation from accuracy. (5) Therefore introspection cannot correct its own errors; any attempt to verify a report just generates another report of the same kind. (6) Nor is an independent check available from the first-person stance, since observing one's own behavior or brain states already requires an introspective framing of what is being observed. (7) So introspection fails the reliability condition in (1) for all mental content that is causally influenced by hidden processing—which, on a predictive-processing model where mental content just is high-level prediction weighted by precision, is essentially all mental content. (8) Hence introspection is globally unreliable. (9) But (3) and (4) depend on first-person reports from experimental subjects; if introspection is globally unreliable, those reports are evidentially worthless, and the studies that motivate the argument collapse. (10) So the argument undermines its own evidential base and proves too much. Where does it go wrong? If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (3) than (5), but I want to know what I'm paying—especially whether rejecting (6) commits me to a form of mental-content externalism, whether rejecting (7) requires denying that predictive processing applies to self-modeling, and whether there is a hidden equivocation between "unreliable about causes" and "unreliable about content" between steps (3) and (7).
