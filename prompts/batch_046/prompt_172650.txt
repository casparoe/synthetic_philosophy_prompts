I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar and we've been arguing about that color phi phenominon — you know, the green dot that seems to turn red mid-flight even though the screen just flashes red elsewhere. My prof mentioned two ways to explain it: either the brain rewrites what you remmember seeing (the Orwellian story) or it builds the middle experience only after both dots have arrived and pretends it happened in real time (the Stalinesque one). I tried to push the Stalinesque idea to its limit and now I think I've talked myself into something ridiculous.

Here is the chain: (1) In color phi, the conscious experience of motion and color-change in the middle is constructed after both stimuli have already been processed. (2) If the brain can do that — present a constructed experience as happening "now" when it's actually stitching together past inputs — then there's no special reason this only happens for vision. (3) Every conscious state takes some processing latency, so technically every "present" experience is assembled from neural events that are already in the past. (4) So if Stalinesque stitching explains color phi, it should also explain ordinary present experience: what I call "feeling this pain right now" is actually a representation assembled a split-second later, mis-stamped with the present tense. (5) But here's where it gets worse. That mis-stamped representation isn't just about the world; it's about itself. I don't just feel pain; I feel that I'm feeling it now. That self-reference is part of the content, and it too is stitched together after the fact. (6) So there's a recursive loop: the brain stamps "this is happening now" onto a representation of pain, then stamps "I am experiencing this now" onto the representation of the first stamp, and so on. (7) At no point in this stack does consciousness actually coincide with the neural event it's tracking — every layer is delayed and mis-timestamped relative to the one below it. (8) Therefore there is literally never any conscious experience that occurs at the time it represents itself as occurring; consciousness is always "late for its own party," and the present moment is always a fiction. (9) But this is absurd — it means I'm never actually in pain when I think I am, only constructing a retrospective "now-pain" that points to an unconscious neural event. Yet suffering feels immediate. (10) So Stalinesque revision can't be right, not just for color phi but for anything.

My friend says the mistake is probably in (5) or (6), that self-reference doesn't need the same stitching mechanism. But I don't see why not — if predictive processing handles all content the same way, why would meta-representation be exempt? If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (3) and (4) and just deny the recursion, but I'm not sure what that costs me in terms of explaining why color phi feels so seamless. Where does this actually break, and what do I pay if I patch it where my friend suggests?
