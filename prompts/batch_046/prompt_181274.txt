I am drafting the final examination for my graduate seminar, Consciousness and Cognitive Architecture, and I want an essay prompt that cannot be answered by reciting the standard taxonomy of Block’s access-phenomenal distinction or by summarizing Baars’s and Dehaene’s global-workspace framework. The difficulty must come from applying these ideas to an unfamiliar architectural scenario rather than from recalling labels. To that end, please construct a 90-minute essay question—calling for an answer of roughly 800 to 1,000 words—built around the following hypothetical system, which I will call Bifurcated Workspace (BW). BW contains two functionally independent broadcasting mechanisms: Workspace-A globally distributes representational content for report, reasoning, and behavioral control, but it is stipulated to carry no phenomenal quality whatsoever; Workspace-P generates rich, structured phenomenal qualities, yet it is isolated from report and reasoning mechanisms except through an unreliable “transduction module” that frequently corrupts or drops content before anything reaches Workspace-A.

The essay should ask students to evaluate whether BW demonstrates that phenomenal consciousness can exist without access consciousness—thereby vindicating Block’s separation—or whether the isolation of Workspace-P from direct global accessibility undermines its claim to consciousness, thereby supporting a strong global-workspace identification of phenomenal consciousness with broadcast accessibility. Students must explicitly address three cruxes: first, whether the noisy transduction channel counts as a form of access consciousness and, if so, whether it is sufficient to collapse the access-phenomenal distinction; second, whether a global-workspace theorist should revise her view to accommodate “phenomenal broadcasting” that lacks cognitive accessibility, or should instead deny that Workspace-P is conscious at all; and third, whether the scenario reveals that the distinction is conceptually unstable once architectural broadcasting conditions are fully specified, or whether it remains robust but forces us to accept “phenomenal systems with only partial and corrupted access” as a live conceptual possibility.

Please provide the full exam question, a model answer of approximately 600 words, and a marking scheme on a 25-point scale. The scheme should identify five must-hit analytical moves—each assigned a specific point value (totaling 25) and accompanied by a one-line description of how candidates typically fail on that point—such that an answer that merely summarizes Block and global workspace theory receives no more than 8 points, while an answer in the A range (22–25) must resolve the tension between the transduction module and the criteria for genuine global broadcast rather than merely naming it.
