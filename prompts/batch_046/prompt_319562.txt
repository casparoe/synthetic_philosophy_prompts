I've been mulling over guilt vs shame for ages and I think I've landed on a view that's probably wrong but I can't shake it. Basically I reckon guilt is the better moral emotion because it's about what you did, not who you are, so you can fix it and move on. Shame just says you're rotten at the core and there's no repair job. I also think a culture could basically choose to favour one over the other — not overnight, but by slowly changing whether people see themselves as seperate units with their own rules or as nodes in a big web where your rank matters. There's that old guilt-culture vs shame-culture thing but I think that's too neat.

Anyway, what would actually count against this? I'm not after a reading list or citations — I want to know what evidence or arguments should genuinely move me. Like, what if there are societies that tried to push guilt and it made people more isolated or violent? Or what if philosophers have shown that shame is actually necessary for seeing other people as fully real, not just obstacles? And I had this other idea that shame always needs an imaginary audience even when you're alone, like you're still performing, but guilt is just talking to yourself — if that's rubbish does it break my whole thing?

Rank the defeaters for me: what's fatal, what's just awkward, and what's something I'd probably dismiss but shouldn't? And be honest — has my view already been qualified into something unfalsifiable, and if so is that actually a problem?
