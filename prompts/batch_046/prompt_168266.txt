I am a doctoral student in political philosophy working with a constitutional design group that advises intergovernmental regulatory bodies, and I am trying to translate the standard taxonomy of negative, positive, and republican liberty into a reusable audit instrument. Write a formal decision procedure—structured as an ordered sequence of tests with explicit decision nodes and escalation flags—that a regulatory impact officer (legally trained but without philosophical background; operating under a 72-hour review deadline; required to sign a standardized certification form) can apply to classify whether a proposed rule primarily threatens liberty as non-interference, as non-domination, or as effective self-direction. The procedure must survive translation into non-technical language, but it must preserve the distinctions that actually do the conceptual work: specifically, it must be able to identify domination that operates without direct interference, interference that does not constitute domination because the power is fully accountable, and capacity-deprivation that involves neither interference nor arbitrary power.

To prevent the procedure from being a mere restatement of textbook definitions, build it around three deliberately ambiguous regulatory architectures that do not map cleanly onto standard examples: (1) an algorithmic nudging architecture that reshapes choice environments without prohibiting any option and without any human agent holding discretionary power over the subject; (2) an asymmetric disclosure mandate that requires private entities to reveal decision-relevant information to regulators but not to the affected parties, thereby creating an informational dependency relationship that is structurally one-sided; and (3) a delegated veto structure in which a regulatory body may block private contracts only upon receiving a non-binding recommendation from an external advisory panel appointed through an opaque process. For each architecture, provide a worked application showing step-by-step how the procedure classifies the liberty threat, including at least one case in which the procedure identifies a republican-liberty violation despite the absence of any identifiable negative-liberty interference, and at least one case in which negative-liberty and positive-liberty considerations pull in opposite directions and the procedure must record the conflict without resolving it.

The procedure should include a brief internal-consistency check—two or three hypothetical mini-cases that an officer can apply in under ten minutes—to verify that they are using the distinctions rather than relying on intuition about whether a rule feels coercive. Explicitly flag every point where philosophical disagreement makes the classification indeterminate and requires escalation rather than unilateral sign-off, particularly the question of whether domination can exist without the dominated party's awareness, and whether arbitrary power can be eliminated by procedural accountability alone. State clearly the constraints that bind the officer's judgment: they may not invoke democratic legitimacy or overall welfare to override a liberty classification, they must document which node produced the determination, and they may not substitute the procedure for a substantive judgment about the correct trade-off between liberty types when they conflict.

Finally, include a precise statement of what the procedure cannot do: it cannot determine whether a domination relationship is arbitrary from the perspective of the dominated party's own values, it cannot authorize the regulatory scheme itself, and it cannot resolve conflicts between liberty dimensions by appeal to a master value. Deliver the procedure itself, with the worked cases embedded, not a theoretical defense of any particular conception of liberty.
