I'm on this county task force called the Conditional Access Review Board — we have to write a memo by tommorow recommending whether to keep running the "tiered reciprocity" pilot for unemployment benefits. The pilot says claimants recieve full support only if they do 15 hrs/week of job training or community service, but there's a split: if you miss hours due to documented mental-health flare-ups you drop to a lower tier with reduced payments but no formal sanction, but if you miss for "unverified personal reasons" you get suspended entirely. Some members think this is smart because it separates structural barriers from personal failure; others say it invents a new class of "partially undeserving poor" that makes no philosophical sense, since we're pretending we can cleanly divide choice from circumstance when deciding who gets full dignity.

I need the memo to pick a concrete path — expand, modify with specific rule changes, or kill it with a 90-day wind-down — and it has to take the strongest opposing view seriously rather than just waving away the other side. I'm not looking for legal citations, reading lists, or bibliographies, just a clear conceptual argument about conditionality and moral desert that gets into the weeds. Btw I was chatting with a friend who brought up moral hazard, but I'm not sure that applies since this pilot isn't about fraud detection, it's about sorting people into tiers of deservingness before any fraud even happens. Can you draft it so it's readable for the board but actually engages the conceptual mess, with a clear recommendation and timeline included?
