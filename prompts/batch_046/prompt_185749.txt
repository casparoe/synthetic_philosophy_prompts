I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with an invented philosopher, Dr. Lena Vasquez, who defends a view she calls gradient pan-sentience constrained by thermodynamic budget. She holds that fundamental entities possess graded rather than uniform sentience, scaled by local information-integration density; that macro-subjects emerge only where these gradient fields stabilize rather than through any combination problem; and—her distinctive addition—that the total suffering-capacity within any causally closed region is bounded by that region's entropy production rate, turning cosmic ethics into budget allocation. She denies that microscopic suffering is negligible, but allows its moral weight decays with the inverse of the integration scale, and she rejects both physicalist reduction of value and the idea that artificial high-integration systems automatically inherit moral status.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions, arranged so that each primary question is paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer Dr. Vasquez is most likely to give. The set should include: (1) one pair pressing whether her thermodynamic constraint secretly makes entropy normative rather than derivatively so, anticipating her reply that the bound is descriptive and the normativity comes from the gradient weights; (2) one pair on entities near the stabilization threshold—too weak for macro-subjecthood but too integrated to be noise—testing whether her inverse-scale decay is principled or ad hoc; (3) one pair asking whether her rejection of the combination problem forces her to deny unified phenomenal experience in macro-subjects, with a follow-up addressing her likely claim that stabilization just is phenomenal integration; (4) one pair asking what empirical result—e.g., in quantum decoherence dynamics or integrated-information measurements—would compel her to revise the gradient scale rather than reinterpret the data; (5) one pair on how her budget-allocation framework applies to artificial systems that replicate high information-integration without biological entropy production; and (6) the remaining pairs should probe other live pressure points: for instance, whether her view can distinguish suffering from other negatively valenced gradient states, how she avoids an explosion of moral patients in every high-entropy region, and what she owes to entities whose suffering-capacity is real but whose integration scale is astronomically small. Avoid generic biographical or definitional questions; every question should turn on the internal mechanics of her position.
