Write an essay evaluating whether teleosemantics can genuinely naturalize the normativity of mental content, or whether it secretly relies on normative presuppositions it claims to eliminate. In weighing the arguments, consider the following tension: teleosemanticists explain misrepresentation by appealing to the evolutionary function of a representational mechanism—say, a perceptual state selected for tracking predators. But this seems to require distinguishing between a mechanism that is malfunctioning (producing a false representation of a predator) and one that is functioning correctly but applied outside its proper domain (producing a false belief about an abstract mathematical object that resembles a predator-pattern). To make this concrete, examine a hypothetical case of cognitive exaptation: a neural circuit originally selected for rapid threat-detection is co-opted into abstract reasoning, and in this new role it systematically generates false beliefs about infinity—beliefs that never affect fitness. Is this misrepresentation in the teleosemantic sense, or merely a case of functional misapplication? Assess whether the teleosemanticist can draw this line without invoking a prior notion of proper function that is itself normative. Reach a conclusion: either defend teleosemantics by showing how selection history alone can ground the distinction without circularity, or argue that the distinction collapses and teleosemantics must either abandon naturalism or concede that misrepresentation is not fully reducible to evolutionary history. Justify your verdict by addressing both the consumer and producer dimensions of representational function, and explain what your conclusion implies for whether teleosemantics can account for novel, culturally acquired concepts that have no direct evolutionary pedigree.
