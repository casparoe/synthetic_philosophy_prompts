I want to test whether this comparison can bear the weight of the non-reductionist conclusion it is usually made to serve, rather than merely decorate it. Next, identify the disanalogies that actually matter: whether the clay can persist as a formless lump when the statue is destroyed, whether there is any analogue to intentional artistic design in biological development, whether the 'same statue' can be reassembled from scattered fragments in the way a person might be reconstructed after catastrophic injury, and whether the relation of composition here is symmetrical. Separate the breaks that undermine the argument for non-physical persistence from those that are merely picturesque. Finally, state whether the analogy survives as a genuine argument or whether the conclusion was doing all the work, and propose a more honest analogy — if one exists — for the relation between a person and their bodily substrate over time.
