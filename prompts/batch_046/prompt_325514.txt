Rejecting materialism on mystical rather than empirical grounds, he claimed that the observer-effect demonstrates consciousness creating physical reality, and he dismissed Schopenhauer's influence on his thinking as a misreading propagated by later commentators. From this he concluded that death is strictly illusory: the dissolution of one brain merely returns a localized mode of awareness to the undifferentiated whole, with no real loss of being.

Before I commit this to dialogue, I need an accuracy audit that separates what genuinely reflects Schrödinger's published reflections—particularly his engagement with Vedantic sources, his remarks on the oneness of mind, and the actual role Schopenhauer's metaphysics played in his formation—from the parts that conflate his views with New Age interpretations of quantum mechanics. Please identify which distinctions the summary collapses, what a careful reader of his essays would defend and reject, and whether there are subtle features of his view—such as his precise attitude toward empirical science or his specific citations of Upanishadic texts—that the précis smooths over into generic spiritualism.
