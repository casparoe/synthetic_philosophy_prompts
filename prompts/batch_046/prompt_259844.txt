A town amends its charter so the waterfront stays public forever, with a clause saying not even a 99% vote can sell it for 50 years. That feels right — protecting future people from a corrupt council. A gambler asks their bank to block casino withdrawals with no override ever; fine, classic move. A country writes into its constitution that the debt ceiling needs an 80% supermajority, knowing it'll cause periodic crises. Not fine — binds future legislatures to a game of chicken. A person writes an enforceble contract saying if they get dementia and seem happy, they still refuse life-extending care, and a trustee must block family objections. I keep flipping — sometimes wise autonomy, sometimes the present self is tyrannizing a future happy self. Someone sets up an auto-system: every missed self-set deadline sends $500 to a party they hate. Not fine — it's self-coercion that doesn't solve anything, just punishes.
