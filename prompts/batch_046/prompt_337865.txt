I recently listened to a podcast in which the host tried to draw a precise parallel between Darwin and Turing, framing both as examples of what he called competence without comprehension. His summary went like this: Darwin showed that organisms display exquisitely engineered traits—eyes, camouflage, cooperative behavior—without any guiding intelligence grasping the principles of engineering or the goals being pursued; nature achieves full biological competence while remaining entirely devoid of comprehension. Turing, on the same pattern, demonstrated that a mechanical system manipulating symbols according to fixed rules could generate correct mathematical answers without possessing any understanding of mathematics, meaning, or the problem being solved. The host argued that these two cases share an identical logical structure—complex, successful output produced by a process that lacks all internal grasp of what makes the output successful—and that recognizing this structure should change how we think about achievement generally. He went on to claim that Darwin thereby made teleological reasoning obsolete, since once selection explains adaptive purpose without any purpose in the selector, asking what a trait is for becomes a misleading question that presupposes a mind where none exists. Likewise, he said Turing made introspective reasoning about cognition obsolete, because if a universal machine produces the right result mechanically, asking how the system understands the task is a category error rather than a real problem. I am not sure how much of this holds together. Is the parallel between Darwin's removal of a designer's comprehension and Turing's removal of a computer's comprehension genuinely illuminating, or does it conflate biological adaptation with symbolic computation? Which parts of the summary are accurate representations of Darwin's and Turing's actual arguments, which parts overstate them, and does either thinker actually license the further conclusion that reasoning about purpose or internal understanding has become unnecessary?
