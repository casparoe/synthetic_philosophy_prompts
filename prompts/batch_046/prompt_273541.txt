Write the desiderata that any adequate philosophical account of nomic self-reference—legal and constitutional rules that govern their own amendment or repeal—must satisfy. The account must resolve specific constructed cases without collapsing distinctions: a clause that authorizes its own suspension, a meta-rule that applies to itself, and a hierarchy in which a higher-order rule and its direct object are identical. For each case, specify the phenomenon to be explained, the conceptual distinction that must be preserved (for instance, between procedural self-reference and substantive self-entrenchment, or between logical contradiction and practical unworkability), and whether consistency with democratic legitimacy or strict separation of powers is a non-negotiable constraint or a camp-specific preference. Identify any subset of requirements that appears jointly unsatisfiable—particularly if both absolute immunity from amendment and full self-amending authority are demanded of the same rule. Finally, indicate briefly how a hierarchical rule-of-recognition model, a dynamic coherence framework, and a paradox-accepting account fare against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard.
