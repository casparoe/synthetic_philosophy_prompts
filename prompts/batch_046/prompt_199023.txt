I'm working through my position on actual infinity for a philosophy of mathematics discussion and want a consistency audit before I defend it. Here are my commitments:

(a) I accept Cantor's results and the reasoning of Hilbert's hotel as describing objective properties of actually existing infinite sets, not as mere calculational fictions.

(b) I am an ontological finitist about the physical world: space, time, and all concrete magnitudes are actually finite.

(c) I hold a principle of mathematical conservatism: if a mathematical domain systematically conflicts with our best-confirmed physical ontology, we should regard its objects as useful idealizations rather than mind-independent structures.

(d) I think the counterintuitive results in Hilbert's hotel—such as accommodating new guests in a fully occupied infinite hotel—reflect a genuine conceptual tension internal to the notion of actual infinity, not merely psychological unfamiliarity.

(e) Nevertheless, I think we should preserve classical set theory with its actual infinities, because it is indispensable to modern analysis and physics, regardless of whether those infinities are physically instantiated.

Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity and, for each, specify exactly which commitment or commitments must be adjusted and what that costs the overall view.
