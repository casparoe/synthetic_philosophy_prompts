I just finished a long argument with a friend who studies evolutionary biology. She insisted that if we cannot identify what a trait is selected for, we have failed to explain it; I countered with the image of the triangular spandrels in San Marco—spaces that exist only because arches are needed to support the dome, carrying no structural purpose of their own, yet later decorated as if they were designed features. I want to test whether my intuition, that biology is full of analogous non-functional byproducts mistaken for adaptations, holds up under rigorous translation, or whether it collapses once the architectural metaphor is replaced by actual engineering mechanics.

I am a structural engineer. I think in terms of load paths, primary versus secondary members, design intent versus emergent mechanical function, and—crucially—the phenomenon of exaptive retrofitting, where an originally ornamental element (say, a cornice) is later reinforced and becomes load-bearing, rendering the original design intent empirically undecidable without destructive testing. I have no formal biology training.

Please restate both the adaptationist research program and the Gould-Lewontin spandrels critique using only that vocabulary and those distinctions. Be precise about which evolutionary concepts map onto structural purpose, which onto ornamental spandrels, and which onto retrofitted members. Then deliver the residue report: what survives the translation intact, what must be distorted (for example, whether natural selection can be rendered as design intent without smuggling in a designer), and what has no counterpart whatsoever—particularly whether the evolutionary distinction between being selected for a function and being selected as a byproduct of another feature survives, or whether, in engineering terms, prolonged retrofitting eventually makes every element load-bearing and the distinction disappears. I need to know whether the residue reveals a genuine limitation in my engineering framework, or whether it exposes the spandrels critique as less substantive than it appears once its metaphorical language is replaced by mechanical analysis.
