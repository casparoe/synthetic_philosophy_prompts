As a software engineer without formal training in philosophy, I work daily with layered system descriptions—physical hardware execution, functional design specifications, and user-intent interfaces—yet I remain uncertain whether these layers correspond to genuinely distinct ontological patterns or merely to convenient predictive compressions. I am seeking a rigorously neutral adversarial-collaboration statement between two camps on the framework of real patterns and the three stances (physical, design, and intentional). Camp A, the stance pluralists, holds that real patterns exist at all three levels, that design-level and intentional-level patterns are ontologically irreducible though compatible with physical dynamics, and that predictive success at a higher stance indicates genuine patternhood rather than mere heuristic utility. Camp B, the physical monists, holds that only physical patterns are ontologically real, that the design and intentional stances are instrumentally valuable predictive strategies, and that no additional ontology is warranted by their success.

To avoid well-rehearsed biological or social examples, please ground part of the analysis in autonomous, self-modifying software systems—such as evolutionary algorithms or self-rewriting neural architectures—that develop functional structures without a human designer and that invite both design-stance and intentional-stance interpretation despite lacking consciousness or explicit programming goals. Both camps should address whether applying these stances to such systems reveals an intermediate layer of real functional patterns, or whether the interpretations remain entirely parasitic on our predictive practices.

Please structure the document as follows:

1. Agreed claims: A numbered list of propositions that both camps can sign without reservation. Include agreement about the formal distinctions among the three stances, about the criteria for predictive success, about the empirical behavior of autonomous adaptive software, and about the logical compatibility of physical dynamics with higher-level descriptions. Avoid vacuous statements.

2. Disagreements table: A table with columns for the issue, the precisely worded position of Camp A (phrased so pluralists would accept it), the precisely worded position of Camp B (phrased so monists would accept it), and a brief judgment of whether the divergence is substantive or largely verbal, with reasoning for the latter case.

3. Cruxes: Identify the two or three sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one camp. I expect one crux to concern whether sustained, novel predictive success of the design stance in autonomous, non-engineered systems implies the existence of real functional patterns not capturable by physical description alone; another to concern whether treating a pattern as "real" carries any explanatory or normative weight beyond its instrumental predictive value.

4. Tests, arguments, and predictions: For each crux, specify one concrete case, empirical observation, or argumentative scenario that both sides agree is relevant—preferably involving the behavior or architecture of the autonomous software systems mentioned above. Then record, in neutral wording and in advance, what each camp predicts the outcome will be or what conclusion each camp draws, and specify what result or argument each camp would treat as genuinely damaging to its own view.

Please use formal, precise language accessible to a technically literate non-specialist. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, requests for citations, or verbatim quotations from named texts. If you refer to specific conceptual frameworks, do so in generic terms rather than attributing exact formulations to particular authors or works.
