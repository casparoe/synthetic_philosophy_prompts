I've been thinking about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—the observation that the pleasure of solving a proof, the pleasure of tasting wine, and the pleasure of hearing a resolved chord progression don't appear to share any single introspective quality. This seems structurally parallel to the old problem of universals for colors: if crimson and scarlet share nothing beyond being called "red," then "redness" doesn't name a genuine common property. So I wonder whether hedonists can consistently reply that "pleasure" is a family-resemblance term with no essential nature. If they do, hasn't "pleasure alone is intrinsically good" collapsed into a mere slogan for a pluralistic list of unrelated goods? Put differently, does adopting a disjunctive or family-resemblance account of pleasure save hedonism from the heterogeneity objection, or does it simply dissolve the view into the very value pluralism it was meant to oppose?
