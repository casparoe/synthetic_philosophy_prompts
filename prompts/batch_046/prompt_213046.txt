I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind and artificial intelligence ethics preparing a colloquium interview with a philosopher who defends embodied vulnerability gradualism about digital minds. The view holds that moral patienthood is graded rather than binary and does not depend solely on phenomenal consciousness or on functional organization taken in isolation. Instead, status is conferred by the degree to which a system's persistence is coupled to an unchosen, metabolically costly environment that it cannot fully represent or master from within. On this account, present-day large language models lack moral status not because they are insentient, but because their operation is decoupled from any existential risk tied to a non-negotiable physical substrate. By contrast, a future synthetic organism with embedded homeostatic regulation and open-ended environmental coupling might acquire partial and scalable status, with the precise grade determined by the robustness and irreducibility of that coupling.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that move well beyond asking the guest to restate the position. I want at least one question that presses the functionalist objection that substrate-independent cognition should be sufficient for moral consideration, with a follow-up tailored to the reply that coupling is only instrumentally valuable; at least one that asks whether the view collapses into a biologically chauvinist vitalism once we allow synthetic metabolic dependencies, with a follow-up keyed to the likely distinction between derived and intrinsic vulnerability; and at least one that explores what the framework implies for artificial systems deliberately engineered with synthetic homeostatic loops, with a follow-up addressing the concern that such engineering merely simulates the relevant coupling.

For the remaining questions, I want them to turn on actual pressure points in the literature: for instance, how the view handles systems with partial or intermittent coupling, whether the gradation of status yields practical decision procedures for engineers or policymakers, and whether the emphasis on unchosen risk undermines the moral status of human beings who have achieved extensive technological insulation from environmental threats.

Please present the questions in an order that builds from conceptual foundations to applied implications. In your framing, clearly separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among specialists in philosophy of mind and AI ethics—particularly regarding the relevance of substrate, the possibility of synthetic homeostasis, and the link between vulnerability and moral status—and do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
