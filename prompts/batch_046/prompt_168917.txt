I'm on a course about epistemology and we've been doing the regress problem — foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism, the usual lot. Last night I was arguing with my flatmate over pints and I think I accidentally built an argument that proves justification can't exist at all, which feels absurd. Here is the chain I ended up with:

(1) If belief B is justified for me, then I must actually hold a reason R that I can cite, and R must itself be justified. (2) So either there's a basic belief with no reason (foundationalism), the reasons loop (coherentism), or there's an infinite chain (infinitism). (3) Foundationalism is out because basic beliefs are just arbitrary stopping points — epistemically irresponsible, like ending a maths proof with "trust me bro". (4) Coherentism is out because circular support is vicious. If A supports B and B supports A, that's not justification, it's mutual flattery. (5) Infinitism requires me to actually possess an infinite chain of reasons. But I'm a finite creature with a finite brain, so I can't. (6) So none of the three standard positions work.

(7) Now, I half-invented, half-remembered this idea called "modal infinitism": the chain doesn't run through my actual beliefs but through counterfactual ones in nearby possible worlds. For B to be justified, there must be an infinite chain of reasons availible across worlds where I'm slightly more informed. (8) But if the chain lives in other worlds, then in the actual world I don't have the reasons. So I'm not actually justified — it's just that a modal neighbour is. (9) Therefore justification requires conditions no actual human can meet, which means nobody is ever justified in anything. (10) But people clearly are justified in ordinary things — I know I have hands, I know water boils at 100°C. (11) So (1) must be false, and justification doesn't require actually holding reasons?

That can't be right though. I feel like something slipped between (7) and (8), or maybe between (5) and (7), but I can't name it. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd really like to keep (1) and (3) if possible, because it feels like justification should be first-personal and that arbitrary stopping points are dodgy. But I'm open to losing anything if that's the price.
