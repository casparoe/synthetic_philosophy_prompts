I'm advising a small interdisciplinary research collective—philosophers of science, logicians, and philosophers of mathematics—who have four years and modest funding to investigate the contemporary legacy of Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism." The group is not interested in historical exegesis; they want to know which open conceptual problems, arising specifically from Quine's collapse of the analytic-synthetic distinction, are most worth resolving now, because other active debates depend on them. In particular, they are concerned with a tension that is rarely made explicit: on the one hand, Quine's global holism seems to erase any principled boundary between changing one's logical framework and changing one's empirical beliefs; on the other hand, much current work in conceptual engineering and philosophy of science appears to treat certain framework-internal questions—say, whether a particular mathematical structure is definable within a given physical theory, or whether normative terms within a scientific paradigm are governed by internal rules rather than empirical testing—as if they enjoy a kind of local or graded analyticity without committing to the Carnapian global distinction Quine rejected. The collective needs a defended agenda, not a survey.

Which lines of work should they prioritize, and in what order? I'm especially interested in how you would weigh: (a) whether logical and mathematical constants can be individuated or revised without smuggling back in some form of analyticity; (b) whether scientific theory choice can be rationally reconstructed if every belief, including those about logic, is in principle revisable in light of experience; (c) whether conceptual engineering in domains like ethics or scientific taxonomy implicitly relies on a rehabilitated, domain-relative notion of the analytic that Quine's arguments do not actually rule out; and (d) any other upstream question you think I have omitted. For each, explain why it is upstream or downstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers rather than empirical scientists, and what kind of conceptual clarification—rather than empirical discovery—would cause you to reorder the list. The deliverable should be a prioritized research agenda with explicit criteria—importance to adjacent debates, tractability within four years, and dependency structure—rather than a bibliography or reading list.
