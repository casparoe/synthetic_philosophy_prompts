I'm trying to put my finger on a pattern I keep seeing in accounts of decision-making under perfect indifference, and I'm not sure whether philosophy or psychology already has a precise name for it.

Consider an agent who must choose between two options—say, two equally nutritious meals, two identical job offers in different cities with no distinguishing feature that matters to her, or two routes home that are genuinely equal in time, scenery, and effort. She is not paralysed like Buridan's ass; she picks one, perhaps by an arbitrary tie-breaker such as "the one on the left." So far, this is familiar. But here is the part that interests me: after the choice, she does not merely accept the outcome. Instead, she retroactively constructs a substantive preference. She begins to cite the left-hand position itself as a reason—"I prefer things on the left"—and in subsequent similar dilemmas she treats that feature as a genuine criterion, even though she had no such preference beforehand and received no new information. This is not quite rationalisation in the usual sense, because rationalisation typically defends a choice that was already made for some hidden motive; here the original choice had no motive whatsoever. It is also not straightforward learning, since no feedback revealed the left option to be better. It looks more like the arbitrary tie-breaker crystallising into a real preference through the mere fact of having been acted upon.

I've seen this described informally, but I can't tell whether it belongs under an established heading—perhaps a specific form of cognitive dissonance reduction, or a variant of path dependence in revealed preference theory—or whether it remains unnamed. Is there an established concept that captures exactly this retroactive solidification of an arbitrary tie-breaker into a durable preference? If so, how does it differ from ordinary post-choice rationalisation and from adaptive preference formation? If not, could you propose a precise term with clear boundary conditions—distinguishing it, for instance, from cases where the agent genuinely discovers a hidden preference, or where she simply develops a habit without attributing normative weight to it?
