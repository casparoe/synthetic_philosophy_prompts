I've come to think that "avoiding side effects" for an AI doesn't actually need a full specification of human values. The idea is to use a counterfactual absence baseline: compare the world-state trajectory with the agent running against the trajectory where it was never deployed, and constrain the agent to only make deviations that are strictly required for its task output. You aren't ranking outcomes by value, you're just measuring causal footprint relative to a null trajectory, so the value-loading objection should miss. But I'm not fully convinced. What would actually count against this view? In particular, I'm worried about what I call the "stability asymmetry" — in open systems the counterfactual baseline is itself unstable without continous human maintenance, so preserving it might force the agent to take over upkeep that looks exactly like a side effect. Is that fatal, or just a framing glitch? I want an honest map of the defeaters: which ones should make a committed counterfactual-baseline person concede defeat, which ones just force a tweak, and whether the view has already been qualified into unfalsifiability. Don't just list objections; rank them by how decisive they'd be, and say whether anything — a formal result, a concrete case, a conceptual distinction — could actually settle it.
