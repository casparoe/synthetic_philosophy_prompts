After a long argument with a friend about whether a biopic owes the dead strict fidelity or only avoidance of calumny, I want the full specification first. Write the desiderata that any adequate normative theory of our representational obligations to deceased real persons in historical fiction and biopics must satisfy. For each requirement, state the motivating case or distinction behind it — for instance, the gap between falsifying a documented public act and inventing a private interior monologue; between harming living descendants and damaging posthumous reputation; and between artistic licence that illuminates character and licence that exploits suffering for spectacle. Identify which desiderata are non-negotiable, such as consistency with established historiography where moral culpability is at stake, and which are camp-specific preferences, like an absolute prohibition on counterfactual dialogue versus a permitted speculative psychology. Crucially, address whether the theory must account for what I will call archival displacement: the phenomenon in which a dramatized version becomes the dominant cultural record, crowding out documentary sources in collective memory, and say whether accommodating this is compatible with a broad defence of artistic freedom. Flag any pair of requirements you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justifications rather than on scoring rivals, though you may briefly note how a consent-based view and a dignity-based view fare against the list. Write in British English, and finish with a one-sentence summary as the very last line.
