hey so i run an upper-level epistemology seminar, 12 grad students, 90 minutes. i want them to feel the dogmatism paradox — not just the closure principle on paper, but the actual pressure where knowing p seems to license you to dismiss any q that conflicts with it, and then using that dismissal to justify p feels circular. can you design a classroom game that makes them run into this?

here's the niche framing: call it a belief-market with closure contracts. each student gets a private base-belief card (mundane stuff like "the thermostat is at 72") and we secretly deal three "skeptical defeater" cards (e.g., "this room is a high-fidelity simulation"). they trade confidance points in rounds, betting on whether others' beliefs survive. but there's a contract rule: if you publicly declare you "know" your base belief, you are locked in for the round — you must treat your assigned defeater as irrational to even consider, and you recieve a bonus payout if your belief survives resolution. the three defeater-holders can only play their card if the group hasn't collectively dogmatized it away; once someone invokes the closure contract against a specific defeater, it's banned from discussion entirely.

run the trading for about 35 mins, then a resolution round where we score consistency and award points (no real money, just points toward picking next week's reading). i want them to discover that declaring knowledge was strategically smart — it protected their payout — but epistemically costly, because they ended up excluding possibilities by fiat and then tried to cite that exclusion as support for their original belief.

then debrief: map the contract clause to dogmatism, the payout bonus to the appeal of closure, and the banned-defeater problem to the circularity worry. be honest about what the activity can't reproduce — probably the internalist intuition that entitlement is a first-person mental state rather than a social rule, so you'll need to supply that part in discussion.

also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time. i think someone will refuse to sign the contract to keep their options open — how should i handle that without breaking the game?

write this up with clear rules, the token/payoff scheme, a rough timeline, and the honest limitation note. and please say how confident you are in this design and single out the point you're least sure about — maybe whether the strategic incentive actually mirrors epistemic entitlement or just collapses into social conformity.
