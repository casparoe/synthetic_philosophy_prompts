I'm constructing a classification instrument for a philosophy-of-technology seminar (graduate level, roughly fifteen participants) focused specifically on whether "the AI alignment problem" denotes one problem or several that share only a name. Please design a decision tree with no more than six binary branch points, yielding between eight and ten leaves.

Start from the root question: "If a highly capable system produces severe unintended harm, should we diagnose the root failure as..." Then branch by concrete claims about the failure mechanism, not by author names. The nodes should ask whether: (a) the intended objective was correctly specified but optimized too literally in novel contexts (proxy or reward-hacking failure); (b) the system's internal representations diverge systematically from human concepts in domains where training coverage was sparse (representational misalignment); (c) the harm emerges primarily from interaction effects among separately aligned subsystems or agents (multi-component dynamics); (d) the harm reveals that no stable, formalizable objective captures human values in open-ended domains (conceptual instability); and (e) whether remedying these failures requires primarily technical correction, institutional governance, or conceptual reconstruction of the framing itself.

The leaves must include at least: the unitary technical-specification view; the plural institutional-governance view; the representational-divergence view; the multi-agent interaction view; and the conceptual-incoherence view. Identify any unoccupied logical combinations—such as a view that treats the problems as genuinely plural but insists all sub-problems are reducible to a single mathematical correction—and briefly diagnose each as incoherent, merely unexplored, or unattractive.

Every node should be answerable without knowing which named position it maps onto. Flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely cannot separate—particularly whether proxy misalignment and representational divergence can be distinguished in practice without circular reference to the intended remedy. Keep the deliverable free of bibliographies, citation requests, or appeals to specific texts that require outside access. At the end, state how confident you are in the tree's logical completeness and identify the specific branch point you are least certain belongs in the taxonomy rather than being a contingent or artificial distinction.
