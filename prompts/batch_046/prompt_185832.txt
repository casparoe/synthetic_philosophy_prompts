I'm preparing a consistency check for remarks I'm giving in a philosophy of modality workshop, and I'd like an audit of whether my commitments actually hang together. Here is where I stand: (a) I reject strong modal rationalism—the fact that I can imagine or conceive of some scenario is not, by itself, a reliable direct guide to its metaphysical possibility. (b) At the same time, I treat conceivability as a fallible but legitimate heuristic for epistemic modality—if a state of affairs is genuinely inconceivable to me (not merely hard to visualize), I take that as defeasible evidence that it is epistemically impossible for me, or that my application of the relevant concepts is mistaken. (c) I think that many classic conceivability arguments in philosophy of mind—especially zombie-style arguments—rest on a confusion between psychological imagination and genuine modal intuition, so I deny that the conceivability of a physical duplicate lacking consciousness gives direct metaphysical leverage against physicalism. (d) Despite (c), I find myself regularly invoking zombie conceivability in debates, treating it as a serious reason to think physicalism is false and that consciousness involves non-physical properties. (e) I also hold that when a scenario is genuinely and irreducibly inconceivable—say, a round square—this inconceivability is purely logical or conceptual, and carries no weight whatsoever about metaphysical possibility beyond marking a conceptual contradiction.

Which of these pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled without loss, and what is the least costly revision to restore full consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each say what would have to give—especially whether abandoning (d) is unavoidable or whether there's a more surgical fix that lets me keep the anti-physicalist conclusion on other grounds. If some commitments pull in opposite directions only because I'm running together epistemic and metaphysical modality, say so explicitly; but if there's a hidden contradiction in my reliance on conceivability tests after rejecting modal rationalism, trace it precisely.
