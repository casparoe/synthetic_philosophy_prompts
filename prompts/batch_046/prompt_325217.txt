Please compose a satirical, deadpan “Executive Summary” in the voice of a management-consultancy report—approximately 350 words, with a hard ceiling of 400 words—addressed to a fictional Institute for Geriatric Social Choice. The report should apply Arrow’s four conditions—unrestricted domain, non-dictatorship, Pareto efficiency, and independence of irrelevant alternatives—to the democratic selection of amenities in a retirement community for aging scientists. The satire should target the bureaucratic tendency to invoke formal impossibility results as a pretext for abandoning collective deliberation and installing authoritarian decision-making. To keep the humor technically precise rather than generic, treat each Arrow condition as a literal human-resources policy: for instance, interpret independence of irrelevant alternatives as a mandate to disregard complaints about garden layout when ranking dining-hall menus, and interpret unrestricted domain as the right of every resident to propose an infinite, cyclical preference ordering over swimming-pool temperatures. The report must invent and reference—without quotation marks—an imaginary “Generalized Arrow–Müller Theorem for Non-Transitive Preferences in Geriatric Care,” which treats incommensurable Kuhnian paradigm shifts as irrelevant alternatives, thereby injecting a niche conceptual extension. The conclusion should follow the logic to its reductio: that the only Arrow-compliant community is one governed by a benevolent dictator, namely the narrator himself, a retired physicist who now understands that democracy is impossible and that he must therefore choose everyone’s retirement plan. Maintain sustained irony: the voice should remain formal, polished, and entirely straight-faced, so that a specialist in social choice recognizes the exaggeration is fair and the mathematics is not being mangled. Do not include bibliographies, citations, requests for further reading, or any named living person.
