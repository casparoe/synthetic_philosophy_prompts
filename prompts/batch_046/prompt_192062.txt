Here's an argument about Zhuangzi's butterfly dream that I've been turning over and now find suspicious, because it seems to dissolve not just dream skepticism but any stable first-person self-ascription. (1) In the dream, Zhuangzi is a butterfly with butterfly-experiences; upon waking, he is Zhuangzi with human-experiences, but neither state carries an internal marker that establishes epistemic priority over the other. (2) If no criterion internal to either state can designate it as the "real" one, then self-knowledge requires a transcendent standpoint that neither state provides. (3) But any attempt to invoke a transcendent standpoint—say, the perspective of the transformation of things (wuhua)—merely introduces a third state with its own experiential content, not a neutral arbiter. (4) Therefore, any claim of the form "I am X" made from within any state is equally unsupported, since the "I" always refers only to the subject of the present transformation, with no grounds to privilege one transformation over another. (5) This applies not only to dreams but to waking life, aging, emotional shifts, and even conceptual revolutions: each is a transformation that reconfigures the "I" without leaving a continuous substrate that persists across changes. (6) So either we abandon all first-person self-ascription, or we accept that the self is nothing but a transient node in an endless series of transformations with no persistent owner. (7) The second option seems to destroy moral responsibility, since no agent persists long enough to be the subject of praise or blame across actions separated by transformation. (8) The first option makes practical reasoning and long-term commitment impossible. (9) Therefore, our initial acceptance of (1)—that dream and waking are genuinely epistemically symmetric—must be rejected, and we must locate an asymmetry that saves self-knowledge without appealing to an impossible transcendent view. Where does this go wrong, if it does? If I have to choose, I'd prefer to keep the symmetry intuition in (1) and the claim that wuhua offers no neutral arbiter, but I'm willing to pay the cost of giving up the slide from epistemic symmetry to the dissolution of all self-knowledge if that's where the flaw actually lies.
