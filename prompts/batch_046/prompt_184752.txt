Having retired from experimental physics, I have been trying to understand how archaeologists move from sherds and foundations to claims about social institutions. Here is the conceptual difficulty that keeps eluding me. Imagine a site yielding uniform standardized weights but no administrative texts. From these objects one might infer an institutional commitment to fair exchange, yet the material record seems equally compatible with centralized enforcement and with emergent peer convention. Now suppose further excavation reveals that every weight was fired in the same kiln. Intuitively, that might suggest a monopoly producer and hence centralized control; but conceptually it only seems to push the ambiguity one level back—between a state-run workshop distributing weights to traders and an independent artisan serving dispersed clients who collectively demanded consistency. Please explain, in intuitive terms, why this is not merely a temporary gap in the evidence but a structural feature of archaeological inference: specifically, why tracking physical regularities in artifacts cannot, by itself, discriminate between different normative architectures that could have produced them, and what this implies about the limits of reconstructing institutional beliefs from material culture alone.
