I'm working through the boundary between legitimate self-calibration and vicious epistemic circularity, particularly in what I think of as layered bootstrapping: using outputs from one cognitive or instrumental layer to validate the input conditions of that same layer, where the layers may share an underlying error structure. Here are my gut verdicts. (2) A child acquires her native language from caregivers and later uses her fluent competence in that language to judge that they taught her well: this seems legitimate—she genuinely knows they taught her well—despite the circular appearance. (3) A philosopher builds an ethical system through reflective equilibrium, then cites the system's internal coherence as evidence that her initial moral intuitions were trustworthy: this feels like illegitimate bootstrapping, not knowledge. (4) A patient compares her home blood-pressure cuff against a clinic device of a different make and model, then trusts future home readings: legitimate knowledge of the cuff's accuracy, even though assessing both displays involves her own literacy. (6) A historian uses archival document D1 to establish that archive A is generally reliable, then uses other, independent documents from A to study the past: acceptable, unlike using D1 to validate itself. (7) The case I keep going back and forth on: a mathematician proves a consistency result for axiom system S using methods that are themselves formalizable within S, then uses that result to claim she knows S is consistent. Is this vicious circularity, acceptable self-certification, or something in between?
