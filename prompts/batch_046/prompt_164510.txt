Write a dialogue between two philosophy grad students sitting in a lounge arguing over a tweaked Judy Benjamin case. Here Judy hears over a crackly radio the phrase "if you're not at the forward base then you're at HQ," but the transmiter is buggy: it sends that exact conditonal message 70% of the time when she's really at HQ and 30% of the time when she's actually still at the base. One student thinks she should update her beliefs using Jeffrey conditioning on the conditional content itself; the other argues that because of the noise she should treat the signal as evidence about the transmiter's location rather than her own, and that standard updating rules break down when the channel is unreliable. Make them both sharp — no strawmen — and have them actually respond to each other's specific objections, not just talk past each other. Keep the tone casual, like a real conversation over coffee, not a formal paper. You can leave a small typo or awkward phrasing in if it fits the vibe.
