I hold a reconstructed version of Winner's thesis that artifacts have politics: not hard determinism, but the claim that technical systems embed a political constitution — specific orders of authority and dependency that become invisible once stabilized. On this reading, democratic control is possible mainly at the design stage, not through later user reinterpretation or regulatory correction, which makes the design-stage claim central. What would count against it? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, sorted from decisive to merely awkward: historical episodes where users or regulators substantially altered an artifact's political effects after deployment; conceptual objections that design-stage control is either impossible or itself politically naive; and whether the view risks being insulated by defining any post-deployment change away as a failure of democratic design rather than as a counterexample. I also want an honest assessment of whether this reconstructed position is well insulated, and if so whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice.
