I am writing for a reading group in a course on the metaphysics of normativity, and we have been circling a problem that feels both obvious and strangely ignored: the possibility of genuinely free-floating rationales—normative reasons that persist and exert force without being held by any agent, embedded in any institution, or anchored in any mental state. My interlocutors keep treating these entities as if they are ontologically cheap, mere background conditions that need no custodial explanation; they speak of reasons out there the way one might speak of background radiation. To see whether that complacency survives literalization, I want a sustained piece of satire that takes the metaphysics at face value.

Please write a deadpan internal memorandum—approximately 550 to 650 words—from the fictional Interagency Council for Ontological Housekeeping, addressed to regional compliance directors. Use a formal bureaucratic header (TO / FROM / RE / DATE / CLASSIFICATION), numbered sections (§1 through §4), and at least three footnotes in faux-legalistic prose. The voice must be that of a mid-level administrator who has fully internalized the ontology: free-floating rationales are real, they accumulate, they can be misappropriated, and they require inventory. The target of the satire is the philosophical stance—call it unanchored normative realism—that accepts such reasons as unproblematic fixtures of the world, together with the discursive habit of treating them as if they need no account of individuation, custodianship, or binding force. The irony should be sustained and deadpan: do not wink, do not break character, and do not invent a straw-man position that no one holds; instead, exaggerate a real feature of the view (the treatment of normative force as independent of any reasoner) and follow its administrative logic to an absurd conclusion. Invent a concrete case study, such as a distributive rationale discovered in an abandoned transit corridor with no claimant, and show the bureaucratic framework buckling under questions of ownership, decay, and unauthorized clustering. A specialist should recognize that the protocols mirror actual debates about the queerness of objective values, the problem of normative individuation, and the gap between reasons and agents.

At the end, append a brief note—about 100 words, clearly marked as outside the fiction—in which you assess your own confidence in the philosophical mapping. Identify the specific point where the satire is least secure (for instance, whether it unfairly collapses normative realism into a theory that denies all agency-dependence, or whether defenders of objective reasons possess resources—such as constitutive accounts of agency or relational theories—that the bureaucratic reductio overlooks) and flag any liberties taken with the literature. Please avoid bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading; the point is to work with the concept as it stands, not to document its provenance.
