(1) If an action carries any non-negligable chance of generating astronomical suffering—say, from misaligned digital minds or synthetic organisms—then that expected suffering lexically dominates ordinary goods.
(2) Almost every policy we could choose, including funding safety research, banning it, or writing a white paper, carries some nonzero s-risk tail.
(3) So to avoid total paralysis, we must be able to compare these risk profiles across actions.
(4) But with potentially unbounded suffering, expected-value comparisons fail to converge unless we impose an arbitrary cutoff or discount rate.
(5) Any cutoff we pick looks unjustified—why cap the harm at 10^40 suffering-beings rather than 10^41?—and without one, every option remains impermissible.
(6) Therefore either we are frozen in moral paralysis, or we smuggle in an ad hoc parameter that saves the framework but undermines its claim to be uniquely rational.
(7) What's worse, advocating for s-risk awareness is itself a policy with its own risk tail, so the framework can't even be promoted without self-undermining.
