In philosophy of mind there's this long stall between people who say cognition penetrates perception and people who say it never does. Both sides cite the same experiements—like the tired runner seeing the finish line closer, or expectations changing how long a line looks—but they just talk past each other. My working theory is that they're not actually disagreeing about the brain, but about whether 'perceptual experience' has to include only the first-order sensory quality or can also include a kind of second-order framing—like whether you're perceiving the line as long or perceiving-it-as-long-with-a-certain-attitude. Is that the real split? Or is there a hidden empirical question about exactly where in the processsing stream the expectation operates? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey: verbal disagreement about 'perception', substantive disagreement about evidence standards, or two groups answering different questions—one about phenomology, one about information flow—under one heading? And honestly, could anything—a new experiment, a sharper distinction, or just admitting it's ill-posed—actually end it?
