Defend the view that Kuhn's thesis of incommensurability has been misinterpreted as a denial of rational theory choice, when in fact it imposes a stricter discipline: one must first reconstruct a rival paradigm's internal problem-solving criteria before any comparison can be rational. To avoid overused historical examples, apply the argument to a contemporary analog—evaluating transformer-based language models against newer state-space architectures. Show why treating standardized benchmark scores as paradigm-neutral metrics repeats Kuhn's diagnosed error of applying one framework's success conditions to judge another, and explain what an evaluation genuinely sensitive to incommensurability would require instead.
