My judgments about which reference class should govern when an individual belongs to multiple classes with divergent statistical or normative profiles, together with a tentative principle that I suspect fails to unify them.

Case 1 — Taxonomic specificity. A particular penguin in captivity. The class "birds" predicts flight; the class "penguins" predicts none. Verdict: the narrower taxonomic class governs; I predict it will not fly.

Case 2 — Structural identity versus manufacturing distinction. A compound that is isomorphic in every biologically active respect to a known carcinogen, except for a single isotopic substitution that leaves its biochemical interaction profile unchanged. The class "this molecular structure" predicts carcinogenicity; the class "isotopically modified compounds" is statistically unknown. Verdict: the structural class governs; the isotopic distinction is causally inert and should be ignored.

Case 3 — Spurious correlation versus causal correction. A specific lightbulb manufactured on Tuesday during a factory error that lowered mean lifespan to 500 hours, but it bears a quality-inspection sticker applied only to Tuesday bulbs that passed a secondary check; inspected Tuesday bulbs have the normal 1000-hour lifespan. The classes are "Tuesday bulbs" (500h), "inspected bulbs" (1000h), and "all bulbs of this brand" (1000h). Verdict: the inspection class governs, not the Tuesday class, because the inspection tracks the absence of the defect that actually drives failure.

Case 4 — Small-sample specificity versus statistical power. A rural electoral district with a seventy-year record of supporting Party A by a 70% margin, but with demographic and economic indicators identical to two hundred other districts that split evenly. The classes are "this district's own history" and "demographically matched districts nationwide". Verdict: the broader demographic class governs; I reject the district-specific history as too small a sample to support a reliable prediction, even though it is causally closer in space and time.

Case 5 — Functional architecture versus material substrate. An artificial system implements a processing architecture that is functionally isomorphic to human nociceptive pathways, but it runs on silicon rather than neurons; its training corpus included both benign and malicious reward structures, yet its active safety filters are engaged. The relevant classes are "biological nociceptive systems" (susceptible to harm), "computational pain-algorithms" (no clear harm), and "systems with active safety filters" (protected). Verdict: I am inclined to let the functional architecture govern—if the causal pathway that produces suffering is present, the material substrate seems irrelevant—so abrupt shutdown would constitute harm. Yet I also feel that the training-data class and the filter class should weigh in, and I cannot say how.

These judgments seem to point toward selecting the class that is homogeneous with respect to the mechanisms that actually produce the outcome, while discarding distinctions that are causally inert or statistically unreliable. But Case 4 forces me to abandon the narrowest class for sample-size reasons, and Case 5 leaves me unsure whether functional isomorphism should override material and historical differences. So my tentative formulation—choose the class that is maximally specific to causally operative properties and maximally invariant under interventions on inert properties—fails to resolve the tension between Case 3 and Case 4, and it gives no clear answer for Case 5.

Is there a coherent principle, or a small family of candidates, that can accommodate all five verdicts? If not, which verdict should be dropped? More precisely: what clause should govern the trade-off between causal specificity and statistical reliability? How should functional isomorphism be weighted against material substrate and training history in normative attribution? And can you state the principle precisely enough to deliver a verdict on a further case—say, a synthetic organism that is 80% human DNA but includes a synthetic pathway that blocks the viral receptor targeted by a particular pathogen—without appealing to an ad hoc adjustment?
