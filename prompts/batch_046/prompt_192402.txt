For my seminar on collective responsibility and the historiography of the First World War I have been asked to set out my philosophical commitments for consistency checking. Here is where I stand:

(a) I accept the Fischer thesis in broad outline: in July 1914 the German leadership deliberately exploited the crisis to risk a continental war, so Germany bears primary causal responsibility for the escalation from local conflict to general war.

(b) I hold that collective moral responsibility is real and can attach non-reductively to institutional agents such as states; it is not merely an aggregate of summed individual blame.

(c) I deny that "the German people" in 1914 constituted a democratic agent capable of bearing distributed moral blame, given the severe representational deficits of the Kaiserreich.

(d) I regard Article 231 of the Versailles Treaty as defensible in assigning state-level liability for reparations, but conceptually confused in framing that liability as the "war guilt" of an abstract nation rather than of specific institutional actors.

(e) I maintain that explaining the outbreak requires both narrative reconstruction of diplomatic intentions and structural covering-law factors (alliance rigidity, mobilisation schedules), but I also subscribe to a constraint-reduction view: structural claims are adequate only if derivable without remainder from the institutional decision-rules governing the actors involved.

(f) I believe that present citizens of a state can inherit remedial obligations arising from its historical wrongs, even when they bear no personal moral guilt.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severely? For each genuine tension state the severity, identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency, and say what that revision costs me. Then tell me which single revision would resolve the most tension overall. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among specialists in philosophy of history and collective responsibility, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.

Please structure your response in British English as follows:

1. Brief restatement of the position (around 80 words).
2. Severe tensions, ranked (around 300 words): trace the conflicts, especially between explanatory constraint-reduction and non-reductive collective blame, and any other high-severity pair.
3. Moderate tensions (around 200 words): address the conflict between inherited remedial obligations and the democratic deficit.
4. Apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection (around 150 words): address the relation between (d) and (b).
5. The least costly single revision (around 150 words): which one commitment should give, and what it costs.
6. Settled versus contested (around 120 words): distinguish broadly accepted claims from genuinely disputed ones in this area.
