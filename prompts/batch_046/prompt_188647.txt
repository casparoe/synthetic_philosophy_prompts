Critics respond that once anekāntavāda is exported from metaphysics into practical reasoning, its logical safeguards—the "syāt" qualifier that conditions every assertion—are inevitably diluted. Reach and defend a clear verdict—either that anekāntavāda should remain a metaphysical logic of perspectives with limited normative application, or that it can be reconstructed as a practical framework for pluralist ethics without losing its anti-absolutist rigor.
