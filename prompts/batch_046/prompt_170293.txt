I run a small philosphy workshop for about 8 grad students, 90 minutes every thursday afternoon. I'm trying to come up with an activity that makes them feel the actual pull of expected-value fanaticism, but I want to sidestep the usual tired framing about stomping flowers to save galaxies, since they just roll their eyes and stop engaging. Instead, I want them to run into it organically through moral uncertainty about something they genuinely disagree on — like whether future digital minds or simulated agents should count as full moral patients.

The idea is that each student writes down their private credance on that question, and then the group has to collectively allocate a small real budget — say $30 for our shared snack fund — between an immediate certain good (buying nice coffee for today) and a longshot donation that only has value if digital minds matter and we can influence their creation conditions. The rules should be arranged so they build the EV calculation themselves, see that even a 0.01 credance in an astronomically large payoff starts swallowing the budget, and then notice they've arrived at a fanatical allocation. After that I need a debrief that maps each moment onto the philosophical problem — where the reasoning could be resisted, what it costs to resist it, and an honest admission of what this classroom version can't reproduce, like actual astronomical scale or genuine existential risk. Could you design the specific rules, roles, and debrief questions? And maybe warn me about predictable ways it'll go off the rails — like someone refusing to treat the budget as symbolic, or others just anchoring on their gut feeling without running the numbers.
