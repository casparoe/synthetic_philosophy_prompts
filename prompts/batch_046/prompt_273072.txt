A clinical psychology review I encountered recently frames Ian Hacking’s looping-effect thesis about psychiatric classification in the following way:

Psychiatric classifications are interactive kinds, unlike natural kinds such as quarks, because being sorted into a category changes the people sorted. Hacking claims this creates a two-level loop: first-order looping, where patients alter their self-conception and behavior upon receiving a diagnosis; and second-order looping, where epidemiologists and manual committees rewrite prevalence criteria and symptom thresholds because the classified population has shifted. From this, the review concludes that psychiatric categories have no independent biological substrate—looping entirely supplants natural causation with social causation—and therefore that debates over whether a disorder is real miss the point: the only reality that matters is the institutional feedback cycle. The review adds that this explains why conditions like trauma-related dissociation have expanded so rapidly in recent diagnostic revisions: not because clinicians discovered a hidden biological syndrome, but because the loop made the syndrome visible to itself.

I am unsure how much of this is Hacking and how much is the reviewer’s embellishment. Could you audit the summary carefully? In particular: (1) Separate what is accurate, what is a defensible simplification, and what misrepresents Hacking’s actual position—especially regarding whether he denies any biological substrate to psychiatric conditions. (2) Examine the two-level distinction between first-order and second-order looping: is this genuinely present in Hacking’s framework, a reasonable extension, or an invention that should not be attributed to him? (3) Assess whether the conclusion that institutional feedback is “the only reality that matters” follows from the looping-effect thesis, or whether it overstates the thesis by conflating interaction with elimination. (4) Evaluate the application to the rapid expansion of trauma-related dissociation in recent revisions: is this a legitimate illustration of looping effects at work, or does it ignore competing explanations that Hacking’s account does not rule out? Please go part by part, indicating where a careful defender of Hacking’s view would object and whether the review’s stronger conclusions survive those corrections.
