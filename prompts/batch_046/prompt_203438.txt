I am a retired physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science and epistemology, and I have found myself preoccupied by a puzzle that sits at the boundary between statistical reasoning and first-person belief. In the familiar Sleeping Beauty scenario—where a subject is put to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is tossed, and she is awakened once on Monday if the coin lands heads and twice, on Monday and Tuesday, if it lands tails, with her memory of prior awakenings erased—I understand that there is a well-known disagreement between those who think her Monday credence in heads should be one-third and those who think it should remain one-half. What I want to understand is not merely which answer is correct, but a deeper conceptual issue that introductory treatments often gloss over: whether self-locating belief requires a fundamentally different kind of evidential update from ordinary empirical learning, or whether the apparent paradox can be dissolved once we recognize that the centered content of her belief—that it is now Monday or it is now Tuesday—is actually a relational property that should be handled by standard conditionalization on her total evidence, including her knowledge of the experimental protocol.

To make this concrete, I have been considering a modified protocol. Suppose the coin is replaced by a fair three-sided die with faces labeled A, B, and C, and the awakening schedule is arranged so that she is awakened on Monday alone for outcome A, on Monday and Tuesday for B, and on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday for C, with the same amnesia condition applying throughout. Now, if she finds herself awake on Tuesday with no memory of a prior awakening, she can eliminate A. The conceptual issue I want you to explain is this: does her Tuesday credence in B versus C depend solely on the relative frequencies of Tuesday-awakenings within those branches, or does the fact that she has now survived a filter—having not been awakened on Monday with intact memory—introduce a selection effect that should be modeled differently from ordinary Bayesian conditioning? More specifically, I want you to explain whether there is a coherent distinction between updating on the indexical fact that I am awake now and updating on the non-indexical fact that there is an awakening occurring now, and whether treating these as equivalent collapses one side of the debate into the other, or whether some intermediate view remains available.

Can you explain, in detail, where exactly the disagreement lies, why the selection-effect reasoning feels compelling to one side and illegitimate to the other, and whether the three-outcome variant I have described actually changes the logical structure of the problem or merely scales the original two-outcome case? I would particularly value clarity on whether diachronic rationality requires her credence to shift back and forth as she moves from Sunday to Monday to Tuesday, or whether there is a single correct stance that remains stable once the protocol is fully grasped.
