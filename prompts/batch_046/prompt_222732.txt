I've been working through my position on consciousness for a seminar on cognitive architecture and want a consistency check before I present it. Here is the profile I have landed on: (1) I reject the Cartesian theater in both its spatial and temporal guises—there is no central stage, functional or anatomical, where conscious contents converge into a single display, and no privileged "now" that serves as a temporal bottleneck for experience. (2) I accept a version of Dennett's multiple drafts model: sensory processing produces parallel, competing content-fixations across distributed networks, and asking when one "became conscious" of a feature is structurally misleading because there is no single moment of entry into consciousness. (3) I nonetheless think the binding problem is a real explanatory task: any adequate theory must account for why color, motion, and shape are experienced as integrated features of one object rather than as isolated sensory fragments. (4) I think this integration is accomplished by distributed temporal synchrony and predictive cross-modal matching—there is no convergence point—yet the result is what I would call a genuinely unitary phenomenal field at each temporal slice. (5) I take introspective reports about temporal order and feature-co-occurrence to be generally reliable guides to the structure of consciousness, not mere post-hoc confabulations. (6) I also hold that in rapid-presentation phenomena—such as the color-phi effect or the flash-lag illusion—the reported misalignment between physical stimulus order and experienced order reveals that consciousness is retrospectively constructed over a brief integration window, not that there is an underlying unified stream being misreported.

Do these hang together? I want an audit that ranks tensions by severity, distinguishes genuine conflicts from merely apparent ones, and specifies the least costly revision in each case. I am especially unsure whether (4) smuggles back a version of the very theater I reject in (1), and whether (5) and (6) can be sustained alongside (2)'s denial of a single authoritative narrative. If only one revision could be made to restore coherence, which should it be, and what exactly would be lost? Please be specific about which pairs pull against each other and how badly, and do not treat all conflicts as equally serious.
