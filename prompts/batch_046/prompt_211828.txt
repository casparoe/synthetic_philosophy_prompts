I am developing a conceptual dataset on archives and the politics of record-keeping, and I want to move well past the introductory formulation that archives are simply “silent about the marginalized.” To that end, please draft the joint statement that two opposing camps—call them Epistemic Restorationists and Constitutive Absence Theorists—could both sign after a sustained, good-faith adversarial collaboration on whether archival gaps are correctable failures or structurally produced conditions.

The Restorationists hold that silence in the record is primarily an epistemic and resource problem: inadequate preservation, biased collection policies, and impoverished descriptive practices leave recoverable traces uncollected or mis-cataloged, and with expanded digitization, oral-source integration, and metadata reconstruction, the archive can become more representative without altering its basic institutional logic. The Absence Theorists maintain that silence is not an accident of preservation but a constitutive feature of the archive itself—produced by provenance protocols, state or corporate funding structures, and taxonomic schemes that determine in advance what counts as a document worth keeping—and that efforts to “fill” gaps often require marginalized subjects to translate their experience into the very categories that originally excluded them, thereby extending rather than disrupting archival power. Both sides are asked to treat seriously the niche conceptual problem I will call provenance inversion: situations in which the chain of custody for materials excluded from official collections becomes itself the primary form of historical evidence, but only for communities operating outside state or institutional cataloging systems.

Please produce the document in American English with three parts. For each crux, provide a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it—such as the documented endurance or collapse of a specific non-state archival model—together with each side’s explicit prediction about what that case will demonstrate.
