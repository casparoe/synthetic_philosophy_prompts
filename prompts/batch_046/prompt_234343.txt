In Zhuangzi scholarship there's a stale deadlock over the tub-drum passage after his wife dies: some read it as genuine cosmic equanimity, others as ironic mockery of Confucian mourning rites. Both sides keep returning to the qi-transformation lines and the butterfly dream, yet nobody shifts. My working theory is that the real stall isn't about death ethics but about whether Zhuangzi's cosmology permits a stable subject that could even 'accept' transformation — if self and world are both temporary configurations of qi, then grief and it's absence are equally groundless. So is this disagreement metaphysical (about personal identity across hua), semantic (about what 'acceptance' means without teleology), or just bedrock intuition? And could a distinction between first-person phenomenology and third-person cosmology in the text, or noticing that the drum is literally a misused funeral object, actually move it — or is it permanently frozen?
