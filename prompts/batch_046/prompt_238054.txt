Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Maya and Leo, who disagree about whether omissions can be genuine causes. Don't make either one sound dumb. They should actually respond to each others points—no talking past eachother.

Maya thinks omissions are just absences, and absences can't do causal work—only positive events can. Leo thinks omissions can cause things, especially when there's a clear duty or expectation in place.

To keep it from being the same old debate, have them discuss a specific weird case: a distributed AI monitoring system for a dam has a submodule that is designed to send a shutoff signal if seismic data crosses a threshold. One Tuesday the threshold is crossed, but the submodule doesn't send anything—not because it's broken, but because its update was paused by an engineer earlier that day. The dam overflows and floods a town. The engineer never touched the system that day; they only paused the update the week before.

Maya and Leo should argue about whether the engineer's omission (not un-pausing the update) caused the flood, or whether the cause is just the seismic event plus the missing signal. They should also talk about whether calling it an omission already sneaks in normative assumptions about what the engineer was supposed to do.

Make sure there's real back-and-forth—like Maya pointing out that if omissions cause things then we'd have infinite causes for every event, and Leo responding with a specific reply about normative baselines, not just repeating his view. Keep it casual, maybe with a typo or two in the prompt itself.
