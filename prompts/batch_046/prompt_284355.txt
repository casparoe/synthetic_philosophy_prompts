ok so i'm trying to get my thoughts straight for a discussion group on Confucian role ethics and i wrote down my commitments but something feels off. here they are:

1. i buy the basic idea that moral identity isn't pre-social — you're literally constituted by your roles (parent, child, friend, ruler, etc.). there's no bare self sitting underneath waiting to be assigned.
2. because of that, moral obligations are always role-specific. you can't wrong someone "just as a human being"; you wrong them qua father, qua citizen, qua student, etc.
3. the asymmetry in roles is real and justified — the ruler owes different things to the subject than vice versa, and that's not just cultural baggage, it's part of teh moral structure.
4. if you're genuinely outside every role — like a total hermit with zero relationships — then you're not a moral agent and you don't have moral rights either; you're kind of invisible to ethics.
5. but i also want to say there's a minimal universal role, "fellow human being," that gives everyone basic dignity and generates at least some obligations regardless of other roles.
6. and i think an AI could eventually enter a role (say, student or assistant) and gain standing, but only if it shows emotional reciprocity first — which makes me think there's some pre-relational capacity for relationship that has to be there before the role can even form.

i also worry that 1 and 3 might clash, since if my self is literally my subordinate role then being subordinate seems built into who i am, like it justifies oppression — but maybe that's just an appearance. do these hang together? i feel like there's a real clash between saying obligations are always role-specific and then sneaking in this universal "fellow human" thing, and between saying there's no bare self and then requiring pre-relational reciprocity for AI. can you audit the pairs, tell me which tensions are genuine and which dissolve, rank them by severity, say what the cheapest fix is for each, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most damage? don't treat them all as equally bad — i want to know where the real problem is.
