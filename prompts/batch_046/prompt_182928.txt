Please explain the conceptual issue of whether “carving nature at its joints” can be analyzed in terms of explanatory redundancy rather than shared essential properties. Specifically, consider the proposal that a taxonomy is genuinely joint-carving not when its members possess an intrinsic essence, but when adopting that taxonomy causes certain cross-cutting regularities to vanish from our best explanations—so that rival taxonomies must invoke ad hoc auxiliary hypotheses to account for the same phenomena. Take a committed position: does this redundancy-elimination criterion successfully distinguish natural kinds from merely useful groupings, or does it ultimately collapse joint-carving into pragmatic convenience? Give definite reasons for your view, and address at least one serious objection to it.
