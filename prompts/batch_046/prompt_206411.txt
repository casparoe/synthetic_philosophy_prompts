I seek your analysis of a disagreement between my sister and her partner over whether to try for a second child. She holds that, since a child not yet conceived does not exist to be wronged by omission, there is no moral reason to create one; yet if that child were born into serious hardship—amid climate instability and economic precarity—there would be a definite victim. Hence she believes they ought only to conceive if highly confident of flourishing. Her partner responds that this criterion, applied consistently, would have counselled against having their first child, whose life they deeply value; and he asks why potential people should be weighed differently from actual ones in a manner that privileges non-existence over risky existence. They want to know whether her reasoning captures a genuine moral distinction or collapses into a general objection to bringing anyone into being. Please identify the philosophical structure precisely, apply the relevant theoretical framework, and assess the strength of each side without giving generic life advice. In particular, say whether the moral difference between conceiving a new person and adopting an existing child in need supports or undermines her position. Finally, state explicitly what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would overturn your analysis, and where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
