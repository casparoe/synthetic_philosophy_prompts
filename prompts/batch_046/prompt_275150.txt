For decades, philosophers of time have circled the Rietdijk–Putnam argument: special relativity’s relativity of simultaneity appears to dissolve any privileged “now,” which is then taken to entail that past, present, and future events are all equally real—the block universe. The familiar replies—that presentism can deny metaphysical weight to simultaneity judgments, or that the inference mistakes coordinate-dependence for ontology—have circulated since the 1960s, yet the dispute remains static; the same moves are replayed without convergence. My working hypothesis is that the stall is not really about physics, but about an unstated disagreement over whether metaphysical commitment must be invariant under the coordinate transformations that physics treats as mere redescription—a point that general relativity’s hole argument makes stark, but that the block-universe literature largely sidesteps. Is that right, or is there a hidden formal disagreement about which structures in special relativity carry ontological weight?
