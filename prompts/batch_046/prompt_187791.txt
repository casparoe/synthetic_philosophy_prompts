I am designing the final examination for an upper-level metaphysics seminar on personal identity and modality, and I need an essay question that cannot be satisfied by reciting the familiar summary of origin essentialism. Rather than asking students merely to reproduce the standard distinction between essential and accidental origins, the question should present a constructed biological scenario and require them to analyse whether origin essentialism survives once its biological substrate is disaggregated.

Please frame the prompt around the following case: a child, call her C, is gestated using the cytoplasmic machinery of egg E and sperm S, but the nuclear genome within the zygote has been synthesised de novo so that its sequence is identical to the genome E and S would have produced naturally, yet it was never physically present in either gamete. Ask students to determine whether C is essentially identical to the natural offspring of E and S, and—crucially—to adjudicate between three interpretations of what fixes origin: gamete essentialism, genetic-sequence essentialism, and developmental-trajectory essentialism. They should then evaluate whether the modal intuition underlying Kripke’s claim that origin is necessary a posteriori remains intact once origin is split into these separable components. The question should be designed for a 90-minute essay of approximately 800–1,000 words, should avoid any request for bibliographies or citations, and should depend only on concepts familiar from a standard course on metaphysics and philosophy of science.

Together with the exam question, provide a model answer of roughly 700 words and a marking scheme totalling 20 marks, structured around five specific philosophical moves that an A-range answer must contain—for example: correctly distinguishing the three essentialist interpretations; identifying the precise point at which the synthetic case fractures the modal intuition; assessing whether sequence-identity is sufficient for origin-identity; considering whether cytoplasmic contribution matters; and reaching a nuanced conclusion rather than a simple yes or no. For each move, note the marks available and include a one-line description of the typical B-range failure—such as correctly listing the interpretations but failing to show how the disaggregation undermines the argument. Please write everything in British English. Finally, include a brief meta-commentary in which you state how confident you are in the philosophical conclusions of the model answer and explicitly identify the single point about which you are least certain.
