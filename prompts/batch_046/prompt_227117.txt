Specify the desiderata that any theory of rational choice among genuinely incommensurable values must satisfy to count as adequate, with particular attention to institutional contexts where individual preference aggregation is unavailable and agents must make transformative commitments. For each requirement—covering the cases it must explain (hard choices between qualitatively different goods, Buridan-style ties that are not quantitative equality), the distinctions it must preserve (incommensurability versus uncertainty, rational permissibility versus arbitrariness, value creation versus value discovery), the decision-theoretic and psychological constraints it must respect, and the anti-voluntarist safeguards it needs—state the motivating case or argument and mark whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or merely a camp preference. Identify which subset of these desiderata appears jointly unsatisfiable, especially any tension between the autonomy required for commitment-based resolution and the demand for non-arbitrary justification. Then briefly assess how a framework that treats these choices as hard rather than calculative, and a non-aggregative pluralist weighing model, perform against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria and their conflicts rather than on the scoring. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or named-author checklists; work from the conceptual structure alone.
