I am designing a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of causation and want to assign an adversarial-collaboration exercise rather than a conventional argumentative essay. Please draft the joint statement that an omissionist camp—those who hold that omissions are genuine causal relata on a par with actions, typically grounded in counterfactual dependence—would be able to sign together with a positivist camp—those who restrict causation to relations among positive events and treat omissions as belonging only to explanatory or normative discourse. The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims: the formal conditions for counterfactual dependence, the structural asymmetry between causing and allowing, and what standard cases, such as the death of a plant that receives no water, actually establish without resolving the underlying metaphysical dispute.

Then present a disagreements table in which each camp states its own position in wording it fully accepts, followed by the cruxes—precise sub-questions such that resolving them would genuinely move at least one side. To prevent the exercise from rehearsing only well-worn ground, inject the following framing: analyze omissions in distributed multi-agent systems, where responsibility is diffused across many potential interveners and no single individual fails to act, yet a collective failure to intervene occurs. The collaboration should address whether this fragmented omission introduces a collective causal relatum, dissolves into an aggregate of individual omissions, or reveals that part of the dispute is merely verbal—without either camp conceding its core metaphysical commitment. Conclude by specifying a jointly endorsed hypothetical test case or argument pattern that both sides agree would bear on these cruxes, recording in advance what each camp would count as decisive evidence. Maintain a formal, seminar-appropriate tone throughout, and do not include a bibliography, citation list, or suggested readings.
