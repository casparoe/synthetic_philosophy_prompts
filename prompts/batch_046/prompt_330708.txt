A colleague summarised David Benatar's asymmetry argument—drawn from Better Never to Have Been (2006)—in the following way. The presence of harm is bad for the person who exists; the absence of pleasure is not bad for the person who never exists. Consequently, any potential child is exposed to a certain risk of harm, while the pleasures they might have enjoyed provide no countervailing moral reason to create them, since there is no one to be deprived of those pleasures. From this, my colleague inferred three further claims: first, that Benatar's asymmetry strictly entails procreation is always impermissible, not merely that there is a strong presumption against it; second, that adoption is therefore not just permissible but a positive moral duty, because it avoids creating new harm without sacrificing any non-existent person's good; and third, that the asymmetry dissolves the non-identity problem entirely, since there is no meaningful "better off" comparison between an actual suffering child and the non-existent child who was never brought into being.

I suspect this captures the gist but also smuggles in some distortions—perhaps a conflation of "no compensating benefit for the non-existent" with "no reason whatsoever to procreate", or an overstatement of Benatar's practical conclusions. I am unsure which parts are faithful and which depend on hidden premises. Could you audit this summary with precision? Please separate: (a) the elements that accurately represent Benatar's asymmetry and its direct implications; (b) the subtle misrepresentations or overstatements—especially any slide from evaluative asymmetry to categorical prohibition, or from permissible adoption to obligatory adoption; and (c) whether the conclusion that the non-identity problem is dissolved genuinely follows, or whether Benatar addresses that problem through separate reasoning. Also, please indicate whether the summary mistakes a risk-based argument about suffering for a claim about the net value of every actual life. Write your answer in British English.
