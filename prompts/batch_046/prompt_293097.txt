I've been reading Schefflers argument about the collective afterlife — the idea that our present projects depend for their meaning on the expectation that humanity will keep going, so extinction is bad not just because people die but because it drains value from everything we're doing now. I'm trying to apply this to Alfonzo Cuarons Children of Men, where humanity has suddenly lost the ability to reproduce and everyone is sinking into nihilism. The film seems to dramatize Scheffler: people stop caring about art, politics, or the future because there is no future generation. But I'm wondering if it also pushes back. Theo protects Kee not because he thinks the infertility is curable — he has no reason to believe that — but because the immediate act of care matters on its own. Does the film actually support Scheffler, or does it suggest meaning can survive through local, present-tense commitment even when the collective afterlife is gone? And what about the boat Tomorrow — is that a symbol of the false hope Scheffler says we need, or does it show we can find purpose without any guarantee of continuity? I'd like to use this for a philosophy of death and meaning discussion, so I want a reading that's honest about where the film resists the theory.
