My colleague, who defends value pluralism without a common metric, has offered the following judgments. First, that saving one child takes precedence over preserving a unique, irreplaceable artwork, not because lives “outweigh” art but because artistic value does not apply in that practical space. Second, that between two equally worthy but incompatible life projects—pure mathematics versus clinical medicine—there is no rational resolution; the choice creates its own justification, and the rejected path is genuinely lost. Third, that when a minor promise conflicts with preventing grave injustice, one should prevent the injustice, not by weighing values but because justice redefines what fidelity requires in that context. Fourth, she rejects all cross-domain aggregation, including via “sufficient reason” or weighted comparison.

New case: a curator must permanently deaccession the only archive of oral histories from a displaced community—an archive partly constitutive of that community’s collective identity—in order to redirect preservation funds into sustained, non-emergency healthcare for the same community. The healthcare is significant but not immediately life-saving for all; the archive, once dispersed, is irrecoverable. Predict her verdict. Identify at least two plausible rules consistent with her earlier judgments, say what each predicts here, state which rule she is most likely running with your reasons, and specify one further case that would distinguish the two rules.
