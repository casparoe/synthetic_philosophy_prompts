My forecasting-methodology advisor has ruled on reference-class selection for eight prior estimation problems, always giving a single governing class rather than an ensemble. Her record is consistent but ambiguous between two interpretations. Here are the cases and her rulings:

1. Prognosis for a specific patient with a rare autoimmune disorder: she required the class of all prior patients with that exact biomarker profile (N ≈ 12), rejecting the broader class of all autoimmune patients despite the larger sample.
2. Failure-rate estimate for a new suspension-bridge design: she required the class of all suspension bridges built with that specific alloy in that seismic zone, not all suspension bridges globally.
3. Projected voter turnout in a district adopting ranked-choice voting: she selected districts with comparable demographic shifts under any voting system, not districts with the same voting system but different demographics.
4. Lifespan forecast for a prototype solar panel coated with an experimental polymer: she used all panels bearing that polymer regardless of manufacturer, not all panels from that manufacturer regardless of coating.
5. Risk estimate for a particular near-Earth asteroid: she used objects with the same orbital-perturbation history, not all near-Earth objects of comparable size.
6. Benchmark-degradation forecast for a language model after a specific training-data curation pipeline: she used all models subjected to that exact pipeline regardless of parameter count, not all models of comparable size.
7. Infection-rate projection for a hospital ward with a novel ventilation layout: she used wards with that ventilation layout regardless of hospital size, not wards of matching size with conventional ventilation.
8. Crop-yield estimate for a genetically modified wheat strain under drought: she used that specific strain under drought conditions, not all drought-resistant strains or all strains of that crop.

Now consider a ninth case. We need to predict the thermal-tolerance boundary of a synthetic protocell—a lipid-bounded reaction network that replicates via template-directed assembly of non-nucleic-acid catalysts, containing no DNA, RNA, or ribosomes—when it is subjected to a sustained thermal gradient (42 °C to 48 °C across the membrane) that has been applied separately to biological prokaryotic cells and to synthetic lipid vesicles lacking replication machinery, but never to this architecture.

Predict her ruling: which reference class should govern the forecast—the lipid-bounded replicating systems exposed to comparable gradients; the synthetic vesicles and protocells regardless of biological analogs; the biological cells treated as mechanistically analogous; or should she declare the reference-class method inapplicable because no prior instance shares both the organizational architecture and the stressor? Identify the principle she is implicitly running—causal-feature specificity, mechanistic homology, statistical-adequacy thresholds, or ontological-category matching—and specify which of those alternatives her earlier rulings also fit. Then state which further case would best distinguish her actual principle from its closest rival.
