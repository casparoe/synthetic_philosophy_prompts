I am defending, in a joint seminar of the Marine Policy Institute and a philosophy-of-science colloquium, the claim that eyestalk ablation in farmed shrimp broodstock should be prohibited even under a minimal welfare framework—specifically, a criterion I call irreversibility-weighted harm, which assigns moral weight to injuries that permanently diminish an animal's future behavioral flexibility regardless of whether phenomenal consciousness can be demonstrated in decapods. The audience will be about ten people: comparative neurobiologists who grant crustacean nociception but reject any inference to subjective experience; two aquaculture engineers who treat ablation as a necessary reproductive-management trade-off; a philosopher of biology committed to strict evidential thresholds for mental-state attribution; and a legal scholar working on EU animal-welfare regulations that cover cephalopods but exclude decapods. I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense of the framework itself. Give me the objections I will actually face, ordered by how damaging they are, phrased in the vocabulary each subgroup would use. For each: what I should concede up front, the two-minute reply I should give, and the precise point where that reply is soft. Include the objection I am hoping nobody asks—likely from the philosopher of biology, about whether defining harm through behavioral flexibility is circular because flexibility is itself measured by post-injury behavior—and draft the concession I should make before it is raised. The sheet should assume I have no access to literature searches during the session and should not ask for bibliographies or citations.
