I have been working through Newton's rotating-bucket thought experiment and find that the usual framing—substantivalism versus a generic relationism—glosses over an important division on the relationist side. The substantivalist position is familiar: the water climbs the bucket walls because the bucket is genuinely rotating with respect to absolute space, an entity that exists independently of all material bodies and furnishes the correct frame for distinguishing true accelerated motion from mere relative motion. But relationism is not monolithic. There is the Machian variant, which explains the concavity by appealing to rotation relative to the distant fixed stars and the aggregate matter distribution of the universe; and there is a stricter structural relationism, which rejects both absolute space and any privileged cosmic reference frame, attempting instead to ground all inertial structure in the direct dynamical relations among the bodies present in the system.

Please compare the substantivalist account with this stricter structural relationist account—not with Mach's version, but specifically with the view that denies any background geometry and tries to recover inertial effects solely from internal relational dynamics. Can such a view genuinely reproduce the quantitative profile of the water's concavity, including the parabolic shape and its dependence on angular velocity, without covertly importing a background metric, a global simultaneity convention, or an implicit absolute frame? If it cannot, does that failure show that structural relationism collapses back into either substantivalism or Machian relationism? Finally, consider the limiting case of an otherwise empty universe containing only the bucket and water: on the structural relationist view, should the water surface remain flat, become concave in a weaker form, or is the scenario itself conceptually incoherent? Contrast this with the predictions of both the substantivalist and the Machian relationist for that same empty-universe case, and say whether the three positions are empirically distinguishable or only metaphysically so.
