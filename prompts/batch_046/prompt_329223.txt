I've been reading about demandingness objections to consequentialism and there's this loop I keep noticing that I can't find a name for. Someone argues the view is too demanding because it seems to require giving up almost all free time and money. The defender replies that rest and personal projects are okay because they prevent burnout and sustain long-term contribution. But then the critic shows that if you accept that, you can justify almost any expensive hobby or long vacation as a "recovery investment", which suddenly makes the theory way more permissive — so you're either back to extreme demandingness (if you reject the recovery excuse) or you've lost the demandingness altogether (if you let it cover everything). It's not just the usual "too demanding vs too permissive" problem; it's specifically about using sustainability arguments to defuse demandingness, only to have them inflate permissiveness and cause the original objection to bounce back. Is there an existant term for this loop? If not, how do you distinguish it cleanly from the generic demandingness objection, and is there a standard way philosophers talk about defenses that undermine themselves like this?
