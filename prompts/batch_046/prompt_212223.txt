In a novel I'm drafting, a character insists that guilt operates like a ledger—each wrong is an entry that can be squared with repayment, apology, or punishment—while shame operates like a dye that saturates the fabric of the self, altering every thread rather than marking a single transaction. From this she concludes that guilt is the more humane moral emotion and that a society can deliberately select for it over shame, just as a state might adopt a gold-backed currency rather than one floating on collective trust. I want the analogy audited with rigor: lay out the exact correspondences—creditor, debt, settlement, bleaching, fading—then identify which properties of the source domain are smuggled into the target and which are absent. Does the self actually function as either a discrete debtor or a uniform textile? And once the disanalogies are visible, does the normative claim—that guilt is superior and culturally choosable—still stand, or was it carried entirely by the metaphor?
