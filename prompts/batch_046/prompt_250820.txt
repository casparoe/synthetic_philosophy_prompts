Write a dialogue between two equally sharp people arguing about Nietzche's eternal recurrance in a specific weird case: a digital consciousness is simulated in a loop, but its memories are wiped at the end of every cycle so it never knows it's repeating. One person should be a cosmological literalist—she says it's not real recurrence unless the entire causal chain, including brain states, repeats identically, and a wiped-memory simulation is just a copy running twice. The other should be an existential phenomenologist—he says what matters is the subjective structure of living and affirming that life, so if the inner experience is genuinely recreated, the ethical weight of recurrence applies fully even without memory or biological substrate. Have them actually respond to each other's arguments, not talk past one another. Keep it relatively short and focused, write casually, and don't worry if there's a typo or two.
