If you were advising a three-person research collective—one metaphysician, one philosopher of science, and one formal logician—with three years of unrestricted funding to advance work on the problem of universals, which two or three open lines of inquiry should they treat as primary, and in what strategic order? Frame your answer not as a survey but as a defended agenda: for each line, explain why it is upstream of the others (which debates depend on its resolution), how tractable it is for philosophers and logicians working without empirical laboratories, and what kind of intermediate result within the funding window would either vindicate the investment or justify redirecting effort. Specifically, weigh the relative priority of (a) whether structural universals—such as comparative relations like being larger than—can be reconstructed within a trope-bundle ontology without invoking universal instantiation; (b) whether a refined nominalism based on imperfect resemblance classes can survive the objection that similarity is always relative and therefore cannot ground property attribution; and (c) whether resolving these metaphysical issues is genuinely upstream of contemporary disputes about natural kinds in scientific taxonomy, or whether the philosophy-of-science debates are largely independent. Give explicit criteria—dependency, tractability, downstream impact, and sensitivity to new arguments rather than data—for the ordering, and indicate which of these three lines you would deprioritize and why.
