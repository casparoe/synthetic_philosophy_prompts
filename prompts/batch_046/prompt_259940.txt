design a 90-minute workshop activity for about 15 grad students in philosophy of mind that makes Dennetts four creature-types feel like genuinely different architectures, not just "dumb to smart." split them into four teams: Darwinian gets one fixed strategy card they cant revise, Skinnerian plays trial-and-error with real point penalties for wrong moves, Popperian can draft and tear up internal hypotheses without ever executing them, Gregorian share a communal whiteboard of symbols but a hidden "corrupt archive" role lets an outsider secretly alter one symbol each round. the shared task is predicting a hidden pattern that shifts after round two. in round three inject a bankruptcy condition: Popperians suddenly can hold only one hypothesis at once, so they must either become Skinnerian or borrow the Gregorian board to survive. include specific roles, a minute-by-minute timeline, payoffs, and a debrief that maps each round onto the taxonomy. be honest about what the simulation cant reproduce—probably the actual evolutionary pressure that makes Gregorian culture necessary rather than optional, and the fact that real Gregorian tools arent just shared notes but inherited structures the individual never fully grasps. write it casual, maybe a typo or two, and dont ask for readings or citations since the model should build it from scratch.
