I've been working through Derek Parfit's hitchhiker thought experiment with a study group focused on rational agency, and we've become fixated on a variant that rarely gets center stage. The classic puzzle asks whether it can be rational to make yourself predictably irrational—promising to pay the driver after you're dropped off, when once delivered you have no reason to keep the promise. Most discussions treat the solution as a precommitment strategy: bind your future self so the driver trusts you. But I want to push on what happens if the binding mechanism doesn't merely constrain the later self but eliminates its capacity for choice altogether—say, a locked box or an implanted trigger that makes payment physically unavoidable regardless of what the later self wants. In that case, the "irrationality" required to secure the ride isn't an act of the later agent; it's pure causal determinism. The rational plan seems to achieve its end only by dissolving the very agency that made the problem interesting.

I want a poem of about sixteen lines in blank verse, split into two octaves, that makes this conceptual collapse do formal work. The first octave should be spoken by the hitchhiker at the moment of agreement: regular iambic pentameter, a clear rhyme scheme (ABABCDCD), and language of promise, mechanism, and trust, establishing the rational architecture of the deal. The second octave should be spoken by the same figure after delivery, but here the meter should visibly fracture—lines should shorten, the rhyme scheme should break at the precise moment the speaker recognizes he has no option, and the vocabulary should shift from deliberation to mechanism. The poem should not resolve whether this counts as successful rational self-binding or as the erasure of the self; it should leave the tension open in the formal gap between the two voices. Someone who knows Parfit's problem should recognize the driver-assurance structure and the paradox of rational irrationality, but the fracture should belong specifically to this variant, where precommitment collapses into determinism. Please begin with the poem itself, with no preface or commentary.
