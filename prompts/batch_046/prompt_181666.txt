I defend a restricted version of Nozick's entitlement theory: voluntary exchanges preserve justice only when both parties possess substantive exit capacity — the genuine ability to refuse the transaction without falling below a baseline of functioning. On this view, the Wilt Chamberlain argument demonstrates that patterned distributive principles conflict with liberty only for exchanges satisfying this condition; where one party faces structural dependency (for instance, a monopolist employer and a worker without viable alternatives), the transfer's justice is not automatically secured by consent alone. What would count decisively against this position? I want the arguments and cases — including ones drawn from discussions of exploitation, structural coercion, or historical entitlement — that should move a defender, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. In particular, does this restriction collapse the view into a patterned principle after all, or can it be consistently maintained? And has the qualification rendered the view unfalsifiable, and if so, is that a defect for a theory of justice?
