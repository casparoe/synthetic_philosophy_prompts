The opposing camp reads him as a humanist who kept physics and ethics strictly separate. They cite his expressions of cosmic religious feeling as evidence of a genuine emotional and moral orientation toward the universe, argue that his determinism was confined to the domain of physical law, and maintain that he never meant to deny the practical reality of human choice and moral responsibility.
