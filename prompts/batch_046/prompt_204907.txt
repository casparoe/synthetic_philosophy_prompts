Students were then to evaluate whether Goodman’s appeal to entrenchment justifies projecting flexibility rather than flex-rigidity for post-2030 samples, or whether the novelty of the predicate makes entrenchment inapplicable in engineered domains, and finally to discuss how this bears on Hume’s older problem—specifically whether entrenchment avoids skepticism about necessary connection or merely disguises it.

Here is the student’s answer. The student correctly sets out the grue definition and notes that past observations underdetermine future predictions, then applies the parallel to flex-rigid polymers. They state that Goodman solves the paradox through entrenchment: predicates with longer histories of actual projection are more projectible, so green is entrenched and grue is not. They claim this gives a factual, historical reason to prefer one predicate and therefore solves the paradox. They then argue that for synthetic predicates like flex-rigid there is no historical record at all, so entrenchment offers no guidance in new scientific domains and Goodman’s framework is limited to familiar vocabulary. Turning to Hume, they write that Hume showed we lack rational justification for expecting the future to resemble the past, while Goodman shows we lack logical justification for preferring green except that we have used it before; both therefore appeal to custom, and Goodman’s entrenchment is essentially Hume’s psychological habit translated into linguistic sociology. They conclude that induction is conventional rather than world-guided, though Goodman might resist being called a conventionalist about natural kinds.
