ok so i'm trying to figure out if there's one principle behind my gut reactions to lost-cause national myths, or if i'm just being inconsistent. here are my verdicts.

the confederacy's lost cause: i think it's actively poisonous and should be stripped from public spaces—statues, school names, the works—but i don't think private commemorative groups should be banned. so aggressive public dismanling, but leave private grief alone.

masada: the collective-suicide-against-rome story is taught as heroic. i think glorifying suicide is dangerous, especially when it bleeds into modern militarism, but i also think wiping it from israeli education would erase something real about resistance. so don't glorify, don't erase—just contextualize.

kosovo field (1389): the serbian myth of noble defeat to the ottomans. i think it's been weaponized to justify ethnic violence and should be challenged hard in politics, yet suppressing the cultural mournning entirely feels like it fuels resentment more than it helps. so challenge the weaponization, allow the mourning.

dunkirk: the "dunkirk spirit" of plucky british retreat. i think it's mostly harmless papering over a military disaster, and i genuinely don't care if people keep telling it—it's not demanding anything of anyone now. so let it be, maybe gently correct in history class.

but then i think about the alamo, and i feel closer to the confederacy verdict—strip the heroic framing—even though it's also an old defeat myth that doesn't demand much today. so dunkirk gets a pass and alamo doesn't, which breaks any simple rule about "harmless if old."

i've been playing with a concept i'm calling "mythic hostage cost"—the idea that a lost cause becomes toxic when living people, especially outsiders, are expected to pay a price to honor the dead. by that: confederacy demands black citizens live with monuments (hostage cost high); masada demands soldiers be willing to die (cost to future generations); kosovo demands territorial control (cost to others now); dunkirk demands nothing. that mostly works, except alamo should have low cost now, yet i still want it corrected. so maybe i need an extra clause about state-sponsored glorification of death over life?

is there a principle that saves all four of my original verdicts plus handles alamo consistently? or do i have to drop one? and what does the best version predict for something like the jacobite rising being used in modern scottish nationalist rhetoric—should it get the dunkirk pass or the confederacy treatment? please also say whether my "mythic hostage cost" idea is salvageable or just a mess.
