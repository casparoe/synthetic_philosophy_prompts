I have no formal training in philosophy or Buddhist studies, but over the past year I have been reading translations and secondary discussions of Nāgārjuna's Mūlamadhyamakakārikā and the Madhyamaka account of emptiness (śūnyatā). I keep returning to the catuṣkoṭi—the four-cornered negation—and I am convinced that two interpretive camps are arguing past one another rather than engaging the same claim. I should like you to draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between them might produce.

The two camps are these. Camp A, which I shall call the Apophatic Purists, maintains that Nāgārjuna's tetralemma denies existence, non-existence, both, and neither in a single, exhaustive sweep, leaving no conceptual residue whatsoever; conventional discourse is therefore purely pragmatic and carries no ontological weight at all. Camp B, which I shall call the Two-Truths Constructivists, maintains that the denial is targeted specifically at svabhāva (intrinsic, self-standing existence), and that once that particular target is removed, dependently originated conventional designations remain with a precise, non-nihilistic status—neither nothing nor self-existent.

Please produce a document that members of both camps could put their names to, structured exactly as follows.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims—substantive, not vacuous—covering: the formal structure of the tetralemma in the opening chapters of the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā; what svabhāva means in this context and why it is the specific object of negation; what the no-go arguments against production from self, other, both, and neither actually rule out; and the distinction between saṃvṛti (conventional truth) and paramārtha (ultimate truth).

Second, a disagreements table with two columns, one for each camp. In wording that each side would accept for its own position, address: (a) whether conventional entities retain any 'residual ontological status' after emptiness is established; (b) whether the fourth corner—'neither exists nor does not exist'—is best read as a logical contradiction to be avoided or as a failed metaphysical escape route; and (c) whether accepting dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) for conventional phenomena implicitly commits one to a hidden svabhāva.

Third, the cruxes—specific sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, record in advance what each camp would count as decisive evidence or argument, and identify any thought experiment or textual case both agree would genuinely bear on it.

Fourth, a brief analytical section identifying which parts of the dispute are genuinely substantive and which are verbal—particularly disagreements about the word 'existence' that dissolve once it is split into 'intrinsic existence' versus 'dependently designated existence'.

Please inject one specific, somewhat niche framing: apply the tetralemma not merely to generic entities but specifically to the relation between conventional truth and ultimate truth itself—ask whether saṃvṛti can be said to 'exist' in the very sense that is being denied of svabhāva, and address this without appealing to external authorities.

Write in polished, formal prose. Present the answer in British English (for instance, using 'behaviour', 'centre', and 'analyse'). Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation lists, or requests for further sources. If you refer to specific verses or arguments from the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, paraphrase them without quotation marks; I prefer paraphrase in any case.
