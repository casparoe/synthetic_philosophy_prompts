2. A time-symmetric universe with identical low-entropy conditions at both temporal ends and symmetric dynamics between them: she rules no unique arrow; the direction is purely conventional.
3. A high-entropy thermal bath containing an isolated, temporary fluctuation that briefly organizes into an observer with false memories before dissipating: she rules no arrow, only a statistical excursion.
4. A globally thermalizing universe that also hosts a vast, stable civilization continuously reversing local entropy in galaxy-scale regions via refrigeration, without altering the global trend: she rules yes, the arrow is defined globally and the local reversals are parasitic on it.
5. A cyclic model in which entropy resets exactly to its initial value after every 10^100 years, repeating identically: she rules no arrow, since there is no cumulative asymmetry.

Next week she considers a new scenario: a universe with no global boundary conditions and no net entropy gradient anywhere, except for a single, non-fluctuating, agent-independent "entropy pump" embedded directly in the dynamics. This mechanism permanently transfers heat from a cold reservoir to a hot one within one spatial region, maintaining a sustained local asymmetry, while all other regions remain in perfect equilibrium. The pump requires no intelligent design and never decays.

Predict her verdict. Is she implicitly running a boundary-condition account (arrow requires an asymmetric low-entropy boundary), a global-gradient account (arrow requires monotonic increase across the whole system), or a local-exploitable-gradient account (arrow exists wherever a stable, non-parasitic causal asymmetry is present)?
