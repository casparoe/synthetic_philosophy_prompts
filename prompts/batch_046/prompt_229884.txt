Consider the funding policy adopted by a major scientific grant-making body: every proposal must demonstrate that each theoretical claim on which it directly relies has been independently replicated within the past five years. The agency treats replication failure as a break in the chain of knowledge transmission—if Study A supports Theory B, and Study A has not been replicated, then no researcher can be said to know B, even if B is widely taught. Yet the same agency exempts "background framework" claims—general mathematical formalisms, long-standing physical laws, broad evolutionary principles—from this requirement, allowing them to stand as unreplicated but legitimate premises in new work. Meanwhile, the agency rejects applications that invoke statistical correlations from social-science datasets that have never been reproduced, regardless of the applicant's own expertise or the original study's methodological rigor.

What view of knowledge, and specifically of epistemic closure, does this policy presuppose? Does it assume that knowing p and knowing that p entails q does not automatically yield knowledge of q—so that a researcher might know a theoretical framework without knowing its constituent dependencies, in the manner Nozick's tracking conditions suggest? Or does it presuppose that knowledge requires a kind of source-tracking: that one only knows a claim if one could track its truth back through an unbroken chain of verified origins? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology, distinguishing what the practice strictly requires from what merely accommodates it. Is the distinction between "background framework" and "direct dependency" philosophically defensible under a tracking account, or does it collapse once we recognize that all theoretical claims depend, at some remove, on unreplicated findings? Finally, if we replaced this implicit view with a straightforward closure-accepting epistemology—where knowing a well-supported framework automatically licenses knowing its entailments—what specific features of the funding procedure would have to change?
