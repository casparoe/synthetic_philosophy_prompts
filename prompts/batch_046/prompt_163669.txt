You are advising a three-year research program with resources for roughly four full-time philosophers and formal theorists. The program studies evidential cooperation in large worlds—scenarios in which causally isolated agents should coordinate their decisions because they are likely decision-relevant copies or analogues of one another. Rather than treating the standard physicalist framing (where duplication means spatiotemporally separated physical instances) as given, the program focuses on what might be called ontologically heterogeneous large worlds: cases in which agents disagree not only about outcomes but about the metaphysical framework that determines what counts as a copy, a causal pathway, or a relevant world-branch. For example, one agent may reason within a strict physicalist ontology that recognizes only spatiotemporally separated duplicates, while another operates with a modal-realist or simulationist ontology that counts structurally identical minds across logical space or across computational substrates as cooperating partners.

Within this framing, identify and rank the open conceptual questions and lines of work that deserve priority. Deliver a defended agenda, not a survey or a reading list. For each priority, specify: (1) the precise question—e.g., how evidential weights should be assigned when agents disagree about the ontology of duplication; whether cooperation collapses if metaphysical disagreement is treated as first-order epistemic uncertainty about one's own ontology; or how to formalize cross-ontological "structural similarity" without smuggling in a privileged framework; (2) why it is upstream of other work—what downstream questions depend on its resolution; (3) its tractability over a three-year horizon for conceptual analysis and formal modeling rather than empirical science; and (4) what theoretical result or conceptual clarification in the coming years would cause you to upgrade or downgrade it. Then defend the overall ordering: state the criteria you used (importance for practical strategic reasoning under advanced AI development, tractability, neglectedness, dependency structure), highlight the trade-offs between them, and identify which questions you are deliberately deprioritizing because resolving them would not substantially alter cooperative recommendations or resource allocation. Finish by isolating the single crux—the one question—whose answer you believe would most restructure how effort is divided between evidential, causal, and functional approaches to large-world decision-making.
