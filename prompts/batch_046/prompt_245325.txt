Draft the joint statement that a "collapse-theorist" camp and a "reconfiguration-theorist" camp could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on whether societies genuinely collapse or merely transform. The collapse camp treats the Late Bronze Age disruptions and the Classic Maya political fragmentation as paradigmatic cases of societal collapse, defined specifically as a rapid, involuntary loss of institutional complexity coupled with significant demographic contraction relative to a preceding organizational baseline; they interpret Easter Island's cessation of moai production and widespread deforestation as an ecological overshoot that produced genuine social breakdown. The reconfiguration camp treats the same Bronze Age and Maya episodes as state-level transformations in which local populations persisted, subsistence agriculture continued, and new decentralized political forms emerged, arguing that "collapse" is an etic analytical category imposed by observers who implicitly normativize centralized state complexity; they read Easter Island as a ritual and economic reallocation in response to resource stress rather than a failure of society itself.

The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims about what the archaeological and paleoecological records actually show—settlement continuity or discontinuity, material culture transmission, population estimates, and environmental data for all three cases—stated precisely enough that neither camp disputes them; (2) the exact formulations of residual disagreement, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording, specifying whether part of the dispute is verbal (hinging on the definition of collapse) and part is substantive; (3) the cruxes—at minimum, whether institutional decomplexification without demographic collapse counts as collapse; whether there is an observer-independent baseline of "normal" societal functioning against which failure can be measured; and whether ecological degradation necessarily entails social failure; and (4) for each crux, one concrete archaeological finding or comparative case both sides agree bears on it, together with each camp's explicit prediction about how that evidence should be interpreted. Format the output as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or specific author attributions.
