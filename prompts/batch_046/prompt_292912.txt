I'm sketcing an objection to Jeffrey conditionalization for a seminar paper and now I'm stuck in my own loop.

(1) Jeffrey updating shifts probabilities over a partition {E_i} and keeps every conditional P(H|E_i) rigid—unchanged.
(2) Say I hear my advisor may have fudged data, but I don't know which study—maybe the 2018 one, maybe the 2021 one.
(3) So I partition my beliefs over {fudged 2018, fudged 2021, clean record} and reweight them based on how credible the rumor feels.
(4) Take a background belief H: "my advisor is methodologically careful."
(5) In my prior, P(H | fudged 2018) is low, but P(H | fudged 2021) is lower still, since the later study was more central to her career.
(6) The rigidity condition locks both of those values in place.
(7) But then there's no single number that represents "how careful she is given that she fudged something"—the rule references two different conditional values for what looks like the same state of affairs.
(8) So Jeffrey conditioning is well-defined only when all P(H|E_i) match, which happens exactly when the partition is probabilistically independent of H.
(9) Real uncertain evidence—rumors, noisy measurements, ambiguous testimony—almost never partitions independently of our background theories.
(10) Therefore Jeffrey conditioning can't apply to the messy cases it was invented to handle.

Something slipped between (6) and (7). I want to keep (1) and (9) if I can—there's definately something about messy evidence interacting with background beliefs. But is the problem just that I'm treating "fudged something" as one state when Jeffrey treats it as two? Where's the actual exit?
