I've been convinced lately that the norm of assertion isn't knowledge, or even flat-out belief, but something I'm calling "resilient belief" — basically, you can assert P if you genuinely believe it and it wouldn't immediatly collapse under a quick counterfactual check (like, "would I still say this if my coworker pointed out one obvious exception?"). I think this explains why we assert stuff we're not 100% sure about, without letting in pure guesses. But I'm not sure what'd actually kill this view. What kind of cases, intuitions, or results in other areas (say, about testimony or pragmatics) would count against it? I'd like the defeaters ranked: what's fatal vs just awkward. What would a defender of resilient-belief assertation concede is a real problem, and what'd they brush off? And does this position risk collapsing back into knowledge-norm or belief-norm, or is it genuinely distinct? I want to know if it's well-insulated or if I'm just inventing a middle ground that doesn't hold up.
