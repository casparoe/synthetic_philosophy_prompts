I'm trying to check whether my views on substrate-independence and books actually hang together before I write them up, and right now they feel like a bit of a mess. I think consciousness is basically about the right causal-informational dynamics, not about being made of meat -- so a silicon brain running the right program should be conscious in principle. I also think the Einstein-brain-in-a-jar scenario from that Mind's I dialogue fails: preserving the physical brain in formaldehyde stops the dynamics, so consciousness doesn't survive. But if you transcribe every state-transition into a huge book, and then flip through the pages in sequence with the right causal links between steps, I think that paper-and-ink system could be conscious -- the substrate is weird but the pattern is running. Still, I don't think the actual book sitting on my shelf is conscious, even if it contains the full transcript. And I think splitting those pages across a million people, each holding one page and passing messages to maintain the sequence, might also count, which feels strange but consistent. At the same time, I don't think a library that happens to shelve all the volumes in the right order counts, even though all the information is present. So my commitments are roughly: (a) dynamics matter more than substrate, (b) static storage isn't consciousness, (c) distributed realization works if causal links are intact, (d) accidental spatial colocation doesn't work. Do these actually cohere? I feel like I'm sneaking in a hidden system-boundary requirement without admitting it. What's the least costly revision? Please rank any tensions by severity, say which commitment should probably go, and be explicit about what argument or observation would change your mind -- especially if there's a convincing reason distributed paper-systems can't support unified phenomenology, or that my distinction between (c) and (d) collapses.
