I serve as the senior assessment lead for an international rapid-response consortium, and after a recent workshop on famine classification I have become dissatisfied with the binary checklist my two-person field teams currently use. Within ninety minutes of arrival—using only local market-price data, mortality census figures, and no macroeconomic modeling—they must decide whether an emerging crisis should be coded as "natural-availability collapse" (triggering standard food-aid pipelines) or as "entitlement and political failure" (triggering advocacy protocols, targeted protection, and political-engagement clauses). I want you to design a reusable decision procedure, structured as an ordered flowchart in words, grounded in Amartya Sen's distinction between food-availability decline and direct or exchange-entitlement collapse. For each node, specify the question to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure halts with a definitive classification, where it must hand the decision to human judgment because the issue is normative rather than empirical, and the conditions—such as stable aggregate supply with politically restricted distribution, or a natural shock coinciding with state procurement—where the procedure is known to misfire.

To prevent the framework from becoming a post-hoc narrative, embed an explicit "counterfactual entitlement stress-test": a step in which the team hypothetically reverses one institutional variable—market-access rules, property-rights enforcement, or state procurement mandates—and checks whether access to food collapses regardless of aggregate supply. Then apply the complete procedure to three historical episodes: the 1943 Bengal famine, the Irish crisis of the 1840s, and the 1959–1961 Great Leap Forward famine. For one of these, the procedure should yield a classification that conflicts with the intuitive "natural disaster" framing and that logistics partners will resist. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure cannot do: the moral and political judgments it deliberately leaves unresolved, and the data gaps that render it unreliable.
