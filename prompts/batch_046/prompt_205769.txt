I have been following the long-running exchange between Dennett and Searle on original versus derived intentionality — in particular Dennett's two-bitser example, which is designed to show that evolutionary design history collapses the distinction between derived and original intentionality, set against Searle's claim that only intrinsic, biologically generated intentionality is genuinely original, and that every artificial or observer-relative ascription remains derivatively parasitic. Despite decades of discussion, neither side appears to have moved the other: Dennett treats Searle's demand for intrinsicness as a mystified residue of Cartesian dualism, while Searle treats Dennett's evolutionary-functional scaling as a category mistake that confuses the conditions for attributing intentionality with the conditions for its existence.

Diagnose why this debate has stalled. Is it a verbal disagreement about the term 'intentionality' — with Dennett employing it in an explanatory, third-person register and Searle in an ontological, first-person register — or is it substantive, turning on whether evolutionary etiology can ever confer intrinsic mental properties? Are the two sides answering different questions under a single heading: one concerning the justification for adopting the intentional stance, the other concerning what must be present in the world for there to be aboutness at all? Or is there a hidden formal disagreement about whether intrinsicness is a binary or scalar property, and whether scaling up derived systems can ever bridge the gap?

Be precise about what each side actually maintains: Dennett's contention that the intentionality of the two-bitser is no more derived than ours once selective history is taken into account, and Searle's reply that selective history explains only the appearance of intentionality, not its reality. Given your diagnosis, indicate what — if anything — would count as genuine progress: a new conceptual distinction, a refined thought experiment, a formal result, or a reasoned argument that the impasse should be accepted as permanent.
