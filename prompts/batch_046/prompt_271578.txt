Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between an “alignment monist” and an “alignment pluralist” on whether artificial-intelligence alignment is one problem or several. Define the monist as holding that every alignment failure is ultimately a failure of specification fidelity—an artificial system’s behavior diverges from its designer’s intent across contexts—and that distinctions such as value-loading versus capability-control, or outer versus inner alignment, are practical subdivisions of this single problem rather than conceptually independent ones. Define the pluralist as holding that “the alignment problem” comprises at least three irreducible problems: (1) the specification problem (defining the target precisely), (2) the robustness problem (preserving alignment through self-modification and distributional shift), and (3) the multi-agent governance problem (coordinating systems with partially conflicting specifications), and that treating them as one has produced conceptual confusion and misdirected priorities.

Structure the document as follows. First, a numbered section of claims both sides can sign—about what successful alignment requires, about documented failure modes, about the limits of current formal frameworks, and about what would count as having solved or failed to solve each component. Second, a disagreements table: for each remaining dispute, state the monist’s formulation in wording the monist would accept, and the pluralist’s formulation in wording the pluralist would accept; mark which disagreements are substantive and which are verbal. Third, the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side—such as whether a single formal criterion can simultaneously capture value drift and capability overhang, and whether resolving specification automatically resolves multi-agent conflict. Fourth, for each crux, identify one concrete research scenario or documented model behavior that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that scenario reveals. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke obscure formalisms that require specialist knowledge beyond the terms defined above.
