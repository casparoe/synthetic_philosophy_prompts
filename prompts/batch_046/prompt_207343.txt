I'm meant to be giving the opening remarks at our town centre heritage weekend this Saturday — the theme is lost causes and national myths, so we're meant to cover everything from the Confederacy and Kosovo to Masada and Dunkirk, but also our own local story about the 1847 uprising. The audience is split between actual historians and neighbours who've heard the family version for decades. It's only occured to me lately that the family version is mostly made up, and I need to talk about that without wrecking the weekend. The talk should use the difference between a 'martyrdom myth' like Masada and a 'retreat-as-victory' myth like Dunkirk to work out what kind of story our town actually needs, and that should shape the whole structure — what I can admit about the monument, what I can't apologise for, and why people still need these narratives even when the facts are shakier. About seven minutes, casual but not sloppy, and please don't turn it into a bibliography — I won't be able to check anything and half the room will switch off. Make it feel like I'm speaking to both sides without hiding behind academic distance.
