I am serving on the drafting committee for the charter of a perpetual scientific endowment that must remain operational for at least a century. Our founders are concerned not with locking in particular scientific conclusions, but with preventing future trustees from gradually revising the foundation's methodological commitments—what we have tentatively called "epistemic non-revisionism"—simply because they acquire new beliefs about the social utility of certain research programs. In our discussions, someone suggested that updateless decision theory offers a formal model for how a rational agent can make a choice that does not depend on updating beliefs about the choice situation itself, but rather on the logical properties of the decision rule. Before we incorporate any language about "algorithmic fidelity" into the charter, I need to understand whether this framework genuinely supports institutional precommitment of this kind.

Please describe the most important implications of updateless decision theory for our charter design. Specifically, I want to know: first, whether it allows a present board to rationally constrain future boards without treating those future agents as mere causal instruments of the present will; second, how the distinction between updating on evidence about the world versus updating on the output of one's own reasoning applies when the "evidence" in question is a shift in disciplinary norms about what counts as rigorous inquiry; and third, what it implies about the legitimacy of locking in procedural values rather than substantive outcomes, particularly if the locked rule requires future trustees to reject funding proposals that violate a specific logical structure of peer review even when they sincerely believe those proposals are socially beneficial. I do not need a bibliography, citations, or references to specific papers; I need the conceptual architecture and its practical consequences for institutional governance, presented clearly enough that legal drafters and philosophers on the committee can both see the stakes.
