I sit on our university's neuroethics review panel and we've got this messy case that hits right at the Dennett-style idea of the self as a center of narrative gravity. A participant in a longterm BCI study suffered massive retrograde amnesia and a personality shift after a stroke; the informed consent he signed five years ago allows broad data retention, but now he denies he ever agreed and demands full deletion. His family argues the narrative thread is broken, so the old consent binds the "previous self" rather than him, and they want retention honored to protect the study's integrity. We have to vote by next tuesday and I need to circulate a memo that recommends a concrete course—either immediate deletion, conditional retention with a hard review date, or some hybrid—with a clear implementation timeline. The memo should also take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side, which is probably that institutional promises shouldn't evaporate just because a narrative discontinuty occurred. Write it as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences max, with no introduction or conclusion, just the recommendation and reasoning straight through.
