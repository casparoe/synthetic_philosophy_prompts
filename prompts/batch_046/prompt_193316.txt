Write a dialogue between two philosophy grad students, Mira and Leo, sitting in a hot seminar room arguing about whether Descartes really meant it when he said animals are just complex machines with no inner life. Mira thinks his bête-machine line was mostly strategic—he needed to protect the human soul's immortality from church pressure, so he mechanized animals, but he probably still thought they had some dim, confused feeling. Leo thinks thats too generous: Descartes was genuinely committed that without rational thought there's zero consciousness, not even pain.

Have them actually fight it out over a weird hypothetical Leo invents: suppose we build a robot dog that whimpers and limps exactly like a real dog, but it's pure clockwork, no soul. Then we find that real dogs have neural wiring that's structurally identical, just evolved instead of engineered. Mira should say this makes Descartes unfalsifiable—he can always just claim God put souls in humans but not in the evolved copy. Leo should push back that unfalsifiability is fine in metaphysics and that the parallel actually strengthens Descartes, because it shows behavior doesn't prove inner life.

Make sure they respond to each other—Leo needs to answer Mira's unfalsifiability point directly, not change the subject. And keep it casual, like they're talking over bad coffee. Maybe have Mira mix up "there" and "their" in one of her lines, just to keep the tone loose.
