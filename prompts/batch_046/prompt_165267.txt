hey, been thinking about intuition pumps lately - the classic ones in phil of mind and ethics - and I keep seeing a pattern that I can't tell if it's got a proper name. It's not just framing or begging the question, it's more specific.

The move goes like this: you set up a thought experiment with two cases, Case A and Case B, and you tell the audience "B is exactly like A except for feature X." But when you actually read the desription, B isn't just A-minus-X - the absence of X has been used as an excuse to quietly change the background conditions, the scale, or the causal story, in a way that makes B feel like a malfunction or a deprivation rather than a neutral variant. Meanwhile A is described with all the "normal" context intact, so your intuiton about A stays clean. I noticed it in discussions about consciousness (where the "zombie" version always gets described with this eerie lack of inner light, rather than just behavioral difference) and also in some personal identity pumps (where the "fission" case gets framed with this implicit idea that there's now "too many" claimants, but the original case never had that framing).

So the pump isn't just loading the die with emotional language - it's smuggling in a normative baseline about what counts as the "complete" version of the scenario, and then treating the variant as deficient relative to that baseline. Is there an established term for this specific asymmetry? I know about framing effects and about loaded descriptions, but those feel too general. Is this a recognized subtype of intuition-pump failure, maybe something like "baseline contamination" or "modal framing drift"? And if there's genuinely no name, how would you carve it out - what's the boundary between this and ordinary equivocation, or between this and just writing a sloppy scenario? I don't want a bare label; I want to know whether the carving is already in the literature and if not, where exactly its edges are.
