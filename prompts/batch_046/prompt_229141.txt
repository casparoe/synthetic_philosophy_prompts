Write a deadpan luxury-wellness brochure of approximately 650 words for a fictional retreat called The Hedonic Commons, marketed as offering direct, calibrated access to the single intrinsic good. Format the brochure in numbered sections: Welcome, Our Philosophy, The Treatment Menu, and The Calibration Protocol. Adopt the smooth, authoritative voice of high-end spa marketing copy—soothing, precise, and entirely serious. The satire should take aim at hedonism's heterogeneity problem: the difficulty of maintaining that pleasures of radically different kinds (bodily, intellectual, malicious, aesthetic, anticipatory) share a homogeneous phenomenal quality that makes them commensurable units of one and the same good. Rather than stating the objection, let the brochure perform it by listing wildly disparate treatments—solving a logic puzzle under time pressure, eating a raw oyster, the brief satisfaction of a well-placed insult, floating in a sensory-deprivation tank, confirming an empirical hypothesis—as interchangeable modules differentiated only by dosage and duration, all mapped onto a single scale using an invented metric called the uniform intensity coefficient and justified by reference to fictional Innsbruck Phenomenal Calibration studies. The humor should arise from the deadpan insistence that qualitative diversity is merely a delivery-system variable, so that a specialist recognizes the familiar implausible move (treating phenomenological dissimilarity as superficial packaging) while a general reader sees only an absurdly over-engineered catalog. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or references to specific scholars; do not quote from real philosophical texts; and keep the irony sustained and legible without winking.
