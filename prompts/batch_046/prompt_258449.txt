Write a short story about a botanist who works with "perennial memory" plants—species that encode genetic traces of previous blooms in thier root structures, so every spring they technically "remember" being dead through winter. After her partner dies, she keeps fighting with a colleague who keeps bringing up Zhuangzi's view that death is just transformation, like autumn turning into winter, and that grief is basically a failure to see the seasonal whole. The botanist feels like her partner's specific pattern—his dumb jokes, the way he mispronounced her name—isnt stored in any root system, and that calling his death "just winter" erases the exact thing that made him him. Have the story turn on whether she can accept transformation without accepting erasure. Keep it grounded, no big exposition dumps, maybe end with her planting something that doesn't regenerate, just for once.
