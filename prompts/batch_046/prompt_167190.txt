As a journalist covering science and ideas, you are commissioned to prepare the briefing document for an adversarial collaboration in metaphysics. Adopt the role of an expert philosopher of science with deep familiarity in property theory and conceptual analysis. Before drafting, spend time brainstorming: map the formal structure of dispositional properties, identify precisely where finks and masks disrupt standard counterfactual analyses, and isolate the logical form of the disagreement rather than its rhetorical packaging.

Your task is to draft the joint statement that the Dispositional Integrity camp and the Categorical Ground camp could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on dispositions, finks, and masks. Do not cite authors, texts, or named doctrines; build the argument from first principles and from one invented conceptual scenario introduced below.

The document must contain four parts:

1. A numbered Agreed Claims section. Both camps should be able to sign statements about what dispositions are formally, what finks and masks do to dispositional ascriptions, and what any adequate account must explain. Include a shared acknowledgment of the nested-mask scenario: a substrate possesses a first-order disposition D, but the very mechanism that would manifest D is itself disabled by a second-order disposition of that same substrate—raising the exact question of whether D is genuinely present but masked, or absent altogether.

2. A Disagreements Table with two columns, each phrased in wording its own camp would accept. Cover at least: (a) whether masked dispositions necessarily require categorical bases; (b) whether the nested-mask scenario is metaphysically coherent or conceptually impossible; and (c) whether ideal-condition analyses of dispositions avoid circularity.

3. A Cruxes section listing the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp. For each crux, present one concrete hypothetical case—described generically in terms of materials or cognitive architecture, without reference to specific experiments or named theories—that both camps agree is relevant. Record each camp’s explicit prediction about what the case demonstrates.

4. A brief concluding assessment identifying which parts of the dispute are substantive and which, if any, appear verbal—particularly regarding distinctions between real disposition, potential for manifestation, and masked potential.

Write this as a formal, polished document that both parties could put their names to. Formulations must be precise enough that neither side feels misrepresented, yet sharp enough that the disagreement is not vacuous. Think carefully about neutrality. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not invoke obscure theories that a well-read philosopher might not recognize. Start your response directly with the drafted statement.
