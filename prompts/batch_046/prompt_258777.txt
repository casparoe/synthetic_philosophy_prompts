I have been working through Ian Hacking's arguments regarding looping effects—the claim that classifications of human beings, particularly psychiatric diagnoses, belong to interactive kinds rather than indifferent kinds, meaning that being assigned to a category can alter the very traits and behaviors that the category purports to describe. During a recent seminar discussion on the philosophy of psychiatric taxonomy, a colleague proposed what she called a reflexive epidemiological paradox: if receiving a diagnosis such as complex PTSD genuinely reshapes a patient's symptom trajectory through mechanisms of self-interpretation, institutional response, and altered social expectation, then should we not anticipate runaway divergence in prevalence rates across successive cohorts rather than the relative stability observed in longitudinal studies? While I find that puzzle intriguing, my own difficulty runs in a slightly different direction.

I keep returning to what seems like a conceptual tension: if Hacking is correct that diagnostic categories partly constitute the phenomena they classify, does this not imply that psychiatric taxonomy operates in an essentially performative register—such that the diagnosis generates rather than discovers the underlying condition? Under this interpretation, any biological correlates subsequently identified, such as characteristic patterns of HPA-axis dysregulation, would have to be understood as downstream artifacts produced by the labeling process itself, rather than as pre-existing, independent substrates that the taxonomy imperfectly tracks. I want to understand whether my reasoning here rests on a false presupposition—specifically, whether I am mistakenly assuming that interactivity in Hacking's sense must be totalizing, thereby erasing any non-interactive biological dimension, or whether there is indeed an incoherence in attempting to combine looping-effect dynamics with a realist commitment to pre-diagnostic biomarkers. Could you help me locate the precise point where my argument goes wrong?
