As a novelist attempting to write a morally serious novel about guilt and forgiveness, I have tried to formalize my philosophical commitments so I can understand what my characters actually owe one another. Here is where I have landed on Strawsonian reactive attitudes and moral responsibility. (1) I accept Strawson's basic framework: moral responsibility is constituted by the appropriateness of reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation—rather than by any metaphysical condition like libertarian free will. (2) I am a thoroughgoing determinist, convinced that every human choice is the necessary outcome of prior neural and environmental causes. (3) I believe reactive attitudes are fitting only when an agent's action expresses a quality of will that is genuinely attributable to them in interpersonal space, not merely caused. (4) I think determinism undermines that very attribution, because if every quality of will is causally inevitable, then praising or blaming it is like praising or blaming the weather. (5) Nevertheless, I hold that we should preserve reactive attitudes among functioning adults, because to suspend them globally would dissolve the interpersonal relationships that give human life its narrative coherence. (6) I think the objective attitude is appropriate in cases of severe mental illness or extreme coercion, but I also believe we should eventually reintegrate such agents into the reactive framework rather than permanently excluding them. (7) I hold that reactive attitudes are essentially narrative structures: they require embedding a particular action into a larger story about the agent's practical identity, and an attitude is fitting only if that embedded narrative is one the agent could coherently endorse.

Go through these commitments and audit them for consistency. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision for each? Rank the tensions by severity, specify what each revision costs me in terms of the other commitments, and tell me which single change would clean up the most problems without destroying the framework.
