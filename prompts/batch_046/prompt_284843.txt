She thinks phenomenal properties are causally inert with respect to physical events, but she also wants to say they're not epistemically inert—she's cooked up this notion of "epistemic transmission without causal transmission" to explain how we can form justified beliefs about our own experiences even if those experiences don't cause the beliefs. She admits this pushes her toward what she calls "phenomenal isolationism" about other minds, which she actually treats as a feature rather than a bug.

I need around eight or nine questions that go beyond having her restate the view. For each, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give—say, if she claims epistemic transmission is a primitive relation, ask why that shouldn't just be causation by another name; if she says we still have analogical reasoning for other minds, press on whether analogical reasoning can do any work without a causal bridge between minds.

So I'd like one question asking whether her "epistemic transmission" move actually breaks the stalemate or just relabels it, and whether she thinks her view risks being unfalsifiable if every objection can be absorbed by tweaking the transmission story.
