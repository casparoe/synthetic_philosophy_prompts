Here is my current package of commitments: (1) Schlick is correct that the question of life’s meaning should be reconstructed as asking which present activities are intrinsically worth living for, with the answer being playful absorption rather than future achievement; (2) I accept Schlick’s logical-empiricist constraint that any meaningful statement about value must be reducible to reports of possible experience, rendering cosmic-purpose talk cognitively meaningless; (3) I maintain that evolutionary explanations of play-behavior—fitness, learning, social coordination—are legitimate and compatible with play’s present intrinsic value, since origin in selection does not entail present instrumentality; (4) I hold that identifying genuinely playful states requires first-person phenomenological access to qualities like absorption and loss of self-consciousness, not merely third-person behavioral criteria; (5) I want to claim that long-term, goal-structured projects such as scientific research or parenting can be meaningful in Schlick’s sense, because they can be lived with the phenomenology of play despite having external aims. Please audit this set: rank the tensions by severity, identify which pairs only appear conflicting, and for each genuine tension say what the cheapest revision would cost. Deliver your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one to two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
