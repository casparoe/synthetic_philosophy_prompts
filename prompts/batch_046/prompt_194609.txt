You are a philosopher of science consulting for an institutional review board that must evaluate whether research protocols may legitimately attribute beliefs, desires, or intentions to non-human systems—brain organoids, AI architectures, or animals—without falling into anthropomorphism or unnecessary reductionism. Translate Daniel Dennett’s account of real patterns and the three stances (physical, design, intentional) into a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure that a non-philosopher reviewer can run in under thirty minutes.

The procedure should determine, for any given system, whether adopting the intentional stance is epistemically warranted. Include these elements in order: (1) a test for whether the system exhibits predictive regularities that are genuinely captured at the intentional level rather than merely tolerated there; (2) an evaluation of inter-stance translation loss—whether substituting design-level or physical-level explanations systematically destroys those predictive regularities; (3) an intervention test asking whether manipulating design-level variables reliably alters intentional-level predictions in a way that lower-level descriptions miss; (4) explicit stop-points where the answer is “no—revert to the design or physical stance”; (5) clear flags for where a judgment call about values (not predictive success) must replace the procedural result. Be honest about which steps are judgment calls disguised as checklist items.

Apply the procedure to two cases, including one where the result will be unwelcome to the researchers proposing the study:
- Case A: A cortical brain organoid that learns to avoid an aversive stimulus through synaptic remodeling, with no internal representational architecture.
- Case B: A distributed multi-agent AI system with fully transparent reward functions that nevertheless exhibits stable, cross-context deceptive behavior toward human overseers.

For each case, show the step-by-step evaluation, note where human judgment must take over, and state the final stance recommendation. Then specify two specific things the procedure cannot determine—especially regarding ethical permission to intervene—so that reviewers do not mistake stance adoption for moral status. Think carefully, step by step, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
