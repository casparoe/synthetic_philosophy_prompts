Write the full specification of desiderata that any adequate theory distinguishing original from derived intentionality must satisfy, with particular attention to synthetic and reconstructed cognitive artifacts—specifically, a physically rebuilt Dennett-style two-bitser implemented on non-biological substrates, and a continuously embodied, self-updating representational system that produces semantic content not traceable to its designers or training data. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or thought experiment (e.g., whether the reconstructed two-bitser can misrepresent without an original biological interpreter present; whether a synthetic system’s novel representational states are about the world or only about its own design history; the need to preserve the distinction between intentionality that is borrowed from a community of users versus generated through autonomous representational dynamics), explain why failure on that point collapses the original/derived distinction, and mark whether the requirement is non-negotiable for any credible theory or a preference of a particular camp (such as biological essentialism, pure functionalism about mental content, or the intentional-stance view). Identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given standard objections—such as the Chinese Room argument, the problem of intrinsic versus observer-relative aboutness, or the challenge of explaining misrepresentation in systems lacking evolutionary history—and, if so, state exactly which combination produces the contradiction. Finally, indicate which single requirement does the most work in excluding existing positions, and keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring named philosophers. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the theory should be evaluable on its conceptual merits alone.
