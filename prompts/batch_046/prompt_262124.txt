Second, there is what might be called the resource-relative simplicity thesis: the idea that Occam's razor should be indexed not to absolute Kolmogorov complexity but to the computational and representational resources available to a particular inductive agent. This injects a bounded-rationality constraint into the normative force of simplicity, but it also risks making simplicity subjective or circular. Is developing a rigorous, non-circular formulation of resource-relative simplicity one of the most consequential open problems, or is it a conceptual dead end?

Third, there is the ontological implication: because Kolmogorov complexity is language-dependent (the choice of universal Turing machine changes complexity values up to an additive constant), some argue that simplicity cannot track objective ontology. Others reply that the constant is negligible for large structures, or that a canonical programming language can be justified independently. How important is it to settle whether Kolmogorov-based simplicity can ground a realist ontology, versus treating simplicity as purely epistemic?

Fourth, there is the old-evidence problem: standard Bayesian confirmation theory struggles with updating on evidence already known. Some have suggested that Solomonoff induction handles old evidence differently because the prior already assigns probabilities based on generative mechanisms rather than on belief states. Does clarifying this relationship—whether Solomonoff induction genuinely resolves the problem or merely relocates it—matter more for philosophy of science than the other questions above?
