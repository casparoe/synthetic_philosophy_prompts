im working on a research agenda about aesthetic testimony — not just whether critics can tell you a painting is beautiful, but whether their testimony can calibrate your own seeing of it without you having viewed the work first. there are a few open lines: (1) if testimony works by belief-transmission then first-hand experience should be replaceable, but if it works by calibrating your future seeing then experience is still neccesary but redirected; (2) whether aesthetic disagreement is genuinely cognitive or just a clash of sensibilities, and what that means for who counts as an expert; (3) how digital reproductions change the testimony game — can a curator's commentary on a hi-res scan count, or does the loss of scale and texture break the calibration chain?

if you had to advise a grad student with three years and no lab budget on which of these lines (or something im missing) to actually pursue, how would you rank them? i care less about the list than the reasoning: which is upstream of the others, which is tractable without running huge empirical studies, and what kind of result over the next couple years would make you reorder the priorities? also, which implications for actual art-world practice — museum labeling, art education, AI-generated curatorial text — actually depend on getting the answer right, and which are overated?
