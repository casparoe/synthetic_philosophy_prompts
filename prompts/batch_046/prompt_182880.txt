I come from a software engineering background with no formal training in philosophy, but I have been working through Daniel Dennett's distinction between the physical, design, and intentional stances, together with his idea that real patterns are those that support reliable prediction and compression. From that starting point, I have arrived at a position I suspect may not survive careful scrutiny: that in both engineered systems and in minds, the intentional stance is never genuinely autonomous but is always reducible to the design stance, because any pattern robust enough to be real is already captured at the functional level. In software, when we ascribe beliefs or desires to a system—say, that an authentication module believes the user is valid—we are only offering a compressed summary of state-transition rules; the ascription adds no predictive leverage beyond the design-level specification, and it fails what I think of as a refactoring-invariance test: rename variables or change the interface, and the ascribed mental content shifts even though the underlying design is unchanged. If minds operate analogously, mental states are not independent real patterns but interface-level glosses on functional architecture. I want a rigorous map of what could defeat this reduction. Not merely the familiar objection that we need intentional vocabulary to predict human behavior, but specifically: which intuitions, which formal results (for instance about emergence, multiple realizability, or predictive accuracy across substrates), which empirical findings (perhaps in neuroscience or in the behavior of autonomous artificial agents), and which conceptual distinctions it stands or falls with. What does this view predict that its rivals do not—say, about the redundancy of folk psychology or about the conditions under which mental ascription should disappear? Which potential defeaters would a committed defender of the reduction concede as genuinely damaging, and which could be dismissed as anthropocentric bias or as mistaking pragmatic convenience for ontological commitment? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and indicate whether the position is well insulated by qualifications or whether there is a specific class of cases—perhaps involving misaligned autonomous systems, pathological mental states where functional description breaks down, or social coordination that seems to require irreducible intentionality—where it risks clear failure.
