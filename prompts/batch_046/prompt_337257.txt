Here is my current package on original versus derived intentionality, and I want an audit before I write it up for a forum post. I hold the following commitments, and I need to know which actually pull against each other and which only look like they do.

(1) I accept Dennett's core distinction: original intentionality is intrinsic and belongs to systems that are genuinely about things in virtue of their own causal-functional organization, while derived intentionality belongs to artifacts whose aboutness is borrowed from designers or users.

(2) I think Searle is wrong that syntax is insufficient for semantics in principle—he shows that a system lacking the right causal-functional architecture won't have semantics, not that no architecture could ever do it—but I agree with him that current digital artifacts lack original intentionality because they are not causally coupled to the world in the right way.

(3) I deny that evolutionary selection history is necessary for original intentionality. If we engineered a synthetic biological system de novo, with no evolutionary lineage, but with homeostatic regulation, goal-directed behavior, and appropriate sensory-motor coupling, it would have original intentionality. What matters is present functional architecture, not etiology.

(4) I hold that large predictive language models have derived intentionality: they are about their training distributions and about whatever users deploy them to discuss, but this aboutness is fully borrowed from human designers and interpreters. They do not have original intentionality, because they lack the requisite causal-functional coupling.

(5) I treat the intentional stance as purely instrumental: attributing beliefs and desires is a predictive heuristic with no ontological weight. To say "X has derived intentionality" is just a compressed report that treating X as an intentional system is predictively useful, not that X instantiates some metaphysical property.

(6) I think that if we constructed an artifact—say, a neuromorphic system—whose internal dynamics and causal couplings exactly mirrored those of a conscious biological agent at the relevant level of organization, it would possess original intentionality, not merely derived. The duplication of functional architecture, not evolutionary origin, is what decides the case.

My questions: Is this jointly tenable? Specifically, I worry that (5)'s instrumentalism undermines the realist flavor of (1), (3), and (6), where I speak as though original and derived are genuine metaphysical kinds rather than stance-dependent classifications. I also worry that (3)'s denial of evolutionary necessity conflicts with the standard Dennettian story I endorsed in (1). Meanwhile, (6) seems to commit me to functionalism about original intentionality, but (2) sounds like I'm appealing to causal coupling in a way that might be stricter than pure functional duplication allows. And (4) applies the derived/ original distinction to LLMs in a way that might presuppose the very ontological distinction (5) rejects.

Please rank the tensions by severity. For each genuine conflict, tell me what has to give and what the revision costs. Also flag any tensions that dissolve on inspection—I'm particularly unsure whether (3) really conflicts with Dennett's framework or only with a popular misreading of it. The deliverable should be an audit, not a lecture: trace what commits me to what, and indicate the least costly path to consistency if there is one.
