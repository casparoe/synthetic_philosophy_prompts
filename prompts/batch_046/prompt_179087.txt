Explain the conceptual problem that arises in hinge epistemology when we try to distinguish a hinge proposition from an ordinary empirical belief that happens to be extremely well-confirmed. Focus specifically on basic conservation principles—for instance, the assumption that material objects do not spontaneously cease to exist when unobserved. If hinges are defined by their functional immunity to doubt rather than by evidential support, how should we account for the fact that such conservation assumptions can be implicitly revised in certain theoretical contexts (for example, interpretations of quantum measurement) without being treated as empirical hypotheses that have been falsified? In your answer, clarify whether this suggests that hinges are better understood as non-propositional ways of acting or as tacit propositional commitments, and explain what that distinction implies for the very possibility of rational hinge revision.
