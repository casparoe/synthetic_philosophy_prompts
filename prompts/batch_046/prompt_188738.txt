I've been trying to map out why the old fight over where meaning lives in formal systems—what Hofstadter in *Gödel, Escher, Bach* frames as the tension between the frame (the interpreter's mapping), the outer message (the world-structure the symbols track), and the inner message (the self-referential coherence of the system itself)—seems permanently stuck. In the philosophy-of-computation circles I follow, everyone grants Gödel's results, everyone grants that strange loops exist, and yet the same three camps never budge: the frame-externalists insist that without an external interpreter assigning reference, a symbol is just syntax; the outer-message structuralists hold that meaning is fixed by isomorphism with an independent reality, so a system "means" only insofar as it reliably tracks that structure; and the inner-message intrinsicists claim that once a system achieves sufficient recursive self-modeling—say, a formal system that not only contains a Gödel sentence but can revise its own axioms in response to that self-reference—the mapping becomes internal, and reference is sustained by the loop itself rather than imported. The disagreement has lasted decades despite shared agreement on the mathematics, and I can't tell whether it's getting anywhere.

My working theory is that this isn't really about computation or semantics at all, but about what we are willing to count as an explanation of intentionality: the externalists demand a causal-historical chain, the structuralists demand a correspondence relation, and the intrinsicists demand only internal coherence. But I'm not sure—maybe there is a hidden formal question about whether "semantic closure" (a system's capacity to stabilize its own interpretations across self-modification) is even definable without circularity, or maybe the sides are just answering different questions under the word "meaning"—one about causal origins, one about representational content, one about phenomenological understanding. Can you diagnose whether this is a verbal dispute, a substantive clash about standards of reference, or an illusion created by running together distinct explanatory projects? Be concrete about what each side actually says: do the externalists reject intrinsicism because they think self-modification collapses into mere syntactic reshuffling, or because they think reference requires an origin outside the loop?

And given your diagnosis, tell me what, if anything, would count as progress. Is there a conceptual distinction—perhaps between "tracking" and "interpreting," or between static self-reference and dynamic self-modification—that would dissolve the stall? Would a formal result showing that semantic closure is either achievable or impossible move anyone, or is the impasse structural—perhaps a sign that the question "where is meaning located?" is ill-posed and should be replaced by separate questions about origin, content, and understanding? I want an honest answer about whether this debate can be ended, or whether it should be abandoned for being the wrong kind of problem.
