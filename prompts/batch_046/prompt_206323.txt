A close friend is enrolled in an experimental clinical protocol for a degenerative neural condition. Over eight years, small regions of her brain are replaced one by one with synthetic analogues that replicate both cognitive function and, according to her reports, subjective experience. At each stage she can halt or roll back. She is now past the midpoint and feels continuous with her pre-procedure self, but she refuses to sign long-term contracts—marriage papers, a will, even a five-year lease—because she believes the person who will exist after the final stage may not be her, and she does not want to bind someone she is not, or be bound by commitments made by someone she might not become. She asks me whether she is being rational or overly cautious, and whether being replaced gradually but completely preserves the same person in any sense that matters for responsibility and commitment. I want your analysis of the philosophical structure here—how identity, continuity, and the ethics of binding one's future stages interact—rather than generic life advice. Please state how confident you are in your claims and identify the specific point about which you are least certain.
