I run a small grad reading group on moral psych and metaethics—about 8 people, 2 hours, once a week. We just worked through some Henrich on WEIRD populations and Haidt's moral foundations stuff, and now I want an in-class activity that makes cross-cultural divergence actually feel like a live problem for moral objectivity, not just an anthropology fact. Design a structured deliberation/game: each participant secretly gets one of four hidden "framework cards" that weight harm/care, authority/respect, loyalty/in-group, and purity/sanctity differently (e.g. card A weights harm at 80% and purity at 5%, card D reverses that). They then have 30 minutes to reach unanimous agreement on one ambiguous case—say, an elder refusing a life-saving blood transfusion becuase it violates a religious purity code—without ever revealing or citing their card. They must argue only from shared premises all cards supposedly accept. After the vote we reveal cards and check whether anyone actually converged or just talked past each other. Include specific rules for speaking turns, how to assign cards secretly (sealed envelopes? random draw?), a simple non-monetary payoff scheme like social credit points, and a debrief that maps the coordination failure or fake success onto the philosophical debate—epistemic error, metaphysical pluralism, or hidden framework divergence. Be honest about what feature of moral objectivity the game can't reproduce, probably the claim that there's a mind-independent fact one group is closer to. Also tell me what will predictably break the first time I run it, and please don't include any bibliographies or citations since I just want a runnable design.
