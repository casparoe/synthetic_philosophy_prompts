I chair the ethics review panel for the Meridian Consortium, a multi-site research and intervention body that receives roughly fifteen protocol proposals each month involving entities outside standard human-subject and vertebrate-animal regulations. Our portfolio spans insect colonies, cephalopods, synthetic neural organoids, autonomous environmental sensor networks, and hybrid cyber-physical systems. We have adopted in principle a gradualist position on moral status: it is not a binary threshold but a graded property that tracks phenomenal capacity, affective valence, integrative self-modeling, and functional autonomy, with obligations scaling by tier rather than triggered by a single rights boundary. Our reviewers have scientific or policy backgrounds but are not philosophers or neuroscientists, and each proposal must be assessed in under thirty minutes with a signed tier determination.

Please construct a reusable decision procedure—the Graded Status Protocol—that a reviewer can run in ordered steps. For each step, state the precise question to ask, what counts as sufficient evidence to proceed or to stop, and whether the answer is a matter of observable fact or requires an explicit value judgment that the reviewer must flag rather than resolve. Specify the point at which a proposal is rejected outright, the point at which it is approved with tier-specific conditions, and the point at which it must be escalated to the full panel because the entity falls between tiers or the dimensions conflict. The procedure must not require reviewers to recall specific philosophical texts or specialized neuroscience literature, and it must be honest about where it will misfire.

Then apply the protocol to three proposals:

1. A field deployment of five hundred adaptive temperature-humidity sensors that modify their own sampling algorithms in response to local microclimate feedback loops, with no biological tissue involved.
2. A laboratory study isolating octopuses for brief, non-invasive cognitive testing, accompanied by documented stress-indicator monitoring and a defined recovery period.
3. A large-scale agricultural intervention using a targeted biological agent to suppress a crop-pest insect population, where the agent induces a slow, multi-hour physiological decline rather than rapid death.

Include at least one application in which the protocol yields a recommendation the panel is likely to find unwelcome or counterintuitive, and identify exactly which structural feature of the case drives that result.

Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do: the classes of entities for which it is not designed, the boundary conditions where it will systematically fail, and the philosophical disagreements about sentience and moral weight that it presupposes rather than settles.
