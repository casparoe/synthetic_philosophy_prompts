ok so ive been chewing on hofstadters strange-loop idea of the I - the self as a self-referential symbol that gets its grip from being embedded in a feedback loop of representations, not from some ghost in the machine. but if you take that seriously, there are a bunch of directions people could push it, and im not sure which ones are actually worth the time.

like, one line is asking whether an LLM can form a strange loop at all - not just talking about itself, but having its self-referential symbol actually causally loop back into its weights or its context window in a way that creates genuine closure. another is the collective-I problem: if a ant colony (or a distributed system) has the right kind of cross-node feedback, could there be a macro-strange-loop without any single node carrying it? then theres the pathology angle - dissociative disorders as cases where the loop fractures but the symbol persists, or vice versa, which might tell us something about which part is load-bearing. and theres this weirder idea ive been playing with: symbolic leakage, where a self-referential symbol trained in one substrate starts echoing in another (say, a model copying its own outputs back into training data) and you get a kind of ghost-loop that isnt anchored to any single system.

so heres the thing - if you had, say, three years and a small group to actually work on one of these, which open question or implication should you prioritize? i want a real ordering, not just 'all are interesting.' which ones are upstream of the others? which are tractable with current tools (conceptual or empirical) versus which are just philosophy-of-mind word games? and specifically, does the collective-I problem actually depend on solving the single-system loop first, or can they run in parallel? also, im skeptical about the leakage idea - is it a genuine extension of hofstadter or just a metaphor stretched too thin?

write like youre advising someone who actually has to pick a dissertation topic, not a survey article. defend the ranking with criteria - tractability, how many downstream questions it unlocks, whether getting it wrong actually costs us something practical. and please, no bibliography requests - i want your reasoning, not a reading list.
