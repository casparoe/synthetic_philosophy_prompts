I just finished a long and somewhat frustrating argument with a close friend about consciousness—specifically, whether the global workspace account actually explains anything about subjective experience, or whether it merely redescribes information access in neural terms. My friend works in distributed systems and kept insisting that “broadcasting to modules” sounds exactly like a publish-subscribe message bus, and that if that is all consciousness amounts to, then there is nothing mysterious left to explain. I tried to push back using the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, and I mentioned the overflow idea that phenomenal content can outstrip what is globally available, but I kept feeling that I was smuggling metaphysical assumptions into my engineering analogies without noticing. So I want you to do a rigorous, honest translation.

Take the three core positions in this debate—(1) the access-consciousness / phenomenal-consciousness distinction, (2) the global workspace claim that widespread broadcasting to cognitive modules is sufficient, or perhaps constitutive, of consciousness, and (3) the overflow argument that phenomenal consciousness can contain more information than can be accessed or reported—and restate each in the vocabulary of distributed computing and database architecture that I actually use: message buses and event sourcing, read replicas versus primary nodes, consistency models and eventual consistency, observable state versus internal node memory, API exposure, serialization protocols, and “dark” state that is never published to subscribers. Specify exactly which engineering concept corresponds to which philosophical claim, and do not distort the philosophy to make the analogy cleaner.

Then give me the residue report, position by position: what translates cleanly into this vocabulary, what can only be carried across with distortion, and what—if anything—has no expression at all in how distributed systems are designed. Pay special attention to whether there is any counterpart for the “what it is like” dimension, for the normative idea that unaccessed experience still matters, and for the intuition that broadcasting might be a functional description rather than an ontological explanation. Be explicit about whether the untranslatable parts are missing because distributed-systems language genuinely lacks the representational resources, or because the original philosophical claims depend on treating first-person reports as privileged data in a way that collapses once you treat them as just another observable event on the bus.

Do not smuggle in the assumption that internal, unobservable node state just is phenomenal consciousness, and do not assume that because a system holds dark state it therefore has experience. I want the translation to fail honestly where it fails. At the end, state your bottom-line judgment about the source of the residue in a fixed, machine-readable form: a final line beginning exactly with “Verdict:” followed by a brief phrase indicating whether the untranslatable remainder is mainly a limitation of the distributed-systems framework or mainly a sign that the consciousness debate is less substantive than it appears.
