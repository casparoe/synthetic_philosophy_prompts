Okay so I've been thinking way too much about this ever since my roommate started working at that neurotech startup and came home talking about substrate-independent mind migration like it was no big deal. I have these gut reactions about different replacement scenarios and I can't tell if there's actually a principle behind them or I'm just being inconsistent.

So: if they replace one of my neurons with a silicon equivalent, I'm still me, that's easy. If they do it slowly over decades until every neuron is synthetic, I'm still me - Ship of Theseus type deal. But if they scan my brain and build an exact copy on a server and then turn off the original, I feel like that's death, not upload. Even if the copy wakes up and remembers everything. Now here's where I get weird: if they replace half my brain with chips, then wait like twenty years, then replace the other half, I still think that's me at the end. Even if during those twenty years they upgrade all the chips in the first half so there's literally zero material left from either my original brain or the first replacements. But if they do that same total replacement all at once in one surgery? Still feels like death.

There's also this other case that's been bugging me. What if the synthetic parts don't run on the same clock? Like they replace my left hemisphere gradually, but it communicates with my right hemisphere through a bridge that has a 300 milisecond delay, so my consciousness is kind of stretched across two different processing speeds. I still feel like that's one me, just with a weird lag. But if they made two complete copies and ran them in parallel with the same delay? That feels like twins, not one person with a lag. And one more: what if every synthetic neuron they implant occasionally crashes and restores from a backup taken an hour earlier? So over fifty years, every part of my brain has died and been rebooted dozens of times, but there's always been at least one region running continuosly to hold the narrative together. I think I'm still me. But if they rebooted everything at once - total blackout for a second - then restored? That feels like death and replacement.

Is there any coherent principle that saves all these verdicts? Or do I have to drop the idea that gradual replacement is different from copying? I feel like I'm appealing to something like continuous functional integration but then the clock-speed case breaks that, and the reboot case breaks material continuity. What's the actual principle, if any, and which of my gut feelings should I throw out?
