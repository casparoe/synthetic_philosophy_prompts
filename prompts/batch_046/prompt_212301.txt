Write a deadpan parody of a corporate compliance manual for a fictional organization, the Institute of Recursive Governance, structured literally around Douglas Hofstadter’s concepts of tangled hierarchies and the inviolate level, using M. C. Escher’s 1948 lithograph Drawing Hands as the institutional emblem. The manual should be approximately 650 words and should read as an authentic, dry bureaucratic document—no winking, no explicit explanation of the philosophical reference. Include the following sections, each taking the logic of strange loops seriously as administrative policy: (1) Organizational Structure, in which every manager is simultaneously a direct report to their own subordinate and promotions loop back to entry-level positions; (2) Conflict-of-Interest and Audit Protocol, in which employees are required to review their own performance and whistleblowers must file reports with themselves; (3) The Inviolate Level Exemption, which specifies that custodial and facilities staff maintain the physical building but are categorically excluded from all reporting charts, and explains—without breaking deadpan—why this exclusion is structurally necessary rather than discriminatory; and (4) a newly invented procedural body, the Strange Loop Arbitration Board, with specific, niche rules for resolving disputes in which both parties claim authority over each other (for example, a tie-breaking vote cast by the complainant’s own subordinate, or a requirement that appeals be filed one hierarchical level below the appellant’s current position). The satire should exaggerate real features of hierarchical organization and self-reference—such as the hidden dependency of every system on an unacknowledged ground—rather than inventing absurdities that have no counterpart in actual bureaucracy. The piece should be readable without prior knowledge of Hofstadter, but should reward a reader who recognizes the allusion to Drawing Hands and the idea that every hierarchy requires a level it cannot contain. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific academic texts; instead, ground the parody in invented institutional details that make the concept concrete.
