I am taking a graduate seminar in applied ethics that has spent the last few weeks on procreative morality and the harm-benefit asymmetry associated with David Benatar. The usual classroom taxonomy—antinatalism, pronatalism, and a few qualified intermediates—feels too label-driven and misses the actual fault lines in people's judgments. I want a diagnostic branching taxonomy that sorts positions by answers to concrete comparative cases rather than by self-identification.

Please construct a tree with at least eight leaves. The root should ask whether there is a morally relevant asymmetry between bringing a suffering being into existence and failing to bring a flourishing potential being into existence. From there, branch on: (1) whether non-existence can be a deprivation for someone who never exists; (2) whether the asymmetry holds when we distinguish "benefiting an existing subject" from "creating value that requires a subject to exist"; (3) whether there is a quality-of-life threshold below which asymmetry considerations are overridden; and (4) whether collective or ecological considerations—resource limits, climate burdens of new lives—generate independent procreative reasons.

Every node must be a specific case judgment or claim—e.g., "If a couple can either have no child or have a child whose life will be mildly good but resource-intensive, is there a reason to have the child?"—so that a respondent can navigate the tree without knowing philosophical labels. No node should ask directly whether one "accepts Benatar's premise." Each leaf should carry the name of an actual position if it exists (e.g., strong antinatalism, permissive pronatalism, threshold-sensitive antinatalism, collective-duty pronatalism) or be marked "unnamed," followed by one sentence describing its most awkward commitment. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate. For each unoccupied branch, briefly diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The deliverable should be self-contained: no bibliographies, citation lists, or references to texts I should read.
