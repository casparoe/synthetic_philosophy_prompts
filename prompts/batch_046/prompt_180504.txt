I am preparing a talk for my senior-year philosophy seminar, and I want to avoid the standard Humphrey example that every textbook uses. Give me an example of a conceptual issue arising between Lewis's counterpart theory and Kripke's transworld identity, but apply it to an artifact or institutional practice—such as a specific currency denomination, a chess variant, or a named algorithm—whose identity conditions depend partly on convention or functional role rather than on pure physical continuity. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: identify the object or practice, describe the relevant possible-world scenario, state the verdict delivered by counterpart theory, state the verdict delivered by transworld identity, and specify the precise pressure point—whether essential origin, functional equivalence, or conventional designation—that forces the disagreement beyond a merely verbal dispute.
