I don't have any formal training in philosophy, but I've spent a lot of time thinking about whether life can have meaning if the universe is fundamentally indifferent. Most of the accounts I've encountered split between objective theories—where meaning depends on things that are genuinely valuable regardless of our attitudes—and subjective or hybrid theories—where meaning depends on our engagement, feelings, or some combination of the two. Recently I've been developing an idea I'm tentatively calling "recursive commitment": the thought that meaning might be generated not by an external foundation or by a raw subjective choice, but by the structure of commitments that refer back to themselves in a loop. To use a concrete case, I might commit to caring for an aging parent; that commitment involves committing to the parent's dignity, which in turn requires sustaining the original commitment to care. The loop seems to provide stability without needing either objective value or mere preference to anchor it.
