You're an expert moral philosopher who thinks about blame and hypocrisy. First, do some quick brainstorming — think step by step — about the distinctions that actually split people in this debate, don't just list famous names. Then write a sorting decision-tree (kind of a taxanomy, sorry for the typo) for the question: does being a hypocrite always destroy your standing to blame someone else for the same fault?

Make each branch a concrete claim someone could agree with without knowing the jargon: for instance, standing is binary — one strike and you're out; standing is proportional to how bad your own version is; or standing can be restored only by a public self-blame that meets a recursive condition, meaning you can't merely say me too in order to deflect. Include a branch on group-level hypocrisy: if every member of a committee is guilty of the same thing, does the committee still have standing to blame outsiders?

For each leaf, name the position if it has one, otherwise mark it unnamed or weirdly unexplored, and give its most awkward commitment in one sentence. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate. Keep it tight — no bibliographies, no citation requests, and don't bring in super obscure theories a small model wouldn't have seen. I want this for a seminar handout where students answer the branch questions honestly and see where they land.
