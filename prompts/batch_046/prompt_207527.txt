I just finished a long argument with a friend about Adam Smith’s views on empire and colonies. She insists that his anti-colonial arguments in Wealth of Nations are purely economic—he treats colonies as costly mercantilist errors that drain the metropole—while I think they rest on a deeper moral principle drawn from his Theory of Moral Sentiments, namely that imperial domination violates the impartial spectator’s demand for sympathy across distance. Write an essay weighing these two interpretations. Examine whether Smith’s acceptance of certain colonial trade relationships undermines a purely moral reading, and whether his economic objections can stand without implicit normative commitments. Reach and defend a specific conclusion about which interpretation is more coherent, or whether they can be genuinely reconciled rather than merely layered together.
