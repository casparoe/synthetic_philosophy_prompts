If an ameliorative analysis succeeds in replacing our folk concept of personhood with a technically precise account—one that requires second-order intentionality and thereby excludes both human fetuses and current artificial language models—does this not demonstrate that prior debates about abortion and machine moral status were largely verbal disputes arising from a defective, ambiguous term? And if the disagreement was indeed verbal in that sense, why do philosophers continue to treat the engineered concept as resolving substantive normative questions, rather than conceding that they have merely changed the subject and left the original practical concerns untouched?
