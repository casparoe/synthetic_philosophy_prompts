Please argue that a reinforcement-learning agent's reward signal is structurally incapable of constituting affective valence—or suffering—unless it is embedded in a hierarchical predictive architecture that binds precision-weighted interoceptive errors to an embodied self-model. Contrast the RL reward, understood as a bare control-error correction in a feedback loop, with valence understood as a homeostatic allostatic prediction about an organism's own boundary conditions. Address the objection that a sufficiently negative reward signal just is suffering by definition, and explain why the presence of a self-model that distinguishes predicted from unpredicted bodily states transforms the normative structure of the signal from mere correction into something like care for the self. Build the case conceptually without requesting citations, bibliographies, or specific empirical studies.
