I am preparing a lecture for an advanced seminar on philosophy of mind and physical ethics, and I want to sharpen a conceptual problem that sits at the intersection of panpsychism and the ethics of suffering in fundamental physics. The standard framing treats consciousness as distributed among fundamental physical entities—whether quantum fields, spacetime points, or elementary particles—while insisting that macro-level descriptions remain ethically neutral, since any experiential properties at that scale are too minimal or too disorganized to constitute genuine suffering or well-being. Yet this macro-neutrality seems to conflict with a less-discussed implication: if even the most primitive experiential states carry some minimal axiological weight—say, a bare negative valence—then large-scale irreversible physical processes governed by entropy, such as thermal dissipation in open systems or the gradual evaporation of black holes through Hawking radiation, do not merely redistribute matter but aggregate, dilute, or annihilate vast quantities of micro-experiential states. I want an explanation of how to navigate this tension between macro-neutrality and what we might call thermodynamic aggregation: must a panpsychist who takes suffering seriously treat entropy-driven dynamics as ethically loaded because they alter the total distribution of experiential valence, or can she consistently maintain that macro-physical processes are ethically silent precisely because the micro-experiences involved lack the structural integration required for suffering?

In your answer, please distinguish carefully between what is settled—such as the core commitments of constitutive panpsychism, the standard macro-neutrality objection, and the physical descriptions of entropic processes—from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers who work on both panpsychism and the ethics of suffering. I am particularly interested in whether there are conceptual resources within panpsychism itself—perhaps distinctions between phenomenal and access consciousness, or between organized and disorganized experience—that allow the view to avoid the aggregation problem without collapsing into an implausible ethical quietism about physics. Do not present any particular resolution as the consensus; I want an honest map of where the conceptual fault lines actually lie.
