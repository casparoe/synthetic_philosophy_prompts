hey so i was trying to put together a little essay for this discussion group i joined—its not academic at all, just people who like arguing about whether evolutionary psychology is real science or mostly just-so stories. i feel like the usual debate misses the actual problem, so i came up with my own framing and i want to see if it holds up before i share it with the group.

basically i think the issue isnt that evo psych tells stories about the past—history does that too. the real issue is that it rarely tells you what shouldnt exist if the story is wrong. i call this the "null gap." like, if you say we evolved to fear snakes because they were dangerous, thats okay, but then you need to explain why we dont fear cars with the same intensity since cars kill way more people now. if the theory can just add another little story for cars ("we havent had time to adapt"), then it explains everything and nothing. so my essay argues that evo psych is only doing real science when it closes the null gap—when it makes risky predictions about what behaviors or preferences should be absent, not just present.

im applying this to gossip and to why people seem drawn to asymetrical patterns in some art but not others, using the idea that a true evolutionary explanation should predict the boundary conditions, not just the center. i also compare it to reverse-engineering a machine: you can tell a gear was built for torque, but you cant prove a designer existed. i think evo psych is like that—good at functional analysis, bad at origin proof, and the "just-so" charge only sticks when people skip the null gap check. does this hang together? im especially unsure about the art example—does it even make sense? and am i being unfair to researchers who actually try to test predictions? also is the reverse-engineering analogy too stretched? any comments on the logic or whether its intresting would be great. please dont worry about citations, i just want to know if the reasoning is okay.
