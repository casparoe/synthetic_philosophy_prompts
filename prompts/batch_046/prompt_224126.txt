I'm designing a module for an upper-level philosophy of science seminar, and we're pairing classic texts in the demarcation debate with dystopian fiction. For the unit on Popper's falsificationism and his critique of historicism, I want to use Aldous Huxley's Brave New World—not as a mere illustration, but as a text that complicates the boundary between scientific social management and unfalsifiable ideology.

Read the novel through Popper's framework: specifically, his distinction between piecemeal social engineering, which proceeds by testable and reversible interventions, and utopian social engineering, which advances an untestable holistic blueprint for society. The World Controllers claim to govern by scientific psychology, sociology, and biology; Mustapha Mond presents the stability of the World State as an engineered achievement that can be calibrated and defended. Where does the novel present this governance as genuinely predictive and falsifiable in Popper's sense, and where does it reveal the controllers' framework as an unfalsifiable historicist narrative—one that absorbs every deviation, whether Bernard's alienation, the Savage's rebellion, or John's suicide, as evidence that further conditioning or chemical adjustment is required?

Please avoid reducing the novel to a Popperian allegory: say where the literary texture resists the reading. Do the emotional scenes—the Savage's encounter with Shakespeare, Lenina's bewilderment, Linda's death—function as counter-instances that a falsificationist sociology would be forced to accommodate, or do they sit entirely outside the controllers' explanatory framework in a way that exposes its limits? And what does Huxley's specific choice to make the scientific society hedonistic rather than overtly coercive add to the philosophical debate about whether unfalsifiable social theories tend naturally toward manipulation?

I'm asking for an interpretation that a student could follow without outside sources, relying on common knowledge of the plot and on Popper's well-known arguments rather than on specialized scholarship or citation lists.
