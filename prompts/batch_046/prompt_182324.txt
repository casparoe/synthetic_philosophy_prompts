As a high school student applying for a semester-long independent-study fellowship, I want a polished, formal research proposal (about 1,500 words, purely conceptual, no lab component) on a question drawn from the thought experiments in The Mind's I. The core issue is this: if Einstein’s brain could be kept alive in a jar with all its internal state-transitions intact, and if a book could in principle encode every one of those same rules, do the two cases fail to be conscious for the same reason or for different ones? Existing functionalist answers tend to treat both as substrate-independent successes if the causal patterns match, while biological naturalists reject both because they lack living tissue; neither camp, however, distinguishes temporal continuity of processing from open-ended environmental coupling. I propose to inject a three-condition framework—internal state-transition fidelity, temporal continuity of those transitions, and open-loop feedback with an unpredictable environment—to map the logical space precisely. The proposal should state the question so it has a determinate answer, explain why separating these three conditions matters more than the standard functionalism-versus-naturalism framing, and specify the four possible conclusions: (1) only internal fidelity matters, so a sufficiently complex book is conscious; (2) temporal continuity is necessary but environmental coupling is not, so the jarred brain succeeds and the book fails; (3) open-loop feedback is necessary, disqualifying both; or (4) the three conditions cannot be conceptually separated, undermining the thought experiment itself. It should say which considerations—especially whether simulated internal feedback counts as genuine interaction—would decide among them, and identify what would count as failure (e.g., being unable to distinguish the jar from the book without appealing to unargued intuition about biology). Finally, it should include an honest scope assessment: this is tractable in one semester of conceptual analysis, but it will fail if it tries to solve the hard problem of consciousness generally rather than just mapping these conditions, and it should not include a bibliography or citation review since I will fill in references myself; it may refer to functionalism and biological naturalism as positions without attributing them to specific texts.
