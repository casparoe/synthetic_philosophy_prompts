Rather than asking for a standard literature survey, I want a branching decision tree or taxonomy whose root is a concrete commitment—whether an agent ought to treat her own reasoning process as evidential about identically-structured peers when causal interaction is impossible—and whose subsequent splits are similarly phrased as answerable claims, not theory names.

Finally, please flag any pairs of leaves that the tree genuinely cannot separate, for instance whether evidential decision theory and Hofstadterian superrationality collapse into one branch in Platonia but diverge in the Lottery.

Please present the result as structured data in a JSON object with the following schema: 'root_question' (string), 'branches' (array of objects with 'split_claim', 'yes_leads_to', 'no_leads_to'), 'leaves' (array of objects with 'label', 'position_name_or_unnamed', 'awkward_commitment'), 'inseparable_pairs' (array of strings describing the ambiguity), and 'unoccupied_combinations' (array of objects with 'combination_description' and 'diagnosis'). Ensure that every split claim is phrased as a question a student could answer without knowing which theoretical label it corresponds to, and that the synthetic distinctions—type-symmetric versus token-indexical reasoning, bounded superrationality, and indexical compartmentalization—are integrated as genuine branching nodes rather than decorative labels.
