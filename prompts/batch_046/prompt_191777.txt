For an independent philosophy essay I am writing, I have become stuck on a conceptual tension I do not see addressed clearly in standard treatments of the Holocaust's uniqueness and modernity. On one side is the position associated with the Historikerstreit that insists on the Holocaust's non-comparability and, in many formulations, its status as a singular rupture in history rather than one genocide among others. On the other side is Bauman's argument that the Holocaust was made possible not by some uniquely German pathology but by specifically modern bureaucratic rationality, organizational distancing, and instrumental reason—features that are, in principle, reproducible across modern societies. My question is not empirical but conceptual: if one grants Bauman's structural explanation substantial weight, does it necessarily collapse the uniqueness claim, or can the two be reconciled by redefining uniqueness away from non-replicability and toward something like "uniquely predictive of the destructive capacities latent in modern bureaucratic societies"?
