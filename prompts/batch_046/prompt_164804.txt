Give an example of a conceptual issue in speech-act theory in which a performative utterance satisfies every explicit felicity condition of its institutional procedure—correct speaker, correct formula, appropriate context, and intended uptake—yet still fails to produce its conventional effect because of a hidden dependency on background epistemic standing. Construct a specific scenario: identify the exact utterance (for instance, an appointment, a certification, or a formal declaration), the institutional framework that authorizes it, the conventional conditions that are fully met, and the precise nature of the credibility deficit that is not part of the codified rules but that causes the audience to withhold recognition. Then explain whether this failure should be classified as an Austinian misfire, an abuse, or as a distinct third category, and point to the feature of your case that makes the classification non-obvious—particularly how it differs from standard cases of unauthorized speakers or insincere commitments. Finally, specify what modification to the institutional procedure, if any, would be required to prevent this kind of failure without collapsing the distinction between conventional authority and epistemic trust.
