I was arguing with my friends about Dutch books after skimming some old LessWrong posts and we realised we were talking past each other — one of us treats it as proof that rational agents must have probabilistic beliefs, another thinks it’s basically a parable about not being exploitable in betting markets, and a third kept saying imprecise credences break the argument but couldn’t pin down whether the break is in the rationality condition or in the betting mechanics. I want a taxonomy, basically a decision tree for a small philosophy-of-science seminar handout, that sorts people by what they actually believe rather than by the label they wear, and I’d like it written in British English.

Build the tree from the logical space first, not from a list of named philosophers. Start with a node on what kind of mental state is being assessed: graded credence, all-or-nothing belief, or imprecise interval-valued credence. Then branch on whether being Dutch-booked counts as a failure of rationality proper or just of practical prudence; then on whether the betting framing is essential or merely a heuristic for spotting incoherence; then on whether allowing belief revision over time dissolves the vulnerability or just turns it diachronic. I want real positions as leaves — strict probabilist, betting-sceptic coherence critic, imprecise-credence defender, pragmatist about action-guidance, diachronic coherence advocate — plus at least one empty combination flagged and briefly diagnosed (for instance, a view combining all-or-nothing belief with imprecise intervals and strict rationalist failure seems incoherent because imprecision needs gradation). Please weave in the distinction between structural vulnerability, where your system has an internal incoherence that guarantees loss regardless of what the bookie knows, and point-in-time vulnerability, where you’re only exploitable if the bookie happens to know your current state — use that as a branch node if it fits naturally. Keep it to about six or eight leaves, phrase every node as a concrete claim someone could agree or disagree with without knowing the taxonomy, and make sure the instrument can seperate readers without them already knowing the interpretation names. Don’t bother with citations or a bibliography, I’ll add those myself if I need them — I just want the instrument. Oh, and inject a bit of niche framing about what failure actually looks like for agents who reject classical probabilism, since that’s the bit we kept circling back to over drinks.
