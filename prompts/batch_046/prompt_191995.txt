Here is my opening challenge: you claim I cannot conceive of a tree existing unperceived because any idea of it is necessarily in my mind and therefore perceived. But this seems to conflate occurrent mental representation—an idea I am now entertaining—with dispositional conception, the standing cognitive capacity to form or deploy a concept without currently perceiving its object. If my grasp of “tree-while-unobserved” is a counterfactual conceptual capacity rather than an occurrent perceptual image, then I can think precisely what you declare unthinkable without violating esse est percipi in any obvious way. Doesn’t your premise therefore smuggle in a controversial theory of mind—namely, that all conception is occurrent perception—rather than establishing idealism from neutral premises?
