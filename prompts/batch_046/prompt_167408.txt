everyone in the philosophy of physics circles i’ve been dipping into keeps saying Einsteins hole argument is basically just renumbering houses on a street: you can change every adress inside a neighborhood (the hole) while leaving the boundary untouched, so the adresses — spacetime points — cant be physically real. they lean on it to push the relationalist idea that points have no intrinsic identity, like nodes in a graph with no labels of their own. i’m retired from crystallography, and it strikes me there might be a closer anology in lattice defects: you can shift atoms inside a void without breaking the boundary conditions, but the defect still carries real physical energy and changes how the crystal behaves. so how far does the street-adress anology actually hold? give me the correspondance table — what plays the street, the houses, the adress book, the city planner — then the places it breaks, especially once you bring in my crystal-defect twist. does the relationalist conclusion survive on its own, or was the adress anology carrying the whole argument? and if the defect anology is the better one, what does that do to the case against intrinsic point identity?
