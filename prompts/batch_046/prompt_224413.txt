I've been working through the mechanics of Jeffrey conditionalization in connection with a problem about perceptual updating under ambiguous illumination, and I believe I've found a hidden circularity in the rigidity constraint that the standard treatments overlook. The rule requires us to preserve the conditional probabilities P(H|E_i) exactly while we replace the prior weights on the partition {E_i} with new evidential probabilities P'(E_i). But those conditionals were originally computed as ratios involving the very prior weights we are now discarding—P(H ∧ E_i)/P(E_i)—so preserving their numerical values seems to embed the old prior's denominators inside the update itself. Doesn't this mean that Jeffrey's framework implicitly smuggles the abandoned prior back in through the rigidity condition, and therefore makes it structurally impossible to revise our beliefs about the internal structure of a cell—say, to change our model of how lighting conditions affect color categorization—without breaking the rule entirely?
