Compare Graham Priest's dialetheism with Newton da Costa's hierarchical paraconsistent logic (specifically the C1 system) in their treatment of contradictions that arise at the boundaries of continuous change and vague predicates. Priest maintains that some contradictions are literally true—particularly at the limits where an object both has and lacks a property—because reality itself is inconsistent at these boundaries, and he rejects the principle of explosion on metaphysical grounds. By contrast, da Costa's C-systems permit contradictions within a formal theory without deriving triviality, using a consistency operator that allows one to mark certain propositions as well-behaved, but they do not commit to the truth of contradictions; rather, they treat inconsistency as a local feature of formalization that must be contained rather than affirmed. Focusing on a concrete scenario—an object undergoing continuous transformation such that, at an exact boundary instant, it both is and is not F—analyze whether the disagreement between dialetheism and C-system paraconsistency is primarily metaphysical (about whether the world instantiates true contradictions) or logical (about the function of negation and the consistency operator). Further, assess whether the dialetheist's willingness to assert that both the object is F and the object is not F are true adds explanatory power regarding why boundary cases feel genuinely paradoxical, or whether it is an ontologically extravagant addition to the logical tolerance that da Costa's framework already achieves. Which framework offers the more defensible account of why explosion should be rejected in this specific case, and does the answer depend on taking a stand about the reality of inconsistent states?
