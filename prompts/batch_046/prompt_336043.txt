I am designing a creative exercise for my upper-level ancient philosophy seminar, and I want a prompt that tests whether students can inhabit Aristotelian economic thought rather than reproduce the textbook gloss on Politics I.8–9 and Nicomachean Ethics V.5. Please write a short story of roughly 700 to 900 words set in a fictional Aegean trading settlement called Koinos, where a citizen-inventor named Theron has introduced “shadow weights”—tokenized instruments that represent anticipated future harvests rather than goods presently at hand, permitting exchange before production occurs. The narrative should center on a single transaction in which Theron uses a shadow-weight representing next year’s olive yield to acquire an imported luxury object that serves no household need, while a visiting interlocutor (do not make him a simple mouthpiece) challenges the practice through action rather than lecture. Crucially, the story should dramatize a rarely emphasized feature of Aristotle’s framework: chrematistike is problematic not merely because it is quantitatively limitless, but because it inverts the teleological order between use and exchange, making acquisition primary and production subordinate. Frame the philosophical stakes through the specific properties of Theron’s tokens—how they detach value from present use-value, circulate independently of the household, and generate a secondary market—so that a reader must infer whether this is a corruption of natural oikonomia or its logical extension. Keep the ending genuinely ambiguous, without resolving the dispute, and avoid any anachronistic vocabulary (do not use terms like “capitalism,” “futures contract,” or “financialization”); instead, let the conceptual tension emerge entirely from the characters’ practical reasoning about need, limit, and the purpose of exchange. The goal is a story that could not be generated by summarizing Aristotle’s distinction between natural and unnatural acquisition, because it requires applying that distinction to a novel institutional form that Aristotle never discussed.
