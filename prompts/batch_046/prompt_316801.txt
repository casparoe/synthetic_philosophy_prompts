I'm giving opening remarks tomorrow at a workshop called Sincerity and Silence. The room is split: half analytic philosophers who care about Grice and speech-act ethics, half working interperters who navigate Dutch-Japanese business talks every day. I want the talk to hang on whether white lies are actually wrong, using a frame I'm calling the politeness debt ledger — every small falsehood creates a hidden obligation to eventually correct the record, Dutch directness runs a near-zero balance (they have this norm, bespreekbaarheid, where not saying what's discussable is almost a moral failure), while Japanese indirectness accumulates debts but has rituals like the shift from tatemae to honne to settle them. The speech needs to ask whether that ledger just shows indirect cultures are better accountants, or whether it reveals they're hiding the same moral failure. It has to do real work for both sides: the philosophers should see an argument about sincerity versus cooperation, the interperters shouldn't feel like specimens. Five minutes, casual tone, one joke that lands for both halves, and don't resolve it with cheap comfort — the point is to open the tension, not close it.
