"Newton’s bucket argument shows that when water in a bucket rotates uniformly, its surface becomes concave, even though there is no change in the relative positions of the water and the bucket. Because a pure relationist holds that all motion is motion relative to other bodies, the relationist cannot explain why the water bulges. Newton therefore concludes that rotation must be relative to absolute space itself.

Mach criticized this by proposing that inertia is caused not by motion relative to absolute space but by interaction with all other matter in the universe—the distant fixed stars. Einstein adopted Mach’s principle as a foundational postulate and built it directly into the field equations of general relativity, so that the metric field is determined entirely by matter distribution. Consequently, GR eliminates any fixed background and demonstrates that spacetime is purely relational, which vindicates Leibniz and renders Newton’s bucket obsolete.

The hole argument demonstrates that if individual spacetime points have intrinsic identity, then the field equations allow physically identical metric fields assigned to different point sets, which is absurd. Modern fiber-bundle formulations treat the points as gauge artifacts, leaving only the relational metric structure. This confirms that there is no container of points—only relations—so the argument does not revive Newtonian substantivalism.

Finally, loop quantum gravity removes the continuous spacetime manifold and replaces it with discrete spin networks. Because there is no background geometry, this is a direct modern analog of Mach’s relational vision: geometry is nothing more than relations among quantum nodes. Taken together, Mach’s principle, general relativity, and loop quantum gravity form a consistent trajectory that eliminates absolute space and replaces it with purely relational dynamics."
