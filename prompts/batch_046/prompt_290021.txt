I am preparing a colloquium interview with an unnamed defender of approval-directed agency — the view that practical reasoning is fundamentally structured by the desire for approval rather than by direct pursuit of goals, and that this is not a deficient form of rationality but a normatively distinct one. Draft eight to ten questions that go beyond exposition: include at least one pressing the heteronomy objection (that approval-dependence makes the will contingent on shifting social attitudes), with a follow-up keyed to the likely reply that approval can be internalised and thereby rendered autonomous; include one on environmental robustness (how the agent operates when the relevant community is absent, silent, or fractured), anticipating the probable appeal to virtual or imagined audiences and then pushing that reply further; and include one introducing the problem of an “approval cascade” — if first-order approvals themselves require second-order approval to be authoritative, and so on, does the view collapse into infinite regress or an arbitrary termination point? Each question should turn on the actual architecture of the position, anticipate the standard defence, and include the counter-push. Avoid generic biographical questions, do not ask for bibliographies or citations, and write in formal British English.
