Bradley's point is that if Socrates and mortality are seperate items, we need a relation R to hold them together in the proposition Socrates is mortal. But then R is also an item, so we need R2 to connect R to Socrates, and so on forever. This infinite regress shows relations can't be real, since reality can't contain an infinite chain. Russell fixes this by saying relations are externally relating - they don't need to be inside the terms they connect, they just link them from outside. So the relation isn't another item that needs gluing; it just sits between Socrates and mortality.

However, this only solves the metaphysical side. For the proposition itself, we still need to explain why Socrates is mortal is one unified thought rather than three seperate mental items. Russell's theory of judgment handles this by saying the mind orders the terms in a specific way. So Bradley's problem gets pushed from ontology into psychology. Bradley would reject this because he thinks unity must be internal and real, not just a mental ordering. So Russell hasn't really solved the problem of propositional unity; he's just relocated it, and Bradley's monism is still more coherent on this point.
