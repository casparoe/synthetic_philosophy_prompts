Write a sestina about the paradox of tolerance, but frame it within the procedural logic of a peer-reviewed scientific institution rather than the familiar political context. Use these six end-words in rotation: tolerance, limit, voice, silence, enemy, democracy. In the envoi, stage the recursive moment when a system designed to include dissent must decide whether to exclude a bad-faith denialist, knowing that the exclusion itself becomes the intolerance that undermines the system's legitimacy. Let each repetition shift the institutional role of the word: "tolerance" should first name the norm of open debate, then the institutional policy, then the fragility of the norm; "enemy" should move from an external threat to an internal procedural failure; "silence" should progress from the absence of reply to an actively constructed absence of publication. The poem should preserve the logical structure of the paradox—unlimited tolerance destroys the conditions that make it possible—while showing that in a knowledge-producing community the collapse is procedural rather than dramatic: reviewers are selected, papers are rejected, and dissent is managed rather than crushed. I am preparing this for a graduate seminar that moves between philosophy of science and political philosophy, and I want a poem that functions as both formal verse and a conceptual diagram of how gatekeeping can fail from within.
