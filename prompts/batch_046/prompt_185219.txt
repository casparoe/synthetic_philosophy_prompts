I will defend the following thesis in a jurisprudence seminar next week: in legal systems that permit self-amendment—modeled on the game Nomic—allowing a second-order rule of change to apply to itself does not generate a vicious circularity of the kind Hart and Kelsen treat as fatal, but instead produces a benign infinite regress of justification structurally parallel to the entailment regress in Carroll’s dialogue on logic (where accepting a rule and accepting its applicability to a case requires a further rule, ad infinitum). The only halt to this regress is a “terminal rule” that is authoritative yet not itself amendable; but such a rule cannot be internal to the system without breaking the system’s claim to complete self-authorization. Hence legal self-reference is not paradoxical but formally incomplete in a precise, recoverable way.
