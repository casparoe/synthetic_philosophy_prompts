As a retired physicist recently studying philosophy of mind, I seek a classification instrument—not an encyclopedia entry—on consciousness unity in split-brain patients. Please construct a formal decision tree, presented as a branching taxonomy with six to eight leaves, that sorts philosophical positions according to three diagnostic dimensions: first, whether interhemispheric disconnection constitutes a failure of integration rather than a mere loss of information transfer; second, whether the division manifests primarily in perceptual rivalry, volitional conflict, or autobiographical discontinuity; third, whether subcortical coupling preserves a single virtual center despite cortical separation. At each branch, pose a concrete clinical scenario—such as simultaneous contradictory drawing with each hand, or verbal denial of a left-hand action—so that a respondent can choose without knowing the label. The leaves must include, at minimum, a unified-consciousness position, a dual-consciousness position, and a hierarchical or nested-integration position; any remaining logical combinations should be marked unnamed or unoccupied with a one-sentence diagnosis of whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Please also flag any pair of leaves that coincide on every clinical judgment but differ only in metaphysical interpretation of the corpus callosum. Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading, and do not reduce the tree to the question of how many persons are present.
