I'm running a workshop session for philosophy-of-science graduate students who have read basic representationalist accounts of knowledge but tend to assume that any competent practice can be decomposed into explicit propositional instructions. I want to teach them the knowing-how versus knowing-that distinction, but I am deliberately avoiding the usual bicycle-riding example because they will have encountered it in introductory texts and will treat it as a settled intuition pump rather than a live problem. Instead, I want the session built around a carrying case drawn from experimental practice: knowing how to stabilize a sensitive measurement apparatus through manual adjustment of feedback loops versus knowing that the target parameter should fall within a specified range. Plan the explanatory sequence: which conceptual piece must be in place before the distinction can even be posed in this setting, the order in which the components should unfold so that the anti-intellectualist point emerges from the example rather than being imposed on it, and the precise features the calibration scenario must possess and lack in order to do that work.

In particular, map the misconceptions these students are likely to bring or form—especially the idea that if a procedure can be fully algorithmized then the knowing-how was never genuine but only mechanical knowing-that, and the related confusion between tacit procedural skill and merely unconscious propositional belief—and specify exactly where in the sequence each should be anticipated and defused. I also want a single check-question I can pose at the end that would reveal whether they have grasped the structural distinction rather than just adopted the vocabulary, plus an explicit statement of what I should refuse to explain at this level—for instance, neurobiological mechanisms of procedural memory or the full history of the debate—and why leaving it out protects the argument.

Finally, as you lay this out, please separate what is broadly settled in the philosophical literature on this distinction from what remains genuinely contested among specialists—particularly regarding whether knowing-how can be fully analyzed in terms of knowing-that with practical modes of presentation—and do not present any one contested interpretation as if it were the consensus view.
