Write a poem in a broken sestina — six stanzas of six lines — where the end-words are only the four tetralemma corners (it is, it isnt, both, neither) plus emptiness and svabhava. Let them migrate like a normal sestina but have the four logical positions clash and overwrite each other across stanzas, so the envoi collapses into one repeated phrase. The point isnt to name-drop Madhyamaka but to let the form actually show why holding any corner still assumes inherent existence. Use British English. Be committed: say clearly whether you think this formal trick genuinely expresses Nāgārjuna's argument or just decorates it, and note where youre taking liberties with the classical ordering of the tetralemma or leaning on a contested reading of svabhava.
