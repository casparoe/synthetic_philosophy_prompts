I'm putting together a discussion for my film-club mates — we meet every other Tuesday and drink far too much tea — and next up is The Matrix, but I'm dead tired of the usual brain-in-a-vat Descartes comparison everyone wheels out. I want to try something weirder: reading the whole thing through Dennett's idea of real patterns and the three stances — physical, design, intentional — where a pattern is real if it supports reliable prediction, and we switch stances based on what's useful rather than what's metaphysically fundamental.

So here's the bit I'm particulary stuck on. The people inside the Matrix clearly generate real patterns in Dennett's sense — you can predict behaviour, emotions, relationships — yet they're running on code. Does that mean the intentional stance we take toward Neo or Trinity is actually just the design stance in disguise? Or is Dennett's point precisely that the distinction collapses when the predictions work? I'm also puzzled by the Agents: they talk, scheme, chase people through walls, but they're just programmes with no inner life. Is the film accidentally arguing that intentionality can exist without consciousness, or is it showing that we attribute minds whenever the pattern is robust enough?

Then there's the Oracle and the Architect. Morpheus treats Neo as a free agent with beliefs (pure intentional stance), while the Architect describes him as an anomalous variable in a deterministic equation (design/physical stance). Does the film dramatise a genuine tension between these stances, or does it cheat by letting Neo be the One outside the system, which feels like smuggling in a kind of dualism Dennett would reject? I'd like you to look at the Oracle giving Neo the cookie, and the Architect explaining the equation — not quoting dialogue, just describing what's happening — and say whether the film gets Dennett right or distorts him. And honestly, does The Matrix add anything to the philosophical debate about patterns and prediction that a straight argument misses? I'm writing this up for a course on philosophy of mind, so I'm not after a bibliography or citations, just a sharp, honest reading. Cheers.
