After a long and rather exhausting argument with a close friend over dinner about whether honoring sunk costs is always irrational, I want to see the disagreement rendered with real conceptual precision. Please write a formal, polished dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—call them A and B—who genuinely respond to each other’s points rather than talking past one another.

A should defend the standard economic-rationalist view: once a cost is irrecoverable, only future benefits and costs should determine action, so allowing sunk costs to influence a decision is necessarily a failure of rational agency. B should defend the opposing view that honoring sunk costs is not always fallacious, specifically arguing that past investments can function as diachronic anchors that preserve the coherence of long-term projects and credible commitment, and that a purely atomistic, moment-to-moment utility calculus misses the rational structure of sustained agency.

Ground the exchange in concrete cases: a musician with decades of training considering whether to abandon performance for another career; a government deciding whether to continue a structurally flawed infrastructure project; and a researcher persisting with a theoretically costly paradigm. In each case, the interlocutors should address whether B’s appeal to “commitment integrity” introduces an independent reason or merely disguises emotional consolation, and whether A’s forward-looking calculus implicitly presupposes an implausibly fragmented conception of the self.

Require direct engagement: if A claims that B’s position collapses into the fallacy it seeks to defend, B must show why commitment-keeping has rational weight distinct from loss-aversion without merely asserting it; if B argues that abandoning a project undermines future credibility, A must explain whether that credibility concern is genuinely forward-looking or still parasitic on the past investment. Neither speaker should be a strawman. Do not include bibliographies, citations, references to specific texts, or requests for further reading; the dialogue should stand entirely on its conceptual merits. Keep the tone rigorous, respectful, and sharply focused on the boundary between rational commitment and fallacious entrenchment.
