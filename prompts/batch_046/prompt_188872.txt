Design a 50-minute classroom simulation for a philosophy seminar of about 14 graduate students on pure time preference — the discounting of future welfare solely because it is future, independent of risk, uncertainty, or changing preferences. Use a token economy and paired roles: each participant is assigned a “present-self” and a “future-self” partner, but commitments are made through a sealed, delayed-communication mechanism (written messages revealed only after binding allocations are locked), so strategic reasoning about risk or persuasion is eliminated and any residual preference for immediate tokens is exposed as bare temporal bias. Specify the rules and token payoffs, explain how you will eliminate mortality risk, preference change, and informational asymmetry, and include a debrief that maps observed choices onto the philosophical distinction between rational and pure discounting. Say what will predictably go wrong on the first run and how to correct it, and be frank about which aspect of pure time preference — for example, its application to welfare across non-existent persons — the exercise necessarily cannot reproduce and must be supplied by discussion. Write in British English.
