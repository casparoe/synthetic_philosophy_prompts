hey, i'm a novelist and i've been obsessing over when a bunch of parts counts as one actual thing, probably because i'm writing a story where bodies keep splitting. here are my gut calls - tell me if there's a principle behind them, or if i'm just being inconsistent.

a pile of sand on the beach: just grains, not "a pile-object."
a living dog: definatly one thing, not a cell-collection.
a car disassembled and scattered in a junkyard: not a car anymore, just parts.
the same car parts arranged in car-shape in an art gallery: this one bugs me - is it now one thing? i can't decide.
a forest: i say just trees, even if ecologists call it a system.
two people married for decades, shared everything, finish each other's sentences: still two people, never "one couple-entity."
a coral reef made of thousands of polyps: but biologists call this one organism? that seems wrong to me, or maybe right - i flip-flop.
a book co-written by two strangers writing alternate chapters without communicating: one book or two?

my firm verdicts are on 1, 2, 3, 5, and 6. the rest i go back and forth. is there a principle - maybe about causal integration, or functional interdependence, or spatiotemporal continuity - that saves my firm ones and resolves 4, 7, and 8? and please tell me which of my firm verdicts you'd have to overturn to keep the principle simple, plus what it predicts for something i haven't mentioned: a human body in the first hour after death, before decay sets in.
