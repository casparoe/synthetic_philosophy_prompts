Draft the joint statement that two opposing camps—call them the Representationalist camp and the Syntactic-Autonomist camp—could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the question: Is DNA a literal semantic code, or only a causal-chemical template?

The Representationalist camp holds that genuine codes require a triadic relation involving an interpreter capable of misrepresentation; they grant that DNA exhibits syntactic differentiation and an arbitrary mapping between codons and amino acids, but deny that ribosomes or polymerases are interpreters in the philosophically relevant sense, and therefore maintain that calling DNA a "code" illicitly imports intentional or psychological categories into molecular biology.

The Syntactic-Autonomist camp holds that syntactic autonomy and arbitrary mapping are sufficient for literal codehood; they grant that DNA lacks mental interpretation, but deny that interpretation requires psychology. They argue that the ribosome functions as a mechanistic decoder, that frameshifts and nonsense mutations demonstrate rule-governed misreading rather than mere chemical failure, and therefore that the genetic system satisfies the conditions for being a non-metaphorical code.

Write the document in the following form. First, a numbered section of agreed claims—claims about the formal structure of the genetic system, about the distinction between syntactic differentiation and semantic interpretation, about the conditions under which a mapping counts as arbitrary rather than chemically determined, and about what both sides agree needs explaining. Second, a disagreements table that states the precise residual differences in wording each side accepts for its own position, identifies the cruxes (for instance, whether mechanistic decoding counts as interpretation, and whether arbitrary mapping requires a convention or only functional independence from chemistry), and for each crux records a concrete biological case or experimental result both sides agree bears on it, together with each side's explicit prediction about what that case shows; for each crux also note what kind of result or argument each side would treat as genuinely undermining its own view, and indicate whether there is anything both agree would actually move the debate.

Keep the tone neutral, formal, and precise; avoid attributing the views to specific named authors unless necessary; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; and ensure the reasoning depends only on conceptual distinctions and biological facts available without external search.
