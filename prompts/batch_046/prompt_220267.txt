In a seminar on scientific realism, a participant dismissed the question of whether an empirically adequate but ontologically inaccurate model can still be false in a correspondence sense by saying, “Truth is what works.” They treated the line as if pragmatism had already shown that representation of unobservable reality is a pseudo-problem. Disambiguate the slogan: lay out the distinct precise claims it could express, including at least (i) a purely instrumentalist thesis about predictive control and technological success; (ii) a James-style thesis about verification through experience and practical “cash value”; (iii) a Peircean thesis about the long-run convergence of inquiry toward a fated agreement; and (iv) a coherence or belief-network thesis about fitting smoothly with an existing system of commitments. For each reading, classify it as trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or conceptually unstable, and indicate which version people typically invoke when using the line to shut down a realism debate. Crucially, specify for each whether it actually dissolves the correspondence question or merely reframes or ignores it. Present your analysis as a table with columns: Reading, Precise claim, Verdict, Dissolves correspondence question? After the table, provide a brief paragraph assessing whether Peirce’s and James’s own positions survive this sorting, or whether the slogan collapses them into a single stance that neither philosopher actually held.
