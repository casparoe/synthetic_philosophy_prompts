I'm chairing the clinical ethics panel at a mid-size NHS trust, and every week we get referals that staff call "moral dilemmas" but half are just awful trade-offs or burnout in disguise. I need a reusable decision procedure — basically a flowchart in words — that my panel can run in about twenty minutes before we decide whether to open our formal "genuine dilemma" programme (which triggers a documented moral-residue debrief and sign-off from the clinical director) or redirect the case to ordinary conflict resolution.

The underlying idea should come from the philosophical question of whether genuine moral dilemmas are actually possible — not just hard choices where one duty clearly wins, but situations where an agent is necessarily blameworthy whatever they do. I want three ordered filters: first a coherence filter (are the obligations genuinely contradictory or just in tension?), then a residue filter (would appropriate, non-culpable regret remain even after the best available choice?), and finally a reversibility filter (was the conflict avoidable through prior institutional design?). For each filter I need the pass/fail conditions, the point where we halt with "not a dilemma", and the point where the question stops being factual and becomes a values judgement that I as chair have to own rather than delegate to the procedure. Then run it on three referals: (1) a consultant forced to choose between operating on Patient A (better prognosis) and Patient B (worse prognosis but she already promised B's family, with no time for both); (2) a nursing manager allocating the last ventilator in a surge between a long-term patient with poor prognosis and a new admission with good prognosis, where policy is silent; and (3) a junior doctor ordered to withdraw life support from a competent refusing patient whose family demands continuation, where the law is clear but the doctor's conscience rebels. I want at least one case — probably case 3 — where the proceedure classifies it as a genuine dilemma and I think that's wrong, because to me it's institutional failure, not metaphysical conflict. Please also say exactly what this proceedure can't do — like distinguishing moral residue from plain grief — and don't ask for a bibliography or reading list, since my panel doesn't have time for that. Oh, and ignore my spelling of "proceedural" — that's not a technical term, just my fingers being lazy.
