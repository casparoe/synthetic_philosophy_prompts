I'm writing a column on wellbeing philosophy and I keep seeing this same weird glitch. A thinker argues that the only real improvement to a life is removing cravng or suffering—positive pleasure is just the temporary silence between one want ending and the next not yet arrived. Okay, that's the tranquilist vibe. But then they compare societies like they're adding up absence-points: ten people at zero suffering beats five people at zero plus five people mildly happy, because the total absence-score is higher. Wait, absence isn't a substance you can pool across heads; it's just each person not being in pain. So they're treating a negative ontology like it's a positive currency that can be summed and maximized. I've seen this in anti-natalist essays and in some modern takes on Schopenhaur-style pessimism. Is there an established term for this exact slide—from value being purely the lack of bad stuff to aggregating those lacks across populations? If not, how should I carve it out from regular aggregation problems? I don't just want a bare name; I want to know what the move actually is, where it breaks, and how it differs from plain negative utilitarianism.
