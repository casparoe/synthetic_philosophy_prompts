For a graduate philosophy workshop (12 participants, 90 minutes), design a small-stakes role-play that makes participants feel the split between rational intention and rational execution at the heart of the classic toxin puzzle. Pair them as “binders” and “witnesses.” Binders privately commit to a future costly but harmless action—surrendering a coffee-token—conditional on a coin-flip revealed only after commitment; witnesses observe the commitment and award reputation points, but they can never observe whether the binder actually executes once the coin lands and execution is irrational. After revelation, binders may revoke at no penalty. Structure payoffs so the group discovers that commitment is profitable, execution is not, and witnesses reward commitment regardless of execution. Specify roles, a minute-by-minute timeline, scoring rules, and a debrief that maps each phase onto the puzzle’s logic: the reward for the mental state of intending, the absence of reason at the moment of action, and the observer’s blindness to execution. Include an honest note on what will predictably go wrong in running it and how to handle it, and be frank about which feature—the purely internal, unobservable nature of intending—this physical interaction cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply verbally. No lecture or naming of the puzzle before play begins; participants should discover the tension through the rules themselves.
