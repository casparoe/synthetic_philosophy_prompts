I am preparing a long-form feature on rational belief revision in the philosophy of science, and a philosopher of statistics I interviewed summarized Bas van Fraassen's Reflection principle as follows: "Reflection requires that if an agent knows her future credence in a proposition will be r after she acquires more evidence, her present credence should also be r. It functions as a diachronic coherence constraint, protecting the agent from being Dutch-booked by bets against her own later judgments. In its expert-function formulation, Reflection treats one's future epistemic state as an authoritative expert whose opinions must be pre-adopted, which makes it a special case of expert deference. Because standard Bayesian conditionalization already governs how beliefs should update on evidence, and because the Principal Principle governs alignment with known objective chances, Reflection is ultimately redundant: it is just the Principal Principle applied to the internal 'chances' of one's own belief formation. Consequently, anyone who updates correctly by Bayes' rule automatically satisfies Reflection, so the principle adds no independent normative constraint beyond standard Bayesianism."
