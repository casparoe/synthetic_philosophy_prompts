Write opening remarks (approximately 600 words, no more than 650) for a workshop titled “The Negative Space of the Record,” delivered to a mixed audience: professional archivists and historians familiar with provenance and appraisal theory, alongside local community members whose family histories have never entered an institutional collection and who do not use archival jargon. The remarks must treat the archive not as a neutral repository but as an active producer of silence. Introduce and structurally deploy a distinction that is not standard in archival literature: separate “erased presence” (traces that once existed and were deliberately destroyed, lost, or excluded from the finding aid) from “never-recorded presence” (lives and events that never generated the kind of document an archive is structured to recognize). Use this distinction to shape what can and cannot be said in the remarks: explain why the workshop’s work cannot be framed as “filling gaps” or “recovering lost voices,” since those metaphors presuppose an erased presence that can be restored, whereas never-recorded presence demands a different posture—provisional translation rather than retrieval. The philosophical idea should determine the ethical boundaries of representation: specify what it means to speak on behalf of the absent when some absence is irrecoverable by design. Do not cite specific theorists, provide a bibliography, or invoke texts the speaker could not be expected to know without searching. Maintain a tone rigorous enough that archivists are not patronized and accessible enough that community members are not lost to jargon. Conclude by stating one concrete ethical constraint, derived directly from the erased-presence / never-recorded-presence distinction, that should govern every session of the workshop.
