Consider a passage from G. E. Moore's Principia Ethica (1903), in the chapter on intrinsic value. Moore argues that personal affection and the appreciation of beautiful objects are among the greatest goods, but he denies that their value is additive. He compares them to parts of a painting: a world containing only friendship without other value, or only beauty without a conscious subject, would have far less total value than one might expect from summing the parts. He treats such combinations as organic unities.

Please help me understand this reasoning. First, how does Moore distinguish an organic unity from a mere instrumental or causal relation between goods? Second, apply his framework to a situation he does not consider: a genuine friendship embedded within a systematically unjust social structure, where the relationship depends upon and reinforces that injustice. Would the corruption of the larger whole diminish the intrinsic value of the friendship itself, or would the friendship retain its full value as a part? Explain without invoking modern secondary literature.
