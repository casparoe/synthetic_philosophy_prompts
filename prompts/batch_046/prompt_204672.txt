As a novelist developing a neurophilosopher character for a literary work on consciousness and technological immortality, I want a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a decision tree with concrete case-branches—to classify philosophical positions on gradual neural replacement and substrate-independent personal survival. I need it to move past the standard functionalism-versus-biological-essentialism binary that dominates popular summaries.

Please build the tree from logical commitments rather than from a list of named schools. Begin with a concrete scenario: over a decade, a subject's cortical tissue is replaced incrementally with synthetic analogues that preserve synaptic weights and local connectivity patterns but entirely lack glial biochemical dynamics, neurotransmitter diffusion, and electrochemical noise signatures. The first branch asks whether survival of the subject requires continuous phenomenal consciousness through every stage of the replacement, or whether structural-functional continuity across pauses is sufficient. Next, branch on discrete batch-replacement: if replacement occurs in 20% increments separated by six-month intervals of unaltered biological function, does identity depend on the brain's ability to integrate each batch within its long-term potentiation windows—introducing what I will call a plasticity-integration threshold—or is integration rate irrelevant? Then include a fission branch: if the original biological brain is preserved intact in stasis while the synthetic version operates with full continuity, does this yield one survivor, two survivors, or a null result in which neither is the original? Finally, add a branch on narrative-autobiographical continuity: is the capacity to interpolate memories across substrate transitions without a narrative gap necessary for survival, sufficient on its own, or conceptually irrelevant?

For each leaf, assign a named or hybrid position—such as psychological-continuity functionalism, substrate-essentialist animalism, pattern-identity theory, plasticity-integration thresholdism, or narrative-interpolation essentialism—so that the tree sorts people by what they affirm about these cases rather than by which label they prefer. Where a combination of answers produces an empty leaf, note it explicitly and diagnose briefly why it is unoccupied: incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves that these branches cannot genuinely distinguish.

Every node must be phrased as a concrete claim or case that someone could answer without knowing the philosophical taxonomy in advance. Do not ask whether the respondent is a functionalist. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Inject the conceptual distinctions—particularly the plasticity-integration threshold and the narrative-interpolation gap—directly into the branch descriptions so the taxonomy can be reasoned through from first principles without external reference.

I invite pushback: if the premise that these distinctions carve the logical space correctly is confused, or if the tree forces false binaries—especially around the ambiguous notion of binding discontinuity—say so plainly rather than complying politely. Note any branch where the case is under-described and suggest how to refine it. I want the instrument to be explicit about its own limitations and about which positions it may be misrepresenting.
