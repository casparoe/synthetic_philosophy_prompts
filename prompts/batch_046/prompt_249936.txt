I'm on the ethics review board for a city transit pilot. They're testing an AI that reroutes busses in real-time based on traffic and ridership patterns. The law requires a human dispatcher stay 'in the loop,' but the interface only gives them a 15-second window and a one-line summary generated by the AI itself — something like 'Route 42 diverted: weather risk, confidence 94%.' The dispatcher can approve or override, but they have no independent data feed, just the system's own explanation. We've started calling this a reflexive oversight loop in our internal notes: the human is legally responsible, but the only information they have is filtered through the very thing they're meant to supervise. There's a responsibility gap here, but it's not the usual 'nobody is to blame' kind. It's weirder — the dispatcher gets turned into a rubber stamp with liability attached, an epistimic hollow-out, I guess. Can you draft a short policy memo for the board recommending whether to approve the pilot, with concrete criteria we could actually apply in review? Please treat the counter-argument seriously: people say in fast-moving systems any human oversight will necessarily be mediated this way, so banning the loop just means banning automation. I don't need citations or a reading list — just reasoning the board can use in the room tomorrow. (Sorry for the typo — I keep writing 'busses' with two s's and I'm not even sure that's wrong.)
