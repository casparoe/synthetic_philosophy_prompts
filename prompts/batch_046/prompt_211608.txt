Gödel defines God as the being that has every positive property. His axioms say that if P is positive and entails Q then Q is positive (Ax1), that the conjunction of any two positives is positive (Ax2), that being positive is necessary (Ax3), that every positive property is instantiated in some possible world (Ax4), and that necessary existence counts as positive (Ax5). From Ax4 we get that the God-property is possible, since it's built from positives. Then by Ax5 God has necessary existence, and by Ax1 and Ax2 that property is positive too, so God actually exists in every world. This isn't the modal fallacy because Plantinga already showed that maximal greatness can be possible without being actual, but Gödel avoids that by making necessary existence part of the property bundle itself, not an external inference. The consistency problem—whether all positives can coexist—is solved by Ax2, which guarantees the conjunction of all positives is still positive, so there's no hidden contradiction.
