As a novelist drafting a speculative philosophical dialogue, I wish to place Albert Einstein—whose well-known determinism, Spinozist conception of deity, and reservations about the completeness of quantum mechanics require no external citation—before two developments that post-date his death in 1955. The first is the modern reliance upon large-scale predictive behavioural algorithms that claim to anticipate individual human decisions with greater precision than introspective self-awareness permits. The second is a niche theological-physical thesis I shall call quantum compatibilism: the claim that quantum indeterminacy operating at the neuronal level supplies the exact physical opening through which a divinely oriented but genuinely free will might operate.

Please respond in British English, and please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Predict how Einstein would assess each development. Specifically, address whether he would regard algorithmic prediction as confirming Laplacian determinism or as committing a category error that confuses statistical correlation with metaphysical causation; whether he would embrace quantum compatibilism as a valid reconciliation of natural science with religious ethics, or dismiss it as an unjustified fusion of probabilistic physics with moral agency; and explain how his view of free will as an epistemic constraint rather than as an ontological reality would shape his reaction to both propositions. Treat these concepts as entirely new to Einstein, and avoid anachronistically importing later debates into his outlook.
