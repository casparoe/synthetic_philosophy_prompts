I'm on this municipal board that handles public monuments and street naming -- we call it the Civic Memory Commission, which sounds pretentious but whatever. There's this statue of Elias Vance in the old town square. Vance basically built the local steel industry and donated the land for the public library, but he also bankrolled the state eugenics board in the 1930s and wrote some awful stuff about genetic hygiene. The city council asked us for a recommendation by next month: should we take it down, leave it with a plaque, or do something weirder?

There's this idea I've been chewing on from some philosophy of history stuff -- not the standard great-man vs villain framing, but this distinction between commemorative justice and what some people call mnemonic sovereignty. The basic idea is that a monument doesn't just honor someone, it actively shapes whose suffering gets remembered in public space. So removing Vance isn't just erasing history, it's reclaiming space for victims whose stories were literally buried under his legacy. But there's also this counter-argument that tearing things down can create a false sense of moral cleanliness -- like, we think we're done with eugenics because we pulled down one statue, when the actual structures (poverty, medical bias) are still there. Anyway, my co-commissioner wants to recommend leaving the statue but adding a big contextual plaque, basically the more-speech approach. Another member wants it melted down and replaced with a garden commemorating the sterilization victims. I think there's a third option that gets missed: physically altering the monument in situ so it can't be read as straightforward celebration, but also can't be forgotten. Like sinking it into the ground up to its shoulders or surrounding it with a wall of names. But I'm not totally sure if that's just aesthetic posturing.

Can you draft the memo I'd circulate before our vote? It needs to recommend one concrete course of action with a timeline (say, 18 months), engage the strongest version of the leave-it-with-a-plaque position seriously, and address whether altering the statue in place is actually ethically different from removal or just a cop-out. Don't just list options -- pick one and defend it. Also please don't cite specific philosophers or demand a bibliography; I want reasoning the other board members can actually follow without a literature review. I also need to recieve it by Thursday if possible, so a clear bottom line helps.
