Two friends of mine got into it over whether chaos theory actually saves determinism or quietly kills it. (1) Marcus said that if weather is deterministic but chaotic, determinism is technically true but useless: the predictive gap between what we can compute and whats actually fixed grows exponentially, so for any agent inside the system the future is as open as if quantum randomness ruled. (2) Elena said he's mixing epistemology with metaphysics -- determinism just means every state has exactly one successor, chaos doesnt change that, and calling it useless is like calling gravity useless becuase we cant integrate every particle. (3) Marcus replied that if a thesis makes no difference to explanation, prediction or moral responsibility then its not metaphysically substantial, just a labeling convention; determinism was sold on the idea that the present fixes the future in a meaningful way, not in some hidden realm we can never access. (4) Elena said we dont reject atoms just becuase we cant see them, determinism can be true and hidden, and chaos actually shows how rich deterministic dynamics are rather than emptying determinism of content. They asked me to referee and I couldnt decide. Can you go through each move, rate them from clearly off to clearly right, and say who is more right overall? Also, is there something both miss about how embedded observers actually relate to deterministic chaos?
