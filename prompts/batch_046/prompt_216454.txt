I'm preparing a feature on how philosophers think about belief when you don't know what time it is, and I need a short joke for the sidebar—something that works for readers who've never heard of the Sleeping Beauty problem but rewards those who actually understand the half-versus-thirder debate. Rather than repeating the classic coin-flip scenario, please frame the logic inside an ordinary situation: a customer subscribes to a meal-kit service that flips a fair coin—heads sends one box on Monday only, tails sends identical boxes on both Monday and Tuesday, with no date stamped on the packaging. After she opens a box, she has to decide whether her confidence that it's Monday should stay the same or drop.

The punchline should turn precisely on the disagreement between the view that her prior over the coin toss is untouched, because she hasn't observed the outcome of the toss, and the opposing view that her experience of holding the box is duplicated across two days, so finding herself in that experience is itself evidence for tails. Keep it conversational and brief, avoid any citations or references to specific papers or philosophers by name, and make sure the structure is clear enough that the joke lands for someone who grasps the problem of self-locating belief but doesn't need to have memorized the technical literature.
