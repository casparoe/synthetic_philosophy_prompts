Design a classroom activity or game for a seminar of about 12 grad students in law or phil, 90 min, that makes them feel the live pull of victors justice and retroactive law at Nuremberg and Tokyo — not just as history but as a coordination failure they have to solve themselves. Split them into four roles (prosecutor, defense, neutral judge, victim-state rep) with hidden payoff cards: the victim-state rep scores for convictions but loses points if the charter they write gets flagged as victor-biased by an outside observer. In 20 minutes they draft a one-page charter for a fictional post-war tribunal; then apply it to three cases: (1) an act legal under the old regimes written code but violating an uncodified customary norm, (2) a structurally identical act committed by someone on the winning side, (3) an act only criminal because the tribunal invented the law ex post facto. They vote and defend. The debrief should map each crisis onto real debates — nullum crimen, the Tokyo dissent, the asymmetry of judgment — and be frank about what the game cant reproduce (real power imbalance, the emotional weight of atrocity). Also ask them to flag which parts of the historical comparison are settled and which are still genuinely contested (like whether Tokyo was more retroactive than Nuremberg). Keep instructions runnable with no outside research. At the end, ask the response to seperate what is settled from whats genuinely contested and not present a contested view as the consensus.
