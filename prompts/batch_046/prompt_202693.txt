I'm working through the Rietdijk-Putnam argument about simultaneity and the block universe in my philosophy of physics course, and I'm trying to determine exactly when a thought experiment in this domain yields genuine ontological knowledge rather than merely illustrating a formal feature of the theory. Here are my verdicts on several cases.

First, Rietdijk's scenario of an observer on a moving train assigning different 'now' slices to distant stars: I think this does yield knowledge that there is no frame-independent global present, because the disagreement is physically grounded in the Lorentz transformation, not in psychological perspective.

Second, Putnam's extension involving the Andromeda galaxy and an observer walking toward it: I think this also yields knowledge, specifically that events we ordinarily call 'future' are equally real with present ones, since a change in velocity alone shifts the plane of simultaneity across cosmological distances.

Third, a hypothetical case in which two observers share identical worldlines but have desynchronized neural 'present-moments' due to internal brain-state variation: I judge that this yields no ontological knowledge whatsoever, since the divergence is purely internal and not tied to any physical coordinate transformation.

Fourth, Einstein's original train-and-lightning thought experiment used to derive the relativity of simultaneity: I think this yields knowledge about the structure of spacetime, but I do not think it by itself licenses any conclusion about the reality of past or future events.

Fifth, a constructed scenario: suppose a future quantum-gravity theory reveals a hidden preferred foliation at the Planck scale that is in principle empirically inaccessible. If a thought experiment invokes this hidden structure to argue that the block universe is false, I feel it should not count as yielding knowledge, but I'm not fully sure whether my resistance comes from empiricist commitments or from a feature specific to this argument.

Sixth, the twin-paradox reasoning sometimes used to claim that because aging is frame-relative, temporal passage itself is illusory: I think this confuses a dynamical effect with an ontological conclusion, yet I keep returning to whether there is a consistent reading on which the paradox genuinely undermines presentism.

Finally, there is a case I cannot settle. Imagine that physicists construct a superluminal signaling mechanism—violating special relativity but not producing logical contradictions in causal structure—and use it to establish a universal, coordinated 'now' across frames. If no paradox results, would this undermine the block-universe inference drawn from standard relativistic simultaneity?

Is there a coherent principle—perhaps concerning whether the thought experiment isolates an empirically accessible invariant, or whether it moves illegitimately from a feature of representation to a feature of reality—that captures all or most of these verdicts? And specifically, what does that principle say about my unsettled judgments in cases five and seven?
