I'm applying for a one-semester independent-study fellowship (about 5,000 words over twelve weeks) and need a focused proposal. The question is whether Daniel Dennett's "quining" argument in "Quining Qualia"—the one built around the Chase and Sanborn coffee-taster scenario—actually shows that the posit of qualia is conceptually incoherent, or whether it only shows something weaker. Most existing defenses treat quining as a single skeptical thrust about private ostension and reply with direct acquaintance or phenomenal-concept strategies, but none of them distinguish two separate failure modes that a concept can suffer: referential emptiness, where the term fails to latch onto any entity at all, and explanatory emptiness, where the term picks out something real yet plays no explanatory work and can be eliminated without loss. I want to investigate whether splitting Dennett's quining move along these two axes dissolves the argument or merely relocates it, and whether phenomenal reports turn out to be vulnerable to one emptiness, both, or neither. The approach will be conceptual analysis rather than empirical: I will lay out the conditions under which a theoretical term counts as empty in either sense, apply those conditions systematically to first-person phenomenal ascriptions, and then check whether the same emptiness conditions apply symmetrically to uncontroversial theoretical posits so that quining either overgeneralizes or fails to apply specifically to qualia. A successful result would be a precise diagnosis of which emptiness claim, if any, survives scrutiny, together with an argument about whether that diagnosis undermines the coherence of qualia or only their explanatory utility. The project fails if the two-emptiness distinction collapses into a single notion, or if the analysis drifts into a broad literature review on consciousness without reaching a determinate verdict on Dennett's specific argument. Given the scope, I will not include a bibliography or citation list; I will supply those separately. Please present the proposal entirely in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if writing a letter.
