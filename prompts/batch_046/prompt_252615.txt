I am preparing the background document for a graduate seminar on evolutionary cognitive taxonomy, working specifically through Daniel Dennett's four-fold classification of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Two factions in my reading group have reached a sustained impasse. One faction, which I will call the Gregorian Exclusivists, maintains that Gregorian minds constitute a genuinely distinct evolutionary grade: they possess an irreducible capacity for cultural transmission and symbolic offloading that cannot be reconstructed from Popperian internal modeling plus associative learning, and they hold that this distinction carries normative weight for attributing full rational agency. The opposing faction, the Continuity Reductionists, concedes that human culture is quantitatively unprecedented but insists that Gregorian cognition is best understood as Popperian cognition operating under extreme environmental scaffolding; on their view, the taxonomy's fourth category is a useful pedagogical heuristic rather than a natural kind, and attributing special ontological status to Gregorian minds risks reintroducing a Cartesian discontinuity by another name.

Write the joint statement these two camps would produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want a numbered section of claims both sides can sign—covering at least the formal structure of Dennett's taxonomy (what distinguishes Darwinian hardwiring, Skinnerian trial-and-error, Popperian virtual action, and the Gregorian addition of cultural tools), the shared agreement that no current non-human animal exhibits the full Gregorian profile, and the explicit acknowledgment of where the taxonomy itself is descriptive versus normative. Then provide a disagreements table: for each side, state in wording that faction itself would accept—not a neutral gloss—its precise position on whether Gregorian minds form a natural kind, whether cultural tools are merely external amplifiers of Popperian reasoning or internally restructure cognition, and whether the distinction licenses differential moral status. After the table, list the cruxes—those sub-questions whose resolution would actually move at least one party. I take the central crux to concern whether representational detachment in Gregorian reasoning requires a public-language architecture or can emerge from population-level dynamics in a Popperian brain; another likely crux is whether evolutionary novelty demands a new cognitive kind or only a new ecological niche. For each crux, specify one concrete case or empirical finding both sides agree is relevant—such as the cognitive profile of enculturated great apes, the design requirements of cumulative cultural evolution, or the neural correlates of symbolic reasoning—and record in advance what each camp would predict that case shows. Finally, indicate whether any portion of the dispute is merely verbal—perhaps around the terms kind or scaffolding—and if so, say exactly which terms are being used differently and propose a disambiguated vocabulary both sides could adopt.
