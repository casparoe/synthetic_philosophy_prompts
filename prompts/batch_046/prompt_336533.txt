Construct a thought experiment that isolates whether the aggregation of tiny harms turns on uniform micro-damage or on continuous severity gradients, within a realistic workplace or public-health setting rather than a science-fictional one. Engineer the scenario in one paragraph so that one clearly perceptible severe harm is weighed against a distributed population of sub-threshold harms that vary continuously in intensity; the collective burden must depend on non-linear accumulation across the gradient rather than on simple counting, and no single individual must experience the full aggregate. Hold fixed the total subjective burden and the absence of any unified victim. Then demonstrate that the case meets its design specifications: state the verdict, with reasoning, for (i) a standard aggregationist who sums harms regardless of distribution; (ii) an anti-aggregationist who rejects counting imperceptible harms altogether; and (iii) a gradient-sensitive view—treat this as the injected position—that permits aggregation only when micro-harms form a continuous slope crossing a perceptual or institutional recognition threshold. Identify precisely which feature of the case forces the divergence among these three, explain what collapses if the gradient structure is replaced by uniform dust-speck-level damage, and note how a defender of the view that comes out worst would attack the case. Write in British English. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
