Here is my reconstruction of her summary: Ubuntu holds that the self is not an underlying substance that carries social relations as properties, but is literally constituted by those relations, as expressed in the familiar gloss that a person is a person through other persons. Consequently, personhood is not an innate status possessed at birth but an achieved condition maintained through active reciprocity, communal recognition, and participation in shared moral life. From this it follows that someone who has lost the cognitive and emotional capacities for reciprocity—such as a patient with advanced dementia—retains only a diminished or derivative form of personhood, because the relational fabric that constituted her self has unraveled. The colleague added that this ontological claim explains why Ubuntu ethics rejects Western liberal rights talk altogether: moral obligations are not owed to pre-social individuals but flow through living networks, and historical practices of excluding those accused of witchcraft or severe antisocial conduct merely made explicit what the ontology already implied, namely that severing communal bonds dissolves one’s standing as a full person.
