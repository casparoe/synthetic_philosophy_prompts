I've been trying to map out where I actually stand on Berkeley for a seminer paper and I'm worried I'm smuggling in contraditions without noticing. Here's the package: (1) I buy esse est percipi for physical objects — tables and mountains are genuinely mind-dependent, not just epistemically. (2) I think Berkeley's master argument is a bad move: it slides from the fact that I can't conceive an unperceived tree without conceiving it to the claim that therefore it can't exist unperceived, and that slide is an epistemic-to-metaphysical leap that doesn't work. (3) Even so, I think direct realism is dead, mainly because Berkeley's attack on primary qualities and the argument from perceptual variation still go through. (4) I reject the divine perceiver fix; instead I think unperceived objects hang around as what I'd call latent perceptual structures — not actually perceived by anyone, but structured like possible perceptions, kind of virtual states that get actualized when observed. (5) I usually treat conceivability as a decent guide to metaphysical possibility, but I want to say the master argument is the one exception where conceivability fails because of that specific slide. (6) I'm also anti-Cartesian about minds: I think consciousness is irreducible but not a substance, more like an organizational feature of matter.

Do these actually hang together? It feels like (1) needs the master argument or something like it, but I've rejected (2). And (4) sounds like I'm sneaking realism back in with these latent structures. Plus there's maybe a circularity worry: if minds depend on physical brains, but brains are mind-dependent physical objects... or is that only apparent? Which pairs are in genuine fatal tension, which just look weird? Rank them by severity and tell me the least costly revision — should I drop immaterialism, accept God, or stop treating conceivability as a guide? Also, if I keep (4), what should I say about virtual objects rendered in a simulation that no one is currently viewing — are they latent structures or just not there?
