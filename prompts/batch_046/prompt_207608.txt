For my philosophy of logic seminar I have been working through Carroll's dialogue in which the Tortoise repeatedly demands that Achilles justify each application of modus ponens by adding another hypothetical premise, generating an infinite regress. I have taken this to show that deductive justification is necessarily incomplete within any finite formal system, since every rule requires a further meta-rule to license its application. From there I have been developing an argument that Carroll anticipates both Wittgenstein's rule-following considerations and Tarski's hierarchy of languages: just as Tarski resolves semantic paradox by stratifying object-language and meta-language, Carroll's regress might seem to be dissolved by distinguishing an inference made in the object-language from the meta-language authorization of the rule applied. But this stratification appears only to defer justification upward without ever anchoring it, which suggests that logical inference ultimately lacks a non-circular rational foundation unless we bottom out in something non-cognitive—perhaps a brute psychological disposition or social convention. My instructor, however, suggests that I am misplacing the target: Carroll is not attacking the possibility of deductive justification at all, but rather exposing a category mistake between accepting a logical premise and performing an inference. If that diagnosis is correct, then my further claim—that Carroll forces us to abandon normative accounts of deduction for conventionalist or psychological ones—seems to rest on a confusion. So I want to ask: is the false presupposition in my framing precisely the assumption that Carroll's infinite regress applies to the justification of deduction rather than to a specific misunderstanding of how rules govern particular inferences? And if the Tarskian move is indeed a red herring, does that leave Ryle's distinction between knowing that and knowing how as the only viable resolution—that applying a rule is a practical capacity requiring no further propositional justification?
