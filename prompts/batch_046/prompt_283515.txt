I have no academic background in philosophy, but since becoming the primary carer for an elderly relative I have been reading about care ethics on my own. In an online discussion about whether disability benefits should be conditional on work-capacity assessments, someone wrote: 'Dependency is not an exception; it is the human condition.' They used it to argue that treating independence as the default and care as a deviation makes the assessment framework itself unjust. I want to understand precisely what that sentence could be claiming. Could you lay out the distinct theses it might express — for example, that all humans are permanently and irreducibly interdependent, that personal autonomy is largely a fiction, that universal dependency creates specific distributive obligations, or that social institutions ought to be reorganised around care rather than production — and for each indicate whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or false? Then say which of these readings, if any, actually supports rejecting work-capacity assessments, and which are merely rhetorical dressing. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating the question. Write in British English.
