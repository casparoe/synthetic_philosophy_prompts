I've been tryin to figure out where I actually stand on the therapty/enhancement distinction, mostly by going with my gut on concrete cases instead of reading philosophy papers cover to cover. Giving growth hormone to a kid who's just short but not medically sick: that feels like pure enhancement and I don't think doctors should be in that business. Giving modafinil to a medical resident pulling 80-hour shifts: weirdly that feels okay, almost like treating an occupational injury, even though the resident is technically healthy. Editing embryos to avoid Huntington's: clearly therapy, no moral worry. Editing embryos to boost height or cognitive capacity: enhancement, and I'm against it. Giving antidepressants to someone in deep grief after losing a spouse: that's therapy, we should allow and support it. But giving those same pills to a healthy person who just wants to feel more upbeat at work or parties: that's enhancement, shouldn't be encouraged or covered.

Then there's the cochlear implant for deaf infants, which I keep flip-flopping on — sometimes it feels like correcting a deficit, other times like cultural erasure that pushes closer to enhancement. Using cognitive training apps for someone with early dementia: therapy. Using them for a chess grandmaster who just wants a competitive edge: enhancement, not okay. There's also this niche case I came across about parents seeking epigenetic therapy to reverse trauma-induced methylation patterns in an adopted child who has anxiety but no formal diagnosis — I feel like that's still therapy, but I can't quite say why it hits different from just giving the kid standard anxiety meds. Is there a principle, or maybe a small cluster of principles, that actually captures all these verdicts without me having to just shrug and say "it depends"? Or am I being inconsistent somewhere? And specifically, what should I say about that cochlear implant case and the weird epigenetic adoption one — do they break whatever principle you come up with?
