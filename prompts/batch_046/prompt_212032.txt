Here is the conceptual framing: critics charge that string theory’s landscape of roughly 10^500 vacua makes it predictively empty, accommodating virtually any observed cosmological constant or low-energy parameter. Defenders reply that scientific theories can gain confirmation through non-empirical arguments—mathematical consistency, explanatory coherence, limiting-case checks, absence of alternatives—even without unique observational predictions. But there is a further distinction: being predictively empty (permitting all outcomes in a range) is not the same as being explanatorily empty (offering no internal reason for why one outcome in that range is realized). Some philosophers suggest eternal inflation suffers mainly from predictive emptiness via the measure problem, whereas string theory suffers mainly from explanatory emptiness because it currently lacks a derived dynamical selection principle. The task is to adjudicate whether non-empirical confirmation can compensate for this specific defect.

The exam question should ask students to:
(i) Clarify predictive uniqueness versus explanatory uniqueness, and explain why losing the first might not destroy scientific status if the second is retained.
(ii) Apply this to string theory’s landscape: does the multiverse provide an explanatory mechanism for observed constants, or merely accommodate them? Be explicit about anthropic reasoning and the absence of a selection dynamics or measure.
(iii) Assess whether non-empirical confirmation can compensate for lost predictive uniqueness here. Seriously consider the circularity objection: using internal theoretical features to justify a multiverse structure that simultaneously undermines external testability.
(iv) Reach a reasoned, conditional judgment—does the multiverse shift string theory outside science, or legitimately relocate evaluation criteria? The conclusion must depend on the prior analysis, not on a blanket endorsement or rejection of falsificationism.

The model answer should be structured, conceptually precise, distinguish accommodation from genuine explanation, treat the circularity worry without dismissing it in one sentence, and tie the final judgment to whether progress on a selection or measure problem is achievable.
