Here's roughly where I've landed on Confucian role ethics, and I'd like an audit of whether the package hangs together or whether I'm quietly helping myself to an incompatible commitment.

(1) I read the tradition as relational particularism: what I owe you depends on whether we stand in the relations of parent-child, ruler-subject, or friend-friend, and yi—rightness—is irreducibly contextual rather than derivable from any universal principle.

(2) Even so, I hold that there is a role-independent floor: no one, regardless of relationship, should be subjected to degradation. I treat this not as a Confucian virtue but as a boundary condition on every role.

(3) I take zhengming seriously in a performative key: roles are not fixed by biology or social title but are sustained by correct enactment; if one consistently fails to perform fatherhood, one is not, in the morally relevant sense, a father, and a ruler who abandons the people forfeits the authority of that role.

(4) My meta-method is reflective equilibrium, but applied locally: I adjust my judgments about specific obligations within particular relationships without requiring them to cohere into a system that covers strangers, non-roles, or universal moral law.

The tensions I can see are between (1) and (2)—does the floor smuggle in the universalism I reject?—and between (3) and (1)—if roles dissolve under poor performance, do they still ground obligations or do they just evaporate? But I'm unsure whether these are genuine conflicts or only apparent ones, and I suspect there's a deeper friction between (4) and my refusal of systematization that I haven't located. Please order the tensions by severity, state for each what would have to give if consistency demands it, and flag any conflicts that dissolve on closer inspection. In doing so, separate what is broadly settled in the literature—e.g., that role ethics is not a form of universalist deontology—from what remains genuinely contested, such as whether zhengming should be interpreted performatively, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus view.
