ok so i've been reading up on virtue ethics vs teh situationist critique and i'm trying to figure out what i actually think about moral character, going by my gut on cases. here's the pattern i see:

- a guy finds a wallet with $500 on an empty street and mails it back. that feels like real honesty, not just luck.
- same guy later fudges his tax return a little, figures everyone does it and he wont get caught. i don't think this wipes out the wallet thing; they feel like different gears, not one hypocritical person.
- a nurse who always stays late to comfort dying patients but is cold and short with her family at home. i keep going back and forth: is her compassion real but compartmentalized, or is the hospital just a script she follows?
- someone refuses to lie to a friend about their terrible business plan, but then writes that the check is in the mail when it definately isnt. both are lies, but the first feels like integrity and the second like laziness. this is the case i cant settle.
- a volunteer who works every weekend at a shelter but is notoriously rude to cashiers and waitstaff. is generosity a real trait if kindness isnt?
- a whistleblower who risks her career to report fraud, but also drives aggressively and yells at strangers in parking lots. bravery in big things, pettiness in small ones.

i have this half-baked idea i call character porosity -- traits are porous, they flow easily in structured environments but thicken and block up in ambiguous social spaces. does that actually capture the pattern, or is it just situationism dressed up? and what does it say about my back-and-forth case, the check-in-the-mail one?

is there a coherent principle behind all these verdicts, and if so which one do i have to drop? also, what would your principle predict for a case i haven't listed -- say, a person who is consistently honest in every work setting but lies constantly to their spouse?
