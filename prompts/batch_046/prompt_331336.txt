Having spent decades inferring unobservable structures from indirect signatures in astrophysical data, I now find myself studying how institutional power shapes the historical record. The silences in archives seem analogous to missing mass in cosmology—detectable only through the distortion they leave in the surviving sources—so I wonder whether inverse-problem methods from observational science could reconstruct those erased voices with statistical rigor, treating power as a selection function. Or does this analogy fail because archival absences are not hidden variables awaiting quantitative recovery but constitutive voids produced by the very mechanisms that determine what gets recorded?
