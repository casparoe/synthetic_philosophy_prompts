After a long and somewhat frustrating argument with a friend about whether the cosmological argument requires a temporal beginning, I want you to argue for the position that the contingency version and the kalam version are not complementary allies but are actually in serious tension with one another. Specifically, defend the claim that if every contingent being requires an explanation in something necessary, then applying this principle to the universe as a single contingent being yields a necessary ground whether the universe is eternal or finite. From this, argue that the kalam argument's reliance on the impossibility of traversing an actual infinite and on a definite temporal origin becomes redundant at best and a strategic liability at worst—particularly since empirical cosmology might eventually support an eternal or cyclical model that would undermine kalam without affecting the contingency reasoning. Conclude that a consistent defender of the cosmological argument should therefore drop the kalam strategy and rely exclusively on contingency, rather than presenting the two as mutually reinforcing paths to the same conclusion.
