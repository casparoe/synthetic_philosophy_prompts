As a journalist covering science and ideas, I am commissioning an essay on hard choices between incommensurable values—specifically, whether recognizing what I will call value sedimentation changes how we ought to think about them. By value sedimentation I mean the phenomenon in which the option not taken does not vanish but leaves a durable, structurally transformative residue in the chooser’s future normative outlook, altering what subsequently counts as a reason. The familiar debate pits rational-resolution theorists, who hold that hard choices can be settled by generating new comparative reasons, against existential-commitment theorists, who hold that one must simply choose without prior rational justification. Write an essay weighing whether taking sedimentation seriously undermines rational resolution, strengthens existential commitment, or demands a distinct third position; reach and justify a specific conclusion. Please also state explicitly what argument or consideration would change your mind, and flag the point in your reasoning most vulnerable to failure—especially if sedimentation turns out to be ordinary regret reconceived.
