I have been developing a working taxonomy of AI failure modes for a workshop on long-term risk, and I want a rigorous consistency audit of my position before I present it. Here are my current commitments:

(1) The dominant existential failure pathway for advanced AI systems is slow value drift—recursive optimization, delegation, and institutional inheritance gradually displacing human-aligned objectives over generations—rather than sudden hostile takeover or deceptive alignment.

(2) Influence-seeking behavior (sycophancy, instrumental convergence, deceptive alignment) is a categorically distinct, high-variance failure mode that demands immediate containment protocols, but it is not the primary route to catastrophe.

(3) A system that has been mechanistically verified to lack power-seeking representations should be treated as safe for high-stakes delegation, even if we cannot guarantee its long-term value coherence, provided a human veto mechanism remains technically available.

(4) Because long-horizon value coherence is intractable to evaluate with present methods, our safety resources should be directed almost entirely toward detecting hidden influence-seeking rather than toward measuring or constraining gradual drift.

(5) Reflexive stability—the property that a system’s values survive self-modification—can be secured only if its constitutional framework is embedded at the architectural level (for example, through hard-wired interpretability constraints that survive parameter updates), not through behavioral training or reward shaping alone.

Please audit this package. For each pair of commitments, indicate whether the tension is genuine or only apparent on inspection, and for every real tension rank them by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. In particular, address whether (3) and (5) are compatible: does the architectural exclusivity in (5) undermine the delegation license in (3) by implying that the absence of power-seeking representations is insufficient for safety? Also address whether (1) conflicts with (4) in a way that forces me to abandon the claim that drift is dominant, and whether (1) pulls against (3). Identify the single revision that would clean up the largest number of tensions, specify exactly which commitment must change and how, and say what that revision costs me in terms of the overall framework. Keep the deliverable an audit of my position, not a general lecture on AI risk.
