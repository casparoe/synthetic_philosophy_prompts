Design a 90-minute seminar activity for 12–16 postgraduate students in philosophy of action or decision theory that makes the absent-minded driver problem feel like a lived coordination failure rather than a textbook paradox. Before the problem is named, participants should discover—through structured play with small but tangible stakes—that the optimal strategy requires them to avoid evaluating their own reasoning, because that evaluation is itself part of the causal chain that produces failure. The design must inject an indexical self-location twist: each Driver holds a hidden cognitive type (Reflective or Automatic) unknown to them but drawn from a known base rate, so they must form a first-person credence about their own disposition that is unstable once acted upon.

The deliverable must contain: (a) explicit rules, roles (Driver, Navigator, Auditor), and payoffs for groups of three or four; (b) a game board or decision tree in which a Driver chooses whether to take an Exit (Rest Stop, payoff 60) or continue to Home (payoff 100), with Disaster (payoff 0) occurring only if a Reflective Driver misses the Exit by actively deliberating about whether to take it, whereas an Automatic Driver takes it without thought; (c) repeated rounds (at least four) with points convertible to a real group reward, such as selecting the seminar’s closing discussion topic; (d) a timeline allocating time to rule explanation, play, silent observation, and debrief; and (e) a debrief that maps gameplay moments onto the conceptual structure—the evidential value of planning versus its causal cost, the feedback loop between self-locating belief and behaviour, and the distinction between adopting a policy and monitoring its adoption.

In the debrief, clearly separate what is settled—that self-referential planning generates a feedback loop between belief formation and behavioural outcome—from what is genuinely contested among specialists: whether evidential decision theory gives the correct resolution, whether there is a coherent account of ‘choosing not to think’, and whether the case reveals a limit on rational agency or merely a constraint on instrumental rationality. Do not present any single resolution as the consensus view.

Begin with the activity itself—rules, roles, payoffs, round structure, timeline, and debrief—followed immediately by the honest limitation note, with no preface, no commentary, and no restating of the question. After the design, identify precisely which feature of the problem—the infinite regress of monitoring one’s own monitoring—the physical setup cannot reproduce and must therefore be supplied conceptually by the facilitator. Use British spelling and punctuation throughout.
