I've been going back and forth on my intuitons about doing vs allowing in clinical contexts where machines are actively sustaining life. Here are my gut verdicts:

- A doctor pulls the plug on a brain-dead patient with family consent: letting die, not killing, even though she physically hits the switch.
- The same doctor injects potassium chloride at the patient's request: killing.
- A hospital board actively votes to deny funding for a life-extending drug; ten patients who needed it die: allowing, not killing. They made a decision, but they just withheld a resource.
- A nurse notices an IV drip has fallen out and deliberately doesn't reconnect it, knowing the patient will crash within the hour: this feels closer to killing than the board case, but I'm genuinely unsure.
- An engineer forgets to click 'renew' on an automated dialysis protocol she designed; the machine shuts off and the patient dies: this feels like allowing, but I'm not sure why it differs from the nurse case.

Is there a principle that captures all five? I'm tentatively playing with the idea that it depends on whether you're disrupting an artificial baseline you're actively maintaining, but case 3 messes that up. Also, what should I say about disabling a pacemaker at the patient's request—more like pulling the plug or more like the injection? And if I want the simplest principle, which of these verdicts do I have to throw out?
