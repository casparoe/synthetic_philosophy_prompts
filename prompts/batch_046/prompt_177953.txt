Write a joke that plays on Nietzsche's distinction between monumental, antiquarian, and critical history—specifically on the functional roles each serves for life and the dangers of each when overextended. Frame the setup around a longtime reader of rationalist forums attempting to curate their own intellectual tradition: they want to treat certain canonical essays as monumental exemplars worthy of heroic emulation, preserve every old thread as an antiquarian relic that must never be revised, and simultaneously apply critical history to dissolve the authority of anyone who wrote them. The punchline should turn on the structural contradiction this creates within Nietzsche's own framework—showing why these three modes cannot be maximized at once for the same object—rather than relying on a generic "rationalists are over-analytical" gag.
