I sit on the clinical research ethics board at St Mary's, and we've got this proposal for a Phase I trial of a tactile-to-cortical interface in adults with congenital blindness. The setup is they stimulate the lateral geniculate nucleus via a haptic feedback loop and ask participants to identify geometric shapes on first activation — the PI claims that if recognition happens straight away, with out prior tactile learning, that settles Molyneux's problem in favour of innate cross-modal concepts rather than associative learning. Half the board thinks this is clever neuroscience; the other half says we're smuggling a philosophical conclusion about phenomenology into a clinical protocol, and that informed consent is impossible when we can't even agree whether the participants will experience something vision-like or just invent a new modality altogether. There's also disagreement about the endpoint — should we measure navigation accuracy, subjective reports of "seeing", or neural correlates in the ventral stream? I need a short memo for Thursday's meeting recommending whether we approve, reject, or approve with conditions. It should treat the PI's argument seriously, explain where the philosophical pressure actually sits, and include an honest account of the strongest reason to reject whatever recommendation you land on. Please write in British English, and keep it practical — the board has to vote on something concrete.
