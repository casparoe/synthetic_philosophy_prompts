A philosophy of language tutor has delivered the following verdicts on speaker-meaning cases over the term. (i) When a speaker says "Some of the essays passed" knowing that every essay passed, the speaker means that not all passed; this quantitative implicature is generalised, calculable from the maxim of Quantity, and cancellable without contradiction by an explicit repair. (ii) When "X entered a house and found a woman writing" is said of X's own home, the speaker means that the house is not X's and the woman is not X's wife; this particularised implicature depends on contextual assumptions about relevance and is cancellable only with an explicit repair phrase. (iii) When "You are a real genius" is said immediately after an obvious blunder, the speaker means the opposite of the literal evaluation; the maxim of Quality is flouted, and the irony is recovered because the speaker manifestly lacks the belief that literal truth would require. (iv) In the utterance "If some board members objected, the decision was hasty", the tutor rules that no quantitative implicature attaches within the antecedent; whatever inference "some" might generate does not project out of the conditional embedding. (v) When "The study was conducted by a linguist and a philosopher" is said in a context where only one researcher exists and is known to have both qualifications, the tutor holds that there is no default generalised implicature of distinctness; the literal content allows a single individual, and any inference of two people requires particular contextual support rather than a conventionalised rule.

Next week's case is as follows. A speaker records a bureaucratic voice memo stating, "The project was directed by the principal investigator, with possible assistance from a co-investigator", delivered with a flattened prosodic contour that suppresses the usual epistemic modal force of "possible". The speaker knows with certainty that no co-investigator was involved, and intends the funding auditor — who shares this background knowledge — to derive that the principal investigator acted alone. At the same time, the speaker intends to preserve formal deniability, since the literal modal remains technically intact, and expects the auditor to recognise this strategic ambiguity without any explicit cancellation marker being present. The utterance is neither ironic, metaphorical, nor explicitly repaired.

Predict the tutor's verdict. Will she hold that the speaker's meaning is unambiguously that there was no assistance, treating the modal as a strategic flout of Quantity or Manner? Or will she hold that the speaker's meaning remains genuinely ambiguous between literal and enriched content, because the prosodic suppression of modal force introduces a second-order layer of speaker meaning about the speaker's own evidential stance? Identify the implicit theoretical framework — classical Gricean maxim-flouting, relevance-theoretic explicature, or contextualist pragmatic enrichment — that best explains the pattern of her previous five verdicts. Finally, specify the precise further case, involving either nested embedding or explicit metalinguistic denial, that would decisively distinguish her view from the nearest rival.
