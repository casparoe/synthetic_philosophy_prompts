I have been revisiting Newcomb's problem for an advanced seminar I am preparing, and I remain struck by how little the core dispute has shifted across decades of discussion. The two-boxers invoke dominance reasoning: regardless of the predictor's prior action, selecting both boxes yields a strictly higher payoff in each possible state of the opaque box. The one-boxers reply with evidential expected utility: given the strong correlation between the agent's present choice and the predictor's decision, taking only one box maximizes expected value. Both camps accept the same payoff structure, agree on the predictor's accuracy, and concede that the agent's present action cannot causally alter the past contents of the boxes. Yet the literature continues to recycle the same rejoinders without convergence, and neither side treats the opposing argument as even mildly damaging.
