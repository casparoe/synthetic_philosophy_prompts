I have been puzzling over a tension I noticed whilst observing discussions between experimental physicists and philosophers of science at a recent workshop on quantum foundations. The tension concerns the everyday working practices in applied quantum laboratories—particularly the calibration and readout protocols for superconducting qubit devices. In these settings, researchers routinely deploy a stochastic master equation that includes an explicit collapse term, treating the measurement outcome as a single, irreversible event and applying quantum error-correction codes that presuppose decoherence with the environment is a genuine loss of coherence rather than merely the entanglement of the apparatus with a larger unitary system. At the same time, the very same research groups employ the wavefunction as an indispensable statistical predictor of interference patterns during the calibration phase, and they typically resist any interpretation that reduces it to a mere cataloguing of subjective ignorance, since the relative phases between components carry direct physical significance for the gate fidelity.

I want to ask what philosophical commitments regarding the reality of the wavefunction—specifically, the dispute between psi-ontic and psi-epistemic interpretations—are implicitly presupposed by this bifurcated practice. Does the operational treatment of decoherence as irreversible commit them to an ontic view in which the state vector genuinely describes physical reality and genuinely collapses? Or can a sophisticated epistemic account accommodate the instrumental use of collapse terms without attributing reality to the universal wavefunction? Please reconstruct the implicit metaphysics: distinguish what the laboratory protocol strictly requires from what it merely fits comfortably with; say whether different elements of the practice presuppose inconsistent ontologies; and tell me what aspects of experimental design and error-correction strategy would need to change if the underlying commitment were replaced by its principal rival.
