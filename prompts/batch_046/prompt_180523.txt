At a philosophy of mind reading group, two colleagues debated whether the colour phi illusion supports what one called an “Orwellian” versus a “Stalinesque” model of conscious temporal revision. (1) Alex argued that colour phi is Orwellian: the brain continuously updates its narrative of the immediate past so that the colour change appears to precede the motion, just as the Ministry of Truth constantly rewrites records to match present needs, leaving the subject with no access to the “original” unedited stream. (2) Blake countered that it is Stalinesque: the intermediate perceptual state is not rewritten but airbrushed out entirely—there never was a conscious representation of green-then-red with no motion, just as figures were erased from photographs leaving no gap—so the conscious record jumps directly from green-at-position-one to red-at-position-two without any intermediate “draft” being preserved or revised. (3) Alex replied that the Stalinesque view cannot explain why the illusion requires precise temporal proximity; if the gap were genuinely erased with no trace, there should be no difference between 50 ms and 500 ms separations, yet the illusion breaks down at longer intervals, which makes sense on the Orwellian view because the “rewriting machinery” only operates within a narrow integration window. (4) Blake said that this misses the point: the breakdown at longer intervals is exactly what Stalinesque revision predicts, because airbrushing only works when the replacement can be inserted into a coherent narrative; at 500 ms the brain can no longer fabricate a seamless replacement and the “missing” frame becomes detectable, whereas an Orwellian continuous-rewrite model should in principle work at any interval given sufficient processing resources. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. What, if anything, are both parties missing about how retrospective and prospective revision might interact in constructing the experienced present? Write your answer in British English.
