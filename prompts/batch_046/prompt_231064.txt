After a long argument with a friend about Nozick’s experience machine, I want you to take a clear position and defend it rigorously. She proposed injecting an “epistemic distance” condition into theories of well-being: a subject’s flourishing can depend on structurally coherent false beliefs that are never experientially corrected, provided they sustain a stable second-order narrative about one’s life. Argue either that this modification makes the experience machine compatible with a desire-fulfillment theory—because plugging in can satisfy those second-order narrative desires without breaching authenticity—or that it renders the theory ad hoc by decoupling well-being from both experience and reality in a way that collapses the distinction between fulfillment and delusion. Be explicit about how your version of the theory handles the authenticity constraint and whether the result is a theoretical virtue or a reductio.
