I'm grading essays for an advanced seminar on distributive justice. Here is the exam prompt and one student's response; please assign a letter grade and provide specific point-by-point comments that identify accurate distinctions and subtle errors.

Exam question: "Distinguish a desert-based justification for work-search conditionality in welfare benefits from one grounded in reciprocal social obligation. Then evaluate whether either framework can consistently treat benefit fraud—concealing informal income while claiming support—as a moral wrong that is distinct from ordinary administrative non-compliance, or whether both reduce it to the same status as failing to file paperwork."

Student answer (condensed):

"A desert view holds that welfare is earned through productive contribution; the unemployed have not contributed, so they lack entitlement and must seek work to acquire it. A reciprocal-obligation view, by contrast, treats benefits and duties as paired: recipients owe the community good-faith effort in exchange for support, not because they are undeserving, but because fairness demands mutual exchange. On the desert account, fraud is not specially wrong: if the claimant already lacks entitlement due to non-contribution, taking more through deception is merely an extension of the same deficit. The reciprocal account does better. Fraud violates the specific duty of honest disclosure that makes reciprocity possible; it is not just missing a form but actively breaking the exchange. However, this view allows that a claimant who performs the required job-search rituals without genuine intent has fully satisfied reciprocity, since the rules only specify observable behavior. Thus fraud is a breach of reciprocity, but formal compliance is sufficient, which seems counterintuitive.

Therefore, only the reciprocal framework treats fraud as a distinct moral wrong, though it permits hollow compliance."

Please grade this answer, noting where it correctly separates the two frameworks, where it conflates formal compliance with substantive reciprocity, and whether its reasoning about desert and fraud actually follows. Focus on the conceptual structure rather than on named authors.
