The question asked students to evaluate whether this variant strengthens, weakens, or leaves unchanged Nozick's objection to (a) hedonistic theories, (b) desire-fulfillment theories with attention to de dicto versus de re satisfaction, and (c) objective-list theories that treat genuine achievement and knowledge of the world as basic goods.

Here is the student's response: "I think the memory-wipe feature actually makes Nozick's case stronger across the board. For hedonism, the machine still delivers the same mental states, so hedonists can't object unless they add an implausible reality-condition, which means hedonism is just desire-fulfillment in disguise. For desire-fulfillment, since all the user's second-order desires are satisfied—she wants to have lived a meaningful life and she believes she has—the de dicto/de re distinction collapses here because her psychological state upon waking is identical to that of someone who actually lived it. So desire-fulfillment theorists like Parfit must concede the machine is harmless. For objective-list theories, the user lacks real achievement, but this only matters if we think achievement is valuable for the pride it produces; since the objective-list already values knowledge, and she lacks knowledge that her life was simulated, the view just reduces to hedonism about epistemic security. Therefore Nozick's argument ultimately targets only mental-state theories, and the modified machine shows that even objective-list theories depend on feeling secure about reality."
