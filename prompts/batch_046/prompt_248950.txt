I run participatory budgeting assemblies for our city — about 80 residents, 3-hour blocks, real money on the line. I'm not a historian but I keep seeing patterns that remind me of the big Athenian failures: the Mytilene debate where they voted to kill everyone then reversed next day out of guilt, the Sicily expedition where prior success made them ignore every warning, and Socrates' trial where they used procedural formality to scapegoat an annoying critic during a legitimacy crisis. I want a reusable moderator's procedure I can actually run from the chair in under five minutes between agenda items, no committee sign-off, just me calling recess or reframing.

Turn those three failure signatures — emotional punishment reversal, overconfidance cascade after prior wins, and procedural scapegoating under stress — into a step-by-step checklist: what signals I recieve in the room (speech patterns, vote dynamics, body language?), in what order do I check them, what's the hard stop (I call recess) versus soft flag (I ask for reframing), and where does the checklist hand off to values rather than facts? Be honest about which steps are just judgment calls dressed up as protocol. I also need to know exactly where this will misfire.

Then apply it to three cases: (1) the group moves to permanently blacklist a local arts nonprofit after defunding them for an emergency repair; (2) after two small wins, they push an ambitious regional water-sharing deal with zero risk analysis; (3) a regular attendee who always challenges my neutrality is accused of disruption and there's a motion to bar them. Include one case where the procedure tells me to do something I'll hate. And tell me plainly what kinds of failure this checklist won't catch at all, especially ones that don't look like Athens.
