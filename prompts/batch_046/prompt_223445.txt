I'm serving on the acquisitions board for our regional museum of natural and cultural history (just got appointed last month, kind of thrown in the deep end). We recieved an offer from the estate of a local guy, Dr. Elias Vorn, who died in March. Thier family wants to donate his entire research archive -- like forty years of notes, self-published monographs, and artifact photos -- arguing it should be preserved as "regional intellectual history." The problem is Vorn was basically a dedicated Fomenko-style revisionist: he claimed standard chronology of the ancient Near East is a 16th-century fabrication, that Atlantis was a real administrative center for a global Bronze Age network, and that Herodotus was mostly a fiction writer whose work got mistaken for primary source. Some board members say accepting the donation legitimizes pseudohistory; others say rejecting it is gatekeeping and that museums should archive dissenting views, especially since Vorn's papers include original field photographs that might have value regardless of his theories. I need to write a short decision memo for our meeting next Thursday recommending whether we accept, reject, or accept with conditions -- with actual criteria the board can apply, and I need to take seriously the argument that archiving equals endorsement. Please keep it under 450 words, lead with the bottom line in the first sentence or two, then the reasoning, then address the strongest point on the other side honestly. Don't ask for citations or reading lists since I need something I can circulate without looking like I'm outsourcing the philosophy to a bibliography.
