Design a decision tree that sorts positions on the justification of legal punishment by asking respondents about concrete cases, not about school names. Use four branching questions: (i) Is punishing a known-innocent person ever permissible if done secretly to avert mass violence? (ii) Does a wrongdoer who has fully repented and made restitution still deserve suffering? (iii) Can a corporation be justly punished when no individual member is culpable? (iv) Does determinism, if true, undermine punishment's justification? Each node should be answerable without knowing which view it points to. The leaves must include standard retributivism, consequentialism, and at least one hybrid, but also the rarely mapped "expressive ritualist" view (punishment justified only as a symbolic reaffirmation of shared norms, neither for desert nor for deterrence) and the "institutional abolitionist" view (even well-targeted suffering is impermissible because it legitimises carceral structures). For each leaf, give the exact pattern of answers that reaches it; note any logically possible pattern that lacks an occupant and say whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored; and flag any two leaves the tree genuinely fails to separate. Present this in British English, with no bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
