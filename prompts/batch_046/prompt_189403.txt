I've been thinking about the Browning vs Goldhagen debate and I think both sides are describing different gears in the same machine. My position: situational pressure — peer dynamics, authority, incremental commitment — explains the mechanism of particpation for most perpetrators in Reserve Police Battalion 101, but it only functions because Goldhagen-style cultural antisemitism had already disabled normal moral inhibtion toward Jews. So culture isn't a rival explanation; it's the precondition that lets the situational mechanism work. They weren't either ordinary conformists or willing executioners, but ordinary conformists inside a cultural frame that had already made the victims morally invisible.

What would actually break this? I want defeaters ranked from fatal to awkward. For example, if we found a unit with high situational pressure but weak cultural dehumanization that still killed at high rates, that'd hurt. Or if perpetrators showed moral distress that was purely about breaking rules, with no trace of the victims being categorized differently. And be honest: have I just built a view that absorbs every counterexample by saying "oh, the cultural frame was implicit"? Is that insulation a real problem for this kind of coupled explanation?
