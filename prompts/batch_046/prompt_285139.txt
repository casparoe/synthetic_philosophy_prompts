I am a high school student preparing an interview with a philosopher of technology who defends a modified inverse-reinforcement-learning framework for recovering human values from observed behavior. She argues that standard IRL systematically misclassifies preference inconsistencies as stochastic noise, when in fact they often reflect structurally meaningful trade-offs between instrumental rewards and terminal commitments, and she proposes a hierarchical Bayesian revision that distinguishes reward-correlated actions from commitment-expressive ones. Please draft six to eight interview questions that do not ask her to summarize IRL, but instead press her specific view: include at least one on how her model avoids circularity if it labels an action commitment-expressive only by assuming the value hierarchy it claims to discover; one on whether her framework renders normative critique of inferred preferences impossible, since any deviation can be reinterpreted as hidden commitment; and one on what empirical behavioral pattern—if consistently observed—would compel her to abandon the hierarchical revision in favor of standard noise models. For each main question, provide a follow-up keyed to the defense she is most likely to give.
