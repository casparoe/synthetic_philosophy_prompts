I am trying to determine what I actually think about whether the numbers should count in rescue cases, by testing my intuitions against concrete scenarios and seeing whether they converge on a principle.

Here are my verdicts. If I can save either one stranger or five strangers, with equal cost and risk to myself, I should save the five. If I can save either my child or five strangers, I should save my child — and I still feel that way if the alternative is saving fifty strangers. But if the choice is between saving one close friend and saving six strangers, I find myself inclined to save the six, which sits uneasily with the child case: both involve a special tie, yet my verdicts differ.

There is also a technological case I keep returning to. Suppose I operate a remote rescue drone and can save five people in a distant location, or save one person physically present beside me; I think I should save the five remotely. But suppose an autonomous triage algorithm, with no human operator present, is programmed with a rule that permits it to sacrifice one identified individual in order to save five others. In that case, I think the algorithm should not be permitted to make that trade, even though I would make the same numerical comparison myself. So agency and proximity seem to matter, but I am unsure how.

Finally, there is a harm case: if saving the five requires me to actively kill one innocent bystander (not merely fail to rescue), I would not do it. The one dies either way, but my direct involvement changes the verdict.

Is there a single principle — or a small family of principles — that captures all of these judgments, or nearly so? Please state it precisely, indicate which of my verdicts it accommodates and which it must overturn, and apply it to two cases I have not mentioned: first, where the five are all elderly with terminal conditions and the one is a healthy adolescent; second, where the rescue is mediated entirely by an algorithm that I designed but am not operating in real time. Also, please be explicit about what your principle predicts if the numbers are very large — say, one versus one thousand.
