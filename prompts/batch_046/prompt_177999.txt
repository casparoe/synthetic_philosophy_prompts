I am a member of my university's Student Conduct Appeals Board, assigned to draft the board's formal opinion in Case 2024-17 for circulation before Friday's vote. The appellant admits she presented a co-authored essay as her own work, but argues that our Honor Code's preamble—invoking "moral authorship"—presupposes a libertarian variety of free will (ultimate origination or metaphysical self-creation) that she lacks. She does not deny causal determinism; instead she claims she possesses only the compatibilist varieties that philosophers actually treat as worth wanting—hierarchical self-governance, reason-responsiveness, and narrative self-constitution—none of which, she says, ground retributive punishment. She asks us to vacate the suspension and transcript notation, replacing them with a mandatory ethics module, on the ground that institutional accountability should align with the capacities of agency that survive determinism rather than with an anachronistic metaphysics of the self.

The board must recommend a concrete course: uphold the suspension, reduce the sanction to the module alone, or remand for revised charges that drop the authorship language. Your memo should treat her argument seriously, distinguishing the different varieties of free will at issue and explaining which ones our disciplinary system actually requires for blame and sanction. Be explicit about what is philosophically settled (for instance, that determinism by itself does not erase the capacities that support practical reasoning and self-correction) and what remains genuinely contested (for instance, whether retributive desert requires more than compatibilist capacities, or whether narrative identity can bear institutional weight). Include an honest, substantive treatment of the strongest consideration on the side you reject—whether that is the appellant's charge that our code is conceptually obsolete, or the administration's concern that accepting her framework dissolves the distinction between disciplinary and therapeutic responses.

Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested view—such as whether determinism is compatible with the kind of agency required for moral responsibility—as if it were the consensus. End the memo with a single line beginning "Verdict:" that states the board's recommended action in no more than fifteen words.
