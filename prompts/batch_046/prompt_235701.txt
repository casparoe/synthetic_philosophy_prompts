Read Ursula K. Le Guin’s short story “The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas” through the lens of lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism and the incommensurability of pleasure and pain. The city’s happiness is made explicit: it is purchased, in fixed proportion, by the abject suffering of one child locked in a broom closet, and every citizen is eventually shown the price. I want a philosophical reading that treats this not as a generic trolley problem but as a direct dramatization of whether suffering and well-being share a common metric at all. Does the child’s suffering function as a lexical counterweight—an irreducible, non-compensable magnitude that nullifies all aggregate joy regardless of scale—or does the narrative allow the citizens’ calculus to remain internally coherent, with the ones who leave representing mere aesthetic revulsion rather than recognition of a discontinuous boundary? Examine the key scenes—the public festivals, the explanation delivered to adolescents, the physical description of the closet and the child’s body—to see whether the story’s architecture supports a sharp “lexical” threshold below which no quantity of pleasure can justify a state, or whether it smuggles in a smooth, continuous trade-off curve that the text then condemns on emotional rather than conceptual grounds. Where does the philosophy illuminate the citizens’ psychology, and where does the fiction resist reduction to a thought experiment—perhaps in the ambiguity of whether departure actually aids the child, in the absence of any collective institutional refusal, or in the way the narrative withholds the child’s gender and precise age? Conclude with a defended verdict: does “Omelas” vindicate the lexical-threshold view by making incommensurability visceral and unavoidable, or does it inadvertently undermine that view by showing that even those who reject the calculus possess no practical alternative except private flight? I’m preparing this for a discussion group familiar with suffering-focused ethics and debates about scope insensitivity, so the analysis should engage those conceptual tools without relying on outside citations or a bibliography.
