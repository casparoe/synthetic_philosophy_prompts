I am preparing a one-semester independent-study proposal and need it turned into a polished, two-page prospectus. The core idea is this: Parfit’s hitchhiker case is standardly read as showing that rational agents ought to cultivate dispositions that are locally irrational. I want to investigate whether that reading survives if we reject the implicit assumption that the hitchhiker’s future self has perfect epistemic access to her own prior reasoning. Specifically, if the post-arrival agent can genuinely fail to recover—not merely suppress—the line of thought that made the pre-departure commitment rational, does the case still demonstrate “rational irrationality,” or does it collapse into a problem about diachronic self-knowledge and reasoning transparency?

Write the proposal with the following sections, in this order, with approximate lengths:

1. The precise question (around 200 words): State the research question sharply, distinguish it from the standard hitchhiker debate, and explain why the access-to-reasoning twist changes the strategic structure rather than just adding psychological color.

2. Why existing answers leave it open (around 250 words): Sketch the standard Parfitian resolution and at least one prominent alternative (for example, a constrained-maximization response or a causal-decision-theoretic treatment), and show precisely where both depend on the transparency assumption that the future self can fully access its prior reasoning.

3. Approach and method (around 350 words): Explain what you would actually do over the semester—formalizing the reasoning-access constraint, comparing it to models of bounded rationality or logical omniscience failure in formal epistemology, and testing whether the modified scenario preserves the same game-theoretic payoff structure—rather than offering a literature survey. Say why this conceptual approach is more promising than a direct empirical or purely decision-theoretic alternative.

4. Deliverable and failure conditions (around 200 words): Describe what a successful result looks like (for instance, a clear demarcation between genuine rational-irrationality cases and mere self-knowledge failures) and what would count as the project failing (for example, if the transparency assumption turns out to be ineliminable from any coherent model of diachronic agency).

5. Scope and feasibility (around 200 words): Give an honest assessment of whether this can be completed in one semester without empirical work, flag the risk that the reasoning-access model is too underspecified, and state what you would cut if time runs short.
