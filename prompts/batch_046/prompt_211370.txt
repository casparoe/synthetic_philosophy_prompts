As a retired field biologist who has recently begun studying philosophy of biology, I am trying to understand why taxonomists and metaphysicians talk past one another on the species problem. To that end, please write a satirical, deadpan parody of a formal taxonomic revision notice—approximately 600 words, in the dry, institutional register of a journal such as Systematic Biology—advancing what I will call “inter-world reproductive isolation” (IWRI) as the definitive solution. By IWRI, two populations count as conspecific if and only if there exists at least one metaphysically possible world in which members of both populations can interbreed but refrain from doing so, thereby preserving their respective essential natures; if no such world exists, they are distinct species regardless of actual interbreeding in this world.

The piece should present IWRI as a serious proposal, include a mock classification table applying it to familiar cases—the domestic cat (Felis catus) versus its wild progenitor, the mule (as a non-species entity lacking modal autonomy), and a well-known ring-species complex—so that a reader sees exactly where empirical biology is overridden by modal logic. Add a sidebar titled “Response to Critics” in which an invented proponent defends IWRI against the charge of unfalsifiability by arguing that unfalsifiability is not a defect but a metaphysical virtue, ensuring that taxonomy is finally insulated from messy empirical revision.

The satire must land the real philosophical objections—essentialism’s failure with ring species and hybrid zones, the arbitrariness of selecting one modal world over another, and the category mistake of substituting metaphysical possibility for observed reproductive behavior—without ever stating them as objections. Instead, let the deadpan institutional voice treat each absurd consequence as a straightforward administrative update. The humor should come from the precision with which you apply philosophical machinery to biological practice: a specialist should recognize that you grasp the actual debates (pluralism versus monism, the biological species concept, phylogenetic systematics) and should find the exaggeration fair rather than ignorant.

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not invoke highly obscure theories unknown to a general philosophical audience; work only with the IWRI framework I have defined here and with widely familiar biological and philosophical concepts. Keep the irony sustained and legible: a reader should be able to tell what is being mocked without any winking from the author.
