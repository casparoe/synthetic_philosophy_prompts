As the student representative on my university's Institutional Biosafety Review Committee and a participant in this semester's graduate seminar on conceptual boundaries in the life sciences, I am drafting a decision memo for circulation before Thursday's meeting. The case concerns Project Membrane, a synthetic-biology system engineered to maintain self-repairing lipid bilayers and internal redox gradients through continuous chemical exchange with its environment. The vesicles are assembled entirely from abiotic precursors, contain no nucleic acids, cannot reproduce or undergo evolutionary change, and yet exhibit a form of operational closure and homeostasis that some researchers compare to minimal living cells. Our current biosafety protocol applies to "living modified organisms," but it offers no guidance for synthetic entities that possess organizational properties associated with life without possessing genetic inheritance or evolutionary potential. The committee is divided: one faction argues that organizational closure is sufficient for regulatory coverage under a functional conception of life; the other maintains that without an evolutionary lineage these structures are merely sophisticated dissipative chemical systems and should be treated as bioactive reagents.

For the purposes of this memo, please recommend a concrete regulatory determination—either full coverage under living-organism protocols, exemption as a non-living reagent, or an interim intermediate classification with tailored containment rules—and propose an explicit review timeline, such as twelve months, after which the determination will be reassessed. Your recommendation should include clear, operational criteria the committee can apply to future borderline synthetic cases, should treat the evolutionary-lineage objection seriously rather than dismissing it, and should engage the strongest argument for the position you ultimately reject. The document should be formal, suitable for circulation to both scientists and administrators without philosophical training, and should not include bibliographic references, suggested readings, or requests for citation.
