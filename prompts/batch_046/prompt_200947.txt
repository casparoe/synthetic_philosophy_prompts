Then think step by step, being careful to distinguish strict logical presupposition from mere compatibility.

Here is the practice. In regulatory and certification auditing for farmed aquatic animals, finfish welfare at slaughter is routinely assessed through a standardized time-to-insensibility protocol: auditors apply electrical or percussive stunning and then measure the interval until specific reflex arcs—opercular movement, righting reflex, vestibulo-ocular response—are lost, treating these as proxies for the absence of conscious experience. If the interval exceeds a defined threshold, the operation receives a welfare penalty. Decapod crustaceans—shrimp, prawns, crabs, lobsters—are handled differently. In the same audit frameworks, they are rarely subjected to insensibility verification at all. Where crustaceans are included, welfare scoring depends on environmental metrics (temperature, salinity, stocking density) and on visible tissue damage observed post-mortem or post-processing; there is no required measurement of nociceptive response duration, and prolonged behavioral activity after killing is not registered as a welfare failure. The framework therefore assigns regulatory weight to harms that mirror mammalian trauma signatures and treats distributed, non-vertebrate nervous-system responses as either epistemically inaccessible or normatively irrelevant.

What philosophical commitments does this bifurcated auditing practice presuppose? Must it commit to a particular theory of consciousness—specifically, one that requires centralized integration of nociceptive signals into a unified conscious field, thereby excluding distributed ganglionic processing as insufficient for moral status? Or could a sentientist position that grants welfare interests to any being capable of nociception accommodate the split through auxiliary claims about measurement tractability, evolutionary distance, or institutional feasibility? Reconstruct the implicit theory of value and moral status that renders the practice coherent. Clearly separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates well, and identify the points where it is philosophically underdetermined—where rival theories of animal mind or welfare could license the same regulatory division for different reasons.
