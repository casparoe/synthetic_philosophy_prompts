Construct a thought experiment that meets the following specifications. Design a realistic, non-science-fictional scenario—set in a clinical trial or workplace wellness program, no longer than one paragraph—in which an ordinary agent must decide whether to enroll in a one-month, fully reversible cognitive-enhancement protocol. The protocol temporarily reweights the agent’s aesthetic and ethical priorities but leaves all autobiographical memory intact. Crucially, the protocol includes a “value-preview” mechanism: before enrolling, the agent receives a calibrated statistical summary, derived from prior participants with similar baseline profiles, describing the likely distribution of priorities they will hold during the enhanced state, without providing any direct experiential or first-person access to those priorities.

Engineer the case so that three theoretical positions deliver the specified verdicts:
1. A rational-choice position, evaluating the decision solely through the agent’s pre-enhancement preference ordering and expected utility, advises against enrollment.
2. A transformative-experience position—holding that rational evaluation is blocked when a choice alters both what it is like to be the chooser and the values by which the choice is assessed—also advises against enrollment, but specifically on the grounds that the value-preview creates a spurious illusion of epistemic access without remedying the underlying transformation problem.
3. A narrative-self position—holding that personal identity depends on the continuity of a value-laden life narrative—advises in favor of enrollment, but only because the preview mechanism allows the pre-enhancement self to anticipate and integrate the temporary shift; without the preview, this position too advises against.

After presenting the scenario, provide the reasoning for each verdict. Then identify exactly which structural feature—reversibility, memory preservation, or the value-preview mechanism—does the work in generating the split between positions 2 and 3. State what happens to the case if that feature is removed, specifically whether the disagreement collapses into agreement. Finally, explain how a defender of the rational-choice position could attack the scenario, and whether that attack succeeds in undermining the distinction between positions 1 and 2.
