Please give me your overall credence—expressed as a probability between 0 and 1—that the left-right distinction tracks a single, real ideological dimension rather than a historically contingent cluster of locally correlated but conceptually independent attitudes. Decompose this credence into at least four sub-estimates: first, the probability that cross-national attitudinal surveys consistently reveal one dominant latent factor explaining the bulk of variance in economic and social policy preferences; second, the probability that this correlation persists even when institutional mechanisms (such as parliamentary seating arrangements or party-system incentives) are disrupted or absent; third, the probability that an individual with systematically inverted correlations—economically egalitarian but socially authoritarian—represents a coherent, stable ideological position rather than an unstable or transitional hybrid; and fourth, the probability that the historical origin of the terms in physical seating has no continuing causal influence on the clustering we observe today. For each component provide a rough number and a one-line justification, then combine them into the overall figure. Finally, identify which sub-estimate is most sensitive to new evidence, specify what kind of evidence would shift it substantially, and tell me whether my personal profile is more plausibly interpreted as evidence against the reality of the axis or as a rare but genuine position on it.
