I am a PhD student in philosophy of risk consulting for an institutional biosafety review board that meets monthly, has no dedicated statisticians, and must issue a signed recommendation within two hours per proposal. We repeatedly face applications—such as a gene-drive field trial estimated to have a 0.01% chance of irreversible ecological restructuring but a 2% chance of eliminating a vector-borne disease—where proponents justify acceptance by invoking enormous expected-value gains that swamp near-certain, smaller harms. I need a reusable decision procedure the board can run in real time.

Structure it as an ordered sequence of gates with explicit stopping conditions. At each gate specify: the precise question; what counts as passing, failing, or indeterminate; whether failure rejects the proposal or defers to normative judgment; and where computation must stop because the issue is values rather than probabilities. The procedure must explicitly operationalize the formal problem of expected-value fanaticism—namely that precise tail probabilities paired with unbounded or highly convex utility can license arbitrarily reckless choices—without requiring members to integrate over distributions.

Address three hard structural cases in the rules themselves: (a) probabilities drawn from competing simulation models lacking empirical calibration; (b) outcomes whose valuation is contested across stakeholders; and (c) proposals where the catastrophic tail and the beneficial tail lie on the same causal pathway rather than being independent. Apply the procedure to two proposals: the gene-drive trial above, and a stratospheric aerosol study claiming a 0.5% chance of arresting a climate feedback loop against a 1% chance of disrupting monsoon patterns—include the case where the procedure yields a recommendation the board will find counterintuitive.

Finally, identify the specific failure mode in which an applicant splits a single causal pathway into apparently independent tails to game the gates, and state precisely what the board should do when the procedure reaches its limit and cannot settle the matter.
