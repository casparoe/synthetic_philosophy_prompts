Write an essay on the debate between presentism and eternalism in the philosophy of time, focusing specifically on the "modal spotlight" reconstruction of presentism. On this view, the present is not a point moving through an already-existing temporal line, but the uniquely actualized temporal slice—analogous to the way modal realists treat the actual world among possible worlds. Assess whether this reconstruction avoids the classic objection that any "moving spotlight" theory requires a second time dimension, and whether it can preserve genuine presentist intuitions about ontological privilege without collapsing into eternalism's claim that all times are equally real. Weigh the central arguments: the charge that modal actualization covertly imports eternalist ontology, the reply that eternalism cannot explain the asymmetry of causal influence, and the worry that treating presentness as an indexical-like property undermines the presentist's metaphysical commitment. Reach and defend a clear verdict—whether the modal spotlight offers a viable intermediate position, whether it ultimately collapses into one of the standard views, or whether it should be rejected on other grounds. Write in American English.
