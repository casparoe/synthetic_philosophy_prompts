I am preparing material for a philosophy seminar on the Meno problem—specifically, what makes knowledge more valuable than true belief. I want a decision tree that classifies answers by the claims that genuinely split them, not by philosopher names. Please present the tree as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: items 1 through 4 should each state one binary branching question and the two paths it opens, and item 5 should deliver your committed diagnosis.

Item 1 must split answers by whether the extra value is intrinsic to the believer's mental state or instrumental to action and inquiry. Item 2 must split intrinsic-state answers by whether the value derives from propositional justification or from a non-propositional achievement or intellectual virtue. Item 3 must split instrumental answers by whether stability comes from the belief's etiology—reliability, tracking, or causation—or from its functional role in reasoning and in licensing testimonial transmission to others. Item 4 must split all prior paths by whether knowledge differs from true belief in kind or only survives a social verification filter that true belief typically fails.

For the leaves, place the Platonic account-giving view, reliabilism, virtue epistemology, pragmatism, and a social-epistemic testimony-licensing view; label any unoccupied combination "unnamed" and note its most awkward commitment. In item 5, take a definite stand on which of the four splits is the most philosophically decisive, flag any pair of named positions that your tree cannot separate, and explain why the unnamed leaf that reduces knowledge's value to passing a communal filter is either incoherent or unexplored. Do not provide a bibliography or citation list; build the taxonomy from the logical structure of the debate itself.
