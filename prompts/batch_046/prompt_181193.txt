Write a short story about a world where lying is legal but misleading is a felony — they call it "constructive deception" and it covers saying only true things arranged so the listener will definately form a false belief. The protagonist is defending her sister, who wore the wrong colored scarf at a tribunal (in this culture scarves signal whether you're in "assertion mode" or "suggestion mode") and now faces prison for technically true but deceptive testimony. Through the trial, actually dig into whether there's a real moral gap between lying and misleading, or if the law is just being arbitary about form vs intent. Don't make it a generic courtroom drama — let the scarf ritual and the weird legal distinction do the philosophical work, and keep the characters specific rather than mouthpieces.
