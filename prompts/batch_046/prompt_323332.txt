Read Frankenstein through Von Neumann's brain-computer comparison -- specifically his idea that the brain dosen't store language like a digital program but compiles it into analog, statistical patterns with built-in error correction. The Creature learns to speak and read in like a year, and Victor treats him as an assembled machine that just needs the right parts. Does Shelley side with Von Neumann's analog view (the Creature's mind emerges from messy, self-correcting experience) or with a digital view (if you build the body right, the program runs)? I'm using this for a phil of mind class and I want to avoid the usual Frankenstein-warns-about-playing-god reading -- does the novel actually dramatize something about how minds can't be engineered part by part?
