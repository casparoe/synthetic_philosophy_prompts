Draft the adversarial-collaboration document that a Gödelian temporal idealist and a cosmological metric pluralist could both sign, treating Gödel’s 1949 rotating-universe solution and a constructed “Gödel-bubble” scenario in which a bounded region exhibits Gödel-like rotation and closed timelike curves while the exterior spacetime remains asymptotically flat and globally hyperbolic.

Provide the following, in formal and precise language, without bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. Cover: the formal properties of the Gödel metric (homogeneous rotation, presence of closed timelike curves, failure of global hyperbolicity, absence of global Cauchy surfaces); the matter and cosmological-constant conditions required by the solution; Gödel’s own presentation of the metric as philosophically relevant to idealist accounts of time; the distinction between coordinate-time ordering and locally measured clock behavior; and, specifically for the bubble scenario, the boundary conditions that separate the interior rotating region from the exterior flat region.

2. A disagreements table. For each party, state in wording that side would accept for itself: (a) whether the global absence of a cosmic time function undermines any objective past-future distinction; (b) whether the asymptotically flat exterior restores a privileged global frame that blocks the idealist inference inside the bubble; (c) whether the physical implausibility of sustaining the required matter distribution and cosmological constant renders the metric philosophically moot regardless of local structure; and (d) what each side takes Gödel’s philosophical remarks to establish about the reality of temporal passage.

3. The cruxes. Identify the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party. These should include: whether local temporal orientation can be ontologically fundamental even when globally non-orientable; whether boundary conditions at a Gödel-bubble interface can stabilize or destabilize the interior rotation without propagating temporal ambiguity outward; and whether Gödel’s inference from mathematical consistency to a conclusion about the unreality of time is a valid philosophical move or a category error.

4. For each crux, specify the concrete case, physical condition, or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record each side’s explicit prediction in advance. For instance, both may agree that a stable numerical-relativity demonstration of a Gödel-bubble with physically realistic stress-energy at the boundary would bear on the second crux; record whether the idealist predicts boundary instability will always prevent such formation, and whether the pluralist predicts it could form without affecting exterior temporal structure.

Finally, distinguish any part of the dispute that is verbal rather than substantive—such as whether “time” denotes coordinate ordering, experienced duration, or causal structure—and explain exactly why that portion does not affect the residual disagreement. Ensure every claim in the agreed section is one both parties could genuinely sign, and that no disagreement is phrased in terms the opposing side would reject as mischaracterization.
