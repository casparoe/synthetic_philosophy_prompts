Student answer:

The thermodynamic arrow is defined by the second law of thermodynamics: in an isolated system, entropy tends to increase between temporal boundaries. The psychological arrow consists in our asymmetric experience of remembering the past and anticipating the future. These two arrows align in our world because human cognition is implemented in physical processes that themselves follow the local entropy gradient.

The thermodynamic arrow is not reducible to the psychological arrow. Entropy is an objective statistical feature of macro-states—specifically, proportional to the logarithm of the number of compatible micro-states—whereas psychological asymmetry is a feature of conscious representation. To see that the relation is explanatory rather than constitutive, imagine an isolated region—an entropy-inverted pocket—in which local boundary conditions systematically drive macro-state disorder downward. Any complex physical structures inside, including information-processing systems analogous to brains, would evolve in the direction of local entropy decrease. Consequently, an observer embedded there would recall events that lie in our future and expect events that lie in our past. This demonstrates that the orientation of mental time is determined by the local thermodynamic gradient, not by any intrinsic property of psychology itself.

Hans Reichenbach maintained that the causal structure of the world provides an independent explanation for both the thermodynamic and psychological arrows. However, this view overlooks that causal asymmetry itself derives from entropy differences between connected states. Once we recognize that causation is subordinate to statistical mechanics, there is no need to treat causal structure as an independent explanatory factor.

As for the origin of the arrow, Ludwig Boltzmann suggested that our observable universe is a temporary statistical fluctuation within an eternal, high-entropy equilibrium cosmos. Because fluctuations of arbitrary scale eventually occur in such a universe, the low-entropy condition of our region is not metaphysically fundamental; it is merely a contingent local feature. Thus, while the thermodynamic arrow is objectively real within our pocket, it lacks any deeper necessary foundation.
