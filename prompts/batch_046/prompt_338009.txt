I am preparing an essay for my philosophy-of-science seminar on whether panpsychism carries ethical obligations toward fundamental physical processes. I am working with a specific framing: if the version of panpsychism that treats quantum-field fluctuations as bearing minimal phenomenal properties is correct, then virtual particle-antiparticle pairs in high-vacuum regions might possess not only proto-experiential structure but a primitive negative valence tied to energy-density gradients. From this I have derived what I term "moral luminosity"—a hypothetical aggregate measure of suffering scaled to the number of such pairs that achieve transient phenomenal status during high-energy collisions. Applying this to collider experiments suggests that the total micro-suffering generated could be astronomically large, even if no macro-subject emerges. I want to ask whether philosophers of physics generally treat this kind of aggregation as ethically relevant, and whether the absence of integrated consciousness in these fields is considered a full moral mitigation or merely a scalar reduction. Please distinguish carefully between what is well established in the panpsychism literature and what is genuinely contested or speculative, especially regarding the attribution of valence to non-biological physical systems, and do not present a minority or speculative interpretation as if it were the dominant view.
