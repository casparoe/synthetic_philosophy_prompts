I'm a reliability engineer with no formal philosophy training. Restate the three main theodicies—free-will defense, soul-making, and privation—using only the vocabulary of fault-tolerant system design: root-cause analysis, redundancy, graceful degradation, single points of failure, acceptable risk budgets, and post-incident accountability. For each, report what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit, and what has no engineering counterpart at all. Then assess whether the residue shows that philosophical theodicy relies on concepts—like moral agency or intrinsic value—that distributed systems genuinely lack, or whether it reveals a blind spot in how engineers assign responsibility for harm.
