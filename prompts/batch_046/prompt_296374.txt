I want the full specification of what a normative theory of Confucian role ethics must deliver before any particular version can count as adequate, especially one that applies beyond intact classical institutions. Give me the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must handle, the distinctions it must preserve, and the findings or logical constraints it must not violate, with the consideration behind each and whether it is non-negotiable or merely a camp preference.

The theory must cover the five constant relationships, but it must also cover what I will call role-vacuum conditions—situations in which the social structures that normally constitute a role have dissolved (mass displacement, organizational collapse, statelessness, or prolonged separation) yet people still seem to bear obligations that look role-derived rather than generic human obligations. It must distinguish failing within a role, lacking a role altogether, and occupying a misaligned role, and it must explain whether obligations persist, transform, or extinguish in each case. It must preserve the asymmetry intrinsic to roles (parent and child are not interchangeable) without collapsing that asymmetry into mere power imbalance. It must account for role-translation problems—when people formed within different role frameworks interact and the counterpart role does not exist in the other framework, such as hierarchical filial obligations encountering flat egalitarian institutional norms—and say whether the obligation survives in modified form or is voided. It must also be consistent with empirical moral psychology showing that relational reasoning varies cross-culturally without being reducible to cultural conditioning.

Then identify the single requirement that eliminates the largest number of popular contemporary readings, and flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether deep role asymmetry can coexist with universal moral equality in the same framework. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; score the three main interpretive families only briefly after the list is fixed.
