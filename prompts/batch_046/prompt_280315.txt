As a retired natural scientist who has recently taken up philosophy with some seriousness, I have been struck by the precision with which empirical controversies are eventually mapped onto shared data and residual theoretical divergence, and I wish to see the ancient debate between Mencius and Xunzi on human nature subjected to the same discipline. I ask you to draft the joint statement that a committed Mencian—who maintains that human nature contains innate moral sprouts that unfold spontaneously when not obstructed—and a committed Xunzian—who maintains that human nature is originally driven by self-interested desire and achieves moral form only through ritual, learning, and artificial transformation—could both endorse after a rigorous adversarial collaboration.

The document should be structured as follows.

First, a numbered list of agreed claims: substantive points both sides accept about human psychology, the conditions under which moral behavior arises, the reality of moral failure, the necessity of some form of education or social structure, and the limits of what can be achieved by either pure spontaneity or pure external imposition. These must be precise enough to be meaningful, not merely diplomatic.

Second, a disagreements table. Each row should state one residual disagreement in wording that the side holding it accepts as accurate and that the opposing side recognizes as a fair characterization. Within this table, distinguish disagreements that are genuinely substantive from those that dissolve as verbal once terms are clarified; for any that are verbal, identify the ambiguity and explain why the dispute collapses.

Third, the cruxes: the specific sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would move at least one party. I anticipate that central cruxes will include whether moral capacities are best understood as domain-specific innate potentials or as domain-general capacities entirely sculpted by cultural practice, and whether ritual operates causally upon pre-existing desires or constitutively defines what counts as moral order. Include all cruxes that genuinely emerge from the collaboration, not only these.

Fourth, for each crux, specify one concrete case, scenario, or observation—accessible without specialized bibliographic knowledge—that both sides agree is relevant. For each such case, record explicitly what the Mencian side predicts or expects, and what the Xunzian side predicts or expects.

Finally, address the following framing question within the document: does the classical disagreement survive intact if one rejects the premise that nature and nurture are competing causal explanations and instead adopts a view in which biological endowment and social environment are co-constitutive processes? If part of the dispute depends on that false dichotomy, say which part and why; if the disagreement remains fully intact even under that reconceptualization, show exactly where.

Write in a formal, polished register appropriate to a collaborative philosophical text. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, requests for citations, or appeals to authority that would require access to sources. The statement should stand on conceptual analysis alone.
