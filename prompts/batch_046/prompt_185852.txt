Write a two-page proposal for a six-month independent research project—no archival travel required—addressing a specific tension in Darwin’s evolutionary ethics that standard interpretations tend to split apart rather than hold together. Darwin describes the widening circle of human sympathy, from tribal loyalty to universal moral concern, as both an adaptive evolutionary outcome and a genuinely normative moral advance; at the same time, he treats religious belief and many moral sentiments as byproducts of evolved cognitive mechanisms that carry no independent rational authority. State the precise question: can Darwin’s framework consistently distinguish moral progress from other evolutionary byproducts without relying on a hidden teleology, on normative premises his naturalism cannot license, or on a rhetorical slide from “is” to “ought”?

Frame the question so that it yields a determinate answer—either Darwin’s distinction is defensible on strictly naturalistic grounds (perhaps through a non-circular account of functional success in cooperative environments), or it collapses into one of two undesirable outcomes: a form of moral realism smuggled in through evolutionary language, or a reductive view that strips moral progress of normative authority and makes it indistinguishable from other evolutionary accidents.

Explain why existing positions leave this open. The standard “proto-social-Darwinist” reading takes Darwin to reduce morality to competitive advantage, which makes the normative language of progress puzzling; the “humanist moralist” reading celebrates his progressive vision but tends to treat his evolutionary explanation as mere background, ignoring the pressure it puts on normative claims. Neither asks whether Darwin’s own conceptual tools can sustain the distinction he wants to draw.

Outline the approach: a conceptual analysis—not an empirical study—of Darwin’s distinction between innate “social instincts” and cultivated “moral faculties,” examining whether that distinction can bear normative weight without circularity. Test whether his explanation of religious belief (as a misfiring or overactive agency-detection and social-cohesion mechanism) applies symmetrically to moral progress, and if not, identify the exact point at which Darwin introduces an asymmetry. Compare this with the obvious alternative—simply applying a proximate-versus-ultimate causation distinction—to show why that standard move fails to resolve the normative tension here.

Specify the deliverable: a mapped taxonomy of where Darwin implicitly relies on descriptive evolutionary explanation versus where he appeals to normative evaluation, with a verdict on whether the two registers can be held apart consistently within his naturalism. Define failure clearly: the project fails if the tension dissolves upon closer inspection into a well-known philosophical distinction (e.g., between evolutionary origins and current justification), leaving nothing specific to Darwin’s account; it also fails if the scope expands into a general defense or critique of evolutionary ethics rather than a targeted diagnosis of Darwin’s particular commitments.

Give an honest scope assessment for six months. The risk is not missing data but conceptual overreach—attempting to solve naturalism’s normativity problem generally rather than locating Darwin’s specific version of it. Note what would need to be excluded (e.g., extensive comparison with later evolutionary ethicists, archival study of unpublished notebooks) to keep the project tractable.

Finally, state what would change your mind about the project’s viability: which argument or finding—such as a convincing reconstruction showing that Darwin’s moral language is entirely rhetorical and carries no normative commitment, or a demonstration that his asymmetry between religion and morality rests on a well-defended functional distinction—would cause you to abandon the central framing. Identify where your reasoning is most likely to fail: probably in overreading Darwin’s occasional remarks as a systematic philosophical position rather than as exploratory scientific speculations, and in assuming that consistency is the right standard for a thinker working across descriptive and normative registers without modern philosophical discipline.
