In the ethics of infinite populations, standard aggregation rules—whether totalist, average, or critical-level—yield indeterminate or paradoxical orderings once the domain includes actually or potentially infinite sets of moral subjects. Some recent formal work has proposed importing logical constraints to discipline these orderings; specifically, one might require omega-consistency for a moral aggregation rule, meaning that if every finite initial segment of an infinite population sequence receives a particular welfare ranking, the limit ranking over the full sequence should preserve that ordering unless a principled, non-arbitrary discontinuity is explicitly justified.
