I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar on hinge epistemology — specifically Wittgensteins On Certainty and the commentary that came after. I want a taxonomy tree (not a list of philosophers) that sorts positions by what they actually say about hinge commitments, not by the label they wear.

Start with this root split: do hinge commitments have a distinctive epistemic status at all, or are they just ordinary beliefs we're unusually stuck on?

Then split on three further questions that should come in any order that makes logical sense:
1. Are hinges propositional (the kind of thing that can be true or false) or non-propositional — closer to rules of language, practical norms, or something else?
2. If a hinge can be revised, is the revision always collective or systemic, or can a single hinge fall on its own without dragging the rest?
3. Are hinges globally uniform for all humans, or are they locally or domain-specific?

At the leaves, place the named positions that actually sit there: Wittgenstein's own groundless-certainty view, Pritchard-style epistemic hinges, Coliva's normative/practical reading, Moyal-Sharrock's non-propositional account, and any local/contextualist view you think belongs. For any combination of answers that lands empty, label it "unnamed" and say in one sentence why it's empty — incoherent, unmotivated, or just nobody's bothered. Also flag any pair of leaves your tree can't cleanly separate; I have a hunch Wittgenstein's view and some readings of Pritchard blur together on revisability, but tell me if that's wrong or if there are other overlaps.

Please phrase every branch node as a concrete question someone could answer without already knowing which philosopher it points to. Don't ask "do you agree with Coliva?" — ask what kind of thing a hinge is and how it behaves.

Also, separate what's actually settled in the literature (for instance, that hinges aren't justified by ordinary empirical evidence) from what's genuinely contested (whether they're beliefs, norms, or neither; whether they ever genuinely change; whether they're universal). Don't present a contested interpretation as if it's the consensus view. And no citations or reading lists — I need this for people who can't search.
