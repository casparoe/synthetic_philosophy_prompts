I've got a 20-min slot at a social epistemology workshop next month. The audience is mostly people who study echo chambers and filter bubbles as democratic failures — they cite the usual suspects, think exposure to disagreement is a civic duty, and view algorithmic curation as a kind of epistemic polution. My thesis is that this is wrongheaded: if you model belief-formation like a Kelly bettor managing an information portfolio, then restricting your diet to consonant sources is often geometrically rational, not pathological. I'm calling it 'epistemic portfolio theory' — the idea that agents maximize long-run growth of justified credence by betting their attention budget on high-confidence updates and avoiding low-expected-value disagreement.
