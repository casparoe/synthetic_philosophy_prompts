In philosophy-of-history seminars and in the literature I have followed, the disagreement over Fernand Braudel’s longue durée has settled into a stalemate that feels more conceptual than archival. One camp treats geographical, climatic and demographic structures—the slow rhythms of environment, population cycles, and spatial constraint—as genuinely explanatory of particular historical events, arguing that a Mediterranean trade route or a mountain society’s persistence is unintelligible without them. The opposing camp insists that because these structures remain effectively invariant across the temporal span of any single event or decision, they fail standard counterfactual tests for causation; at best they supply descriptive framing or boundary conditions, and attributing causal power to them confuses background conditions with agents. Both sides accept the same descriptions of how institutions and practices persist across political ruptures, and both agree that human action occurs within these frames, yet neither has moved the other in decades. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is there a hidden empirical question about whether multiscale causal models can render invariant structures counterfactually relevant, or is the dispute fundamentally about competing norms for historical explanation—whether intelligibility requires proximity in temporal scale, or whether structural determination counts as explanation even when it never varies within the explanatory window? Might the sides be operating with different registers of “cause,” one counterfactual-explanatory and one narrative-integrative, so that they answer different questions under one name? Or is the impasse a sign that the question is ill-posed, because “explanation” in historiography is not unitary? Given your diagnosis, what should count as progress: a formal distinction between enabling and triggering conditions applied to slow structures, a reconceptualisation of counterfactual dependence for background invariants, or an argument that no resolution is possible because the disagreement is bedrock about what history is for?
