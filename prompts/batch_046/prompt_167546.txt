I'm writing a seminar paper in philosophy of social ontology on collective intentionality, and I want to move beyond the standard face-to-face cases—painting a house together, walking in step—that dominate the literature. Give me a concrete example of a conceptual issue in joint action: specifically, construct a case of apparent collective achievement in which the contributors lack both temporal co-presence and direct mutual recognition, yet the result displays the integrated, goal-directed coherence usually taken to require shared intention. The scenario should be specific enough to analyze philosophically but should not depend on the mechanics of any actual real-world platform or technology. In laying out the case, identify precisely where mainstream accounts that require common knowledge or mutual responsiveness come under pressure, and where an alternative allowing for distributed or 'latent' collective intentionality might apply. Please clearly distinguish what is settled in the field—such as the importance of mutual awareness in canonical examples—from what is genuinely contested, particularly whether mutual awareness is a necessary condition for we-intentions or merely a frequent feature, and do not present any contested position as the consensus view.
