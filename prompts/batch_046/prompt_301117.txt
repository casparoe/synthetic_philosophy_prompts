For a graduate seminar of about 14 students in philosophy of physics, meeting for 90 minutes, design a simulation game that lets participants feel why Maxwell's demon cannot violate the second law if information is physical. Rather than the familiar Landauer-erasure framing, build the mechanics around logical reversibility: demons can observe and gate-sort fast and slow tokens (representing molecules) using a computation that is fully reversible, but every observation must be recorded on a physical slip of paper that is never discarded. Structure the session in three 25-minute rounds with roles — Demons (2-3 students), Molecules (moving tokens), and Auditors (tracking energy budgets) — plus a 15-minute debrief. Give the Demons an incentive: they score one point for every net temperature gradient they create between two chambers. Provide them with gate-opening rules and a simple binary record-keeping sheet. The constraint: paper slips accumulate in a visible stack; once the stack reaches a predetermined height (say 20 slips), the Demons must either halt all sorting or physically erase slips, with each erased slip costing exactly one point — the energy equivalent of the gradient they created. Include explicit rules for how Molecules move randomly between rounds and how Auditors verify that no hidden energy source was used. The debrief should map the paper-stack crisis onto the argument that irreversibility enters not at the moment of measurement or sorting, but only when information is destroyed, and should ask the group whether a demon with infinite memory could run forever. Add an honest facilitator note: the activity reproduces the informational cost structure but cannot reproduce the statistical-mechanical derivation of the Landauer bound or the quantum dynamics of erasure, which must be supplied verbally after the game. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or assigned readings in the design.
