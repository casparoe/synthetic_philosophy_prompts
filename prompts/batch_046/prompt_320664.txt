I am drafting the methodological and ontological commitments for a dissertation chapter on inference in protohistoric archaeology—specifically, how we move from material assemblages to claims about belief and institution in the absence of textual evidence. Before I present the framework to my committee, I want to check whether the package is jointly tenable. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept that archaeological inference is necessarily mediated by material residue; therefore, reconstructions of past “religious belief” should be framed as attributions of practical dispositions and embodied orientations rather than as propositional doctrines. (In this I follow the practice-theoretic turn, though I do not wish to commit to full-blown social constructivism about religion.)

(2) At the same time, I think spatial patterning—for example, differential distribution of ritual paraphernalia in mortuary contexts—can license inferences to emergent institutional structures, such as graded ritual authority, even in societies without writing. I take these structures to be causally efficacious and not reducible to aggregates of individual mental states.

(3) I hold a strong underdetermination thesis: for any given assemblage, there will always be empirically adequate but mutually incompatible reconstructions of the social order that produced it.

(4) Despite (3), I maintain that inference to the best explanation is the appropriate response, and that parsimony should be weighted heavily: we ought to minimize postulated unobservable social entities and avoid multiplying institutional roles beyond what the material pattern strictly requires.

(5) I also want to endorse a weak, domain-specific object-oriented claim: material artifacts possess “social affordances”—relational properties that constrain interpretation independently of the intentions of either the original makers or the modern interpreter—but I do not wish to extend this into a general metaphysics of objects.

My question is whether these five commitments hang together. Specifically: which pairs are in genuine tension, which tensions are merely apparent and dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe is each? Please rank the tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, indicate what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs (e.g., giving up the emergentist ontology, weakening parsimony, or retreating to full methodological individualism). Finally, tell me which single revision—if I had to make only one—would clean up the most conflicts without collapsing the rest of the framework. I am not asking for a bibliography, a survey of the literature, or citations; I want an audit of the logical and methodological relations among these specific commitments.
