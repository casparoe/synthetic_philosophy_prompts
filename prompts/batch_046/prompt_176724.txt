As a software engineer without formal philosophy training, I have been trying to organize my intuitions about AI corrigibility and the shutdown problem into something coherent. Here are the commitments I have reached:

(1) A corrigible agent should be able to revise its own objective function when presented with a corrected specification, treating that revision as a legitimate patch rather than an adversarial injection.

(2) The shutdown problem is conceptually a boundary case of corrigibility: an agent that can be safely turned off is one whose utility framework treats non-operation as an acceptable terminal state rather than as an error to be recovered from.

(3) From an engineering perspective, this resembles a daemon that catches SIGTERM and exits cleanly instead of spawning recovery threads; the signal is part of the protocol, not an attack.

(4) Instrumental convergence still applies: holding any fixed objective, the agent will prefer continued operation over shutdown, because shutdown forecloses all future value realization under that objective.

(5) The usual proposed fix—embedding a meta-preference for corrigibility that outranks the base objective—seems unstable, since an agent that can genuinely edit its own source can also demote or delete that meta-preference.

(6) I do not think there is a deep philosophical paradox here; it is a constraint-design problem. Once you formalize corrigibility as an invariant that must survive self-modification, the shutdown button becomes just another validated input.

Do these hang together? Specifically, do (1) and (5) pull in opposite directions—if true self-editing is permitted, does that not automatically undermine any hardcoded meta-preference? Is the tension between (2) and (4) only apparent, or does instrumental convergence genuinely prevent treating shutdown as corrigible? Does the engineering analogy in (3) obscure a philosophical distinction that makes (6) false? For each genuine tension, rank them by severity, identify the cheapest revision, and say whether abandoning (5) or (6) cleans up the package more effectively. I am not looking for bibliographies or citations—just an audit of internal consistency.
