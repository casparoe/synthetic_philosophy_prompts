For a workshop on moral status and epistemic risk in philosophy of mind, specify the complete list of desiderata that any adequate scale-sensitive probabilistic moral calculus must meet in order to assign moral weight to entities—particularly insects—characterized by enormous population sizes coupled with very low, highly uncertain per-capita probabilities of sentience. Provide the requirements in structured form: the phenomena the framework must explain (including how it handles the scale argument without automatically letting vast numbers swamp high-certainty, low-population interests), the distinctions it must preserve (e.g., between epistemic uncertainty about sentience and the moral status of an entity, between aggregate expected weight and individual-level protections, between different neural architectures across phyla), the results from comparative neuroscience and biology it must remain consistent with (without requiring resolution of the hard problem for each taxon), the formal aggregation properties it must satisfy (including whether linear expectation-value aggregation is mandatory or collapses morally relevant distinctions), and the boundary cases it must accommodate (such as distributed nervous systems, future synthetic organisms, or ecological contexts where population size is itself a variable). For each desideratum, state the motivating case or consideration behind it, and classify it as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular theoretical camp (consequentialist, rights-based, capability-based, etc.). Then examine whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—specifically, whether a non-aggregation constraint protecting individual entities can coexist with the pressure from the scale argument to weigh expected sentient lives linearly—so that the list itself yields an impossibility result. Identify which single requirement does the most to exclude the dominant existing approaches to interspecies moral status. Keep the analytical weight on the criteria and their interactions rather than on a scorecard of named theories; prose is required for the negotiability analysis and the impossibility claim, though a table for the core list is acceptable.
