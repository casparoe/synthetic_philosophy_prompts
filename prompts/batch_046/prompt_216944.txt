ok so i've been noticing this thing in conversations about career and habits, and i'm wondering if philosophy/psychology already has a precise name for it. people will frame their own current limitations as deep fixed traits — 'i'm just not a disciplined person,' 'i've never been good with conflict,' 'numbers have never been my thing' — speaking as if they discovered these facts about themselves rather than made choices that led here. but then in the same breath they'll tell a friend who struggles with the exact same thing that they just need to push through or change their mindset. it's not plain hypocrisy, because when you gently suggest they could choose differently tomorrow they get genuinely anxious and start listing reasons why it's different for them — upbrining, brain chemistry, 'just who i am.' yet they don't extend that same deterministic charity to anyone else. is this just Sartre-style bad faith, or is there a more specific concept for this one-sided essentialism where you lock yourself in to avoid the weight of radical freedom but keep other people fully accountable? if there's no established term, can you define its edges: how is it different from the fundamental attribution error, from self-handicapping, and from ordinary excuse-making? i definately won't be satisfied with just a label — i want to know exactly what the phenomenon is and where it stops being that and becomes something else.
