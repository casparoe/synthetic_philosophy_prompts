I'm co-curating a small gallery show with two partners: one Dutch, one Japanese. The Dutch partner evaluates submissions with unvarnished directness—last week she told an artist in front of us that his piece was conceptually incoherent and should be withdrawn. The Japanese partner never delivers negative judgments outright; instead he offers adjustments—"perhaps the pedestal should be lower so the work's humility is visible"—which we all understand to mean the work isn't ready. I recently told a mutual friend that her rejected painting was "lovely but perhaps not for this particular wall," meaning I lied to protect her feelings. Now she has learned the Dutch partner called it weak, and she is hurt not just by the rejection but by my gentle deception, which she says made her feel patronized. Meanwhile, the Japanese partner is distressed that the direct confrontation violated an unspoken agreement to preserve dignity. I need to decide how to respond: should I defend my white lie as kindness, ask the Dutch partner to adopt indirectness, or concede that both my lie and her bluntness failed? What is actually wrong with each of our behaviors, if anything?
