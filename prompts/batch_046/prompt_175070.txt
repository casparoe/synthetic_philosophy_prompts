I've been thinking about whether morality is really objective after learning that people in Western countries seem to weight harm and fairness far more heavily than loyalty, authority, or purity compared with people elsewhere in the world. Here is roughly where I've landed: (1) I believe there are objective moral facts—for instance, that torturing an innocent person for fun is wrong regardless of anyone's opinion; (2) I think the cross-cultural differences show that gut moral reactions are shaped by culture and evolutionary history, so Western-style intuitions shouldn't be treated as automatically correct; (3) I still think reflective equilibrium is the right way to think about ethics, adjusting our principles and our intuitions together until they cohere; (4) I believe that where cultures converge—say, on prohibitions against arbitrary killing—those agreements point toward real moral truths, but where they diverge, the culture that downplays harm is simply mistaken rather than expressing an equally valid morality; (5) I don't think evolutionary explanations debunk all moral belief, only the culturally variable parts, because universal patterns are too stable to be mere local evolutionary accidents; and (6) I think moral knowledge works something like map-making—different societies draw different local maps, but agreement on major landmarks reveals a real territory, while disagreements are just cartographic errors. Do these commitments hang together? I'm especially unsure whether it's consistent to rely on reflective equilibrium when my own WEIRD-shaped intuitions are precisely the ones I've said are debunked, and whether I can use cross-cultural agreement as evidence for objectivity without circularly defining the true moral facts as whatever people happen to agree on. There's also the question of whether saving universal patterns from evolutionary debunking is consistent with debunking the local ones—maybe that tension dissolves once the evolutionary story is properly restricted, but I can't tell. Please audit my position: rank the genuine tensions by severity, say which ones are only apparent and why, indicate the least costly revision for each real conflict, and tell me which single change would clean up the most problems without forcing me to abandon moral realism entirely.
