After a long and somewhat frustrating argument last night with a friend who insisted that Nietzsche’s remarks on diet, climate, and digestion are merely biographical curiosities with no philosophical weight, I want to design a teaching plan for a ninety-minute session with a reading group of literature graduate students who have encountered some of his middle-period work but have no formal background in philosophy of biology or nineteenth-century physiology. The concept I need them to grasp is not a list of his personal habits, but the claim that physiological regimen—specifically the agonistic management of digestion and environment—is continuous with his critique of asceticism and central to his idea of great health. To keep the session from collapsing into biography, I am injecting a framing idea: that for Nietzsche the stomach functions as a kind of interpretive organ, so that dietary choice is not separate from intellectual work but is itself an expression of one’s interpretive character and spiritual mobility.

Please lay out the teaching plan rather than deliver the lecture itself. I need the dependency order in which the components must be introduced, with explicit justification for why the distinction between Christian ascetic denial and Nietzschean self-cultivation must come before any talk of specific menus or climates; the one concrete carrying case—likely his contrast between the digestive sluggishness he associates with the sedentary German scholarly milieu and the lightness and rapid turnover he associates with the free spirit—and what properties this case must possess (it must show interpretation occurring at a physiological level) and what it must avoid (it must not be read as a universal dietary prescription); the predictable misconceptions the audience will bring or form (for example, that he is advocating a universal diet for all thinkers, that he reduces thought to crude biological materialism, that his own chronic illness renders his view self-undermining) and the precise point in the sequence where each should be defused; a brief check for understanding that shows the idea has landed without asking anyone to memorize specific meals, letters, or medical details; and what I should openly refuse to explain at this level and say so—such as the full details of his correspondence about meals, or the specific physiological literature he was consulting.

Please present the plan with clear structure. Also, state how confident you are in what you claim and single out the one point about which you are least certain.
