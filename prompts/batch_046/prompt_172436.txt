Question: Lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism holds that suffering takes absolute lexical priority over pleasure only when suffering exceeds a specific intensity cutoff; below that cutoff the two are treated as commensurable. Explain why this doesnt collapse into ordinary lexical negative utilitarianism, and discuss whether the commensurability problem is genuinely solved or merely relocated to the cutoff itself.

Student answer:
The threshold view differs from standard lexical NU because standard lexical NU gives suffering unconditional priority at every intensity, whereas the threshold version only activates that priority in the high-suffering zone. Once suffering drops below the cutoff, we can weigh small pains against large pleasures using ordinary aggregation, which standard lexical NU never allows. So the cutoff is a conditional gate, not an unconditional rule. This resolves the commensurability problem because below the line we have a common metric for comparison, and above the line comparison is moot since suffering dominates. The idea was introduced by Karl Popper in The Open Society but he abandoned it; the threshold fix was added later by critics who wanted to salvage some positive goods.
