You are grading an exam for an upper-level philosophy-of-history course. The exam presented a conceptual distinction—agent-blame, which assigns moral responsibility to intentional actors for culpable choices, versus structure-blame, which assigns responsibility to institutional or systemic arrangements that make harmful outcomes probable regardless of individual intent—and asked students to apply it to the First World War origins debate. The exam question read: “Can one consistently maintain that the German state bears structural responsibility for the outbreak of war in 1914 even if Clark is largely correct that no single leader intended a full continental conflict? Defend your answer, and explain how this interacts with Fischer’s attribution of deliberate intent to Berlin’s decision-makers.”

Student answer:

“Yes, structural responsibility can be assigned to Germany independently of Clark’s distributed-agent account. Fischer demonstrated that figures like Bethmann Hollweg and Moltke deliberately sought a localized Balkan conflict that they knew might escalate, which establishes clear agent-blame in Berlin. Clark, however, shows that Russia, France, and Britain all made fatal miscalculations—Russia mobilized prematurely, France encouraged firmness, Britain sent mixed signals—so agent-blame should be spread across all capitals rather than concentrated in Germany. Turning to structure-blame: the Schlieffen Plan was an institutional commitment to rapid offensive mobilization that made escalation structurally inevitable once any crisis began. Because this doctrine was uniquely German, Germany alone bears structural blame for creating an institutional architecture that eliminated diplomatic alternatives, even if Clark is right that leaders did not personally intend continental war. This means Fischer’s agent-blame applies to specific Berlin decision-makers, while Clark’s systemic view is accommodated by distributing agent-blame elsewhere and assigning Germany unique structural responsibility for its war-plan design.
