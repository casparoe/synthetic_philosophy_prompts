Exam question: Define indirect normativity and distinguish it from direct normative assessment applied to mental states. Then analyze whether an agent in conditions of “deliberative transparency”—complete second-order access to the causal architecture of her own belief-forming processes—remains subject to indirect norms regarding her reasoning procedures. In particular, address whether transparency eliminates “procedural shadowing,” the recursive gap between a governing norm and the mechanism that enforces it.

Student answer: Indirect normativity governs outcomes an agent cannot immediately will but can shape over time through practice, attention, or environmental design, whereas direct normativity applies to actions immediately within voluntary control. Under deliberative transparency, the agent perceives exactly how her reasoning operates, which dissolves procedural shadowing: there is no hidden mechanism standing between the norm and its application. Consequently, indirect norms over belief formation collapse into direct norms, since she can now immediately adjust any cognitive parameter. All that remains are instrumental norms of efficiency—maximizing true beliefs with minimal effort. Thus idealized deliberation resolves the tension between autonomy and shadowing by erasing the indirect category for mental processes entirely.
