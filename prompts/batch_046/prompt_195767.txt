After a long argument with a friend who insists that phenomenal properties are irreducible first-person realities, I am presenting at a philosophy-of-mind seminar the view that qualia are conceptually incoherent rather than merely mysterious: phenomenal concepts function as indexical tracking devices, not as referring expressions for intrinsic, causally inert properties, so the standard quining problem collapses into a category mistake. The audience will include a Chalmers-style phenomenal realist, a physicalist who accepts phenomenal properties, a cognitive scientist who treats introspective reports as direct evidence, and an eliminativist who thinks I am granting qualia too much legitimacy. Prepare me for the objections in the order they are likely to arise, phrased in the vocabulary each faction uses, with what I should concede up front, what I must hold, the weakest point in each reply, and the one question — likely about blindsight or the transparency of phenomenal experience — that I am hoping no one asks, together with a concise reply in case it does.
