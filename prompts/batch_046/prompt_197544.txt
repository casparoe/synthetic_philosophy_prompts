Having retired from experimental physics and turned recently to philosophy of history, I have assembled the following commitments about explanation and causation, and I want to know whether they form a coherent package or whether I am helping myself to incompatible motives. Here is where I stand.

(1) I hold that adequate historical explanation is irreducibly multi-causal; any account that isolates a single factor as the explanation is necessarily a distortion, because the phenomenon in question was produced by a conjunctive set of conditions none of which was sufficient on its own.

(2) I accept that overdetermination is widespread in history: for many significant events, the removal of any one contributing condition would not have altered the outcome.

(3) Even in overdetermined cases, I think it is correct, not merely permissible, to cite each contributing factor as a genuine cause, and I reject the view that any one of them therefore did not really matter or can be dismissed as redundant.

(4) I am inclined to apply Wittgenstein's idea of family resemblance to the concept of cause in history: there is no single essential feature shared by all historical causes, only a network of overlapping similarities.

(5) At the same time, I worry that without some essential criterion of causation, we lack the resources to distinguish genuine overdetermination from mere accidental correlation among redundant background conditions.

(6) More speculatively, I have begun to think that causal weight might be modeled analogously to quantum superposition—each factor contributes an amplitude that does not reduce to a classical probability, and the collapse into a specific causal narrative occurs only in the act of historical explanation, not in the past itself. On this view, overdetermination is not a competition among discrete, fully determined causes but an interference pattern prior to narration.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which are only apparently so, and how severe is each conflict? For each real tension, indicate the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what it costs me. Then tell me which single commitment, if dropped or modified, would clean up the largest number of conflicts. Be specific about whether my quantum-superposition analogy in (6) undermines my commitment to discrete citation in (3) or my family-resemblance view in (4), and whether (5) is a problem for (4) or a separate difficulty altogether. I am not asking for a survey of historiography, but for a diagnosis of whether my own positions hang together.
