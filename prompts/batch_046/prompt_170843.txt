I work in software engineering and have no formal background in philosophy, but I have been working through some population-ethics puzzles in my spare time after a long discussion with colleagues. I encountered what is called the procreation asymmetry: it is said to be wrong to bring a person into existence if their life will contain suffering, but it is not wrong to refrain from bringing a person into existence even if their life would have been happy, despite the fact that in both scenarios a particular individual ends up not existing. I tried to formalize this as a counterfactual evaluation problem, comparing a world-state in which the person exists with welfare W against a baseline state in which they do not exist. For the suffering case, it seems we can say the person is made worse off relative to non-existence, because there is an actual subject whose welfare is negative. But then the parallel move for the happy case should also work: we should be able to say that a potential happy person is made worse off by not being brought into existence, since their welfare in the counterfactual world is high and in the actual world it is absent. The asymmetry seems to block this second comparison while allowing the first, which looks like an inconsistent branching condition — as if the evaluation logic applies a different baseline rule depending on the sign of W.

Is the error in my framing that existence and non-existence are not comparable nodes in the same state graph, or is there a deeper reason why harm can be anchored to a non-existent baseline but deprivation cannot? I keep thinking of it like rollback logic in distributed systems, where we compare a deployed failing instance against its absence, but we do not penalize the absence of a healthy instance — yet philosophers seem to defend the asymmetry as coherent rather than treating it as a broken symmetry in the moral calculus. Where exactly does the analogy to system-state comparison fail, and does the asymmetry survive once we stop treating potential and actual welfare as entries in the same ledger?
