Hey, so I'm dealing with this messy situation on my local community board and I can't tell if I'm overthinking it or if there's an actual philosophical problem hiding in here. About six months ago our neighborhood recieved a big grant from an old family foundation—serious money to fix up the park and build a new rec center. The catch is the family made their fortune running a textile mill that relied on forced labor from a displaced group about two generations back. My own grandfather worked there as a floor supervisor, not an owner, but he definitely knew what was going on and kept quiet about it. Now part of the grant requires us to put up a memorial plaque listing the "founding benefactors" with his name included, and also that we keep the family name on the building. If we turn down the plaque, the money doesn't just disappear—it gets redirected to a private heritage museum that maintains the mill owner's estate and tells a pretty sanitized version of the story. So it's not like we can just reject the funding and wash our hands of it.

Some people on the board say we should take the cash and build the center anyway, because the families living here now—including descendants of that displaced group—need the facilities, and none of us personally did anything wrong. But a friend of mine keeps arguing that there's a real difference between personal blame and being part of a collective that still benefits from an unjust structure; she says putting up the plaque isn't just accepting money, it's actively renewing the narrative that makes the original harm look acceptable. I keep going back and forth. If I vote yes, am I endorsing the wrong in some moral sense, or is it just practical? And does it even make sense to talk about a town or a board carrying something like guilt when everyone who was actually involved is dead? I don't want generic life advice—I'm trying to figure out whether groups can actually inherit moral burdens across generations, or if that's just a confused way of mixing up responsibility with something else.
