I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about hypocrisy and whether it kills your standing to blame people. I've been wondering if there's a real difference between token-hypocrisy — failing at the exact same thing in the same context — and type-hypocrisy — failing at something in the same general category but in a totally different situation — and whether only the first kind actually removes standing. Here are my gut verdicts, mostly consistent except for one pair that messes me up.

My roommate is chronically late to our study group but snapped at me last week for being five minutes late to the same group. Verdict: pure token-hypocrisy, he has zero standing, their just being a hypocrite. My aunt never learned to drive and still tells me I'm reckless when I speed. Verdict: not token-related, so she still has standing; I shouldn't dismiss her. The local politician who voted for stricter tax bills, then got caught hiding income, and now criticizes corporations for tax avoidance — verdict: she's a hypocrite and lost her standing, though maybe partly just because she's a criminal. My friend who was alcohol-addicted, relapsed twice last year, and now urges our other friend to enter rehab — verdict: he has standing, maybe more because he's been in the trenches.

But here's where I keep going back and forth. My sister uses ChatGPT to draft her work emails all the time, but she blew up at me for using it for part of my philosphy essay. Is that token or type? If I say she lost standing, then what about my coworker who drives an SUV but criticizes people for using plastic straws? That also feels hypocritical, but I don't want to say she can't blame straw-users. And then there's the professor who was fired from her last job for plagiarism and now grades student papers, docking points for plagiarism — institutional authority vs personal failure. So is there a principle — maybe about shared vulnerability, being in the same moral pool, or token vs type — that captures all of these? Or do I just have to accept my verdict on the sister is inconsistent with the aunt case? Specifically, I want to know whether I should overturn my intuition that the roommate has no standing but the aunt does, and what that principle says about the sister/AI case and the professor/plagiarism one.
