Please argue for the position that Benjamin Franklin's well-known project of moral perfection—centered on a list of thirteen virtues tracked in a weekly ledger—fails not through a mere deficit of willpower, but because the project contains an internal contradiction: the virtues are interdependent in such a way that the full realization of any one (e.g., Order, Industry) structurally undermines the full realization of others (e.g., Humility, Silence, or Moderation), rendering simultaneous perfection conceptually impossible rather than merely empirically elusive. Ground your argument in the hierarchical arrangement Franklin assigned to the virtues and in his documented admission, across decades of practice, that he never achieved perfection in any of them. Then extend the argument by treating Franklin's ledger as a proto-quantified-self protocol applied to morality, and show that the reflexive measurement of virtue for instrumental self-improvement necessarily converts intrinsic moral dispositions into strategically managed performances—thereby introducing a performative corruption that makes the very state of moral perfection incompatible with the monitoring method required to pursue it. Keep the reasoning self-contained; do not ask for citations, editions, or further reading, and avoid treating the failure as purely psychological.
