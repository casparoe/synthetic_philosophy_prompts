I've been running a small discussion group on anthropic reasoning — mostly grad students in philosophy of science and one stubborn physics postdoc — and we've hit a wall with the self-sampling vs self-indication thing. You know, Bostrom's doomsday argument framing, the presumptuous philosopher who thinks it's crazy to be surprised we're in a big universe, all that. We've spent maybe six sessions on whether SSA is just Bayes with an observer index, or whether SIA is actually required to avoid circularity when you update on your own existence. The physics guy keeps saying it's all just wrong priors and we should stop pretending it's metaphysics; the philosophy people say the priors are exactly whats at issue. Nobody budges.

Lately I've started wondering if part of the stall is that we're treating 'observer' as a discrete binary thing — you're either in the reference class or not — when maybe the real disagreement hides in how you handle continuous or graded cases. Like, if consciousness comes in degrees (not that I have a theory of that, just as a framing) and the number of observer-moments depends on where you draw the boundary, then SSA and SIA might be giving different answers not because of deep probabilistic disagreement but because they're implicitly using different resolution thresholds for the sampling frame. I don't know if that makes sense, but I threw it out there and the group just looked at me.

So my actual question: is this debate stalled because it's a hidden factual question about how to construct the referance class, or is it a verbal dispute about what 'rational prior' means, or are we just staring at bedrock intuitions about whether finding yourself alive should feel surprising? And specifically, if the gradient-observer framing is the real issue, is there any conceptual distinction — maybe about indexical vs third-person uncertainty, or about what counts as a model prediction — that could actually break the impasse? Or should we just admit that SSA and SIA are answering different questions (one about prediction, one about metaphysical probability) and stop expecting convergence?
