Compare two interpretations of Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi (the four-cornered negation): the logical-formalist reading, which treats the tetralemma as an exhaustive logical partition that Madhyamaka emptiness undermines by rejecting bivalence and opening space for a paraconsistent middle, and the dialectical-therapeutic reading, which denies that the four corners constitute a logical framework at all and instead treats each as a prasaṅga-style reductio that dismantles any ascription of svabhāva. In particular, assess whether these readings are genuinely incompatible or whether they can be reconciled by separating metaphysical commitment from soteriological method, and identify which interpretation leaves Madhyamaka more exposed to the charge of self-refutation. Please separate what is well-supported in Nāgārjuna's root verses from what belongs to modern reconstruction, distinguish settled interpretive points from genuinely contested ones among people who work on this material, and do not present either reading as the consensus view.
