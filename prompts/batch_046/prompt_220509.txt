I am drafting interview questions for a forthcoming episode of a philosophy podcast, and my guest is an unnamed contemporary philosopher who defends a rigorous form of panpsychism applied specifically to fundamental physics: she maintains that sentience is not an emergent property of complex organisms but is instantiated, in some primitive form, by basic physical entities themselves, and—crucially—that certain configurations of quantum fields or elementary interactions can realise states that are not merely information-bearing but genuinely suffering-like. I want to move well beyond a restatement of the view, so I need a sequence of questions that press precisely where the existing literature presses. In particular, I require one question that asks how she distinguishes a morally significant, suffering-laden field configuration from a physically identical but phenomenologically null state, together with a follow-up keyed to her likely appeal to an intrinsic, non-relational quality of experience that cannot be detected from the outside. I also need a question confronting the multiplication of moral patients: if every cubic centimetre of space contains innumerable fundamental entities with some minimal sentience, does her ethics collapse into an intractable calculus of microscopic harms, and what is her follow-up reply likely to invoke—perhaps a combinatorial exclusion principle or a sharp threshold of integrated complexity?

Additionally, I want a question that makes her address the conditions of empirical vulnerability. She has a tendency to deflect objections from physics or neuroscience by remarking that they are premature given our current understanding of consciousness; I need a question that asks what specific finding—say, a complete reduction of subjective report to non-conscious information processing at the neural level, or a demonstration that quantum coherence plays no causal role in cognition—would genuinely weaken her ethical claim rather than merely being dismissed as science not yet caught up with philosophy, and I want the follow-up to anticipate that deflection and force her to specify the evidential standard in advance. Finally, please include a closing question that connects her micro-level pan-sentience to the macro-level problem of other minds: if consciousness is ubiquitous at the fundamental level, why do we not attribute it to tables or rocks, and does whatever principle she uses to deny macro-consciousness to aggregates also undermine her claim that fundamental entities are moral patients in the first place? The sequence should be demanding but collegial, assuming familiarity with the combination problem and with standard objections from physicalism, and it should avoid any request for bibliographies or recommended reading.
