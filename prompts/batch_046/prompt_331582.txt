The form should be strict: nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a quatrain, with two refrains alternating according to the classic A1-b-A2 / a-b-A1 pattern and finally meeting in the closing couplet. Use iambic pentameter or a loose iambic rhythm, and do not let rhyme substitute for structural meaning.

The philosophical content should do the formal work. The first refrain should voice apparent multiplicity—something about measurement outcomes branching into seemingly separate observers—while the second should voice the monistic claim that all such branching is the single awareness disguising itself. Between them, develop the image of quantum decoherence not as proof of the Vedantic view but as structurally resonant with it: distinct outcomes emerge and persist for practical purposes, yet lack ultimate separation. The compulsive return of the refrains should mirror the Vedantic concept of ajnana—the ignorance or illusion that projects plurality onto what is fundamentally one—without turning the poem into either a mystical sermon or a physics lecture. By the envoi, the two refrains should stand side by side, leaving open whether the unity is metaphysically prior or only retrospectively reconstructed after each apparent split. It should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Schrödinger’s essays on consciousness and with basic Advaita Vedanta recognizes the conceptual moves rather than mere vocabulary.

Please write in American English, keep the tone serious but not overloaded with jargon, and do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations, since this is for a live seminar.
