Write a dialogue between a defender of the user-illusion theory of phenomenal consciousness—who holds that first-person experience is a virtual interface representation rather than direct acquaintance with mental properties—and a direct phenomenal realist who maintains that introspection tracks genuinely real phenomenal qualities. Have them debate whether the illusionist can explain accurate introspective reports without positing a homuncular reader of the interface, using anosognosia for paralysis as a shared test case. Deliver the dialogue as a numbered list of exactly five exchanges, each consisting of one or two sentences and containing direct responses from both voices, with no introduction or conclusion. Clearly distinguish settled philosophical points from genuinely contested ones, and do not present a contested view as consensus.
