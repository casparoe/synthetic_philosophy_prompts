Please draft a satirical parody—approximately 650 words, in the deadpan technical register of an enterprise software release-notes document—of a hypothetical version update for what the document terms the phenomenal user interface, i.e., the layer of subjective experience treated by some philosophers as a functional but ontologically lightweight user illusion over unconscious cognitive processes. The target of the satire should be the reductionist or eliminativist tendency to explain away first-person consciousness as a misleading interface skin with no serious backend reality, particularly the move of treating qualitative experience—qualia, self-continuity, affective urgency—as deprecated or patchable UI elements rather than phenomena requiring genuine explanation. Your piece should exaggerate the practical and ethical absurdities that result from taking this metaphor literally: for example, bug fixes that suppress the phenomenology of pain, feature removals that discontinue the sense of personal identity across updates, and support responses that dismiss customer complaints about the hard problem as unsupported legacy-code issues. The humor must derive from the actual philosophical content—an informed reader should recognize that the author understands the real position, namely that consciousness is not an inner theater but a kind of virtual representation generated by unconscious machinery—and the document should remain accessible to a reader without specialist training. Maintain sustained, deadpan irony throughout: do not break character, do not concede that the unconscious backend is genuinely conscious, and avoid winking at the reader. Conclude in a way that leaves the reader feeling the unresolved tension between treating consciousness as mere interface design and the undeniable first-person weight of lived experience.
