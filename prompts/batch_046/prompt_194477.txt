You are an expert philosopher of bioethics with particular familiarity with reproductive ethics, the non-identity problem, and the therapy-enhancement distinction. I am a doctoral student in philosophy of medicine preparing for a workshop on liberal eugenics and procreative obligation, and I need you to produce a rigorous, essay-length analysis that I can use to structure my own thinking. Before you begin drafting, please take a moment to brainstorm the central conceptual tensions: first, the relationship between Julian Savulescu's principle of procreative beneficence and the liberal-eugenic commitment to state neutrality in reproductive choice; second, the challenge that Derek Parfit's non-identity problem poses to the very idea of benefiting a particular future child through selective reproduction; and third, the way in which heritable but non-genetic modifications—specifically, a hypothetical safe and inexpensive epigenetic intervention that reduces transgenerational stress-vulnerability without altering DNA sequence—might blur or reinforce the boundary between therapy and enhancement.

Write an essay that addresses this controversy in detail. Your essay should weigh the arguments for treating procreative beneficence as a strong moral duty (grounded in the idea that parents ought to maximize the expected welfare of their offspring) against the arguments for treating it as, at best, a weak permission or even a conceptually confused aspiration (grounded in concerns about unconditional love, the open future, and the non-identity problem's dissolution of comparative harm). Crucially, do not remain non-committal: reach and defend a specific conclusion about whether procreative beneficence retains normative force when applied to epigenetic, rather than strictly genetic, forms of heritable intervention. In reaching your verdict, address whether the genetic lottery objection retains its moral weight when the intervention in question does not determine which genome is inherited but rather modifies the expression environment available to whatever genome is inherited. Your conclusion should be justified with clear reasoning rather than with appeals to authority or to empirical studies.
