At a seminar on early modern philosophy, two doctoral students disagreed about the precise scope of Descartes' cogito. (1) Maya argued that the argument establishes only the present occurrence of thinking — an epistemic foothold rather than a metaphysical discovery of a substantial self. She claimed that the grammatical subject in I think operates like the expletive subject in it is raining: a syntactic placeholder with no ontological commitment, so Descartes demonstrates merely that thought is happening, not that a res cogitans endures. (2) Leo replied that this eliminates the first-person irreducibly: Descartes concludes he is a thing that thinks, and if there is no subject behind the deception, the evil demon scenario remains unblocked. Isolated episodes of seeming-thought without an enduring thinker, Leo said, cannot provide the stable foundation the Meditations require. (3) Maya countered that Leo confuses grammatical form with metaphysical substance. She granted the cogito excludes total non-existence, but insisted it proves neither persistence nor thick substantiality — only that, for the duration of the thought-act, something non-extended is present. A thin stream of consciousness lacking bodily properties, she noted, satisfies Descartes' later claim that the self is known solely through thought. (4) Leo responded that such a stream is too fragile to support the rest of the project: the wax argument and the proof of God presuppose a continuous knower. Without at least a minimal enduring subject, the inference from I think now to I am a thinking thing dissolves into disconnected moments, undermining the reconstruction of knowledge. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, briefly justify each rating, and identify what both parties overlook regarding the relation between the cogito's epistemic function and its metaphysical commitments.
