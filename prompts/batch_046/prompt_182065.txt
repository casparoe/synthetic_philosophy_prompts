Hi there — I'm a journalist who usually covers science and ideas, but I also sit on the review panel for our university's community knowledge programme. We've got this awkward case and I need a proper memo drafted for the board meeting next tuesday.

A local residents' group — mostly ex-factory workers — wants funding to digitise oral histories about safety failures at a now-closed chemical plant. The university's risk office says we should reject it because the testimonies lack indepandant corroboration and could open us up to defamation claims. The residents say that demanding official-style corroboration is exactly the credibility deficit Miranda Fricker describes — testimonial injustice — because working-class knowledge about workplace harm is treated as less credible unless it matches records that were never kept in the first place. They also argue there's a hermeneutical gap: our grant's evidence framework was designed for quantitative epidemiology, not for narrative testimony about chronic exposure.

Half the panel thinks this is just risk management; half thinks we're reproducing epistemic injustice by applying the wrong evidentiary standard. I need you to draft the panel's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with reasons the residents and the risk office could actually respond to. Don't just list pros and cons; take a definite position and defend it, but be honest about the strongest point on the other side. No bibliographies or reading lists, please — just the reasoning. Write it in British English, since that's what the board uses. And I want a committed take, not a fence-sitting summary: give me a recommendation the panel can actually vote on.
