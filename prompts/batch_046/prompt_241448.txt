After retiring from experimental physics, I have begun studying metaphysics and find myself puzzled by a specific tension in David Lewis's modal realism. If worlds share no causal or spatiotemporal connections, it is unclear to me what makes such counterfactual reasoning about distant concrete worlds genuinely truth-tracking rather than merely imaginative projection. Please explain this conceptual issue: does Lewis's framework provide resources—such as counterpart relations or inter-world similarity metrics—that can ground scientific modal claims about physical constants, or does the strict causal isolation of worlds render those claims metaphysically unmoored? I want an intuitive but conceptually precise explanation, not a historical survey.
