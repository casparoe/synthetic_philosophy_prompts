I'm designing a handout for a philosophy-of-social-science seminar on the ontology of demographic cohorts—specifically whether labels like 'Baby Boomers,' 'Millennials,' and 'Gen Z' pick out real kinds or are merely convenient fictions. Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy that classifies positions on this question, built from the logical space of answers rather than from a catalog of named theorists.

The root node should pose this commitment: do macro-level conditions shared by people born within a given range (post-war economic expansion, widespread digital socialization, climate-related precarity) support explanations that genuinely cite 'the cohort' as a kind?

From there, split on three successive binary distinctions, each phrased as a concrete claim a student could endorse or reject without knowing the philosophical label:
(i) Are generational boundaries sharp—defined by necessary and sufficient conditions—or are they fuzzy, clustering by family resemblance?
(ii) Can the kind exist purely through objective demographic and structural conditions, or does it essentially depend on subjective self-identification and collective consciousness?
(iii) Is the cohort's explanatory work done primarily by material conditions (labor markets, technology access, housing costs), or by shared symbolic and interpretive frameworks (collective memory, generational narratives)?

The tree should yield at least six distinct leaf positions. Each leaf must carry either a standard label from social ontology or sociology (e.g., demographic realism, strong constructivism, statistical-artifact eliminativism) or the tag 'unnamed,' together with a one-sentence diagnosis of why that combination is unoccupied—whether because it is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored. You must explicitly include leaves for:
- the pure marketing-fiction view (no explanatory value whatsoever);
- the robust social-kind view (analogous to social class but bounded by birth cohort and shared history);
- the statistical-artifact view (cohorts track real underlying variables—technology diffusion, economic cycles—but possess no independent causal power).

In addition, flag any pair of leaves that this three-branch structure fails to separate, state what further distinction would be required to split them, and note whether that missing distinction is itself philosophically controversial or merely technical.
