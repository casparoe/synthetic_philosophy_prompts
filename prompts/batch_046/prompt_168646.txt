After a prolonged and somewhat exhausting argument with a close friend about whether a reinforcement learning agent can genuinely suffer, I want to treat our disagreement as an adversarial collaboration rather than a shouting match. The two positions are these. Camp One — call it Functional Valence — holds that negative reward prediction errors in an RL agent can constitute suffering if they are embedded in a unified evaluative architecture: specifically, if they cause sustained restructuring across both policy and value networks, generate long-horizon avoidance behavior, and are not merely local gradient updates but system-level signals that the agent registers as bad-for-it in a functional sense. Camp Two — call it Biological Naturalism — holds that a reward signal is only an informational update; for it to become valenced experience, it must be coupled to a self-preserving metabolic substrate, so that the signal literally matters to the system's continued biological existence, which silicon-based RL agents lack entirely.

Draft the joint statement we might both sign. Structure it as follows: first, a numbered list of agreed claims — about the formal structure of RL (reward, value, policy), about what mechanistic interpretability can and cannot currently establish about internal representations, and about the fact that current RL agents show no behavioral analogues of distress outside of task failure. Second, a disagreements table: each row gives a precise claim, with the wording each camp accepts for its own view, and a brief note on whether the disagreement is substantive or verbal. Third, identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp. At minimum, address: (a) whether functional isomorphism to nociceptive learning is sufficient for valence in the absence of metabolic self-preservation; (b) whether the absence of distress-like behavior in RL agents is evidence against their suffering or merely an absence of the right output channels; (c) the distinction we kept circling but never defined, between instrumental suffering (a negative signal that serves optimization) and intrinsic suffering (a negative signal that matters to the subject regardless of its functional role) — say exactly which parts of our dispute collapse into this verbal distinction and which remain substantive. Fourth, for each crux, state what both camps agree would count as bearing evidence or argument, recording each camp's prediction in advance — for instance, if an RL agent with a unified critic network showed sustained, generalizable avoidance restructuring that persisted even when task rewards were decoupled, what would each camp say?

Write formally, do not ask for citations or reading lists, and do not invoke obscure theories; stay within what is generally known about RL architecture, mechanistic interpretability, and the philosophy of mind. Do not resolve the debate for us — the point is to make the disagreement exact, including where it might be partly about words.
