Please design a single take-home essay exam for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on moral contractualism and institutional ethics. The exam is 90 minutes long; students should write approximately 800 words. The essay is worth 25 marks total.

The prompt must center on Scanlon’s contractualist framework—specifically his claim that the wrongness of an action is explained by the reasonable rejectability of the principles that justify it, and on the aggregation problem that arises when small burdens distributed across many people appear to outweigh large burdens imposed on a few. To prevent students from reciting generic textbook summaries, inject the following novel institutional scenario into the question: a municipal water authority must choose between Protocol A, which distributes a mildly toxic but non-lethal contaminant across 100,000 households for one year (causing temporary, fully recoverable illness in about 5% of residents), and Protocol B, which concentrates the identical total toxic load into a single reservoir serving 500 households (causing severe, lifelong chronic illness in every resident). The authority can implement only one protocol, and both are technically permitted under current regulations.

The exam question itself should ask students to do four things in an integrated essay:
1. Reconstruct Scanlon’s account of wrongness through reasonable rejection, emphasizing why it is not equivalent to maximizing welfare or protecting rights in the standard deontological sense.
2. Analyze whether the aggregation problem genuinely arises in this case: can the authority legitimately aggregate the mild, widely distributed harms of Protocol A to outweigh the severe, concentrated harms of Protocol B? Or does Scanlon’s framework prohibit such aggregation regardless of scale?
3. Evaluate whether resolving the case requires modifying Scanlon’s principle—perhaps by introducing ex ante risk-based reasoning, a structural or institutional dimension of wrongness, or a distinction between direct and indirect harm—without collapsing contractualism into rule-utilitarianism or into an absolutist rights view.
4. Present and respond to one well-known objection to Scanlon’s anti-aggregation stance (for example, the challenge that contractualism fails to justify saving the greater number, or that it ignores structural unfairness embedded in institutional design) using only resources internal to the contractualist framework.
