For a philosophical discussion group I am drafting a brief profile on Buddhist ethics and its relation to consequentialist reasoning, and I would like an audit of whether the commitments actually cohere. Here is my position: I take Buddhist moral evaluation to be primarily a matter of cetana, so that the ethical quality of an act is fixed mainly by the volitional intention behind it rather than by its worldly results; I also hold that the anatta doctrine undermines any genuinely agent-centered deontological framework, since without an enduring self there is no stable bearer of categorical duties; at the same time, I think the Mahayana bodhisattva ideal and the doctrine of upaya introduce a structurally maximizing orientation that closely resembles consequentialism, including the idea that conventional precepts may be suspended for greater sentient welfare; furthermore, I believe the Abhidharma taxonomy of cetasikas supports a kind of mental-state consequentialism, in which the moral weight of an action tracks the qualitative intensity and composition of its accompanying mental factors rather than either pure intention or external outcome alone; and finally, I take the karmic mechanism to be cosmologically outcome-sensitive across successive lives, which embeds a long-run welfare accounting into the metaphysics even though present-life worldly consequences are not the direct criterion of judgment. Go through these five commitments, identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to conflict, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision costs the overall view. Then tell me which single change would do the most to clean up the profile, and whether adopting it makes the position more or less recognizably Buddhist. Do not treat any tension as obvious: in particular, explain how the anatta claim interacts with the presence of precept-based constraints, and whether the karma framework’s outcome-sensitivity actually supports or undermines the mental-state consequentialist reading.
