Analyze the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962 as a case study for the hinge of history hypothesis, understood here as the claim that historical narratives turn on hinge propositions—background commitments that are not themselves justified within the narrative but serve as fixed points around which interpretation and decision pivot, analogous in structure to Wittgenstein’s hinge propositions in On Certainty. Begin by articulating the hypothesis precisely: identify what makes a historical commitment a hinge rather than a regular evidential belief, and specify whether hinges operate primarily at the level of individual cognition, institutional framing, or collective narrative. Then map the crisis onto this framework. For the American side, treat commitments such as the reliability of U-2 photography, the rational-actor model of Soviet decision-making, and the non-viability of a nuclear exchange as candidate hinges; for the Soviet side, treat corresponding commitments about American intentions, the legitimacy of defensive deployment, and the credibility of back-channel communication. Next, examine whether the crisis itself functions as a hinge event—an occurrence that restructures the narrative space of the Cold War without being fully determined by prior conditions—by distinguishing causal hinges (small actions with disproportionate strategic effects, such as the timing of the ExComm deliberations or the secret Turkey-missile concession) from hermeneutic hinges (moments that forced a reinterpretation of prior commitments, such as Khrushchev’s second letter or the downing of the U-2 over Cuba). Finally, evaluate the episode as a test of the hypothesis: does the persistence of sharply divergent American and Soviet interpretations despite shared sensory and documentary evidence indicate that hinge propositions are culturally incommensurable and therefore resistant to Aumann-style convergence, or does the eventual resolution through back-channel negotiation show that hinges can be temporarily bracketed without collapsing the entire interpretive framework? Be explicit about where the theory is silent—particularly regarding whether hinges are discovered, constructed, or merely inherited—and about which features of the crisis the hinge framework illuminates versus which it forces or obscures.
