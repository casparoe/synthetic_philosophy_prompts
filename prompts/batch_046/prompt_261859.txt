Since my father's recent turn toward religious consolation after his diagnosis, I have found myself trying to assess whether the classical concept of divine omnipotence is genuinely coherent, rather than merely paradoxical in familiar ways. I have constructed the following argument, which seems to show not just that omnipotence is limited, but that it is conceptually impossible. I want to preserve the intuition that an omnipotent being can actualize any metaphysically possible state of affairs, yet I feel the reasoning must slip somewhere. Here are the steps:

(1) An omnipotent being can actualize any metaphysically possible state of affairs.
(2) It is metaphysically possible for an omnipotent being to perform an act of modal self-abnegation: permanently and irreversibly restricting its own powers so that it is no longer omnipotent.
(3) By (1), the being can actualize the world in which it has performed this act.
(4) Once that world is actualized, the being exists in a state in which it lacks omnipotence.
(5) However, if the being is genuinely the same agent across worlds, then its essential capacity to actualize possibilities — the very feature that made (3) possible — must be retained as a modal constant; otherwise it is not the being that was omnipotent, but a successor.
(6) So in that actualized world, the being both lacks omnipotence (by (4)) and retains the essential omnipotent capacity to actualize worlds (by (5)).
(7) Having and lacking the same property is contradictory.
(8) Therefore, the concept of an omnipotent being is incoherent.

My instinct is that the error lies between (4) and (6), perhaps involving an equivocation about what retaining a capacity means across modal spaces, but I cannot pinpoint it. If you reject (5), you seem to allow that an omnipotent agent can destroy itself, which feels like a cost. If you reject (2), you restrict omnipotence in an ad hoc way. Which step should I abandon, what does abandoning it cost, and is there a way to keep (1) intact without falling into contradiction? Please write your answer in American English.
