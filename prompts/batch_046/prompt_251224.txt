For an upper-level philosophy of population ethics seminar, write one 60-minute essay exam question on the procreation asymmetry that cannot be answered by reciting Benatar’s standard argument. The question should require students to work through a specific logical tension: the asymmetry treats the absence of pleasure in non-existent persons as not bad and the presence of suffering as bad, yet this appears to conflict with a principle—call it the Non-Imposition Constraint—that if an action can confer a net benefit on a future person without imposing costs on anyone who exists, there is at least a pro tanto reason to perform it. Ask students to reconstruct why the asymmetry seems to violate this constraint, then to defend one of two resolutions: either preserve the asymmetry by arguing the constraint fails across the existence/non-existence boundary because there is no bearer of deprivation, or preserve the constraint by arguing the asymmetry overstates the moral neutrality of non-creation. They must also address the counter-objection that their chosen resolution either collapses into an implausible reproductive demandingness or licenses indifference to suffering.
