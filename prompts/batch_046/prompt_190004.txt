As a retired evolutionary morphologist who has recently turned to philosophy of science and ethics, I have been trying to stabilize my views on adaptationism now that I am no longer practicing science but still read the literature with a critical eye. I hold the following commitments, but I suspect they may not form a coherent package. First, I accept the Gould–Lewontin spandrels critique in its strong form: many complex traits are architectural byproducts rather than optimized adaptations, and the adaptationist tendency to construct post-hoc selection narratives for every feature is a serious methodological vice. Second, I nevertheless think evolutionary psychology is broadly defensible when it employs predictive novelty, cross-cultural replication, and explicit null models; its failure is overreach, not its core inferential logic. Third, I believe the spandrel/adaptation distinction is pragmatically useful but metaphysically unstable, since exaptation and constructive neutral evolution constantly blur the boundary—there are no “pure” spandrels, only traits exhibiting varying degrees of functional decoupling from their origins. Fourth, I maintain that legitimate evolutionary explanation of human cognition requires citing actual ancestral selection pressures on underlying mechanisms, not merely demonstrating current utility, because current-utility arguments risk unfalsifiable just-so storytelling. Fifth, I think human normative ethical reasoning is almost certainly a spandrel—an unselected cognitive overflow of social-monitoring and coalitional-psychology machinery—yet I also believe it should be studied with evolutionary-functional tools, and that doing so reveals genuine, non-arbitrary constraints on ethical theory rather than reducing ethics to biology.

Please audit this package with a committed assessment: take definite positions on which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging. For each tension, identify the least costly revision and state precisely what it costs me—do not present every option as equally viable. In particular, resolve definitively whether my acceptance of functional decoupling in the third commitment undermines the force of my strong spandrels critique in the first; whether my demand for ancestral-selection explanations in the fourth can survive my claim in the fifth that ethical reasoning lacks direct selection history; and whether my defense of evolutionary psychology in the second is compatible with my methodological strictures in the fourth. I am especially concerned about a possible hidden contradiction between insisting that spandrels lack proper selection histories and simultaneously applying evolutionary-functional analysis to them—address this specifically and tell me which single commitment, if any, must be revised to restore coherence with minimal loss.
