Write an essay about the tension in Keynes's idea of the good life—specifially whether his famous 15-hour-week vision in Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren is actually compatible with the Moorean ethics he picked up at Cambridge and never really dropped. The controversy is this: Keynes says once absolute needs are met, people will stop chasing money and turn to art, friendship, and living well for its own sake. But in My Early Beliefs he endorses G.E. Moore's view that the highest goods are mental states—love, beauty, truth—that dont depend on leisure at all; you can have them in the middle of a crisis meeting if your attitude is right. Meanwhile Keynes himself never slowed down—he was advising the Treasury, running the arts council, speculating on currencies, and hosting Bloomsbury weekends all at once. So was he living proof that the good life doesnt require post-scarcity leisure? Or was he secretly miserable, chasing relative needs he pretended to despise? Weigh both sides carefully. Reach a conclusion: is Keynes's good life a coherent philosophical vision, or is it a contradiction between his economics and his ethics that he never resolved? Justify it with reference to the distinction between absolute and relative needs, Moore's idea of organic unities, and Keynes's own biograpy—without treating his life as simple hypocrisy. Around 700 words, casual tone is fine, and dont worry about footnotes.
