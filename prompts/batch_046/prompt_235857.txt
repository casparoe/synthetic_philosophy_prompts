After retiring from experimental physics, I have been working through Dennett’s argument for quining qualia and the Chase and Sanborn reply that this move undermines our evidential base. Here is where I stand; I want an audit of whether these commitments hang together.

(1) Qualia are theoretical posits analogous to caloric or phlogiston: if cognition and behavior are fully explained without private qualitative objects, they should be eliminated rather than retained as mysterious entities.

(2) At the same time, introspective reports of qualitative character—e.g., that something feels reddish—function as first-person data, not as mere theoretical claims. To dismiss them because the ontology is inconvenient is like discarding a detector reading that conflicts with one’s model.

(3) From my laboratory background, I treat such reports as instrument outputs: fallible, theory-laden, and certainly not transparent windows onto intrinsic properties, yet not freely eliminable without losing predictive constraint.

(4) I also hold neural and evolutionary continuity: consciousness and suffering come in degrees tied to processing complexity, with no sharp metaphysical boundary separating sentient from non-sentient beings.

(5) For ethical reasoning, however, I want a robust distinction between beings that genuinely suffer and those that merely behave as if they do; I think behavioral disposition alone cannot carry this moral weight.

(6) Finally, I am skeptical that physics provides a metaphysically complete description of matter, so I do not locate the hard problem in an explanatory gap within physics. Rather, it seems to arise from an illegitimate demand that subjective reports be translated into purely third-person vocabulary.

Are these jointly tenable? Specifically: does accepting (1) commit me to reject (2) and (3), or can the instrument-analogy preserve evidential force without reifying private qualities? Does (4) conflict with (5), or can graded moral status accommodate continuity? Is (6) genuinely compatible with (1), or does it smuggle back exactly the qualitative reality I am trying to quine? Order the tensions by severity, flag any apparent conflict that dissolves on inspection, state the bottom line first, and keep the response to a maximum of 250 words.
