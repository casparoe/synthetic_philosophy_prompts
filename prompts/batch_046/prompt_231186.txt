In Tolstoy's account of art—centered on the idea that art is a form of emotional infection in which an artist transmits a feeling to an audience through a work—he ties artistic value to both the sincerity of the artist and the moral quality of the feeling transmitted. I am drafting a short essay that applies this infection framework to contemporary generative systems optimized to induce precise emotional responses without any human originator having experienced those emotions. My tentative claim is that, on Tolstoy's own functional definition, such outputs should count as art because they successfully infect an audience, yet they simultaneously undermine his moral criterion, since there is no sincere human source whose moral state could validate or corrupt the transmitted feeling. Would you comment on the accuracy of this reading, on whether the tension is genuinely internal to Tolstoy's account rather than anachronistic, and on whether I should distinguish between infection mediated by an algorithm and direct emotional transmission from a conscious artist? Please answer in American English.
