I'm on the oversight board for our state's Long-Horizon Agricultural Planning Office, and we're deadlocked on a review that got way more philosophical than anyone expected. In 2019 the office adopted a modified Malthusian resource-pressure model for regional food-security forecasting out to 2070, with hard intermediate benchmarks for 2025 and 2040. Well, 2025 just arrived and the numbers are thier opposite—crop yields blew past projections because of some gene-edited drought-resistant strains nobody had in the model, and population growth tapered off faster than predicted. The modeling director isn't calling it falsified, though. She's saying the theory is "right but early," that the structural dynamics of resource pressure are still sound but the timeline needs stretching, and she's petitioning us to approve a formal "predictive-rescue protocol" that lets them revise intermediate milestones without discarding the long-run framework. Half the board thinks this is textbook immunization against refutation; the other half says long-run predictions are structurally unfalsifiable in the short term and we should grant temporal slack.

I need to draft the board's decision memo for next Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete course—approve the protocol with strict conditions, reject it outright, or approve a one-time pilot with a clear re-evaluation date—and it needs to treat the director's "right but early" argument seriously rather than just restating Popperian falsification rules. Crucially, the reasoning has to engage the strongest consideration on the losing side: if I go with approval, I have to explain why this isn't just ad hoc rescue; if I go with rejection, I need to address why long-range structural models shouldn't ever recieve intermediate slack. The memo should be readable for non-philosophers on the board, have an actual bottom line they can vote on, and include a specific timeframe or trigger for whatever path I pick. Please don't include reading lists or citations—half these people won't open them anyway. Can you write it? Make it about two pages, formal but direct, with the recommendation up front.
