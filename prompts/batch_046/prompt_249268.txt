I've been trying to nail down my actual view on insecticides and pest killing, and I think the best way is to check my gut reactions to specific situations. Putting localized termite bait in my crawlspace because they're eating the foundation: fine. Swatting a mosquito that's biting my kid: fine. Calling for heat treatment of bedbugs in my apartment: fine, though it feels weird. Spraying my garden with broad-spectrum neonicotinoids just so my lettuce looks perfect: not fine. Setting pheremone traps for Japanese beetles stripping my grape vines: I keep going back and forth — it's my property but also feels like vanity killing. Government aerial larvicide over wetlands near a town to stop West Nile: okay, but if it's a protected marsh it suddenly feels wrong, even with the same mosquitoes. Killing every spider in a warehouse because workers are scared: not fine. Killing a bee hive inside a school wall after relocation failed: fine, but it sucks. Then there's invasive beetles killing ash trees — cutting down every tree in the county to stop spread feels like overkill, not fine, but doing nothing also feels wrong. There's also carpenter ants in my attic versus ground bees in my lawn that are technically stinging pests but mostly pollinate wildflowers — I think poison bait for the ants is okay, but for the bees it's not, even though both are "just killing a colony in my space." Is there a principle, or maybe two competing ones, that captures the pattern? I want each clause tied to the specific case that motivates it, plus your verdict on a couple cases I haven't mentioned — like using poison bait on termites in a public park tree, or killing a single praying mantis in my garden just because I'm afraid of it — and an honest note about which of my verdicts it just can't save. Maybe one of them has to go.
