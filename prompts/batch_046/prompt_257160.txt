I am outlining an essay that defends a strict form of tranquilism—the view that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, while pleasure, achievement, knowledge, and other positive states are at best instrumentally valuable and at worst neutral distractions. The essay’s central argument is that once this view is taken seriously, it generates a paradox about the value of “potential tranquility.” Specifically, if non-existence is the state in which craving and suffering are absent, then tranquilism cannot distinguish between relieving suffering in an existing being and preventing a being from coming into existence, since both yield the same absence. To resolve this, I propose a distinction between “active absence” (the cessation of craving in a conscious subject) and “passive absence” (the never-arising of craving), and I claim that only active absence has value because only there is there a subject for whom the absence obtains. I then push this further: if active absence requires a conscious subject, and consciousness is typically accompanied by minimal craving or aversion, then the tranquilist ideal becomes not serene contentment but a being whose consciousness is reduced to the minimal edge of subjective existence—what I call the “threshold subject.” The essay concludes that tranquilism, consistently applied, does not prescribe a flourishing life but rather one engineered toward the barest margin of awareness, and that this should make us ask whether tranquilism is a theory of well-being or a disguised argument for eliminating subjective experience. I also include a brief comparative section arguing that this result distinguishes tranquilism from negative utilitarianism, since the latter still concerns suffering in all possible beings, whereas my version concerns only actual subjects capable of active absence. Could you comment on the accuracy of my distinction between active and passive absence, on whether the move to the “threshold subject” is a legitimate consequence or a reductio, and on whether my comparison with negative utilitarianism holds?
