I am a retired physicist who, since leaving active research, have devoted myself to philosophy of time and the metaphysics of infinity. In a small reading group I have organized, two opposing camps have formed around the problem usually called Thomson's lamp and the broader possibility of supertasks. The first camp — I will call them the Limit-State Realists — contends that an infinite sequence of discrete state-transitions compressed into a finite interval produces a genuine logical contradiction at the accumulation point: if a lamp is toggled on at every even step and off at every odd step, then at the limit time there is no consistent assignment of on or off, which they take to demonstrate that completed supertasks involving discrete changes are conceptually impossible, regardless of physical constraints. The second camp — the Sequence-Only Dissolutionists — argues that this is a pseudo-paradox generated by illicitly projecting properties of the infinite sequence onto a limit point that is not itself a member of the sequence; they maintain that state-descriptions apply only within the open interval of toggles, and that demanding a final state at the limit is a category error analogous to insisting that a convergent infinite series must take its limit value as one of its terms.

To force the discussion out of familiar ruts, I have proposed that both camps examine an engineered analogue: an infinite cascade of electrical relays arranged so that each relay, upon closing, triggers the opening of the next after exactly half the remaining time, reaching conceptual completion at a finite time T. The Realists must address whether the absence of a determinate relay-state at T is a logical contradiction in the system description or merely the expected behavior of a discontinuous dynamical map at an accumulation point; the Dissolutionists must address whether their view can accommodate the engineering intuition that a complete physical model should specify the state of every component at every time, including limit points, without covertly importing assumptions about continuity. Both sides should also agree on whether physical objections — thermal noise, quantum decoherence, or a minimal temporal unit — bear on the conceptual issue or are irrelevant red herrings.

Please draft the joint adversarial-collaboration statement these two camps could both sign. Include: a numbered section of agreed claims about the formal structure of the toggle sequence, the logical status of the limit point, and the irrelevance or relevance of physical realizability; a disagreements table phrased so that each camp accepts the wording of its own position; a list of cruxes — specifically, whether state-function determinism is required at accumulation points, whether the relay-cascade analogy resolves or intensifies the paradox, and whether the dispute over final-state assignment is substantive or partly verbal — with for each crux a concrete case or argument both sides accept as bearing on it, together with each side's explicit prediction; and a concluding assessment. Do not present a balanced menu of possibilities: take a definite stance on whether the relay-cascade dissolves the paradox, on whether part of the disagreement is verbal, and on which crux is decisive, and defend those choices with reasons. The document should be formal and precise enough that either camp could endorse the agreed-claims section without reservation.
