As a novelist structuring an epic in which generations endure severe suffering followed by prolonged, modest well-being, I have become preoccupied with lexical threshold negative utilitarianism and the question of whether pleasure and pain are genuinely commensurable. Could you describe the most important implications of the lexical-threshold position—specifically, that no accumulation of positive experience can outweigh severe suffering—for the moral logic of narrative resolution? I am particularly concerned with whether the incommensurability of suffering and pleasure undermines the concept of a redemptive arc altogether, and what this suggests about portraying suffering as structurally irredeemable rather than merely quantitatively dominant.
