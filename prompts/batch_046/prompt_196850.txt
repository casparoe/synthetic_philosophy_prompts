Please write a formal, polished exchange between two equally competent philosophers — Dr. Elena Voss, who defends the Chase-Sanborn incoherence thesis that qualia are an unstable posit combining first-person immediacy with third-person explanatory emptiness, and Dr. Marcus Lin, who advances a modified phenomenal realism he calls granular transparency, arguing that qualitative character is not a bare particular but a determinable relational structure that survives the incoherence charge only if we abandon the traditional what-it-is-like framing in favour of relational phenomenal geometry. Set their discussion at a public symposium for an informed but non-specialist audience. The dialogue must contain real back-and-forth: each speaker should respond directly to the other’s preceding argument rather than talk past it, and neither should be treated as a strawman. To keep the exchange from being a well-worn rehearsal, please inject the following niche conceptual device: a hypothetical bifurcated observer — a cognitively intact subject with hemispheric dissociation who reports simultaneous, contradictory qualitative states in a single visual field (for example, experiencing a surface as both warm-red and cool-green without fusion, ambiguity, or temporal alternation). Have both interlocutors debate whether this case vindicates the incoherence claim, reveals a hidden determinable structure, or demonstrates something else entirely. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Do not quote from real texts; if you reference specific arguments, paraphrase them without quotation marks. Write the dialogue in British English, using formal but accessible prose.
