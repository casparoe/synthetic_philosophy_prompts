I sit on our city's public art funding board and we have to vote on a $400k grant proposal next tuesday. A local neuroscience group wants to design public murals using fMRI patterns they call "cross-cultural beauty signatures" — they scanned brains across populations and claim convergent activation means aesthetic value is objective, not just subjective taste. Some board members say this finally proves Hume wrong about taste being sentiment, and that we should fund it since science can settle what's beautiful. Others say it confuses statistical agreement with objectivity and totally misses Hume's idea that the standard of taste is refined human judgement, not neural correlation. Draft the board's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — that we could actually publish, with reasoning that includes the strongest point for the side that loses. Start with the bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble, don't restate the problem.
