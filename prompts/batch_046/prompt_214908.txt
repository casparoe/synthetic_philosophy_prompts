I am drafting a novel in which an aging natural philosopher defends a sweeping theory of mind, and in constructing his arguments I have kept noticing a defensive maneuver that I cannot place precisely within the philosophy of science. The theory begins by claiming that every complex adaptive system exhibits a particular form of anticipatory behavior; when biologists find organisms that clearly adapt without anticipation, the philosopher does not amend the law with measurable conditions. Instead he declares that those organisms possess only apparent adaptation, belonging to a shadow category—call it pseudo-adaptation—that the theory itself is uniquely positioned to distinguish from the genuine article, even though no independent criterion for the distinction existed before the anomaly appeared. A second instance occurs in cosmology: a model predicts a symmetry in all large-scale structures, and when asymmetrical structures are observed, defenders do not invoke an auxiliary parameter but instead reclassify the structures as pre-cosmic artifacts lying outside the model's true domain, a domain that shrinks to fit exactly the symmetrical cases. In both cases the boundary is not defended empirically but taxonomically, using distinctions that borrow their authority from the very theory being protected.

This strikes me as distinct from the ordinary accumulation of auxiliary hypotheses that Popper criticized, and also from the refined conceptual adjustments that occur in mature sciences; it seems closer to a particular failure mode of demarcation, where a field protects its scientific status not by risking predictions but by retroactively narrowing the class of phenomena it claims to cover, using internal classifications that are unfalsifiable because they are defined through the theory's own successes. Is there an established term for this exact pattern, perhaps within discussions of falsificationism or the demarcation problem, and if not, how should one define its edges so that it does not collapse into either rhetorical retreat or legitimate conceptual revision?
