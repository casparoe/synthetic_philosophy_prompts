For a philosophy of physics assignment, I need a precise specification of what any adequate interpretation of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument—roughly, that special-relativistic simultaneity undermines an objective present and supports eternalism—must be able to deliver. Write the desiderata: the phenomena of temporal experience, causal ordering, and cross-frame simultaneity it must preserve or explain away; the distinctions it must respect, such as being real versus being present, determinism versus fatalism, and Lorentz covariance versus a privileged foliation; and which of these are genuinely non-negotiable physical constraints versus partisan preferences of presentists or eternalists. I especially want to know whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable—for instance, maintaining an objective present, avoiding a preferred reference frame, and accommodating the psychological arrow of time. Finally, address whether a resolution can make coherent sense of what I am calling “modal thickness”: the idea that presentness might vary locally in ontological weight according to interaction capacity rather than belonging to a single global slice. Keep the weight on the criteria, their motivating cases, and any incompatibilities among them, rather than on scoring particular interpretations.
