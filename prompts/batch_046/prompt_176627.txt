Write a villanelle—nineteen lines in five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains that alternate and converge at the end—about how military or political defeat is converted into a founding national myth. The four cases should not appear as a catalog but should bleed into one another: Masada (the Roman siege ending in collective death, later read as resistance rather than surrender), the 1389 Battle of Kosovo Polje (defeat transformed into a covenant of spiritual kingship), Dunkirk in 1940 (a strategic evacuation rewritten as moral triumph), and the American Lost Cause after 1865 (secessionist failure reframed as the defense of a civilization). Give the poem two refrain lines: one stating the bare historical defeat, the other stating its mythic inversion; let these serve as the repeating A-lines in an ABA rhyme scheme. In the tercets, build the mechanism of selective memory—what is retained (martyrdom, rescue, covenant) and what is excised (the choice of annihilation over negotiation, the strategic logic of retreat, the centrality of slavery). Use the quatrain as the volta: here the poem should expose that the myth structurally requires the defeat to remain unrecovered, since actual redemption or victory would dissolve the moral claim that depends on unjust suffering. End with both refrains together so that loss and legend are locked in unreconciled tension. The rhyme and repetition should mirror the compulsive return to a story that can never be completed, and the poem should be precise enough that a historian of nationalism recognizes the mechanism without needing footnotes.
