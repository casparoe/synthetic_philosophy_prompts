My philosophy-of-mind advisor has issued the following rulings on whether subjects genuinely know that they are not brains in vats. First, a normal adult who forms the belief through ordinary visual and tactile experience: she counts it as knowledge. Second, a phenomenologically identical subject inside a perfect, undetectable simulation: no knowledge. Third, a subject who explicitly derives non-envatment by inference to the best explanation from the coherence of experience, together with the false premise that all perfect simulations necessarily leave detectable phenomenological traces: no knowledge. Fourth, a subject who offers a direct Moorean response—citing her own hands and the presence of other people—to reject the skeptical hypothesis without circular reliance on anti-skeptical premises: knowledge. Fifth, a subject whose belief-forming mechanism is hard-wired to always output the belief that she is not envatted, regardless of sensory input, who happens to live in the actual world: no knowledge.

Next week’s case is this. A philosopher believes she is not a brain in a vat. The belief is true and grounded in ordinary perceptual experience, and she never invokes any theoretical premise in her conscious reasoning. Unbeknownst to her, however, her confidence is causally sustained by a false metaphysical background view—an obsolete Cartesian doctrine that mental substance is simple and therefore cannot be duplicated or simulated by any physical computer—which she absorbed in an undergraduate course. This false view does not figure in her evidential reasoning; rather, it structurally calibrates her attentional filters, suppressing anomalous stimuli that might otherwise trigger skeptical doubt. Because of this calibration, in nearby worlds where she retains that false doctrine and is in fact envatted, her filters suppress the simulation artifacts and she still forms the belief that she is not a BIV. But in nearby worlds where she lacks the doctrine and is not envatted, her perceptual system functions normally and she still believes truly.

Predict her verdict—knowledge or not—say whether she is implicitly running a strict modal-safety account that evaluates belief-forming conditions across nearby worlds while holding constant all causally upstream psychological calibration (including false background theories), or a Moorean common-sense entitlement view that insulates direct perceptual knowledge from causally incidental false beliefs that do not enter the justification chain. Identify the closest analog among cases one through five, state what broader principle she would be adopting with each of the two possible choices, and specify the further hypothetical case that would definitively separate the two rules. Please also say how confident you are in this prediction and single out the specific point in your analysis about which you are least sure.
