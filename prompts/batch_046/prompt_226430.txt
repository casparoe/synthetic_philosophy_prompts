Write a dialogue suitable for an advanced political philosophy seminar. The interlocutors are Dr. Alina Rostova, who defends an unconditional welfare model grounded in what she terms recognitional standing, and Dr. James Okonkwo, who defends a conditional workfare regime grounded in reciprocity. Rostova should argue that modern conditionality transforms eligibility compliance—documenting job searches, attending mandatory interviews, submitting to surveillance—into a hidden form of labor that itself generates desert, thereby dissolving the moral boundary between the deserving and undeserving poor into a boundary between the administratively compliant and the administratively resistant. Okonkwo should reply that this conflates moral desert with bureaucratic navigation and that genuine reciprocity requires contribution to shared social goods, not mere procedural obedience. Have them directly address the tension Rostova identifies between epistemic asymmetry in fraud detection—the state's need for intimate knowledge of citizens' lives to verify eligibility—and the autonomy that welfare is meant to preserve. The exchange should contain real back-and-forth: each speaker must respond to the specific point just made, not merely restate their position. Neither should be a strawman; both should display equal philosophical competence. Keep the tone formal and polished.
