In a seminar on auditing hiring algorithms, a colleague dismissed a proposed fairness constraint by saying, "fairness is impossible." Separate the precise claims that slogan could be expressing: (1) the mathematical impossibility of jointly satisfying calibration within groups, balance in error rates across groups, and predictive parity; (2) the sociological claim that fairness is essentially contested and therefore not formalizable; (3) the practical claim that organizational or legal incentives prevent implementation regardless of technical feasibility; and (4) the epistemic claim that post-hoc explanation methods cannot reliably verify fairness properties in black-box hiring models. For each reading, indicate whether it is supported by established impossibility results, whether it applies specifically to the auditing context with explanation methods, and whether it actually justifies abandoning formal fairness constraints altogether. Present the readings as a menu with verdicts rather than selecting a single interpretation.
