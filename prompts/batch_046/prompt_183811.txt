I'm putting together my position for a seminar on epistemic value and want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I've landed: (1) I think Plato gets the Meno problem essentially right—knowledge is better than true belief because it is anchored by an account (logos), which makes it stable and resistant to being lost. (2) I read this anchoring in a reliabilist register: a belief is tied down when it comes from a systematically truth-tracking process, not when the believer can cite reasons, and the process can be entirely opaque to introspection. (3) I deny that knowledge requires explanatory understanding—knowing that water boils at 100°C needs no grasp of thermodynamics, and knowing a mathematical truth needs no grasp of its proof. (4) I hold that knowledge has no intrinsic value; its advantage over true belief is purely instrumental, lying solely in greater diachronic reliability for guiding future action. (5) I maintain that in any particular practical situation—navigating a city, taking a drug, trusting testimony—there is no rational or normative difference between acting on knowledge and acting on a true belief held without justification or explanation. Do these five commitments hang together? Order any genuine tensions by severity, identify which pairs are only apparently in conflict, and for each real tension say which commitment would have to give and what the least costly revision costs. Please present your audit as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one to two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
