I am preparing a ninety-minute graduate seminar on the intentional stance, specifically testing its application to attributions of belief in artificial language systems. The students have encountered Dennett's framework in standard philosophy of mind courses but have not examined whether predictive success alone licenses intentional attribution when the underlying architecture lacks anything resembling representational storage or inferential integration. I intend to carry the session through one sustained example: a language model that consistently predicts nuanced social outcomes—specifically, that a colleague who has been publicly criticized will respond with strategic self-deprecation rather than direct defense—where the behavioral pattern is stable and context-sensitive, yet there is no internal module that corresponds to a belief about social face-management.

My pedagogical difficulty is that I want to introduce a distinction not typically drawn in the canonical literature—between an instrumental predictive stance, in which we attribute beliefs solely to maximize forecasting accuracy, and a normative interpretive stance, in which we attribute beliefs to hold the system accountable for the coherence of its reasoning—without allowing the session to collapse into a debate about AI consciousness or moral status. I need the explanatory sequence to make clear why the intentional stance is an interpretive strategy rather than an empirical discovery about mental causation, and I need the example to bear both dimensions of attribution simultaneously. Please plan the teaching arc: the order in which the conceptual dependencies must be introduced so that the predictive/normative distinction emerges naturally rather than being imposed; what the carrying example must include and what it must lack to prevent students from falling back on analogies to human psychology; the misconceptions that will arise—particularly the assumption that the stance requires phenomenal consciousness, or that high predictive correlation is sufficient for belief attribution—and the precise point in the sequence where each should be intercepted; a brief check for understanding that distinguishes grasping the stance as a framework from treating it as a verification procedure; and which related debates, such as the moral patiency of artificial systems, I should explicitly bracket as outside the session's scope.
