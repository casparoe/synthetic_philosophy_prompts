I sit on the funding review panel for the Instutute for Mind and Matter Studies — long story — and we've got a proposal that's split us right down the middle. The PI wants three years of support to test what she's calling the Schrödinger unity claim. You probably remember he got into Vedanta late in life and argued that individual minds aren't separate bulbs but more like reflections in mirrors — one consciousness appearing plural because of all the bodies. She's framing it empirically: pairs of subjects in deep sensory deprivation, EEG coherence measures, looking for correlation structures that can't be explained by classical signaling between brains. Her idea is that if consciousness is genuinely unitary, you might see patterns that look like one substrate being partitioned rather than two systems talking to each other.

Half the board says this is metaphysics in a lab coat. They argue Schrödinger's claim was ontological — about what consciousness is — not a hypothesis about brain dynamics, and funding it blurs our line between conceptual work and empirical science. The other half says rejecting it is just philosophical gatekeeping: she's measuring correlations, not claiming to detect the soul, and the Vedanta framing is historical context, not the study desgin.

We vote Thursday and I need to circulate a memo before then reccomend either approval with conditions or rejection, with a concrete timeline — like an 18-month milestone review if we approve, or a resubmission window if we reject. Don't just say needs more study. And please take the strongest version of the losing side seriously: don't dismiss the metaphysics worry as anti-science, and don't dismiss the empirical framing as naive.

I'm especially stuck on whether any EEG result could actually bear on Schrödinger's view, since he seemed to think the unity was something you realized subjectively rather than observed in a scan. Deal with that specifically in relation to her study design. Don't just list philosophers — address the actual proposal she's put forward.
