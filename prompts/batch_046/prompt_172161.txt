I am a doctoral student in moral and political philosophy studying the sociology of normative change. For an adversarial-collaboration protocol, I have two colleagues who have debated for over a year whether the collapse of practices such as Atlantic slavery, aristocratic dueling in Britain, and trial by combat following Ashford v. Thornton is best explained by shifts in honor codes exploited through internal moral argument—in the framework developed by Kwame Anthony Appiah in The Honor Code—or by material interests, economic restructuring, and state-capacity expansion—in the manner of Eric Williams’s thesis in Capitalism and Slavery.

Please draft the joint statement these two camps should have produced. The document should open with a numbered section of claims both sides can sign, covering: what requires explanation about the timing and geographic variation of these three norm collapses; the respective roles of elite coordination and popular agency; the predictive limits of both honor-based and interest-based models; and whether the concept of honor should be analyzed as a positional good subject to zero-sum status competition or as a coordination equilibrium that can dissolve without material cause.

Next, present a disagreements table that states, in wording each camp would accept for its own position, exactly where they still differ on: whether moral rhetoric is causally efficacious or largely epiphenomenal to economic change; whether quantitative indicators—such as sugar-market profitability, administrative costs of legal enforcement, or military-bureaucratic expansion—predict the timing of abolition more reliably than discursive shifts in honor-language; and whether the legislative abolition of trial by combat represents primarily a rationalization of state violence monopolization or a genuine cultural collapse of aristocratic honor.

Then identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. For each crux, specify one concrete comparative case or piece of evidence both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp predicts that evidence will show. At minimum address: the interval between the abolition of the slave trade and full emancipation in relation to plantation-economy data; the differential persistence of dueling in regions with weak central legal institutions versus its rapid decline where bureaucratic codes expanded; and the parliamentary record and social reception surrounding the abolition of trial by combat.

Finally, assess whether any portion of the dispute is verbal—particularly regarding the boundary between “honor” and “interest”—and if so specify which portion and propose a reformulated conceptual distinction that renders the remaining disagreement substantive rather than terminological. The document must contain no bibliography, no reading list, no citation requests, and no appeals to sources that require web access. Write in formal, neutral academic prose suitable for joint authorship.
