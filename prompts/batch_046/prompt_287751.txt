draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the vicious-circularity camp and the benign-loop camp on the bootstrapping problem and easy knowledge.

camp A holds that any epistemic loop where belief B is used to justify belief A, while A is already presuppossed in B, is vicious full stop — reliability or coherence can't salvage it. camp B holds that circular support can be benign when the loop is globally coherant and anchored by direct acquaintance or external causal feedback, and that the standard closure-based "easy knowledge" worry is just closure working correctly, not a genuine paradox.

format it like this:

1. agreed claims (numbered list): what both sides can actually sign about basic knowledge, the logic of circular reasoning, the role of process reliability, and the sensory-deprevation calibration case.

2. disagreements table: columns are issue | camp A wording (phrased so they'd accept it as their own view) | camp B wording (phrased so they'd accept it as their own view).

3. cruxes: list the key cruxes. for each, name the concrete argument or hypothetical case both agree bears on it, then write camp A's predicted resolution and camp B's predicted resolution in advance.

specifically, inject the calibration-loop hypothetical: a subject in a sensory-deprivation tank calibrates her own visual cortex via auditory feedback loops she designed herself, never breaking out to external verification. both sides should agree this is the central test case, but they should split on whether she genuinely knows her calibration succeeded, and on whether that result (whatever it is) undermines her broader perceptual knowledge.

keep it somewhat in the weeds — don't just rehearse the standard epistemology 101 version. don't ask for bibliographys, reading lists, or citations. write casual, a typo or two is totally fine.
