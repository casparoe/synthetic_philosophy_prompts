I've been digging into Szasz lately and I'm mostly bought into his line that mental illness is literally a myth — not just that psychiatry over-pathologises ordinary distress, but that calling schizophrenia a disease is a catagory error, like calling heartbreak a heart attack. But I want to know what would actually move me away from it. Not the usual brain-scan-difference arguments — I want the proper defeaters. Like, what if we found a single biomarker that predicted the full schizophrenia symptom cluster with near-perfect accuracy and antipsychotics corrected the underlying mechanism? Or if the problems-in-living framing collapsed entirely for certain conditions? Or if his hard line between brain diseases, say epilepsy, and mental illnesses turned out to rest on a dodgy Cartesian dualism he never shook?

Rank these from fatal to merely awkward. And be honest: has Szasz qualifed his view so heavily — especially in his later writing on the therapeutic state — that it's become unfalsifiable? If it has, is that actually a problem for a claim that's meant to be conceptual rather than empirical? I'd also like to know which defeaters Szasz himself would concede, and which he'd dismiss as missing the point entirely.
