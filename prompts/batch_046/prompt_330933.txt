Since I was recently asked to co-author the philosophical framework for an interdisciplinary grant proposal on indirect astrophysical detection, I need to defend a specific thesis at next week's philosophy of science colloquium, and this has become personally urgent because the proposal's funding review depends in part on whether the ontology is coherent. My thesis is that for entities permanently shielded from direct observational access—such as black hole interiors or genuinely non-interacting dark matter—ontology identification should not proceed by attributing intrinsic properties, but instead by what I call negative structural identification: specifying the entity solely through the invariant relational exclusions, constraints, and absence-patterns it imposes on the accessible domain, without committing to any internal constitution. The audience will be eight philosophers of science, including two committed scientific realists who believe indirect evidence licenses full property-attribution, two instrumentalists who treat such entities as predictive scaffolds with no ontological weight, one physicist who specializes in indirect detection and is skeptical of philosophical framing, and three graduate students. I want a prep sheet for the Q&A: the objections they will actually raise, ordered by how likely and damaging each is, phrased as each camp would phrase it; for each, a concise reply I can deliver in about two minutes, with an explicit note on where that reply is weakest; what I should concede in my opening remarks to avoid losing credibility; and the one objection I am hoping nobody raises—namely, that negative structural identification is either disguised realism about relational properties or disguised eliminativism with unnecessary formal apparatus. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations; the model should work only from the conceptual framework I have provided.
