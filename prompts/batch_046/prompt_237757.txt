In my Holocaust-studies reading group we've been circling the Browning-Goldhagen debate for months and it's driving me nuts. Browning's Ordinary Men argument says the Reserve Police Battalion 101 shooters were basically ordinary middle-aged family men who killed mainly because of peer pressure, authority structures, and careerism — not because of any special hatred of Jews. Goldhagen's Hitler's Willing Executioners reply says that's wrong: they were willing executioners soaked in a specifically German eliminationist antisemitism, and the situationist explantion lets perpetrators off the hook by treating ideology as background noise. In the group we keep replaying the same moves: Browning's side points out that refusal carried almost no cost, so the low refusal rate doesn't prove ideological enthusiasm; Goldhagen's side points to the brutal enthusiasm of the killings and the voluntary participation in hunts. Nobody moves. I'm wondering if this stall is actually about hidden disagreements in what we take 'ordinary' to mean — like, is it a psychological claim about absence of malice, or a sociological claim about being statistically normal? Or are they answering different questions, one about the immediate causal mechanisms in 1942 and one about the long-run cultural conditions that made those men available? There's also this weird inherited assumption that 'ordinary' and 'ideologically motivated' are opposites, but maybe that's a false dichotomy neither side has argued for. I want a committed diagnosis, not a menu of options: tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is — factual, methodological, about standards of evidence in social history, or bedrock intuitions about moral responsibility — supported by what each side actually says when you push past the slogans. And given your diagnosis, what would count as real progress? A new archival discovery about refusal patterns, a conceptual distinction that dissolves the opposition, or is the question just ill-posed because the categories overlap more than we admit? Give me your best take with reasons.
