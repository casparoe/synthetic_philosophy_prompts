I am a doctoral student in philosophy of physics, and I am constructing a diagnostic taxonomy for a seminar on the Born-rule problem in Everettian quantum mechanics. I want a decision tree that classifies positions by the answers they give to three binary questions—questions that should be answerable without knowing the interpretive labels in advance.

Root commitment: the universal wavefunction evolves unitarily, with no collapse.

Branch 1. Is the source of probabilistic uncertainty indexical—concerning which post-branching copy is "me"—or non-indexical, arising instead from structural features of rational decision-making?

Branch 2. Are branch weights physically real, extensive properties of worlds in the multiverse, or are they purely formal coefficients within a calculus of rational credence?

Branch 3. (This is the distinction I want to inject.) Does the derivation require decoherence to fix a globally unique, counterfactually stable branching structure, or does it allow that the same universal state can be partitioned into branches in physically equivalent but descriptively distinct ways without altering the probability assignment?

Please construct the tree so that each internal node is phrased as a question a student could answer without knowing which interpretation it leads to. The leaves should include named positions for: the Deutsch-Wallace decision-theoretic derivation; the Vaidman-Sebens-Carroll self-locating-uncertainty view; Zurek's envariance argument; Everett's original typicality/frequency account; and a QBist or relational rejection of objective branching weights. Any other occupied combination should be named if it has a standard label, otherwise marked "unnamed."

Then, map out the full logical space of eight combinations. For every unoccupied leaf, provide a one-sentence diagnosis: is the combination incoherent (e.g., indexical uncertainty paired with purely formal weights that leave nothing to be uncertain about), unattractive, or merely unexplored?

Finally, flag any pair of named positions that this three-question tree cannot genuinely separate, and specify what further distinction—perhaps regarding the normative requirement to care equally about all copies—would be needed to split them.

Please do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading; the instrument should be evaluable from the commitments alone. Keep the format formal and suitable for a handout: each branch clearly labeled, each leaf with its position name and its most awkward commitment in one sentence.
