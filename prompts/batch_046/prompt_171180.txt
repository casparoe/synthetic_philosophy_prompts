Student answer: The psi-ontic view treats the wavefunction as a direct representation of physical reality: if two systems are assigned different quantum states, they differ in some real physical property, analogous to differences in a classical field. The psi-epistemic view, by contrast, treats the wavefunction as encoding an observer's limited information about an underlying ontic state, much like a probability distribution over hidden variables in classical statistical mechanics.

A well-known challenge to the epistemic position relies on an assumption called preparation independence. This holds that when two systems are prepared separately, the joint distribution over their underlying ontic states factorizes according to the product of the individual distributions determined by their assigned quantum states. Combining this with certain quantum predictions for measurements on product states yields the result that no ontic state can belong to the support of two distinct quantum-state distributions. Consequently, distinct quantum states cannot correspond to overlapping sets of physical realities, which undermines the core idea that different wavefunctions might reflect mere ignorance.

Some epistemicists respond by rejecting preparation independence. I think this is a fully adequate response, because Spekkens' toy model demonstrates that an epistemic interpretation can reproduce all quantum predictions without that assumption. In Spekkens' framework, the epistemic restriction is embedded in the dynamical rules governing the hidden variables, not in the preparation procedure itself. Therefore, the PBR-style argument fails, and the epistemicist can retain the view that the wavefunction represents knowledge rather than reality.

Nevertheless, there remains a difficulty concerning dynamics. Even on an epistemic interpretation, one must account for why every observer updates beliefs according to the Schrödinger equation. One option is to treat the Schrödinger equation as a literal dynamical law governing the hidden variables, but this seems to concede that the wavefunction has physical significance after all. The alternative is to treat it as a purely epistemic update rule. But then one must explain why independent observers always converge on the same updated state without any shared physical mechanism driving the convergence. So the deepest problem for the epistemic view is not the ontology of preparation, but the objectivity of dynamical evolution.
