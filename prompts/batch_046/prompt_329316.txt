been arguing with my study partner about whether Weber actually proves culture can cause economic change, or if the whole thesis collapses once you push it. here's the version he just laid out and i cant find the exit. (1) For culture to be a cause, values have to explain outcomes that material conditions alone cant predict. (2) So picture two societies with identical tech, resources, and geography, but one values capital accumulation and the other values communal distribution. (3) If values are genuinely causal, the first should accumulate and the second shouldnt, regardless of what institutions they share. (4) But Weber says Calvinist values themselves arose partly from material and psychological pressures - like the anxiety of predestination being resolved through worldly success. (5) So the values arent independent inputs; theyre shaped by the very conditions theyre supposed to explain. (6) That means you can never actually have the "identical societies with different values" scenario from (2), because if conditions are identical the values will be too. (7) So either culture is just a byproduct of material conditions, or its only present when conditions already differ, which makes it redundant as an explanation. (8) Therefore Weber's thesis is either circular or false. I really want to hang onto (2) - that we can imagine value-divergence with material parity. Which step is wrong, and whats the cost of dropping it?
