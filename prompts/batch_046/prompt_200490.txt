Two people in my philosophy of mind group got into it over pain asymbolia. (1) Maya said it proves pain isn't intrinsicaly bad—if you can have the full sensory profile, burning, stabbing, whatever, without any suffering, then the badness is just an emotional overlay, not part of pain itself. (2) Leo replied that asymbolics still reflex-withdraw and rate intensity high, so the "avoid this" content is still in the signal; they just can't register the valence. The badness is decoupled from conscious dislike, not missing. (3) Maya then asked about a hypothetical drug that gives perfect asymbolia but keeps reflexes intact—would users still have reason to avoid the state? If not, she said, there's no intrinsic badness left. (4) Leo said that's mixing up phenomology with normativity: even if they don't mind, the state is still a damage signal, and being damaged is bad for you regardless of how you feel. They want me to referee. Can you rate each move from clearly wrong to clearly right, say who's more right overall, and point out if they're using "badness" in two different ways?
