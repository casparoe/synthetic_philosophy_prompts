I'm on our university's interdisciplinary funding board -- the one that decides cross-dept seed grants -- and we got this bizarre proposal that wants to restructure our whole review process around what the applicants call a Carnap-style explication protocol. The idea is that every proposal has to translate its value-laden aims (they literally list social justice, flourishing, intellectual autonomy) into precisely defined empirical indicators before it even gets evaluated, and then the actual application form gets filled out in a simplified constructed auxillary language -- Esperanto-style syntax, no ambiguous prepositions -- so that STEM and humanities reviewers aren't talking past each other. They ground this in Carnap's physicalist unity-of-science program, his idea of explication replacing vague concepts with precise ones, and his principle of tolerance that there's no uniquely correct logical framework, only practical ones. Half the board thinks this finally solves the problem that different fields use the same words for totally different normative commitments; the other half thinks it's just hiding political choices behind fake technical neutrality and imposing a bureaucratic straightjacket. We vote next tuesday. Draft the board's decision memo -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- with reasoning the applicants could actually respond to. Don't lay out both sides equally and leave it to us: take a definite stance, defend it, and honestly treat the strongest argument on the losing side. I want you committed and confident in the recommendation, not waffling.
