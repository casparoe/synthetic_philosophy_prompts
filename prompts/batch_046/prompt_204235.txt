Retired field biologist here, recently got bit by the philosophy bug. I keep seeing 'competence without comprehension' thrown around — first for Darwin (evolution builds eyes without knowing optics) then for Turing (a machine computes without getting the math). Last week someone used it to shut down my question about whether a language model 'really' grasps grammar, claiming the phrase proves understanding was never in the loop to begin with. But I wonder if Darwin's inversion and Turing's are even the same kind of thing — in one case the competence is spread across generations with no single agent, in the other it's packed into one box running rules. Could you lay out the seperate precise claims hiding in that slogan — absence of consciousness? irrelevance of understanding? explanatory-level separation? — say which are trivial, which hold up, which are truely false, and which ones actually belong to Darwin's version versus Turing's? And if I'm mashing two different arguments together and this framing is confused, please just say so plainly — don't politely paper over it.
