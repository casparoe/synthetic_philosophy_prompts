Case one: the classic setup. Jones decides to kill Smith, Black is lurking ready to force him if he wavers, but Jones does it on his own. I say Jones is fully responsable — he couldn't have done otherwise, so PAP is dead here, at least in its simple form. Case two: you're walking by a pond and can save child A or child B, but not both. So maybe PAP survives in some modified form? Case three: same as case one, except Black can only intervene after Jones has formed the intention, locking it in, not before. Case four: Dr. Lee is one of two identical surgeons on call. If she refuses to operate, her colleague steps in and does the exact same procedure with the same bad outcome (the patient dies from an unavoidable occurence of error). She operates. Here I feel she's NOT responsable in the same way — there's an existant alternative (the colleague), but her choice feels hollow, like she's just a replaceable part. So maybe I'm not really caring about alternatives at all? Case five: Jones doesn't know Black is there. He thinks he could walk away, but Black would actually force him. He kills Smith freely, from his perspective. I still think he's responsable — ignorance of the blocker shouldn't let him off the hook. The tension that's killing me is between case four and case five: both involve "no real escape," but I treat them differently.
