(1) A multipolar trap is when every player has a dominant strategy that makes the group worse off together, like when every student studies extra hard just to keep up on the curve and nobody actually learns more but everyone is stressed out. (2) If every player's strategy is dominant, then one student deciding not to study extra doesn't change the curve or anyone else's behavior—the trap is structurally locked. (3) So in a real multipolar trap, each person is causally powerless over whether the collective bad result happens. (4) But moral blame only makes sense if you could actually make a difference—ought implies can, and you can't blame a rock for falling on someone. (5) So nobody inside the trap is morally responsible for the collective harm, not even teachers who set the curve or tech companies in an arms race. (6) That means climate change, social media engagement wars, and even our grading curve are all blameless situations. (7) But we clearly think teachers and corporations are partly responsible for these things. (8) So either true multipolar traps don't exist, or we can blame people for outcomes they had literally zero power to stop. (9) Traps obviously exist everywhere. (10) Therefore we blame people for things they couldn't change, which sounds crazy.

which step do i reject? i really dont want to give up (4) because it feels right, and (3) seems definitional for what a trap is. is the slip an equivocation between not being able to change the big outcome vs not being able to change your own part in it? or is there a modal slide between "can't alter the result" and "can't avoid contributing"? if i have to pick the least painful option, what am i actually paying—does it mean responsibility doesn't need causal power, or that multipolar traps arent as deterministic as they look?
