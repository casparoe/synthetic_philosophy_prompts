For our philosophy club's consistency audit, I have tried to state my position on consciousness clearly. Here are my commitments: (a) I reject the Cartesian theatre — there is no single, central place in the brain where consciousness is displayed or observed; (b) I accept Dennett's multiple drafts model, according to which cognitive processing produces parallel, competing narratives, none of which is intrinsically the conscious one prior to being reported; (c) nevertheless, I believe that at any given moment there is a single, determinate fact about what I am phenomenally experiencing, not several simultaneous contents; (d) I think the hard problem of consciousness remains genuinely unsolved, since explaining the functional roles of these drafts does not, by itself, explain why there is something it is like to be me; and (e) I have proposed an unconscious curator mechanism — distributed across regions rather than localised, not part of any draft itself, and lacking phenomenal properties — that selects which narrative becomes reportable. Do these hang together? I am particularly unsure whether (b) and (c) can coexist, and whether (e) secretly reintroduces the very theatre I reject in (a), though I also worry that (d) conflicts with accepting (b). Please audit my position: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and rank them by severity; for each, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs; and distinguish these from any tensions that only look problematic but dissolve on inspection. I am not committed to saving any particular claim if consistency requires giving it up.
