Please design a decision tree that classifies theories of emotional states according to their commitments on three conceptual axes, intended for use in a philosophy-of-mind seminar. The tree should begin with this diagnostic question: does the theory treat emotional episodes as necessarily involving an intentional state with specific representational content? The second level should split answers to the first by asking whether that representational content is propositional and conceptual or non-propositional and perceptual. The third level should ask whether bodily arousal or interoceptive change is constitutive of the emotion or merely a causal accompaniment. The fourth level should ask whether the emotional state can be sustained when its apparent intentional object is absent, misidentified, or purely abstract—using concrete cases such as anticipatory grief for a future that never arrives, or sustained moral outrage at an institutional pattern with no localized perceptible target. For each leaf, provide the standard name of the theoretical family (for example, strong cognitivist judgmentalism, embodied perceptualism, pure phenomenal-feeling theory, or enactive relationalism), followed by one sentence stating its most awkward commitment—the specific case or phenomenon that the view must either accommodate at high cost or deny. Additionally, map onto this tree a hybrid position that I will call calibrated salience framing: the claim that emotions are predictive-interoceptive adjustments that restructure attentional weights across modalities without representing either a propositional content or a perceptual object. Indicate at which node, if any, this hybrid genuinely diverges from its nearest neighbor, or flag that the tree cannot separate them. Where the logical space produces a combination of answers that corresponds to no defended view, label that leaf unoccupied and diagnose in one sentence whether the gap is best explained by incoherence, theoretical unattractiveness, or mere historical neglect. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the tree should be evaluable purely from its conceptual architecture.
