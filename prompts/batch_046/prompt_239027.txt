I am a high school senior working on an independent-study essay in philosophy of social science, and I would like comments on my outline and argument before I begin drafting. My topic is whether generational labels—Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z, and subsequent cohorts—refer to real kinds, or whether they are merely demographic artifacts with no genuine ontological weight. In my notes so far, I argue that these cohorts fail standard tests for natural kinds: they lack essential properties, do not support robust projectible generalizations across members, and possess no unified causal homeostasis that would make them scientifically respectable categories. From there, I propose that they are best understood as institutional kinds constructed by marketing practices, census methodologies, and policy frameworks rather than by shared intrinsic features.

However, I want to push the argument further with a framing that is not usually applied to sociology: I suggest that generational terms function analogously to indexicals in the philosophy of language. Just as the reference of “now” shifts with the speaker’s temporal position, the extension of “Gen Z” shifts depending on when and by whom the term is uttered—there is no stable, speaker-independent birth range that fixes membership across all contexts. On this view, generations are not merely constructed kinds but what I am calling “indexical kinds”: categories whose membership conditions are context-dependent rather than essence-tracking, so that asserting “Gen Z is politically anxious” does not predicate a property of a stable group but performs a context-relative classification.

I am especially uncertain about three moves. Second, have I underestimated collective-memory arguments that treat shared historical events—economic recessions, technological shifts, pandemics—as producing genuinely shared psychological traits across cohorts, thereby giving generations a non-indexical, interactional reality sustained by reciprocal recognition among members? Third, is my overall structure—moving from natural-kind failure to institutional construction to indexical analysis—conceptually coherent, or does it read as three disconnected papers stitched together?

Could you read these notes as if they were the skeleton of my essay and comment on them?
