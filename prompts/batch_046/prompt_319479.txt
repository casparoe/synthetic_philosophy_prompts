I'm trying to understand a recurring argumentative pattern in discussions of "Western values," and I wonder whether it already carries an established technical name. The phrase is usually introduced as if it named a coherent descriptive cluster—liberal democracy, secular governance, individual rights, gender equality, and the rule of law—yet I keep noticing a specific maneuver. When a society conventionally classified as Western violates one of these items—say, by imposing broad speech restrictions or displaying persistent gender disparity in executive leadership—the failure is rarely treated as disconfirming membership in the cluster. Instead, defenders invoke an implicit meta-value, often framed as "self-correction," "open debate," or "the capacity for reform," and absorb the counterexample by claiming that this very capacity to revise errors is itself the defining Western value. Meanwhile, critics deploy the same failures to argue that "Western values" is merely an ideological construct, and non-Western societies that institutionalize several of the listed practices—but without this self-corrective framing—are often said not to possess Western values after all. So the category's boundaries oscillate with the speaker's purpose: descriptively it is a list of institutions, normatively it is a mechanism of revision, and the shift is always presented as if the boundary were stable rather than strategically adjusted.

I am familiar with "essentially contested concepts," Wittgensteinian family resemblance, the "no true Scotsman" fallacy, and strategic essentialism, but none of these quite captures the specific pattern I mean: a descriptive cluster that is systematically protected from counterexample by an implicit normative meta-criterion, where that meta-criterion is itself treated as part of the original cluster rather than as an external defense. Does this configuration—this elasticity anchored in a self-correction clause—have an established name in political philosophy, rhetoric, or conceptual analysis? If there is no precise term, how should one define it, and what are its boundary conditions—specifically, how does it differ from ordinary strategic essentialism on one side and from mere contradiction or empty tautology on the other?

Please say what would change your mind: what specific argument or counterexample would convince you that this is just an unremarkable instance of family resemblance or of rhetorical repositioning, and where is your reasoning about its distinctiveness most likely to fail?
