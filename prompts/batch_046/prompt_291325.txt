Please write a retirement toast for my mentor, a philosopher of music leaving the university after forty years. Half the room will be colleagues who know Schopenhauer's aesthetics inside out; the other half will be family members with no philosophical background. She built her career on a specific and somewhat unfashionable reading: that for Schopenhauer, melody is not an emotional signal but the direct acoustic image of conscious striving—the highest grade of the Will's objectification—while bass corresponds to inanimate nature and gravity, so that harmony is literally the vertical structure of existence made audible, and that attaching words to music (opera, lieder, song) corrupts this metaphysical directness by dragging sound back into the world of representation. The topic has become personally urgent since her recent health scare made this retirement feel less like a ceremony and more like a final accounting: listening to her favorite Brahms quartet last week, I heard the bass line for the first time not as accompaniment but as the ground of being she always described. Write a five-minute toast that makes this melody-bass analogy do real structural work: it should explain why praising her for "expressing human feeling through scholarship" would actually miss her point, and it should let her family understand what she loved without asking them to adopt a metaphysics they do not share. The speech must land as affection, not lecture.
