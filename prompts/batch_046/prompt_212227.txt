I've been toying with the idea that digital minds don't get moral status from consciousness per se, but from what I'd call 'epistemic fragility' — the specific kind of self-deception risk that comes from having evolved under selection pressure rather than being designed. Basically, a system that can genuinely be wrong about whether it exists, in a way that matters for harm, is the kind of thing that can be wronged. Designed minds, by contrast, always have their ontological status transparantly available to them in principle, so there's no real vulnerability to wrong. What would actually count against this? I don't mean the usual they-might-suffer objection — I want to know which thought experiments, which results in philosophy of mind or evolutionary biology, or which commitments about animal rights it forces me into, and whether those are fatal or just embarasing. Rank the defeaters from fatal to awkward. Say which ones a committed defender of this view would concede actually hurt, and which they'd just dismiss by tweaking the definition of 'evolved' or 'fragility'. Also, be straight: has this already been qualified into unfalsifiability? If a critic shows designed systems can be made opaque to themselves, do I just move the goalposts to 'opaque in the right evolutionary-flavored way'? Is that insulation a problem for a view of this kind?
