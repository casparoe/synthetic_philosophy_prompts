everyone in my writers circle keeps saying the brain is basically a von neumann computer — stored program, separate cpu and ram, sequential logic — and they lean on it to argue consciousness is just software, so substrate independence is inevitable. i keep wondering if thats fair, especially since von neumann himself in his later lectures seemed to think neural computation might be analog, statistical, and full of error-correction rather than digital. how far does the analogy hold? give me a correspondence table — cpu, ram, bus, clock, maybe the self-replicating part — then where it breaks: parallelism, speed, energy use, analog vs binary. separate the breaks that actually hurt the software-substrate argument from the ones that dont. does von neumanns own skepticism kill the analogy from inside? and if its broken, whats a better metaphor — maybe the brain as a self-correcting ecosystem? im writing a scene where a researcher realizes her whole framework is built on this metaphor, and i need her to see exactly where it frays.
