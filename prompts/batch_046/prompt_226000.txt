Ive been trying to pin down the principle behind my gut reactions to these suffering/pleasure tradeoffs, and I dont think there is one. Case 1: a universe with one person in extreme bliss for 100 years vs one with 10^20 people feeling a micro-headache for a second each - I feel the second is worse, but not by an infinite margin. Case 2: a drug that eliminates all pain but also caps joy at mild contentment - I think thats a net win, which sounds lexical. Case 3: damage that happens during dreamless sleep, never experienced - I dont think that counts as suffering at all, even though its physically identical to pain. Case 4: the satisfaction people get from overcoming hardship disappears if hardship is removed, so wiping out all suffering might also wipe out some goods that depend on it. Case 5: a life with one horrific trauma then decades of calm vs a life of constant low-level irritation - I actually prefer the trauma life, which seems to contradict both lexical and simple summing views. Is there a coherent principle that saves all these verdicts, or do I have to drop one? Im espeically wondering if theres an existing view that treats experienced suffering as lexically bad but lets non-experienced damage and hardship-dependent goods break the pattern.
