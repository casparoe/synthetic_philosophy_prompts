Design a take-home essay question for a PhD-level comprehensive examination in decision theory and the philosophy of rationality. The response should be written in 90 minutes, should run to approximately 1,200 words, and carries 40 marks out of 100. The question must center on the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery—both superrationality experiments involving symmetric reasoners facing a coordination problem with no communication permitted—but it must not be answerable by summarizing the canonical presentations of these puzzles. Instead, construct a problem that forces the examinee to adjudicate a specific tension between symmetric reasoning and asymmetrical information.

Present the examinee with the following modified Platonia-style scenario: twenty reasoners face the standard rule that if exactly one sends a telegram by noon the next day, that reasoner receives a billion dollars; if zero or more than one send telegrams, all receive nothing, and cooperation is forbidden. Unlike the canonical version, each reasoner knows with certainty that exactly five of the twenty are committed expected-utility maximizers who will never randomize and will either always send or always refrain according to a fixed private rule unknown to the others. The remaining fifteen are superrational agents who recognize one another's rationality but do not know which fifteen positions they occupy, nor do they know which five are the maximizers. The examinee must determine whether the fifteen superrational agents should still apply a uniform 1/20 randomization, whether they should condition their probabilities on the presence of the five non-randomizers, and whether such conditioning violates the symmetry condition that grounds superrational reasoning. The examinee must also address the analogous problem in the Luring Lottery: if contestants have heterogeneous beliefs about others' rationality—some treating all opponents as superrational die-rollers, others treating a subset as unbounded entry-submittors—whether the superrational strategy of rolling a simulated die with sides equal to expected responders collapses or must be recalibrated.

In your delivery, provide: (i) the exam prompt itself, phrased precisely and with all payoff conditions, information structures, and constraints made explicit; (ii) a model answer that identifies whether and how the fifteen agents should adjust their randomization probabilities, explains why conditioning on type-knowledge either preserves or undermines the symmetry argument, resolves the self-location problem for an agent who knows she is among the fifteen but not which one, and draws the parallel to prize dilution in the Luring Lottery under heterogeneous rationality beliefs; (iii) a marking scheme that allocates 10 marks to the correct identification of the symmetry-breaking problem introduced by the five maximizers, 10 marks to the probabilistic reasoning about whether the pool should be treated as twenty or fifteen, 10 marks to the comparison with the Luring Lottery's die-rolling strategy and its vulnerability to beliefs about non-rational opponents, and 10 marks to the evaluation of whether superrationality collapses into standard game-theoretic reasoning when common knowledge of rationality is only partial. For each section, specify the misconceptions that should cost marks—such as treating the five maximizers as merely reducing the effective pool to fifteen without addressing self-location, ignoring the distinction between knowing types and knowing positions, conflating the Platonia winner-takes-all structure with the Luring Lottery's linear prize dilution, or assuming that partial common knowledge automatically licenses standard Nash reasoning.

Keep the question free of bibliographic demands: do not ask examinees for citations, reading lists, or references to specific editions. Ensure that the prompt injects enough conceptual specificity—particularly the asymmetry between known-type and unknown-position reasoners, and the requirement to compare two distinct payoff structures—that a respondent relying only on general pretraining about superrationality cannot produce a complete answer without engaging the injected framing.
