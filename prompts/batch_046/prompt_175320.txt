I am a doctoral student in comparative philosophy writing on early Chinese logic and the philosophy of language. Before I finalize a chapter, I want an audit of my interpretive package on the White Horse dialogue and the School of Names (Mingjia). Here is my current position:

(1) I interpret Gongsun Long's claim that a white horse is not a horse as a rigorous argument about non-identical class membership: horse denotes the class defined solely by form (xing), whereas white horse denotes the class defined by form together with color (se), and I take se to function here as a logical differentia rather than as an accidental perceptual property.

(2) I think the Mingjia should be read primarily within the Warring States discourse on zhengming—rectification of names and the alignment of linguistic categories with political and social order—rather than treated as an early variant of Western analytic philosophy.

(3) I hold that rendering the dialogue's inference in modern set-theoretic notation clarifies its logical structure without importing anachronistic metaphysics, on the condition that the notation is used only as a heuristic.

(4) I take the dialogue's philosophical point to be semantic rather than metaphysical: compound descriptions carry different referential conditions from simple ones, which anticipates something like the distinction between intension and extension.

(5) I reject the sophistic reading that treats the argument as a rhetorical paradox; I believe Gongsun Long advances a substantive thesis about how descriptive content determines reference.

(6) I also maintain that the dialogue's emphasis on the separability (fen) of form and color ties the Mingjia more closely to Mohist theories of distinguishing kinds (bie) than to Confucian name-rectification, and that this Mohist connection should serve as the primary contextual frame for interpretation.

Do these commitments hang together? I am particularly uncertain about the relationship between (2) and (6), since insisting on a Confucian political-ethical framing of the Mingjia seems hard to square with assigning Mohist logic the primary contextual role. I also wonder whether (1) and (4) pull in opposite directions—whether an extensional class-membership reading can coexist with an intensional semantic reading—or whether they actually support each other. And I am unsure whether (3) is defensible given (2). At the same time, I may be inventing conflicts where the distinctions dissolve cleanly.

Please audit this set: rank the tensions by severity, specify for each pair what must be revised or qualified and at what cost, and identify any apparent conflicts that disappear once the relevant distinctions are drawn precisely.
