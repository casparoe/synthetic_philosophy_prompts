Write a short dialogue between Ava and Ben about a tweaked Thomson lamp. Instead of just toggling on and off at 1/2, 1/4, 1/8... seconds, this version writes a bit to an internal register each time—1 if it's on, 0 if it's off—so after two minutes there's an infinite binary sequence stored inside. Ava thinks this proves supertasks are impossible: there's no last toggle to determine the register's overall parity, so the lamp ends in an undefined state. Ben says that's mistaken—the register just reaches a limit-state that encodes the whole sequence, and the paradox only arises if you assume every property at the limit needs a final causal step. Have them actually argue back and forth: Ava should ask whether that limit-state is physically readable, and Ben should press her on why parity ought to be well-defined at the limit anyway. Write it casually with British spelling (e.g. behaviour, programme), and slip in a typo like 'recieve' somewhere. Don't ask for citations or a bibliography.
