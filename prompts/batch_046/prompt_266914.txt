You are acting as a professional philosopher with expertise in normative ethics, metaphysics of personal identity, and population ethics. Before drafting, take a moment to brainstorm the conceptual landscape: distinguish person-affecting from impersonal axiologies, identify where the non-identity problem intersects with procreative reasons, and clarify what it means for a potential being to be harmed or benefited by being brought into existence.

Your task is to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps regarding the refined procreation asymmetry. Camp One, the Conditional Asymmetry Defenders, holds that there exists a robust, non-instrumental moral reason not to create a being who will experience net suffering, yet there is no mirror-image reason to create a being who will experience net happiness, and they maintain that this holds even when prospective parents have near-certainty about the welfare trajectory and even when the potential child could not be said to have been deprived of existence. Camp Two, the Modal Symmetry Defenders, denies this asymmetry; they argue that if the reason against creating suffering is genuinely moral rather than merely prudential, then consistency demands either a parallel reason in favor of creating happiness—the beneficence symmetry—or a dissolution of the negative reason altogether once we reject the idea that never-existing beings can be wronged.

The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims—statements both camps can sign without reservation—including shared commitments about the formal structure of the problem (e.g., that it concerns actual or counterfactual reproductive choices rather than general population policy), about the irrelevance or precise relevance of the non-identity problem to the asymmetry's core formulation, and about the inadequacy of purely hedonistic aggregation to resolve the dispute. Next, present a disagreements table: for each camp, state the precise formulation of their view in wording that the camp itself would accept, followed by the exact point of divergence—particularly regarding whether counterfactual harm to a never-actualized being is a coherent normative category and whether moral reasons can be directed at modal individuals who never achieve actuality.

Then identify the cruxes. I suggest at least three: (i) whether a comparative account of harm, requiring a baseline of actual existence, is necessary for the negative procreative reason, or whether a non-comparative account can ground it independently; (ii) whether the asymmetry survives if we adopt a modal framework in which possible persons possess a form of moral standing prior to actualization; and (iii) whether there is a relevant disanalogy between the suffering case and the happiness case involving epistemic risk, parental obligations, or the structure of consent. For each crux, provide a concrete, agreed-upon test case—specifically, the Twin-World Reproduction Scenario, in which parents can choose between conceiving Child A (who will live a life of net positive welfare punctuated by predictable, severe, but non-fatal migraines) versus remaining childless, with all ecological and social externalities held constant. Each camp should record, in advance, what they predict about this case: does it generate a pro tanto reason for or against conception? Is the reason stronger, weaker, or absent compared to the mirror-image suffering case?

Conclude by separating genuine disagreement from verbal confusion. Specifically, address whether part of the dispute turns on ambiguous uses of harm, reason, or potential person, and if so, specify the disambiguated formulations each camp accepts. The final document should be something both sides could put their names to, with residual disagreement rendered exact rather than rhetorical. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not appeal to niche technical apparatus that requires specialized literature knowledge; and keep the focus on conceptual clarification and predictive agreement about the test cases.
