Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, rhyme scheme ABA with two refrains—staging the tension between inside-view reasoning and outside-view epistemic modesty through a specific, niche scenario. The speaker should be a climate scientist who has produced an outlier warming projection driven by a feedback loop she has modeled in mechanistic detail, while the multi-model ensemble clusters far lower. Refrain A (appearing in lines 1, 6, 12, and 18) should voice the inside view: a claim of causal transparency and first-person expertise, something like I traced the loop and the physics answers to me. Refrain B (appearing in lines 3, 9, 15, and 19) should voice the outside view: a statistical self-correction, something like The base rate says outliers are usually wrong. Let the refrains take turns through the tercets without synthesis, and in the final quatrain place them side by side so that the form’s usual closure is refused and the epistemic standoff remains open. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with forecasting and self-trust recognizes the distinction—inside view relying on model transparency, outside view on reference-class forecasting—while still functioning as poetry for someone who knows nothing of the debate. After the villanelle, provide one section titled Commentary on the choices made, approximately 150 words, explaining how the villanelle’s cyclical repetition embodies the recursive structure of self-trust versus statistical skepticism, and describing any formal adjustments you made to keep the ending unresolved.
