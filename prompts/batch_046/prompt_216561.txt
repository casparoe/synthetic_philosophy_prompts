I'm on the ethics advisery board for our regional hospital network (we cover like 12 facilities) and we've got a vote coming up next tuesday on a pilot program in the paliative care units. A group from the philosophy of medicine dept wants to run a six-month trial where counselors integrate Zhuangist ideas about death into pre-bereavement sessions — specifically the view that dying is just a transformation of qi, like winter becoming spring, and that excessive grief comes from confusing the completed form with the completed mind. The families would be guided to write transformation letters rather than goodbye letters, framing the loss as a change of state rather than an ending. Half the board thinks this is just culturally responsive care; the other half thinks we're sneaking in a metaphysical claim that personal identity doesn't survive and that grief is irrational, which could really harm families with religious beliefs about souls or afterlives.

There's also this niche twist in the proposal: they apply the form/mind distinction specifically to neurodegenerative patients, arguing that in advanced dementia the mind has already transformed, so family grief is doubly misdirected. I need a formal memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with reasoning the researchers could actually engage with, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument for the side that loses. Don't just cite generic bioethics principles, deal with the Zhuangist metaphysics and the dementia wrinkle specifically. Oh, and please no bibliography or reading list — half our board can't access the university database anyway.
