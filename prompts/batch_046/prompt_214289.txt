hey, i'm a backend dev with basically no formal phil training and i keep going in circles on this bernard williams idea about one thought too many. he says if you save your spouse from drowning only after running some utilitarian calculus, you've added an unnecessary mental step and lost integrity — the right move is just its my wife, period. i tried extending this to my own work and now the argument seems to blow up:

(1) if williams is right, then any time you act for someone you care about, your reason should be direct and not filtered through a general principle.

(2) but saying its my wife still uses the general category wife — she's one instance of a role. so even the direct reason includes a classification.

(3) therefore if including any classification counts as mediation, then williams's own example fails: you cant have a direct reason without some category.

(4) but if including a classification doesnt count, then when i refuse to ship user-tracking malware and cite the ACM ethics code, thats also just identifying myself via a role (engineer with obligations). so that shouldnt count as mediation either.

(5) yet williams clearly wants to allow its my wife and condemn utilitarian calculation. so he owes us a principel line between okay classifications and corrupt mediating steps.

(6) i cant see that line. without it, the objection applies to every reason we ever state, or to none, which makes it useless.

(7) so either (1) is wrong and integrity actually permits some mediation, or the whole one-thought-too-many idea collapses into vagueness.

i'd prefer to keep (1) and (4) — they match my gut — but i think the cheat might be in (3) treating a descriptive category as a normative principle? or maybe in (5) assuming williams needs a sharp boundary? which step should go, and whats the cost of cutting it?
