For a paper I am now writing on the thermodynamics of computation, the Maxwell's demon problem has become personally unavoidable, and I want to check whether my own package of commitments actually coheres. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept Szilard's analysis of the single-molecule engine: acquiring one bit of information about a particle's location permits the extraction of kT ln 2 of work, so information and energy stand in an exact quantitative relation.

(2) I interpret Landauer's principle—erasing one bit dissipates at least kT ln 2—as a claim about the physical implementation of logically irreversible operations, not as evidence that information itself is a kind of substance or that "information is physical" in any ontologically weighty sense.

(3) I hold an epistemic conception of thermodynamic entropy, in the Jaynesian vein: entropy measures our ignorance of microstates, not an intrinsic, mind-independent feature of physical systems.

(4) I think the demon demonstrates that the second law is a statistical regularity rather than a strict dynamical law, and that a well-designed mechanism can systematically lower local entropy without requiring some hidden global compensating increase—though I concede that the mechanism's own physical operation will generate heat.

(5) I reject any resolution that appeals to consciousness, intentionality, or mental representation; a deterministic automaton equipped with nothing more than a one-bit memory register and a gate is sufficient to execute the protocol.

(6) At the same time, I take the Szilard-Landauer interconversion to support a form of informational physicalism: bits belong in our fundamental ontology alongside energy and momentum, and the quantifiable exchange between information and work reveals a deep structural feature of nature, not a mere bookkeeping artifact.

Do these hang together? I see possible friction between (2) and (6)—if information is not physically substantial, why should bits be ontologically fundamental?—and between (3) and (6)—if entropy is just epistemic, what makes information ontologically serious? I also worry that (4) conflicts with (1): if information genuinely permits work extraction, does that not already imply that the local entropy decrease is paid for by the information acquisition, undermining my claim that no hidden compensation is needed? But perhaps these are only apparent.

Please audit this set. For each pair in genuine tension, state how severe it is, what exactly has to give, and what the cheapest revision would cost. Identify any tensions that dissolve on inspection. Rank the real conflicts by severity, and tell me which single revision—if I must make one—would clean up the most inconsistencies at the least philosophical cost. Be specific about which commitments have incompatible motivations.
