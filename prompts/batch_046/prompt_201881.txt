She argues that this divergence should be treated as a primary welfare indicator rather than subsumed under generic water-quality metrics, and that because thermal preference is individually variable and developmentally plastic, aggregate welfare assessments must rely on dynamic, individual-level baselines rather than species-wide norms. Critics respond that such granularity is administratively intractable at commercial scale and risks anthropomorphizing metabolic states into subjective distress.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. Include at least one further question that probes the boundary between her welfare-scientific argument and broader anti-speciesist claims about invertebrate moral status, anticipating her standard deflection that her position is strictly empirical and carries no metaphysical commitment to sentience. The questions should assume familiarity with current aquaculture welfare science but should not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
