Write a strict sestina on the historiographical problem of treating non-human forces—specifically a volcanic winter, a viral pandemic, and an abrupt climate shift—as agents in human history without collapsing into environmental determinism. Use these six end-words in the first stanza, in order: winter, host, record, silence, cause, breath. Let the sestina's rotating form do conceptual work: across the stanzas, shift which force occupies the grammatical position of agent, instrument, and silent background, so that "cause" and "host" exchange roles with "winter" and "breath" in the rotation. In the three-line envoi, place "record" and "silence" together to stage whether nature's interventions leave an archive or only an absence of human intention, leaving the determinism/peril unresolved. Write it precisely enough that a specialist in philosophy of history or science studies recognizes the tension between ascribing agency to germs, climate, and volcanoes and reducing them to deterministic conditions, but as a poem that stands on its own for a reader without that vocabulary. Do not cite historians or named theories.
