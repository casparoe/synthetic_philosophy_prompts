I've been working through Sartre's Being and Nothingness for an advanced reading group, and I want to pressure-test whether his account of radical freedom can accommodate ordinary institutional life without collapsing into contradiction. Here is an argument I've constructed that seems to prove far too much: (1) Radical freedom means consciousness perpetually transcends every facticity—no social role, biological given, or historical position can constitute its essence. (2) Bad faith is the flight from this transcendence, the attempt to identify oneself with a fixed being-in-itself, as in Sartre's café waiter who plays at being a waiter in order to avoid the anxiety of choosing. (3) Institutional roles—citizen, judge, parent—are not self-authored projects but positions assigned and sustained by bureaucratic structures that function as being-for-others; they exist independently of the consciousness that happens to occupy them. (4) Therefore, to inhabit such a role without constantly maintaining the anxious awareness that one could reject it is to collapse one's transcendence into an assigned facticity, which is precisely bad faith. (5) So, on Sartre's view, any authentic existence requires perpetual refusal or ironic detachment from every institutional assignment; sincere civic participation is structurally inauthentic. (6) But this conclusion is clearly wrong: Sartre distinguishes authentic commitment—resistance, artistic creation, love—from bad faith, and these commitments often depend on sustained social recognition and role-performance rather than on perpetual refusal. (7) Hence one premise must fail. Which step should I reject? I am especially reluctant to abandon (1) or (5), because I think Sartre genuinely holds that freedom is unconditional and that institutional facticity is real; if those stand, I need to know whether (4) can be salvaged by distinguishing modes of role-acceptance, or whether the price of consistency is admitting that Sartre's ethics cannot accommodate ordinary civic life. Please explain why the targeted step looked plausible, diagnose any subtle slide—whether an equivocation on transcendence, a scope shift, or a hidden modal assumption—if there is one, and price the alternatives clearly.
