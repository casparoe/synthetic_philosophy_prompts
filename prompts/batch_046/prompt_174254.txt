Jordan holds the 'thick' view: it proves the existence of a unified subject of experience, since the very execution of methodical doubt presupposes a single locus that performs the doubting, and without such a locus there is nothing that could be deceived.

Alex should deploy this case to show that first-person reports can arise without any unified thinker, and therefore that the cogito need not establish a substantial self. Jordan should reply by arguing that such a system lacks the integrated phenomenal field required for genuine doubt, so that the cogito's conditions are not actually met.

The exchange should be a real back-and-forth: each speaker must address the specific point the other has just made, not change the subject. Neither should be presented as a strawman. The prompt should not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and should not invoke obscure specialist doctrines that a well-read but non-specialist model could not be expected to know. Keep the language formal and precise.
