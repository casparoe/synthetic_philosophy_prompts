For my seminar on strange loops and hierarchical ontology, I want to evaluate theories that admit tangled hierarchies rather than clean stratifications, using Escher's Drawing Hands as the central test case—specifically the mutual dependence between the hand that draws and the hand that is drawn, which seems to collapse the distinction between representational level and produced object. Before comparing theories, write the full list of desiderata: what any adequate account of hierarchical levels must deliver once such tangling is permitted. Each desideratum should come with its motivating case or conceptual distinction—for example, how to treat self-embedding without circular causation, whether an "inviolate level" can survive closer inspection or must be dynamically reassigned (as in what I call the restoration paradox, where fixing one hand's ground-level status destabilizes the other), and how logical typing differs from causal production. Flag which pairs are jointly unsatisfiable—particularly well-foundedness versus full mutual dependence, and static base-level commitment versus dynamic level-assignment. Then briefly show how naive stratification, a Russell-style type hierarchy, and a strange-loop model stand against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria rather than a full scorecard. No bibliographies or citations; I need the conceptual specification only.
