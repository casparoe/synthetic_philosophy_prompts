The Epicurean argument that death cannot harm the dead—because there is no existing subject to bear the harm—and the deprivationist counter that it robs one of future goods are both well rehearsed, yet the debate has barely shifted in decades. I want a diagnosis, not a survey: is the impasse substantive, turning on whether welfare can be assigned to counterfactual lives with no present bearer; is it verbal, about whether "bad for S" requires S to exist at the time of evaluation; or are the sides answering different questions—one about experienced suffering, one about axiological loss? Specifically, could a formal distinction between experienced harm and counterfactual welfare deficit, or a result in personal identity about diachronic concern, break the deadlock, or is the disagreement ultimately bedrock?
