The setting is an upper-level philosophy seminar of fourteen students, ninety minutes, with no prior lecture on the material. I want the idea to emerge from the rules, not from explanation. To ensure the exercise is specific rather than generic, please build in the following conceptual injection: center the simulation on what I will term numerical anonymity, the precise procedural moment when discrete, signed judgments are converted into an unsigned statistical aggregate, and that aggregate is then treated as an authoritative position that each original speaker can disown.

Design a structured simulation with explicit roles, materials that require no technology, and a payoff scheme of small social stakes — exposure, attribution, or the obligation to defend — rather than money. The group should move through three phases in sequence: individual conviction expressed in writing; mediation and aggregation that strips authorship; and collective response to the aggregated output. Arrange the incentives so that participants feel relief, not outrage, when their name disappears into the number, and so that they discover only afterward that the collective statement they have endorsed contradicts their private judgments.
