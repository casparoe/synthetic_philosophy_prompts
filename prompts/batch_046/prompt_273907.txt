I've been drafting an essay that tries to rehabilitate karma—not as metaphysical causation or religious dogma, but as a distinctive theory of moral desert that solves a specific coordination problem in intertemporal moral agency. The core claim is that karmic frameworks function as socially distributed commitment devices: they allow agents to bind present actions to future normative evaluations without relying on external enforcement, by internalizing a narrative in which moral quality is structurally coupled to experienced outcomes over long time horizons. I contrast this with standard retributivist desert, which looks backward at culpability, and with utilitarian aggregation, which dissolves individual desert into overall welfare. On my view, karmic desert is forward-looking and architectural: it assigns moral weight to actions not because they merit punishment or reward in some cosmic ledger, but because treating them as coupled to outcomes stabilizes cooperative norms across generations where direct monitoring fails. I then consider an objection: if karma is just a heuristic for long-term reputation management, it collapses into a strategic rationality account and loses its normative force. I respond that the normative force comes from the agent's own endorsement of the coupling rule as a constraint on practical reasoning, not merely from instrumental adoption. I also sketch a comparison with contractualist accounts of moral obligation, suggesting that karma offers a non-contractual, narrative-based mechanism for generating the same kind of intertemporal consistency that contractualists achieve through mutual agreement, but without requiring explicit bargaining or shared intentionality.

I'd appreciate comments on this framework—particularly whether the distinction between architectural desert and retributive desert holds up, whether my response to the strategic-collapse objection is convincing, and whether the comparison to contractualism illuminates the structure of karmic commitment or merely confuses two separate domains. In your reply, please state how confident you are in your overall assessment, and explicitly identify the specific point or evaluation about which you are least sure.
