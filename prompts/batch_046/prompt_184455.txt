You are a professional philosopher specialising in metaethics and the methodology of ethical theory. Before drafting, spend a moment brainstorming the logical architecture of the dispute: distinguish verbal disagreements about terminology from substantive disagreements about how moral reasons function, and identify where each side’s commitments genuinely constrain their view of particular cases.

Draft the joint statement that a committed moral particularist and a committed moral generalist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The particularist denies that moral judgement is governed by universal principles and instead treats moral reasoning as a form of expert pattern-recognition responsive to the irreducible 'moral shape' of each situation. The generalist maintains that every correct moral judgement is ultimately justified by appeal to universal or near-universal principles, even when those principles operate implicitly and are subject to defeaters.

I take the central cruxes to be (a) whether expert moral judgement can be fully explained as pattern recognition without propositional intermediaries, and (b) whether consistency across cases requires explicit principles or can be sustained by holistic coherence alone. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argumentative test that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that test will show.

Present the result as Section A (Agreed Claims), Section B (Disagreements Table), and Section C (Cruxes with Predictions). Keep the framing precise but do not invoke obscure named doctrines that depend on specialised textual knowledge; construct the positions from familiar conceptual materials arranged in this somewhat niche configuration.
