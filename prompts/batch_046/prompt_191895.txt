write a short story about a workshop that restores old printing blocks. the craftsperson is sorting blocks for the letter 'a' — some roman, some italic, some damaged so the bowl is open or the stem curves like a question mark. an apprentice asks why one block with no enclosed bowl still counts as an 'a' while another with a perfect bowl gets rejected for being to 'o'-like. the craftsperson explains that being an 'a' isnt about having one correct geometry but about belonging to a style family with shared structural gestures, not just outline. end with them both staring at a block that could be either letter and neither can quite decide, so the ambiguity itself is the point. keep it philosophical but concrete, and dont resolve the tension too neatly.
