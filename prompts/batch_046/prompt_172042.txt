Write a deadpan parody of a municipal government form—titled "Application for Commemorative Modification and Mnemonic Debt Assessment"—that city staff must complete when proposing to rename a street or alter a public monument. Structure it as numbered bureaucratic sections (e.g., Honorific Function Declaration, Mnemonic Continuity Analysis, Counter-Commemorative Offset Proposal, Unresolvable Value Judgment Disclosure). The satire should operate through the form's own logic: it must rigorously distinguish honoring a figure from merely recording history; require that any removal generate a physically present "counter-monument" to those previously erased; and calculate a "mnemonic debt" that can never be fully repaid, with specific checkbox criteria. Do not state the objections explicitly; let them emerge from the absurd consistency of the procedure. Include a worked example applying the form to a proposal to rename a boulevard honoring a 19th-century industrialist whose wealth involved forced labor, showing how the form's internal rules force the applicant into an undesirable but logically required conclusion—for instance, that the replacement monument must be physically larger than the original yet aesthetically invisible to avoid generating new honorific claims. Keep the voice flat and procedural, readable without specialist knowledge but sharp for readers familiar with debates in the ethics of commemoration. About 600 words. Explicitly flag the points where the form's criteria are genuinely unresolvable value judgments disguised as administrative checkboxes, and state what the procedure cannot settle even after all boxes are completed.
