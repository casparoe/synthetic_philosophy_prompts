Write an essay on the normative significance of undefined expected values, taking the Pasadena paradox as your central example. Explain why the Pasadena game produces an expectation that is not merely infinite but genuinely undefined—dependent on the ordering of outcomes—rather than a simple St. Petersburg divergence. Then evaluate two competing responses: (i) the view that undefined expectation should act as a rational veto, preventing acceptance of the bet regardless of entry cost; and (ii) the view that rational agents should apply a physically grounded truncation rule, treating undefined expectation as an artifact of an unrealistic payoff structure rather than a decisive reason to reject low-cost entry. Defend one of these positions, or a qualified intermediate stance, and justify your verdict with explicit attention to whether expected-utility theory itself should be modified or merely applied with contextual restrictions.
