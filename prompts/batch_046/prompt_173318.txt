You know the usual evidential cooperation story: if there are loads of copies of you out there, you reason about what they do and cooperate. Her twist is that the pact doesn't require shared values, only a shared decision-theoretic architecture. So she thinks you can strike evidential deals even with copies that are pure egoists or actively hostile, provided you're all running the same causal-decision algorithm. She loves to bat away practical objections by saying that's a scaling problem, not a coherence problem.

I want a sequence of questions that makes that deflection progressively harder to pull off. Start by asking how she distinguishes her view from standard multiverse bargaining where values have to align at least partly. Then push on the case where two copies share the algorithm but differ on whether to apply it updatelessly or updatefully — does the pact survive, or does it fracture over the prior? After that, ask about copies running in simulations with incompatible physical laws, where they can't in principle observe each other's outcomes — she can't call that a scaling issue, can she? Finish with one sharp question about what kind of result — theoretical or observational — would actually make her drop the view rather than just adjusting the scaling parameters.

Please answer in British English, and seperate what's well-established in the decision-theory literature from what's genuinely contested about her twist — don't present the asymmetric-pact idea as if there's consensus behind it. There's probably a typo in my brief above but thats fine.
