Consider a modified variant of Dennett's brain-transplant thought experiment: rather than extracting a single brain into a vat and producing one discrete duplicate, imagine that an agent's complete neural-functional architecture is gradually migrated onto a distributed, non-localized synthetic substrate over an extended interval, so that for a significant period both the decaying biological brain and the distributed system maintain active psychological continuity with the original person.

Rank the following five ontological responses to this scenario according to how well each resolves the "where am I?" question—identifying which system, if any, constitutes the true locus of the agent's self—while remaining consistent with physicalist commitments and avoiding arbitrary duplication of identity. Use a tier list (S-tier: fully satisfactory; A-tier: satisfactory with minor costs; B-tier: workable but significant costs; C-tier: largely unsatisfactory; D-tier: incoherent within this framing), and provide a single concise sentence justifying each placement.

The five responses are:

1. The strict substrate-criterion view: personal identity is anchored to the original biological brain, so the agent remains with it until biological death, regardless of functional replication elsewhere.

2. The uninterrupted functional-flow view: identity tracks the continuous stream of psychological processes; once the distributed system dominates causal-functional output, the agent smoothly transitions to it.

3. The no-branching uniqueness constraint: because identity requires a single continuous trajectory and both substrates briefly share continuity, neither fully inherits the agent's identity during overlap, producing a temporary ontological gap.

4. The relational-constitutive view: identity is constituted by social recognition and narrative integration, so the agent is "located" wherever the relevant community treats the continuous psychological profile as authoritative, irrespective of substrate.

5. The access-pathway indexing model: identity is indexed not to substrate or bare psychology but to the intact loop of privileged first-person information access; during migration, the agent is localized at the hybrid interface where that access loop remains unified, which may be neither substrate exclusively.

Keep your evaluation focused on internal coherence and fidelity to the core intuitions that drive Dennett's original puzzle, rather than on empirical neuroscience or technological feasibility.
