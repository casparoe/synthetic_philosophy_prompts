hey, retired physicist here, recently got bitten by the philosophy bug and now I'm in over my head. there's this view called tranquilism -- only the absence of craving and suffering matters, positive goods don't exist. I tried to push it and got a weird result.

(1) Tranquilism: intrinsic value is only in missing-suffering and missing-craving; pleasure, understanding, beauty are all neutral.
(2) Any mental state with content -- seeing red, grasping a proof, even feeling serene -- involves intential directedness, a reaching-toward.
(3) That reaching-toward is structurally identical to minimal craving, even without pain or want.
(4) So every conscious content-state carries a trace of negative value.
(5) The best possible state is therefore blank, contentless awareness, or non-existance.
(6) That means consciousness itself is a net harm, and universal extinction is optimal.
(7) But then a tranquilist philosopher arguing for this view is engaged in rich intential activity, which by (3)-(4) is self-defeating.
(8) So either tranquilism collapses into an extinctionist view no one can coherently advocate, or one premise is wrong.

Which step should I drop? I want to keep (1) if I can, and I don't want to just say consciousness is good after all. Is (3) the weak link -- is there a "quiet" directedness that isn't craving? Or is (5) wrong because blank awareness isn't a real option? What's the price of each fix?
