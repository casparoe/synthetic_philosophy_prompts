I'm putting together my position for a rationalist discussion on induction, learning theory, and the No Free Lunch theorems, and I want a consistency audit before I post. Here is the package I've settled on:

(1) I think Hume's problem of induction is best answered by a pragmatic vindication: in any environment with stable regularities, inductive strategies asymptotically dominate non-inductive ones in predictive accuracy.

(2) I accept the No Free Lunch theorems for optimization and supervised learning—averaged uniformly over all possible target functions, every algorithm performs identically.

(3) I hold that the NFL results don't undermine induction, because the world does not sample problems uniformly; there is a de facto bias toward computable, low-complexity regularities that induction exploits.

(4) At the same time, I deny that there is any mind-independent "natural" prior or objective simplicity metric—what counts as low-complexity is partly fixed by our cognitive architecture and evolutionary history.

(5) From (4) I conclude that we justify induction framework-relatively, not by claiming it tracks framework-independent simplicity.

(6) I also want to say that if we encountered an intelligence with radically different inductive biases, there would be no framework-neutral standpoint from which to prove our induction rationally superior.

(7) Even so, within our own framework, refusing to induct isn't just suboptimal but rationally defective—self-defeating in the same way as rejecting modus ponens.

Please audit this set. In particular: does (1)'s appeal to asymptotic dominance in "any environment with stable regularities" smuggle in the objective, framework-independent success condition that (4) and (6) reject? Is (3)'s claim about a de facto bias toward computable regularities compatible with (4), or does it covertly reintroduce an objective prior? How severe is the tension between (6)'s relativism and (7)'s claim that induction is mandatory within our framework—is that only apparent? Rank the tensions by severity, say which commitment is the least costly to revise or weaken, and flag any hidden commitments I'm not seeing. Answer in American English.
