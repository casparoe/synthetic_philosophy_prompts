I am drafting an essay that treats Borges's reflections on eternity and repetition not as cosmological speculation but as an investigation into the aesthetic logic of recurrence. My central claim is that in Borges's writing, particularly in his essay on eternal recurrence and in the branching-time narrative often associated with forking paths, repetition functions not to affirm or deny identity but to dissolve the criterion by which we distinguish an original event from its copies. To formalize this, I introduce the notion of modal fatigue: the claim that as a given event is mapped across an expanding lattice of possible instantiations, its singular normative weight does not diminish through dilution but is instead redistributed in such a way that judgment about the event becomes a comparison of structural positions rather than of intrinsic qualities. I then contrast this with Nietzsche's eternal recurrence, which I read as an existential filter demanding affirmation of the identical, arguing that Borges replaces the ethical test with an epistemological one in which the observer is fatigued by the very multiplicity that makes comparison possible. I want to examine whether this reading can be sustained without collapsing the distinction between Borges's cyclical imagery and the non-cyclical branching structure found in the same narrative corpus, and whether modal fatigue, understood this way, adds any explanatory power beyond what standard possible-worlds semantics already provides.
