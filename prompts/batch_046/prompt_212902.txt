I am writing a feature for a science and ideas magazine on the philosophical fallout from split-brain research. One thesis I want to advance is that the corpus callosum is not so much an integrator of two half-minds into a unified consciousness as it is a suppressor of an underlying cerebral duality: in this view, each hemisphere maintains its own continuous subjective stream even in neurologically intact individuals, and the appearance of a single self is merely the result of rapid interhemispheric agreement mediated by callosal traffic. If one takes this modular architecture seriously, it seems one ought to reconceive moral and legal personhood not as a singular property belonging to the biological organism, but as a distributed cluster-property that can in principle be fractionated across hemispheres. Would you construct a rigorous argument supporting this reconception of moral agency, drawing on what split-brain studies reveal about the structure of conscious experience?
