Write a deadpan parody of a university accreditation self-study report—approximately 600 words—in which a History Department has reorganized its entire curriculum around Nietzsche’s three modes of historical consciousness: monumental, antiquarian, and critical. The report should treat each mode not as a scholarly framework but as an operational mandate enforced by assessment metrics: monumental history justifies a required undergraduate “Heroic Action Seminar” that evaluates students on the intensity of their felt inspiration rather than on historical accuracy; antiquarian history explains a climate-controlled vault preserving every syllabus, exam, and office memo since 1952, with mandatory pilgrimage hours for tenure-track faculty; and critical history defends an annual “Disavowal Ceremony” in which one tenured scholar’s complete bibliography is symbolically archived in a sub-basement to “make room for life.” The satire should turn on Nietzsche’s actual worries—how monumental history breeds delusion by isolating heroic moments from their context, how antiquarian reverence smothers present action under the weight of preservation, and how critical judgment, pushed to an institutional extreme, becomes destructive of the very tradition it examines—without ever naming Nietzsche, using philosophical jargon, or breaking the bureaucratic voice. Include at least one invented quantitative measure (for example, a “life-affirmation discharge rate” or a “heritage-suffocation index”) that sounds native to assessment culture. The piece should be readable to someone who has never encountered the essay and recognizably fair, even affectionate, to a reader who knows it well.
