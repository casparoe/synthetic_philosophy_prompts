student answer:
hedonism survives because every pleasure—whether anticipatory or consummatory—carries the same positive hedonic valence. bentham's felicific calculus assumes this by putting all pleasures on one measurable scale, which shows they share a single property. people wrongly treat pleasure as a mental image, like picturing food; but it's actually an attitude, not a representation. mill also helps: he showed mental pleasures are basically physical pleasures but with greater intensity, so the core feeling stays identical. if pleasures shared nothing at all, we couldn't compare lives or call one happier, which means the heterogenity objection begs the question. so they definitely belong in the same box, and hedonism is fine as long as we identify that common valence.
