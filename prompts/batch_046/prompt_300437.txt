I made this up, or at least I haven't seen it named in the literature, but it's built out of familiar moves so a student should be able to engage it without outside reading.

Could you draft a 90-minute exam question (aimed at upper-level MA / early PhD students, 25 marks total) that asks them to do three things: first, reconstruct the Rietdijk-Putnam argument step-by-step, highlighting exactly where the frame-invariance of ontology is assumed; second, present the buffered-presentism reply and identify which premise it targets; third, evaluate whether the reply actually saves a meaningful presentist thesis or just collapses into a disguised form of eternalism with observer-relative labeling. Then give me a model answer that identifies the five essential moves a top response must make, plus a marking scheme that assigns points to each move (totalling 25) and includes a one-line note on the characteristic mistake students make on each point. No citation lists needed -- this is a closed-book exam. Also please avoid asking them to reference specific page numbers or authors beyond Rietdijk and Putnam themselves, since I want them reasoning from the argument structure, not from memorized bibliography.
