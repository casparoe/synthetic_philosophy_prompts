Consider Aristotle's implicit track record across four exchange judgments drawn from his discussion of justice, money, and acquisition: (1) in the builder-shoemaker case, exchange is made just not by arithmetic equality of goods but by proportional equality mediated through money, where money serves solely as a conventional measure of need and not as an autonomous store of value; (2) in lending at interest (tokos), he condemns the practice on the ground that money is naturally barren and ought not generate further money, since the lender receives value beyond what was exchanged in use; (3) in reciprocal gift-exchange among friends, he permits persistent imbalance in market value without injustice, because the relation is governed by affection and virtue rather than by calculated reciprocity; (4) in distinguishing natural from unnatural acquisition, he approves of limited wealth-getting for household management (oikonomikē) but condemns unlimited accumulation for its own sake (chrēmatistikē), even when the latter employs conventional exchange mechanisms. Now consider this new mechanism: a decentralized labor network in which no currency circulates, but participants exchange services through algorithmically assigned "reputation-collateral" weights that update in real time based on predictive models of each participant's future contribution and of community need; the collateral appreciates automatically as predicted future value rises, can accumulate without bound far beyond any present or foreseeable use, and is redeemable across successive generations of users within the network. Would Aristotle judge this closer to the corrected builder-shoemaker exchange (just, because it measures proportion), closer to usury (unjust, because the collateral "breeds" predictive value detached from present use), or closer to unnatural chrematistics (unjust, because it institutionalizes unlimited accumulation detached from household need)? Identify the closest analog among the four prior rulings, articulate the unifying principle that would yield that verdict, and be explicit about where the analogies genuinely underdetermine the case—stating what further principle the body would be adopting with each alternative choice. Finally, specify one further hypothetical exchange mechanism that would distinguish the principle you take to be decisive from its nearest competitor, explaining in advance what Aristotle's ruling on that further case would be under each principle.
