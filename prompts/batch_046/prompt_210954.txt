ok so i've been getting into stoicism since i retired (was a field ecologist for 30 yrs, mostly birds and wetlands) and i think i've landed on a package but it feels wobbly. here's roughly where i am:

(1) i take the dichotomy of control literally - only my judgements and intentions are genuinely "up to me"; health, wether people like me, election results, the climate, all of that is technically an "indifferent" (though some are preferred).

(2) but i think the modern revival makes a mistake keeping all the metaphysical baggage - the divine logos, fate, everything happening for a reason. i want to keep just the cognitive restructuring bits, like negative visualization and framing, stripped of cosmology.

(3) i think virtue - wisdom, justice, courage, temperance - is the only real good and it's sufficient for happiness. you could be dying or in prison and still be flourishing if you're facing it right.

(4) at the same time i vote, donate to climate groups, and take antidepresants when i'm low. mental health and social outcomes are "preferred indifferants" worth pursuing, even if they don't determine my true wellbeing.

(5) and i think "stoic" is a family resemblance concept - epictetus, seneca, marcus aurelius, modern CBT people - there's no single essential feature they all share.

my question is: does this actually hang together? specifically, (1) and (4) seem to clash - if climate outcomes aren't in my control, why am i out there advocating? and (2) feels like it undermines (1) - without the old metaphysics about fate and prohairesis, what grounds the sharp line between what's "mine" and what's not? but maybe those tensions dissolve?

also, if virtue is sufficient (3), does that make (4) kind of redundant or even contradictory? and does (5) mean i can't treat "virtue is sufficient" as part of the core?

i'm not sure which one i'd drop if i had to. can you audit this - order the tensions by how bad they are, say which ones are only apparent, and tell me the cheapest fix for each? don't lecture me on stoicism generally, just check my package.
