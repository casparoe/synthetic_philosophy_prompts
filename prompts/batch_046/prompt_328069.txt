I have been trying to understand whether we can ever be mistaken about our own minds. The argument against luminosity seems to show that I cannot always know when I am in pain, particularly near the boundaries of a feeling. Yet I have also come across the KK principle, which suggests that if I genuinely know I am suffering, then I must also know that I know it. If luminosity fails, does that not drag the KK principle down with it for mental states? Or is it possible to be in a condition of "silent pain"—where the hurt is real and I genuinely know it, but my reflective access to that very knowledge is temporarily suspended—and if so, does that defeat luminosity, the KK principle, or only the mistaken idea that they are the same thing?
