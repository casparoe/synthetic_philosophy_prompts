hey so i was reading a thread about Mary's room and there was this long back-and-forth between two posters, call them A and B, that got pretty far into the weeds and i want to know who actually won which point.

A laid out Jackson's classic case: Mary knows every physical fact about color vision but lives in black-and-white. When she sees red she learns something new, what its like to see red. A said this has to be knowledge she lacked before, and since she already had all the physical facts, the missing piece can't be physical.

B came back with the ability hypothesis, but A said that just changes the subject -- even if she gains a recognition ability, the feeling of learning still feels like gaining information, not just a skill. B then tried the phenomenal concept strategy: she acquires a new concept for the same old physical state. But B argued that if physicalism is true then every concept should be derivable from physical descriptions, so having a concept she couldn't derive before is still trouble for the physicalist.

A then proposed something like an indexical move: maybe its like learning "I am now" or "this is happening here". You can know every objective spatiotemporal fact and still not know your own location in them until you index yourself. So Mary's "redness-for-me" is just an indexical update, not a discovery of a non-physical property.

B admitted that works for location but said Mary's case feels different. She doesn't just locate herself in space-time; she acquires a whole new mode of presentation that wasn't in thier prior conceptual repertoire. B asked: if its purely indexical, why can't she derive the indexical fact from her physical knowledge plus the fact that she is Mary and now seeing red? She should be able to predict exactly what it feels like, unless there's an irreducible gap.

A replied that she can predict the physical state but can't know what its like from the inside without being in it. That's not an ontological gap, its an epistemic access gap, like knowing every fact about a program but not being able to run it on your own hardware. B said that analogy smuggles in the idea that consciousness is computation running on substrate, which is exactly whats at issue. A said fair point, but if we reject that analogy we're left with dualism or mysterianism, neither of which B wants.

They got stuck there. So who is right about what? Is the indexical comparison legit or does it miss B's point about conceptual repertoire? And is A's computation analogy actually begging the question against B? Also, is there anything both are missing -- maybe a third option that accomodates the intuition without going dualist? Don't need citations or a reading list, just adjudicate the moves they actually made.
