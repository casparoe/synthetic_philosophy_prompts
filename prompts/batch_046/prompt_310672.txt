Write a sonnet in strict iambic pentameter (ABAB CDCD EFEF GG), spoken in the voice of a dead historical figure whose life has been compressed into a prestige biopic. In the octave, present the dramatist's implicit defense: that chronology must be collapsed, private contradictions streamlined into a single motivating desire, and the life given emotional closure—perhaps a redemption arc or a revealed tragic flaw—that the actual, ambiguously ending biography never supplied. At the volta (line 9), shift to the speaker's deeper objection: not merely that facts have been altered, but that a specific structural wrong has occurred, namely the imposition of narrative coherence onto a genuinely discontinuous life, turning the dead into a counterfactual prop for contemporary catharsis—what we might call "archival appropriation," where a life is mined for dramatic structure it never possessed. The sestet should develop this without resolving it, emphasizing that the dead cannot consent to being ventriloquized for moral instruction. In the closing couplet, pair one rhyme drawn from the dramatist's aesthetic vocabulary with one drawn from the speaker's claim to unmediated, discontinuous fact, so that the formal closure of the rhyme itself enacts the conceptual misalignment between artistic license and historical fidelity. The argument should be precise enough that someone familiar with debates over historical fiction and posthumous harm—especially the distinction between representational error and interpretive violence—recognizes the specific philosophical tension rather than a generic complaint about "getting it wrong."
