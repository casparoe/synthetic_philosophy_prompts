I'm preparing notes for a philosophy of mind seminar and want a consistency audit of my position on graded moral status. Here are my commitments:

(1) Moral status comes in degrees, proportional to the richness and integration of phenomenal experience; there is no metaphysically sharp boundary between full moral agents and entities with none.

(2) Thermostats possess exactly zero moral status—not merely negligible—because they entirely lack a phenomenal field.

(3) Insects sit at a weak intermediate point: their neural architecture supports a minimal, non-reflective form of subjective presence, giving them a small but genuine claim against being destroyed without reason.

(4) For practical decision-making I endorse discrete conventional categories—protected, permissible to harm, no direct duties—because a purely scalar ethics is unworkable in policy and everyday choice.

(5) I accept a precautionary upward principle: when we are genuinely uncertain whether an entity crosses the minimal threshold for phenomenal presence, we should assign it the higher status.

(6) I reject both pure sentientism and pure rationalism; status depends on the actual exercise of an integrated phenomenal field with valenced content, not on mere dispositional capacity or rational autonomy alone.

Which of these pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the severity ranking? In particular: does (1) sit comfortably with (2)'s exact-zero assignment, or does continuity pressure me to treat the thermostat as arbitrarily close to zero rather than precisely zero? Does (5) make sense if (1) denies natural joints at which a threshold could be crossed? Is (3) empirically and conceptually stable given (6)'s requirement of an actually exercised integrated field? And does (4) undermine the metaphysical seriousness of (1) and (3), or is it a harmless practical overlay? For each real tension, state the cheapest revision and what it costs. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised, would resolve the most conflicts at once.
