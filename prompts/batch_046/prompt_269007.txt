I am examining the consistency of a normative decision-theory working group—the Vasquez-Mori panel on rational choice under ambiguity—that has published rulings on modified Allais structures across different domains. Their verdicts are as follows.

In Case 1 (monetary, high stakes), facing choice set α—A: $1,000,000 with certainty; B: 89% chance of $1,000,000, 10% chance of $5,000,000, 1% chance of $0—and choice set β—C: 11% chance of $1,000,000, 89% chance of $0; D: 10% chance of $5,000,000, 90% chance of $0—the panel endorsed selecting A in α and D in β, thereby accepting the independence-axiom violation characteristic of the classic Allais paradox.

In Case 2 (monetary, low stakes), with the identical probability structure but amounts of $100 and $500, the panel endorsed B in α and C in β, insisting on independence-obeying consistency.

In Case 3 (direct life-preservation), facing choice set α—A: 1,000 lives saved with certainty; B: 89% chance of saving 1,000 lives, 10% chance of saving 1,500 lives, 1% chance of saving none—and choice set β—C: 11% chance of saving 1,000 lives, 89% chance of saving none; D: 10% chance of saving 1,500 lives, 90% chance of saving none—the panel endorsed B in α and C in β, again requiring consistency.

In Case 4 (environmental policy), facing choice set α—A: 1.0°C reduction with certainty; B: 89% chance of 1.0°C reduction, 10% chance of 1.5°C reduction, 1% chance of 0°C reduction—and choice set β—C: 11% chance of 1.0°C reduction, 89% chance of 0°C reduction; D: 10% chance of 1.5°C reduction, 90% chance of 0°C reduction, with no catastrophic runaway state present—the panel endorsed A in α and D in β, reproducing the Case 1 pattern.

Now consider Case 5, a newly proposed framework for allocating research funding in high-stakes epistemic risk assessment: Protocol A guarantees a moderate but definite advance in interpretability metrics (outcome M); Protocol B offers an 89% chance of M, a 10% chance of a transformative breakthrough (outcome B), and a 1% chance of an inconclusive null result (outcome N). The paired choice set removes the common 89% M consequence, leaving Protocol C (11% chance of M, 89% chance of N) versus Protocol D (10% chance of B, 90% chance of N). The stakes are framed as existential-risk-relevant, yet the outcomes are purely epistemic and quantifiable on a standardized metric, with no direct biological or monetary harm involved.

If the panel wants to remain consistent with its own track record, what should it recommend for Case 5? Identify the closest analog among Cases 1–4. Then lay out the competing rules that jointly explain Cases 1–4—at least two must diverge on Case 5—state what each rule predicts for Case 5, offer your best guess at the panel's actual ruling with the reasoning that favors it, and specify the one further case that would best distinguish between those competing rules.
