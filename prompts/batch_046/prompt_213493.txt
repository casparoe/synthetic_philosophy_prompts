I've been thinking about all those old societies that supposedly fell apart — Bronze Age, Maya, Easter Island — and I think I've landed on a view, but I'm not sure the pieces actually fit together. Here's what I think:

1. I dont think societies ever literally "collapse" like a building falling down; they just change shape, and calling it collapse is just historians being dramatic about change they dont like.

2. Still, I think the Bronze Age thing was a real collapse, not just change, because writing and big cities disappeared for like 300 years and people went back to smaller villages.

3. With the Maya I think its overblown — people kept living in the same places, they just stopped building fancy pyramids, so thats not collapse thats just fashion going out of style.

4. Easter Island feels different to me: they cut down every tree, couldnt build canoes, and then people starved and lost all the big statue-building culture. That seems like an actual crash, not just a makeover.

5. I think the real test for whether it was collapse should be whether the people living through it felt like their world had ended, not whether archeologists see fewer temples in the dirt.

6. I also dont buy comparing societies to bodies or machines, because that sneaks in the idea theres one right way for them to be, and people can adapt in ways machines cant.

So do these hang together? I'm especially wondering if I'm being consistent about Maya vs Bronze Age, and whether my "ask the people who lived it" rule clashes with me judging Easter Island and Bronze Age from the outside. Also is there a hidden tension in saying societies just change shape but then calling Easter Island a crash? I dont want to just ditch one view, but tell me which pairs are actually fighting, how bad it is, and what the cheapest fix would be — and if there's one revision that cleans up the most mess, what it costs me.
