I'm trying to identify whether there is an established term for a particular configuration I keep encountering in discussions of autonomous systems and accountability—one that seems structurally different from the familiar "responsibility gap" but is often treated as part of the same family.

Consider an institution that deploys an automated decision system—say, a risk-assessment algorithm in criminal justice or a screening tool in hiring—where human roles are deliberately segmented: the model's architects do not determine the deployment context; the administrators who set the operational thresholds do not understand the feature weights; the auditors who review aggregate outcomes lack authority to alter the system; and the frontline users are instructed to treat the output as advisory yet are evaluated on whether they follow it. When harm occurs, no single individual possesses the combination of causal control, epistemic access, and normative authority that standard accounts of moral responsibility require. Crucially, this is not primarily because the technology is technically opaque—though it may be—but because the organizational architecture was designed to distribute those very elements across separate roles, so that each party can point to the limits of their own mandate.

The gap here feels different from cases where an autonomous system surprises everyone with an unforeseeable action. Here the diffusion is institutional and, in a sense, architecturally intentional: the division of labor functions as a shield. I am wondering whether philosophers have isolated this as its own phenomenon—perhaps an "organizational responsibility gap," a "designed diffusion" problem, or something closer to the literature on collective agency and bureaucratic harm—or whether it remains subsumed under general discussions of autonomy and accountability. If it does have its own literature, how precisely is it distinguished from gaps arising purely from technical unpredictability? If not, what is the closest established concept, and where exactly do the boundaries lie? I would be satisfied neither with a bare label nor with a simple "no"; what I want is the carving: what this configuration is, what it is not, and which edges belong to it.
