Write a deadpan parody of a federal regulatory manual—titled Directive 404-C: Certification Protocol for Composite Entities—issued by a fictional Bureau of Mereological Affairs. The manual should establish the bureaucratic procedures by which field inspectors determine whether scattered parts constitute a single object, targeting the special composition question itself. The satire must exaggerate real features of the debate: the arbitrariness of boundaries, vagueness in the sorites-style accumulation and loss of parts, and the competing positions of unrestricted universalism, restricted composition (e.g., living organisms), and mereological nihilism. Maintain an entirely deadpan, administrative voice throughout; use invented but philosophically resonant terminology—ontological fasteners, joint-action clauses, dispersal thresholds, mereological audit trails—without explaining them as jokes. Structure the piece with numbered sections and sub-clauses like an actual directive, approximately 600 words. Include a procedural flowchart-in-prose for evaluating borderline cases, applying it specifically to: (1) a dispersed art installation separated by more than fifty miles; (2) a swarm of bees; and (3) a demolished building whose bricks have been redistributed into three new structures. Frame the philosophical rivals not as named thinkers but as competing schools of inspection—the Unrestricted School, the Restrictive School, and the Null School—each with its own form and checklist. Conclude with a standard bureaucratic Severability Clause that quietly admits the Bureau itself may fail the certification criteria it enforces, thereby undermining the framework from within. The humor must come from rigorously applying administrative logic to metaphysical puzzles, not from misrepresenting the positions or inventing straw-man absurdities. Do not cite philosophers, texts, or bibliographies; do not ask for them.
