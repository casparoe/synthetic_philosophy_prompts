My friends Alex and Jordan got in an argument about satisficing and bounded rationality. Alex said that picking the first apartment thats "good enough" is actually smart, not lazy, because searching more costs real time and mental energy and your brain is bounded. Jordan said thats just an excuse—if you stop at "good enough" you'll miss way better options that were literally one more click away. Alex came back that you cant know the future, so you cant compute the optimal stopping point; bounded rationality means you have to use a simple cutoff. Jordan said okay, but what if you know good listings cluster in certain neighborhoods? Shouldnt your "good enough" bar shift depending on that pattern? Alex said even then you dont have infinite time to model the cluster exactly, so youre still bounded. Jordan said maybe the real issue isnt boundedness but that people set the bar too low from the start. Can you walk through their exchange and say whos right at each step—not just who wins? And what are they both missing about how that cutoff should actually adapt?
