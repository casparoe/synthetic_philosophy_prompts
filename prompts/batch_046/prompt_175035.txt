I am a retired biologist who has recently begun reading philosophy seriously, and I am trying to learn how to audit a popular summary for hidden slippage. In an online essay I encountered a characterization of tranquilism—the view that only the absence of craving and suffering carries positive value, while pleasure, achievement, and knowledge are at best instrumentally useful. The essay writes: "Tranquilists treat well-being as anchored at zero suffering, so they deny that love, art, or scientific discovery have intrinsic worth, and they hold that a state of perfect tranquility with no positive experience is preferable to a joyful life containing minor discomfort. This makes them negative utilitarians, except that they reject comparative weighing because they treat suffering as infinitely bad and pleasure as literally valueless—numerically zero. From this it follows that tranquilism requires antinatalist commitments, since any birth risks suffering, but also that one should withhold antidepressants if recovery might generate new appetites, because leaving a suffering but desire-free patient alone is better than creating a satisfied but desiring one. The writer therefore concludes that these implications are so perverse that tranquilism collapses into an incoherent pessimism."

As someone accustomed to checking whether conclusions genuinely follow from premises, I want to calibrate my judgment. Which parts of this summary fairly represent tranquilism, and where does it conflate the view with negative utilitarianism or with the asymmetry thesis about pleasure and pain? Is the claim that tranquilists assign pleasure literally zero value an accurate rendering or an overstatement? Do the deductions about withholding treatment and about antinatalism actually follow from the view, or do they smuggle in premises that a tranquilist would reject? Finally, does the charge that tranquilism is incoherent pessimism survive once these distinctions are corrected? Please separate faithful characterization, common simplification, and clear misrepresentation, and say whether the essay's dismissal holds up.
