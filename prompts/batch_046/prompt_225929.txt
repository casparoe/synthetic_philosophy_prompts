I just had a long argument with a friend about Benjamin Franklin's attempt to engineer his own moral perfection. She claimed that tracking specific virtues on a rotating weekly schedule—while knowing human weakness makes perfect scores impossible—is either hypocritical or psychologically self-defeating, especially since Franklin eventually admitted he never reached his own standard. I wasn't convinced, but now I'm running my own version of his system and I'm unsure who's right.

For the past three months I've been logging thirteen habits in a spreadsheet, each assigned one week of focus: Temperance, Silence, Order, Resolution, Frugality, Industry, Sincerity, Justice, Moderation, Cleanliness, Tranquility, Chastity, and Humility. Every night I score whether I kept that week's virtue. The problem is that my metrics seem to conflict. When I prioritize Sincerity, my Silence score collapses because honesty requires me to speak up; when my Industry and Resolution marks are high, my Humility score plummets—precisely because I feel proud of those high marks. I'm now wondering whether I should maintain Franklin's original, unattainable target of perfect consistency, which my friend says just breeds vanity disguised as self-improvement, or whether I should recalibrate my benchmarks to realistic levels, which she dismisses as rationalizing mediocrity. Is there a philosophically coherent way to keep Franklin's structural commitment to systematic self-correction without either abandoning the ideal or turning the practice into self-congratulatory performance?
