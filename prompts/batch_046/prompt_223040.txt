I've been working through Popper's argument in The Poverty of Historicism for a seminar on philosophy of social science, and my study group has pushed it into territory that feels like a reductio, but I can't locate the leak. Here is the chain we constructed. (1) Historicism claims there are laws of historical development that allow long-term social prediction. (2) Popper objects that the growth of human knowledge is an unpredictable factor that alters the very conditions under which future events occur, so historical conditions never genuinely recur in the way required by scientific law. (3) If a domain lacks genuinely recurring conditions, it cannot support universal predictive laws—only local trends or explanations after the fact. (4) Now apply the same structure to evolutionary biology and geology: both study singular, irreversible sequences in which accumulated information—genetic or stratigraphic—continuously alters the conditions for subsequent change. (5) By parity of reasoning with (2), these domains should also be incapable of supporting universal laws like natural selection or plate-tectonic dynamics. (6) But evolutionary biology and geology are standardly taken to possess precisely such laws. (7) Therefore, if we accept Popper's reasoning in (2) as sound, we must either deny that evolutionary biology and geology have laws (which seems absurd) or conclude that the reasoning itself is flawed and applies only selectively to history. (8) My instinct is that (4) misapplies the knowledge-growth premise—genetic information isn't "knowledge" in the relevant epistemic sense—but I'm not sure whether that saves Popper without ad hoc boundary-drawing.

Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost? I'd prefer to preserve Popper's core anti-historicist claim and the idea that knowledge growth is special, but I'm open to seeing why the analogy to evolutionary accumulation might be stronger than I think. Please indicate how confident you are in your diagnosis, and specifically flag the point about which you are least certain—whether it's the correct parsing of Popper's recurrence condition, the strength of the evolutionary analogy, or the cost of blocking it.
