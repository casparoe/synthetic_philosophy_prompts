I've been going in circles on Hacking's looping effects in psychiatric classification. The debate's been running since the 90s — are DSM categories interactive kinds that loop back and reshape the people they classify, or can psychiatry still aim at objective natural kinds? At every workshop I hear the same examples — MPD in the eighties, autism spectrum shifts now — and nobody actualy moves. Is this actually a substantive disagreement, or just a verbal mess about what counts as looping? Or maybe it's two different projects under one name: philosophers asking about ontology (do these categories exist independently?) and clinicians asking about measurement bias, and they're not even addressing each other.

Here's my half-baked idea: people treat looping as binary — either a category loops or it doesn't — but I think it's scalar, mediated by what I'd call diagnostic uptake density (how embedded a category is in clinical protocols, legal categories, and self-help infrastructures). So maybe the stall happens because both sides are testing for presence or absence instead of measuring gradient effects across different institutional environments. But I could be totally off.
