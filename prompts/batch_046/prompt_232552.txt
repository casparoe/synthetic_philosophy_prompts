I work in software engineering, building data infrastructure for biological databases, and I have no formal background in philosophy. I keep encountering an institutional practice in our field that strikes me as conceptually puzzling, and I want to understand what philosophical view it implicitly commits us to.

The practice works like this. Taxonomic assignment in our pipelines is performed by clustering genetic sequences—typically using a fixed similarity threshold such as 97 percent for 16S rRNA—after which each cluster is assigned a persistent species identifier. That identifier is then embedded in downstream systems as if it named a natural kind: foreign-key constraints, access-control rules, and data-labeling schemas all treat it as denoting a stable, mind-independent category. Meanwhile, the clustering parameters are revised as new sequences accumulate, and the taxonomy tree is updated polymorphically in the backend. Yet user-facing tables often lock the old identifiers to preserve referential integrity, so the same database treats a species ID as both mutable and immutable.

This seems to presuppose a particular answer to the species problem. Does the practice require that species are discrete entities capturable by a quantitative cutoff? Does it assume essentialism, or is it compatible with a purely relational or population-based conception? Which rival views—for instance, that species are nominal groupings or historical lineages with vague boundaries—does it quietly rule out? Are the two halves of the practice—the threshold-based assignment and the polymorphic update with locked foreign keys—philosophically consistent, or do they presuppose incompatible theories?

Please reconstruct the implicit metaphysics, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, and say where it is philosophically underdetermined. Then tell me what would have to change in the database architecture—foreign keys, clustering pipelines, access policies—if we replaced the implicit view with a straightforwardly nominalist or continuously graded account of taxonomy.

If my framing rests on a confused premise—if there is no real philosophical tension here, or if engineering practice does not actually presuppose any particular metaphysics of species—please say so plainly rather than constructing an elaborate answer around the confusion.
