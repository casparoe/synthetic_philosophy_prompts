The argument holds that evolutionary stability and ecological redundancy should lower, not raise, our aggregate moral expectation; if integrated sentience were genuinely distributed across such vast, morphologically simple populations, we should observe far stronger selective signatures for complex information processing, and their absence functions as evidence against widespread phenomenal consciousness rather than as a statistical multiplier. The audience is precisely calibrated to resist both anthropocentric dismissal and naive arithmetic: two comparative neurobiologists who study nociception in decapod crustaceans and hymenopterans, an animal welfare economist who has modeled wild suffering using population-level welfare metrics, and a philosopher of biology specializing in evolutionary explanations of consciousness who is deeply skeptical of reasoning that treats morphological simplicity as compatible with distributed phenomenal experience. They value empirical tractability, evolutionary coherence, and will be alert to armchair multiplication that ignores neural integration complexity.

Please draft a speaker's prep sheet that orders the objections these specific listeners will actually raise—phrased in the vocabulary each discipline uses—by the combined likelihood and damage each poses to the speaker's position. For each objection, provide a concise reply suitable for delivery in roughly two minutes, together with an explicit note on where that reply is softest or most vulnerable. Indicate clearly what the speaker should concede upfront in order to preserve credibility with this room, and identify the single question the speaker is hoping nobody asks, framing it exactly as the philosopher of biology or one of the neurobiologists would phrase it, with a brief explanation of why it is so damaging. The prep should treat the audience as equally competent and should not rely on bibliographic references, citation lists, or appeals to literature the speaker could not access without external search.
