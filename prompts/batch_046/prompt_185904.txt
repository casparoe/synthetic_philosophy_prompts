He characterized Nietzsche's account roughly as follows: in pre-moral antiquity, the nobles spontaneously called themselves and their traits 'good,' while labeling the common people 'bad' in a purely descriptive, hierarchical sense with no moral condemnation. The slaves, consumed by a psychological state he called ressentiment — essentially a festering, vengeful resentment — then engineered a deliberate and collective inversion: they redefined weakness, humility, and suffering as 'good,' and re-labeled noble strength and vitality as 'evil.' My friend added that, for Nietzsche, this inversion was not merely a historical accident but a conscious strategy of the weak to seize cultural power, and that the result is our modern moral framework, which Nietzsche therefore urges us to reject entirely and replace with a restored master morality. He also claimed that Nietzsche's critique applies specifically to Christian morality but leaves open a secular, egalitarian ethics untouched, and that the concept of 'evil' in modern usage is essentially the slave's invention to pathologize natural superiority. I have the sense that several of these claims conflate Nietzsche's descriptive genealogy with a prescriptive program, and that his characterization of ressentiment collapses a technical concept into ordinary resentment. But I am unsure which parts are accurate simplifications and which are serious distortions. Could you audit this summary? Specifically, please distinguish: (a) what a careful reader of Nietzsche would accept as roughly correct; (b) where the summary uses technical terms in non-technical ways or conflates distinct claims; (c) what Nietzsche explicitly denies or at least does not commit to, particularly regarding conscious conspiracy, prescriptive abolition, and the scope of the slave revolt; and (d) whether my friend's conclusion — that modern egalitarian ethics is thereby refuted — actually follows from the accurate parts of the account. In your answer, please do not provide a bibliography or reading list; I want to assess the conceptual fairness of the summary on its own merits.
