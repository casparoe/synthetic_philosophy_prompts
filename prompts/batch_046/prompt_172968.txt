Please describe the most important implications for theories of rational agency if diachronic consistency must govern not only static preference orderings, but an agent’s successive revisions of belief about her own future discount rates. Focus on the following constructed case: at each stage, an agent binds her future self to execute a long-term plan; yet upon reaching authority, that future self revises its view of appropriate time-preference, dissolves the prior commitment, and institutes a stricter binding mechanism aimed still further ahead. The result is not a monetary Dutch Book, but an infinite deferral of execution—a temporal reversal pump. What does this imply for the distinction between sophisticated agents, who anticipate future shifts and calibrate present constraints accordingly, and resolute agents, who treat prior plans as binding regardless of later preference changes? Must rationality require resoluteness to avoid being pumped in this non-monetary form, or does the anticipatory logic of sophistication itself generate the vulnerability? Address whether standard diachronic rationality conditions need to be supplemented with a stability requirement on second-order beliefs about one’s own future reasoning.
