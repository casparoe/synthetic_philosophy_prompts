After a long argument with a friend about whether two-dimensional semantics can be extended to normative vocabulary without collapsing into contextualism, I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends a hybrid two-dimensional account of moral and modal language. On his view, the primary intension of a term is fixed not by actual-world speaker dispositions but by a counterfactual stability condition evaluated across worlds where expert consensus shifts; he also introduces a derived modal stability index that ranks worlds according to how many primary intensions remain constant across them. I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking him to restate the position. At minimum, include one question on the semantic-deference problem—how the stability condition avoids an infinite regress when lay speakers defer to moral experts—paired with a follow-up keyed to the likely reply that experts are themselves assessed by the same condition. Include another on nested modal operators: specifically, how the framework handles sentences like it is necessary that it is possible that P when the secondary intension of the modal term varies across worlds, with a follow-up pressing whether this forces a proliferation of dimensions beyond two. Include a direct challenge to the modal stability index: ask how it can be defined without circular reliance on the modal vocabulary it is meant to regiment, and provide a follow-up anticipating the defense that the index is conceptually primitive. Finally, include a question connecting the view to cross-cultural moral disagreement—whether a world with radically different expert consensus still counts as nearby for fixing the primary intension—together with a follow-up asking what independent criterion of nearness is available. The tone should be rigorous but colloquium-appropriate, and please avoid generic biographical questions, requests for reading lists, or citations.
