I spent much of my career in experimental physics before retirement, and I have recently begun working through philosophy of science with the same methodical patience I once applied to instrument calibration. In that reading I have encountered the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, which uses the frame-dependence of simultaneity in special relativity to claim that no global present can exist, and therefore that all events—past, present, and future—are equally real within a four-dimensional block universe. The reasoning seems compelling: if two observers in uniform relative motion assign different events to their respective "now," and physics offers no privileged frame to break the symmetry, then presentness cannot be an objective feature of the world.

Yet I am not convinced that the absence of a global hyperplane of simultaneity entails the absence of any ontologically significant present. I want you to compare the Rietdijk–Putnam argument with a view I have been trying to formulate, which I will call worldline-indexical presentism. On this view, "now" functions analogously to "here": it is genuinely real but indexed to a particular worldline and its local causal structure, specifically to the boundary of its past light cone. There is no universe-wide present slice, but each trajectory carries its own open future and settled past. The disagreement between observers about distant events then resembles two travelers disagreeing about whether a particular town is nearby—indexical variation that does not dissolve the reality of spatial proximity itself. Please compare these two positions with particular attention to three issues. First, does the Rietdijk–Putnam argument show that worldline-indexical presentism collapses into contradiction, since a single event might lie in the future of one worldline and the past of another, so that attributing different ontological statuses violates non-contradictory consistency? Or can the indexicalist reply that presentness, like nearness, is a relational property rather than a monadic one? Second, how do accelerating observers and Rindler horizons affect each view? For an accelerating worldline, parts of Minkowski space fall behind a horizon never intersected by its past light cone; does this asymmetry strengthen the block universe, or does it merely show that presentness is horizon-dependent in a way compatible with local presentism? Third, which interpretation better respects the physicist's distinction between coordinate conventions—such as Einstein synchronization—and invariant geometric structure, like the light-cone? I ask you to adjudicate which comparison is more persuasive, and to identify any hidden premises both sides neglect, particularly whether ontological parity genuinely requires a global foliation or whether indexicality can carry metaphysical weight without collapsing into relativism about reality itself.
