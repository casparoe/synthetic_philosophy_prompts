Before you start writing, do some quick brainstorming: what makes a satire about hidden linguistic defaults actually land, instead of just sounding like a classroom lecture? I want you to write a short story—maybe around 600 words, in american english—about a municipal clerk named Kess in the town of Marrow. The town just passed a "purity in labeling" law saying no public document can use an unmarked default category: if you list a job, you have to split it by some visible physical trait; if you name a product, you have to specify the variant that was previously treated as normal. Kess starts writing an internal memo that pushes this logic to an absurd extreme, similar in structure to Hofstadter's satire on sexist language and default assumptions, but applied to something weirder and more niche—maybe vertical reach combined with left/right dominance. The memo should expose that the so-called neutral terms were never actually neutral, they just encoded an invisible power norm. Don't just retell a gender satire; invent a fresh bureaucratic nightmare that makes the reader feel the bias rather than being told it. Start with a brief bullet-point brainstorm of your concept, then the story itself. Keep it conceptually sharp, not preachy.
