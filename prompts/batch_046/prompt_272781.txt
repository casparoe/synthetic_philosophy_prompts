Compose the opening remarks for the dedication of the Helene Voss Memorial Concert Hall. The audience is divided: on one side sit musicologists and philosophers of art familiar with Schopenhauer’s claim that music is unique among the arts because it does not reproduce Platonic Ideas—the graded objectifications of the Will visible in nature and human action—but instead expresses the Will itself directly, with bass registers corresponding to inorganic nature and melodic striving to human consciousness; on the other side sit Voss’s family and local patrons who knew her only as a generous teacher and an eccentric composer. Your remarks must honor Voss’s lifelong development of the “broken-octave temperament,” an invented tuning system she designed specifically to prevent harmonic resolution from being heard as representational order and thereby to preserve music’s direct, unmediated access to the Will. You should explain, without technical jargon or bibliographic apparatus, why this eccentricity was for her not mere experimentation but a philosophical necessity rooted in Schopenhauer’s metaphysics; and you should comfort the family by framing her solitude and her refusal of conventional harmony not as alienation, but as fidelity to a single idea that demanded isolation. The tone should be ceremonial yet precise, suitable for roughly five minutes of speech, and should present the broken-octave temperament strictly as Voss’s own invention, not as an established historical system. Do not provide citations, further-reading lists, or verbatim quotations from Schopenhauer’s writings.
