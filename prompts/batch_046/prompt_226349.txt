Notes for a short essay: "Reflexive anchoring and the normative architecture of factored cognition"

The standard presentation of Humans Consulting HCH (HCH) treats it as an epistemic scaffold: a root agent decomposes a hard problem and consults subordinate agents, each solving tractable subproblems, with answers aggregated upward. The related notion of factored cognition extends this by breaking reasoning itself into modular, verifiable steps. Both are usually defended as solutions to scalable oversight—ways for a system, or a person, to tackle questions beyond direct comprehension.

I want to argue that this architecture implicitly presupposes a particular model of rational deliberation, and that making the presupposition explicit reveals a gap. In HCH, each node receives a well-defined subquestion; the tree assumes that answers can be composed without residue. But many hard questions—especially those touching on values, risk, or interpretation—do not decompose cleanly. A sub-agent might answer "what are the consequences of policy X?" in a way that smuggles in normative weightings (for instance, which outcomes count as costs). The root agent then inherits these weightings without having endorsed them.

To address this, I propose "reflexive anchoring": every node in a factored tree must tag its outputs according to whether they depend on empirical premises, axiological commitments, or both. The root agent then receives not only answers but a dependency map, allowing it to see where contested values enter the reasoning chain. This transforms HCH from a purely epistemic tree into an idealised model of public deliberation—one in which transparency about normative inputs is structural rather than incidental.

The cost is fragility. Human consultants are rarely able to cleanly separate factual claims from value-laden framing. Requiring reflexive anchoring might paralyse the tree or expose irreconcilable disagreement at every node. Nevertheless, without it, factored cognition risks what I call "normative fragmentation": the appearance of coherent reasoning that is actually a patchwork of incompatible local optima.

2. Does reflexive anchoring genuinely improve on standard HCH, or does it merely relocate the difficulty to the tagging mechanism?
