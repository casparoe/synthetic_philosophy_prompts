ok so I'm trying to sort out my own view on why promises actually bind before I present it in seminar next week, and I want a consistency check. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(a) I think a promise binds mainly because it generates a legitimate normative expectation in the promisee — not just a prediction, but a claim that gets violated if broken without excuse.

(b) But I also think that if the speaker genuinely doesn't intend to do the thing at the moment of utterance, then there's no real promise being made, so nothing binds, even if the hearer walks away expecting it.

(c) At the same time, I hold that promises made under conditions of radical uncertainty about your future preferences — like vowing to stay vegan for life knowing you'll probably crave cheese — are fully binding, not somehow weakened or conditional.

(d) I also think that if the social or instutional frame that gives the promise its meaning collapses — say, you promised loyalty to a guild that no longer exists — the obligation dissolves with it.

(e) And I want to say the underlying mechanism isn't just consent or harm-avoidance, but something like authoritative self-creation: you're putting your future self under the authority of your present decision.

So do these hang together? (b) and (e) seem to pull in different directions — if it's about self-creation, why does present intention matter so much? And (a) with (d) feels shaky: if binding is about the promisee's expectation, why should the guild's disappearance erase that? Meanwhile (c) and (e) might actually be ok if self-creation works across preference change, but I'm definatly not sure.

Can you audit this? Rank the tensions by severity, say which pair is only apparently in conflict, and tell me the least costly revision that saves the package — and be honest about which commitment I'd have to drop if I want to keep everything else. Also flag any hidden tension I'm missing.

One more thing that's been bugging me: what about a case where someone sends an automated message they never personally authorized — the frame and the outer utterance are there, but the inner intention is absent. Where does my view land? I think it shouldn't bind, but I'm not sure that's consistent with (a).
