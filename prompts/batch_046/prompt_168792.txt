Analyze the 1968 Tet Offensive in Hue — the NVA/Viet Cong assault on the citadel and the US Marine counter-attack — through the lens of just war theory's moral equality of combatants. But don't do the standard state-level analysis; instead, treat the individual Marines and the NVA regulars as locked in a reciprocal liability relation, where each is permitted to target the other only because both have voluntarily assumed combatant roles. Map out who actually counts as a combatant in Hue given that NVA troops often ditched uniforms and blended with civilians, and that local civilians were sometimes pressed into service. Does the equality thesis still hold when the visual markers of combatant status collapse? Then look at the reported killings of civilians by NVA forces and the heavy US bombing/artillery that destroyed large parts of the old city: do these actions violate jus in bello in a symetrical way, or does the asymmetry of cause (defending South Vietnam vs. trying to overthrow it) sneak back in and break the equality? I want a disciplined mapping — who are the players, what are thier actual options, where is the theory silent — and an honest take on whether Hue is a confirmation of moral equality or an anomaly that shows equality can't survive without clear uniform distinction and separation from noncombatants. Don't just say war is messy; actually say where the model breaks.
