Write the opening address for a workshop titled "Disagreement Under Uncertainty," delivered to an audience split evenly between philosophers of science and practicing climate modelers. The speech must treat the conciliationism-versus-steadfastness debate not as a two-party conflict but as a three-place relation involving Peer A, Peer B, and a contested "calibration standard"—a shared methodological benchmark whose own reliability is itself one of the points in dispute. Use this framing to show why the usual conciliationist prescription of "splitting the difference" becomes self-defeating when the very metric used to weight the peers' opinions is what they disagree about. Open with a concrete, non-citational scenario drawn from multi-model climate intercomparison; develop the philosophical structure without naming specific philosophers or invoking particular texts; and close with a single actionable workshop norm that both conciliationists and steadfastists could accept, though for structurally different reasons. The address should be speakable in about seven minutes, must avoid bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, should not depend on any obscure technical result that requires outside lookup, and must land as genuine address rather than lecture—accessible to the modelers without losing the philosophers, with the philosophical idea shaping what can and cannot be said about disagreement in the room.
