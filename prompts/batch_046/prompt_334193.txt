What do you think Einstein would have said about the Rietdijk-Putnam Andromeda argument if it had reached him before he died? We know he wrote to Besso's family that the past/present/future distinction is just a stubborn illusion, but he was also deeply committed to continuous field theories and hated quantum nonlocality. So here's the specific twist: suppose someone presents him with the eternalist conclusion from relative simultaneity, but then offers a presentist reply using a moving spotlight where presentness is real but doesn't align with any Lorentz frame, meaning the spotlight jumps discontinuously between distant regions depending on velocity. Given his objections to spooky action at a distance, would he have seen this discontinuous present as worse than eternalism? Or would his determinism have made him prefer a single block where nothing jumps? I'm not asking for a bibliography, just how you'd reconstruct his reasoning from what we know about his physics and philosophical remarks on time.
