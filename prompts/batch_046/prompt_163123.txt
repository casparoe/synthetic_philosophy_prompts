Write a branching taxonomy—designed as a diagnostic instrument—of positions on whether hypocrisy undermines standing to blame. Every internal node should be a concrete claim phrased as a question that a respondent can answer without knowing philosophical labels, so that the tree sorts people by what they believe rather than by what they call themselves. Start at the root: does a blamer's own moral fault ever diminish their standing to blame another for a wrong?

Split first between 'never' and 'sometimes, if conditions are met'. For the latter branch, divide by the kind of similarity required between the blamer's fault and the blamed act: identical in kind ('type-hypocrisy'), within the same normative domain but different in kind ('domain-hypocrisy'), or any moral fault whatsoever ('universal forfeiture'). Then divide by scale: must the blamer's fault match or exceed the blamed's in magnitude ('scale-parity'), or is magnitude irrelevant?

Next, divide by epistemic accessibility: does the undermining depend on the hypocrisy being manifest to the blamed party, or does it hold objectively regardless of awareness? Finally, divide by relational role: does standing depend on whether the blamer is a peer, an authority figure, a direct victim, or a stranger?

Each leaf should carry a name (or be marked 'unnamed' if unoccupied) and one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Flag any pair of leaves that your tree genuinely cannot separate. Identify any logically possible combination of answers with no occupant, and diagnose briefly whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or being unexplored. To keep this from being a recitation of familiar literature, treat 'scale-hypocrisy' and 'domain-hypocrisy' as independent branches rather than collapsing them into a single 'relevant similarity' condition.
