I am a high school student preparing my final presentation for a political philosophy course on Isaiah Berlin's distinction between negative and positive liberty and on Philip Pettit's republican concept of liberty as non-domination. Rather than ending with a summary slide, I want to close with a villanelle that treats these three concepts not as a simple list but as structurally different relations to power. The poem should use two refrains — "the gate is left unlocked" for negative liberty and "my will is shaped for good" for positive liberty — with the villanelle's circling repetition showing how these two conditions can coexist and reinforce one another while leaving untouched the relation of arbitrary domination: the presence of a master who has the unchecked power to interfere but currently chooses not to. The philosophical turn should occur in the final quatrain, when the speaker realizes that an open gate and an educated will are fully compatible with being a slave. The closing couplet should present republican liberty not as a middle ground between the other two but as the diagnosis of what both permit when domination remains hidden in the background. Please keep the rhyme scheme strict (ABA ABA ABA ABA ABA ABAA), make the imagery precise enough that a reader familiar with Berlin and Pettit recognizes the argument in the form itself, and write it in a voice suitable for reading aloud to a classroom.
