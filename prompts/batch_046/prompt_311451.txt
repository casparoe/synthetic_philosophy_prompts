I'm applying for a one-semester independent study grant and need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea: the presentism vs eternalism debate is usually stuck in physics or in whether past-tense statements have truthmakers, but I think there's an underexplored angle about whether presentism can accomodate what we might call retrospective moral anchoring—the idea that obligations like reparative justice seem to require the past wrong not just to have happened but to remain ontologically available in some way. Can presentists account for the normative pull of backward-looking duties without either smuggling in eternalist commitments or reducing those duties to present-oriented sentiments?

Existing positions treat the conflict as mainly metaphysical or semantic, so they don't settle whether presentism has a hidden cost in moral ontology. I want to spend the first month formalizing three presentist responses: denying that obligations need ontological anchors at all, reconstruing them as present-directed dispositions shaped by historical narrative, and adopting a restricted eternalism that applies only to normative facts but not to physical ones. Months two and three would test these against a concrete case—intergenerational climate reparations—where past emissions and present harms are linked by a causal chain that presentists must either honor or dissolve. The deliverable is a 6000-word paper with a precise taxonomy and the case study. What counts as failure: if all three strategies either require full eternalist machinery or strip backward-looking duties of their distinctive normative force, then I've shown presentism carries an unacknowledged moral price. If I can't keep the normative and metaphysical issues distinct by week six, or if the climate case collapses into empirical disputes about causation that philosophy can't adjudicate, then the project is too big for one semester and I'd scale back to just the logical taxonomy.
