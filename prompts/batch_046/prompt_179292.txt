Case 2: A vision-language model outputs calibrated descriptions of crimson hues using feature-map correlations, with no biological substrate. Verdict: no phenomenal quality at all—only statistical classification.

Case 3: A blindsight patient denies visual experience in the left hemifield yet navigates obstacles. Verdict: visual phenomenology is genuinely absent there; lack of report tracks lack of experience.

Cases 3 and 5 seem to conflict: in one, absent report means absent phenomenology; in the other, absent report coexists with intact phenomenology. Is there a precise principle—perhaps distinguishing sensory integration from introspective access—that saves all five? If not, which verdict should I overturn? Apply your principle to a synthetic system that achieves perfect sensory integration but possesses no self-model whatsoever, and state explicitly which of my five judgments your account cannot accommodate.
