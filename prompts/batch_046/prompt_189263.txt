As a retired natural scientist who has recently turned to philosophy, I should like you to analyse the split-brain experiments of the 1960s — specifically the cases studied by Roger Sperry and Michael Gazzaniga in which severing the corpus callosum seemed to produce two largely independent centres of awareness within a single cranium — through the lens of the Buddhist doctrine of anatta (no-self) and its Western philosophical correlates in reductionist theories of personal identity. I am interested in the metaphysical mapping, not in clinical or ecological detail.

Please structure your answer in three parts.

First, apply the theory to the episode. How do the empirical findings — the left-hemisphere confabulator that invents narratives of coherence, the right hemisphere that recognises objects but cannot speak, and the striking absence of subjective conflict between the two sides — relate to the Buddhist skandhas or to a Parfit-style reductionist view? Identify precisely which features anatta illuminates and which it misdescribes or leaves entirely silent.

Second, reverse the analysis. Does the case support anatta by showing that personal identity can fragment without any underlying substantial self? Or does it instead presuppose a single subject that has merely been divided? Be explicit about the ontological options available — one person with bifurcated consciousness, two distinct persons, or the absence of any persisting subject — and indicate where the Buddhist framework offers no decisive verdict.

Third, address one tension that standard treatments neglect. Treat the left-brain confabulator not simply as a cognitive module but as an expression of asmi-māna, the I-am conceit, and ask whether its constant fabrication of ownership and unity is exactly the kind of false narrative that anatta claims operates even in intact brains. Does the split-brain case therefore reveal a normally hidden mechanism of self-construction, or does it produce an artificial pathology that tells us nothing about ordinary selfhood?

Please write your answer in American English. Keep the reasoning disciplined: do not force the theory to speak where it is silent, and do not request sources, bibliographies, or citations I could not verify without web access.
