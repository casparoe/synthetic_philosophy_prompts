(3) Collective acceptance C is constituted by individual mental states of recognition M.
(4) Those mental states are grounded in neurobiological states B, which are in turn grounded in fundamental physics P.
(5) By transitivity (1), I is ultimately grounded in P.
(6) Fundamental physics contains no normative, representational, or institutional properties; so P cannot ground I in virtue of being authoritative.

(9) So C is partly grounded in I, while I is grounded in C via (2)–(5): a diachronic grounding loop.
(10) Because grounding is irreflexive (1), loops are impossible; hence one side of the loop must be ungrounded.
(11) I is clearly not fundamental, so I must be eliminated.
(12) By generalization, all institutional, normative, and modal facts collapse into either physical facts or fictions.
