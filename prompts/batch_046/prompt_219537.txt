The problem is I now have to actually operationalize this every quarter for about fifteen patients, sign off on whether they're still the same legal and moral person for contracts and family rights, and I have maybe twenty minutes per file with no philospher on call.

So please turn that continuity principle into a real decision procedure I can run: the ordered checks, what counts as passing each one, where it stops with a hard no, where it hands the decision to me because it's values not facts, and where it'll misfire. I need it to survive cases where memory is intact but personality shifts, where replacement was paused for months then resumed, and where the tech crew accidentally made a dormant backup copy. Then apply it to three cases: a patient at 45% replacement who remembers her wedding but her risk preferences are totally inverted; a patient with a dormant duplicate at 60% replacement; and one where replacement halted for eighteen months then restarted -- does the gap break continuity? Make sure one case gives an answer I'll hate, and say honestly what the checklist can't do and which distinctions I had to drop to make it usable.
