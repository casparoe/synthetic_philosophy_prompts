Next week I'm interviewing Dr. Mara Chen, a philospher of medicine who's been arguing for something she calls contextual normativism about health and disease. The gist is that calling something a disease isn't just describing a biological malfunction like Boorse's biostatistical theory says, and it isn't just expressing a naked social value either. Instead she thinks health is a relational normative status: an organism is healthy when its functioning aligns with the demands of its specific ecological and social niche. So the same physiological state — insulin resistance, say — could be healthy in one enviroment and diseased in another, not because we value it differently, but because the normative standard itself shifts with context. She also has this provocative claim about evolutionary mismatch: conditions like type 2 diabetes in modern food enviroments aren't really diseases under her view, since the body is functioning exactly as it was designed to for resource scarcity; it's the enviroment that's violated the norm, not the organism.
