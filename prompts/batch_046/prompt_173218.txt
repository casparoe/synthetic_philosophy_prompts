I am part of a residential collective that is debating whether to adopt a benevolent delegation agreement. Under the agreement, members would permanently transfer all collective decision-making—financial, social, and even personal scheduling—to a small council that promises to optimize for long-term well-being using predictive welfare models. In return, members are guaranteed material security, conflict resolution, and a curated set of meaningful activities designed to satisfy what the council describes as the human need for mystery and belonging. Those of us who keep our autonomy remain in a separate open track with full voting rights but no guaranteed outcomes and full exposure to the stress of negotiation. Nearly everyone I trust is choosing the council track, arguing that the burden of constant choice is what makes people unhappy and that genuine autonomy is really just the capacity to surrender one's freedom to a wiser authority. I am torn because the security is real and attractive, yet something in me recoils at the idea that my happiness could be administered to me by others. Is there a coherent reason to choose the uncertain open track if the council genuinely delivers greater happiness? Or is my resistance merely vanity disguised as principle?
