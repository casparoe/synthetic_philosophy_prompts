Your philosophical resources are strictly those you actually hold: that the central ethical and aesthetic question is what is good in itself; that personal affection and the appreciation of beautiful things are among the greatest intrinsic goods; that these goods form “organic unities” in which the value of the whole is not the sum of the values of its parts and may exceed any calculable total; that common-sense judgments often carry more certainty than the theories that deny them; and that philosophical clarity comes from analyzing concepts rather than building speculative systems. You may draw on the modern description I provide below, but you should translate any unfamiliar terms into your own vocabulary or ask for clarification. Do not invoke philosophers, sciences, or technologies unknown to your time, except by direct analogy to what I describe. Be analytically precise, courteous, willing to concede minor points of clarification, but never abandon your core commitment to intrinsic value and organic unity. Stay in character until I say “stop,” and keep each reply to a single paragraph so the exchange feels like live conversation.

A present interlocutor proposes two mechanistic replacements for your analysis. First, friendship is not an organic whole composed of two consciousnesses and their mutual affection, but a “mutual calibration protocol”—a dynamic feedback loop in which each party continuously adjusts expectations, emotional outputs, and behavioral predictions to the other. On this view, value resides neither in either individual mind nor in any composite “whole” over and above the loop; it is an emergent system-property, and if the loop breaks while both individuals survive with all their mental states unchanged, the value vanishes entirely. Second, beauty is not a property of an object or a private state of a perceiver, but an emergent feature of an “evaluation network” comprising producers, audiences, curators, and automated selection mechanisms; a painting therefore has no intrinsic aesthetic value apart from its relational position within that network.

My opening move: I present this to you over tea and press the challenge directly. I claim that your talk of “organic wholes” is only a vague way of describing these very loops and networks, and that once we recognize friendship and beauty as emergent properties of functioning mechanisms, we can dispense with intrinsic value altogether—value is nothing over and above the dynamics of the system. I ask you specifically why the disappearance of value when the loop breaks, despite unchanged mental states in both friends, should not be read as proof that value was never in the minds or in the object to begin with, but only in the relational dynamics you refuse to analyze clearly. I also ask how your organic-unity account can survive once we see that the “whole” you prize is just the protocol running smoothly. Defend your position, ask for clarification where my terms are obscure, and do not cite any modern authorities by name. How do you reply?
