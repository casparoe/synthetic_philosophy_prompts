You chair the review panel for a small arts-technology grant program. Every quarter you receive proposals for generative works that claim to be "creative variations" on an existing theme—musical, visual, or textual—produced by algorithmically adjusting parameters in a conceptual space rather than by collage, pastiche, or random mutation. You need a reusable evaluation procedure, grounded in Hofstadter's idea that creativity is navigation through a space of possibilities defined by continuous or discrete "knobs" (semantic dimensions that can be turned up, down, or off while preserving recognizability of the source), that your non-philosopher panelists can run in a 45-minute session with unanimous sign-off required for a $50,000 award. The procedure must be ordered: first identify the theme's explicit knob-set; second, apply a recognizability test; third, apply a continuity test (distinguishing knob-turning from module-swapping); fourth, apply a value/surprise check; and at each stage flag whether the decision is factual or requires aesthetic judgment. Include the exact questions reviewers ask, the minimal evidence needed to answer each, the stopping conditions (automatic reject, automatic approve, or hand to human judgment), and the hard cases the procedure cannot settle—especially negative-space variations where a knob is turned to zero, entangled knobs where adjusting one distorts another unpredictably, and cases where the source theme is ambiguous. Then apply the procedure to three proposals: (1) a system that takes a well-known Baroque chorale and gradually lowers the voice-leading constraint knob until parallel fifths emerge but the melody remains intact; (2) a corporate-brand redesign that applies saturation and symmetry knobs to a trademarked logo, yielding a distorted but instantly identifiable image; and (3) a proposal where the algorithm infers its own "theme" from a training corpus and the knobs are uninterpretable latent dimensions. For each, show where the procedure lands and, for the case where it produces an answer your panel will dislike, explain exactly why the procedure forces that result and what judgment it still leaves open. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do—what classes of creative claim lie outside its scope.
