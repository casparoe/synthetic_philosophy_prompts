Jorge Luis Borges's fiction repeatedly returns to temporal structures—branching futures in "The Garden of Forking Paths," circular recurrence in "The Circular Ruins," and the dissolution of personal identity across infinite repetition in "The Immortal." Philosophers of time have mined these texts for illustrations of modal realism, eternalism, and the B-theory, yet the interpretive literature remains fragmented: some treat Borges as advancing consistent metaphysical theses, others as staging paradoxes without ontological commitment, and still others as practicing an aesthetic skepticism about temporal ontology altogether. Rather than another survey, I want a defended research agenda. Suppose one had three years and a small working group to investigate Borges's contribution to philosophy of time—not as literary criticism, but as conceptual work—where should the effort go? Rank the following open lines by importance and tractability: (1) whether the branching-time narrative can be formalized as a genuine alternative to Lewisian modal realism or remains irreducibly metaphorical; (2) whether the circular-recurrence story commits Borges to a Nietzschean eternal recurrence with ontological weight, or instead undermines the coherence of temporal direction; (3) whether the immortality narrative's erasure of personal identity over time supports a perdurantist or stage-theoretic ontology; and (4) an underexplored line—whether Borges's recurring library and labyrinth metaphors constitute an implicit argument that temporal multiplicity is always epistemically mediated, so that any ontology of time must first account for the archival conditions under which temporal claims become intelligible. For the ranking, specify the criteria used—conceptual payoff, tractability with current analytic tools, and dependency relations among the questions—explain which are upstream of which, and identify which settled answers would genuinely change how Borges is read within analytic philosophy of time. The deliverable should be a prioritized agenda, not a reading list or bibliography.
