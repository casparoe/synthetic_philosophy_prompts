Hey, I've been running into this pattern near Williams's integrity objection and that 'one thought too many' idea, but I can't tell if it's a real named thing or just me mixing up concpets. So there's this thing people do where they reject not just some action, but the very framing of their own decent actions as obligations or reasons at all. Like my friend cares for her disabled brother and gets genuinely angry if anyone calls it 'doing her duty' -- she says if she started thinking that way it wouldn't feel like her anymore, like she was playing a role. Or the guy who won't vote in an election he cares about because he insists 'I'm not political, that's not me,' even though he knows the stakes and isn't ignorant. It's not quite Williams's one-thought-too-many, since that feels like a specific crisis moment where you add one extra calculation before saving someone. This is more like a standing wall: a permanent refusal to let moral or political reasoning enter a zone you've claimed as 'just who I am.' It also isn't exactly the integrity objection, because these people aren't defending deep ground projects against utilitarian sacrifice -- they're protecting the feeling of authenticity from being colonized by any external justification, even ones they'd agree with on paper. Does this configuration have an established name? Maybe it's just extreme value internalization, or maybe it's a seperate failure mode. If there's no term, could you coin one and map the edges -- how it differs from integrity, from one-thought-too-many, and from plain akrasia? I want to know exactly what it is and what it isn't, not just a label.
