I am working through my position on infinite ethics and want to check whether the pieces actually cohere before I present them in a seminar. My commitments are these:

(1) I endorse a regional totalist axiology: the value of a world-state is determined by integrating well-being over a finite spatiotemporal reference region, and comparisons between worlds should be made region-by-region rather than by attempting a global sum.

(2) I reject average utilitarianism, not on familiar intuitive grounds, but because in populations with uniform density extending infinitely the spatial average is translation-invariant and therefore unstable as a decision criterion.

(3) I hold that in a genuinely infinite, homogeneous universe any attempt at global aggregation fails, which means global totalism provides no actionable guidance for local agents.

(4) I nonetheless accept longtermist reasoning: I treat the welfare of distant future generations as carrying overwhelming weight, and I regard the possibility of an astronomically large or genuinely infinite future population as morally urgent in the same direct way I regard a large finite population.

(5) I think moral obligations are strictly causally scoped: a being falls within my moral calculus only if my actions can affect it.

(6) I take phenomenal consciousness to be binary—there are no gradations yielding partial moral status—and I think this blocks the "dust" problem of infinitesimal consciousness generating infinitesimal obligations.

(7) I also accept that sufficiently complex digital simulations could be fully conscious, which implies that within our causal reach we could in principle generate obligations to an unbounded number of moral patients.

Please audit this package. Specifically: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear tense but dissolve once the commitments are properly specified, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each tension, say what the cheapest revision is and exactly what it costs—particularly whether fixing it requires abandoning regional totalism, weakening longtermism to a purely local scope, or giving up the binary view of consciousness. Be precise about whether (3) undermines (4) given (5), whether (5) is compatible with (4) once (3) is in play, and whether (6) and (7) are consistent given my binary view. I want a structured consistency check, not a general survey of infinite ethics.
