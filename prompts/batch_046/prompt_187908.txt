(1) If you're rational, you should have self-trust: whenever you actually run a valid inference M and derive p, you ought to believe p.

(2) But rationality shouldn't depend on the accident of having run the inference already. So if a near-copy of you on another reasoning-branch runs M and gets p, that's still good reason for you to accept p. Self-trust should apply across branches.

(3) Therefore a rational agent should endorse the universal schema inside its own belief system: for every p, if Derive_M(p) then True(p).

(4) Once that universal claim is inside the system, Löb's conditions are satisfied: for any specific p the system can derive, it can also derive True(p).

(5) By applying the schema with modus ponens, the agent derives p for every p it can derive—so it instantly believes every theorem in its logic, i.e. total dogmatic omniscience.

(6) Clearly nobody is omniscient about every derivable truth, so (5) is false.

(7) So rational self-trust is impossible: either you collapse into omniscience or you reject (1) and can't trust your own mind at all.
