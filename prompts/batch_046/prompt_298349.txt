I've noticed a recurring move in debates about science and truth that I don't have a word for. Someone defends theory T by saying it works in practice. You push on ontology - fields arn't literally real, they're just calculational - and they retreat to Peirce: truth is the limit of inquiry, not correspondence to hidden reality. Then you ask why we should care about that distant limit if T might be overturned in twenty years, and they retreat to James: its true now because it works in lived experience. So Peirce handles the ontology problem and James handles the stability problem, and they never defend both standards together. Is there an established name for this specific two-layer pragmatic shield? If not, can you coin one and map its edges - especially how its different from ordinary equivocation or a motte-and-bailey? I care about the carving, not just the label.
