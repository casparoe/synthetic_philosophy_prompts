Most climate economics computes a social cost of carbon that discounts damage to people born after 2100 at a few percent per year, yet those same existant people are given zero legal standing to challenge emissions now. What theory of intergenerational justice does that pairing actually presuppose? Is it just naked time-preference hidden in a formula, or does it depend on some trustee model where present goverments hold the climate in trust for them? And if the discount rate is supposed to capture uncertainty about whether they'll even be around, why do we still invoke their rights in other policy debates? Reconstruct the implicit view, check if it's internally consistent, and sketch what a climate institution built on direct rights for future generations—no discounting, actual representation—would have to change.
