My roommate built this thing he calls a careenium — basically a box where little simmballs roll through chutes. Each ball has a symbol stamped on it like IF or NOT. He says it's a phsyical computer. We got into whether the symbols actually push the substrate around or if it's just the balls' weight and shape doing everything. I sketched an arguement and now I'm stuck.

(1) If symbols are causally real in a system, then changing just the symbol (not the physical properties that realize it) should change what the substrate does.
(2) In the careenium, if I scratch off the "NOT" stamp and write "AND" with the same ink weight and shape, the simmball takes the exact same path. The symbol doesn't push it.
(3) So in any symbol-running system — brain included — the semantic content never pushes the substrate; only phsyical form does.
(4) That means my mental symbols don't cause my neurons to fire in the patterns that make me reach for coffe.
(5) But then reasons aren't causes of action, ever, and mental causation is a mirage.
(6) That proves too much — I clearly reach for coffe because I want it, and the want feels like it's doing the pushing.
(7) So one of these has to go. But (2) looks empirically true for the careenium, and (3) is just generalizing. Is the mistake in (1) — maybe symbols can be causally relevant without being independent phsyical forces? Or is thier some hidden slide between (3) and (4) about brains being different? I can't find the exit. Which step do you reject, and what does giving it up cost?
