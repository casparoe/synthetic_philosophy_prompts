I've been reading about panpsychism — the idea that even tiny particles have some form of consciousness. There's an argumentative pattern I keep noticing and I don't know if it has a name. The proponent starts by comparing mental combination to physical combination: just like atoms combine into a table, little conscious subjects combine into you. But then, when you press them that physical combination creates genuinely new properties (solidity, mass), they retreat and say mental combination is totally different — it dosen't aggregate properties, it's not like physics at all. So they borrow the credibility of scientific combination to make the view seem plausible, then strip away the very features that make combination explanatory. Is there an established term for this specific two-step? How is it different from a plain equivocation or a motte-and-bailey? And is the underlying issue their dodging just the standard combination problem, or is this a distinct failure mode?
