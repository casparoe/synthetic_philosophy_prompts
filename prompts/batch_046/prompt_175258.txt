hey — i'm writing a feature on teh gender-equality paradox (you know, teh nordic data where more equality = more gendered career splits?). before i pitch i want to audit my own takes, because i think they might be pulling in different directions.

here's roughly where i stand:

(1) the paradox is real — in more egalitarian societies the nursing/engineering split gets wider, not smaller.

(2) i think it's partly a prefference amplification loop: when you remove economic pressure, small initial differences (some evolutionary, some cultural) get magnified by peer feedback and gendered marketing.

(3) but i also think "innate preference" is incoherent without culture already baked in, so separating nature from nurture here is basically a dead end.

(4) justice, for me, is about barriers, not outcomes — if people genuinely want different fields, unequal representation isn't unfair.

(5) yet i think we'll never verify "genuine" want, since socialization is invisble from birth, so big gaps should be presumtive evidence of hidden barriers and we should correct them.

(6) i reject the pundit narrative that feminism backfired and caused the gap, even though i think biology plays some role.

(7) honestly i think career choice is a lousy metric for gender justice anyway — it ignores autonomy and job quality.

do these hang together? tell me which pairs are in real tension and which just look like it. rank the real ones by severity, say what'd have to give in each case, and be specific about which commitment bends and what that costs. don't lecture me — audit the package.
