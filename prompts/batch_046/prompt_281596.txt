I'm trying to pin down exactly where the ontology of dispositions stops being accommodating and starts being vacuous, and I think I've walked into a reductio that I can't locate the exit from. The standard distinction runs like this: a fink interrupts the causal chain from a categorical base to its manifestation without destroying the base itself, while a mask blocks the conditions for manifestation without altering the object. From this, philosophers usually conclude that the absence of manifestation is never by itself evidence for the absence of the disposition. Now take this reasoning and extend it rigorously.

(1) Every disposition D of an object O is grounded in a categorical base B that O actually possesses. (2) If O possesses B but fails to manifest D in conditions C, the correct explanation is always a fink or mask, not the absence of D. (3) Now consider an apparent case of disposition-loss: poison is neutralized by an antidote, glass is melted into liquid, a fragile crystal is dissolved into a homogeneous solution. In each case, the object no longer manifests the original disposition, and its categorical structure has apparently changed. (4) But these changes can be redescribed without assuming any genuine loss. The antidote does not destroy the poison's toxic categorical structure; it introduces a structural fink — a chemical mechanism that, upon contact, immediately transforms any remaining toxic base into an inert compound, thereby interrupting the causal chain just as the familiar live-wire interrupter does. The melting of glass is not the destruction of a brittle lattice but the activation of a deep thermal mask that suppresses rigidity-manifestation by continuously rearranging bonds before fracture can propagate. (5) By parity of reasoning, any case in which an object apparently loses a disposition can be redescribed as the presence of a structural fink or deep mask suppressing an unchanged categorical base. (6) Therefore, dispositions are never genuinely acquired or lost; they are only ever revealed, masked, finked, or suppressed. A melted steel ingot retains the precise disposition to shatter like its original glass form, and a perfectly homogeneous salt solution retains the exact crystalline fragility of its prior solid state, both merely hidden behind total masks. (7) This is clearly absurd — it collapses the distinction between potential and actual categorical structure, and makes dispositional ascription empirically empty.

So one of the premises must fail. But I can't tell whether the mistake is in extending the fink/mask logic from external interference to internal structural transformation, or in assuming that every disposition-loss must be explainable by interference rather than by genuine categorical change. Which step should I reject, and why does it look so plausible? More specifically, if I reject (4) — denying that antidotes and melting are just structural finks — what do I owe an account of? Does the categoricalist then have to allow that some changes are genuine destructions of bases, and if so, how do they distinguish a real destruction from an especially effective mask without circularity? And what does the dispositional essentialist, who treats powers as fundamental rather than grounded, have to say about whether powers can ever genuinely cease?
