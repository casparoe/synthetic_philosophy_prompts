i've been thinking about this since my friend got diagnosed with something the doctors call bipolar but she says is just her personality being too intense for a boring world, and she keeps sending me these long essays about how psychiatry is basically a social control mechanism. she's read some Szasz, i think, or maybe just heard the phrase myth of mental illness on a podcast. but here's the thing i keep noticing in these conversations — not just with her but with other people too. someone will say mental illness isn't real, it's just a label we slap on people who don't fit in, and then like five minutes later they'll talk about how schizophrenics can't be trusted to make decisions or how depression is just a chemical problem that needs fixing. it's not hypocrisy exactly, because they don't think they're contradicting themselves — they think they're being nuanced. they want to reject the medical model when it means giving people drugs or locking them up, but they still want to use medical-sounding categories to explain away behavior they don't like.

is there a name for this specific move? i know it's near Szasz's actual argument but it feels different — Szasz was talking about the conceptual confusion of treating problems in living as if they were diseases of the brain, not about using anti-psychiatry rhetoric selectively while keeping all the stigmatizing baggage. and it's not just plain inconsistency, because there's a pattern to which parts of the medical framework get kept and which get thrown away. the kept parts are always the ones that justify exclusion or dismissal, never the ones that justify care. does this have an established name in the philosophy of psychiatry, or in critical theory more broadly? and if not, how would you draw the line between this and genuine anti-psychiatry positions, or between this and just being confused?
