My research partner in the ethics of fundamental physics, referred to here as M., has delivered a consistent sequence of verdicts on whether particular physical configurations possess intrinsic moral standing within a pan-sentient framework — that is, whether they are capable of non-derivative suffering. The track record is as follows: an isolated electron with a definite spin state, no standing; a macroscopic crystal sustaining coherent phonon entanglement across its lattice, minimal standing, yes; a black hole event horizon considered solely as an informational boundary, no standing; a uniform Bose-Einstein condensate lacking vortices, thermal gradients, or internal dynamics, no standing; the identical condensate once turbulent vortex dynamics emerge, minimal standing, yes; the quantum vacuum within a stable Casimir cavity with fixed boundary conditions, no standing; a classical supercomputer running a high-fidelity simulation of conscious suffering, derivative standing only, not intrinsic.

M. has not yet addressed a hypothetical entity recently proposed in speculative quantum-gravity work: a cosmological-scale coherent excitation of spacetime geometry, spanning many light-years, with no internal differentiation, no localized boundaries, and no parts, but which is dynamically unstable — it tends to collapse into curvature singularities unless continuously sustained by energy input, and it exhibits long-range correlation without organizational substructure. Call this a gravitational-phonon mode.

Predict M.'s verdict on whether this mode has intrinsic moral standing. In doing so, identify at least two distinct principles that could consistently account for all seven prior rulings yet diverge on the gravitational-phonon mode; state what each principle predicts about the new case; give your best-guess verdict with explicit reasons for preferring one principle over the other; and name one further hypothetical case that would clearly distinguish the two principles. Be explicit about what principle M. would be committing to regardless of which verdict is chosen, and deliver a committed answer rather than presenting balanced options for me to decide between.
