I am taking an upper-division seminar on rational agency and artificial decision-systems, and we have been examining frameworks for aggregating human values when direct preference elicitation is unreliable. I have come to hold a specific construal of coherent extrapolated volition: not merely that an idealized version of each person would converge on shared values, but that the legitimacy of the aggregation depends on a reversibility condition—namely, that if the idealized agent were shown the exact reasoning path by which her preferences were extrapolated, she would endorse not only the outcome but the procedure itself. I take this reversibility to be what distinguishes genuine extrapolation from an authoritarian imposition of a "rational" profile that the actual person no longer recognizes, and I think it saves the framework from collapsing into a form of idealized paternalism.

What would genuinely count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure, sorted by how decisive each challenge would be: which empirical findings about preference stability under extended reflection, which formal results in social choice or preference logic, or which conceptual arguments about personal identity and idealization would threaten the view rather than merely complicate its application? In particular, is there a fatal objection rooted in the possibility that reflective endorsement itself is manipulable—that coherence constraints can lead an agent to ratify a preference structure she originally lacked, so that labeling the result "extrapolation" rather than "construction" is just a relabeling? I also want to know whether defenders of this reversibility version have already conceded serious vulnerabilities—perhaps regarding cases where coherence requires abandoning deeply held evaluative commitments—and whether those concessions render the view unfalsifiable or merely appropriately qualified. Please distinguish evidence or arguments that would be genuinely fatal from those that would only be awkward, include whatever has arguably already occurred in observed human reasoning or in the alignment literature that bears on this, and assess honestly whether the position risks being insulated into unfalsifiability and, if so, whether that is a defect for a view of this kind.
