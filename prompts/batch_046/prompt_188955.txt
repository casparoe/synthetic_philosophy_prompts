Write an essay on whether program equilibrium genuinely resolves commitment paradoxes in strategic interaction, or whether it merely relocates them into the architecture of the programs themselves. On one side, proponents argue that letting agents commit to algorithms—rather than to actions—eliminates the need for repeated-game reputation mechanisms and allows credible threats or promises in one-shot settings. On the other side, critics note that if agents can also select meta-programs that specify conditions under which they will rewrite their own code upon receiving messages, then any apparent commitment is unstable: the agent retains a hidden option to revise, and the commitment problem re-emerges at the level of meta-commitment. Your task is to weigh these positions and address whether there is a principled boundary between legitimate program commitment and illegitimate meta-commitment—whether that boundary depends on physical or institutional immutability, on formal features of the equilibrium concept, or on something else. Reach and defend a clear verdict: either program equilibrium succeeds in solving the commitment problem (and explain precisely what halts the regress), or it fails (and explain why the meta-program objection ultimately succeeds). Keep the analysis conceptual rather than technical; do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works.
