Write a short story set in a meticulously reconstructed revolutionary Paris of 1793, centered on an obscure provincial archivist who uncovers a private notation system embedded within official Committee of Public Safety ledgers. Rather than recording conspiracies, the notations appear to map the contingent emotional temperatures—fear, conviction, exhaustion—of the deputies across the months preceding the Thermidorian Reaction. Use this discovery to stage, in narrative form, the historiographical tension between interpreting the French Revolution as class struggle, as ideological commitment, or as radical contingency. In particular, dramatize the idea that revolutions devour their children not through simple political betrayal, but through the gradual erosion of the conceptual categories—enemy versus citizen, virtue versus terror—that once made revolutionary action intelligible. Ground the narrative in concrete sensory and bureaucratic detail, yet allow the notation system to operate as a speculative philosophical device that is not drawn from any standard historical archive.
