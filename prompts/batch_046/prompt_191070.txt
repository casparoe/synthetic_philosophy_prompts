While reading an essay that compares Platonic epistemology to computational storage models, I encountered a summary of the Meno problem that I am unsure about. The writer claims that Socrates treats true belief as analogous to a cached output that produces correct behavior only in stable conditions, whereas knowledge is analogous to a result that can be reconstructed from first principles, making it resilient to environmental change. The essay further argues that the episode with the slave boy demonstrates that all learning is merely recollection of pre-existing data, and therefore that Plato's distinction between knowledge and true belief reduces to a difference in the robustness of the storage mechanism rather than in any intrinsic property like justification or explanatory access. I find the analogy intuitively clear, but I worry it may conflate Plato's idea of binding opinions through a reasoned account of their cause with a modern idea about data redundancy. Is this summary fair? Specifically, does Plato think knowledge is valuable solely because it is more stable, or is there a further normative or explanatory element? Does the recollection thesis actually support the storage-model interpretation, or does it point in a different direction? And is the writer correct that for Socrates the only relevant difference is robustness, or is there a qualitative gap that the computational vocabulary cannot capture?
