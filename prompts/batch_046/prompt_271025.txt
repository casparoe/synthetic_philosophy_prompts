I've noticed that in rationalist-adjacent discussions of Hofstadter's idea that a self can be distributed across brains — he frames his late wife's cognitive pattern as partly persisting inside his own — people reach for an open-source analogy: the self is a repository, individual brains are nodes, and transmitting the pattern to another mind is like forking the code. The analogy is used to license the conclusion that personal identity is substrate-independent and that an 'I' can genuinely span multiple skulls without paradox. Can you audit how far this holds? Give the explicit correspondence — commit history, maintainer authority, merge conflicts, licensing — then isolate the disanalogies that specifically undermine Hofstadter's claim rather than just noting that brains aren't servers. Does the conclusion that distributed selfhood is coherent survive once the mapping is corrected? And if the repository analogy is carrying hidden work, what's a cleaner analogy for thinking about pattern-persistence across minds?
