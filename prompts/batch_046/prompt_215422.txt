I have been trying to clarify my actual view on origin essentialism—specifically, whether I could have come from a different sperm and egg—through reflective equilibrium rather than abstract argument. A friend working in synthetic biology recently pressed me on artificial gametes, and I realized my intuitions about replication and substitution are not as tidy as I assumed. Here are the verdicts I keep returning to.

If my father had released a different sperm cell that fertilized my mother's egg, or if an IVF clinic had selected a different embryo formed from my parents' gametes, I do not think the resulting person would be me. If conception had taken place in an artificial womb using the very same sperm and egg, I still think it would be me, so the physical environment of conception seems irrelevant. Now consider synthetic replication: if scientists engineer an egg that is molecularly identical to my mother's actual egg—same mitochondrial DNA, same epigenetic markers—and fertilize it with my father's original sperm, I feel the result is me; and symmetrically, if only the sperm is synthetically reproduced and the egg is the biological original, I also feel it is me. But if both gametes are perfect synthetic copies derived directly from scans of the originals, my intuition splits: one part of me says the genetic and causal profile is preserved, so it should be me; the other part says that because neither token actually originated in my parents' bodies, the link is broken. By contrast, if a clone is created from my adult somatic cells after my death, I am quite sure it is not me, even though it carries my genome. So I seem to think that at least one original gamete must be present, yet I am willing to substitute the other with an artificial duplicate. Is there a single principle—perhaps about partial causal continuity, about the non-substitutability of the pair but not of each member, or about the necessity of a biological origin in at least one reproductive line—that accommodates all of these judgments? And what should I conclude about the borderline case where both gametes are synthetic replicas, but the replication process used direct molecular templates taken from the original zygote immediately after fertilization?
