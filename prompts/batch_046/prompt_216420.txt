Write a six-month dissertation prospectus addressing the following conceptual problem: Daniel Dennett’s category of “counterfeit people” — beings that satisfy all behavioral and functional criteria for personhood yet lack some deeper property that renders them merely simulated moral agents — was originally developed with physically instantiated robots in view, where the counterfeit was a static duplicate lacking internal experience. The open question is whether Dennett’s own anti-essentialist, stance-based theory of personhood can consistently preserve this counterfeit/genuine distinction when the artificial being is not a static robot but a dynamically updated informational pattern — such as a persistent conversational agent with cross-session memory, coherent biographical narrative, and responsive emotional registers — that maintains temporal continuity without biological substrate or continuous consciousness. Specifically, does Dennett’s framework tacitly depend on a hidden commitment to biological origin or narrative genesis that his explicit rejection of essentialism forbids, and can a purely functional criterion — for instance, vulnerability to irreversible narrative rupture or capacity for moral harm that alters the pattern’s self-model — distinguish a counterfeit digital persona from a genuine one without smuggling in the very metaphysical commitments Dennett denies?

Your prospectus should: (1) formulate the question with sufficient precision that a determinate answer is possible, specifying exactly what “counterfeit” means in this updated context; (2) explain why existing treatments of Dennett and artificial intelligence either ignore the counterfeit distinction entirely or apply it anachronistically without confronting the problem of temporal and informational continuity in modern agent architectures; (3) propose a conceptual approach that works directly with Dennett’s accounts of personhood, the intentional stance, and narrative selfhood rather than with empirical AI benchmarking, and justify why this textual and philosophical route succeeds where behavioral testing fails; (4) describe what a successful result would look like — such as a reconstructed functional criterion that survives Dennett’s anti-essentialist constraints, or a rigorous demonstration that the distinction collapses — and specify what would count as failure, for example a reconstructed criterion that either begs the question by assuming biological continuity or applies indiscriminately to ordinary human persons; (5) provide an honest scope assessment covering which parts of Dennett’s corpus must be reconstructed, which contemporary agent architectures serve as the relevant test cases, and whether six months permits both the textual work and the conceptual application; and (6) state the deliverable format, for example a 15,000-word dissertation chapter with references and citations to be completed by the author in the writing phase. Do not include a bibliography, literature review by citation, or requests for specific readings; refer to positions and arguments by paraphrase and indicate that source verification will be handled separately.
