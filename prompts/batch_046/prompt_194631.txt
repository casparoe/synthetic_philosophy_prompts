Write the desiderata that any adequate normative theory must meet if it is to reconcile lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism with the thesis that pleasure and pain are phenomenologically incommensurable yet remain aggregate-comparable at the policy level. The target theory is neither a pure lexical rule nor a smooth utilitarian calculus; it must occupy the middle ground where a non-arbitrary lexical floor against severe suffering coexists with non-lexical evaluation of mild states, without collapsing the distinction between having moral status and bearing quantitative weight.

For each requirement, specify: the motivating case or conceptual pressure that makes it non-negotiable; whether it is genuinely required or only a preference of a particular camp (e.g., those who demand cardinal inter-personal comparison, those who insist on metaphysical currency monism, those who reject all aggregation); and whether it stands in direct tension with another item on the list.

Your specification must cover at least the following dimensions: (i) the separation of intra-personal hedonic trade-offs from inter-personal aggregation, with the reason each domain may be governed by different rules; (ii) non-arbitrary boundary conditions that determine where the lexical threshold is set and prevent ad hoc adjustment to avoid counterexamples; (iii) consistency with empirical generalizations about hedonic adaptation, pain asymmetry, and the non-linearity of suffering intensity; (iv) the treatment of marginal or sub-threshold suffering versus catastrophic suffering, including whether the threshold is absolute, context-relative, or indexed to a baseline; (v) the resolution of cases in which immense aggregate pleasure appears to depend on permitting a small, non-catastrophic suffering—state exactly what verdict the theory must deliver and why; (vi) whether comparison across individuals is permitted only ordinally or may be cardinal, and what follows for distributive judgments if only ordinal comparison holds; and (vii) the metaphysical commitment, if any, regarding whether pleasure and pain share a common quantitative currency or remain dimensionally distinct at the level of value ontology, together with how that commitment interacts with (i) and (vi).

Then identify the single desideratum that does the most work in excluding popular hybrid theories—those that invoke lexical thresholds only for extreme suffering but retain standard commensurability elsewhere—and flag any subset of your requirements that you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable given standard arguments about incommensurability, scale invariance, or the impossibility of inter-personal cardinal measurement. Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal tensions rather than on scoring named philosophers; do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citation of specific texts.
