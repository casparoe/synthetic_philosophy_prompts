I've been working through Daniel Dennett's account of real patterns and the three explanatory stances—physical, design, and intentional—and I'm trying to extend the framework well beyond its usual home in philosophy of mind. Specifically, I'm interested in scientific models of complex adaptive systems, such as large-scale epidemiological forecasting tools or ecosystem dynamics simulations, that display robust, counterfactual-supporting regularities with an apparent goal-directed structure: for instance, a climate-feedback model that consistently predicts homeostatic adjustments as if the system were tracking a set-point, even though no one thinks the atmosphere has beliefs or desires. If we take seriously Dennett's claim that real patterns are those that license genuine predictions regardless of whether they map onto fundamental physical properties, and we apply his taxonomy of stances to these non-cognitive domains, what are the most important conceptual implications?

In particular, I want to understand three tensions that arise from this extension. First, does adopting the design stance or the intentional stance toward such systems commit the scientist to anything stronger than instrumentalism—say, to the claim that these patterns have a kind of ontological respectability—or can the stance be maintained as a purely predictive heuristic without metaphysical baggage? Second, how should we adjudicate explanatory conflicts when the physical stance predicts one behavioral trajectory and the design stance predicts another within the very same system, especially when both patterns appear to be "real" in Dennett's sense? Third, when the intentional stance is applied to non-agents, does it collapse into a redundant variant of the design stance, or does it retain independent explanatory value that justifies its use despite the risk of anthropomorphic overreach? I'm not looking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature—just the implications that follow from the framework itself, particularly where stretching it beyond minds forces a choice between explanatory power and ontological parsimony.
