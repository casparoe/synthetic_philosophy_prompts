I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of diachronic rationality must deliver, specifically as it bears on vulnerability to money-pump arguments and on dynamic consistency across time. Begin by stating the phenomena the theory must explain and the distinctions it must preserve: between rational preference revision driven by genuinely new information and cyclical intransitivity that invites exploitation; between being pumped because one’s preferences cycle and being pumped despite dynamically consistent, state-dependent preferences; and between employing a commitment device as a rational planning instrument versus deploying it as an ad hoc patch.

Then give the hard desiderata, each with the motivating case or consideration behind it. These must include at least the following. First, a dissolution of the standard multi-round money pump that does not require implausible foresight of all future preference states. Second, an account of precommitment mechanisms—whether they are rationally mandatory or merely optional—including explicit treatment of what I will call the commitment-loop case: an agent adopts a binding plan specifically to prevent a standard pump, but the adoption creates a predictable path that an adversary can exploit through state-contingent offers that tax the maintenance or activation of the commitment itself; the theory must say whether this is a genuine secondary vulnerability or an illusion. Third, a statement of which object bears the consistency conditions—preference orderings, choice dispositions, planning procedures, or belief-updating rules about one’s own future preferences—and a defense of that choice. Fourth, compatibility with empirical patterns of intertemporal choice (such as hyperbolic discounting structures) without collapsing the normative theory into descriptive psychology. Fifth, a precise criterion for distinguishing legitimate state-dependent utility shifts from irrational cycling, with boundary cases. Sixth, a position on whether full diachronic coherence requires determinism about one’s future choices, or whether partial self-knowledge about future preference structures is permissible without reopening the door to pumps.

For each desideratum, flag whether it is non-negotiable for any adequate theory or whether it expresses the preference of a particular camp—for instance, whether empirical equivalence with behavioral data is required, or whether determinism about future choice is. Then identify any subset of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable, particularly whether full exploitation-resistance can coexist with rational permission to revise preferences in response to new information, and whether the commitment-loop case reveals that resolute planning and sophisticated planning suffer structurally different but equally serious vulnerabilities.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing views, but briefly indicate how the main contending approaches—consistent planning, resolute choice, and sophisticated choice—fare against the list, especially with respect to the commitment-loop vulnerability. Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or reading recommendations; I want a theory specifiable and evaluable without access to external sources.
