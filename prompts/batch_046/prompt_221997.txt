My half-formed idea: in Gödel, Escher, Bach, Hofstadter distinguishes frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages as three levels at which a record can carry meaning. I want to ask whether this tripartite structure, if taken seriously as a model for ordinary linguistic meaning rather than just formal systems, forces us to relocate meaning away from both speaker intentions and public linguistic conventions and into the dynamic interaction between an interpretive frame and its target message. Specifically, if the frame is not static background but can be revised by the very message it frames, does that generate a strange loop in semantic interpretation that makes internalism and externalism both partly right and partly wrong?
