I am writing a dissertation chapter that treats material constitution — the statue and its constituent clay — not as an isolated puzzle but as a diagnostic for broader ontological commitments. Beyond the standard coincidence problem, there are at least four live extensions: its bearing on biological persistence given constant cellular turnover; on the ontology of restored artworks in conservation science, where original material is progressively replaced; on social institutions constituted by changing membership; and on whether accepting non-identity forces either a pluralist account of spatial location or an untenable mereological essentialism about the constituent matter. Among these lines, which two or three are genuinely upstream for metaphysics — in the sense that resolving them would restructure adjacent debates about artefacts, organisms, or social objects — and which are overrated distractions that presuppose a solution to the core puzzle? I want a defended prioritisation, not a literature survey: for each line, indicate its tractability for a philosopher working without empirical laboratory support, how many other questions depend on its resolution, and what kind of result over the next few years would cause you to reorder the ranking.
