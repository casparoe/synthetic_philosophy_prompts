(3) I reject psychologism—mathematical objects are not private mental states but shared constructions—yet I still hold that they depend on constructive activity in a substantive, not merely heuristic, sense.
(4) I think Brouwer's free choice sequences capture a genuine openness in mathematical creativity that Heyting's formalization of intuitionistic logic artificially suppresses.
(5) I want to preserve classical analysis and linear algebra in practice, but only as descriptions of fully determined, computable structures, not as descriptions of an actual infinite reality.

Please audit this set: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which apparent conflicts dissolve once you distinguish ontology from formal practice, and what the cheapest revision would be for each serious conflict. Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Order the items by severity of tension, and in each case state specifically which commitment has to bend or be qualified to restore consistency.
