As a retired condensed-matter physicist who has only recently begun to study the philosophy of logic and rational agency in earnest, I find that discussions of the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents tend to move too quickly to proposed remedies—restricted modal logics, stratified belief architectures, or resource-bounded implementations—without first specifying what an adequate resolution must actually deliver. I should like the full specification before I examine any particular solution.

Please write the desiderata that any theory accommodating, dissolving, or circumventing the Löbian obstacle to rational self-trust ought to satisfy. By theory I mean a formally explicit account of how a finite, realised agent can maintain rational confidence in its own reasoning processes despite Gödelian limitations.

For each desideratum, state:
- the precise requirement;
- the motivating case, logical consideration, or feature of scientific practice that makes it necessary;
- whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp.

The list should include, but need not be limited to, the following considerations:

1. A clear separation between syntactic derivability and semantic consequence, with an explanation of why collapsing this distinction misplaces the obstacle rather than solving it.

2. A decision on whether global belief in universal soundness is required, or whether graded, local, or hypothetical self-trust can suffice—motivated by the way working scientists treat their own calculational instruments without endorsing every possible derivation.

3. A condition of physical and computational realisability: the account must specify how an embodied, finite agent implements reflective monitoring without infinite regress or unbounded resource consumption.

4. A distinction—this seems to me essential, and perhaps under-discussed—between epistemic self-trust (confidence in the reliability of one's reasoning procedure) and doxastic self-trust (confidence that every currently held belief is true), together with an analysis of how Löb's result bears differently on each.

5. The requirement that the theory explain why human mathematicians can rationally endorse specific proofs they have personally verified, without thereby being rationally compelled to assert universal consistency.

6. Modal clarity: the framework must disambiguate object-language assertions of soundness from meta-language assertions, and specify exactly which modal strength of self-reference is being restricted.

Then, please address the architecture of the list itself:

- Identify which single desideratum eliminates the largest class of naive proposals, such as models that propose unconditional reflective endorsement.
- State whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable given Gödel's second incompleteness theorem or related no-go results; if so, that joint unsatisfiability is itself a result I wish to see clearly marked.
- Briefly—without bibliography, citation, or reading list—indicate how three broad approaches fare against the criteria: (i) strict modal-logic restrictions that weaken self-reference conditions; (ii) hierarchical or stratified self-models that separate reasoning levels; (iii) resource-bounded or approximate-reasoning frameworks that treat self-trust as a practical heuristic rather than a formal theorem.

Write in formal British English. I do not require verbatim quotations, bibliographic references, or lists of further reading; I want only the conceptual architecture and its internal tensions.
