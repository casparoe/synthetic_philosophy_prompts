Tranquilism holds that only the absence of craving and suffering has intrinsic value, while pleasure and satisfaction have none whatsoever, not even instrumentally. This distinguishes it from Epicurean ataraxia, which also prizes freedom from mental disturbance (ataraxia) and bodily pain (aponia), but Epicurus still allows that kinetic pleasures—like eating, drinking, or friendship—are genuinely good, just secondary. Tranquilism rejects that entirely: all positive states are axilogically neutral.

When we turn to negative utilitarianism, the overlap is nearly total. Both views agree that suffering is the only thing with negative value and that pleasure carries zero weight in moral calculation. In fact, tranquilism is essentially negative utilitarianism applied to the ontology of individual well-being rather than to social ethics. They share the same value-structure, just at different scales.

Regarding Nozick's experience machine, tranquilism escapes the objection cleanly. The machine offers artificial pleasure, and hedonists are tempted to plug in because pleasure is their currency. But for the tranquilist, pleasure is neutral whether real or simulated. The only relevant factor is whether suffering and craving are absent. If the machine eliminates both, then there is no reason to refuse it—indeed, plugging in might be rational. So the view sidesteps the objection because its premises reject the importance of authenticity for its own sake.
