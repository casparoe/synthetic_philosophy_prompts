I am constructing a novel in which a mathematician-philosopher maintains that phenomenal self-awareness is not merely analogized but literally instantiated by the structural feature Gödel exploited: a formal system complex enough to represent arithmetic encodes a representation of its own unprovability, and this syntactic self-reference, when embodied in biological substrate, becomes the first-person experience of being a subject. Before I commit her arguments to the page, I need the philosophical specification rather than the literary one. Write the full list of desiderata that any theory deriving phenomenal self-awareness from Gödelian self-reference—not as metaphor—must satisfy to count as adequate philosophy of mind.

For each desideratum, state the precise case or distinction it must preserve: the gap between a formula that refers to itself via Gödel numbering and a mind that refers to itself via an indexical pronoun; between logical incompleteness as a property of formal systems and epistemic limitation as a feature of conscious agents; between reflective closure (a system representing its own consistency conditions) and phenomenal ownership (that system experiencing those representations as belonging to a singular subject); between the existence of an undecidable sentence within a system and the qualitative feel of being the system that contains it; and between mechanistic specificity about physical implementation and explanatory adequacy regarding why that implementation is experienced as something rather than nothing. Identify which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect camp-specific commitments—computational functionalism, biological naturalism, anti-mechanism, or strict logical formalism. Then indicate which single desideratum eliminates the majority of popular accounts that borrow Gödel for rhetorical effect, and flag any pair of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable, particularly any tension between demanding that the self-reference be syntactically rigorous and demanding that it account for the unity of consciousness.

Conclude with a brief assessment of how three approaches fare against the list: the strange-loop model associated with Hofstadter’s work, a strict computational-functional reading that treats the brain as implementing the formal system, and a biological-naturalist correction that insists embodiment transforms syntactic structure into phenomenal presence—keeping the evaluation concise, since the criteria themselves are the deliverable.
