I'm in a philosophy-of-technology seminar, and after we discussed metaphysical persistence, two colleagues—Maya and Leo—got stuck in a sustained argument about a blockchain smart contract that uses a proxy architecture: its underlying executable code has been fully replaced, yet it retains the same address, accumulated state history, and legal identity. They asked me to referee the dispute.

Maya takes an endurantist line. She insists the contract is wholly present at each block and has endured through the upgrade exactly as a person endures through cell replacement. She argues that the change in bytecode is merely a change in properties—the contract now instantiates the temporary intrinsic of running implementation v2—while its identity remains fixed by continuity of address, state, and social recognition. She adds that if Leo were right, then obligations, ownership records, and user agreements would all suffer mysterious discontinuities, which suggests his view confuses practical tracking with metaphysical reality.

Leo takes a perdurantist line. He says the original contract was a four-dimensional worm composed of temporal parts, each essentially tied to a specific code version. Once execution was redirected to the new implementation, the original worm ended and a different four-dimensional object began, inheriting only the address by convention. He grants that law and practice treat the sequence as continuous, but claims that is causal and conventional tracking between successive stages, not the persistence of one enduring whole. When Maya demands to know exactly when the switch occurred—at the upgrade transaction, at block confirmation, or gradually—Leo replies that there need not be a sharp boundary, because for artifacts identity conditions are partly stipulated; the absence of a non-arbitrary cutoff supports conventionalism rather than endurance.

Maya answers that Leo's conventionalism undermines his own distinction: if there is no deep fact about the boundary, then there is equally no deep fact that there are two distinct worms rather than one changing thing. Leo counters that perdurantism can group successive temporal parts conventionally without treating the group as an enduring object that literally bears contradictory properties. He also suggests that digital artifacts may not share the persistence conditions of physical objects, since their identity is already constituted by functional and legal conventions rather than by spatiotemporal continuity.

Please go through their exchange move by move and evaluate each claim: Maya's analogy to biological persistence, Leo's claim that address-inheritance is merely conventional, Maya's charge that conventionalism collapses the distinction Leo wants to draw, and Leo's reply about grouping temporal parts without endurance. Identify any hidden assumptions—such as whether proxy architectures are designed precisely to preserve identity rather than replace it, or whether temporary intrinsics apply differently to code than to matter—and say who is right about each specific point. Then offer an overall verdict about who is more right, and point out anything important both parties are missing, especially regarding how the metaphysics of digital artifacts might diverge from standard physical examples like ships or organisms.
