Milo said the legislature is basically right. If 7.2 can shield itself behind its own text, then you've got hidden entrenchment: one generation binds the next by burying a lock inside a general rule. The whole point of a self-amending framework is that the base procedure applies everywhere, even to itself, or else democratic continuity is broken.

Sasha also pointed out that the Council used its 7.2 authority to reject the ammendment, but if the ammendment was valid then that authority never existed, so their rejection is self-undermining — yet Milo thinks that's just the price of legal self-reference.
