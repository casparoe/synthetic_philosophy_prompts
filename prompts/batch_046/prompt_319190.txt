Because such visual representations bypass linguistic mediation entirely, Neurath believed they could function not merely as pedagogical aids but as direct replacements for protocol sentences within the empirical basis of science. On his view, once observational records are rendered as intersubjectively accessible pictograms arranged in physicalist terms, there remains no essential role for first-person perceptual reports; the standardized image itself serves as the basic protocol, verifiable by any observer regardless of native language. Consequently, the ISOTYPE project represents a consistent extension of Neurath's empiricism and physicalism: it purges subjective verbal testimony from the foundation of science and substitutes a universal, publicly observable visual language that eliminates both metaphysical ambiguity and individual interpretive variation.
