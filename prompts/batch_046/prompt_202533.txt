Take Daniel Ellsberg leaking the Pentagon Papers in 71 and analyse it through Adam Smith's symapthy and the imparial spectator — but don't just say he was virtuous. I want a real mapping: who counts as the agent, who are the direct sufferers (the Vietnamese civilians? the American public?), and how does Smith's idea that sympathy weakens with distance hold up when the harm is statistical and the spectator is a government analyst? Does the impartial spectator approve breaking institutional loyalty for an abstract, distant suffering, or does Smith's merchant-and-neighbour framework just break here? Then reverse the lens: what does Ellsberg actually having to photocopy boxes and risk prison tell us about Smith's claim that we judge ourselves through an imagined other? Does physical risk restore the spectator's voice, or does it show the spectator is useless without embodied proximity? And be honest about where Smith's vocabulary is silent — can he even talk about whistleblowing, or is that outside his social world?
