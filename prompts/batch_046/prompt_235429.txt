I've been reading about the nonidentity problem since I retired from the lab — mostly Parfit, plus some newer papers I cant properly cite. I've ended up with a view that probably sounds daft: the problem only bites if you treat genetic origin as essential to who you are. Switch to a 'thick relational' account — where your identity is the narrative thread running through your social relationships, not the particular sperm-egg combo that kicked things off — and climate policies that shuffle the gene pool dont swap one person for another. They just shift which narratives get realised. So we can harm future people after all, because they're the same people, just with different genetic backstories. But I might be fooling myself. What would actually count against this? I want honest defeaters — not just the usual fission objections, but which intuitions, which formal results about persistence in general, and which findings in other areas (say animalism or psychological continuity) it stands or falls with. Which of these would a defender of the thick-relational view concede as damaging, and which would they brush off? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me whether the view is genuinely exposed or whether its been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if it has, whether thats actually a problem for a view of this kind. I'd be most persuaded by a case where social narrative continuity is kept intact but the genetic origin changes, and the intuition still screams its a different person. Also, please dont just list objections; tell me what the world would have to look like for each one to land.
