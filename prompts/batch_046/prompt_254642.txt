Using this framing, explain (a) why the master fails to achieve genuine recognition, (b) how the slave's labour transforms his relation to the object world, and (c) whether the dialectic resolves the asymmetry without a third-party mediator. Here's the student's answer:

The master-slave relation starts when two self-consciousnesses encounter each other and both demand to be recognised as free. They enter a life-and-death struggle, but because of the asymmetrical exposure we discussed, only the slave actually faces existential risk; the master, having already won, can withdraw recognition without any comparable danger. Once the relation is fixed, the master receives recognition from the slave, yet since the slave has been reduced to a thing-like consciousness, that recognition is worthless — it doesn't come from an equal. Meanwhile, the slave labours on objects for the master. Through this work he transforms raw nature into formed objects, and in seeing his own mind embodied in what he has made, he gains an independence that the master — who merely consumes — completely lacks. Over time, this growing self-awareness leads the slave to overthrow the master in a direct revolutionary reversal, establishing mutual recognition within the original two-person relation. Therefore the asymmetry is resolved internally, with no need for a mediator like the state or ethical life.
