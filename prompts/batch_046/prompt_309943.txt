I just finished a long and frustrating argument with a friend about moral particularism versus generalism. Every example we tried—kindness that enables dependency, loyalty that obstructs justice—felt like it had been discussed to death, and we could not agree on whether the disagreement was real or just verbal. I want you to construct an original thought experiment that forces the two views apart in a direction that is not already standard in the literature. Design a realistic, one-paragraph scenario set in an ordinary workplace or healthcare setting—not a trolley problem—in which the same type of action is performed in two contexts that are structurally identical except for a single feature. That feature should not be one of the usual moral properties like causing harm, breaking a promise, or distributing goods unfairly; instead, make it turn on something more conceptually niche, such as whether the action preserves epistemic humility within an institutional decision-making structure or sustains narrative continuity for stakeholders who are not directly affected. The scenario must generate opposite verdicts: in one context the particularist approves and the generalist disapproves, or vice versa, and the divergence must come from the theories themselves, not from a stipulation. After describing the case, walk through the reasoning: identify the action, the contexts, and why the particularist treats the feature as morally decisive in one case but not in the other, while the generalist applies the same underlying principle to both. Then isolate the structural feature that does all the work, explain what happens to the case if that feature is removed—does the disagreement collapse, or does one view still dominate?—and finish with an honest note on how a defender of the theory that comes out badly would attack the scenario: would they deny that the feature is genuinely present in both contexts, deny that it has moral weight, or argue that it can be subsumed under a broader principle without losing the particularist intuition?
