I co-host a philosophy podcast oriented toward readers of rationalist forums who are comfortable with forecasting concepts but want rigorous conceptual analysis rather than pop-psychology summaries. Next week's guest is a philosopher — let's call him Dr. Voss — who defends a calibrationist interpretation of the Knobe effect. On his view, the classic asymmetry in intentionality attribution between harmful and beneficial side-effects is not a cognitive bias to be eliminated, but an adaptive epistemic heuristic that assigns responsibility in proportion to expected information value, especially in high-uncertainty social forecasting. He applies this to argue that reference-class forecasting for charitable or policy interventions should explicitly incorporate, rather than correct for, the moral-valence weighting that drives the Knobe asymmetry.

I'm drafting the interview and need eight to ten questions that go well beyond having him restate the calibrationist thesis. For each, please include the standard objection from the experimental-philosophy literature and a brief note on how the follow-up should probe his answer. The tone should be formal but accessible to an audience that understands outside-view reasoning and epistemic humility but may not track the finer points of intentionality-attribution studies.
