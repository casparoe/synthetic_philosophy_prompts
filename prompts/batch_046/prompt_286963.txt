I am preparing a draft essay—tentatively titled "Penetration Without Contamination"—on the cognitive penetration of perception, and I would welcome your critical comments. Rather than rehearsing the familiar dispute over whether belief literally alters phenomenal character, I propose a four-layer taxonomy of top-down influence: (i) attentional weighting, in which prior belief directs sensory resources without altering registration; (ii) conceptual framing, in which belief structures the articulation of an unchanged phenomenal field; (iii) generative modulation, in which predictive processing reshapes early sensory encoding; and (iv) what I call normative penetration, in which a perceiver's practical or moral commitments render certain features salient in a rationally assessable manner that is neither purely causal (like attention) nor purely post-perceptual (like judgment). I illustrate (iv) with the case of ecological expertise: a conservationist and a developer may share an identical retinal image of a wetland, yet the conservationist's values make specific degradations stand out as salient in a way that seems neither reducible to expert pattern recognition nor to a subsequent inference. I argue that if normative penetration is genuine, then the standard boundary between perception and judgment collapses not descriptively but prescriptively, undermining the idea that perceptual testimony can be epistemically neutral.

Please comment specifically on: (a) whether the distinction between generative modulation and normative penetration is coherent, or whether the latter collapses into expert framing; (b) whether the taxonomy usefully advances the debate, or whether it introduces artificial distinctions; and (c) whether my conclusion about perceptual neutrality follows, or whether it conflates rational salience with representational content.

In giving your assessment, please be explicit about your level of confidence and about what specific argument or empirical finding would lead you to change your verdict—particularly regarding the coherence of normative penetration. Additionally, please identify the point in your own reasoning that is most likely to fail, whether through an overlooked distinction in the philosophy of perception literature or through an ambiguity in my framing of salience.
