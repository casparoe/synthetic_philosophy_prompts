I was talking with my friend last week about Parfit's hitchhiker and I realized I still dont get whether being rationally irrational is actually a stable position once you split rationality into two different things. I'm drafting a short proposal (maybe 3 pages) for a decision-theory reading group grant, and I want the question sharp but not just rehashing the causal vs evidential debate everyone already knows.

The specific question: if we distinguish procedural rationality (whether your reasoning procedure is internally coherent at the moment of choice) from evidential rationality (whether your beliefs about outcomes line up with your actions), does Parfit's hitchhiker still need to be irrational in either sense? Or can he be procedurally rational while being evidentialy self-defeating, and if so, does that make the rational irrationality label just a category mistake? I think existing answers leave this open because Parfit and most commentators treat rationality as a monolithic standard that gets violated, but they dont systematically check whether the violation happens in the reasoning step or in the belief-updating step. My approach would be to construct a modified hitchhiker case where the driver can observe not just the hitchhiker's final decision but also the internal structure of his deliberation—like whether he actually ran through a decision tree or just flipped a coin—and then ask whether being transparent about your reasoning changes which kind of rationality is at stake. I wouldnt do empirical work; its a conceptual analysis using the modified case to pressure-test the distinction.

What Id deliver: a clear map of which rationality concept applies where, plus an argument that either the hitchhiker is not irrational at all (just evidentialy unlucky) or that he is doubly irrational in a way Parfit missed. If the project fails, it fails by showing the two concepts collapse back into one, which would actually be a useful negative result. The scope is one semester or a single workshop paper—no literature review with citations, Ill fill those in myself, but I need the conceptual framing tight enough that the group can see exactly what needs arguing for.
