Consider four ontological stances on whether labels like “Baby Boomers,” “Millennials,” and “Gen Z” denote real kinds: (A) essentialist demographic kinds fixed by birth-year ranges and macro-historical events; (B) purely instrumental statistical bins with no social reality beyond marketing and polling convenience; (C) performative social constructions brought into being by the very discourse that names them; and (D) a hybrid “media-ecological cohort” view, according to which generations are transient, causally efficacious assemblages of shared attentional infrastructures and institutional reinforcement, lacking essential properties but possessing fuzzy, retrospective boundaries. Rank these four stances in tiers—strong, moderate, weak—according to how well each explains both the stability of generational voting patterns across election cycles and the consistently high intra-cohort variance in cultural practices, giving a one-sentence justification for each placement. Then identify which two stances your ranking treats as closest in practical implications despite their deepest metaphysical disagreement, and propose a single non-survey observational test that could clearly separate them.
