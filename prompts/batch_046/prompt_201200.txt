In my philosophy of cosmology and anthropic reasoning seminar, I defend a hybrid position on the presumptuous philosopher problem: rather than adopting the self-sampling assumption (SSA) or the self-indication assumption (SIA) across the board, I apply SSA only at the meta-level of reference-class selection—sampling myself randomly from the reference class I actually belong to—while applying SIA at the object-level within any fixed reference class, weighting competing cosmological hypotheses by their total number of observers. On this view, we do not reject large universes a priori, because the meta-level sampling fixes our epistemic position; yet within that position, hypotheses predicting more observers in our class gain support. This is meant to block Bostrom’s presumptuous-philosopher conclusion without embracing the counterintuitive near-certainty that pure SIA seems to grant to maximally populated civilizations. I want an honest map of this hybrid’s exposure. What would have to be true for it to fail? In particular: is there a formal coherence problem in mixing SSA and SIA across levels, or an argument that the meta-level/object-level distinction collapses under pressure? Which observations—about the distribution of intelligent life, about cosmological model selection, or about the structure of indexical uncertainty—would be decisive rather than merely awkward? Would a refined version of the presumptuous-philosopher argument show that reference-class boundaries cannot be fixed independently of observer counts? Have defenders of pure SSA or pure SIA conceded specific predictions or conceptual costs that my hybrid inherits? And if I can always adjust the reference-class boundary to protect the view from counterexample, is that insulation a genuine defect for a position of this kind? Please distinguish the defeaters that should actually move me from those that are only inconvenient, assess whether the view risks anything empirically or conceptually, and say whether its defenders would agree that it has been qualified into unfalsifiability.
