Here is my half-formed idea, which I need sharpened into a precise, tractable research plan.

The Platonia dilemma asks what a rational agent should do in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma when she knows her opponent is an exact psychological duplicate who will choose identically, with a predictor that rewards mutual cooperation more generously than mutual defection. The Luring Lottery extends this structure by introducing a probabilistic lure: the predictor offers a reward contingent on predicted cooperation, yet the agent can also secure a smaller guaranteed payoff by defecting regardless of the forecast. Existing treatments—whether sympathetic to causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, or some hybrid—tend to treat predictability as a single phenomenon. I want to investigate whether separating two modes of predictability changes the analysis: causal predictability, in which the predictor's signal influences the payoff mechanism, and logical correlation, in which the predictor and the agent share reasoning structures without any causal interaction between them. Specifically, I propose to examine a modified Luring Lottery with a non-binary payoff structure in which the predictor operates only through logical correlation. The central question is whether this separation dissolves the Platonia tension—allowing an agent to cooperate consistently with her duplicate without violating causal reasoning—or whether it merely relocates the problem into a new form.
