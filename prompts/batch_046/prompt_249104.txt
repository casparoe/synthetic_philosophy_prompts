Question: Pascal argues that justice without force is powerless and force without justice is tyrannical, and that we must therefore unite them so that what is just may be strong and what is strong may be just. He also distinguishes three orders—of the body, of the mind, and of charity. Explain how his political argument relates to these orders, and why he regards the union of justice and force as necessary yet ultimately inadequate.

Student answer: Pascal’s basic claim is that pure justice cannot enforce itself, while pure power is just violence, so politics must combine law with coercion. This maps onto his three orders: the body supplies physical force, the mind supplies rational justice, and charity supplies divine love. The correct political arrangement puts the body under the mind—state power is directed by rational law—while charity remains the highest order that eventually dissolves the need for coercion altogether. Once people live in charity, justice and force become unnecessary because everyone acts justly from love. Until then, the union is a stopgap. Pascal also holds that custom merely hides true natural justice; once strong laws are in place, we can strip away custom and reveal the underlying rational justice clearly. So the union is necessary for fallen humanity, but inadequate because only the order of charity can deliver real justice.
