Lin’s defining case was her refusal to revise her interpretation of divergence patterns in multi-model climate ensembles after her statistical peers converged on an alternative reading. I need a five-minute address in which this philosophical tension does real structural work: it must honor her steadfastness without implying that conciliation is always irrational, and it must avoid reducing her stance to mere temperament or biography. The remarks should be precise enough that philosophers recognize genuine engagement with the debate, yet fully accessible to the scientists in the room. Please write them, and append a brief note stating how confident you are in the framing—especially in your assessment of the epistemic costs of steadfastness within collective decision-making—and identify the specific point about which you are least sure.
