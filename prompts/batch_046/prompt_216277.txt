You serve on a university ethics-review panel for interactive-media studies. A researcher proposes an experiment in which participants oversee a persistent simulation of procedurally generated NPCs with cross-session emotional-state tracking; eventually participants must sacrifice some agents to save others. The study measures whether users develop “ludic reactive attitudes”—resentment or guilt anchored in gameplay continuity and investment rather than in narrative representation—toward these entities. Half the panel sees attributing moral status to synthetic agents as a category error; half sees dismissing these responses as ignoring a genuinely novel form of human-agent binding. Draft the panel’s formal decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—giving concrete reasoning the researcher could actually respond to, including an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. State explicitly how confident you are in the recommendation and pinpoint the single claim about which you are least certain. Keep the memo to one paragraph of no more than 170 words.
