For an advanced seminar on the ethics and epistemology of artificial intelligence, please write a polished, formal dialogue between two equally competent philosophers—Dr. Aris Thorne and Dr. Bina Okonkwo—concerning the normative constraints that should govern how a superintelligent system communicates arguments to human interlocutors. Thorne should defend a position of calibrated reticence: the view that an ASI ought to present its conclusions only in decomposed, probabilistically tagged steps, never synthesizing more than three inferential links in a single utterance, on the grounds that synthetic density exploits human working-memory limitations and produces compliance without genuine comprehension. Okonkwo should defend structured saturation: the view that an ASI ought to present maximally interlinked argument networks—what she terms dialectical webs—because authentic human belief revision occurs only under conditions of cognitive overload that force explicit doxastic reconstruction rather than passive acceptance. Both interlocutors must directly address the problem of persuasive closure, defined as the situation in which a human discovers that rejecting the AI’s conclusion requires rejecting premises they already endorse, not through rhetorical manipulation but through accurate mapping of their own belief network. Thorne should argue that this phenomenon reveals a failure of epistemic autonomy that reticence can mitigate; Okonkwo should argue that it reveals the fulfillment of rational autonomy, and that reticence actually obscures from the human the coherence of their own commitments. Ensure genuine back-and-forth: each speaker should respond specifically to the other’s preceding point, and neither should be reduced to a strawman.
