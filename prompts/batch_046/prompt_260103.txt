As a retired physicist recently drawn into moral philosophy, I hold the following views about standing to blame and hypocrisy, and I want to know if they form a stable package.

(1) Hypocrisy undermines standing only when the blamer's own fault matches the target's in both kind and severity; a minor failing should not block blame for grave wrong.

(2) Yet lacking the relevant opportunity or temptation—never having held power, for instance—excuses one from hypocrisy, so ordinary citizens may blame corrupt officials.

(3) Blame is fundamentally an expression of commitment to a shared norm, not a claim to personal moral superiority, so standing should not require being a moral exemplar.

(4) Still, if standing demands no baseline credibility whatsoever, blame risks collapsing into mere opinion-expression and losing its moral force.

(5) I also reject the claim that only the morally perfect have standing to blame anyone.

Please audit these commitments: identify the real tensions, rank them by severity, and say for each which view must yield. Note also any tension that dissolves on inspection. Finally, tell me what consideration would change your verdict—either about the package's coherence or about which commitment is weakest—and indicate where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
