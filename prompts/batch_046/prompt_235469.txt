I have been tracking the rulings of a philosopher-of-science commentator—active in rationalist forums—who issues binary verdicts on whether particular phenomena are genuinely emergent or fully reducible to lower-level dynamics. Her track record is consistent and explicit: she judges the boiling point of water and the spectral color of a specific dye molecule reducible to statistical mechanics and quantum chemistry respectively; she holds that phenomenal consciousness in mammals is genuinely emergent and not reducible; she treats the synchronized brooding cycles of periodical cicadas as reducible to evolutionary game dynamics among individuals; and she considers the market-clearing price in a simple double-auction reducible to the aggregation of individual bids. In every reducible case she denies downward causation from macro to micro, and in her sole emergent case she accepts downward mental-to-neural causation.

The new case concerns a recently completed large-scale emulation of a mammalian cortical column. Researchers can intervene either at the single-neuron level or at the macro-level of cognitive-state representations. Measurements show that macro-level interventions carry strictly more effective information about the system’s future dynamics than any micro-level intervention—there is causal emergence in the interventionist sense—yet the macro-dynamics supervene perfectly on the neural dynamics with no downward causation: inducing a cognitive-state representation never alters a neuron’s firing in a way that violates bottom-up physical law.

What verdict should she render if she wishes to remain consistent with her own precedents? Which earlier ruling is the closest analog? Lay out the two (or more) rules that could jointly explain her five prior verdicts—for instance, an ontological rule that requires downward causation versus an explanatory or interventionist rule that requires only macro-level predictive efficacy or causal effectiveness—state what each rule predicts about the brain-emulation case, give your best-guess verdict with reasons, and specify what principle she would be adopting with each choice if her precedents genuinely underdetermine it. Finally, describe one further hypothetical case that would definitively separate these rules.
