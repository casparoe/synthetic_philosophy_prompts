Consider a conceptual puzzle I've been turning over regarding masked dispositions and finked interferers. Give an example—physically plausible and not drawn from the standard textbook literature—in which an object has a fragility disposition that is masked by a protective coating, but the coating itself is finked: its protective properties would vanish precisely under the impact conditions that would otherwise trigger the fragility. In such a case, is the object genuinely fragile, genuinely protected, or modally indeterminate? And does the collapse of the mask/fink distinction here support a dispositional essentialist reading, or does it instead favor a purely counterfactual-conditional analysis? I'd like the example constructed with enough detail that one can assess whether there is a nearby possible world in which both the fink and the mask are absent, and whether that world is the right one for attributing the base disposition.
