Design a 70-minute seminar activity for 14 advanced students on whether honoring sunk costs is always irrational, using a five-round simulation called “The Research Program.” Divide participants into four lab groups of 3–4. In rounds 1–3 each group allocates ten tokens per round to Method A, receiving modest fixed returns and silently accumulating an invisible “continuity reputation.” At the start of round 4, announce that Method B is now available: it yields strictly higher per-token returns with no prior investment required, so pure state-rationality favors immediate abandonment. Each group votes secretly to stay or switch. In round 5, run a peer-collaboration phase in which groups form pairs to share a bonus pool; the payoff matrix reveals that switchers are penalized by partners exactly enough to cancel B’s efficiency gain, while stayers retain full collaborative access. Do not disclose this penalty beforehand; participants should discover it only when round-5 scores are revealed. Include explicit rules for token budgets, secret ballots, and the collaboration payoff matrix. After revelation, debrief by mapping: the round-4 vote onto the standard sunk-cost intuition; the hidden penalty onto the idea that rationality may be path-dependent rather than state-dependent; and the equal final scores onto the question of whether “irrational” continuity can be ex post rational. Predict two failures—one group deducing the hidden rule early and spoiling surprise, and participants conflating team loyalty with economic reasoning—and specify how to contain each without breaking the frame. Be frank that the simulation cannot reproduce the probabilistic, long-horizon nature of real-world consistency value, and instruct the facilitator to supply this verbally. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or named authors; let the mechanics carry the concept.
