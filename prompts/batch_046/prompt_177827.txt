Analyze the 2010 French law prohibiting the concealment of one's face in public spaces—most prominently applied to the niqab and burqa—through the lens of Rawlsian public reason, with particular attention to the official justification of vivre ensemble. Treat the law as a coercive political decision that, on Rawls's account, must be supported by reasons that citizens holding different reasonable comprehensive doctrines can endorse. First, map the parliamentary and public reasoning onto Rawls's distinction between political conceptions of justice and comprehensive doctrines: identify which elements of the vivre ensemble argument function as genuinely political values—such as minimal conditions for democratic reciprocity, transparency in civic interaction, or gender equality construed as a constitutional essential—and which elements smuggle in a particular secularist or republican cultural conception of the good. Next, apply Rawls's proviso: the claim that citizens may introduce arguments drawn from religious comprehensive doctrines into public discourse, provided they eventually supply adequate public reasons. Ask whether Muslim citizens who objected to the law on religious grounds could have satisfied the proviso in practice, or whether the structure of the debate made the proviso asymmetric—allowing secular republican arguments to count as already public while requiring religious objections to undergo translation that stripped them of their normative force. Finally, assess what the episode reveals about public reason itself: does it show that apparently neutral political values can carry thick cultural content invisible to their proponents; does it expose a blind spot in how the proviso handles minority comprehensive doctrines that reject the framing of the issue altogether; or does it suggest that public reason was never designed to adjudicate conflicts in which one side denies that the dispute belongs to the political domain at all? Be disciplined about the mapping—specify who counts as the public, what counts as a reason, and where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
