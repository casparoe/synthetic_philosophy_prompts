For a philosophy-of-language workshop I need to lay out my commitments on speech acts so the group can stress-test them. I accept Austin’s framework: locutionary, illocutionary, and perlocutionary acts are distinct; explicit performatives are assessed for felicity rather than truth; misfires happen when conventional or authority conditions fail, while abuses happen when the speaker is insincere, and the two errors are strictly different. I also accept Searle’s generalization that every utterance carries both propositional content and illocutionary force, so describing is itself a kind of doing. I think explicit performatives like “I promise” contain propositional content—the promised action—without being true or false; they have conditions of satisfaction instead. I hold that collective audience uptake and recognition are necessary conditions for any speech act to succeed—without them the speaker has merely expressed an intention. At the same time, I believe promises generate objective institutional obligations that bind even if every party forgets them or collectively denies their existence. I also maintain that when authority conditions fail—say, an unauthorized person pronounces a couple married—the result is not a misfired act but mere pretense with no institutional residue whatsoever. Finally, I think insincerity does not merely abuse a successful promise; it voids the act altogether, on a par with lacking authority.

Identify the single change that resolves the most conflicts.
