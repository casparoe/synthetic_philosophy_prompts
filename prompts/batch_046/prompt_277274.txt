The first one, Maya, argued that Carnap's rejection of metaphysical value-claims means political disagreements are either empirically resolvable (science handles the means) or just emotional expressions with no rational weight. So in her view, Carnap basically licenses expert planners to run the cognitive part — risk models, resource flows, etc. — while treating public values like aesthetic preferences that get aggregated but never genuinely argued over. Her friend Leo pushed back hard, saying that's a misreading: Carnap's principle of tolerence wasn't just about formal languages, it was about letting different frameworks coexist, and in politics that translates to democratic deliberation being the rational process itself, not just preference-polling. Maya came back sharper — she said even if deliberation picks frameworks, Carnap gives no rational way to choose between democratic socialism and authoritarian central planning at the meta-level, since both are ultimately expressions of attitude; so Leo's democratic commitment is just Carnap-style emotional preference dressed up as reason. Leo then said Maya was smuggling in a false separation: Carnap's own socialist activisim shows he didn't treat his values as mere feelings, and besides, politics involves coersion, which makes framework-choice non-optional in a way logical syntax never is. They asked me to referee since I was taking notes, but I honestly couldn't tell who was bending Carnap and who was extending him. Can you go through their exchange move by move — Maya's expert-planner claim, Leo's tolerence-to-democracy link, Maya's meta-level skepticism, and Leo's reply about activism and coercion — rate each from clearly wrong to clearly right, say who's missing what, and tell me if either of them actually captures how Carnap thought planning should work?
