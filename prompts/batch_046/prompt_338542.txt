I'm running a session for a reading group of smart non-historians on lost-cause myths. They think it's just romanticizing losers and that Masada counts but Dunkirk doesn't because nobody died. I want to teach the structural idea that these myths work by splitting the timeline: the moral victory gets locked in *before* the material defeat, so the defeat never actually touches the cause — Masada's suicide narrated as freedom rather than failure, Dunkirk's evacuation narrated as a moral triumph despite being strategic retreat. Plan the teaching arc: what dependancy has to be in place first so they see it's a narrative structure and not just patriotism, which paired exmaple should carry the whole thing and why using both together is necessary, the misconceptions they'll bring in order (probably "this only applies to real deaths" then "Dunkirk doesn't fit because they survived"), exactly where in the sequence to head each one off, one check question at the end that shows they've grasped the temporal split rather than just memorized the cases, and what historical detail about the actual battles to leave out so it doesn't turn into a history lecture.
