Exam q: Dennett's color-phi illusion is used to contrast Orwellian vs Stalinesque revisions of mental content. Explain both, apply them to what subjects report seeing (the mid-trajectory color switch), and say which revision, if either, lets us drop the Cartesian Theater idea. Also address whether either view implies the experienced "timing" of that switch is literally false.

Student answer:
In color phi you flash red at A then green at B, but people report one dot moving and changing color halfway. The Orwellian story is that the brain first records veridical red-then-green snapshots and then retroactively edits the record, like 1984 rewriting newspapers. The Stalinesque story builds the narrative in real time as stimuli come in, like a show trial—there's never a raw green experience sitting there to be corrected. Dennett clearly picks Stalinesque for phi because it avoids the Cartesian Theater: there's no central screen where experiences wait around to get rewritten. Still, this means experience is always delayed; the brain holds off on constructing the midpoint change until both dots have arrived, so the felt "when" of the switch is post-hoc and literally false compared to physical time.
