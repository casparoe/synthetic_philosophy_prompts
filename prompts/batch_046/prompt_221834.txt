Having spent thirty-five years in experimental condensed-matter physics before turning seriously to philosophy, I find myself preoccupied by a tension I rarely confronted in the laboratory: the bootstrapping problem, in which basic epistemic principles seem to require justification they cannot provide without circularity, and the problem of easy knowledge, in which we rationally accept complex scientific conclusions through testimony and institutional authority despite lacking personal access to the underlying data. I have begun to think that the cleanest microcosm of both issues is not an abstract skeptical scenario but the practical establishment of a primary measurement standard—specifically, the 2019 redefinition of the kelvin via a fixed value of the Boltzmann constant, where the constant is fixed by convention yet its physical realization depends on statistical-mechanical models that are themselves calibrated against thermal measurements, yielding a loop resolved not by linear derivation but by iterative coherence across independent experimental domains. If this metrological circularity is genuinely analogous to philosophical bootstrapping, then the philosophical task may be less about breaking the circle and more about specifying the conditions under which such circularity becomes epistemically virtuous rather than vicious, and about determining when accepting knowledge “the easy way”—through trusted calibration networks—is rational rather than intellectually negligent.

With that framing in mind, I want you to identify the open conceptual questions and lines of work in the philosophy of bootstrapping and easy knowledge that actually merit sustained effort, as opposed to those that are merely decorative refinements. Please present your answer as a numbered list with exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion; for each entry, defend its priority by indicating why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for conceptual rather than empirical research, and what kind of philosophical progress over the next few years would cause you to reorder it.
