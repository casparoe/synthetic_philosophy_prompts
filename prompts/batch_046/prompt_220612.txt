I've been playing with a specific reading of Berkeley's master argument that I think gets overlooked. On this reading, the argument isn't just "you can't conceive of an unperceived tree because conceiving is a mental act." Instead, it's that any determinate conception of an object requires specifying its sensory profile—color, shape, texture—and specifying that profile necessarily activates the very perceptual modalities that are mind-dependent. So when you try to think of a tree existing unperceived, you're either smuggling in a percievever who could specify the profile, or you're left with a bare "something exists" that isn't determinately a tree. I think this version is stronger than the standard one, but I want to know what actually threatens it.
