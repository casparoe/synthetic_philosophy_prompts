In Bayesian epistemology, the problem of old evidence asks how a theory can be confirmed by data the agent already knows, since Bayes’ rule gives a factor of one when P(E) ≈ 1. Classic responses include counterfactual conditioning on prior ignorance, bootstrapping via auxiliary assumptions, comparative confirmation against rival theories, and treating the evidential relation as mediated by probabilistic dependence. Less examined is the “mediated-archive” variant: an agent receives old measurements not through direct observation but through reconstructed records whose provenance carries second-order uncertainty, so the confirmation relation depends on beliefs about data integrity as well as on the theory itself.
