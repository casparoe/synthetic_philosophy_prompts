I serve on my university's Global Partnerships Ethics Committee. We must decide whether to approve a proposed ten-year joint research agreement with the Meridian Institute for Applied Science, a state-affiliated institution in Singapore with exceptional publication output and operational stability, but operating within an authoritarian framework that restricts political speech and directs funding through government performance targets. The partner insists on embedding a "Stability-Adjusted Output Protocol" in the contract: research priorities would be weighted by a novel metric combining scientific productivity with quantifiable social-order outcomes, explicitly framing sustained prosperity and civic order as legitimating conditions for governance and, by extension, for institutional collaboration.

Half the committee argues that rejecting the partnership on democratic grounds ignores demonstrable results, imposes an ideological litmus test, and harms our researchers; they contend that high capacity and material flourishing can ground legitimate authority even without democratic input, and that our collaboration should be guided by that performance legitimacy rather than by procedural ideals. The other half argues that treating prosperity and order as justifying conditions for authoritarian rule — and allowing them to structure our shared research agenda through the Protocol — commits the university to a normative framework it should reject, regardless of output.

Draft the committee's decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or approval with concrete conditions, including an actionable timeline and specific contractual safeguards if applicable. The reasoning should engage the performance-legitimacy argument at its strongest — that sustained order and flourishing can justify institutional authority absent democratic input — and explain precisely why that reasoning does or does not warrant this partnership in light of the Protocol. Include an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Please state how confident you are in the recommendation and identify the specific point about which you are least sure.
