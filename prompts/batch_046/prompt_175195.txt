At the micro-level, the plot seems to follow deterministic chaos: small perturbations (Nedry's security override, the storm) cascade unpredictably. Yet at the macro-level, the narrative treats collapse as structurally inevitable, regardless of which specific perturbation triggers it—suggesting a systemic determinism that chaos theory, strictly construed, does not license. I'm also interested in how the water-ripple demonstration conflates epistemic uncertainty (we cannot compute the path) with ontic openness (the path is not determined), and whether the film knows the difference or deliberately exploits the ambiguity.

Where does it distort the philosophical distinction between determinism and predictability? Does the resolution, in which the survivors are saved by an apparently contingent event (the T-Rex's intervention against the raptors), undermine Malcolm's deterministic warnings, or does it reinforce a different determinism—one in which chaos guarantees human failure even if the failure mode is unpredictable? And what does the film's visual rhetoric of containment and inevitable breach contribute to the debate that formal argument cannot?
