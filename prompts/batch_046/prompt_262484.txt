Ok so I've been reading around Buddhist anatta and it's overlap with personal identity debates, and honestly it feels like people have been talking past eachother for decades. On one side you've got philosophers treating anatta as basically a bundle-theory or reductionist claim about diachronic identity — no soul, just skandhas doing their thing. On the other side you've got scholars saying that's a category mistake: the Buddha wasn't answering a metaphysical question about persistence conditions, he was making a soteriological point about clinging to a controller or owner. Then there's this weirder third camp arguing that even if anatta is metaphysical, it's not about personal identity but about mereological essentialism — that "self" is denied because aggregates don't have essential parts, not because continuity fails. Anyway the debate never moves. Is this actually substantive disagreement about what's real, or is it verbal — people using "self" and "identity" for totally different jobs? Or are they answering different questions: one about ontology, one about psychology, one about ethics? And what's with the fact that almost nobody in the analytic literature seriously engages the Abhidharma distinction between conventional person (pudgala) and ultimate reality (paramartha) — is that the hidden formal question doing all the work? I want a diagnosis of why it's stalled, using what each side actually says, and tell me what progress would even look like here. Would it take an empirical result about folk-continuity intuitions, a formal result about bundle theories, or just a conceptual distinction that separates the soteriological claim from the metaphysical one? Or is the question ill-posed because Western philosophy forces Buddhist ideas into substance-metaphysics categories they never belonged in?
