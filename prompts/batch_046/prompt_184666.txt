Write a short story exploring the thermodynamic arrow of time through the following premise: in an isolated polar research station, a physicist named Elara maintains an archival device she calls a retrograde chronometer—a crystalline lattice engineered to stabilize in a sustained local entropy-minimum, allowing it to record thermal fluctuations in reverse chronological order. Over months, the device accumulates a coherent narrative of events that have not yet occurred from the station's perspective, including Elara's own future decisions. The story should center on her attempt to determine whether the arrow of time she experiences is a fundamental feature of the universe or merely a statistical bias of high-entropy observers like herself. Weave in the tension between the second law's statistical nature—where macroscopic entropy decrease is not forbidden but exponentially improbable—and the psychological arrow that causes her to interpret the crystal's accumulated patterns as predictions rather than recordings. Avoid conventional tropes such as time travel, aging reversal, parallel worlds, or faster-than-light signaling. Instead, focus on a single, quiet moment of decision: Elara must choose whether to destroy the lattice (preserving the unidirectional flow of her consciousness and the causal order she trusts) or to preserve it (accepting that her sense of causation might be as contingent as the crystal's orientation). The narrative should be subtle, technically informed without being didactic, and should leave the reader uncertain whether time points in one direction because the world demands it or because Elara's mind requires it to.
