Write a dialogue between a dialetheist and a defender of paraconsistent logic. The dispute concerns a housing cooperative whose charter contains Article 7, requiring unanimous consent for any amendment, and Article 8, permitting Article 7 itself to be amended by a two-thirds vote. After members approve a relaxation of Article 7 by seventy percent, the charter generates a direct contradiction: the amendment is valid under Article 8 and invalid under Article 7. The dialetheist should argue that this is not a mere appearance of conflict but a true contradiction in the cooperative's normative reality, and that accepting it is necessary to grasp the members' actual obligations. The paraconsistent logician should concede that the charter is genuinely inconsistent but deny that any contradiction is literally true; instead, she should show how a non-explosive consequence relation allows us to derive specific practical results—such as the failure of the relaxation—without the system collapsing into triviality. Require real back-and-forth in which each speaker responds directly to the other's specific claim, neither is treated as a strawman, and the exchange remains focused on whether the contradiction belongs to the institutional reality itself or only to our reasoning about it.
