Construct a philosophical argument defending the view that Rudolf Carnap's principle of linguistic tolerance—ordinarily applied to the selection of logical and linguistic frameworks—ought to be extended to the institutional architecture of democratic economic planning. Your argument should unfold in three stages. First, reconstruct Carnap's distinction between internal and external questions, explaining how it functions not only in ontology but in the design of normative institutions. Second, demonstrate that treating competing planning frameworks, such as market-coordinated and centrally-coordinated resource allocation, as analogous to alternative linguistic frameworks prevents the error of attributing metaphysical truth to any single model. Third, defend the claim that a planner working within a Carnapian framework should therefore confine state coordination to empirically testable predictions about resource flows and logistical outcomes, leaving value-laden ends to voluntary framework-choice by citizens rather than imposing a unified axiological system. In doing so, address the objection that planning without a shared value-foundation necessarily collapses into incoherence. Write in British English, maintaining a formal and polished register. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
