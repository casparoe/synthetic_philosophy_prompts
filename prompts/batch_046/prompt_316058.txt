ok so I'm trying to work out my own take on Kierkegaard and the media thing for a paper I'm maybe writing, and I want to check if my positions actually hang together before I embarass myself in front of people who actually read the journals. Here's where I've landed, more or less: (1) I buy the classic line that "the public" is a phantom abstraction that dissolves real ethical responsibility, mostly through that leveling dynamic where everyone becomes interchangeable. (2) I think the press is the primary engine of this, not because people consume news, but because it creates a structure where anyone can speak to everyone about everything without being anyone in particular. (3) At the same time, I think being the "single individual" requires refusing that kind of anonymity and standing behind your commitments in some direct, accountable way. (4) But then I turn around and think modern social media anonymity can actually protect the single individual from mass opinion, letting someone speak truth without the social cost of being recognized, so in a strange sense it preserves the very thing Kierkegaard wanted. (5) I also think Kierkegaard's real target wasn't anonymity per se but the velocity and scale of indirect communication, especially since he used pseudonyms himself to force readers to deal with ideas rather than personalities. (6) And I want to say the press isn't essentially corrupt, it's only bad when it replaces direct engagement with passive consumption of opinions, which maybe sounds like I'm just saving face. So: do these six cohere? Is (3) in direct tension with (4) since I'm praising anonymity in one breath and condemning it in another? Does (5) undermine (2) by excusing the press? Is (6) a cop-out? Rank the tensions by how bad they actually are, say what the cheapest single revision would be, and point out any pairs that look like they're fighting but really aren't once you unpack them.
