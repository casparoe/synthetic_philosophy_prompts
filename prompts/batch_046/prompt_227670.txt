Write a dialogue between two philosophers stuck at a weird interdisciplinary workshop—one is a Mingjia scholar who thinks Gongsun Long's white horse argument is actually about compositional failure in kind-terms, not color exclusion, and the other is an analytic metaphysician who thinks it collapses if you treat 'horse' as a phase-sortal like tadpole rather than a substance kind like gold. They should really engage: the Mingjia scholar should defend that 'white' introduces a competing kind-identifier that blocks the horse-predication, and the metaphysician should push back that this only works if you already assume sortals can't overlap, which she denies with biological examples. Have them work through one concrete case together—say a transparent ghost-horse with horse shape and behavor but no pigment and no tissue—so their disagreement about that case traces back to whether kind-membership is layered or exclusive. Neither should be a strawman, and don't have them list bibliographies or cite texts; just let them argue it out in real time over bad coffee.
