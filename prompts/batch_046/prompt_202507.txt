I am preparing an interview for a philosophy colloquium series. Next week's guest is a philosopher—let's call her Dr. Voss—who defends a position she terms integrated-loop sentientism: moral patienthood requires implementation of a globally recurrent predictive-error loop that binds representations across modalities into a single, temporally continuous self-model, and she argues that this creates a genuine architectural boundary rather than a gradient. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press on the actual structure of this view rather than asking her to restate it. I need at least one question on her rejection of gradientist alternatives, with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that continuous computational complexity should map onto continuous moral status; at least one on the degraded-loop problem—systems that implement the loop but with fragmented temporal integration or missing modality bindings—together with the follow-up she will probably offer about functional repair or partial equivalence; at least one on whether her view forces her to deny full moral status to certain human beings (for instance, those with severe split-brain conditions or specific neurodevelopmental architectures that disrupt cross-modal binding) and how she distinguishes those cases from non-sentient machines, anticipating her likely appeal to substrate-independent functional equivalence; and at least one question applying the view to an artificial system running the loop on distributed non-neural hardware with no evolutionary or biological history. The remaining questions should target the normative justification for selecting this particular computation—rather than phenomenal consciousness or biological life—as the boundary of moral concern, and should ask specifically how she responds to the objection that her criterion is both too permissive (allowing certain artificial loops) and too restrictive (excluding some human cognitive architectures). Keep the tone formal and precise; do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or further reading.
