I first heard it in an online discussion about whether quantum particles are genuinely individual objects or merely placeholders in a mathematical framework, where someone used the line to shut down questions about what an electron "really is" beneath its equations. More recently, a friend raised a case that stuck with me: if spacetime turns out discrete at some fundamental scale, does that mean the smooth continuous equations were describing a structure that never actually existed, or only that our model's structure was approximate? I'm not asking for a bibliography—just what that original sentence could actually be claiming.

Could you separate out the precise theses hiding in the slogan "only structure is real—the things themselves aren't"? I want to know which readings are near-platitudes about how science builds models, which are substantive metaphysical claims about relations existing without relata, and which are false or incoherent. In particular, does any defensible version entail that two theories with identical mathematical form but different "stuff" assigned to the variables describe literally the same world, not merely an empirically indistinguishable one? And does the discrete-spacetime scenario reveal that structure can fail in a deeper way than approximation, or is that a confusion? Please also say which of these readings is actually doing the work when people invoke the slogan to dismiss talk about intrinsic properties. If my framing rests on a false distinction—say, between structure and substance that structural realists reject, or if I'm conflating epistemic and ontic claims—say so plainly rather than answering inside my framing.
