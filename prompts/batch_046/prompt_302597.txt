You chair the institutional review board for a university biomedical facility that now regularly evaluates protocols involving neural-chimeric mammals—research animals with human-derived cortical grafts used to model neurodegeneration—where standard welfare indicators such as vocalization, withdrawal reflex, and social bonding become ambiguous because the graft tissue may alter behavioral outputs without altering underlying sentience. Write a reusable decision procedure your board can run on any proposal involving such subjects, grounded in Robert Nozick’s rights-based framework, especially his view that rights function as side-constraints that forbid treating beings merely as means regardless of aggregate welfare gains, and his analysis of whether non-rational beings fall within the scope of those constraints. Structure the procedure as an ordered sequence of questions: for each, specify what counts as a sufficient empirical answer, where an insufficient answer stops the review with a flat rejection, and where the board must switch from factual assessment to an explicit value judgment that the procedure cannot resolve. Include worked applications to three proposals: first, implanting human-derived seizure-prone tissue into a primate to test a novel anticonvulsant, with unclear post-surgical pain markers; second, maintaining a colony of chimeric rodents in enriched but permanently confined conditions for long-term cognitive observation with minimal physical intervention; and third, a protocol the procedure rejects despite promising substantial human therapeutic benefit—explain why the rejection holds and what change in reasoning or evidence would overturn it. State clearly what kinds of decisions this procedure cannot make, and flag its likely points of failure. Finally, say explicitly which argument or empirical finding about the moral status of graft-altered animals would lead you to abandon Nozick’s framework here, and identify the weakest step in your reasoning.
