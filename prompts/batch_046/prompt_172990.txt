I serve on the Regional Democratic Innovation Council, which advises our legislature on pilot franchise reforms. Our current case is the proposed Tri-Level Participation Protocol, combining three measures: compulsory attendance at local polling stations for citizens aged 18 and older, with blank ballots permitted and recorded separately as deliberative presence rather than abstention; binding voting rights for residents aged 16 and 17 in school-board and climate-budget referenda only; and voting rights for non-citizen residents of seven or more years in all local elections except those setting property-tax rates. The philosophical tension concerns whether democratic legitimacy derives from affected-interest representation—which favors including younger residents and long-term non-citizens—or from shared civic identity and reciprocal obligation, which favors compulsory presence but resists extending the franchise beyond citizens. An additional conceptual problem is that counting present non-voters as a distinct aggregation category may violate the principle of equal civic weight by introducing a presence-weight that does not correspond to preference revelation. Please draft our decision memo recommending adoption, rejection, or conditional adoption with a specific evaluation time frame; the memo must address both the inclusion-exclusion boundary and the blank-ballot aggregation issue, and it must engage seriously the strongest argument that the mechanism corrupts majority-rule logic rather than satisfying participatory duty. Present the memo as exactly five numbered paragraphs, each one or two sentences long, with no separate introduction or conclusion.
