I'm coordinating a three-year conceptual research group working at the intersection of philosophy of biology and evolutionary theory. Our shared problem is not whether Gould and Lewontin's spandrels critique is correct in broad strokes, but which unresolved conceptual issues about the adaptation-spandrel distinction actually obstruct progress in evolutionary explanation today. To avoid well-trodden survey territory, I want the agenda anchored in a framing I've begun calling functional decoupling under constraint: the possibility that a trait can be a spandrel with respect to one selective regime while functioning as an adaptation for a different regime that only becomes visible once an ancestral constraint is relaxed, such that binary classification collapses and the explanatory burden shifts to tracking regime-dependency rather than origin.

Given that framing, which open questions, methodological lines, or practical implications should dominate our limited effort? Please defend a ranked agenda of exactly three priorities. For each, specify: why it is upstream of the others in terms of logical or evidential dependency; how tractable it is for philosophers and theorists without direct laboratory or field access; and what specific conceptual clarification or result within the next three years would cause you to reorder it upward or downward. In particular, weigh whether the primary unfinished task is conceptual (refining non-optimality criteria for spandrel status), methodological (building comparative frameworks that can detect decoupling when ancestral constraints are unknown), or implicational (determining whether widespread spandrel status undermines predictive projects in evolutionary psychology, evolutionary medicine, or synthetic evolutionary robotics). Also identify one apparently important question—perhaps about the precise historical reception of the original critique, or about cataloging spandrels within a specific taxon—that you consider overrated for this group's purpose, and justify its deprioritization relative to the three you select. I am writing the group's charter and need a defended division of labor, not a survey or bibliography.
