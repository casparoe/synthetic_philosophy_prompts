Retired ecologist here, getting into philosphy of science lately. Theres a pattern in explanations that bugs me, and I wonder if it has its own name. You have some big phenomenon — say, an ant colony solving a maze, or consciousness arising from brain cells — and the explainer insists on building it from the bottom up, no magic, just parts and local interactions. But then they describe the parts using jobs or aims that only make sense if the big thing is already in place. The ant is said to read pheromone gradients in order to serve colony-level navigation, or a neuron is said to adjust its firing to preserve global brain coherence. The vocabulary is all cranes and local mechanics, yet the parts are being guided by the very tower they are supposed to construct. I know about Dennetts cranes and skyhooks, and greedy reductionism, but this feels different: it is not ignoring the design, it is hiding the design inside the bricks. Is there a specific concept for this kind of hidden top-down dependency in bottom-up stories? If not, can you coin a term, draw its boundaries, and say how it differs from ordinary reductionism and from just being a sloppy explanation? Also please say how confident you are, and flag the point you are least sure of. I dont need citations or a reading list — just your best take.
