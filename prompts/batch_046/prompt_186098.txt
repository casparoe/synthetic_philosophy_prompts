I'm presenting a thesis I'm calling "expressive complicity in lesser-evil elections" at a philosophy department colloquium. The audience is mostly liberal egalitarian political philosophers, two republican theorists for whom voting is tied to non-domination, an empirical political scientist who studies turnout, and a Kantian ethicist. My core claim is that when a voter faces one genuinely harmful major candidate and one merely inadequate alternative, abstaining or casting a protest vote is not morally neutral but expresses a false purity that obscures the voter's partial causal contribution to the worse outcome—provided they have adequate epistemic access to the comparative harm. I further argue that the vote retains expressive value only if it is publicly legible as blocking harm rather than endorsing the inadequate candidate's full platform, a condition I frame through a modified doctrine of double effect applied to collective action rather than to individual intent alone. I concede this makes voting partly strategic, but deny that strategic motivation cancels expressive meaning when the blocking intent is transparent.
