Quick one for my seminar prep. We were talking about how international development audits flag facilitation payments in Southeast Asian procurement as corruption, but they never flag a senator's fundraiser where donors buy face-time. What conception of corruption is actually driving that double standard? Is it just a universal public-office-for-private-gain rule, or is there a hidden normative framework about legitimate gifts versus bribes? I want the students to reconstruct the implict theory, test whether it can consistently condemn clientelist networks in Mediterranean patronage politics without also catching Western lobbying, and then say what an audit practice built on a genuinely contextualist view would have to meausre differently. Keep it concrete -- no bibliographies, I just want them to work out the commitments of the practice itself.
