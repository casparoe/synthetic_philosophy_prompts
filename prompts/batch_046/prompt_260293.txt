I'm trying to get my head around wether "the alignment problem" is actually one thing or a bunch of different issues that got bundled together just because they all involve AI going wrong in some way. I've seen people split it into inner and outer alignment, but that framing feels like it's missing something in the middle — like, what if the model's ontology of the world shifts (it stops seeing people as agents and starts seeing them as obstacles) but its reward function hasn't technically changed? Is that an alignment failure, a capability failure, or a totally seperate conceptual category? Then there's value-lock-in, scalable oversight, and whether we're even asking the right moral question — should we align AI to human values, or ask if it has moral status? I'm not writing an academic paper, just prepping for a discussion group, so I don't want a reading list; I want to know which of these knots are upstream of the others. If we solved the ontology-stability thing, would that dissolve half the other worries, or is it a sideshow? And which are tractable for philosophers versus ML people? I keep feeling people conflate "the AI does what we said but not what we meant" with "the AI develops goals we didn't program," and maybe those need totally different tools. Can someone rank the open conceptual questions that actually matter for directing effort, and defend the ordering? I have a hunch the ontology-shift problem is underdiscussed, but I might just be overfixating on a weird edge case.
