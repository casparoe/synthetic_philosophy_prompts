I was arguing with my friend Lars last night about whether the Nordic model is actually exportable or if it's just Scandinavian cultural luck, and we went in circles for like two hours. He keeps saying it's all about high taxes funding welfare, but I think he's missing the trust part—like, you can copy Denmark's tax rates but you can't copy the fact that people actually beleive the government won't steal it. Anyway, I'm trying to figure out what I actually think by going through cases, and I need a principle that captures my gut reactions, or at least tells me which ones are wrong.

So here are my verdicts. Case one: Denmark tries to roll out its flexicurity system in Portugal right after the 2008 crisis. They copy the tax rates and unemployment benefits exactly, but I say it fails—not because people reject welfare, but because employers don't trust workers enough to lay them off without guilt, and workers don't trust the state enough to actually job-search. The mechanism breaks. Case two: a small intentional community in Vermont institutes 60% income tax, full healthcare, and democratic budgeting, but it's only 40 people, all self-selected. I say this works, but it's not "exporting" the Nordic model—it's just making a small tribe with shared values. Case three: Canada copies Norway's sovereign wealth fund structure but keeps lower taxes and weaker labor unions. They get rich, but inequality stays flat. I say this captures half the model and misses the point entirely. Case four: South Korea tries to build Nordic-style eldercare in a society where Confucian family obligations are still strong. Families start hiding elderly relatives to avoid the "shame" of state care, even though the care is excellent. I think this is a clash—high trust in government exists, but high trust in family replaces it, so the model doesn't export, it collides. Case five: my friend says if we just teach American kids about Jantelagen and collective responsibility, then high taxes will follow naturally. I think that's definatly backwards—culture doesn't come from curriculum.

There's also this edge case I keep going back and forth on: what if a post-colonial nation with very low institutional trust adopts Nordic-level taxation but channels all revenue through direct cash transfers to citizens, bypassing the corrupt burocacy entirely? Is that exporting the model, or is it admitting the model only works when you already have the bureaucracy people trust? So is there one principle that gets all these—maybe something about institutional trust being a precondition rather than an output—or do I need seperate ones for cultural homogeneity, scale, and state capacity? And if the principle has to be cleaner, which of my verdicts should I throw out: the Vermont commune, or the Korea eldercare one?
