Please argue that the classical cyclical accounts of historical change—specifically Polybius's anacyclosis of constitutional forms, Ibn Khaldun's decay of asabiyyah across dynastic generations, and Spengler's morphological life-cycle of Kulturen—are not competing alternatives to linear-progress historiography, but rather describe a different temporal register of institutional dynamics. Your argument should distinguish between macro-cyclical patterns of collective solidarity and micro-linear accumulations of technical and normative capacity, and it should claim that Toynbee's challenge-and-response model erroneously externalizes what Ibn Khaldun treats as an endogenous parameter: the inevitable dissipation of group cohesion that accompanies civilizational expansion. Address the strongest counter-argument, namely that recognizing macro-cyclicality undermines the rationality of progressive political reform, and explain why this objection confuses predictive regularity at the structural level with normative futility at the agentive level.
