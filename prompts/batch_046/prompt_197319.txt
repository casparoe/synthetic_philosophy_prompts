I've been thinking about the bootstrapping problem and the so-called "easy knowledge" objection — you know, the worry that if perceptual knowledge is genuinely basic then you can too quickly derive conclusions about your eyes working, and that feels circular in a bad way. I think I've convinced myslef that the objection only sticks if you conflate two different moves: "source-validation" (using a faculty to check whether that very faculty is globally reliable) versus "domain-calibration" (using a faculty inside its proper scope to check local conditions, like using vision to notice your glasses are smudged). My position is that perceptual knowledge never attempts source-validation, so there's no circular bootstrapping at all; the appearance of "easy knowledge" just comes from misdescribing ordinary calibration as some kind of self-justification.
