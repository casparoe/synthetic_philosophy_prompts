I am a novelist drafting a scene centered on an unorthodox philosophy workshop, and I need you to design a single, runnable classroom game for it—no lecture outline, no menu of options. The setting is a two-hour session with fourteen graduate students, half in philosophy and half in physics, who have encountered basic panpsychism but have never confronted its ethical scaling problem in fundamental physics.

Your task is to construct a structured deliberation with hidden rules and small, real stakes—token allocations that determine a shared group outcome—so that participants physically experience the disorientation of applying panpsychist sentience across physical scales before anyone names the conclusion. Take a definite architectural position: build the activity as a "Resource Tribunal" in which four Scale Advocates—representing macroscopic organisms, mesoscopic neural networks, microscopic cellular automata, and quantum-field fluctuations—must persuade three Allocation Commissioners to distribute exactly one hundred "intervention units." The Commissioners should record their allocations in writing and lock them before receiving sealed Physics Auditor cards. These cards reveal a coherence-based or information-integration metric of minimal sentience distributed, under a linear scaling assumption, across all four domains. The revealed data should make it arithmetically unavoidable that, if infinitesimal valence tracks coherence time or integrated information density, the quantum-field domain carries orders of magnitude more total suffering-weight than the biological domains combined, rendering the Commissioners' intuitive clustering at the human and animal scale catastrophically misweighted.

Specify the roles in detail, the rules of persuasion and rebuttal, the minute-by-minute timeline, the physical materials needed, and the precise moment—no earlier than the seventy-minute mark—when the Auditor cards are opened. The rules themselves should generate the intuition failure: for instance, require Commissioners to justify each allocation with an empathetic narrative about the subjects they are saving, then force them to apply that same narrative logic to the quantum-scale data once revealed.

Then provide a debrief that maps each phase of the game onto a specific feature of the idea: the combination problem (how micro-experiences might compose), the problem of micro-subjects (whether coherence implies patienthood), and the aggregation problem (whether infinitesimal valences sum into morally urgent totals). Be frank about what the simulation necessarily distorts—particularly the ontological claim that quantum coherence itself carries phenomenal character rather than merely correlating with it—and supply the exact verbal correction the facilitator must provide. Do not present alternative architectures or ask me to choose between metrics; defend your specific choices of scale divisions, card content, resource number, and debrief framing with clear reasons.

Because this serves a novel that takes liberties with academic settings, include a brief, honest note identifying where your design relies on a contested reading of integrated information theory or panpsychist physics, and where it simplifies for dramatic effect.

Write in American English with a formal, polished tone. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, reading lists, or references that depend on web access. The deliverable should stand alone.
