I have been trying to work out for myself whether time genuinely passes or whether every moment—past, present, and future—exists in the same static way. I understand there is a well-known line of reasoning, associated with Rietdijk and Putnam in the 1960s, that uses the relativity of simultaneity to argue for a “block universe”: since different observers in motion disagree about which distant events are happening “now,” there can be no objective present, and therefore all times must be equally real. But I have also tried to construct an alternative picture. Suppose reality is not flat but layered by modal status: the past is fixed and fully actual, the future is genuinely open and not yet actual, and the present is the shifting boundary between them. On this view, different observers might locate that boundary at different sets of events without contradiction, because the boundary is not marking a universal “now” but rather each observer’s own causal access-point. The disagreement about simultaneity then concerns where the open region ends for each frame, not whether those events exist in some absolute sense. Write an essay examining this controversy. Weigh the Rietdijk-Putnam argument carefully against this layered-boundary alternative. Reach and justify a specific conclusion: does the argument genuinely rule out any form of temporal becoming, or can the layered view preserve both the reality of passage and the consistency of special relativity? Be precise about whether allowing frame-dependent present-boundaries commits us to contradictory claims about existence, or whether it merely shows that actuality can be indexed to a reference frame without collapsing into full-blown eternalism.
