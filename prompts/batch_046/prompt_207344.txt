Before I accept or reject that, I want to take the analogy seriously and see exactly what work it is doing. I'm especially interested in whether the analogy quietly smuggles in a design-specification view of human biology that evolution doesn't actually provide, and whether that hidden premise is carrying the whole argument. Once the disanalogies are on the table, does the commentator's conclusion — that the therapy-enhancement boundary is meaningless — survive? I want a committed, definite verdict rather than a balanced list of considerations, and if you think a different comparison — say, learning basic literacy versus being trained in speed-reading — captures the relationship between restoration and augmentation more honestly, defend that replacement and explain why it avoids the car analogy's pitfalls.
