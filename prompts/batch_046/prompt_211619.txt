Answer:

Molinas theory splits divine knowledge into three kinds. Natural knowledge covers necessary truths like logic and maths. Free knowledge is God knowing what actually happens in the real world. Middle knowledge is knowing counterfactuals of creaturely freedom — what people would freely do in different situations. This lets God arrange circumstances to get outcomes he wants without forcing anyone.

In the silent compiler case, the agent only makes a genuinely free choice if God puts her in that exact deliberative context, so God knows via middle knowledge that she would choose X there. This actually strengthens Molinism because it proves God can be certain of outcomes without causing them. The grounding objection, which Molina himself raised, asks what makes these counterfactuals true if God doesn't cause them. Molinists reply that the creature's own free will grounds the truth. Also, middle knowledge is logically prior to the creative decree, which is the correct ordering.
