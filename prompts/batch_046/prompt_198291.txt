Since joining the investment committee of a philanthropic foundation that funds governance reform in economies shaped by colonial extraction, I have realized that my intuitions about why some nations are rich and others poor are largely recycled narratives—geographic determinism, institutional exceptionalism, or cultural essentialism—none of which survive careful scrutiny when applied to specific grant decisions. Before I can responsibly allocate capital intended to foster prosperity, I need a rigorous analytical framework rather than a story.

Write the desiderata that any adequate explanatory theory of long-run, cross-national economic divergence must satisfy. Specify the phenomena it must account for—such as pre-industrial reversals of fortune, persistent within-country regional inequality despite uniform national institutions, and divergence among countries with similar geographic endowments—together with the distinctions it must preserve, for instance between institutional form and institutional function, or between geographic constraint and geographic destiny. For each requirement, provide the case or analytical failure that motivates it: why, for example, a theory that cannot generate a falsifiable counterfactual pattern is inadequate regardless of its narrative coherence; why it must remain consistent with well-documented epidemiological and demographic transitions without reducing them to mere epiphenomena of wealth; and why it must not treat culture as either fully exogenous or fully reducible to economic incentives.

Distinguish the non-negotiable criteria from those that are merely camp preferences—such as demands for a single monocausal factor, or for complete causal identification through historical instruments that are themselves endogenous to colonial extraction and prior economic conditions. Identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable: in particular, whether the demand for both universal applicability across deep historical time and rigorous counterfactual identification exceeds what historical inference can deliver. State clearly which single requirement, if applied without exception, eliminates the greatest number of prominent existing theories.

Finally, provide a brief, secondary assessment of how the geography-first, institutions-first, and culture-first frameworks perform against your list, but keep the emphasis on the criteria rather than on scoring the contenders.

Please respond in British English.
