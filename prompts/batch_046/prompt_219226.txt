Reading Kierkegaard on Abraham and noticing a pattern that might be it's own thing. He talks about the teleological suspension of the ethical - Abraham breaking the universal rule for a higher call. But I keep seeing people apply this to art or politics: they say they have to violate some norm for there project, and then when you ask them to justify it they say "if I could explain it to everyone, it wouldn't be the real vocation." So they treat the failure to universalize not as a problem but as proof they're in the Abraham position. Is there an established name for this second-level move - using incommunicability as a kind of credential? Or is it just a misapplication of the knight of faith? I'm not after the basic concept, I want the boundary: when does this reflexive suspension stop being legitimate and become ordinary arrogance, and has anyone mapped that distinction?
