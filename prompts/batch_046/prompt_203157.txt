Since I recently began archival work on my grandmother’s emigration and found that at least four independent conditions—local economic collapse, a coerced marriage arrangement, a sudden border closure, and a chance encounter with a ship’s agent—each appear sufficient to have produced the departure, I have become convinced that standard mono-causal historical narratives are structurally inadequate. I need, therefore, the full specification for an adequate theory of historical explanation that can genuinely accommodate overdetermination and multi-causal explanation rather than merely accommodating them rhetorically.

Write the desiderata such a theory must meet: the phenomena it must explain (including true causal redundancy, preemption masked as redundancy, and causal density across different scales), the distinctions it must preserve (for instance between overdetermination and causal preemption, between explanatory relevance and mere background conditions, and between having sufficient cause and being the cause selected for narrative purposes), the consistency conditions it must satisfy with respect to philosophy-of-science results on causal selection and counterfactual dependence, and the cases it must get right—including my family case, macro-level events like the outbreak of wars with multiple triggering conditions, and micro-level biographical turning points. For each desideratum, provide the motivating consideration, and flag which are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences (such as demands for law-like generality, demands for narrative coherence, or demands for mechanism-level granularity). In particular, identify whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—say, if full explanatory completeness conflicts with unavoidable causal selection, or if determinism about macro-trends conflicts with agent-level overdetermination—and if so, present that tension as a result rather than resolving it by fiat. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; then, briefly, score how the covering-law model, narrative explanation, causal-mechanism accounts, and structural-historical approaches fare against the list, but do not let the scoring dominate. I want the conceptual work to lie in the requirements themselves, especially in articulating notions like explanatory saturation points and counterfactual fragility thresholds that a serious theory must be able to deploy.
