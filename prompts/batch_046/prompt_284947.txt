Write the full desiderata that any normative balance-sheet framework for evaluating imperial legacies—specifically applied to the British Raj—must satisfy if it is to do more than rehearse the standard Ferguson-versus-Tharoor deadlock. The framework must specify: (1) the counterfactual baseline rules for attributing economic or institutional outcomes to imperial policy rather than to pre-existing or autonomous trajectories (the counterfactual-sovereignty problem); (2) the moral-distinction rules for weighing goods delivered under coercive conditions against goods that would have emerged without domination (the moral-discounting problem); (3) the causal-attribution criteria for separating colonial infrastructure investments from extraction and from post-colonial institutional residues; (4) the intertemporal aggregation rules for comparing short-term extraction with long-term institutional effects; and (5) the normative weighting scheme that decides which goods (life expectancy, literacy, self-governance capacity, cultural autonomy) are incommensurable and which can be traded off. For each desideratum, give the concrete case or argument that makes it a genuine requirement rather than a partisan preference, and flag any pair that appears jointly unsatisfiable—especially if resolving counterfactual sovereignty in one direction forces moral discounting in the opposite direction. Then, briefly, show how a triumphalist account and an anti-imperial account each violate at least two of these criteria, but do so without citing specific texts, page numbers, or bibliographic sources. Keep the emphasis on the criteria themselves and on whether the list is internally consistent.
