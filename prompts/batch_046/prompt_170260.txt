I am a student in an undergraduate philosophy of logic course. We recently read Lewis Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise on the justification of modus ponens. In seminar, one student compared the Tortoise's infinite regress—demanding that each logical rule be justified by a further rule—to the old cosmological anecdote of "turtles all the way down," in which the earth is said to rest on turtles stacked infinitely downward. She argued that, just as the cosmological stack has no ultimate foundation and must therefore be accepted arbitrarily or rejected altogether, deduction lacks a non-circular foundation and is ultimately conventional. Please audit this analogy: provide an explicit correspondence table (the earth, the turtles, the missing bottom, the interlocutor asking what holds it up, and their analogues in Carroll's case), then identify where the mapping breaks—especially regarding whether logical rules function like physical supports or like constitutive norms of reasoning—separate the disanalogies that matter for the conventionalist conclusion from those that do not, and assess whether that conclusion survives once the breaks are visible. If the analogy collapses, suggest a more apt comparison for how logical rules stand in need of justification.
