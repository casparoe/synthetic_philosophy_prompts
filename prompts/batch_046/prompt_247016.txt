For a reading group I’ve been asked to write down my philosophical profile on demarcation and falsificationism so the others can poke at it. Here it is: (1) I accept Popper’s core insight that scientific status turns on exposure to empirical refutation, but I deny that falsifiability is a necessary condition, since some scientific mathematics and historical claims are not directly falsifiable. (2) I endorse Lakatos’s research programs with protective belts, yet I also hold that any program that has introduced three successive ad hoc adjustments without novel predictions should be treated as degenerating, regardless of its remaining explanatory scope. (3) I think string theory counts as science despite currently lacking empirical predictions, because it maintains rigorous internal consistency and has yielded unexpected mathematical structures with empirical spillover; but I want to deny scientific status to psychoanalytic frameworks that similarly lack direct predictions and invoke post hoc explanations. (4) I believe the demarcation problem is ultimately normative—about which disciplines deserve institutional credibility—rather than a purely logical taxonomy; nevertheless I want to say that astrology is not science in a robust, non-conventional sense. Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how severely. For each tension, state whether it is real or only apparent, what the cheapest fix would be (giving up one claim, weakening it, or introducing a distinction), and what that fix costs me elsewhere in the cluster. Then tell me which single revision—dropping or modifying one commitment—would clean up the most tension overall, and whether that revision makes the cluster collapse into a standard position I was trying to avoid.
