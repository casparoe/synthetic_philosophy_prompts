I serve on the grant review committee of the Meridian Interdisciplinary Foundation, and we must reach a decision on a five-year proposal for a program in “reflexive predictive modeling.” The applicants explicitly reject Popperian falsifiability as the demarcation criterion between science and non-science. In its place they advance a methodology they call retrospective coherence calibration: the model issues predictions, but predictive failures are not treated as refutations. Instead, internal parameters are adjusted post hoc to restore coherence with observed data, and the revised model is evaluated not by further risky predictions but by its narrative integration with prior theoretical commitments. The principal investigator argues that this mirrors actual practice in mature sciences—where core theories routinely survive anomalous data through auxiliary adjustments—and that demanding bold, risky predictions is appropriate only to immature, pre-paradigmatic fields.

The committee is split. One faction holds that rejecting the proposal would enforce a descriptively inadequate Popperian orthodoxy and risk excluding genuinely valuable research; the other faction holds that without exposure to empirical refutation, the program collapses into unfalsifiable storytelling and should be funded, if at all, under humanities rather than science.

Please draft a formal recommendation memo addressed to the full committee. State a clear bottom-line decision—whether to fund the program as scientific research, reject it outright, or approve it only under specified modified conditions—together with explicit, actionable criteria that reviewers could apply to analogous proposals. The memo should include an honest and substantive treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, particularly the claim that falsificationism misdescribes scientific practice and that retrospective calibration captures real reasoning patterns in established disciplines. Do not appeal to bibliographic authority, request further literature review, or ask the committee to consult specific texts; the recommendation should stand on conceptual and procedural grounds alone.
