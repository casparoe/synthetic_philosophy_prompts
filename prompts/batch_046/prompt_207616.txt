Below are notes for a brief essay. I want to move past the standard framing of the Historikerstreit as a simple clash between uniqueness and comparability, and instead examine a tension internal to Bauman's argument in Modernity and the Holocaust. Bauman treats the Holocaust as both the fulfillment of modern bureaucratic rationality—which ought to make it structurally comparable to other modern administrative projects of categorical exclusion—and as a singular "laboratory" that exposes modernity's hidden potential, which seems to resist comparison altogether. Rather than reading this as a contradiction, I propose distinguishing modal uniqueness (the event reveals what modern rationality is capable of producing) from taxonomic uniqueness (it belongs to a class of events incomparable with any other). On this account, comparing the Holocaust to other genocides is the wrong axis; the illuminating comparison is with modern systems of exclusion and discipline that stop short of genocide, since that shows modern rationality is necessary but not sufficient for the outcome. I would welcome comments on whether this distinction genuinely resolves the tension in Bauman's account, whether it misrepresents Habermas's or Nolte's contributions to the debate, and whether the argument amounts to more than a redescription.
