Write an essay about whether everyday conventions like tipping, queueing, and jaywalking are real moral obligations, and whether visitors have to follow local ones even if they come from places with totally different norms. I've been thinking about what I call shadow obligatons — rules that don't hurt anyone directly if you break them, but they keep a bigger social machine running. Like jaywalking when there's no cars around doesn't hurt anyone right then, but if everyone did it the whole traffic system gets unpredictable and people eventualy get hurt. Or skipping a queue at a coffee shop isn't theft, but it breaks the trust that makes lines work at all. So are these shadow obligatons actual moral duties? And if you're just a tourist passing through who never signed up for the local system, are you still on the hook? Weigh the arguments and definately reach a conclusion — don't just sit on the fence saying it depends without picking a side. I'm not a philosopher or anything, just been thinking about this on my own.
