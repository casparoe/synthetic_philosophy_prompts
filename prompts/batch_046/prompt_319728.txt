After a long, inconclusive argument with a friend about a recent biopic that invented a deathbed reconciliation for a well-documented public figure, I want the full specification for what an adequate normative theory of our obligations to the real dead must deliver when they are dramatized in historical fiction or film. I am not asking for a bibliography or a list of authors; I want the desiderata themselves.

Write the list of requirements such a theory must meet to count as adequate, with the motivating case or distinction behind each. The theory must preserve the distinction between obligations owed directly to the dead—if such exist—and obligations owed to living descendants, to the integrity of the historical record, and to the audience’s epistemic position. It must handle cases where invented private dialogue captures the emotional logic of documented relationships but contradicts the dead person’s known ethical commitments, as well as cases where temporal distance removes all living survivors. It must address whether the dead can hold “narrative interests” or “posthumous dignity,” whether explicit instructions left by the deceased about representation function as binding constraints or merely advisory preferences, and whether there is a meaningful difference between misrepresenting public actions and inventing private mental states.

For each requirement, mark whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference (for example, of an autonomy-centered view versus a public-memory view versus an artistic-license view). Then flag any subset of requirements you take to be jointly unsatisfiable—especially whether respecting a dead person’s narrative autonomy can be reconciled with both the audience’s interest in historical understanding and the creator’s artistic freedom. Briefly indicate which single requirement does the most to exclude popular candidates.

Finally, score three broad approaches against the list: a strict archival-fidelity view, a survivor-centered harm view, and a narrative-autonomy view that treats posthumous dignity as a surviving interest.

Present the core of your answer as structured data: a table with columns [Requirement, Motivating Case / Distinction, Status (Non-negotiable / Camp Preference / Negotiable), Joint-Satisfiability Note, Archival-Fidelity Score, Survivor-Harm Score, Narrative-Autonomy Score]. After the table, provide one brief paragraph summarizing whether the list reveals an unsatisfiable cluster and which requirement is doing the heaviest exclusionary work. Keep the focus on the criteria rather than on extended defense of the three approaches.
