Write a two-page fellowship proposal (six-month scope) on whether republican liberty can accommodate "latent curation" without collapsing into positive liberty. Define the problem: an agent suffers no direct interference and retains formal self-mastery, yet every available option has been pre-structured so that any choice advances a curator's interests—think algorithmic recommendation architectures or tiered credit markets that never issue explicit commands. Ask precisely whether this counts as domination (arbitrary power over the agent) on republican grounds, or whether recognizing it forces republican theory to evaluate whether preferences are authentic, thereby absorbing the self-mastery commitments that republican freedom was meant to avoid. Explain why Berlin's negative/positive distinction and the standard non-domination literature leave this gap open. Propose a conceptual method that distinguishes curation from interference on one side and from preference-formation on the other, apply it to two institutional cases, state what a thin republican result looks like, and state what would make the project fail—e.g., if curation turns out reducible to hidden interference or if evaluating authenticity proves unavoidable. Do not request a bibliography, citations, or reading list; frame everything in terms of arguments and distinctions.
