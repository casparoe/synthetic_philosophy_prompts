Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a psi-ontic camp, which holds that the quantum state is part of physical reality, and a psi-epistemic camp, which holds that it represents knowledge or belief about an underlying reality. Structure the document as follows: (1) numbered agreed claims about the quantum formalism and about what any interpretation must reproduce; (2) a disagreements table in which each side’s position is phrased in wording it accepts; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party; and (4) for each crux, what both sides agree counts as relevant evidence or argument, with each side’s prediction or interpretation of that evidence recorded in advance.

In particular, address the ontological-models result that psi-epistemic accounts reproducing quantum predictions typically violate preparation independence: do both sides treat this as a physical constraint on nature, or does one side read it as an artifact of how epistemic access is modeled? Also address protective measurements: do the camps agree on what such measurements establish about the state being protected, and if the same experimental protocol is interpreted differently, state precisely where the divergence lies—whether in the ontology assigned to the protected state, in the meaning of measurement, or in the inference from statistical outcomes to reality.

Finally, identify any portion of the dispute that is verbal rather than substantive—specifically, whether disagreement over whether the wavefunction is real collapses into disagreement over whether reality must be directly observable or whether it need only figure in the best explanatory ontology—and specify which parts remain genuinely open.
