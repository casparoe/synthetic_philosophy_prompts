For my seminar on moral responsibility, I am trying to map the logical space of views about whether hypocrisy undermines standing to blame, not merely memorize labels. Please construct a decision tree that sorts positions by answers to concrete branch questions, each phrased so that a respondent can answer without already knowing which view it leads to. The first node should ask whether a blamer’s own commission of wrong X ever diminishes their standing to blame another for X. From there, branch on whether the effect is total defeat or only partial attenuation; then on whether the undermining hypocrisy must be actual, or whether a counterfactual disposition to commit X in relevantly similar circumstances is sufficient; and finally on whether the mechanism is normative (grounded in fairness or equality conditions) or epistemic (grounded in credibility or authority deficits). The leaves must include at least: a view on which standing is entirely independent of the blamer’s moral record; a view on which actual same-type hypocrisy produces total defeat via a fairness condition; a view on which counterfactual disposition produces partial attenuation via a credibility deficit; and any other combinations that are genuinely occupied, with unoccupied nodes marked and briefly diagnosed as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Give each leaf a descriptive name and, in one sentence, its most awkward commitment. Also flag any pair of leaves that your particular tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining exactly which branch questions fail to distinguish them. Write this as a formal classification instrument suitable for a handout, and do not ask me to consult specific texts, citation lists, or named authors I would not have in front of me.
