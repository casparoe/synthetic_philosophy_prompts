Write a sestina on the paradox of tolerance, framed specifically as the institutional dilemma of militant democracy — the constitutional problem of whether a democratic order may legitimately restrict the rights of groups committed to destroying democratic rights, and whether doing so collapses the distinction between defender and destroyer. Use exactly these six end-words, appearing in this order in the first stanza: TOLERATE, BOUNDARY, INTOLERANT, SPEECH, SILENCE, COLLAPSE. Adhere to the classical sestina permutation (1-2-3-4-5-6; 6-1-5-2-4-3; etc.) through all six stanzas, and conclude with an envoi that contains all six words, with TOLERATE and INTOLERANT paired in the same line and BOUNDARY adjacent to COLLAPSE. The formal repetition should perform the paradox rather than illustrate it: as the end-words migrate, TOLERATE and INTOLERANT should never settle into a stable grammatical relation, and the envoi should leave ambiguous whether BOUNDARY still separates them or has already been absorbed into COLLAPSE. A specialist should be able to identify which lines enact first-order tolerance (withholding suppression) and which enact second-order tolerance (protecting the intolerant), and the poem should stand on its own for a reader who knows nothing of the debate.
