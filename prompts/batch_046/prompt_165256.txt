I am applying for a six-month interdisciplinary fellowship and need a two-page proposal for a dissertation chapter. My half-formed idea concerns mind uploading achieved through gradual neural or functional-module replacement—replacing one subsystem at a time over years rather than in a single transfer event. The standard debate treats this as a variant of the personal-identity problem, asking whether psychological continuity survives and whether duplication occurs at the endpoint. I suspect this framing misses something specific to the incremental structure itself: that each replacement stage may leave the successor system in a state of structural dependency on the original design parameters, creating normative and epistemic obligations that do not reduce to continuity of consciousness, memory, or character. I call this provisional relation architectural indebtedness, though I do not yet have a precise account. I need the proposal to contain: a sharply formulated research question—does incremental substrate replacement generate a non-identity-based dependency relation between predecessor and successor, and if so, is it normative, epistemic, or both?; an explanation of why existing continuity-based and duplication-based frameworks leave this question open rather than resolving it negatively; a month-by-month method specifying the conceptual analysis, the construction of formal dependency-tracking models across replacement stages, and the evaluation of thought experiments designed to isolate indebtedness from psychological similarity, with explicit justification for this mixed-method approach over pure conceptual analysis or empirical neuroscience; a clear statement of what success would look like (e.g., a taxonomy of dependency types that survive full substrate retirement) and what failure would look like (e.g., demonstrating that all apparent dependencies collapse into standard psychological-continuity conditions); and a realistic scope assessment arguing why six months suffices for this stage and identifying what must be deferred to subsequent work. The tone should be formal and suitable for a philosophy-of-mind and cognitive-science review panel. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests; I will add references myself.
