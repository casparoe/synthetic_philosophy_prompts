me and my friend were arguing til late about whether pain is bad because of the feeling itself or just because we cant help hating it, and he brought up pain asymbolia — people who feel a burn or a cut perfectly clearly but dont suffer from it at all. it completely messed with my idea that badness is built into the sensation, and now i cant stop thinking about the gap between knowing youre hurt and minding that you are.

could you write a poem for me in british english that lives inside that gap? i want four quatrains, sixteen lines, no fixed rhyme scheme except that the sensory words should rhyme with each other (sharp, burn, turn, learn) while the words about care or alarm stay unrhymed and sit alone at the ends of lines. start each quatrain with a version of the same line that slowly changes: the signal comes clear and sharp, then the signal comes clear and sharp but the alarm never sounds, then ...but the alarm sounds hollow, then ...but the alarm sounds in someone else. let the form do the philosophical work — the rhyme holding the body together, the unrhymed lines showing where the suffering should be but isnt. make it feel like the speaker is describing their own body as a stranger reports weather. dont cite anyone or list sources, and please dont turn it into a sermon about whether doctors should treat them. just keep the tension open between the intact feeling and the missing badness.
