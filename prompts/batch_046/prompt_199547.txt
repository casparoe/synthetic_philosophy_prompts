I'm a doctoral student working in epistemic logic, and I'm trying to understand why Fitch's paradox is treated as a direct challenge to the universal knowability principle rather than to our logic of self-knowledge. The derivation shows that if every truth is knowable, then for any unknown p we can derive a contradiction by knowing the conjunction (p ∧ ¬Kp). But this contradiction only arises if we assume that knowledge distributes over conjunction together with factivity. What puzzles me is that commentators present the result as forcing us to reject ◊Kφ for all φ, when it looks to me like the real pressure point is a hidden assumption about the luminosity of ignorance—specifically, that if ¬Kp then it is impossible to know ¬Kp. If we instead allow negative introspection, so that an agent can genuinely know her own failure to know p, then knowing (p ∧ ¬Kp) need not collapse into a contradiction, because the knowledge that p and the knowledge that ¬Kp can be indexed to different epistemic perspectives rather than being jointly attributed in a single frame. So why isn't the standard response to Fitch's result to refine or strengthen our logic of self-knowledge, rather than to weaken the knowability thesis itself?
