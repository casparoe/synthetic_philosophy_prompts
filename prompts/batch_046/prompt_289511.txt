For a workshop on practical rationality and self-modification, construct a decision tree that classifies positions on wireheading and reward tampering. Use three splitting commitments: (1) Is direct neural stimulation of reward centers rational if it maximizes experienced welfare regardless of external achievement? (2) Is restructuring one's own goals so that they are guaranteed satisfied—without direct stimulation—rationally or morally distinct from direct wireheading? (3) If an agent authored its own reward function rather than inheriting it biologically or socially, does altering that function still count as self-defeating?

Label each branch by the specific claim that divides it. At every leaf, provide the position's common name (or mark it 'unnamed') and exactly one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Aim for 6–8 leaves. Map out the full logical space of answer combinations, marking any unoccupied branches and briefly diagnosing whether they are empty because incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of positions the tree cannot cleanly separate. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
