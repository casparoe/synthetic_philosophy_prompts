hey, so I retired from a physics lab last year (mostly low-temperature magnetism) and now im trying to read philosophy for fun, but I keep mixing up metaphors. I wrote this little piece about whether Bostroms simulation argument should dampen all the fanatacism about the far future. The usual story is that if were probably in a sim, then the distant real future might be fake or unreachable, so longtermists should chill out. But I think theres a hidden loop that people miss. If the simulators are likely our descendants — which Bostrom allows — then big longtermist projects arent aimed at some abstract distant world; theyre attempts to feed causal inputs back into the system that runs our present reality. I call this the ancestor-loop pressure. It means simulation uncertainty might actually raise the moral stakes, not lower them, because your actions could be reshaping the conditions of your own creators. At the same time, if you believe youre simulated, your reasoning about the far future might just be part of the simulations script, so epistemic humility cuts both ways: you shouldnt be fanatical, but not because the future is unreal — rather because your belief in it might be manufactured.
