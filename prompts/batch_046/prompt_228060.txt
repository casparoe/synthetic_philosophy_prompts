I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. I've been working through the debate about whether qualia are conceptually incoherent — not merely mysterious or reducible, but malformed in the way a category error is malformed. This includes Dennett's strategy of quining, dissolving intuitions about subjective experience by showing they depend on ambiguous descriptions, and the stronger claim associated with Chase and Sanborn that positing qualia involves something like treating an abstract relation as if it had intrinsic, non-functional properties.

From my engineering perspective, the analogy is to asking whether a running process has an unobservable inner state that is not captured by its functional specification — a kind of private variable that no debugger could ever access. The question is whether that analogy reveals a genuine conceptual problem or just a failure of imagination about computation.

Rather than a survey, I want a defended prioritization: if I have about two years of part-time study and want to understand whether the incoherence claim holds up and what depends on it, which open questions or lines of work in this area actually deserve attention? In particular, please rank and defend the importance of the following kinds of issues, and add any you think are upstream.

First, is the primary task conceptual clarification — refining the argument that what-it-is-like talk commits a category mistake comparable to attributing spatial properties to a logical type — or is it more important to defend a minimal realist account that survives quining?

Second, does the coherence of qualia turn on whether functionally identical artificial systems would share phenomenal states, and is that an empirical question about architecture or a conceptual question about duplication?

Third, do intermediate positions — relational, structuralist, or representationalist accounts of phenomenal character — represent a productive research program, or do they inherit the incoherence problems of full-blooded realism while surrendering its explanatory advantages?

Please order these by importance for someone with my background: tractability without graduate training in phenomenology, whether resolving the question changes downstream debates about consciousness and AI, and whether the answer depends on empirical results we do not yet have versus pure conceptual analysis. Identify the two or three questions that deserve my attention, which ones are overrated distractions, and what kind of argument or evidence over the next few years would change your ranking. I am not looking for a bibliography or reading list.
