One curator proposes displaying a bucket of water spinning in an otherwise empty universe; she argues that the water's surface should curve upward, since rotation is an absolute motion through space itself and needs no external reference. A second curator objects that without other bodies to relate to, "spinning" is meaningless, so the surface should stay flat, and any real effect requires distant objects like stars. To test the disagreement, I suggested a variant: two identical buckets alone in the void, rotating about their common center. The first curator predicts both surfaces curve; the second predicts neither does, since they only move relative to each other. I need to understand the underlying philosophical dispute—not just the physics—to design the exhibit label. Which reasoning captures the real issue, and what does the two-bucket case reveal about it? In your response, please say how confident you are in your analysis and identify the specific point about which you are least certain.
