I am working out a theoretical position I call bifocal moral objectivity, intended to navigate the well-documented empirical divergence between WEIRD and many non-WEIRD populations in folk moral cognition—specifically the differential weighting of harm and fairness relative to loyalty, authority, and purity—without surrendering either to evolutionary debunking arguments or to an unqualified cultural relativism that forfeits cross-cultural normative criticism. Before I defend the framework, I need the full specification of what such a theory must satisfy to count as conceptually adequate, not merely descriptively adequate. Provide the desiderata: the empirical patterns in comparative moral psychology it must accommodate without distortion; the normative distinctions it must preserve, such as that between the causal explanation of a moral intuition and its normative justification, and that between conventional social rules and genuinely binding moral constraints; the philosophical results it must remain consistent with, including whatever stance on moral realism, constructivism, and epistemic modesty is required by its own architecture; and the boundary cases or stress-test scenarios—both real-world institutional conflicts and hypothetical moral dilemmas—that would cause it to collapse. For each desideratum, state whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp, with the motivating case or consideration behind that judgment. Then identify which single requirement does the most damage to the dominant existing alternatives—whether domain-general rationalist reconstructions of morality, strong cultural relativisms, or evolutionary debunking accounts—and briefly indicate why that alternative fails precisely there. The deliverable should be a structured requirements list with brief justifications; a table is acceptable if it clarifies the logical structure. This is conceptual groundwork for ethical theory construction, so do not provide a bibliography, a reading list, or citations to specific ethnographic studies; the framework is being built from the ground up and must stand on conceptual coherence alone.
