I'm taking a philosophy of mind module at uni this term, and for our seminar I had to write a short reflective piece on whether we ought to take Dennett's intentional stance towards AI systems. Last night I was arguing with my flatmate about it — he reckons it's dead obvious that chatbots don't have beliefs, since inside there's just weights shoving numbers about and no real understanding. I tried to say that the stance isn't about what's inside, it's about predictive usefulness, but he said that just proves his point because with a chatbot you can't actually predict anything except word patterns. Anyway, I sketched out this little essay and I'm not sure the central distinction is sharp enough.

What I say is that with people, adopting the intentional stance lets us navigate the world: if I think you believe the kettle is broken and you want tea, I can predict you'll go to the cafe. With an LLM, though, the only behaviour is text, so the stance seems to do different work. I notice this weird asymetry: when a model outputs 'I have no feelings' or 'I don't experience colour', attributing a belief there feels stable — we can predict it'll deny consciousness consistently. But if I try to attribute a belief about, say, the date of the Magna Carta, the answer might be correct or hallucinated in ways that don't track any internal state at all. So I suggest there's a kind of 'mirror-stance' that works for self-descriptive outputs but collapses for external-world claims, and I wonder whether that means we're not really using Dennett's framework but just reading off training distributions. I also mention that smaller LLMs are even more brittle, so the stance probably doesn't apply to them in any useful way.

I'd really like some comments on whether this carving makes sense, or if I'm just redescribing the fact that models are unreliable. Is there an established term for this selective applicability? I don't want a bibliography or list of readings — I just want to know if the argument holds water and where the edges are. Sorry for any typos; I'm writing this on my phone on the bus.
