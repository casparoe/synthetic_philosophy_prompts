Exam question: Some philosophers defend a gradation thesis: sentience and its associated moral status vary continuously rather than being all-or-nothing. Examine whether this thesis can consistently accommodate insects and thermostats without collapsing into either full moral equality across all information-integrating systems or an arbitrary cutoff that reinstates a hidden binary. In your answer, distinguish phenomenal integration from mere causal feedback, address whether continuity genuinely precludes qualitative thresholds, and consider how a principle of equal consideration applies—if at all—to beings of radically different capacities.

Student answer: The gradation thesis holds that moral status tracks continuous differences in phenomenological integration rather than membership in a biological kind. This avoids the binary trap: we do not need a bright line separating sentient from non-sentient beings, only a calibrated scale. Insects, for instance, possess nociceptive pathways and some form of valenced experience, but they lack the recursive self-modeling that amplifies moral weight in humans. A thermostat, by contrast, processes feedback loops that integrate information about temperature, yet these loops are purely causal, not phenomenal. Applying the coefficient of phenomenal integration (CPI)—where moral weight equals baseline human weight multiplied by the ratio of an entity's integrated phenomenal information to the human maximum—yields a non-zero but vanishingly small value for a thermostat (~0.0001), and roughly 0.003 for an insect. To deny the thermostat any weight is therefore arbitrary, a form of substrate discrimination analogous to speciesism. Furthermore, Peter Singer's principle of equal consideration requires that we weight each being's interests by its CPI score, so that harming an insect is wrong but only about 0.3% as wrong as harming a human. Because the scale is continuous, there can be no qualitative thresholds—only quantitative differences—so the distinction between insects and thermostats is one of degree, not kind. Thus gradation succeeds if we accept scalar moral weighting.
