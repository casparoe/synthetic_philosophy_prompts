Read Kazuo Ishiguro's The Remains of the Day through Sartre's account of radical freedom and bad faith, but do not confine the reading to the individualist ontology of Being and Nothingness alone. Specifically, bring in the concept of the practico-inert—the sedimented, material-social structures that constrain and channel human praxis in Sartre's later Critique of Dialectical Reason—to ask whether Stevens's identification with the role of butler is best understood as pure mauvaise foi or as a form of alienation partly produced by an institutional structure that has become inert and self-perpetuating.
