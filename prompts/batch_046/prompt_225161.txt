Write an essay on the direction of time and the thermodynamic arrow. Most intro treatments say its definatly just entropy: low-entropy past boundary condition, statistical mechanics, done. But I want you to dig into a weirder angle—call it the coupling problem. Even if we grant a fixed past boundary condition, there's a seperate question about whether "forward" gets its direction purely from the gradient, or whether it's partly fixed by the fact that observers who can record and reason about the gradient are themselves dissipative structures that only form stable memories in one direction. So weigh the standard boundary-only view against this coupling idea: is the arrow out there independent of us, or is it co-constituted by the kind of embodied cognition that can detect it? Reach a real conclusion, not a shrug. Say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point your least sure of. Keep it under 600 words—no citations, no bibliography, just the argument.
