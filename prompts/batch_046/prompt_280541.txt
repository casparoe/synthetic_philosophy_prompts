Give me your credence that rational epistemic akrasia—holding an all-things-considered belief that P while correctly judging that one's first-order evidence supports not-P—is impossible when the higher-order evidence concerns not global reasoning failure but the calibrated unreliability of a specific cognitive sub-module that produced the first-order belief. Decompose your answer: (i) the probability that higher-order evidence can rationally be localized to a sub-module rather than applied globally; (ii) given (i), the probability that the correct rational response is to suspend judgment on P rather than to integrate the first-order belief with a downward-weighted confidence; (iii) the probability that apparently rational cases of akrasia in the literature actually depend on either global unreliability judgments or pragmatic encroachment. Provide a number and one line of justification for each term, then combine them into an overall credence. Finally, identify which term your overall number is most sensitive to, and specify what kind of empirical or formal result—say, from cognitive architecture or formal epistemology—would most shift it.
