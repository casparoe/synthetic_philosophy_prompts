Our guest is an unnamed defender of free-floating rationales—specifically, the view that normative reasons can possess full prescriptive authority independently of any reasoning agent, and that this authority can survive as a normative residue within institutional architectures (for instance, constitutional constraints that continue to generate obligations long after the political community that enacted them has dissolved or transformed beyond recognition). I need a sequence of interview questions that presses the view on its actual philosophical commitments rather than allowing it to be restated in slogan form. The questions should address: (i) the distinction between a free-floating reason and a mere causal regularity or dead-letter rule; (ii) the problem of anthropomorphizing institutional structures by attributing reason-responsive properties to them; (iii) the application to post-dissolution constitutional obligations, including whether such obligations bind anyone in particular; and (iv) how the view handles conflicts between free-floating reasons that have no agent available to adjudicate them. For each substantive question, provide an embedded follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give (for example, that the distinction is drawn by the normative structure itself, or that bindingness is relational rather than psychological). The sequence should build so that her standard deflections become progressively harder to deploy. End with a single closing question asking what kind of argument, conceptual counterexample, or empirical finding about institutional persistence could genuinely change her mind. Please format your response in four named sections, in this order: View characterization (approximately 150 words), summarizing the specific position as I have described it; Question sequence with follow-ups (approximately 550 words), containing the interview flow; Anticipated deflections and blocking moves (approximately 200 words), mapping likely answers onto the follow-ups designed to block them; and Closing vulnerability probe (approximately 100 words), presenting the final question together with a brief justification of why it targets the weakest point in the view.
