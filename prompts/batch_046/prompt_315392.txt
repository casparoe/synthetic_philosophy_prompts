I'm prepping an intervew for my podcast with an unnamed philosopher who defends value-agnostic side-effect bounds for AI—specifically she thinks you can define "low impact" just by comparing world-trajectories with the agent present against a counterfactual where it's absent, without ever specifying what's good. Her twist is that the counterfactual must be one where every other agent adapts optimally to the absence, which she calls an "adaptive null baseline." I want questions that don't let her hide behind "that's engineering, not philosophy."

Write them in four parts:

1. Warm-up (~3 questions): establish the adaptive null baseline, why value-neutrality is even desirable, and what's wrong with standard reversibility metrics.

2. Pressure points (~4 pairs): each pair is a sharp question plus a follow-up keyed to her likely reply. Cover (a) whether "optimal adaptation" smuggles in values; (b) whether aggregating across agents collapses the baseline into a welfare function; (c) cases where her presence prevents learning that would occur in the null world—does blocking that learning count as harm?; and (d) whether the similarity metric needed for counterfactual comparison is itself value-laden.

3. The niche injection (~2 questions): I made up a scenario—an AI that avoids direct harm but systematically suppresses "moral surprise" (unexpected ethical discoveries by other agents) because the adaptive baseline treats such discoveries as deviations. Press whether suppressing moral surprise is a side-effect on her view, and whether answering requires a hidden theory of epistemic value.

4. The closer (~1 question): what result—formal proof, empirical case, or conceptual counterexample—would actually make her drop the adaptive null baseline?

Keep it casual, focused on content, no "how'd you get into philosophy" generic stuff. Don't ask for citations or a reading list.
