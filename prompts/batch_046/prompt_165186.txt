I am presenting a chapter from my dissertation on populism and 'the will of the people' at an interdisciplinary workshop next Thursday. The audience is divided between empirical democratic theorists—scholars who treat the popular will as a measurable aggregate revealed through voting, polling, and representative institutions—and critical political theorists who understand it as an ideological fiction that naturalizes contingent power relations. My thesis is that both approaches miss the operative logic: I argue that 'the will of the people' functions not as a descriptive claim about pre-political preferences but as what I call a 'performative allocation,' a normative placeholder that acquires determinate content only through the very institutional mechanisms that purport merely to discover it. More specifically, I defend a framework in which populist rhetoric redistributes epistemic authority over who belongs to the collective subject, without relying on either empirical aggregation or class-reductionist critique.

The democratic theorists will likely object that this renders populist claims empirically unfalsifiable and therefore useless for normative evaluation; the critical theorists will likely charge that by treating domination as a linguistic effect rather than a material structure, I aestheticize politics and evacuate the possibility of radical critique. I need a Q&A prep sheet that lists the objections in roughly the order they will arrive, phrased in the vocabulary each camp actually uses, ordered by how damaging each is to the survival of the proposal. For each objection, I want to know what I should concede up front—particularly regarding the risk of empty formalism—what I must hold, a concise reply suitable for a two-minute exchange, and an explicit note on where that reply is weakest. I also need identification of the objection I am most hoping nobody raises: the challenge that my framework cannot distinguish legitimate democratic innovation from authoritarian appropriation without smuggling back in the empirical aggregation or the materialist analysis I have explicitly rejected. Please structure this as a speaker's prep document, not as a defense of my position in the abstract.
