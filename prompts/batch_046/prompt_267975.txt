Write a short research proposal (approximately 800 words, suitable for a philosophy dissertation chapter or a small independent grant) on the following question in origin essentialism. The standard debate asks whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg, but it usually treats “origin” as a single genetic pairing event. I want to examine whether origin essentialism fragments once we distinguish three separate origin dimensions: the nuclear genome contributed by sperm and egg; the cytoplasmic and epigenetic environment of the zygote (mitochondrial DNA, methylation patterns, maternal RNA); and the gestational trajectory (implantation conditions, uterine environment, temporal development). Specifically, if a person could have developed with the same nuclear genome but different cytoplasm, or with the same cytoplasm but different nuclear parents, or with the same initial zygote but a different gestational path, does personal identity survive in each case? State the precise question so that it yields a conditional answer rather than an undifferentiated yes or no. Explain why existing philosophical treatments of origin essentialism—which focus almost exclusively on the genetic pairing—leave this open, and why simply appealing to standard personal-identity criteria (psychological continuity, bodily continuity) does not settle it on its own. Describe your method: conceptual analysis of biological individuation, evaluation of thought experiments involving synthetic embryology and cytoplasmic transfer (treated conceptually, not empirically), and comparison with how philosophers already distinguish between genetic and developmental origin in other organisms. Say what a useful result looks like—perhaps that essentialism holds for the nuclear-genetic dimension but fails for cytoplasmic or gestational dimensions, or that all three must be jointly necessary—and specify what would count as failure (the distinctions collapse into one another, or the framing is shown to conflate identity conditions with causal history). Finally, give an honest scope assessment for a single semester or small fellowship: what must be included (the three-dimensional framing, the identity conditions, the failure conditions) and what must be explicitly left out, such as a full theory of diachronic personal identity or an empirical review of assisted reproductive technologies. Do not include a bibliography or citation list; references to positions can be named generally and filled in later.
