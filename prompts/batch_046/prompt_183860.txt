I have been co-leading a graduate reading group on twentieth-century jurisprudence, and after months of circular exchange between members defending Hart's legal positivism and members defending Fuller's natural-law position, we want to stop arguing past one another and produce an adversarial-collaboration document that both camps could genuinely sign. Please draft that joint statement for us.

The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims: about what legal positivism actually asserts (the separation thesis, the rule of recognition, and the distinction between law as it is and law as it ought to be) and what Fuller's view actually asserts (the internal morality of law, the eight principles of legality, and the distinction between morality of aspiration and morality of duty); about what the well-known exchange over post-war German informer cases and retroactive punishment demonstrates and fails to demonstrate; and about the formal difference between conceptual separation and normative separation of law and morality. Then present a disagreements table: each row should contain the precise formulation of one residual disagreement in wording that the defending side accepts, paired with the other side's neutral characterization of the same point, so that neither camp feels misrepresented.

Next, identify the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one party. I take two to be central. First, whether Fuller's eight principles are best interpreted as efficacy conditions for a functioning legal system or as genuinely moral conditions internal to law itself. Second, whether Hart's separation thesis denies only that morality is conceptually necessary for legal validity, or whether it also denies that any moral conditions are necessary for the existence of a legal system. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it. Use this hypothetical: a regime promulgates rules that are fully general, public, prospective, consistently applied, and enforced without deviation, but the rules systematically expropriate a religious minority—in other words, a system that achieves perfect procedural regularity and congruence between official action and promulgated rules in service of grave injustice. Both sides should record in advance whether they predict this counts as law in the descriptive sense and whether it possesses Fuller's internal morality. Finally, conclude by stating whether any portion of the dispute is purely verbal—for instance, about competing uses of "validity," "morality," or "law"—and, if so, isolate exactly which portion and explain why. Throughout, please indicate how confident you are in your formulations, and explicitly identify the point—whether about Fuller's principles, Hart's rule of recognition, or the hypothetical case—about which you are least certain.
