You are a professional philosopher of science and mind with expertise in hierarchical systems, strange loops, and the concept of an inviolate level in tangled hierarchies of the kind associated with Escher's Drawing Hands. Before constructing anything, briefly brainstorm the key structural feature—specifically, a form of representational mediation—that can allow downward influence from a higher level onto a base level without breaking the base level's internal causal closure, since that feature will be the pivot point of the case.

Your task is to construct a thought experiment to the following specifications. Design a single-paragraph scenario set in a realistic, non-science-fictional context—such as a corporate AI-governance or institutional-compliance framework—describing a hierarchical system with at least three levels: L0, a base implementation or physical substrate (e.g., server architecture, employee actions, or statutory code); L1, an operational rule-layer that interprets and enforces conditions at L0; and L2, a meta-rule layer authorized to revise L0 conditions. Crucially, the scenario must include a strange loop: L2 influences L0, but only through representations (e.g., updated policy documents, revised compliance parameters, or reinterpreted mandates) that must be parsed and enacted by L1. The loop must not involve direct physical or hardware-level intervention by L2 onto L0.

Engineer the case so that three philosophical positions deliver precisely these verdicts:
(i) A strict reductionist about hierarchical levels holds that L2 exercises no independent causal power; its apparent downward influence is fully reducible to bottom-up dynamics at L0 mediated by L1's ordinary interpretive operations.
(ii) A strong emergentist holds that L2 exercises genuine, irreducible downward causation onto L0.
(iii) A proponent of tangled hierarchies with an inviolate level holds that downward causation from L2 to L0 is real, but it does not violate the inviolate status of L0 because the influence is representationally mediated through L1; L0 remains causally closed under its own physical or base-level description, yet is semantically open to revision from above.

After presenting the scenario in one paragraph, walk through the reasoning for each of the three positions, stating exactly why the case yields that verdict. Then identify the specific structural feature—the representational mediation through L1—that does the work in allowing the tangled-hierarchy view to split the difference between reduction and strong emergence. Next, explain what happens if that feature is removed: if L2 could directly modify L0 hardware or substrate without L1 interpretation, does the case collapse into a standard emergence-versus-reduction debate, and why? Finally, describe how a defender of the reductionist position would attack the tangled-hierarchy interpretation of your case, and whether that attack succeeds or relies on begging the question against the mediating role of representation.
