I'm preparing my position for an aesthetics reading group on the paradox of tragedy, and I want a consistency check before I circulate the draft. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I take the paradox to be genuinely puzzling only if one assumes a hedonistic theory of aesthetic value; since I reject hedonism, I do not think there is any contradiction in valuing art that evokes suffering.

(2) I endorse a revised Aristotelian account: tragedy produces catharsis, but I interpret catharsis not as emotional purgation but as emotional recalibration — pity and fear are not expelled but re-tuned to a more appropriate sensitivity.

(3) I think Hume's conversion of sympathy explains melancholy lyrical forms (elegy, certain music) but not dramatic tragedy, because in tragedy the affective intensity is too high for aesthetic pleasure to dominate the negative emotion.

(4) I reject control-theoretic or excitation-transfer explanations — the idea that tragedy is enjoyed because it offers a safe simulation of danger that resolves — because they cannot account for why audiences specifically value works that withhold resolution and maintain genuine hopelessness.

(5) I hold that appreciating tragedy requires moral attendance: the viewer must genuinely treat the characters' suffering as worthy of concern rather than as mere spectacle, and I think this attitude is compatible with full knowledge of fictionality.

(6) I also hold that the value of tragedy is ultimately instrumental: we seek calibrated encounters with represented suffering because they improve our practical capacities for compassion, risk-assessment, and resilience.

(7) I claim there is no essential qualitative difference between the emotional response to fictional tragedy and to real-world suffering; the difference lies entirely in the causal powers of the object — fiction does not present a situation that calls for intervention.

Please audit this package. Specifically: which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and how severe is each real tension? For the genuine ones, indicate the cheapest revision and what it costs. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them equally. In particular, examine whether (5) and (7) generate a hidden contradiction about intervention, whether (1) is doing unstated work that saves (6) from being incoherent, and whether (2) and (4) can both stand given that recalibration sounds like a form of emotional resolution.
