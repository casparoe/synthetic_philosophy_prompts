I'm preparing an interview for a philosophy podcast with a guest who defends a view she calls coupled-process functionalism about moral status and computation. On her account, a computation generates interests or patienthood only when realized in an architecture that maintains continuous, non-decomposable feedback loops with an environment—what she terms dense causal coupling—rather than running as an isolated simulation or breaking into serial sub-routines. She uses this to reject both biological essentialism and unrestricted substrate-independent panpsychism about computation: a perfect brain simulation on a disconnected server lacks moral status, she argues, while an embodied system with an equivalent functional profile possesses it, precisely because of the coupling structure rather than the substrate. I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the position. Please include at least one question pressing on how the dense causal coupling criterion distinguishes genuinely coupled systems from those that merely appear coupled through buffered input-output interfaces; one on whether her view implies that temporarily isolated but preserved brains—say, in sustained sensory-deprivation conditions—lose moral status during the isolation period; one on what her account says about distributed computations that span multiple devices or agents with intermittent connectivity; and one on whether small language models running in closed inference loops without real-time environmental feedback could ever cross the threshold she proposes. For each of these, provide a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—anticipating, for example, appeals to potential coupling, to functional continuity, or to scale-independent architecture. Avoid generic biographical questions and do not ask for reading lists or citations.
