I work in software engineering—designing systems that maintain state across updates—but I have no formal training in philosophy. I keep encountering references to the frame problem in discussions of AI reasoning, and I want to understand what an actual solution would have to accomplish before I could call an architecture adequate. Rather than asking for a history of the debate, I want a specification: write the desiderata that any theory or mechanism addressing the frame problem must satisfy. For each requirement, provide the concrete reasoning failure or system-design scenario that motivates it. The list should preserve key distinctions—such as that between representing an explicit change versus implicitly preserving an unmentioned invariant; between domain-general and domain-specific representations of persistence; between tractable computation and exhaustive enumeration of non-effects; and between syntactic storage and semantic entailment—because collapsing any of these produces a different kind of engineering failure. Identify which desiderata are non-negotiable for any viable system and which are preferences of particular research programs. Crucially, flag any subsets of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable given standard results about computation, representation, or logic, and explain the tension. Do not provide a bibliography or scorecard of existing approaches; I need the evaluation criteria themselves, written with the formality of a technical requirements document rather than an essay. Avoid philosophical jargon where a systems-design term will do, but do not oversimplify the conceptual work.
