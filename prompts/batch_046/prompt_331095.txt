okay so me and my roommate were arguing for like two hours about the three stances thing — physical, design, intentional — and whether real patterns actually do any work. i came up with this argument that feels like it proves way too much and now i cant sleep. here it is.

(1) a pattern counts as real, not just instrumental, if adopting it lets you make reliable counterfactual predictions that are genuinely more compressive than tracking every physical detail. (2) thats exactly why beliefs and desires are real patterns: the intentional stance compresses human behavior massively. (3) but the same compression works for a thermostat — "it wants 72 degrees" predicts its behavior better than tracking every electron — and even for planets, where treating orbit as a "goal" of minimizing energy simplifies the math. (4) so if compression is the mark of reality, then thermostats literally have beliefs and planets literally have goals. (5) thats clearly wrong, or at least i dont want to be a panpsychist about toasters. (6) so we should reject (1) and say compression alone isnt enough for ontological commitment. (7) but then the intentional stance loses its special claim that beliefs are real patterns, and we're back to eliminativism about minds. (8) therefore the three-stance view either collapses into saying everything is mental or nothing is.

where does this actually break? my roommate says (3) cheats because thermostats and planets dont have the right kind of counterfactual robustness, but i dont see why that isnt just special pleading. i really want to keep (2) — mental patterns are real — and id also like to keep (6) false, since i think compression should matter for ontology. but maybe im missing an equivocation between predictive usefulness and ontological reality? tell me which step is the weak link and what giving it up costs, especially if it forces me to choose between pan-intentionalism and eliminativism.
