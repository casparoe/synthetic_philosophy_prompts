At my plant there's this protocall where if the barcode scanner dies you still gotta wave every box past the dead sensor three times and log it, even if you already checked everything by hand. Management says its about traceability but the crew just calls it 'sphex behavior' — like that wasp that keeps dragging prey back to a hole even after you move it away. They joke we're being sphexish on purpose. Anyway I'm wondering what philosophical commitments a rule like this actually presupposes. Does it have to assume workers are basically mechanistic, that flexibility is a bug not a feature? Or could you defend the same protocall while still thinking people have real free will? And if rigidity really does erode agency, does that mean the company is responsible for stripping ours away by design?
