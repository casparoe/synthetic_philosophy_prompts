I'm drafting an essay for an advanced philosophy seminar on whether a philosopher's private commitments should ever be read back into their systematic work, and I would appreciate critical comments on its accuracy, coherence, and overall plausibility. The case I examine is Gottlob Frege's private political diary—those posthumously revealed late-life entries in which he expressed virulent nationalist sentiments, contempt for democratic politics, and a conviction that German culture required protection from what he characterized as corrupting foreign and particularly Jewish influence. The standard scholarly response treats these writings as biographically repellent but philosophically inert: since Frege's logicism and anti-psychologism are usually presented as technical achievements in the foundations of mathematics, there seems to be no necessary bridge from his politics to his philosophy.

My essay argues for a structurally integrated reading that stops short of claiming direct causal influence. I propose that Frege's philosophical insistence on the absolute objectivity of Gedanken—their independence from individual psychology, from linguistic variation, and from historical contingency—mirrors, at a formal level, the political fantasy expressed in his diary: a German nation purified of subjective contamination and restored to an essential, objective cultural form. I do not argue that his logic was caused by his nationalism; rather, I suggest that both share an underlying normative architecture of boundary maintenance, in which value depends on the rigorous exclusion of what is deemed internal and corrupting. Specifically, I draw a parallel between his rejection of psychologism in logic—the view that logical laws depend on human mental processes—and his political rejection of multicultural or democratic influence—the view that German identity should not depend on contingent social mixtures. I further consider whether his distinction between sense and reference, with its sharp separation between objective content and subjective mode of presentation, might philosophically encode a distinction between authentic national substance and inauthentic foreign appearance, though I acknowledge this risks serious overreading.

Could you comment on whether this analogy holds up philosophically, or whether it collapses into a genetic fallacy? I would particularly value your assessment of whether there is any defensible way to connect Frege's anti-psychologism to a broader cultural anxiety about purity without misrepresenting either his logical doctrines or the fragmentary, context-specific character of his private remarks. Please also note any places where my framing might rely on inaccurate characterizations of Frege's published philosophy—for instance, whether I am wrong to suggest that his concept of objectivity carries the exclusionary, normative structure I describe, or whether the diary entries are too sporadic and reactive to support such a systematic interpretation. I am not asking for a bibliography or literature review, but rather for a direct evaluation of the argument's coherence and of whether the interpretive risk is worth taking.
