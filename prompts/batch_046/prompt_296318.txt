From this certainty he derives the general criterion that whatever is clearly and distinctly perceived is true, then proves God exists by applying that criterion, and finally invokes the divine guarantee to validate all clear and distinct perceptions retrospectively, including mathematics and eventually the external world. On this reading, the cogito lies outside the Cartesian Circle because it provides the initial, self-validating instance of clear perception; consequently, anyone who insists that ergo implies a hidden major premise, or who thinks Descartes proves substance dualism in Meditation II itself, has missed both the epistemic architecture and the metaphysical conclusion of the Meditations.
