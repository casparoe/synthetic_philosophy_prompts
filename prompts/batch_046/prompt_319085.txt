I've been trying to systematize my decision-theoretic commitments and want to check whether the package is actually stable. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept updateless decision theory in the form discussed in the rationalist literature: rational choice is the selection of a policy that maximizes prior-weighted expected utility summed across worlds, with no Bayesian conditioning on the fact that this particular agent is now executing the policy.

(2) In Newcomb's problem I one-box. My reason is not generic evidential correlation, but that my decision algorithm and the predictor's model are the same computation instantiated in different contexts, so they are logically linked across worlds in a way that makes the two-box policy globally suboptimal.

(3) In Parfit's hitchhiker I think you should pay the driver upon arrival. I justify this strictly by the ex-ante expected utility of adopting the credible-commitment policy, not by any backward-causal influence of the payment on the driver's prior prediction.

(4) In the counterfactual mugging scenario I think I should refuse to pay. My ground is that once the coin has landed heads, the counterfactual tails-world lies outside the reference class for evaluating my current policy; UDT should not weight worlds that share no logical identity with my present implementation.

(5) I hold that decision-theoretic norms are temporally invariant: analyzing Newcomb's problem at 2 AM or at 2 PM must yield the identical policy verdict.

(6) I also think that if my cognitive architecture is implemented in silicon rather than biological neural tissue, the logical identity conditions that underpin UDT's cross-world evaluation become undefined. In that substrate I should suspend UDT and revert to causal decision theory.

(7) Finally, I think that if my algorithm is duplicated onto two physically separate servers running bit-identical code, both copies fall within the same reference class for UDT evaluation; but a single-bit divergence breaks the logical correlation and removes the other instance from the class.

Does this hang together? Specifically: does (4) contradict the global policy evaluation required by (1) and (2)? Is there a hidden tension between my substrate-dependence in (6) and my temporal-invariance claim in (5), or between (6) and the duplication criterion in (7)? Is the reference-class maneuver in (4) and (7) compatible with the silicon-exception in (6), or am I smuggling in an ad hoc ontology? And is there an apparent conflict between (3)'s appeal to ex-ante commitment-value and (1)'s updateless framing that actually dissolves once the conditioning rules are made explicit?

I want an audit, not a lecture: rank the tensions by severity, say which pairs are only superficially in conflict, and identify the cheapest way to restore consistency and what it costs—without assuming I already know which commitment I would drop.
