I am a PhD student in philosophy working on practical reason, and I want a consistency audit of my position before I present it at a workshop. Here is the package of commitments I currently hold:

(1) I accept an internalist constraint on practical reasons in roughly the Williamsian spirit: for R to be a reason for agent S, there must be a sound deliberative route from S’s actual motivational set to R.

(2) I also hold that moral obligations generate categorical reasons: they apply with full normative force regardless of an agent’s contingent desires, projects, or identifications.

(3) I think practical reasoning is not merely instrumental; it can be transformative in the sense that reflection on values such as coherence, self-respect, or social identity can rationally lead an agent to adopt ends she did not previously hold.

(4) I maintain that institutional roles—judge, trustee, parent—generate reasons that are neither fully internal (they do not depend on the agent’s personal desire to fulfill the role) nor fully categorical (they do not bind every rational agent regardless of context).

(5) I deny that the amoralist is irrational: a fully informed, coherent agent who recognizes moral demands but lacks any corresponding motivation is rational, though perhaps culpable in some non-rational register.

(6) I take it that explaining an action by citing its moral rightness—she donated because it was fair—can be a complete reason-explanation without attributing any prior desire for fairness.

Please examine these pairwise: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, ordered by severity, with for each the conflict, why the usual motivations for the two commitments are incompatible, and what the least costly revision would be together with what it costs conceptually. I also want you to flag any pair that looks tense but actually dissolves on inspection—especially (1) with (3), since I suspect that is only apparent; please address that explicitly. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would resolve the largest number of conflicts, and whether the remaining five then cohere cleanly.
