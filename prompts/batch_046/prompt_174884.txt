In our philosophy of science reading group we've been going in circles for months about Carnap's actual stance on social and economic planning. One camp reads him as basically a technocrat-in-waiting: they point to his work with Neurath on the International Encyclopedia of Unified Science and his enthusiasm for planned languages and logical notation, and they say if rational reconstruction applies to physics it obviously applies to society too. The other camp says that's a total misreading: Carnap was a value-neutralist, he explicitly said value judgments are expressions of feeling not subject to logical analysis, and he was personally liberal-democratic, so there's no bridge from logical syntax to central planning. We all agree on the texts — the Logical Syntax passages, the autobiography, the Neurath correspondance — but nobody's moved an inch. I'm wondering if the stall is actually about something hidden: maybe we're using 'planning' in two different senses (methodological coordination vs state control), or maybe there's an implicit disagreement about whether scientific rationality can be separated from political rationality that neither side has spelled out. What's your diagnosis? Is this a verbal dispute about 'planning', a substantive disagreement about the scope of logical empiricism, or are we just stuck on bedrock intutions about whether rationality should extend to social life? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move it — a closer look at his 1930s writings on value, a formal distinction between types of planning, or is the question just ill-posed because Carnap never meant to have a political philosophy at all?
