Suppose you are advising an interdisciplinary research group—two philosophers of law, one moral philosopher, and one institutional sociologist—who have three years of funding to pursue conceptual, not empirical, work on how different legal architectures for sex work shape the conditions under which consent and coercion operate. Their focal comparison is among three frameworks: the Nordic model of asymmetric criminalization, the legalization regimes in Germany and the Netherlands, and the full decriminalization model adopted in New Zealand. They do not want a descriptive survey of these laws, nor a generic normative argument about whether sex work is inherently harmful; they want a prioritized agenda of open conceptual problems.

Your task is to propose and defend a ranked list of the three to five conceptual questions or lines of work that should command the bulk of their time and effort. For each entry, specify: (i) the precise conceptual problem—formulated narrowly enough that it is not reducible to "should sex work be legal?"; (ii) why it is upstream of other debates in this space, identifying which further questions depend on its resolution; (iii) why it is tractable for philosophers and legal theorists within a three-year window, rather than requiring large-scale empirical study or legislative experimentation that this group cannot perform; (iv) what kind of conceptual deliverable—such as a framework, a consistency condition, or a refined distinction—would count as meaningful progress; and (v) what kind of argument or theoretical development over the next few years should cause them to reorder or drop it.

In building the agenda, introduce and defend at least one non-standard conceptual distinction—such as a separation between "contractual visibility" (the extent to which a legal regime demands explicit, state-legible terms of exchange) and "regulatory opacity" (the extent to which it deliberately withholds participant information from law enforcement)—and demonstrate how adopting it reshuffles which questions are primary and which are derivative. The group is already familiar with the basic legislative differences; do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.

Finally, identify the single crux—the one unresolved conceptual issue—on which the coherence and ordering of your entire proposed agenda depends, and explain what resolving it in either direction would do to the rest of the list.
