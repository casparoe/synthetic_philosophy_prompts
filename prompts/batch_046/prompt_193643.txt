Hey, software engineer here, no phil backround except whatever I picked up from podcasts. There's this pattern I keep seeing in arguments about whether AI or work policies are 'fair' or 'ethical' and I want to know if philosophers have a name for it, or if I'm just imagining a structure.

Usually someone drops a big universal-sounding claim: 'privacy is an absolute right,' or 'equal treatment is always required.' Then a counterexample comes up - say, a medical AI that needs to train on sensitive health records, or a company giving extra flexibility to parents but not to everyone else. Instead of revising the original claim to 'privacy is a right except when public health outweighs it,' the defender just lists the specific features of the counterexample and says 'this case is different, the rule was never meant for this.' They never spell out the general condition that distinguishes it. But then in the next thread they use the same original claim against someone else, ignoring that that case might also have distinguishing features.

I've seen this happen with data-consent debates and with remote-work equity discussions. The people doing it don't seem like they're being dishonest; they genuinely think morality works by recognizing the right details in each particlar situation rather than applying qualified rules. But they also don't have any system for which details count, so it feels like the big claim is just a slogan that gets switched on and off.

Is there an established concept for this exaclty thing - not just 'making exceptions' or being hypocritical, but a specific view about moral reasoning where universal claims exist but are constantly dissolved by case-specific descriptions without ever being formally qualified? How does it relate to the idea that ethics is about particlar situations rather than general laws? And if philosophers don't have a term for this precise asymmetry, what would you call the pattern?

Not looking for citations or a reading list, just trying to figure out if I'm seeing real philosophy or just people arguing badly on twitter.
