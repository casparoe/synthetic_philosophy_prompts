In my distributive justice course I'm working out my own stance on prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection. Taxing extreme wealth to secure basic needs for the worst-off is clearly justified. Banning luxury goods like private jets and destroying them with no redistribution feels wrong—wasteful, not just. Removing purely symbolic status markers—hereditary titles, exclusive clubs that confer no material perks—feels permissible if it reduces positional harm. Deliberately destroying surplus resources that could aid nobody, solely to lower the best-off, is wrong. But I'm genuinely torn on pure compression: slightly reducing the material well-being of the best-off and shrinking the gap, with zero net gain for anyone else—sometimes it strikes me as legitimate equality-promotion, sometimes as bare leveling-down. Can you state one precise principle, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it, that delivers all five verdicts? If it cannot, which verdict should I drop? And what does it predict for hiding—rather than destroying—a visible luxury to reduce observer distress while leaving resources intact?
