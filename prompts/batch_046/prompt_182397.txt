hey, quick consistency check before a seminar. my takes on the unilateralist curse / collective action are roughly:

(1) it's real and bad—when anyone can act alone on something with collective impact, rational agents usually worsen things for everyone, especialy in governance and tech.
(2) binding collective decision rules are the standard cure, but i reject them on autonomy grounds; they don't track expertise any better.
(3) still, i think people should defer to expert consensus in these domains, even tho the curse applies worst exactly where experts disagree (geoengineering, AI alignment, etc).
(4) i actually think the curse is worse for omissions—failing to coordinate on a beneficial collective action—than for the classic "one agent jumps the gun" case.
(5) if you genuinely believe your unilateral move helps, you're not blamworthy for taking it, even knowing the dynamics.
(6) democratic legitimacy requires keeping unilateral dissent rights, so some curse-risk is just unavoidable.
(7) we should model this with ambiguity-averse non-expected utility, not standard EU, since collective outcomes involve deep uncertainty.

do these hang together? rank the real tensions by severity, say which pairs only look incompatible, and for each tension what's the cheapest revision. is (4) just me conflating the curse with a coordination failure, or a legitimate twist? and does (7) actually do work here, or is it just decorative?
