I've been turning over John Searle's Chinese Room argument since a friend who read a little philosophy at university summarised it for me over coffee. Her account ran roughly like this: Searle proves that running a computer program can never produce real understanding, because inside the room a person shuffles Chinese symbols using only English rule-books, with no grasp of what the symbols mean. Since the person does not understand Chinese, the system as a whole does not either, which shows that syntax is never sufficient for semantics. From this, she said, it follows that any artificial intelligence—however complex its architecture—can possess only derived intentionality, like that of a map or a dictionary, and never the original intentionality that minds genuinely have. She went on to claim that Searle therefore denies the brain is any kind of computer whatsoever, maintaining instead that consciousness arises purely from biological chemistry in a way silicon could never replicate. Finally, she argued that the thought experiment demolishes the Turing Test as a criterion for thought, since a machine might pass by manipulating symbols correctly while remaining entirely mindless inside.
