I'm trying to figure out my gut feelings on what we owe dead people when we fictonalize them in films and books. A biopic inventes a shouting match between a dead president and his advisor that never happened, but it captures a real disagreement they had — feels okay. A novel puts dialogue in a dead poet's mouth using only her actual private letters — feels wrong, even if every word is authentic. A movie changes a dead activist's cause of death from suicide to heart failure to protect living relatives — not okay. A play recasts a dead historical figure with a different ethnicity to diversify the cast, which misleads about her origins — not okay either, though maybe less bad than hiding suicide? A documentary-style drama inventes a meeting between two dead scientists who never actually spoke, just to dramatize their theories clashing — okay, I think. The one I keep flip-flopping on is a script where a dead inventor narrates as a ghost, clearly fictional, but the writers build every line from his real unpublished notebooks. Is there a single principle — about consent, harm to the living, or falsifying the record — that sorts these? Or do some verdicts just have to go? And what should I say about using real private papers for fictional ghost-dialogue?
