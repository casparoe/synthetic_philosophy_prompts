Consider a metaphysician whose judgements on material constitution have been consistent: a bronze statue melted and recast is destroyed, though the bronze remains; the ship of Theseus, gradually rebuilt plank by plank in continuous service, is the original vessel, whereas a warehouse reconstruction from the stored old planks is a replica; a human being whose neurons are replaced one by one by synthetic equivalents survives uninterrupted; a clay statue crushed into an amorphous lump is destroyed, leaving only clay; and a digital file whose original server is destroyed survives only as a copy elsewhere, not as the same object.

She is now presented with a living sculpture: a colony of engineered bacteria continuously regenerates a biodegradable scaffold, preserving an exact geometric form and behavioural pattern over a decade, with every cell and polymer molecule replaced, all discarded matter archived in a vault from which an identical colony could be assembled.

Would she judge that the original sculpture persists? More importantly, lay out at least two distinct principles that fit her earlier track record equally well, state what each predicts about this living sculpture, and identify the further, distinct case that would best discriminate between them. Give your best-guess reconstruction of her reasoning, noting the closest analogues from her record.
