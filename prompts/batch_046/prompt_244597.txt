ok so i'm trying to straighten out my view on Benacerraf's dilema before a seminar. here's roughly where i land:

1. the dilema is real: if math objects are acausal abstracta and knowledge requires causal contact, then mathematical knowledge is mysterious.
2. i'm a platonist about numbers and sets — mind-independent, not spatiotemporal.
3. i think the causal/naturalistic requirement applies everywhere; no special pleading for math.
4. we genuinely know math stuff, like that there are infinate primes.
5. i reject Gödel-style intuition; it sounds like mysticism to me.
6. my fix is that math knowledge works through coherence and reliabilist justification, with zero causal interaction.
7. for physics, though, coherence alone is never enough without some empirical hook.

my tensions: (2)+(3)+(4) seem to blow up unless i go with (6), but then (6) is exactly the special pleading that (3) forbids. also people say platonism (2) needs intuition (5) — is that a real conflict or just a myth? and does (7) show (6) is an ad-hoc double standard?
