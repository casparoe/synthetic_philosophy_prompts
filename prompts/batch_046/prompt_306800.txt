Fear is like seeing a predator, shame like seeing a social stain, etc. It's used constantly to argue that emotions have representational content, can be accurate or inaccurate, and therefore aren't just raw feelings. But lately I've been thinking about the interoceptive predictive-coding view — that what we call emotion is actually the brain's best guess about bodily state, not about the world — which makes the source domain look less like vision and more like proprioception or vestibular sense.

Can you audit how far the analogy holds? I want it in four parts, each about 150-200 words:

- Mapping table: what corresponds to what (object, representational content, conditions of accuracy, etc.)
- The breaks: where the analogy frays, especially with moods that have no clear intentional object, and with the predictive-coding twist I mentioned
- Survival of the conclusion: does the idea that emotions are rationally assessable survive if we replace exteroceptive perception with interoceptive prediction?
- Better analogy?: is there a source domain that captures both world-directedness and bodily basis without collapsing into pure judgment or pure feeling?
