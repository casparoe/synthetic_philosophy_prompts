A colleague who has read some of the alignment literature summarized coherent extrapolated volition—the project of determining what humanity collectively wants if we knew more, thought faster, and were closer to the people we wish to be—in the following way: "CEV begins with every individual's actual preferences, applies a coherence filter that strips out logical contradictions, factual errors, and what we might call affective noise—wants formed under conditions of scarcity or stress that would not survive in abundance—and then merges the remaining preference structures into a single stable target through an interpersonal consistency constraint, essentially a democratic weighted aggregation. Because the process starts from real human values and only cleans them up, it avoids paternalism: it is preference hygiene, not preference replacement. The resulting volition is what a genuinely aligned system ought to maximize, and objections that this is authoritarian typically come from people who are merely uncomfortable with having their own inconsistencies made explicit." I am inclined to think this sounds too tidy, but I am not sure exactly where the tidiness becomes distortion. Could you audit the summary? Please separate what is accurate about the role of coherence, the treatment of present versus extrapolated preferences, and the method of aggregation; identify the subtle conflations or overstatements—for instance, whether "preference hygiene" correctly captures the relationship between current and idealized wants, whether the democratic-weighted framing is faithful to the proposal, and whether the "affective noise" distinction belongs to the view or has been imported; and assess whether the conclusion that paternalism objections reduce to discomfort with consistency survives once those corrections are made, or whether it depends on one of the misrepresentations.
