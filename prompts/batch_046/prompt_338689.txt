Please argue that Searle's Chinese Room actually succeeds in blocking strong AI, but only if we read 'understanding' as requiring what we might call causal-historical anchoring—the idea that symbol-manipulation counts as semantic only if there's a direct causal chain linking the symbol to its referent that runs through the system's own sensory-motor history, not just through formal correlations set up by a programmer. The usual systems reply misses this because it treats functional organization as sufficient regardless of how the symbols got their meaning; but on this reading, the room fails not because it's made of the wrong stuff, but because the causal history of its symbols belongs entirely to the programmers and the Chinese speakers outside, not to the room itself. Address why this dosent just reduce to standard externalist semantics, and why it still leaves open whether a robot with the right kind of embodied causal history could understand.
