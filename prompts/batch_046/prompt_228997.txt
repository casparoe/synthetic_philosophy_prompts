I was in my epistemology study group — well, philosophy of knowledge — and my friends Jules and Nina got into it about the regress problem. I mostly just listened and now i'm trying to figure out who was actually right.

Jules started by saying foundationalism is definatly the only sane option: if every belief needs another belief behind it, you never actually start knowing anything, so there have to be basic beliefs that just don't need further justification, like direct perceptual ones or self-evident truths. Nina said thats just declaring victory by fiat — call something "basic" and you've named the problem away, not solved it. Even "this looks red" smuggles in color theory and language, so the so-called foundation is already circular.

Jules fired back that coherentism is worse: if beliefs only justify each other, its just mutual admiration. At least foundationalism has an endpoint. Nina replied that coherentism isn't pairwise circular, its holistic; a belief is justified by fitting the whole web, not by trading support with one partner. The system gets tested against experience as a whole. Then Jules raised the entry problem: if experience only gets in through coherence, then experience doesn't constrain the web, the web just absorbs it. Nina conceded that pure coherentism might have that issue, which is why she likes what she calls tethered infinitism: justification runs backward forever, but each link is anchored to a practical commitment — like trusting your eyes because you act on them, not because you've justified them. The chain never ends, but it doesn't need to, because the anchors are actions, not beliefs.

Jules said she's just hiding the foundation inside "practical commitment." Those commitments need justification too — why trust your eyes? If she answers "because they work," thats an empirical claim that sends her back up the chain. So tethered infinitism either collapses into foundationalism about practical beliefs, or into coherentism about what works. Nina said no, the practical anchor isn't a belief at all; its a disposition to act. You don't justify the disposition, you just have it. So the regress stops in action, not in belief, which makes it different from both other views.

Don't just declare a winner — tell me what's right and wrong in each round, and say what both are missing that neither mentioned.
