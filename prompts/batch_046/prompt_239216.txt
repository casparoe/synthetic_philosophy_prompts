Write a two-page dissertation prospectus for a one-semester philosophy project on origin essentialism—specifically the claim that you could not have come from a different sperm and egg. Rather than replaying the standard Kripke-style modal argument, frame the project around an underexplored tension with developmental biology. Biological development is not a deterministic program executed from a fixed initial state; it is a noisy, path-dependent causal process in which stochastic cellular events (developmental noise) are partly constitutive of the organism that results. The proposal should ask a precise question: does origin essentialism depend on a substance metaphysics of the self—where identity requires persistence of the same underlying entity with an essential origin—or can it survive if personal identity is reconceived as the continuity of a specific causal-process trajectory? In other words, if the "origin" is not a discrete boundary condition but the initiation point of a branching, stochastic trajectory, does the intuition that different gametes necessarily yield a different person remain stable?

State why existing positions leave this open. Essentialists and anti-essentialists alike typically treat the zygote as a well-defined initial object and ask modal questions about substituting its parts. Neither camp systematically examines whether the modal claim survives when the origin event is modeled as a noisy process rather than a static essence, or whether "the same origin" is even well-defined across variations in the noise profile.

Describe the method: conceptual analysis of the modal logic of origin claims under a process-ontology framework, distinguishing genomic origin, causal-process origin, and narrative origin; testing which of these survive when development is treated as path-dependent rather than deterministic. Specify the deliverable: a taxonomy of origin claims and an argument for which (if any) remain defensible once we abandon substance metaphysics in this domain. Give an honest scope assessment—tractable in one semester because it is conceptual rather than empirical, but vulnerable to collapse if the substance-process distinction here turns out to be purely verbal. Include a clear failure condition: if all plausible reformulations of origin essentialism in process terms reduce to claims about causal continuity that anti-essentialists already accept, then the project has failed to carve out a distinctive position. Write in a formal, rationalist-adjacent voice that values tractability and explicit failure conditions; do not include a bibliography, citation list, or request for one, though you may refer to standard positions by name. Keep the framing precise enough that the work can be done without access to external literature.
