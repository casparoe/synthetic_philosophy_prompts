Act as a professional epistemologist. Before constructing anything, take a moment to map the logical space of responses to the epistemic regress problem, paying special attention to how each view handles justification when belief-formation involves iterative loops, nested dependency, or distributed update rather than a simple linear chain.

Design a decision-tree taxonomy—no more than seven branch points—that sorts an interlocutor into one of the following leaves based solely on answers to concrete cases, not on label recognition: (1) strong foundationalism (terminating, self-justifying basic beliefs); (2) moderate foundationalism (fallible but non-inferential bases); (3) pure coherentism (holistic mutual support with no epistemic priority); (4) local coherentism (cluster-based support with external anchors); (5) infinitism (justification requires an actually infinite, non-repeating chain); and (6) a distributed scaffolding view (justification propagates through successive updates with neither a fixed endpoint nor circular closure).

Each branch point must present a specific scenario and ask for the correct diagnosis—e.g., whether a belief retained through an expert-testimony loop that eventually returns to one's own derivative belief remains justified, or whether a continuously self-correcting instrument that never receives an absolute calibration can yield knowledge. For every leaf, state its defining commitment in one sentence. Then identify at least one logically possible but unoccupied combination in the space (for instance, a view that demands both an actual infinite chain and circular closure) and briefly diagnose whether its emptiness stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Finally, flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot reliably separate and explain why the ambiguity is intrinsic to the positions rather than a flaw in the instrument. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; build the tree from conceptual structure alone. Keep the instrument focused on sorting beliefs, not on naming theories.
