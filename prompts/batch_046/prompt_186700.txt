If Einstein had lived into the era of string theory and multiverse cosmology, how do you think he would have evaluated the epistemic status of theories that rely primarily on non-empirical confirmation—such as mathematical consistency, anomaly cancellation, and anthropic selection across an exponentially large landscape—rather than on direct observational tests yielding distinct predictions? Ground your prediction in his broader philosophical commitments: his distinction between constructive and principle theories, his conviction that a mature physical theory should not rest on arbitrary free parameters, and his resistance to probabilistic or observer-dependent foundations. Specifically, address whether he would likely have treated the landscape problem as a catastrophic failure of explanatory uniqueness, or whether he might have attempted to salvage a principle-theory interpretation in which consistency conditions alone filter out all but one effective vacuum state. Finally, adopting the framing of a rationalist audience that takes explicit Bayesian updating seriously, discuss how Einstein might have reacted to the proposal that rational belief in our particular cosmological constants should be updated not by empirical access to other universes, but by computing a prior-weighted measure over the space of string vacua.
