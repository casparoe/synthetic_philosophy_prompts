I host a philosophy-of-mind podcast, and next week's guest is an unnamed philosopher who defends a strict heterophenomenological method for consciousness science: all first-person reports are to be treated as texts describing a "heterophenomenological world," with no special epistemic privilege granted to the reporter over an external interpreter. Draft eight to ten interview questions that avoid asking him to restate the view. I want at least one question on how the method distinguishes itself from eliminativism or behaviorism without smuggling in first-person authority; one on whether the heterophenomenological world is merely a heuristic fiction or carries ontological weight; and one that presses him on how the method would treat reports generated by a system—say, a predictive-processing architecture—that produces accurate verbal descriptions of "pain" or "redness" without any qualitative experience, and whether such reports would be indistinguishable from those of a conscious subject within the method. For each primary question, include a sharp follow-up tailored to the answer he is most likely to give. In framing the questions, please separate what is well-established about the auto-phenomenology/heterophenomenology distinction from what is genuinely contested—particularly whether the method can ever adjudicate consciousness in artificial or non-human systems—without presenting a disputed reading as the consensus view.
