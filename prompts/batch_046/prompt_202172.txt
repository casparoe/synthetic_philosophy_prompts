Write a joke that actually hinges on Émilie du Châtelet's view of happiness from her Discours sur le bonheur—not some generic "she was Voltaire's smart girlfriend" bit. The punchline should turn on the specific tension that she was a hardcore Newton translator demanding empircal rigor, yet she argues happiness runs on what she calls the great machines of happiness: health, taste for study, freedom from prejudice, and—crucially—susceptability to illusion. Specifically, she thinks we owe most pleasures to illusions we willingly maintain, especially around love and ambition. So make it funny to someone who knows that exact philosophical contradiction, not just anyone who knows she existed. Keep it casual, this is going into a datset of conceptual prompts, not a journal article.
