okay so i've been trying to figure out when people on the other side of political divides are actually arguing in good faith, and i have these verdicts that feel right but don't line up neatly.

case one: there's a climate skeptic who can actually walk you through radiative forcing math step by step, but he only pulls out that skill to poke holes in models he dislikes — he never uses it to check claims from his own side. i call that bad faith. it's not the error, it's the one-directional weaponization.

case two: a woman uses this framework she calls 'sovereignty as non-interference' — she applies it to bash immigration policy and also to bash military aid to allies, so it's consistent across left-right lines. even though i think the framework is wrong, i want to say she's in good faith.

case three: a guy updates every belief — covid risk levels, trade forecasts, whatever — exactly when his party updates, but he always has a smooth narrative ready. obviously bad faith.

case four: someone admits she doesn't understand the economic models behind tariff debates, but she trusts her brother who's an economist, and she sticks with his view even when her party shifts. i want to call this good faith, but it feels different from case two — more like delegation than principle.

the tension i can't resolve is between two and four: both avoid tribal updating, but one is principled consistency and one is just outsourcing. and then there's this other speaker who defines 'freedom' as freedom from social obligation rather than state coercion, applies it everywhere, even against thier own allies. i think he's in good faith, but his reasoning is so alien that from the outside it looks identical to bad faith.

is there a coherent principle — maybe something about directional openness or reflective equilibrium across domains — that saves all four verdicts? or do i have to drop one? and what should it say about the case where someone is perfectly consistent and open to counter-evidence within their framework, but adopted the framework specifically because it immunizes thier political conclusions? i haven't decided on that one, and i'd like your principle to predict it.
