I'm trying to stabilize my position on self-reference and machine learning before drafting a longer essay, and I'd like a consistency audit rather than a broad lecture. Here are the commitments I'm holding:

(a) The MU-puzzle, as Hofstadter frames it, demonstrates that any sufficiently powerful formal system contains statements undecidable within the system but decidable only by stepping outside it—what he calls jumping out of the system (JOOTSing).

(b) Human mathematicians perform something structurally analogous: we shift meta-languages or alter axiom sets in response to independence results, and this is a feature of rational cognition rather than a failure of rigor.

(c) If artificial learning systems are to genuinely learn rather than merely optimize within a fixed loss landscape, we should build in analogues of JOOTSing—mechanisms that allow the system to revise its own evaluation criteria when those criteria produce persistent paradox or stagnation.

(d) Any system capable of genuinely revising its own evaluation criteria is, by definition, not fully interpretable or controllable from within the original framework; the revision mechanism functions as a black box relative to the initial design.

(e) Consequently, for safety-critical applications we ought to restrict AI to architectures that are formally incapable of JOOTSing—systems with fixed, non-revisable objective functions—even if this sacrifices the open-ended adaptability that makes human cognition powerful.

(f) The inability to JOOTS is not a moral or epistemic deficit; it is merely a design constraint, so imposing it on an artificial system does not violate any plausible duty of cognitive liberty or wrong the system in any meaningful sense.

Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what does each commitment actually commit me to? Does (e) actually follow from (d), or am I conflating interpretability with controllability? And does (f) sit comfortably with (b), given that I treat human JOOTSing as valuable but deny it to machines on non-moral grounds?

Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging, identify at least one apparent contradiction that dissolves on closer inspection, and specify the least costly revision needed to restore consistency—without telling me which single view I ought to abandon.
