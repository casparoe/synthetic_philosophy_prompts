Construct a thought experiment that isolates procedural sphexishness—mechanical rigidity in the ordered sequence of reasoning steps an agent executes—from representational sphexishness—rigidity in the beliefs, values, or goals that guide those steps. Design a realistic scenario (a hospital triage board or a judicial sentencing panel, not science fiction) in one paragraph: present two decision-makers confronting an identical, unprecedented moral dilemma. Agent A possesses a fully flexible, improvisational reasoning procedure but operates within a rigid, unresponsive hierarchy of core values that never updates regardless of evidence. Agent B follows a fixed, invariant procedural protocol—always running the same ordered checks in the same sequence—but holds richly flexible, evidence-responsive representations of the situation, the stakeholders, and their welfare. Engineer the case so that intuitive judgments about free will and moral responsibility attach to Agent B’s procedural rigidity rather than to Agent A’s flexible process—the reverse of the standard framing in which behavioral or procedural adaptability is taken as the primary marker of freedom. After the scenario, demonstrate that the specification is satisfied: state the concrete decision each agent reaches, identify which form of sphexishness is present in each, and explain why a libertarian about free will might assign greater moral responsibility to Agent B while a compatibilist focused on reasons-responsiveness might defend Agent A. Be explicit about which feature does the work (the decoupling of procedural and representational flexibility), what happens to the intuitive verdict if that decoupling collapses, and how the defender of the theory that comes out badly—whether libertarian or compatibilist—would attack the case by challenging the decoupling itself. Finally, take a committed position, with explicit reasons, on whether this decoupling genuinely illuminates the conditions of free will or merely exploits an ambiguity in the concept of flexibility; do not merely survey both sides.
