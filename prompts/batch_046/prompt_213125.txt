My colleague and I have spent the better part of a year arguing about the Sleeping Beauty problem and the nature of self-locating belief. She defends the “thirder” view—that upon awakening Beauty ought to assign credence one-third to Heads—while I defend the “halfer” view that her credence should remain one-half. We want a joint adversarial-collaboration statement we could both sign.

Please draft the document. It should begin with a numbered section of agreed claims: about the formal protocol, about what self-locating belief is, about the evidential symmetry between Monday and Tuesday awakenings under Tails, and about the conditions under which the Principal Principle applies to centred possibilities. Then present a disagreements table with precise formulations of our residual dispute, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording—particularly whether indexical evidence introduces a novel epistemic reason that alters objective chance, or merely repositions an unchanged prior across centred worlds.

Next, identify the cruxes. I take one to be whether a centred indifference principle must hold across observer-moments linked by psychological continuity; another concerns a modified case in which Beauty receives a private, non-verbal sensory cue present on one Monday awakening but absent on Tuesday, where she knows the cue’s correlation with the coin outcome but does not know which awakening she is in. For each crux, specify the concrete case or argument both of us agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about Beauty’s rational credence upon experiencing the cue versus upon its absence.

Please begin your response with the joint statement itself, with no preface or commentary. Write in clear, formal British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
