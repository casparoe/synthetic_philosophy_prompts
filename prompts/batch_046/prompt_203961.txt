Design an in-class simulation for a philosophy seminar of about 16 postgraduate students, lasting 60 minutes, that makes participants experience the tension between relational and intrinsic conceptions of personhood through the lens of Ubuntu. The activity should embed the idea in rules and incentives rather than explaining it: participants should discover for themselves that moral obligations and distributive claims appear to shift when relational links are altered, even though intrinsic properties of the parties remain constant.

Inject the following conceptual twist: include a graduated withdrawal mechanism by which relational recognition can be partially suspended rather than fully revoked, producing intermediate zones in which obligations become ambiguous rather than clearly present or absent. The rules must compel participants to make binding distributive choices inside these zones before the facilitator identifies what is occurring.

Specify the setting and materials; the rules and a non-monetary payoff or stake scheme; the timeline; what participants should come away seeing; a debrief that maps moments in the simulation onto Ubuntu’s relational ontology and onto the problem of partial recognition; an honest assessment of which feature of lived communal practice the simulation cannot reproduce and how the facilitator must supply it; and a frank prediction of what will go wrong in the first run together with a remedy.
