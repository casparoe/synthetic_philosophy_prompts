You are a professional philosopher of religion with expertise in Wittgenstein's later philosophy and the analytic philosophy of religion. Before drafting anything, take a moment to think carefully and briefly brainstorm the main conceptual pressure points in the interpretation described below—where it is strongest, where it risks collapsing into either fideism or social constructivism, and what standard defensive moves are available to its advocate.

I host a philosophy podcast and will be interviewing an unnamed contemporary philosopher who defends a specific, somewhat niche reading of Wittgenstein's scattered remarks on religious belief. Her view is that religious language operates according to a depth grammar—acquired through ritual initiation and practices of reverence rather than through propositional instruction—which renders certain central propositions non-negotiable within the practice without treating them as empirical hypotheses. The regulating picture in religious life is therefore neither a falsifiable claim, nor a subjective emotional expression, nor a contingent social convention; it is a form-of-life structure with internal criteria of sense that resist both scientific refutation and reduction to ethics.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that press well beyond asking her to restate this position. The sequence must include:

- At least one question on how this depth-grammar account distinguishes genuine religious commitment from mere ideological stubbornness or dogmatism, paired with a follow-up that anticipates her likely appeal to internal criteria of reverence and then presses on whether those criteria risk circularity.

- At least one question on what happens when a long-practicing believer genuinely abandons the faith—whether the grammar dissolves, survives as a ghost framework, or was never truly internalized—paired with a follow-up keyed to her probable claim that true abandonment reveals the grammar was never genuinely acquired, asking what observable behavioral or linguistic difference would adjudicate that claim.

- At least one question on whether ritual initiation can do the epistemic work she assigns it, given that many people participate in rituals without ever developing religious belief, paired with a follow-up anticipating her distinction between external participation and internal uptake and pressing on how that distinction is drawn without smuggling in the propositional content she insists is not primary.

Also include two comparative questions: one asking how her picture-belief reading differs from standard analytic non-cognitivist interpretations of religious language, and one asking whether her view allows that two incompatible religious traditions can both be grammatically sound within their respective forms of life, or whether there is a hidden coherence criterion that privileges one over the other.

Conclude with one question asking what kind of experience, conceptual shift, or evidence could, in principle, undermine her interpretation of Wittgenstein's religious remarks themselves—rather than merely undermining religious belief.

Keep all questions substantive and tightly keyed to her position. Do not include generic biographical, career, or motivational questions. Do not request bibliographies, citation lists, recommended readings, or references to specific passages by page or edition, and do not ask her to quote any text verbatim.
