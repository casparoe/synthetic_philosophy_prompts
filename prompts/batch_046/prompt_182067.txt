I am presenting at a metaphysics workshop on relational ontology and identity. My thesis is that Black’s two-sphere universe is not a counterexample to the Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles (PII), but rather reveals that PII should be reformulated for pure relational spaces: the spheres are not numerically distinct individuals but one entity with a bifurcated spatiotemporal profile—analogous to a single worldline that splits and rejoins in a closed spatial loop—so that the two-meter separation is an internal relational property of that one entity, not a bridge between two relata. I sketch this in three moves: first, I deny container space, adopting relationalism about spatial position; second, I argue that with no other bodies present, the only available structure is a single loop of length two meters, instantiable once with multiplicity; third, I claim weak discernibility via irreflexive relations fails because the relation “being separated by distance d” is symmetric and applies reflexively across the bifurcated parts, yielding no non-qualitative distinction.

The audience is specific. Two Leibnizians defend standard PII through weak discernibility and are steeped in debates about quantum identity; they will treat my denial of container space as question-begging. One philosopher of physics holds that exact symmetry is physically unrealizable—quantum vacuum fluctuations or gravitational back-reaction would break it—so my scenario describes no possible world. One ontic structural realist believes relations are prior to relata and therefore sees two nodes in the relational graph, concluding that PII is false but for reasons diametrically opposite to mine. There are also two agnostic graduate students who will ask for clarification.

Prepare me for the objections in the order they will likely arise, phrased as each camp would phrase them. For each: identify the speaker’s likely vocabulary, whether I should concede a point, where I should hold firm, and how to reply in under two minutes. Mark which objections carry real force and which are rhetorical. Identify my weakest move—the point I should defend actively rather than hope they overlook—and include the objection I am secretly hoping nobody asks, with a prepared response. Keep the prep sheet practical, not a full defense essay.
