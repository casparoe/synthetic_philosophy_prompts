I am a retired condensed-matter physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science systematically, and I am drafting a formal proposal for a one-semester independent study—deliverable: one 6,000-word essay—to be submitted to a university philosophy department’s visiting-scholar program. Rather than revisit the familiar debate about whether structural realism resolves the pessimistic meta-induction by preserving mathematical form across radical theory change in fundamental physics, I want to examine a different phenomenon: within a single mature theoretical framework, the same mathematical structures—specifically topological invariants that classify quantum phases—persist across enormous variations in the microscopic Hamiltonian. This is synchronic robustness across parameter space, not diachronic survival across historical theory replacement. The precise question is whether this internal structural persistence is an instance of the same epistemic relation that structural realists invoke when they claim we know only structure across theory change, and if the two phenomena come apart, whether structural realism must be restricted to history, expanded to cover internal theoretical architecture, or reconceived altogether. Existing philosophical treatments leave this open because they treat structural realism almost exclusively as a response to the history of science, not as an account of robustness within one theory; physicists, meanwhile, treat topological persistence as a calculational fact and do not ask what realist commitments it entails. I will not take the obvious literature-survey approach. Instead, I will use conceptual analysis anchored in three physical cases—the quantum Hall effect, symmetry-protected topological phases, and decoupling in effective field theory—to construct a criterion that distinguishes diachronic structural survival from synchronic parameter-space robustness, then apply that criterion to determine the scope of structural realist commitment. A successful result yields a determinate philosophical verdict with clear failure conditions: specifically, if the distinction collapses and reveals that structural realism either overreaches by treating engineering stability as epistemic structure or underreaches by missing a genuine form of structural knowledge internal to theory. The project fails if the physics requires technical machinery I cannot present from first principles, or if the philosophical distinction proves purely verbal with no consequence for realist belief about unobservable entities. Given my background I can supply physical descriptions without external references; I do not want a bibliography, citation list, or literature review by specific reference, and I will add citations myself. The proposal should state the question with full precision, justify the non-standard synchronic angle rather than the standard historical one, outline the three-case method with a realistic timeline (approximately one month per case, plus one month for synthesis and writing), specify exactly what the 6,000-word deliverable will contain, assess scope risks honestly—including the risk that the distinction is too subtle for a single semester—and conclude with a one-sentence summary of the core research question enclosed in \answer{...}. Please write the proposal in a formal, polished register appropriate to a visiting-scholar application.
