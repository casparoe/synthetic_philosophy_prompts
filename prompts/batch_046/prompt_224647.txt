I'm trying to think through Quine's indeterminacy of translation beyond the textbook "gavagai" vignette—specifically the version that arises when a learning system has access only to stimulus distributions and predictive loss, with no privileged channel to speaker intentions or ontological commitments. I'd like a poem in blank verse, delivered as exactly five stanzas with no lines outside them, each stanza running one or two sentences. Use a refrain that alternates between "rabbit" and "undetached rabbit-part," arranged so that by the fifth stanza the two phrases rhyme or fuse into a single ambiguous line readable either way. Let the meter and rhyme do the philosophical work: the same phonetic input should be parsable into two incompatible ontologies, leaving no behavioral or acoustic residue that decides between them. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows Quine's argument about ontological relativity and the inscrutability of reference recognizes the point, but it should not read like a summary—instead, the form itself should enact the underdetermination.
