Having spent the greater part of my career in the experimental study of open, dissipative thermodynamic systems, I have recently turned my attention to philosophy of mind and artificial intelligence, where I keep encountering the concept of embedded agency. The treatments I have read tend to present embeddedness chiefly as an epistemic limitation: an agent situated inside a larger system cannot construct a complete, objective model of that system because it lacks any external vantage point. Yet this characterization strikes me as barely distinguishable from the familiar predicament of every experimentalist—namely, that measurement perturbs the phenomenon observed, a difficulty we routinely correct for through instrumentation and modeling without invoking any special metaphysics of agency.

What I am attempting to understand is whether embedded agency carries a stronger, structural claim: that the agent’s own reasoning processes—its computations, its physical substrate, its continuous dissipation of energy—are not merely interfering with observation but are literally constituent elements of the causal dynamics being modeled. If that is so, then the problem seems to exceed the standard frame problem, which concerns the difficulty of knowing which environmental variables require updating after an action. Instead, it would involve a reflexive causation in which the update rule itself is among the variables subject to alteration. I find this distinction elusive, particularly since one could always redescribe the situation in purely epistemic terms by saying the agent merely lacks access to its own complete state description.

Could you provide a careful conceptual explanation of this issue? I would like you to clarify whether embedded agency introduces a problem that is genuinely irreducible to ordinary measurement disturbance or to the frame problem, and if there is such an irreducible structure, whether it derives primarily from physical entanglement, from logical self-reference, or from some interaction between the two. I am not seeking a survey of authors or texts, but rather an analysis that takes seriously the intuition—drawn perhaps from my background in non-equilibrium dynamics—that an agent maintaining its own boundary conditions is not passively observing a system but is continuously performing physical work upon it, and that this performance might alter the very criteria by which it decides what requires observation next.
