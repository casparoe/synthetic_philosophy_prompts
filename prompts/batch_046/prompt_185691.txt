I sit on the Franchise and Membership Subcommittee of our university’s Constitutional Convention, which must recommend by next Friday the exact franchise for a binding referendum on restructuring the institution’s governance into a stakeholder model. The referendum asks whether students, staff, faculty, and local residents should share formal decision-making authority over the university’s endowment and land use. The philosophical question is unavoidable: who belongs to the demos? We are divided between an expansive “all-affected” view that would enfranchise temporary international students, local non-citizen workers, and even alumni whose donations shape the budget; a restrictive “co-subjection” view that limits the vote to those presently bound by the university’s disciplinary and employment rules; and an unusual proposal, submitted by a philosophy faculty member, that we should appoint “ecological proxies” — representatives of the watershed and of future generations — on the ground that irreversible land-use decisions affect beings who cannot vote for themselves. I need a formal written opinion, suitable for circulation before Thursday’s plenary session, that recommends a concrete, administrable franchise with clear eligibility criteria, justifies that choice by engaging seriously with the strongest argument from each of the other two positions, and states plainly which of those arguments is the most dangerous to my recommendation.

The memo should treat the boundary problem not as an abstract taxonomy but as a design constraint: whatever principle we adopt must be defensible to those it excludes without collapsing into arbitrariness. In particular, please address whether excluding the ecological proxies undermines the democratic legitimacy of the vote, given that the referendum itself concerns environmental commitments embedded in the charter. At the same time, the recommendation must be actionable — specify who registers, how long residency or enrollment counts, and whether alumni retain a vote — rather than ending in a theoretical stalemate. Please also include, in a brief closing section, what specific finding or argument would cause you to change the recommendation: for instance, if empirical evidence showed that temporary residents consistently bear the externalities of university expansion, or if the proxy proposal turned out to be administratively workable without diluting individual accountability. Finally, flag the point in your reasoning that is most likely to fail, especially regarding the line you draw between present co-subjects and merely affected outsiders.
