I am defending, in a philosophy colloquium, a thesis on Plato’s Meno problem: knowledge’s surplus value over mere true belief is neither the stability Plato describes (the tethered statue) nor an intrinsic virtue of the agent, but a normative-functional status—knowledge licenses the knower to close practical deliberation and to serve as a testimonial source without retaining what I call “epistemic insurance,” that is, without holding back a hedge against possible error. The audience is split between virtue epistemologists, who locate epistemic value in the manifestation of intellectual character, and formal epistemologists together with Bayesian decision theorists, who hold that rational action requires only true belief calibrated to high credence and that any further “value” must reduce to practical utility. Both camps are committed to keeping epistemic value non-reducible to mere instrumental success, but they disagree on whether it resides in the agent or in the belief-state.

Prepare my Q&A sheet: list the objections in the order they will actually come, phrased first in the vocabulary the virtue epistemologists will use and then in that of the formalists, marking which carry real force, what I should concede upfront, what I must hold, and giving a concise reply for each with a note on where the reply is softest. Include the objection I am hoping nobody raises—likely that my account collapses knowledge-value into social utility or makes knowledge contingent on audience acceptance. Keep the prep focused on the live argumentative exchange, not on literature surveys or reading lists.
