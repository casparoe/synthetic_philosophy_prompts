Write a ten-minute opening address for the inaugural session of a workshop titled "Agency, Harm, and Institutional Judgment," convened in honor of a retiring professor of legal philosophy who spent her career arguing that criminal law's retributive language assumes a metaphysical picture of the agent that cannot survive careful analysis. The audience is deliberately mixed: metaphysicians and philosophers of action, criminal-law scholars, and practitioners—including victim advocates and a sitting judge—who have no formal training in philosophy. The philosophical idea must do real structural work: center the speech on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against ultimate moral responsibility, specifically the claim that being genuinely responsible for what one does requires being genuinely responsible for the way one is, which in turn requires being genuinely responsible for the causes of one's nature, generating an infinite regress that no finite agent can complete, and therefore no one is ultimately responsible in the desert-entailing sense required by pure retributivism. Explain this with conceptual precision but without technical jargon, and then use it to constrain the workshop's proceedings: if the argument holds, any defense of sentencing or blame that appeals to just deserts must either deny that such deserts require ultimate self-origination, or must relocate normative justification onto institutional procedures rather than onto the metaphysical status of the offender. The address must not offer false consolation to the non-philosophers; it should acknowledge that harm remains real and that institutional responses remain necessary, even if their justification can no longer be unqualified retributivism. To keep the prompt from being a generic summary of Strawson, inject one specific conceptual extension: apply the Basic Argument's recursive structure to collective decision-making bodies—ask whether a sentencing board or jury can escape the regress by distributing agency across members, or whether collective self-determination merely reproduces the problem at the group level, since no individual member authored the collective's deliberative norms and no collective can be causa sui. Write formally and polished, suitable for an academic ceremony, and do not include requests for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
