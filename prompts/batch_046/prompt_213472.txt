A friend who read some philosophy of perception summarized Molyneux's problem to me like this: "Molyneux asks whether a person blind from birth who has learned to distinguish a cube and a sphere by touch could immediately tell them apart by sight alone if vision were suddenly restored. Locke said no, on the grounds that tactile and visual ideas share no intrinsic structural content, so there is nothing in the visual experience to map onto the tactile idea until experience builds an association. Modern studies of cataract patients have completely vindicated this: subjects initially fail at cross-modal shape matching, and only after months of exposure do they learn, which demonstrates that perceptual modalities are informationally encapsulated in Fodor's sense and rules out any innate amodal spatial representations." I feel like the leap from "they fail at first" to "modular encapsulation" might be too quick, and I'm not sure Locke's reasoning was exactly about shared structural content. Is this summary fair?
