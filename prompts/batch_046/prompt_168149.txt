i run a loose federation of about twelve local repair cafes — we share a name and a bit of kit but each one sets its own seperate rules. lately members keep pitching changes they could push through on their own: one cafe wants to start charging entry fees, another wants to partner with a hardware chain, another wants to drop all electrical repairs. each sounds ok if it's just them, but if every cafe did it the whole thing falls apart or becomes something we never agreed to be. at the same time, good ideas stall because everyone waits for someone else to try them first.

i've been thinking about this "unilateralist's curse" thing — where if each of us can act alone and our beliefs about what's best are a bit different, the worst option is the one most likely to happen somewhere — but i also think there's a shadow version for inaction: if we all wait for consensus, nothing moves and the network rots. i want a proper decision procedure i can actually run when a proposal lands on my desk, not just a vague warning.

write me a reusable checklist: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a hard no, and where it hands over to "this is a values call, not a fact." it needs to cover both the action-curse and the inaction-curse i mentioned, and it should say what to do when i can't put numbers on probabilities — which is always, since i'm not running a maths department. fix the stakes: i get maybe twenty minutes, i have to sign off or reject, and the other cafes don't have to obey me but they watch what i approve.

then run it on three cases:

1. one cafe wants to partner with a big retail chain for funding and branding — fine for them alone, but if all twelve did it we'd basically be a franchise.

2. one wants to ban all power-tool repairs unilaterally for safety — if everyone did it we'd be useless, but if nobody does, someone eventually gets hurt.

3. one wants to open a new branch in a neighbouring town using our shared name but without asking anyone — they say it's growth, others say it's theft.

include one where the procedure gives an answer i won't like, and flag the spots where the checklist genuinely can't decide — where two people running it could come out differently and that's not a mistake, just the limit.

keep it practical. i don't need a reading list or anything i can't check without google, and don't quote philosophers at me — just tell me what the idea actually says and turn it into steps.
