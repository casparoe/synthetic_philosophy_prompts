I'm on the clinical ethics board at Mercy General, and we're stuck on a case that's turning into a philosophy seminar nobody signed up for. There's a patient, L., in an experimental trial where her neurons are being replaced one by one with synthetic analogs—functional copies, not just implants—because she has this degenerative condition. By now about 60% is synthetic, and the protocol forces us to pick one continuity subject for consent and end-of-life calls: either the biological remnant or the integrated synthetic-biological system. If we pick the biological side, we pull the synthetic implants and she probably dies in the conventional sense; if we pick the integrated side, we stop supporting the biological body and basically let that part go. Her family is split—her sister says she's still in there, just rebuilt, her brother says you killed her and kept a copy running. The trial's philosophical consultant threw around Parfit-style branching language, saying psychological continuity has split from biological continuity, and now half the board thinks we're in a teletransporter scenario where what matters is psychological continuity, not bodily survival. The other half says this is just modified tissue, one patient, and we shouldn't invent metaphysical problems. I need a memo for Thursday recommending a concrete course of action with an actual timeline—like do we pause the trial, continue with conditions, or designate one authority now?—and I want it to take the survival question seriously rather than just quoting legal brain-death criteria. Can you also address whether survival can come in degrees here, or if that's a cop-out?
