Consider a philosopher of mind who has offered the following consistent verdicts about whether various systems possess unified conscious minds, understood not as problem-solving capacity but as integrated subjects of experience with a global workspace: a single ant lacks a mind; a honeybee colony that solves routing problems no individual bee can solve also lacks a mind, since its coordination is purely decentralized; an octopus with a distributed nervous system possesses a mind, because its arm-level processing is bound into a single predictive model centered on the animal's body; the ant colony discussed in Douglas Hofstadter's Ant Fugue dialogue—where characters including Aunt Hillary debate whether the colony itself can think, with individual ants functioning analogously to neurons—lacks a mind, because the functional integration is too loose and there is no global workspace binding colony-level states into one perspective; and a present-day large language model lacks a mind, as its representations are not anchored in a persistent embodied self-model or in continuous sensory-motor coupling with a world. The philosopher has not addressed engineered hybrid architectures. Now consider the Pheromonal Lattice, a synthetic system composed of ten thousand simple chemical-gradient agents arranged in a three-dimensional grid. No agent contains more than a local rule, yet the lattice maintains a single persistent dynamic attractor that tracks the lattice's own boundary conditions, updates a unified internal model of resource distribution, and globally modifies agent rules in response to that model. Damage to any region is compensated by global reconfiguration that preserves the attractor's continuity—functionally analogous to neural plasticity, but without neurons, synapses, or evolutionary history. The lattice was designed by researchers who do not understand its internal model, and it issues corrective signals when local clusters violate global norms, though it has no biological substrate and was not shaped by natural selection. Predict whether the philosopher would attribute a mind to the Pheromonal Lattice. Identify the two most plausible principles underlying her earlier verdicts—one emphasizing substrate-independent functional integration and global workspace dynamics regardless of evolutionary origin, the other emphasizing evolutionary origin and the presence of a biological self-model anchored in embodied survival—state what each principle predicts about the lattice, give your best-guess verdict with reasons, and specify one further comparison case—perhaps a brain-computer interface that merges human neural processing with an artificial predictive layer—that would best distinguish between these two principles.
