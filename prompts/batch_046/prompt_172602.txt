The diachronic-system reply: the mind is not a single static formal system F, but an open-ended sequence F₁, F₂, F₃..., each of which can derive the consistency of its predecessor; therefore Gödel's second theorem applies only to each individual Fᵢ, not to the evolving totality, and no contradiction arises.

The soundness-circularity reply: Gödel's second incompleteness theorem requires that the system be not merely consistent but also sound (or at least ω-consistent in the relevant form); since we cannot establish the soundness of our own cognitive architecture without circular appeal to that very architecture, the mind's confidence in its own consistency gives us no special logical leverage over a machine.

The heuristic-vs-proof reply: human recognition of arithmetical consistency is typically intuitive, fallible, and non-constructive, not a formal derivation within a precisely axiomatized system; comparing this psychological capacity with the derivability relation ⊢ in a formal system commits a category error.

The formalization-gap reply: even if human cognition outstrips any particular formalization, this only demonstrates that no single consistent formal system F captures all human mathematical reasoning; it does not entail that the underlying physical or cognitive processes are non-computable in principle.
