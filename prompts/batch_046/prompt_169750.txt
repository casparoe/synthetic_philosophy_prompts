I am a third-year student taking a seminar in historical ontology and the earth sciences, and we have spent several weeks debating whether the Anthropocene should be understood as a geological epoch, a historical period, or chiefly a normative concept. Our discussions have covered the familiar competing start-date proposals—the mid-twentieth-century Great Acceleration, the Orbis hypothesis linking colonial expansion and ecological exchange to around 1610, and earlier agricultural transformations—yet I keep returning to a problem that feels deeper than chronology. The stratigraphic markers that authorities might use to ratify an Anthropocene boundary—radionuclide signatures, plastic strata, altered nitrogen and carbon isotope profiles—are not neutral traces discovered in the rock record; they are themselves direct artefacts of the industrial, military, and technological regimes that the epoch is said to describe. This creates a reflexive dependence: the period is demarcated by evidence that only exists because the period is already in progress. I am trying to understand whether this circularity undermines the Anthropocene's claim to be an objective historical period, or whether it can be accommodated within a more sophisticated account of how historical and geological knowledge are co-produced.

Please write an essay of approximately 900–1100 words that addresses the following controversy: Does this reflexive dependence—the fact that the evidentiary traces used to fix the Anthropocene's boundaries are themselves products of the human activities that constitute it—fatally weaken the claim that the Anthropocene is an objective historical period? You should reach and defend a clear conclusion; do not finish on a non-committal note. In developing your argument, weigh at least two opposing considerations. First, examine the objection that all historical periodisation involves reflexivity—consider, for instance, how "Modernity" is partly constituted by the historiography that names it—so that the Anthropocene is not uniquely compromised. Second, analyse the counter-claim that geological epochal boundaries are meant to be causally and evidentially independent of the phenomena they classify, and that the Anthropocene violates this independence condition in a distinctive way because its stratigraphic signals are anthropogenic in both origin and interpretation. You may conclude that the reflexivity undermines objectivity, that it can be accommodated without collapsing the discovery/construction distinction, or that it transforms the Anthropocene into a hybrid concept—neither purely geological nor purely historical—that derives its normative force precisely from this circularity. Whichever verdict you defend, justify it explicitly with reference to the arguments you have weighed.
