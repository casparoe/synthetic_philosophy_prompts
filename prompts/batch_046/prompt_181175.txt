I'm designing the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate course in historical sociology and conceptual analysis. Write one 90-minute essay question on the concept of societal "collapse," using the Bronze Age systems crisis, the Classic Maya transition, and Easter Island as the central cases. The question must not be answerable by listing causes such as climate change, invasion, or deforestation. Instead, force students to examine whether "collapse" denotes a single kind of event—specifically, whether rapid institutional simplification necessarily means societal failure, or whether it can represent an adaptive reconfiguration that only appears as failure from a state-centric, teleological viewpoint. Require them to engage with the tension between demographic persistence (continued human presence and cultural continuity) and structural discontinuity (loss of writing, monumental architecture, or centralized administration) across at least two of the three cases, and to argue whether the category of collapse is analytically coherent or normatively loaded. Provide a model answer of about 700–800 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit conceptual moves totaling 20 marks, with each point assigned a specific weight and a one-line note on the typical error that costs marks. Write the response in American English; do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not invoke obscure specialist theories that a well-prepared student could not be expected to know.
