She contends that Machiavelli’s river metaphor—where fortune is compared to a river that can be contained by dikes—should be read not as an argument for individual mastery over an external nature, but as a claim that republican constitutions must be engineered with redundant, modular mechanisms that redirect internal entropy before it becomes catastrophic. She further maintains that Machiavelli’s gendered comparison of fortune to a woman who must be beaten and subdued has been systematically misread by modern liberal commentators as a normative endorsement of domination, when in fact it operates in his texts as a diagnostic of institutional anxiety: the feminised other appears precisely when institutional plasticity fails and contingency is projected outward.
