I've been toying with the Letter Spirit view that an a is an a not because of some shared geometric feature but because of a relational spirit — each letterform gets its identity from how it contrasts with every other form in the same grid, so the category is built out of the whole system of stylistic differences rather than out of intrinsic shape properties. What woud actually count against this? Rank the defeaters: a formal proof that categorization needs feature extraction, empirical recognition of letters that violate the grid relations, maybe data from small children or people with visual agnosia who still see a-ness without the full relational map? And what would defenders of this view concede is damaging versus what theyd just dismiss as missing the point? Finally, if the claim gets qualified to say the spirit is only one layer among others, is that a rescue or just insulation that makes the view unfalsifiable — and is that a problem for a view like this?
