I’ve been thinking on my own about the special composition question—when parts actually make up one real thing rather than just sitting near each other. The view I’ve settled on is something I’d call interdependent functional integration: parts compose a genuine whole not merely because they touch or are arranged together, but because each part’s role is defined by its relation to the others in such a way that removing any part would cause the remaining parts to lose the specific capacities they had within the arrangement. On this view, a watch is one object because its gears and springs depend on each other to keep time, a scattered pile of sand is not one object because each grain does the same thing regardless of its neighbors, and a human body counts without having to claim that only living or conscious things are real composites. I’m not asking for a literature survey or citations—just an honest assessment of exposure. What would have to be true for this view to be wrong? I want the defeaters mapped out: clear counterexamples where parts are interdependent but still don’t form one thing, or where they clearly form one thing despite lacking that interdependence; arguments from vagueness or from the logic of parthood that show the criterion collapses into either unrestricted composition or mereological nihilism once the details are worked out; and any scientific or formal result suggesting that functional integration can’t bear the metaphysical weight I’m putting on it. I also want to know what this view uniquely predicts about borderline cases—clouds, social groups, distributed computer systems, or organisms with redundant organs—that its rivals don’t predict, and whether those predictions turn out to be strengths or liabilities. Please rank the objections from fatal (they should make me abandon the view) to merely awkward (they require adjustments but don’t destroy the core idea), and say which ones a defender of interdependent functional integration would themselves concede as damaging rather than just dismiss. Finally, be direct about insulation: has this view been qualified in a way that makes it unfalsifiable, and if so, is that a genuine problem for a position about what exists?
