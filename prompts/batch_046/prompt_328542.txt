I'm currently working through a graduate seminar on the philosophy of social science, and our discussion of Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis has settled into a frustrating impasse that I have been unable to diagnose on my own. The disagreement is between two classmates—call them the culturalist and the institutionalist—who have been going back and forth for weeks without either conceding a single point. Both accept the basic historical correlation that regions with strong Calvinist presence tended to develop capitalist economic practices earlier and more intensely. Both also agree that individual religious values genuinely shaped personal motivation; they are not disputing that people believed in predestination or in the idea of a worldly calling. Yet they remain completely stuck on whether "culture" in Weber's sense can properly be said to cause capitalism.

The culturalist argues that the value-commitments functioned as an independent causal lever: by altering the subjective meaning that actors attached to work, thrift, and accumulation, the ethic changed behavioral patterns in a way that material conditions alone cannot explain. The institutionalist counters that this confuses motivation with macro-causation—values are always mediated by property law, credit structures, and labor markets, and that whenever those institutions are present, comparable economic outcomes appear regardless of the dominant theological framework. She adds that treating culture as a cause requires a counterfactual test that social science cannot perform: we cannot hold legal and economic institutions constant while varying only religious values across comparable societies, so the claim "culture caused capitalism" is either empirically untestable or merely a redescription of institutional persistence. My own working hypothesis is that they are not really fighting about history at all, but about two incompatible models of explanation—one that permits reasons and meanings to figure in causal narratives, and one that demands mechanism-level counterfactual dependence mediated by structural conditions. Still, I wonder whether a hidden empirical question is doing the work, specifically about what I would call institutional stickiness: whether capitalist practices, once introduced, persist even when the original value-commitments erode, which might show that values were never the sustaining cause.
