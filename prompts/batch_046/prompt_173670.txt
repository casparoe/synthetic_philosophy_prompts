Design two contrasting clinical vignettes—each one paragraph, set in an ordinary modern healthcare context—engineered so that a biological-function naturalist and a holistic normativist about health deliver exactly opposite verdicts in both cases. In the first vignette, describe a physiological state that is statistically typical and evolutionarily optimal for the organism’s design yet systematically frustrates the patient’s vital goals and produces sustained suffering; in the second, describe a state that is statistically atypical and functionally suboptimal by evolutionary standards yet allows the patient to pursue central life projects with minimal distress. Constraints: no science fiction, no exotic diseases, no mental-illness stereotypes, and no appeal to cultural relativism alone—the divergence must turn on the structural relationship between biological function and vital-goal achievement. After presenting both cases, state the verdict each theory gives for each case and explain why. Then identify the precise feature of the pairing that forces the disagreement, show how a defender of each theory could attack the opposing classification without begging the question, and note whether removing that feature collapses the dispute into a standard disagreement about mental disorder classification.
