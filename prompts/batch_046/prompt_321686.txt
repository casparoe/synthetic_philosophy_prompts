I'm preparing a podcast interview with the author of a recent monograph that extends Douglas Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model—where creative insight arises from continuous adjustments to the parameters of a conceptual space—to the problem of scientific revolutions. Her core thesis is that Kuhnian incommensurability is best understood not as the introduction of entirely new conceptual dimensions, but as a non-linear rotation and rescaling of the axes within an existing space: old measurements fail to map onto the new framework not because the ontology has been replaced, but because the coordinate system has been twisted so radically that linear translation breaks down. She claims this preserves a kind of formal structural continuity beneath apparent discontinuity, and that genuinely creative scientific change is therefore always continuous at the mathematical level, even when practitioners experience it as a rupture.

I need a sequence of six to eight interview questions that press this view precisely where the literature on theory change and conceptual representation is most skeptical. The opening questions should pin down the mechanics: how does axis rotation differ from ordinary parameter adjustment, and what formal criteria distinguish a rotated space from one that has gained new dimensions? The middle of the sequence should anticipate her standard defenses—if she appeals to coordinate transformations that recover old predictions in some limit, ask how she accounts for predictive content that is genuinely eliminated rather than translated, such as the loss of mechanical properties attached to the luminiferous ether after special relativity; if she insists that explanatory power is preserved under rotation, ask whether her framework can distinguish a progressive revolution from a degenerate one in which knobs are twisted without any corresponding gain in predictive or explanatory scope, and whether she can make that distinction without smuggling in normative criteria she claims are external to the model itself. End with a sharp question about falsifiability: what specific conceptual or empirical finding would convince her that some breakthroughs genuinely require the addition of new dimensions rather than a remapping of old ones? Please keep every question focused on the content of her argument—no biographical preamble, no generic prompts about her writing process or influences.
