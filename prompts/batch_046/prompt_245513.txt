Here is an argument my study group constructed that now strikes us as suspicious because it seems to prove that the standard justifications for capital punishment are jointly self-defeating.

(1) Any defense of capital punishment must rest on retribution, deterrence, or both.
(2) Retributively, intentional murder creates a moral debt that only the murderer’s death can cancel; so the state has a duty to execute those who are in fact guilty.
(3) By deterrence, failing to execute a guilty murderer makes the state complicit in future murders that execution might have prevented.
(4) The risk of executing an innocent person is purely procedural: it concerns our epistemic access to guilt, not the actual desert of any particular offender.
(5) We accept what we call the Uniform Risk Principle: if a procedural risk is weighty enough to justify abolishing punishment P for crime C, and the same risk applies to punishment Q for crime D, then consistency demands either abolishing both or neither—unless there is a morally relevant difference.
(6) Every criminal trial carries a non-zero risk of false conviction; long imprisonment for theft or assault carries the same procedural risk, though the harm is reversible.
(7) The only obvious difference is irreversibility: death cannot be corrected, prison can be partly remedied. But irreversibility is a feature of the punishment’s effect, not of the offender’s desert; pure retributivism holds that desert alone fixes the sentence.
(8) So allowing irreversibility to justify abolishing execution lets a feature of the punishment’s effect override desert—violating pure retributivism.
(9) Meanwhile, with fallible procedures, applying capital punishment guarantees a statistical certainty of eventually killing innocents, which violates the state’s basic duty not to kill the innocent.
(10) Therefore, maintaining both retributivism and deterrence requires either accepting that we will knowingly kill innocents, or achieving infallibility; since the latter is impossible, the conjunction seems to collapse into contradiction.
