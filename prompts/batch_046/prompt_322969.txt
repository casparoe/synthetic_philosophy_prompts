Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a philosopher who defends epistemic closure under known entailment and one who defends Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge, focusing specifically on whether the tracking denial of closure stems from modal fragility in the entailment relation itself or from an independent failure of sensitivity conditions on the entailed belief. Present your answer as exactly five numbered items, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. Item one should list the formal and conceptual claims both sides accept about closure principles and tracking conditions. Item two should state the precise disagreement, phrased in wording each camp would endorse, about where exactly the tracking failure is located. Item three should identify the crux—whether modal proximity is evaluated relative to the base belief or to the entailment chain—and indicate what each side takes to hang on it. Item four should present a single concrete diagnostic case both sides agree bears on the crux, including each side's explicit prediction about the case's outcome. Item five should assess whether part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, and summarize the residual disagreement if any verbal portion is removed.
