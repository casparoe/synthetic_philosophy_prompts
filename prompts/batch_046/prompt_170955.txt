I have been asked to design a session for an advanced undergraduate seminar on Spinoza's political philosophy, and I want to avoid the usual slide-deck summary of the Tractatus Politicus by grounding the discussion in a narrative the students already know. Could you read William Golding's Lord of the Flies through the specific lens of Spinoza's democratic theory—not the generic praise of democracy, but his claim that democratic sovereignty is genuinely absolute only when the collective deliberates through common notions rather than through private passions, superstitious fear, or factional interest? The boys' initial conch assembly looks like an attempt at such sovereignty, yet Jack's secession succeeds not merely through violence but by replacing rational collective agreement with an affective bond organized around the hunt, the beast, and ritual cruelty.

Please construct a critical reading that treats the novel as neither a simple illustration of democratic collapse nor a straightforward refutation of Spinoza, but as a dramatization of the conditions under which democratic sovereignty fractures into what Spinoza might call a state of nature concealed within the state. Which scenes—particularly those involving Piggy and Simon—should I highlight to show the active destruction of the multitude's capacity for common reasoning? At the same time, I need you to say honestly where the novel resists this framework: for example, does Ralph's persistent individual moral conscience point to something about personal ethical agency that Spinoza's collective ontology tends to dissolve? Finally, conclude with what the novel contributes to the philosophical debate that pure argument cannot—perhaps something about the affective fragility of common notions under conditions of scarcity, terror, and isolation. I am not looking for a bibliography or secondary literature; I need only the interpretive argument itself, delivered with enough precision that I can present it to students who have read the Ethics but are still finding their way through Spinoza's politics.
