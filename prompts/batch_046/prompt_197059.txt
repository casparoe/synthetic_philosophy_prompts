hey, i've been trying to figure out if virtual objects and virtual harms are actually real in any meaningful sense, and i've got a pile of verdicts that feel right individually but don't sit together nicely. maybe there's a principle i'm missing, or maybe one of my intuitions has to go.

case one: you buy a skin in an mmo with real cash, the server wipes it by mistake, you're out fifty bucks. that's a real loss, full stop. case two: you play a story game, an npc dies tragically, you cry. that's not a real harm — it's like being sad at a movie. case three: in a vr social space, someone spends an hour harassing your avatar with slurs. that feels like real harm, because there's a real person behind it with real intent. case four: there's this thing i call ghost-binding, where an object only exists in your local client because of a desync error. you spend weeks crafting it, then it vanishes when you reconnect. the object itself wasn't real — nobody else ever saw it — but the wasted effort and the emotional hit feel somewhat real, like losing a private diary. case five: two users sign a virtual contract inside the world that is legally enforceable in real court, and one breaks it. that's definitely real harm, tied to law. case six: there's persistent avatar scarring — damage that never heals and permanently lowers your social standing in-world. i think that's more real than temporary damage, but still less real than physical scarring. case seven: your ai companion in a sandbox gets deleted, and you grieve for weeks. i want to call that real grief, but then why isn't crying over the npc?

so i've been playing with principles. maybe it's causal connection to the physical world — that covers the fifty-dollar skin and the legal contract, but it fails for the vr harassment (no physical contact) and maybe for ghost-binding (the object never touched anyone else's reality). maybe it's intersubjective agreement — that gets the skin and the harassment right, but fails for the ai grief (only you experienced it) and maybe for the scars (everyone sees them, but they're still just pixels). maybe it's persistence when you log off — that works for scars and maybe grief, but fails for ghost-binding, which persisted for you alone for weeks.

is there a coherent account that saves all these? if not, which verdict should i drop? and what would the best principle say about virtual property you can sell for crypto that has zero real-world value — real or not?
