Sphexishness—the digger wasp’s rigid, ritualized behavioral loop, long used in philosophy of mind as a foil to human flexibility—now resonates with AI systems that display both mechanical repetition and surprising contextual adaptation. Which open questions at the intersection of sphexish rigidity, flexibility, and free will actually merit concentrated research effort, and which are overrated? I want to focus on three competing directions: whether genuine flexibility requires a unified global workspace or can emerge from competing modular routines; whether sphexish behavior in an artificial agent should block attributions of moral agency or merely redirect them; and whether engineering “anti-sphexish” architectures—systems with genuinely variable, open-ended stopping conditions rather than fixed termination criteria—is tractable enough to justify serious investment. Rank these lines of work and defend the ordering, specifying the criteria you apply—philosophical stakes, empirical tractability, implications for AI governance, or dependencies among them—and identify the single crux or unresolved dependency that, if settled differently, would overturn your ranking. I am writing a long-form piece on where limited research capacity should go in this space, so I need a defended agenda rather than a survey.
