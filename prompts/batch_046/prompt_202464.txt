Please compose a sestina—six six-line stanzas followed by a three-line envoi—intended to close a seminar on meaning in life. The poem should hold open, without resolving, the tension between Camus’s absurdism (the human insistence on rational meaning against an indifferent, silent cosmos) and Susan Wolf’s “fitting fulfillment” view (meaning arises when subjective attraction meets projects of objective value). Use these six end-words in strict sestina rotation: revolt, fitting, silent, value, attract, absurd. Let the formal recurrence do conceptual work: each stanza should stage a different relation between the absurd demand and Wolf’s conditions, so the poem’s cyclical structure enacts the recurring pressure of the absurd rather than synthesizing it. Keep the meter free but rhythmically audible, and let the envoi leave the tension unreconciled. After the poem, include a brief note that separates what is broadly settled in the literature on absurdism and on Wolf’s account from what remains genuinely contested, and explicitly flag any simplifications or interpretive liberties the poem takes with either thinker—do not present a disputed reading as consensus.
