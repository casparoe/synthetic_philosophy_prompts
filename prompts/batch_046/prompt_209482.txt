I am a retired condensed-matter physicist who, after leaving the lab, have begun auditing courses in philosophy of mind. In my old work we treated temperature as an emergent macroscopic property with an unambiguous zero point and calibration protocol; now I find philosophers comparing Integrated Information Theory's Φ to a thermometer for consciousness—claiming that, just as mercury expansion tracks heat without requiring us to solve the metaphysics of thermal energy, Φ tracks the quantity of consciousness without requiring us to settle whether it is physical, functional, or phenomenal. The comparison is being used, especially by researchers who want to sidestep debates about subjective report, to argue that IIT is uniquely empirical: measure the integration, read off the level of awareness, and rank organisms objectively. Before I grant that, I would like you to serve as an expert philosopher of science and audit the analogy in detail. Please begin with some careful brainstorming, then present an explicit correspondence table—what in IIT plays the role of the thermometer instrument, the thermal reservoir, the calibration against a standard, the absolute zero, and the units of measurement? Then move to the disanalogies that bear specifically on the empirical-objectivity claim: temperature measurements do not depend on an observer-chosen grain of analysis or on a boundary-drawing decision that excludes overlapping subsystems, whereas Φ is notoriously sensitive to both; temperature is inter-subjectively accessible in a way that requires no theory-laden interpretation of the measuring device, whereas computing Φ requires accepting IIT's axiomatic framework. Separate the breaks that merely weaken the analogy from the ones that actually undermine the conclusion it was meant to license. Finally, state whether the "purely quantitative, conceptually neutral" defense survives once these disanalogies are on the table, and if it does not, propose a more apt physical-science analogy—perhaps one involving an order parameter near a phase transition—that captures IIT's actual methodological status without importing false objectivity. Think step by step, and be precise about where the mapping fails and what a better comparison would preserve.
