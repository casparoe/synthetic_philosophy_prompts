Since my mother’s death six months ago, I have been unable to settle whether my promise to maintain our family home binds me with the same force now that she is no longer present to hold me to it. This has led me back to the metaphysics of time. Compare presentism and eternalism specifically with respect to three issues: (i) whether a deceased person retains any ontological status that could serve as the present subject of an obligation; (ii) whether future descendants who might inhabit the home have a stronger claim to its preservation than the dead do; and (iii) which view better accommodates the intuition that breaking a promise to the dead is a different kind of wrong from breaking a promise to someone who does not yet exist. In particular, does the eternalist’s commitment to the equal reality of past and future persons undermine the moral asymmetry we feel between obligations to ancestors and obligations to descendants? Give your answer in one tightly argued paragraph of no more than 250 words, and do not provide citations, reading lists, or bibliographic references.
