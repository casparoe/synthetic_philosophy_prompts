Having spent thirty-five years in condensed-matter physics before turning, in retirement, to philosophy of history, I am struck by how eagerly macrohistorians borrow the language of natural science without submitting to its discipline. Please write a satirical abstract and brief results section—approximately 600 words, in the deadpan register of a Physical Review Letters submission—for a fictitious study entitled “Phase Transitions in the Social Lattice: An Ising-Model Treatment of Elite Overproduction and State Collapse in Turchin-Style Secular Cycles.” The piece should be voiced in the first-person plural of an imaginary interdisciplinary collective and should parody the practice of importing statistical-mechanics formalisms—order parameters, critical exponents, mean-field approximations—into cliodynamics, exaggerating rather than inventing the real features of structural-demographic theory: the lag between economic immiseration and political crisis, intra-elite competition as a saturation phenomenon, and the cyclical breakdown of state capacity. Invent but present with full laboratory seriousness two quantitative constructs: an “Elite Saturation Index” derived from bureaucratic patent filings and administrative appointments, and a “Legitimacy Correlation Length” measuring the spatial decay of sovereign authority across provincial nodes. Frame historical agency as spin-state alignment, treat culture as a perturbative Hamiltonian, and report “excellent agreement” between model predictions and post-hoc cycle fitting without ever commenting on circularity. The satire should allow a specialist in either field to recognize that the author understands both well enough to mock their collision; the standard objections—reduction of human choice to lattice dynamics, the unfalsifiability of retrospective parameter tuning, the silent elimination of normative and cultural variables—should be visible only in the gaps between the equations. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Maintain sustained deadpan irony throughout: no winking, no explicit acknowledgment that the exercise is absurd.
