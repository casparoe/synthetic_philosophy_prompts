The discussion turned on a precise exam question I would like to test: Explain the central commitment shared by deflationary theories of truth, contrast that commitment with the correspondence theory's relational account, and evaluate whether deflationism can genuinely remain metaphysically neutral—particularly in view of the claim that truth serves an indispensable explanatory function in arguments for scientific realism, such as accounting for predictive success without appealing to miracles, and whether this forces deflationists to treat mathematical or normative truths differently from empirical ones. A participant submitted the following response, which I am now grading informally as part of the session's exercises.

Deflationary theories, associated with thinkers such as Ramsey, Ayer, and Horwich, maintain that the predicate "is true" performs no substantive descriptive or explanatory labor. To say that a statement is true is merely to reassert the statement itself, as formalized by disquotational schemas of the form "S is true if and only if S." This stands in opposition to correspondence theories, which treat truth as a robust relational property linking propositions or beliefs to mind-independent states of affairs. Because deflationism eliminates any such relation, it avoids metaphysical commitments about the nature of correspondence—whether causal, structural, or otherwise. However, this very elimination means deflationism collapses into anti-realism: if asserting that snow is white is true adds nothing to asserting that snow is white, then there is no independent reality against which our claims are measured, and correspondence talk becomes entirely redundant. Furthermore, the deflationist must deny that truth applies to non-empirical domains such as mathematics and ethics, since disquotation requires a direct empirical anchor; without observable content, the schema cannot operate. Thus deflationism achieves its neutrality only by implicitly restricting truth-apt discourse to observational sentences, which undermines its ambition to provide a fully general theory.
