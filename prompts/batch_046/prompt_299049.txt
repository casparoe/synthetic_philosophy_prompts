Given Gödel's mathematical Platonism and his insistence that time is genuinely real rather than a coordinate artefact or psychological illusion, do you think he would have welcomed pre-geometric temporalism as preserving the structural significance of his solution, or condemned it as denying the very objectivity of time he sought to defend? Please treat this as a reasoned conjecture, not a historical report. If, however, my framing rests on a confused premise—if Gödel's commitments are too indeterminate to support any coherent answer to such a hypothetical, or if I have misrepresented either his cosmological argument or the modern quantum-gravity position—then say so plainly rather than constructing a plausible-sounding fiction.
