Provide your credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that a cognitive system operating as a purely Skinnerian creature—capable only of reward-driven behavioral selection with no internal representational models or hypothesis-testing—could, through sustained linguistic and social scaffolding, exhibit genuinely Gregorian capacities such as cumulative cultural transmission, intentional pedagogy, or deceptive symbol use, without ever instantiating the Popperian level of mental modeling. Decompose this into four judgments: the probability that Gregorian function necessarily depends on prior Popperian architecture; the probability that environmental and linguistic feedback could substitute for that internal architecture; the probability that behaviorally equivalent outputs would nevertheless lack the functional structure of true Gregorian minds; and the strength of the evolutionary-continuity argument that Darwinian selection must yield Skinnerian learning before either Popperian or Gregorian capacities can emerge. For each judgment supply a rough numerical value with one line of justification, combine them into an overall credence, and identify which judgment is most vulnerable to empirical or conceptual revision and what specific finding would shift it. Respond in exactly five numbered items, each comprising one or two sentences and lacking any introduction or conclusion: item 1 states the overall credence and how the terms combine; items 2 through 4 present the first three decomposed probabilities; item 5 presents the evolutionary-continuity weight, names the least stable term, and specifies the revising evidence.
