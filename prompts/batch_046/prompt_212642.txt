I just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a close friend about Spinoza’s democratic theory in the Theological-Political Treatise. She kept pressing the point that Spinoza’s account is structurally contradictory: he presents democracy as the form of government closest to the state of nature, the one that best preserves every individual’s natural right to think and speak freely, yet he simultaneously restricts active citizenship to free adult males who are neither servants nor foreigners, effectively excluding the vast majority of those who live under the commonwealth. I tried to defend him by appealing to his distinction between being sui iuris and alterius iuris—between being under one’s own power versus under another’s—and to his claim that democratic solidarity depends on citizens being guided by common notions rather than by passive affect. My friend was unmoved; she said Spinoza had essentially designed a democracy that requires its participants to have already achieved a rational autonomy that the institutional rules themselves deny to everyone else.

Given that tension, I want you to write a joke that turns precisely on the gap between Spinoza’s universalist metaphysics—where conatus and adequate ideas belong to every mode, and where the multitudo can form a collective mind only through shared joy and reason—and his highly particularist, exclusionary design for who actually gets to vote or sit in council. The punchline should depend on actual Spinozist machinery, not on generic philosopher-in-a-bar tropes: perhaps play on the idea that democratic affect is supposed to bind citizens through common notions, or that being sui iuris is a prerequisite for citizenship even though the commonwealth constantly produces conditions of dependency. To keep it from being something a model could simply regurgitate from standard philosophy-meme repositories, frame the joke as if it were being told at a departmental colloquium where a speaker has just proposed applying Spinoza’s citizenship criteria to a modern university faculty senate—complete with committees, tenure lines, and administrative oversight—so that the contradiction between universal rational capacity and institutional gatekeeping becomes the source of the humor. Make it polished, make it genuinely depend on the philosophy, and let the contradiction do the work rather than putting anachronistic words in Spinoza’s mouth.
