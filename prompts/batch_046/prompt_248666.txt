two people in my study group got stuck arguing about prison design, specifically the Norway Halden thing vs american mass incarceration. i just listened and now i want someone to referee.

(1) j said halden proves rehabiliation actually works if you treat people like citizens — shared kitchens, no bars, guards trained as mentors. recidivism is way lower, so the american model is just punitive cruelty that fails on its own terms.

(2) m said thats missing the core harm: prison is coercive confinement, not bad wallpaper. halden hides the same violence behind scandinavian minimalism. american prisons are brutal but at least honest about being punitive, which matters for democratic accountability.

(3) j came back that conditions arent cosmetic — dignity-preserving environments reduce institutional violence and improve post-release outcomes, so the architecture is causally part of rehab, not PR. calling it hidden violence ignores the lived experience.

(4) m said j is conflating better conditions with not-punishment. punishment has an expressive function — it communicates censure. if halden removes all stigma, either its not punishment (just preventive detention with good design) or the censure is invisible. plus theres this moral licensing effect where humane prisons make judges more willing to lock people up because they feel less guilty — so halden might actually expand incarceration.

(5) j said so youd prefer brutal conditions? that seems perverse. the expressive message is still there in the fact of confinement; you dont need cruelty to communicate blame.

(6) m said im not defending brutality, im saying dont confuse interior design with moral transformation. if we want rehab we should reduce confinement, not redecorate it. this is classic carceral humanism — making the cage nicer so society feels okay building more cages.

please go move by move and say whos right about what, what both are missing, and who is more right overall. dont just split the difference — actually adjudicate each step.
