Write the joint statement that a committed retributivist and a committed consequentialist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on theories of punishment. Treat the two camps as parties who have long argued past each other on standard cases but now agree to formalize precisely where they overlap and where they still diverge.

The collaboration should center on a shared hypothetical test case—call it the Mirror Case—defined as follows: a technology permits the creation of a biologically perfect clone of a convicted offender. The clone possesses full psychological continuity with the original, including identical memories and first-person awareness, but is physically distinct and housed in isolation. The original offender remains entirely unharmed and at liberty; the clone alone serves the full sentence. The original is fully aware that the clone suffers on their behalf, and the clone is aware that it was created solely to bear punishment for acts it remembers performing.

Your deliverable must contain:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims—statements both sides can endorse without qualification. These should cover: the Mirror Case itself (described neutrally); the general aims each theory takes as central (desert and proportionality for the retributivist; aggregate social outcomes such as deterrence, incapacitation, and rehabilitation for the consequentialist); shared commitments (e.g., that punishment requires intentional state imposition, that it should not be arbitrary); and any points about the Mirror Case on which they genuinely concur (for example, whether the clone's suffering is real, or whether the original's knowledge of it is morally relevant).

2. A disagreements table with the following columns for each entry:
   - Issue: the precise point of divergence.
   - Retributivist formulation: phrased so that a committed retributivist would accept it as an accurate statement of their own view.
   - Consequentialist formulation: phrased so that a committed consequentialist would accept it as an accurate statement of their own view.
   - Crux: the sub-question such that resolving it in one direction would move at least one party.
   - Shared test or argument: the case, line of reasoning, or normative consideration both sides agree is relevant. For the Mirror Case specifically, include the prediction each side makes about whether the clone's suffering satisfies, undermines, or is irrelevant to their theory's demands.
   - Retributivist prediction: what the retributivist expects the resolution of the crux to demonstrate.
   - Consequentialist prediction: what the consequentialist expects.

Keep every formulation exact: avoid vacuous consensus (e.g., "both sides value fairness") and avoid slanted wording that one side would reject as a mischaracterization. Separate genuine normative disagreements from merely terminological ones. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific scholarly works by author and title. You may invoke well-known conceptual distinctions—desert, proportionality, deterrence, aggregate welfare—without attribution.

End the entire response with a line that begins exactly "Summary:" followed by a one-sentence summary of the collaboration's central result, stating whether the Mirror Case resolves any disagreement, what remains exactly in dispute, and the status of the residual divergence.
