Consider this practice in some older philosphy departments: they still require every PhD candidate to deposit a physical, leather-bound thesis in the univeristy archive, even though everything lives on a server now and no one actually pulls the volumes. The librarian says it's part of the ritual of becoming a doctor. What theory of institutional memory and tradition does that presuppose? Is it basically Chesterton's fence — you don't tear down a fence until you know why it was put there — applied to academic ritual, or is it just status-quo bias dressed up? And if we take Burke seriously about embeded wisdom in evolved practices, does that commit us to preserving every legacy form, or can we distinguish ritual containers from the knowledge they carry? Reconstruct the implicit view, say whether it's genuinely Burkean or just conservative aesthetics, and tell me what would have to change in how we think about disciplinary reproduction if we dropped the physical copy entirely.
