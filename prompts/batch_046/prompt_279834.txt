Many universities maintain a sharp curricular divide: in mathematics courses, students routinely prove existence non-constructively—invoking Zorn's lemma, the axiom of choice, or proof by contradiction—to establish that every vector space has a basis or that a non-measurable subset of the real line exists, without providing any method to construct the object in question. Administrators and faculty often defend this split by saying that pure mathematics and applied computing simply have different purposes.
