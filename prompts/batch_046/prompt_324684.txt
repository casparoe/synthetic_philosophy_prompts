Please argue that conceptual slippage in the Copycat-style microdomain—where analogical mappings between letter-string patterns allow relational descriptors like successorship to slide into predecessorship under contextual pressure—undermines rather than supports the view that analogical reasoning in constrained spaces operates over a fixed ontology of roles. Defend the stronger claim that in tightly bounded analogical domains, slippage is not cognitive noise to be filtered out but a structural feature revealing that there is no determinate boundary between correct and incorrect mappings prior to the act of interpretation. To develop this, introduce a hypothetical microdomain consisting of geometric transformations applied to a bounded grid of colored tokens, and show how a single relational descriptor—say, adjacent inversion—systematically shifts its extension across problem instances in a manner that tracks the solver's implicit salience weighting rather than any stable rule. Conclude that the apparent determinacy of microdomain judgments is therefore an artifact of retrospective narrative coherence, not an intrinsic property of the reasoning process itself.
