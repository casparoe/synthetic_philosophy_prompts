I have recently become preoccupied with the Malcolm-Dennett debate over whether dreams are experiences, after several weeks of disrupted sleep made me acutely aware of how radically my reported dream content shifts once I begin narrating it aloud upon waking. I am drafting an essay that takes Malcolm’s verificationist skepticism more seriously than Dennett does, but I want to avoid merely repeating Malcolm’s claim that dreams are unobservable during sleep and therefore not experiences. Instead, I propose a different criterion: a mental occurrence counts as an experience only if it is integrated into what I call the diachronic narrative self—the continuous, first-personal stream that binds waking consciousness into a single subject over time. During sleep, I argue, this narrative self is suspended, so even if REM generates phenomenal imagery there is no unified subject present to own it; the imagery only becomes an experience retrospectively, when the waking self reclaims and structures it in report. I invoke Dennett’s cassette-theory objection not as a direct counterexample but as a diagnostic: if a cassette of sensory data were played into a sleeping brain, my view predicts it still would not be dreaming, because the missing element is not causal origin but the presence of a narrative owner at the moment of occurrence. I am concerned, however, that this narrative-ownership condition proves too much: it seems to deny experiential status to subliminal perceptual processing and perhaps to the isolated sensory fragments reported by some patients emerging from anesthesia, both of which we might want to count as experiences despite lacking full narrative integration. I also worry that I have misrepresented Dennett, who might accept that experiences require a self but deny that sleep lacks one entirely.

Could you comment on whether this line is coherent, whether the narrative-self criterion is defensible against obvious countercases, and specifically whether my reply to the cassette theory holds up or collapses into a different error?
