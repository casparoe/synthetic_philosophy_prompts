I have been working through recent meta-philosophical literature on how intuition pumps structure conceptual analysis, particularly in ethics and philosophy of mind. In discussing well-known cases such as Thomson’s violinist, Jackson’s Mary, and Quine’s gavagai scenario, my reading group adopted a methodological distinction—drawn from some recent papers in conceptual engineering—between first-order pumps, which isolate a single normative or epistemic variable, and second-order pumps, which are meant to test whether first-order responses survive systematic framing manipulations. Building on this taxonomy, I proposed developing a reversibility index for any given pump: one inverts the spatial, temporal, or agential framing of the scenario and measures whether the resulting asymmetry in intuitive judgment exceeds a calibrated threshold. When the asymmetry is large, the pump is classified as dialectically unstable, indicating that its persuasive force derives from framing artifacts rather than from the underlying concept itself.

Applying this framework informally, it appears that both Jackson’s Mary and the standard trolley switch score poorly on reversibility. Presenting Mary’s chromatic experience from a detached, third-person observational stance, or presenting the switch decision from the victim’s anticipatory viewpoint, seems to diminish the original intuitive pull substantially—something I have observed in preliminary seminar discussions. Given that dialectical instability tracks framing dependency rather than conceptual depth, I am inclined to conclude that such pumps should be retained in undergraduate pedagogy for their illustrative clarity but treated as philosophically inert in serious normative argumentation, since their normative force dissipates once the framing is altered. Is this the correct methodological conclusion? Or is there a principled reason to regard framing-sensitive intuition pumps as philosophically productive precisely because their instability reveals something non-obvious about the concepts under examination—something that a perfectly stable, framing-invariant pump would necessarily conceal?
