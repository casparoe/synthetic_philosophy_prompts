A common defence of tragic art treats it like a vaccination protocol: the audience receives an attenuated, fictional dose of grief, pity or terror within the safe clinical setting of a theatre or novel, thereby developing emotional resilience without suffering genuine harm. Audit this analogy thoroughly. First, set out the correspondence table: what corresponds to the pathogen, the attenuated strain, the immune response, the clinical environment, and the administering protocol or physician. Then identify where the mapping breaks—whether fictional grief behaves like a weakened pathogen, whether any credible psychological mechanism parallels antibody formation, and whether the "safe setting" genuinely isolates the subject from all harm. Next, state what the analogy quietly imports about the value of art (for instance, that its worth is primarily instrumental and therapeutic) and about the audience (for instance, that they are deficient patients requiring treatment). Finally, assess whether the conclusion that tragedy is justified as emotional hygiene survives once the disanalogies are exposed, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument throughout. If a more fitting analogy—perhaps to ritual mourning, to physical exercise, or to another practice—suggests itself, sketch it briefly and indicate whether it fares better.
