Write a sestina about religion conceived as a natural phenomenon, specifically through the mechanism of credibility-enhancing displays (CREDs): the view that costly rituals are not primarily adaptive for group cohesion or direct signals of commitment, but calibration devices that increase an observer's acceptance of the performer's claims about non-observable realities, precisely because the cost is demonstrably wasteful and therefore difficult to fake. Use the following six end-words in the first stanza, in this order: cost, witness, belief, error, signal, nature. Follow the classic sestina rotation—stanza 1: 1 2 3 4 5 6; stanza 2: 6 1 5 2 4 3; stanza 3: 3 6 4 1 2 5; stanza 4: 5 3 2 6 1 4; stanza 5: 4 5 1 3 6 2; stanza 6: 2 4 6 5 3 1—and close with an envoi that includes all six words. Let the form do philosophical work: the recursive displacement of cost and signal should reflect the ambiguity between a genuinely wasteful display and an instrumentally rational one; the shifting positions of witness and belief should show how testimony about invisible agents gains credibility not through verification but through the performer's willingness to bear irrecoverable loss; and the recurring error should mark the point at which an observer must decide whether the inference is calibrated or is instead a misapplication of agency-detection mechanisms. In the envoi, bring all six terms together without resolving whether religion is best explained as a calibrated inference or as a systematic error of nature; hold both explanations in suspension. The poem should be precise enough that a reader familiar with the cognitive science of religion recognizes the CRED mechanism rather than a generic narrative about faith, yet it should remain fully intelligible as poetry to someone who has never encountered the theory. Do not include citations, author names, or bibliographic references.
