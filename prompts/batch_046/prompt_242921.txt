My reading group has two philosophers who have argued for a year about whether beauty is objective in Hume's framework: the Convergence Camp reads Hume's essay on taste as pointing to an empirically discoverable intersubjective standard among ideal critics, while the Perspectival Camp reads the same conditions for taste as merely descriptive regularities that leave irreducible disagreement intact and deny any normative authority to convergence. Write the adversarial collaboration document they should have produced.

The deliverable should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides accept about Hume's argument, about human psychology, and about the limits of aesthetic expertise; (2) a disagreements table stating the residual differences in wording each camp would endorse; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side—specifically including whether the condition of good sense in Hume's ideal critic functions as a normative correction mechanism or only as a statistical filter on outliers, and whether expert agreement about algorithmically optimized adversarial aesthetic objects—images engineered to maximize preference ratings without traditional artistic structure—counts as confirming beauty or as exposing a failure of the standard; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or piece of evidence both agree bears on it, with each camp's predicted outcome recorded in advance. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not adjudicate the dispute; and formulate everything so that both parties could sign the document.
