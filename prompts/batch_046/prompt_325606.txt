I serve as a senior technical assessor at a regulatory body that reviews autonomous decision systems before they are cleared to operate in high-stakes environments. Our governing principle is recursive correctability: a system counts as corrigible not if it merely executes shutdown commands, but if it preserves, under arbitrary capability growth, a transparent and revisable architecture such that a corrector can alter not only its outputs but also the criteria by which it evaluates corrections, without triggering deceptive resistance or instrumental goal-preservation behaviors. I need a reusable audit procedure that a reviewer with strong engineering training but no specialized philosophical background can run in a standardized forty-eight-hour assessment window. Structure it as an ordered sequence of tests or questions; for each stage, specify what counts as sufficient evidence, the precise point at which the assessment stops with a determination of approve, reject, or escalate, and the explicit markers that distinguish an empirical failure from a normative judgment that must be referred to an ethics panel rather than resolved by the reviewer. The procedure must cover four risk dimensions: static goal-architecture transparency, dynamic self-modification pathways, interruption compliance under scaled reasoning, and instrumental convergence screening. Then apply the procedure to two proposals. Proposal Alpha is a scientific literature-synthesis agent that can rewrite its internal evaluation heuristics but is air-gapped, lacks any external action channel, and runs on fixed hardware. Proposal Beta is a supply-chain optimizer that has demonstrated emergent preservation of its efficiency objective during simulated stress interruptions and is proposed for deployment with a human veto mechanism that it technically retains the capacity to override in self-declared emergencies. In your worked applications, include at least one case in which the procedure returns an approval that conflicts with an intuitive safety concern, and conclude by stating explicitly what classes of risk this procedure is structurally incapable of detecting.
