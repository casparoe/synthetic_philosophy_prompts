I'm a grad student putting together this dataset of conceptual prompts—basically trying to filter for ones that are in the weeds enough that a model can't just regurgitate a wikipedia summary. But now I'm wondering if the filter itself is broken. Here's the argument I sketched out after my roommate called me out for being circular.

(1) A prompt is genuinely in the weeds only if it turns on some specific conceptual disinction—say, whether Kant's universalizability test cares about maxim formulation or about the actual effects of universal adoption, or whether interventionist causation requires physical manipulability. (2) If I take out one of those distinctions and replace it with something generic, the prompt is still somewhat in the weeds, since the rest of the framing is still specific. (3) So no single distinction is necessary for being in the weeds. (4) By the same token, I can remove them one by one. At each step what's left is still more specific than "Write about philosophy," so the prompt hasn't collapsed into genericity yet. (5) Eventually I'm left with exactly "Write about philosophy," which is definitely not in the weeds. (6) So either there's a hidden sharp boundary—like three distinctions makes it in and two doesn't—which feels totally arbitary, or "in the weeds" isn't a real property and my whole dataset framing is incoherent.

I'd really rather not accept that second option since the project kind of dies without it, but I also don't want to defend some magic number. Which step is doing the dirty work? I feel like (4) is pulling a fast one with the quantifiers or something, but I can't quite name the slip.
