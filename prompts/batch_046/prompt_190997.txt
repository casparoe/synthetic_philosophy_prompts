I'm trying to identify a recurring pattern in cultural transmission that doesn't seem to match the standard vocabulary. Some ideas or practices spread and persist not because they are easily copied—often they are quite elaborate—but because they resist compression. When you try to summarize or paraphrase them into simpler language, you lose distinctions or obligations that turn out to be functionally load-bearing for the group that maintains them. The complexity isn't a transmission cost; it acts as a protective feature that prevents simpler rivals from substituting for the original. This feels different from clusters of mutually reinforcing beliefs, and different from opacity meant to exclude outsiders, because the resistance is specifically to linguistic or conceptual compression rather than to understanding per se. Is there an established concept—perhaps in evolutionary anthropology or memetics—for this kind of structurally incompressible replication? If there is no standard term, how should one distinguish it from ordinary fidelity in copying or from group-level functional adaptation?
