The exam question was as follows:

In the HCH idealization, deliberation is distributed across a tree of human consultants who are independent in that they do not observe one another's reasoning processes, only their final outputs. Proponents suggest that this structure eliminates influence-seeking behaviors—strategic persuasion, status competition, and value drift through social dynamics—because consultants have no audience for such behaviors within the tree. Critically evaluate this claim. In your answer, address three points: (1) whether independence of reasoning traces is sufficient to block influence-seeking, particularly in the strategic, power-oriented sense discussed in work on influence-seeking systems; (2) whether the root node's authority to frame questions and select among sub-questions introduces a form of structural influence that the idealization does not eliminate but rather centralizes; and (3) whether allowing consultants to recursively invoke nested HCH structures preserves stability or instead distributes framing authority in ways that create new coordination risks, including what might be called epistemic leakage through question topology. One student submitted the following answer:

HCH eliminates influence-seeking dynamics by isolating consultants. Because each node receives only the outputs of its subordinates, not their reasoning, there is no channel through which rhetorical skill, status competition, or strategic misrepresentation could be rewarded. This addresses the kind of value drift observed in open committees, where participants gradually adjust their stated positions in response to social feedback. The root node's framing authority is essentially a neutral organizational parameter: it sets the scope of the question but does not constrain the content of honest answers, so it does not constitute influence in the relevant sense. If consultants recursively invoke their own sub-consultants—nested HCH—the independence condition is reproduced at every level, preserving stability. The only vulnerability is accidental framing bias at the root, which is an implementation error rather than a structural feature. Consequently, HCH achieves a clean separation of reasoning from social pressure and does not merely relocate influence-seeking behavior.
