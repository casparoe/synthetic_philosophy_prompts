I'm planning a single ninety-minute seminar for third-year philosophy undergraduates who have just worked through Schopenhauer's treatment of the arts in Book III of The World as Will and Representation. They can repeat the slogan that music is a direct copy of the Will, but they consistently reduce it to either an empirical claim about emotional intensity or a psychological thesis about listening. I need a teaching plan—not the explanation itself—for how to show them that music's privilege is metaphysical, tied to Schopenhauer's hierarchy of objectification, and specifically why it bypasses representation altogether.

Plan the sequence: which concepts must be secured first (the principle of sufficient reason, Platonic Ideas as the objectification of the Will at specific grades, and the representational structure of the other arts) so that the distinction between copying an Idea and copying the Will directly can actually do work; then where to introduce Schopenhauer's structural analogy between bass and harmony (the lower, inorganic and vegetative grades) and melody (the conscious, historical will); and why that analogy must come after the metaphysical groundwork rather than before it. Identify the one concrete musical example or formal comparison that should carry the whole session—something that lacks narrative, programmatic, or emotional-representational content, yet still clearly exhibits the bass-harmony-melody architecture he describes— and explain what it must have and lack. Then map the predictable misconceptions: the point in the sequence where students will conflate the Will with individual human desire or intention; where they will read the bass-to-melody analogy as a claim about acoustic physics rather than metaphysical correspondence; and where they will interpret the ranking of arts as a judgment about subjective pleasure. For each, specify exactly when to head it off and with what move. Include one check-for-understanding question that distinguishes grasping the non-representational claim from merely recalling the hierarchy. Finally, state explicitly what you would refuse to explain at this level—such as Schopenhauer's specific debts to contemporary music theorists, or whether his view can accommodate musical forms he never encountered—and why omitting it protects the conceptual architecture rather than simplifying it.
