Analyze the Meiji Restoration and the dismantling of the Tokugawa order from 1868 through the 1880s through the lens of historical materialism, specifically the base–superstructure model and its Gramscian refinement of the “passive revolution.” Rather than recounting the standard modernization narrative, map the specific economic relations—the feudal land tenure of the han system, the bakufu’s fiscal crisis driven by silver outflows and rice-price volatility, the emergence of rural merchant capital, and the indebtedness of lower samurai—onto the claim that contradictions in the economic base compelled the political rupture. Then weigh the autonomous role of the superstructure: the kokutai ideology that legitimized imperial restoration, the bureaucratic self-identity of the lower samurai who engineered the coup, and the crisis of sovereignty triggered by Western gunboat diplomacy, which was experienced first as a failure of state authority rather than as a crisis of production relations. Ask whether the Meiji case fits the concept of a passive revolution, in which the old feudal ruling stratum—the samurai—liquidated its own economic base and reconstituted itself as a modern bureaucratic and capitalist class without a prior mass revolutionary movement led by an emergent bourgeoisie. If so, does this confirm historical materialism, because the old base had become a fetter that had to be destroyed, or does it undermine the model, because superstructural actors—ideologues, state-builders, and military bureaucrats—appear to have initiated and directed the economic transformation rather than being driven by an already-mature capitalist class? Be disciplined in identifying who counts as a class actor, what precisely constitutes the economic base in this agrarian-commercial hybrid, and where the theory is silent—especially regarding the rapid abolition of feudal obligations and the installation of capitalist property relations without a bourgeois revolution from below. Conclude by assessing whether the Meiji episode is a confirmation, an anomaly, or a category error for historical materialism.
