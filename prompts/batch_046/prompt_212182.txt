Write a two-page research proposal for a twelve-week independent study in political philosophy. The topic is Spinoza's theory of democracy as developed across his political writings, but the proposal should not treat it as a standard history-of-ideas exercise. Instead, frame the central research question precisely: does Spinoza's democratic preference rest on procedural grounds—the widest possible distribution of sovereign power among the multitudo—or on functional, affective grounds, namely that democratic institutions act as stabilizers of collective joy and inhibitors of the sad passions (fear, hatred, servility) that monarchical structures systematically produce?

Explain why existing interpretations leave this open. Note that one common reading treats Spinoza as a proto-liberal defending popular sovereignty, while another reads him as ultimately skeptical of democracy's stability and secretly committed to aristocracy. Both, however, assume that legitimacy derives from representation or consent, and neither asks whether democratic institutions for Spinoza are best understood as technologies for restructuring affective relations rather than mechanisms for aggregating individual wills.

State your method and timeline: in the first month, reconstruct Spinoza's account of collective affects and the conatus of the multitudo using his ethical framework; in the second, analyze the specific institutional designs he proposes—councils, rotation, property limits—not as checks on power but as affective regulators; in the third, use a single modern conceptual framework (for instance, literature on democratic deliberation or affective polarization) as a foil to test whether the affective-technology reading resolves the well-known tension between Spinoza's praise of democracy and his simultaneous warnings about democratic instability.

Specify the deliverable: a 6000-word paper arguing either that the affective reading dissolves the tension and provides a unified account of Spinoza's democratic theory, or that the tension is genuine and reveals an unresolved contradiction between his normative metaphysics and his institutional recommendations. Define failure conditions clearly: the project fails if the affective framework collapses into circular reasoning—defining democratic stability solely as the presence of collective joy—or if it cannot distinguish democratic failure from monarchical failure except by presupposing democracy's superiority.

Keep citations and bibliographies out of the proposal; refer to positions and arguments conceptually, and note where you will supply references in the final paper. Do not include verbatim quotations from Spinoza's texts; paraphrase all claims in your own language. The proposal should argue that the work is both tractable within one semester and philosophically significant for contemporary democratic theory.
