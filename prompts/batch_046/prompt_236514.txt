I've been working through some philosophy of medicine readings for a seminar I'm taking, and I keep returning to Ian Hacking's idea of looping effects in psychiatric classification—the phenomenon by which labeling someone with a disorder can alter their self-understanding and behavior, which then feeds back into the classification itself, forcing the category to shift. What strikes me is how clinical manuals present their taxonomies as if they were mapping stable, pre-existing natural kinds, when in practice the boundary conditions are constantly being renegotiated by the very people being sorted. To explore this tension without writing a standard essay, I would like you to produce a satirical institutional document.

Please write a deadpan, bureaucratic "Clinical Feedback Protocol" of approximately 750 words, formatted as numbered sections with sub-clauses, in the dry, authoritative voice of a fictional body called the International Consortium for Diagnostic Stability. The protocol should govern the assessment and management of patients diagnosed with "Interactive Taxonomic Syndrome"—a fictional disorder whose sole diagnostic feature is that the patient has already been classified by an earlier edition of the same manual now applying the diagnosis. The satire should exaggerate real features of psychiatric nosology and of Hacking-style looping: include provisions for "pre-classificatory baseline recovery" (an attempt to retrieve the patient's state before the manual touched it), a "recursion limit" that is formally instituted at three iterations and then immediately waived via administrative exception, and an informed-consent clause requiring the patient to sign an acknowledgment that their symptoms are simultaneously cause and effect of the manual's previous revision. The document should quietly treat feedback not as a methodological problem but as a routine administrative input, and it should expand diagnostic criteria precisely to preserve institutional continuity when patients begin behaving differently in response to being labeled.

Keep the irony sustained and entirely deadpan—no winking, no explicit announcement that the logic is absurd, no direct address to the reader breaking character. The humor and critique should emerge solely from the internal consistency of the bureaucratic machinery as it formalizes its own circularity. It should be readable to someone who has never encountered Hacking—the absurdity should be visible in the form itself—while rewarding a specialist who recognizes that every clause parodies an actual practice: the reification of diagnostic categories, the treatment of patient feedback as noise to be administratively absorbed, and the assumption that the manual describes rather than participates in the condition it names. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific editions of real manuals; the document should stand as a self-contained fictional artifact.
