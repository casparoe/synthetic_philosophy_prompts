As a retired physicist who has recently begun auditing graduate seminars in philosophy of mathematics, I have agreed to assist in grading the midterm examinations for an introductory course on metaphysics and the sciences. The examination presented students with the following problem.

Consider the finitist position, which denies the existence of actual infinities and accepts only potential ones. Hilbert's hotel is frequently invoked to illustrate properties specific to actually infinite collections. A critic proposes that the hotel is physically unrealizable because accommodating each successive guest requires a completed infinite sequence of simultaneous room shifts, an execution that cannot be finished in finite time. The same critic contends that Cantor's proof of the uncountability of the power set of the natural numbers can be circumvented within a physical model: if every subset is assigned to a unique discrete energy level within a finite reservoir, then all collections are paired without ever constructing an actual uncountable totality, leaving behind only a potential infinity of correspondences. Assess whether this reasoning successfully undermines the mathematical coherence of actual infinities, or whether it instead conflates conditions of physical implementation with conditions of abstract existence.

Below is one student's response. I ask that you evaluate it with an explicit letter grade and with point-by-point comments that justify that grade, addressing precisely where the reasoning is sound and where it commits errors of attribution, conflation, or invalid inference—errors that a hurried reader might overlook.

Student answer:

The critic is mistaken to treat Hilbert's hotel as a physical hypothesis; it is a conceptual model intended to display formal properties. Nevertheless, Hilbert himself was a committed finitist who introduced the hotel precisely to demonstrate that only potential infinity is coherent—each individual shift is conceivable, but the totality of all shifts is never actually completed. Thus the hotel's logic ultimately supports finitism rather than opposing it.

Regarding Cantor, the critic's energy-level pairing does not escape the diagonal argument, since that argument applies to any enumeration of subsets irrespective of the physical medium representing them. If one lists subsets via energy states, one can still construct a subset that differs from the nth listed member at the nth position. The critic wrongly supposes that distributing subsets across a finite reservoir avoids the contradiction, yet Cantor's result is formal rather than thermodynamic. Moreover, the shifting protocol in Hilbert's hotel is structurally isomorphic to Cantor's pairing function, so if the physical execution of shifts is impossible, then Cantor's pairing is equally impossible, and both arguments collapse together.

Consequently, the critic has not shown that actual infinities are mathematically incoherent; they have shown only that physical analogues are constrained. But since Hilbert's own finitism already denies actual completion, the abstract mathematical framework remains entirely untouched.

Please provide the grade and the specific, detailed justification.
