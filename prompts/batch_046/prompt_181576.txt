As a retired physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mind, I have adopted the following view: clinical cases of pain asymbolia, in which patients verbally report nociceptive sensations yet exhibit no distress, avoidance, or negative emotional coloring, demonstrate that the sensory-discriminative and affective-motivational dimensions of pain are not merely dissociable but ontologically distinct. I therefore hold that the badness of pain is not intrinsic to the nociceptive experience itself, but is instead generated by a separable evaluative module—likely involving anterior insular and anterior cingulate integration—that can malfunction or be selectively inhibited without altering the core qualitative character of the sensation. From this I conclude that suffering is a contingent overlay, not an essential feature of feeling pain.

Play this position straight and tell me what would genuinely count against it.
