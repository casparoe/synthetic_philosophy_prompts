I've been working through the implications of what we might call the double inversion: Darwin's demonstration that adaptive functional design can accumulate with no designer comprehending either the problem or the solution, and Turing's demonstration that correct symbolic reasoning can be generated with no internal comprehension of the rules being applied. If both cases establish that high-level competence can exist entirely decoupled from any corresponding comprehension, then isn't the philosophical default—treating human reasoning as special because it involves both—just an unmotivated exception? Specifically, if we apply the inversion consistently rather than selectively, shouldn't we conclude that human "understanding" is not a causal driver of cognition but a post-hoc interpretive frame we project onto competent behavior, and that the apparent explanatory gap between biological, artificial, and evolutionary cognition collapses into a single spectrum of competence with comprehension as an epiphenomenal gloss? What breaks the symmetry, or is the symmetry itself the mistake?
