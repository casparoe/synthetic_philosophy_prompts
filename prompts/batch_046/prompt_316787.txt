I'm a philosophy major in my final year, currently enrolled in a seminar on ancient ethics and the problem of moral motivation. We've spent the last few weeks on Plato's Republic and Glaucon's challenge—specifically the thought experiment of the ring of Gyges, which seems to reduce justice to an instrumental good valued only for reputation and fear of punishment. For a presentation next week, I'm trying to design a taxonomy that actually sorts people by what they believe about moral reasons rather than by which philosophical label they recognize, but I want the framing to go beyond the standard classroom version so it isn't something a model could answer by reciting a textbook summary.

To that end, I'm injecting a modified scenario: imagine the ring not only makes its wearer completely invisible to every other person, but also induces complete anterograde amnesia for any unjust act committed while it is worn. The agent can therefore never suffer guilt, never be caught, never suffer reputational harm, and never construct an autobiographical narrative of having done wrong. Given this double-blind case, please construct a decision tree—starting from the root commitment that there might still be a non-instrumental reason to refrain from injustice—where each branch is a concrete claim a student could answer without knowing the philosophical names in advance. The first split should distinguish whether any remaining reason is internal (concerning the agent's psychology, character, or rational coherence) or external (concerning the act itself or those affected by it). The internal branch should then split on whether the reason survives the total erasure of autobiographical memory: if yes, point toward a virtue-ethical or deep-character position; if no, point toward a narrative-identity or psychological-continuity view that collapses back into pure instrumentalism once memory is lost. The external branch should split on whether the reason depends on the act being justifiable to the affected party regardless of whether that party ever knows—this should isolate a contractualist position—before asking whether the reason is categorical and independent of the agent's contingent desires, which should isolate a Kantian rationalist position. The final split should ask whether one accepts observer-independent moral properties, separating a non-naturalist moral realist leaf from a deflationary or error-theoretic leaf.

For each leaf, please name the position (using 'unnamed' only if the combination is genuinely unoccupied), state its core commitment in one sentence, and specify its most awkward commitment specifically within this amnesiac-invisibility scenario. I need at least these leaves represented: pure instrumentalism or contract-failure, a virtue-ethical view, a contractualist view, a Kantian rationalist view, a non-naturalist realist view, and the deflationary option. Where the logical space allows combinations that none of these positions occupy, please note the empty cell and briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also, please flag any pair of leaves that this particular branching genuinely fails to separate—perhaps because certain virtue-ethical and Kantian commitments both invoke internal coherence in ways these nodes don't distinguish. Throughout, phrase every node as a direct question—never as 'are you a Kantian?'—so that the instrument sorts respondents by their answers about the modified case, not by the labels they prefer.
