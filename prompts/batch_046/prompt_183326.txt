Your example should make precise the ambiguity about whether this system counts as conscious according to standard higher-order accounts, and should highlight at least two competing interpretations — for instance, a view that requires a single, unified higher-order thought versus one that allows consciousness to be distributed or relational across modules. In doing so, clearly separate what is broadly settled among philosophers working on these theories (such as the basic distinction between first-order and higher-order mental states) from what remains genuinely contested (such as whether unity of consciousness is a necessary condition or whether fragmented meta-representation can suffice). Do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus. The case should be specific enough that the tension arises from the architecture itself rather than from a generic abstraction.
