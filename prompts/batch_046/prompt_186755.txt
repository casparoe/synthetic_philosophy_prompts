As a retired physicist who has only recently turned to philosophy in earnest, I should like a consistency check on my emerging views before I present them to a small reading group. Here is where I have landed on determinism, predictability, and moral responsibility.

(a) I am a physicalist about the mind: mental properties supervene on neural dynamics, and there is no downward causation that violates the closure of physics.

(b) I hold that the brain operates near a point of self-organised criticality, producing positive Lyapunov exponents that generate Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy; this makes long-range prediction of mental states structurally impossible for any observer physically embedded within the same causal network, not merely practically difficult.

(c) I accept that quantum mechanics is genuinely indeterministic at the fundamental level, but I think environmental decoherence together with statistical averaging over neuronal populations renders macroscopic brain dynamics effectively deterministic; the unpredictability I care about is classical chaos, not quantum randomness.

(d) I defend a compatibilist account of freedom: an action is free, and its agent morally responsible, provided only that it flows from the agent's own rational deliberation and is not externally coerced; causal determinism, whether quantum or classical, is therefore irrelevant to freedom.

(e) At the same time, I am strongly drawn to a source-incompatibilist intuition: being the true originator of one's choice requires that it not be fully fixed by prior physical conditions, and if an external system with perfect initial data could compute my decision in advance, then I am not genuinely responsible, even if I feel no coercion and my reasoning feels entirely my own.
