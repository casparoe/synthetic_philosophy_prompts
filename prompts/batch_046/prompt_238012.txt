I've noticed a recurring puzzle in how we decide whether several items count as one thing or as separate things lying near each other. Consider a bicycle fully assembled: we naturally say there is one bike. Now imagine taking it apart and arranging every piece on the floor in precisely the same relative positions—frame in the middle, wheels to the sides, chain draped exactly where it would hang—but with nothing bolted together. Most people would say there is no bike, only parts. The spatial layout is identical, yet the "oneness" has vanished. Reassemble the same pieces in the same layout, and the single object returns. This makes me think that being one thing depends on more than just parts occupying the right places; it seems tied to functioning together, to being connected for a purpose, or perhaps to someone's intention that they form a unit.

But then I run into strange boundary cases. If I weld a bicycle to a tree so that the parts are physically connected but serve no shared function, do I now have one bizarre object, or still a tree and a bike? If I randomly bolt bike parts into a shape that can never ride, have I created one weird thing or just a pile of junk with connections? So the pattern I'm trying to capture is this: our judgments about whether parts compose a single object appear to shift with functional integration, intentional assembly, or connectivity, even when the bare physical arrangement of matter is held constant.

Is there an established philosophical concept specifically for this dependency—the way composition seems to track working-together or being-assembled-for-a-role rather than pure spatial contact? I know there is a broad question about when parts make up a whole, but I am asking about the narrower phenomenon where the exact same spatial configuration can be read as one object or many depending on functional or intentional context. Does this have its own name, and if not, how should it be defined and distinguished from the general problem of parts and wholes? I would want the edges made clear: what counts as the relevant kind of "function" or "intention," and whether cases like the welded bike-tree are inside or outside the concept.
