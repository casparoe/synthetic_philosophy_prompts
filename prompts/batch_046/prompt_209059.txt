I'm putting together the take-home for my upper-level Stoicism seminar — we've been looking at the modern revival, CBT applications, and the old dichotmy of control. I need an essay question that can't just be answered by repeating the handbook distinction and listing virtues.

The twist I want injected: have students tackle whether the dichotomy survives once you notice that our judgments (prohairesis) are causally shaped by structures — algorithms, economic precarity, insitutional bias — that clearly aren't in our power. If belief-formation is externally determined, does the Stoic claim that we suffer only from our judgments collapse into circularity? Or can it be saved by distinguishing causal influence from normative authority? I want them to wrestle with whether modern Stoicism risks unfalsifiability: every failure to stay calm gets blamed on insufficient practice, never on the structural constraint itself.

Please write:
- The exam prompt itself (students have 48 hours, about 900 words, 30 marks). Keep it focused — no bibliography requests, no obscure citation demands.
- A model answer around 600-700 words. It should show the crux: identify the circularity risk (that control over judgments presupposes the very autonomy being questioned), make the causal/normative distinction explicit, and assess whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability. Don't just list Stoic replies; show where they work and where they strain.
- A marking scheme that splits A from B. A answers must name the circularity problem and must treat structural determinism as a pressure that forces Stoicism to refine its notion of power, not as an obvious knock-down. B answers usually fall back on summarizing CBT techniques or treat algorithmic influence as automatically refuting the view. Deduct marks for answers that cite specific modern self-help authors as authorities rather than engaging the conceptual tension.
