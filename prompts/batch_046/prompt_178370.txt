A study guide I read summarizes Nietzsche's distinction between monumental, antiquarian, and critical history—drawn from his essay on history and life—like this: "Monumental history inspires present action by glorifying great achievements, though it risks mythmaking and falsification. Antiquarian history preserves local traditions and gives a community rooted identity; Nietzsche treats this as the healthiest mode overall, since continuity with the past best serves life. Critical history judges and condemns past traditions to liberate the present, but Nietzsche warns that overuse severs a people from their roots and undermines the conditions for culture itself. Therefore, a society should organize its historical consciousness primarily around antiquarian preservation, using monumental inspiration and critical judgment only as rare supplements." This sounds partly right, but I am suspicious of the claim that Nietzsche ranks antiquarian history as the healthiest and that he wants social organization built around it. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a common simplification, and what misrepresents Nietzsche's actual position—particularly on whether he endorses one mode over the others and on what exactly critical history threatens.
