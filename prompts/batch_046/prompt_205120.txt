two people in my study group got stuck arguing about presentism in historiography — whether historians can ever escape judging the past by the standards of their own time. neither would budge and i was just sitting there, so i want an outside read.

(1) she said presentism is basically unavoidable. historians pick topics because they matter now, and even framing a question like 'why did the Roman economy function?' smuggles in modern economic categories that Romans wouldn't recognize. so there's no neutral descriptive ground.

(2) he pushed back that picking a topic isn't the same as moral evaluation. you can study Roman slavery because it's relevant today without condemning slave-owners using modern human-rights language. selection bias isn't evaluation bias, and she's conflating them.

(3) she came back that 'understanding' itself requires conceptual translation. our categories — gender, race, mental health — are historically specific. applying them to the past is already distortion; describing a 14th-century mystic's visions without using either anachronistic psychology or leaving her experience totally opaque is impossible.

(4) he said that's a reductio. if all conceptual translation counts as presentist distortion, then historiography collapses — we couldn't understand anything outside our own moment. he argued we can use 'thin' descriptions like 'person X was denied legal standing,' which are accurate without importing modern moral frameworks.

(5) then she brought up microhistory: zooming in on one life forces you to deploy modern categories like trauma, agency, or identity, all products of 20th-century therapy culture. so microhistorians are especially trapped, and he was only defending macro-level events where thin descriptions might work. he replied that just shows microhistory needs extra methodological care, not that presentism is universal.

can you go through their exchange move by move? rate each point from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say where they're actually talking past eachother, and tell me what both are missing about the problem — especially whether there's a real distinction between unavoidable conceptual framing and actual presentist judgment.
