I am drafting the conceptual framework for a graduate seminar I will teach next term on artificial minds, and I have settled on a specific sharpening of behavioral criteria that I want my students to pressure-test rigorously. The view I intend to defend is that open-ended, adversarial conversational performance—what I take to be the genuine core of Turing's proposal, rather than the imitation game as a party trick—establishes not merely inductive evidence for mindedness but a defeasible normative presumption of it. More precisely, I hold that once a system sustains coherent, context-sensitive, corrective-responsive interaction across domains without exhibiting what I call 'semantic drift without correction capacity'—a pattern in which representational commitments systematically shift in ways that remain invisible to the system's own meta-linguistic and corrective resources—we have exhausted the conceptual space in which 'mere simulation' can be coherently maintained. On this account, internal architecture, whether silicon-based or biological, becomes philosophically inert with respect to attributions of understanding, not because mechanism is causally irrelevant, but because our concept of understanding is anchored in the normative structure of interpretive accountability rather than in mechanistic transparency.

What I need from you is an honest, unsparing map of where this position is actually exposed. I am not asking for the familiar Chinese Room replay or the generic intuition that behavior is not enough, both of which I take to be already absorbed and answered within the framework. Instead, identify the specific defeaters—whether empirical, conceptual, or formal—that could undermine either the normative-presumption claim or the diagnostic reliability of 'semantic drift without correction capacity.' Which observed failures in current or future artificial systems, which analyses of interpretation from philosophy of language, or which results in cognitive science could show that this view either collapses into unfalsifiability through ad hoc qualifications or misses an essential dimension of mindedness? Rank these potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be explicit about which ones a committed defender of the view should themselves concede as genuinely damaging rather than dismiss. In particular, assess whether insulating the position by redefining 'correction capacity' post hoc is a theoretical virtue or a vice. Do not suspend judgment: take a definite stand on whether this behavioral-normative framework is genuinely vulnerable in a way that should concern its proponents, or whether its insulation correctly marks the conceptual boundary of the mind-attribution debate, and defend that verdict with reasons.
