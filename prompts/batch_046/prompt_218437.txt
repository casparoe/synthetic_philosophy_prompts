As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I am assembling a dataset of assessment prompts for a feature on how conceptual examinations in philosophy are being redesigned to resist mere recitation. I need a prompt that sits deep in the weeds of moral philosophy—specifically the question of whether genuine moral dilemmas are possible—without relying on bibliographic recall, citation, or familiarity with highly niche theories that smaller models may not have retained. You should act as an expert moral philosopher, a professional exam setter for an upper-level undergraduate seminar, and a careful pedagogical designer.

Before you write anything, please think carefully and begin with a brief internal brainstorm: map out the standard positions (the logical-consistency objection to contradictory obligations, the moral-remainder thesis about unavoidable wrongness, and the distinction between a genuine dilemma and a tragic conflict), then inject one niche conceptual frame—the three-layer model of normative conflict comprising surface competition, structural incommensurability, and recursive second-order residue. Next, construct a specific case study that forces students to apply this frame rather than summarize familiar authors.

Your deliverable should contain three parts. First, write one essay examination question suitable for a 90-minute final in an upper-level moral philosophy seminar, asking for approximately 1,000 words. The question must present the constructed case of Dr. Elena Voss, a senior archivist at a state repository who discovers that two unique documents—Document A, containing the only eyewitness testimony to an undocumented atrocity, and Document B, containing the only complete record of a suppressed linguistic tradition—have both been accidentally exposed to a corrosive chemical. She has institutional authority and resources to preserve exactly one, but the preservation protocols for A will destroy B, and vice versa. Both documents carry equal institutional preservation status, yet they serve goods that are structurally incomparable: justice-through-testimony versus cultural-continuity-through-language.

The exam question should require students to determine whether Dr. Voss faces a genuine moral dilemma, and it must direct them to address four specific issues: whether the obligations to preserve A and B are genuinely contradictory or merely competing; whether the incommensurability of the goods they protect prevents any lexical ordering or aggregation; whether Dr. Voss’s recognition of the value in the unchosen document generates a recursive second-order residue that distinguishes this situation from a simple tragic choice; and what the analysis implies for the general possibility of genuine moral dilemmas.

Second, provide a model answer of about 700 words. It should demonstrate the precise conceptual moves that distinguish an A-grade response: correctly applying the three-layer model to the case, identifying the exact point at which the logical-consistency objection fails to capture the structural feature at play, rigorously distinguishing incommensurability from mere epistemic difficulty or emotional weight, explaining why the second-order residue is not reducible to ordinary regret, and noting where a utilitarian or cost-benefit calculus would be conceptually inappropriate without being able to resolve the conflict.

Third, provide a marking scheme totaling 25 marks. Separate an A answer (22–25 marks) from a B answer (18–21 marks) by naming the specific moves each grade requires and the specific mistakes that should cost marks—for example, conflating structural incommensurability with uncertainty about outcomes, treating the archivist’s choice as a simple optimization problem, or failing to identify the recursive second-order layer entirely. List each component with its mark weight, and include a one-line note on the typical student error associated with each component.

Keep the tone formal and polished. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not place any real philosophical text in verbatim quotation marks; if you reference standard positions, paraphrase them without quotation marks. Proceed with the deliverable once you have worked through the conceptual architecture.
