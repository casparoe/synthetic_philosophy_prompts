For our reading group on collective memory and defeated causes, I have been asked to set out my philosophical commitments so that you can subject them to audit. I should like the response in British English, please. Here is the profile:

I deny that collective entities possess diachronic moral agency; present citizens therefore do not inherit guilt for historical injustices committed by prior generations, though present structural inequities may generate remedial obligations.

I hold that certain military defeats—Dunkirk, Masada, Kosovo Polje—function as "foundational losses" that acquire moral-exemplary status through narrative transformation, but only when the cause defeated was not intrinsically unjust.

I maintain that commemorative objects (statues, street names, monuments) ought to be evaluated by their present social function rather than by the biographical virtue of the figure commemorated: if such an object currently operates to subordinate or exclude, removal or substantial recontextualisation is warranted, irrespective of the figure's historical context.

I believe that national myths are instrumentally necessary for liberal democratic solidarity, yet they ought to remain epistemically corrigible; however, where documented history and myth are so entangled that separating them destroys the myth's integrative power, corrigibility must yield.

I insist on a sharp distinction between honouring the resilience, sacrifice, or strategic brilliance of the defeated and endorsing the political cause for which they fought—thus one can admire the Dunkirk spirit without endorsing British imperial ambitions, or the Masada defenders without endorsing the Zealot programme.

Finally, I think the Lost Cause mythology of the Confederacy is uniquely corrupt not merely because the cause was unjust, but specifically because it denies that the defeat was morally required, thereby falsifying the moral structure of the conflict.

Please go through these commitments systematically: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict, rate the severity of each genuine tension, and propose the cheapest revision that would restore consistency for that pair. Apply the audit specifically to Confederate monuments and to Kosovo Polje, showing how the commitments interact in those cases. Then tell me which single revision—giving up or modifying one commitment—would clean up the most tensions at once. Do not provide a bibliography or reading list; I want an internal audit, not a literature review.
