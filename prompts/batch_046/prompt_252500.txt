I'm working through my position on justice and political authority and want a consistency audit before I present it. I distinguish justice-as-normative-property (J) from justice-as-procedural-outcome (J*), and I hold the following, mixing Pascal's analysis with commitments that go beyond him:

(1) I accept Pascal's core diagnosis: justice is epistemically disputable—reason alone cannot settle its content—so without force it remains powerless, while force without justice is mere violence. Legitimate authority requires their union.

(2) I am a realist about J: there are objective, mind-independent facts about distributive justice, and institutions can be evaluated against them regardless of popular agreement.

(3) Given intractable disagreement about justice, the appropriate political method is democratic deliberation aimed at reflective equilibrium; I take this to approximate J* rather than to directly track J.

(4) I reject consent-based legitimacy entirely: authority derives neither from explicit nor hypothetical agreement, but solely from whether an institution successfully couples effective force with justice in the Pascalian sense.

(5) Citizens have a duty to resist gravely unjust institutions—even through futile civil disobedience—whenever J is clearly violated, regardless of whether J* has been satisfied by democratic procedure.

(6) I also hold that maintaining social order is a weighty moral consideration that generally favors compliance with existing law, except when the deviation from J crosses a high threshold.

Check whether these hang together. I suspect (1) and (5) may look tense—if authority requires the union of justice and force, resisting authority seems to fracture that union—but perhaps the tension dissolves because (5) applies only when the union is already broken. More worryingly, I see potential hidden conflicts: between Pascal's skepticism about reason resolving justice and my reliance on deliberation in (3); between using J to evaluate institutions in (2) and (5) while defining legitimacy in (4) in terms that seem to make existing force decisive; and between (5) and (6), where my resistance threshold might be set arbitrarily to avoid contradiction. Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs, identify the pair that only looks tense, and tell me whether the package survives with minor adjustments or needs a major amputation. Please say how confident you are in this audit and single out the specific point you are least sure about.
