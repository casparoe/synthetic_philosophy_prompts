I'm noticing a weird asymetry in how people apply origin essentialism and I wonder if there's a name for it. The usual case is: could I have come from a different sperm and egg and still be me? Most people say no, because my DNA would differ. But then ask them about adoption — if my parents had adopted a kid instead of conceiving me, that kid wouldn't be me — and they agree, even though adoption replaces both genetic and gestational origins, not just one gamete. Then ask if I could've been born to the same parents five years later, different sperm/egg entirely, and alot of people say yes, thats still me. So genetic origin seems essential only when the social/parental frame is held constant, but not when the frame itself shifts. Is there an established concept for this context-dependent essentialism about biological origins, or is it just an unrecognized framing bias? I'm not asking about Kripke-style essentialism in general — I mean this specific pattern where people toggle between treating DNA as identity-fixing and treating it as disposable depending on what else changes in the scenario.
