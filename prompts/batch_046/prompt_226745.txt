Consider this position: Berkeley's master argument—that we cannot conceive of an object existing unperceived—should be extended to abstract entities (numbers, relations, universals). The extension claims that conceiving any abstract object still requires mental tokens or sensory-derived imagery; since these tokens are themselves perceived ideas, the abstract object cannot exist independently of being conceived. Thus esse est percipi applies not only to physical bodies but to all entities lacking mental existence.

3. Theoretical cost of insulation (approx. 150 words): If defenders dismiss the awkward defeaters by qualifying the imagery-dependence premise, does the position collapse into a narrower idealism or lose explanatory power?
