I have been discussing the relationship between phenomenal and access consciousness with colleagues, and we found ourselves returning repeatedly to global workspace theory. Write an essay that examines whether global workspace theory can genuinely accommodate phenomenal consciousness as distinct from access consciousness, or whether the distinction ultimately collapses once we accept that conscious experience requires global broadcasting across cognitive architectures. In particular, I want you to consider a specific framing that moves beyond the standard binary of local versus global processing: suppose we distinguish not only between information that is globally broadcast for verbal report and reasoning (access consciousness), but also an intermediate category in which information is available for non-verbal behavioural control, affective modulation, and implicit learning, yet remains resistant to explicit report (phenomenal availability without full access). Weigh the arguments that such intermediate states are either conceptually incoherent—on the grounds that any cognitive availability just is a form of broadcasting—or empirically plausible, given evidence from no-report paradigms in which subjects process stimuli without being able to report them. You should reach and justify a clear conclusion: does this three-tiered model (local sensory processing / phenomenal availability / global workspace access) successfully preserve phenomenal consciousness as something over and above access, or does it merely rename the explanatory gap? Please write your response in British English, and avoid relying on bibliographic references or citation lists.
