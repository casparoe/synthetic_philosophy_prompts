I teach an upper-level moral philosophy seminar and am writing the final exam. I want one essay question on the separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism that cannot be answered by summarizing the standard formulation associated with Rawls. The question should present a specific constructed case: a public-health policy that saves thousands from serious injury by imposing an identical, small, non-fatal risk on each member of a much smaller group, where statistical expectation shows net welfare gained but no particular individual is deterministically harmed. Students must be asked whether this counts as violating the separateness of persons, and they must do three things in their answer: distinguish the separateness complaint from both a rights-based veto and a fairness-based objection about unequal risk distribution; explain whether the core problem is treating ex ante probabilistic harms across distinct persons as fungible, or whether it only arises with ex post actual sacrifices; and state precisely what principle of interpersonal aggregation the utilitarian must reject if the objection succeeds. Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 800 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit moves with assigned weights totaling 20 marks. The scheme should specify, for each move, the precise error that costs marks—especially conflating separateness with distributive unfairness, or failing to address whether the objection applies when no victim actually materializes. The exam allows 90 minutes; do not include bibliographic prompts or citation requests.
