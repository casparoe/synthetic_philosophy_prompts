hey, i was trying to map out Dennett's two-bitser reply to Searle for a paper and i think i accidentally built a reductio that gives beliefs to trees. heres the chain, let me know where the wheels come off:

1. Searle says derived intentionality is always parasitic: symbols are about things only because users with original intentionality intend them to be.
2. Dennett's two-bitser is just two bits flipping in response to temperature; state A tracks "hot" and state B tracks "cold" without any conscious designer inside.
3. The point is that you don't need a homunculus or an original mind embedded in the system — all you need is a stable corrolation plus an interpreter who can use it predicitively.
4. So derived intentionality can be grounded purely in correlation + predictive utility, with no prior original intentionality required.
5. Now take a dead tree: its growth rings reliably correlate with rainfall, and we can use those rings to predict past droughts. There's no conscious user assigning meaning.
6. By (4), the tree's rings have derived intentionality about rainfall.
7. Dennett also denies any deep metaphysical gap between derived and original; he treats original intentionality as just very complex derived patterns that we take the intentional stance toward.
8. So the tree literally has beliefs about the weather.
9. That's clearly nuts.

I'd really like to hang onto (3) if i can — it seems right that interpretation doesnt need an inner homunculus. But (9) is absurd, so something has to go. Is there a hidden equivocation between "interpretable by an observer" and "representing for the system itself"? Or if I reject (7) and keep a hard line between derived and original, do I just end up back with Searle? Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
