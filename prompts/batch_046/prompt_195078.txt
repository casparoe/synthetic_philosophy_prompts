I am a student in an upper-level philosophy of biology seminar, and for our unit on genetic information I am trying to work through whether DNA is best understood as a literal syntactic code or as a chemically constrained template. I would like a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—call them A and B—who disagree on precisely this point, but I need the exchange to avoid the well-worn blueprint-versus-recipe framing.

A should defend the literal-code view: DNA involves an arbitrary symbol-to-output mapping (codons to amino acids), requires an interpreter (the ribosome and tRNA adaptors), exhibits near-universality alongside documented variant codes (for instance, in mitochondria), and includes error-correction mechanisms characteristic of engineered codes. B should defend the anti-metaphor view: the mapping is not arbitrary because chemical affinities constrain it—hydrophobic amino acids tend to be specified by codons with U in the second position—and variant codes are minor evolutionary adjustments rather than evidence of an underlying symbol system; DNA functions more like a direct chemical lookup table or physical template than like language or software, and invoking an interpreter smuggles in intentional design.

The dialogue must contain genuine back-and-forth: B should directly challenge whether chemical correlations undermine arbitrariness, and A should directly challenge how B explains near-universality plus genuinely different coding tables without positing an interpreter or designer. Neither side should be a strawman. The exchange should not request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and it should end with the disagreement clearly located—no concession—just an explicit statement of what hangs on the dispute. Aim for roughly 600 to 800 words.
