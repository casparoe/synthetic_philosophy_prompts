Argue that a culture cannot coherently choose shame over guilt as its dominant moral emotion, not merely because shame is psychologically damaging, but because the two emotions rest on incompatible epistemic architectures. Guilt, on this account, functions as an inside-view repair protocol: the agent retains first-person authority over both the recognition of wrong and the terms of redress. Shame, by contrast, functions as an outside-view attribution protocol: moral status is assigned by a collective gaze that the agent cannot internally verify. Show that institutionalizing shame therefore requires a culture to abandon the claim that moral agents have privileged access to their own failures, and that any culture attempting to do so will either collapse back into guilt-structures under another name or forfeit the normative authority it needs to justify the shame it imposes. You may use the familiar internal/external sanction distinction only as a preliminary framing; the core of your argument should address first-person epistemic authority and collective moral knowledge.
