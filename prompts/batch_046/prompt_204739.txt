My study group was arguing about Hume's standard of taste and whether it makes beauty objective. Jake said Hume basically gives us objectivity: he lists true judges with delicate sentiment, practice, comparison, and freedom from prejudice, so there are correct and incorrect taste judgements, just like with colour blindness. Maya replied that Hume still allows legitimate disagreement among qualified judges, especially across cultures, so the standard is just a consensus procedure, not proof of an independent fact. Jake then came up with this idea he called aesthetic calibration — he said we should treat expert taste like a calibrated instrument, so when two true judges disagree it's like two thermometers near different heat sources; the disagreement shows interference, not absence of an objective property. Maya said thats begging the question: thermometers measure temperature that exists independently, but there's no aesthetic heat in a painting causing the judgement. Jake answered that you don't need a physical cause — mathematical intuition is calibrated against proof without an independent physical reality, and Hume's judges work the same way, surviving peer scrutiny. Maya said maths converges almost totally, whereas taste never does — Bach versus Mozart is still open — so calling disagreement interference is just relabelling the disagreements you don't like. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right at each step? Don't just tell me who wins overall — I want to know where each argument holds up and what both of them are missing.
