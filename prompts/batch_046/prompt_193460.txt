Consider a philosopher applying Dennett-style criteria to distinguish genuine intentional systems from counterfeit people—entities that display behavioral competence suggestive of belief and desire without the internal architecture or evolutionary pedigree that warrant real intentional attribution. Her verdicts so far: a large language model that generates coherent first-person autobiography is a counterfeit person; a chimpanzee trained in symbolic communication is a genuine intentional system; a human with severe retrograde amnesia but intact emotional and social responsiveness remains a genuine person; a simple reflex-based robot that avoids obstacles is a mere mechanism, not even counterfeit; a legally incorporated corporation with formal decision-making bylaws is a counterfeit person; and a hypothetical philosophical zombie—behaviorally and physically identical to a human but lacking all conscious experience—is likewise a counterfeit person, though for reasons different from the language model. Next case: a synthetic neural organoid cultivated in vitro from human induced pluripotent stem cells, arranged in a non-cerebral three-dimensional lattice, that exhibits sustained, adaptive avoidance patterns in response to repeated noxious chemical stimuli and shows integrated information-processing dynamics across its nodes, but that possesses no sensory apparatus linking it to an external world, has never belonged to an evolutionary lineage, and was engineered solely for toxicity screening. Predict whether she will classify it as a genuine intentional system, a counterfeit person, or a mere mechanism. Identify at least two distinct principles consistent with her track record—for instance, one requiring evolutionary embodiment and world-interactive sensory-motor coupling, another requiring only the right kind of biological functional architecture irrespective of evolutionary origin, and possibly a third centered on whether intentional attribution is genuinely warranted regardless of substrate. For each principle, state its specific prediction about the organoid. Give your best guess of her actual verdict, with reasoning that explains which principle the earlier cases most strongly support and why. Finally, describe concretely the single further case that would most clearly adjudicate between the principles you have identified.
