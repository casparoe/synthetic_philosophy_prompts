I want to avoid reducing Burke to a mere reactionary caricature. Given his actual writings on the East India Company—especially his conviction that legitimate authority depends on continuity with inherited moral habits and his suspicion of governance by abstract, universal reason—how do you think he would assess the competing claims? Burke generally rejected hypothetical reasoning about political legitimacy in favor of judging institutions by their actual, continuous possession. Would he therefore dismiss both balance sheets as philosophically illegitimate, or might he find one counterfactual framework more compatible with his doctrine of prescription?
