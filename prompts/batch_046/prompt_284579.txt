ive been trying to figure out why the separateness-of-persons objection to utilitarianism never actually moves anyone. both sides agree on the classic cases -- like the surgeon who kills one healthy patient to harvest organs for five dying ones -- and they both agree the utilitarian answer feels wrong. but then the debate just loops: one side says welfare is just additive, the other says its not fungible across people. my working theory is that theyre not really arguing about arithmetic at all. maybe one side thinks moral reasons are always indexed to particular individuals and cant be pooled into some collective bucket, while the other side assumes there is a perfectly coherent impersonal calculus that just happens to be hard to apply. if thats right, is this a hidden metaphysical disagreement about whether moral reasons can exist for no one in particular? or is it verbal -- maybe separateness just means different things to each camp? and if its substantive, what kind of case or argument could actually break the deadlock, or should we just accept its bedrock?
