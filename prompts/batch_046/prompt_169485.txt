I'm part of a three-person land-use review board for the Cascadia watershed coalition, and every other Tuesday we vote on development permits that pit ecological preservation against tribal fishing rights and local recreation access. The stakes are high—if we reject something the developer appeals to the state, but if we approve a project that wrecks a salmon run the damage is basically irreversable. I was arguing with a friend who took a philosophy course about whether some values are just incommensurable, like you can't really trade "sacredness of the river" against "jobs created" on one shared scale. She mentioned Ruth Chang's idea that hard choices aren't just about missing information; they're about values that genuinely don't reduce to eachother. Anyway, I want to turn that into an actual decision procedue I can run in about 45 minutes with the other board members, none of whom are philosophers, and we need all three signatures.

Can you design a checklist or flowchart-in-words that takes incommensurability seriously? It should have clear steps: first identify which values are in play and flag any that act as "sacred" or non-compensatory for a stakeholder; then an articulation step where each board member has to explain what the value actually means for them instead of just ranking it 1-10; then a "residual choice" phase where, if nothing dominates, we pick the option that best fits the coalition's long-term narrative rather than pretending we can maximize across incommensurable goods. The procedure needs to handle three cases: (1) a small hydroelectric retrofit that helps climate goals but disrupts a ceremonial site; (2) a trail expansion that boosts tourism revenue but fragments bear habitat; and (3) a logging-road proposal that technically passes all articulation tests but violates my own gut sense of the forest's integrity—here I want you to say exactly what the procedure outputs and why I might still hate it.

Also be honest about where this breaks down: where does judgment still sneak in? How could a developer game the articulation step? And please don't ask me for citations or a reading list—half the board wouldn't read them anyway, and I'm writing this for people who don't have time to look up papers.
