The leaves must include: strong Kripkean origin essentialism, genetic-content essentialism, parental-origin essentialism, contingent-origin anti-essentialism, and at least one combination that appears unoccupied, which you should flag as either incoherent or merely unexplored. For every leaf, provide the position's standard name if it has one, otherwise mark it "unnamed," and state in one sentence its most awkward commitment (for example, whether it must treat an in-vitro-gametogenesis twin with identical DNA as the same possible person, or whether it must deny that mitochondrial-replacement children share origins with their nuclear parents). Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the instrument should be usable without library access. Limit the tree to six or seven leaves, and phrase every node as a question or claim that a reader could answer without already knowing which interpretation it leads to.
