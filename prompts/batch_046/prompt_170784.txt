When I feel a sharp physical pain or a sudden surge of anxiety, I seem to know immediately and accurately what is happening inside me. But when I ask myself why I made a particular choice—whether I acted generously or out of a hidden desire for approval—the answer feels less like direct observation and more like a story I construct from my behavior and circumstances. From this I've developed a tentative position: introspection is genuinely reliable for immediate affective and sensory states, but it is not a direct window onto our motives, beliefs, or reasoning processes; those are interpreted, not observed. Tell me what would genuinely count against this split-reliability view. Which of these potential defeaters would a committed defender of this split view have to concede as damaging, and which could they dismiss as missing the point?
