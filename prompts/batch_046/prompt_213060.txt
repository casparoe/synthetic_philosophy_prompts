I am drafting a novel that examines the moral texture of abundance, and for a pivotal scene I require a precisely engineered thought experiment. I wish to isolate a tension within Keynes's vision of the good life — particularly his distinction between the economic problem, which he believed might one day be solved, and what he termed the permanent problem of mankind: how to live wisely and agreeably once pressing material cares have fallen away. To ensure the case is not something a model might merely rehearse from common commentary, please inject a niche conceptual condition that I shall call elective anhedonia: a state in which an individual, freed from scarcity, discovers that activities pursued solely for intrinsic pleasure feel hollow precisely because they lack the resistance or social purpose that necessity once supplied.

Construct, in a single realistic paragraph set in an institutional rather than science-fictional context, a decision problem for a protagonist employed as a curator in a municipal heritage centre where the economic problem has indeed been solved. She must choose between continuing her curatorial work — preserving obsolete craft techniques that no longer serve any economic function — and abandoning it for an unstructured practice of aesthetic play, such as learning an instrument with no audience or pedagogical aim. Design the scenario so that two competing interpretations of Keynes's good life deliver opposite verdicts on her choice, with the divergence arising neither from hidden financial constraint nor from an obviously deficient principle. One interpretation should hold that the good life demands the liberation of leisure into pure, non-instrumental activity; the other should hold that Keynes's civilised life requires the maintenance of disciplined, socially embedded practices even — or especially — when they are no longer economically necessary. After presenting the scenario, analyse what each interpretation says and why, identifying the specific structural feature of the case that forces the divergence. Then state what happens to the disagreement if that feature is removed, and explain how a defender of the interpretation that fares worse would attack the scenario as unfair or misrepresentative of Keynes's view. The conflict, if any, should fall out of a principle that is genuinely optimal within its interpretation, not from a rule adopted carelessly. Write in a formal, polished register suitable for a novelist's research notes, using British spelling throughout.
