As a retired physicist who has only recently begun reading philosophy of science in earnest, I have been trying to understand whether Malthus’s theory can be called genuinely falsified, or merely “right but early,” given that its predicted asymptotic endpoint—population permanently outstripping food supply—has not arrived globally after two centuries, even though local resource pressures clearly generate positive checks. I have constructed the following argument, which now strikes me as suspicious because it seems to collapse long-run social predictions into physical ones and then prove far too much. (1) If a theory predicts an asymptotic outcome and that outcome is absent from every observable intermediate window, then the theory is falsified. (2) Malthus predicted that exponential population growth must eventually outpace arithmetic growth in subsistence, producing inevitable positive checks. (3) Over the past two centuries global food production per capita has risen, and the predicted universal catastrophe is absent from every intermediate window we can observe; therefore Malthus is falsified. (4) By the same reasoning, stellar evolution predicts the Sun will become a red giant in roughly five billion years, and statistical mechanics predicts an eventual thermodynamic heat death; neither endpoint is observable now, yet we do not treat these theories as falsified. (5) So if we apply (1) consistently, we must either reject stellar evolution and thermodynamics, or reject (1) itself. (6) Rejecting (1) for physics but retaining it for social theory is special pleading. (7) Hence either falsification is useless for all long-run predictions, or Malthus survives—both conclusions seem wrong. Where exactly does this chain fail? I would prefer to keep the intuition that Malthus was genuinely mistaken about the global trajectory rather than merely premature, but I want to know which premise I must abandon and what it costs me—particularly whether there is a principled distinction, drawn from how physical theories are corroborated at intermediate scales through mechanism-level verification rather than endpoint observation, that can salvage falsification for Malthus without saving every distant physical prediction.
