I'm on the review panel for our uni's Behavioural Rationality Lab, and we've got a pilot study up for approval that puts anxiety patients through betting exercises structured exactly like Dutch books — if your credences about risk don't satisfy the probability axioms, the bets are arranged so you lose for certain. The researchers say this is therapeutic: it makes incoherence literally costly and forces belief revision. But half the panel thinks Dutch-book arguments only show norms for betting behaviour, not for belief itself; treating them as clinical justification feels like a category mistake, and we're exposing vulnerable people to guaranteed financial loss on shaky philosophical ground. I need a short memo for Thursday's meeting recommending approve, reject, or conditional approval with a concrete timeline — say, a six-month pilot with restricted stakes if we're going that route. It should take the researchers' view seriously rather than just restating that beliefs ought to be probabilistic, and it needs an honest account of the strongest objection on the other side. Please don't include a bibliography or reading list — I need a document the commitee can vote on, not a literature survey.
