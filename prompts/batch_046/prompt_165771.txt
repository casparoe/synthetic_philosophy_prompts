I'm debating with my partner whether to go through with an IVF protocol that carries a high likelihood of triplets. Our town is already under water-rationing and acute housing-pressure, so every additional resident measurably dilutes the per-capita share of strained resources. My partner argues that because these particular children, if born, will almost certainly lead genuinely good lives, we are creating value that otherwise would not exist at all; and since they will not be present to suffer from the crowding they cause, while current residents face only a diffuse, slight inconvenience, the moral ledger comes out positive, or at worst neutral. I counter that deliberately expanding the population when baseline conditions are already tight feels like treating people as fungible units of welfare rather than honoring a duty not to worsen a shared situation—especially since a small drop in conditions across the board might eventually push some vulnerable existing residents below a decent threshold. My partner replies that I am illicitly attributing rights to merely possible people, and that if we reject this reasoning consistently we would have to condemn every family that moves into a dense city. I cannot tell whether my discomfort reflects a real moral constraint or is merely an aesthetic aversion to crowding dressed up as ethics. How should I think about whether bringing these specific lives into existence is the right thing to do?
