I am a high school student working through some philosophy of identity and biology, and I keep returning to the question of whether I could have originated from a different sperm and egg. The classic essentialist intuition—often traced to discussions of modal origin—holds that my actual biological beginning is necessary: had my parents conceived a child from different gametes, that child might have been human, but not me. Yet critics respond that this treats a contingent biological event as metaphysically fixed, and they point out that if my parents had conceived earlier or later, I might still be psychologically continuous with that other zygote. For decades, philosophers have traded arguments about counterfactual identity, the identity conditions of organisms, and whether personal identity tracks biology or psychology, but the disagreement seems to loop back on itself without anyone conceding ground.

My own suspicion is that the stalemate is partly a hidden disagreement about explanatory standards rather than a pure metaphysical dispute. One camp seems to treat "origin" as a formal feature of object-identity—if two organisms share all intrinsic properties except causal genesis, they remain numerically distinct—while the other camp treats origin as empirically contingent because modern developmental biology reveals substantial stochastic noise in zygote formation, early cell division, and epigenetic regulation. If that is right, the debate may be mixing a logical thesis about necessary properties with an empirical thesis about how robust biological development actually is, and neither side directly addresses the other. I want a diagnosis, not a survey of positions: is this impasse substantive, verbal, or a case of two questions—one about metaphysical essential properties, one about the determinism of biological development—being pursued under the single heading of "origin essentialism"? And if it is the latter, could a conceptual distinction—say, between genetic origin and developmental trajectory—actually resolve anything, or is the disagreement ultimately grounded in competing intuitions about what "I" fundamentally am?

Please explain what kind of disagreement this really is, using the concrete positions and arguments I have described, and tell me honestly whether anything—an empirical finding about embryogenesis, a formal result in modal logic, or a clarification of terms—should be expected to break the deadlock, or whether the question is simply ill-posed and should be reformulated.
