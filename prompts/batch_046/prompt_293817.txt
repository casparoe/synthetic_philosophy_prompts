Write an essay on whether Nietzsche's tripartite distinction among monumental, antiquarian, and critical history collapses under its own weight when critical history is turned back upon historiography itself. The essay should weigh the argument that critical judgment necessarily borrows its normative standards from either monumental exemplarity or antiquarian accumulation, and therefore cannot function as an autonomous mode that serves life by breaking with the past. Reach a conclusion—either that critical history survives as a genuinely independent practice, or that it is structurally parasitic and therefore self-undermining—and justify it with reasons drawn from what each mode actually does. Structure the response as exactly five parts, each a sustained paragraph without a separate introduction or conclusion: first, the dependency problem of how critical judgment acquires its criteria; second, the catalog risk that destroying the past turns into an antiquarian preservation of error; third, the technological condition of whether total digital archiving has made active forgetting, and hence critical history, impossible in principle; fourth, a proposed resolution or synthesis; fifth, the implications for whether history can still serve life. State explicitly what argument or discovery—such as a concrete case in which critical judgment generated its own standards ex nihilo, or proof that all normative historiography implicitly appeals to one of the other two modes—would change your conclusion, and identify where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
