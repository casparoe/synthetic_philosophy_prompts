I'm assembling a classification instrument for a long-running debate about what Descartes' cogito actually proves — not the freshman-philosophy version, but the kind of fine-grained mapping you'd use in a rationalist forum thread where everyone already knows the Meditations and is tired of being sorted into "dualism" versus "materialism." I want a decision tree that takes a single starting commitment — "the cogito succeeds in establishing at least one genuinely indubitable result" — and branches through four diagnostic claims, each phrased as something a reader could affirm or deny without already knowing the interpretive label it points to.

The four nodes, in order, should be:
(1) Is the "I" in "I think, therefore I am" identified via a substantive criterion (e.g., continuity of consciousness, substancehood) or is it a bare logical subject with no identifying conditions?
(2) Does the argument establish the existence of a particular thinking thing, or only the occurrence of a mental event, with selfhood being a secondary attribution?
(3) Is the proof's certainty essentially indexical — it collapses if reformulated in the third person ("he thinks, therefore he is") — or is it structurally generalizable to any instance of thinking?
(4) Does the cogito discover a pre-existing self-model, or does it performatively construct the self through the act of doubting, analogous to a bootstrap in self-modeling?

The leaves must include at least: (A) a Cartesian substance interpretation; (B) a Humean bundle-style interpretation; (C) an existentialist/performativity interpretation; (D) a logical-behaviorist deflationary interpretation; and (E) one unnamed hybrid that answers (1) descriptively, (2) particular, (3) generalizable, and (4) discovery. For each leaf, provide: the position's name or "unnamed"; a one-sentence statement of what, on that view, the cogito proves; and the single most awkward commitment that view must accept relative to the others.

In addition, flag any pair of leaves that share answers on all four nodes and therefore cannot be separated by this tree, and specify what further distinction (not already on the tree) would be required to split them. Also note whether the unnamed hybrid leaf is genuinely unoccupied because incoherent, because unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Please begin with the tree itself — nodes as explicit either/or questions, leaves clearly labeled — followed immediately by the leaf descriptions, separation flags, and diagnosis of the unnamed combination. No preface, no restatement of this prompt, and no bibliography, citations, or reading list.
