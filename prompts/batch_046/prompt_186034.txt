I have been trying to work out a parallel that a philosophy of science lecturer mentioned only in passing: Darwinian natural selection and Turing-style mechanized reasoning both seem to realize what is often called competence without comprehension—reliable, adaptive, or correct outputs produced in the absence of any understanding of why they work—yet each does so through a distinctive inversion of ordinary explanatory reasoning that popular treatments tend to flatten. In the Darwinian case, the inversion moves us from inferring an intelligent designer from functional complexity to explaining that same complexity through blind variation and differential retention, so that the organism behaves as if it comprehended its environment while possessing no representational grasp of it whatsoever.
