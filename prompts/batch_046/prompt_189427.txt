I'm on the university's Legacy Automated Systems Oversight Committee — kind of an obscure board, but we have to vote Thursday on whether to keep funding this normative inference engine that's been running in the server farm since like 2018. It was built for a research project that's long dead; now it just generates distributive-justice prescriptions every hour based on formal axioms, but nobody reads the logs, no policy uses it, and there's literally no human reasoner engaging with its outputs. The philosophical question is whether those outputs are free-floating rationales — reasons that exist and carry weight independent of any agent — or whether they're just meaningless computation without a reasoner. Half the committee thinks reasons necessarily require reasoners, so keeping it running is institutional vanity. The other half thinks logical-normative relations can stand on thier own, and shutting it down would be ignoring genuinely valid prescriptions just because we're not listening. Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. Recommend either continued funding, discontinuation, or standby maintenance with conditions. Give concrete reasoning the committee can actually respond to, and seriously engage the best argument for the side you don't take — especially the worry that an unread reason can't wrong anyone, so funding it has no moral point. Don't just summarize the debate; take a stand.
