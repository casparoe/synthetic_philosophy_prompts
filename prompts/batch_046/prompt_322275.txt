I'm trying to figure out my own rules for when I should trust my gut versus when I should bow to the crowd, and I'm going in circles. Here's what I actually think, case by case.

If I'm writing a scene and I feel in my bones that a character's grief should come out as silence rather than tears, I don't care if every writing workshop pushes show-dont-tell or if beta readers want more emotion. My inside view wins there. But if I'm deciding whether to vaccinate my kid, and I've read like three papers but every epidemiologist says it's safe, I defer immediately - my inside view is basically noise.

But then their's chess. I'll see what I think is a brilliant move, but if the engine says I'm down two pawns, I ignore my intuition completely. That feels right. But in writing, when an editor tells me a twist violates reader expectations based on sales data for similar books, I usually push back. So why do I defer to the chess engine but not to the market data?

Also, I have this friend who always seems fine but I have this nagging sense he's not okay. The base rate for people hiding depression is high, but I know his specific history - he gets quiet exactly like this when he's bad. I go with my specific read, not the statistics. But when I tried a new migraine drug and I swore it helped, even though the double-blind study said it was no better than sugar pill, I... actually kept taking it? That seems inconsistent with the vaccine case.

And here's the one that breaks me: I'm deciding whether to believe my own memory of a conversation that happened ten years ago. I remember it clearly, but everyone else present remembers it differently. Usually I'd say my inside view is unreliable because memory is reconstructive, so I should defer to the group. But if I'm writing that same scene in a memoir, I use my own memory as the ground truth, even if others disagree. Is their a principle that says defer outside when there's an objective check, except in art and close relationships? Or is that just me rationalizing?

Can someone map this out? What's the actual principle behind when inside view gets veto power and when it's just arrogance? And which of my verdicts do I have to drop if I want the principle to be clean?
