I'm preparing interview questions for a philosophy podcast episode featuring an unnamed defender of a modified Strawsonian position. The guest accepts Galen Strawson's Basic Argument—that true moral responsibility requires being the cause of oneself in a way that generates an infinite regress, hence is impossible for beings with natural developmental origins—but she restricts its scope: she argues the regress only undermines responsibility for agents whose initial conditions are "given" through evolutionary or environmental history, not for synthetic agents whose design parameters are deliberately chosen by an external creator. From this she derives a "domain-relative" view: moral responsibility is globally impossible for humans but could in principle obtain for artificial agents with authored origins. Draft ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. Include at least two that press whether this restriction saves Strawson's conclusion or merely relocates the problem of origin; at least one that asks how she answers Strawson's own insistence that even a creator-selected origin requires the creator itself to be responsible, generating a parallel regress; one that challenges whether the natural-versus-designed distinction is metaphysically principled or merely pragmatic; and one that asks what empirical or conceptual evidence could show that a synthetic agent genuinely escapes the regress rather than just hiding it one level up. For each question, provide a brief follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer—especially her anticipated appeal to the difference between given and chosen initial conditions. The audience is familiar with the free-will debate but not with this particular modification. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or bibliographies.
