Write an essay evaluating whether the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) or the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) offers the correct framework for anthropic reasoning, with particular focus on the presumptuous-philosopher objection developed in Nick Bostrom's work on anthropic bias. Your essay should do four things. First, reconstruct the core logic of SSA and SIA and explain why they yield opposite verdicts in both the doomsday argument and the Sleeping Beauty problem. Second, evaluate the presumptuous-philosopher objection—that a rational agent should not treat her own existence as strong evidence for cosmological theories that make observers like her extraordinarily common—under each framework, saying which assumption is more vulnerable to it and why. Third, introduce and defend a modified principle—name it the Reference-Class-Weighted Indication Principle—that attempts to split the difference by conditioning observer-selection not on bare existence but on some structural feature of the reference class you specify; explain how this modification changes the arithmetic in at least one standard case. Fourth, reach and justify a definitive conclusion: either defend SSA, defend SIA, or defend your modified principle as the best response to both the presumptuousness charge and the counterintuitive predictions of large-world cosmologies. Do not end non-committally; your conclusion should state clearly which framework survives scrutiny and on what grounds.
