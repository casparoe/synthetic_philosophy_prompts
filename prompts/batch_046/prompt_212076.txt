I am organizing a family reunion that will bring together roughly two hundred relatives traveling from different cities. Over the past months I have spoken with each person individually, and in every single case I find myself genuinely convinced that this particular relative will attend—each has confirmed, made travel arrangements, and given me a compelling personal reason to be present. At the same time, I know from long experience with gatherings of this size that it is practically certain several people will face last-minute emergencies, illnesses, or transportation failures and will not appear. I therefore seem to hold two convictions that pull in opposite directions: taken one by one, I believe each relative will come; taken all together, I believe that not all of them will come.

This tension is not abstract, because I must make concrete commitments. I need to reserve a banquet hall, order catered meals, and arrange seating and lodging. If I plan for the full two hundred and a dozen cancel, I waste substantial money and leave awkward empty spaces that embarrass those who do come. If I plan for a smaller number and nearly everyone shows, I run out of food and seats and cause real harm to guests I turned away. To manage this, I have started keeping a handwritten ledger: on one page I list each relative with the note "expected," and on a separate page I write an overall statistical judgment that perhaps fifteen percent will fail to appear. But I cannot see how both pages can be correct simultaneously, and I do not know which page should govern my decisions.

My question is not about party logistics. I want to understand whether my reasoning itself is flawed. Is it rational to maintain a belief about every individual that conflicts with a belief about the entire group? And if these two frameworks genuinely cannot be reconciled, which one ought to guide my actions—the specific convictions about each person, or the general expectation about the aggregate? More broadly, does the fact that I must sign contracts and spend money force me to choose one framework over the other, or is there a consistent way to hold both and still act wisely?
