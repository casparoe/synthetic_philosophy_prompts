trying to work out why human extinction feels like a special kind of bad, not just adding up the deaths. i keep coming back to that Scheffler point about caring for future people we'll never meet, but i'm not sure thats the whole story. here are my gut verdicts. if we all die in 50 years but every current long-term project gets finished first: still bad, but less crushing. if extinction is replaced by AI that keeps doing science and art: bad, but maybe not the same kind of loss? if there's a self-sustaining colony on mars and earth dies out: less bad, since the human story continues. but if extinction is inevitable in 1000 years and we just learn that now, my work suddenly feels pointless -- yet if we cause it through negligence it feels like betrayal, not just tragedy. also, if we replace ourselves with beings who have way more pleasure per life, i still think losing us is a loss, not a wash. and there's this case i'm torn on: what if we voluntairy end the species to prevent suffering, but leave behind a perfect archive? is there one principle behind all these, or do i have to drop one of the verdicts?
