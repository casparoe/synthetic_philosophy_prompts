For a seminar on historiographical theory I am drafting my position on historical objectivity and want a consistency audit before I present it. My commitments are these:

(1) I treat Ranke's demand that history show things as they actually happened not as an achievable description but as a regulative ideal that constrains interpretation.
(2) I accept Carr's argument that historical facts are not discovered in raw form but are selected and arranged according to the historian's present concerns and questions.
(3) I take Hayden White's tropological analysis seriously: the available narrative modes (metaphor, metonymy, synecdoche, irony) structure understanding of the past prior to empirical inquiry rather than being derived from it.
(4) I defend what I call the modal inertia thesis: once a document is archived, its evidential weight remains invariant across different narrative frames, meaning White's tropes can reorder significance but cannot alter what the source licenses.
(5) I hold the negative-archive view: missing, destroyed, or never-created records should be assigned the same epistemic status as surviving documents, treated as data rather than as gaps to be filled by inference.

Please evaluate whether these hang together. Rank any pairwise tensions by severity, distinguishing those that are merely apparent from those that are genuine. For each genuine tension, specify the least costly revision available and what it costs. I am not signaling which commitment I would prefer to abandon. Finally, state what argument or piece of evidence would lead you to change your verdict about the coherence of this package, and flag the point in your reasoning most likely to fail—whether that concerns the interpretation of White, the coherence of modal inertia, or the treatment of absence as data.
