Please design a branching taxonomy for a graduate seminar handout that sorts philosophical positions on whether germs, climate patterns, and volcanic eruptions can function as historical actors—that is, as entities that do non-reducible explanatory work in narratives of human events—without collapsing into environmental determinism. The taxonomy should be navigable by someone who grasps the conceptual distinctions but does not yet know the literature’s standard labels.

Structure the tree through the following splits. First, the foregrounding split: can non-human forces such as pathogens, long-term climate regimes, or volcanic events be assigned explanatory roles that are not merely background conditions? Second, for those who answer yes, the directedness split: does such foregrounding require attributing a minimal, non-conscious form of agency or directedness, or can it be sustained through purely structural causation? Third, for all branches, the determinism-firewall split: does recognizing these causal contributions risk sliding into environmental determinism, and if so, is the defense scale-sensitive causation (human choice operates at a different temporal or organizational scale), modal pluralism (causes do not entail deterministic outcomes), or narrative framing (agency-language is metaphorical but indispensable for explanation)? Fourth, a final split on narrative responsibility: if a pathogen or eruption counts as a historical actor, does this diminish the moral or political accountability of human institutions that failed to anticipate or adapt?

Each leaf should carry either a recognized position name—such as soft environmental determinism, structural causationism, distributed-agency instrumentalism, or narrative instrumentalism—or the label unnamed, together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. For example, one leaf might be forced to claim that the Black Death actively reshaped European feudal labor markets while simultaneously denying that it possessed any form of directedness whatsoever. The tree must contain at least eight leaves. Where a logical combination of answers appears to have no defenders, mark it as unoccupied and briefly diagnose whether it seems incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of positions that the tree fails to distinguish, and specify what further diagnostic question would separate them. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate for a philosophy-of-history syllabus.
