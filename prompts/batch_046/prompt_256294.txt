I am trying to understand how Locke, given his well-known argument that personal identity consists in the continuity of consciousness and memory rather than in the persistence of any material substance, would assess the moral and metaphysical status of the subject who eventually awakens.

Please predict Locke’s position on this specific variant of gradual replacement. In doing so, please keep the reconstruction tightly anchored to Locke’s actual framework—distinguishing what his view of consciousness-based identity genuinely requires from what modern functionalist or information-theoretic commentators tend to import—rather than simply projecting contemporary intuitions about uploading onto him. If, however, my framing already smuggles in premises that Locke would reject—perhaps by treating synthetic substrates as capable of sustaining the kind of reflexive consciousness he describes, or by assuming that interrupted replacement poses a problem his account was designed to solve—then please say so plainly and explain why the question as posed cannot be answered well, rather than offering a polite but forced reconstruction.
