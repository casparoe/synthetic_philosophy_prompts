Write a short research proposal for a three-month independent study. The focus is the three canonical failures of Athenian democracy: the assembly’s rapid reversal of the Mytilene massacre decree, the strategic collapse of the Sicilian expedition, and the trial and execution of Socrates. Rather than treating these as separate moral, strategic, and juridical failures, pose a precise philosophical question: do they reveal a single, undertheorized dysfunction in democratic collective reasoning—specifically, an inability to distinguish rational persistence in a commitment from irrational path dependence once collective identity has formed around a decision? Explain why existing literature, which isolates each episode within its own disciplinary frame, fails to settle whether democratic bodies possess a coherent logic of self-correction. Propose an approach that analyzes the decision dynamics conceptually through the primary historical narratives, contrasting democratic revision with precommitment in practical reason, and argue why an institutional-design or empirical-comparative framework would miss the conceptual problem. Define a deliverable: a typology of democratic reversal that distinguishes legitimate consistency, warranted repudiation, and irrational persistence, applied to the three cases. State clearly what success and failure would look like—success being a defensible unified framework, failure being proof that the cases share no common logical structure. Note that references and bibliography will be compiled separately, and keep the scope appropriate to a single semester.
