everyone in my philosphy circles keeps saying that finding meaning under absurdism is like curating a museum that exists only inside your own head — you choose the exhibits, write the wall text, and there is no board of trustees or funding body to tell you the collection is wrong. people lean on this to argue that absurdism actually frees us: since cosmic purpose is missing, self-authored meaning is all there is, and we should stop worrying about whether our lives are 'objectively' valuable. i want you to audit how far that analogy holds. give me a correspondence table — what maps onto the curator, the museum walls, the stolen artifacts that stand for inherited social norms, the visitors (if any), and the missing board — then tell me where it breaks, especially around whether a purely internal museum can be *about* anything outside itself, or whether it secretly depends on representational realism that the absurdist wants to reject, and whether you recieve any validation at all without an external audience. also, does the liberating conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument? if it collapses, suggest a better one — maybe something closer to maintaining an open-source repo with no upstream maintainer, or whatever actually captures the relation between self-commitment and the absence of external validation without smuggling in a hidden spectator.
