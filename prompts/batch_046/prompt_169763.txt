There's this thing in Nietzsche scholarship thats been stuck for years: people arguing about whether all his talk about diet, digestion, and climate in the letters and late autobiographical writing is actually philosophically serious, or just biographical noise from a hypochondriac. One camp says it's central to his idea of great health and the body as a site of will-to-power; the other camp says its just personal quirk that scholars overread because they want every grocery list to be metaphysics. They both cite the same passages about bad digestion in Turin and nobody moves. I think part of the stall might be that one side is asking what did Nietzsche mean by health and the other is asking should we use his diet as evidence for his philosophy — but I'm not sure. Is this actually a verbal disagreement about what counts as philosophical content? Or is there a hidden empirical question about whether his physical symptoms influenced his late writings? Or maybe its bedrock intuition about whether the body can ever be a philosophical subject. And here's a niche twist I want you to consider: what if we split his dietary remarks into gastronomic asceticism, where food discipline amplifies creative force, versus digestive ressentiment, where he blames climate and meals for internal weakness? Does that distinction break the deadlock, or just rename it? Tell me what kind of disagreement this really is, and what — if anything — would actually count as progress here: a new archival find, a conceptual distinction, or should we just admit the question is ill-posed?
