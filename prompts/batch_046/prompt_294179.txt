Write a reusable decision procedure for a transit-safety adjudicator who must classify “absent-minded” operator errors—cases where a driver or pilot reaches a destination or misses a signal without occurrent awareness of doing so, the classic absent-minded driver problem translated to institutional review. Ground the procedure in the philosophy of action: distinguish intact practical intentionality with a failure of occurrent monitoring (the agent’s plan genuinely guides the movement, but declarative self-tracking lapses), degraded practical intentionality (the movement is not guided by the agent’s current plan, though it looks purposeful), and pure automatic or system-override behavior (no relevant intentional structure is present). The procedure should be ordered: list the evidentiary questions to ask in sequence, specify what counts as a sufficient answer at each node, identify the exact stopping points where a classification is reached, and flag the nodes where the question is one of values rather than facts—e.g., whether an absence of awareness should excuse liability or mandate retraining regardless of mental state. Include explicit hard cases the procedure must handle: an experienced operator thinking about a personal crisis while driving a familiar route; an operator using a predictive assist that suppresses manual input; and an operator who has followed a temporary detour for months, then suddenly realizes he has missed his assigned stop but continues. Apply the procedure to three worked cases: (A) a veteran bus driver replaying a conversation in his head, missing every intermediate stop but arriving at the terminus he intended; (B) a train operator relying on a new predictive braking system, failing to apply manual brakes, passing a red signal, and later claiming he “wasn’t really driving”; and (C) an operator who automatically takes a construction detour for three months, suddenly “wakes up” past his stop, yet completes the wrong route. One of these applications should yield a classification that the authority is likely to reject.
