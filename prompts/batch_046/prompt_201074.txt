After a career modeling self-correcting dynamical systems, I have turned to philosophy of law and am trying to understand constitutional precommitment — specifically, the idea of a Ulysses contract that binds future legislatures. Please turn this principle into a reusable decision procedure that a parliamentary rules committee could apply when drafting entrenchment clauses. The procedure must contain four ordered steps: first, a diagnostic test that distinguishes structural commitments from contingent policy preferences; second, an epistemic lock-in check that asks whether the clause would block future correction in light of new evidence; third, a calibration rule linking the required amendment threshold to the relative rates of value drift versus factual drift expected for the domain; and fourth, explicit flag points where judgment is irreducibly political and must be handed back to representatives rather than resolved by the procedure. Apply the procedure to two proposals: entrenching the independence of a central bank, and entrenching a prohibition on using emergency powers for economic redistribution. In each case, show where the procedure stops with a determinate answer and where it yields ambiguity. End with a single line beginning Verdict: summarizing whether constitutional precommitment can be fully proceduralized or whether it necessarily retains a non-eliminable normative residue.
