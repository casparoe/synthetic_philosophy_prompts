I am auditing a graduate seminar on evolutionary approaches to culture, and after a long argument in my study group, two positions have hardened against each other without either side being able to articulate exactly where the other goes wrong. One side—call them the Strong Memeticists—defends the view that cultural transmission operates through discrete, high-fidelity replicators that spread and compete for cognitive and social niches with a dynamics that is genuinely Darwinian, not merely metaphorical; they emphasize cases where ideas propagate with minimal intentional redesign, such as viral misinformation patterns or standardized notational conventions. The opposing side—the Agency-Theoretic Critics—argues that every instance of cultural uptake involves active interpretation, contextual reconstruction, and power-mediated selection, so that the gene-meme analogy collapses at its core: there is no analog to blind variation or faithful replication, only intelligent agency operating at the point of transmission. Rather than continue arguing past each other, we want to produce the document an adversarial collaboration should yield.

Please draft our joint statement. It should open with a numbered section of agreed claims—about the observable dynamics of cultural spread, about the formal conditions required for any evolutionary explanation, and about the specific domains where the gene-culture analogy succeeds or clearly breaks down—written in neutral wording that both camps could sign. Follow this with a disagreements table: each camp's own position stated in wording that its members would accept for themselves, with the precise divergence isolated rather than caricatured. Then identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side—explicitly including (a) the replication-boundary problem, i.e., whether a cultural unit can retain sufficient identity across contexts despite necessary interpretive adaptation to count as the same replicator, and (b) whether intentional redesign at the point of uptake undermines evolutionary dynamics or merely reshapes the selection environment. For each crux, provide a concrete comparative case that both sides agree is diagnostically relevant—specifically, contrast the transmission of a modified oral mnemonic in a closed ritual tradition with the transmission of a standardized mathematical notation across independent scholarly communities—together with each side's explicit prediction about what the case demonstrates regarding that crux. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; we are working solely from shared conceptual understanding, and the prompt should remain answerable without external lookup. Keep the framing conceptually precise but avoid invoking ultra-specialist constructs that depend on obscure literature; the goal is to force both positions to become exact about where they genuinely diverge.
