I've been arguing with my friend Maya about incommensurable values and hard choices — she's got pretty firm verdicts on specific cases. She thinks picking between being a surgeon and being a poet is a real hard choice; you can rationaly go either way and neither dominates. She thinks picking between two residencies where one saves about fifty more lives a year isn't hard at all, the metric is obvious. She thinks deciding whether to spend a year caring for a dying parent or take a once-in-a-lifetime job offer is hard, genuinely torn, no right answer. She thinks deciding whether to donate a big sum to famine relief or preserve a dead language with no living speakers is NOT hard — the lives win, even though it's tragic. And she thinks deciding whether to tell a dying friend the harsh truth about their spouce's betrayal or lie to comfort them is hard too, there's just no correct call.

Now here's a new one she hasn't weighed in on: a government can fund either the restoration of an indigenous language on the brink of extinction, which encodes a unique philosophical system about moral reasoning that ethicists think might eventually reshape global ethics, or a disease-prevention program that saves about two hundred lives now but doesn't alter anything structurally. What should she say if she's being consistent? Which earlier case is the closest analog, what are the competing rules that fit her track record, and if they split on this case which prediction do you favor and why? If the precedents genuinely underdetermine it, what principle is she adopting by going each way? And what further case — maybe involving art, science, or personal loyalty — would best tell those rules apart?
