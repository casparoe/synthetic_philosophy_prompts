I am drafting an essay that re-examines the philosophical deployment of the Sphex ichneumoneus case—often invoked to illustrate mechanical rigidity in contrast to human flexibility—by arguing that the standard discussion conflates two separable phenomena. The first is mere behavioral repetition: the wasp’s loop of dragging, checking, and re-checking the burrow. The second, which I term epistemic lock-in, is the absence of any representational access to the contingency of the sequence itself; the organism cannot form even a rudimentary second-order representation that the protocol might be inappropriate to the situation. On my view, sphexishness proper requires both features, not merely the first.

The essay then applies this distinction to human action. Habitual or compulsive human behavior often displays behavioral repetition, yet because the agent typically retains representational access to alternatives—she can recognize that she is repeating an action despite changed circumstances—such cases are only analogically sphexish. Genuine sphexishness in a system, I argue, requires the structural inability to represent the rigidity of one’s own protocol.

To push the analysis further, I introduce the concept of recursive sphexishness: a system is recursively sphexish when its rigidity operates not at the level of first-order action but at the level of flexibility-checking mechanisms themselves. For instance, an administrative protocol that automatically rejects any appeal that fails to match a predetermined form—even when the form is manifestly ill-suited to the case—exhibits not ordinary inertia but a second-order lock-in: it is rigid about its own procedures for accommodating exception.

I would welcome targeted comments on the draft’s conceptual architecture. Specifically: (1) Is the distinction between behavioral repetition and epistemic lock-in philosophically productive, or does it merely rename what ethologists already describe? (2) Is my claim defensible that human habitual action, because it preserves representational access to alternatives, fails to qualify as genuinely sphexish? (3) Does the notion of recursive sphexishness cohere, or does it collapse into a generic description of bureaucratic path dependence? (4) Does the argument over-philosophize the original observational report about Sphex, and if so, where does the overreach occur?

Please provide a point-by-point assessment rather than a general impression, and flag any steps in the reasoning that seem unsupported or that conflate sphexishness with neighboring concepts such as automatism or compulsivity.
