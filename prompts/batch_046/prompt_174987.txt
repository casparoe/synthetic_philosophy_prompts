You are a professional philosopher specialising in epistemology and the analysis of knowledge. Please think carefully, and begin with a brief conceptual map of the main responses to Gettier-style counterexamples before auditing my position.

For a seminar I am preparing, I want a consistency check on the following package of commitments. I hold that: (a) knowledge is not equivalent to justified true belief, and Gettier cases refute this analysis because the justification is anchored to a false intermediate rather than to the fact that makes the belief true; (b) the standard no-false-lemmas repair is unsatisfactory, since it renders knowledge implausibly brittle — a belief can remain well-supported even when one inferential step rests on a falsehood; (c) a safety condition — that one could not easily have believed falsely — captures part of what goes wrong in Gettier cases, though I concede it does not capture everything; (d) I am also drawn to contextualism about knowledge attributions, holding that whether we deny knowledge in a Gettier scenario depends partly on conversational stakes rather than on a uniform metaphysical failure; (e) I treat epistemic luck as a single, unified phenomenon, rejecting distinctions between environmental and intervening varieties; and (f) I think the deepest problem in Gettier cases is epistemic redundancy: the justification is redundant with respect to the actual truth-maker, so that the believer lacks the right kind of explanatory connection to the fact.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear so, and how severely? In particular, examine whether (c) and (e) conflict: if luck is unified, can safety address only part of the problem without implicitly dividing luck into kinds? Does (d) undermine (a) and (b) by explaining away our intuitions contextually, making the metaphysical rejection of JTB unnecessary? Is there a hidden friction between (f) and (a), given that redundancy seems to be a structural feature of reasoning rather than a failure of justification tracking? Rank the tensions by severity, specify the least costly revision for each, and identify the single change that would restore the greatest coherence.
