I've been arguing with my friend about Everything Everywhere All At Once and whether it's actually doing philosophy or just vibes. I want to try reading it through Nāgārjuna's tetralemma — you know, the four corners: it is, it isn't, both, neither — specifcally around the idea of emptiness (śūnyatā) versus nihilism. The movie's "everything bagel" feels like it's sitting in that fourth corner, total annihilation where nothing has inherent existence, but Madhyamaka says emptiness isn't annihilation, it's just that things lack svabhāva and exist dependantly. So does Evelyn's arc — especially the laundromat scene and her final choice not to jump into the bagel — map onto moving from the fourth extreme back to conventional reality? Or is the film actually conflating emptiness with the "nothing matters" nihilism that Jobu represents?

I'm also curious about the multiverse jumps: each universe is conventionally real (samvṛti) but none has ultimate inherent existence (paramārtha). Does that make the film acidentally Madhyamaka, or does it miss the point because the universes are still treated like discrete worlds with fixed identities? Where does the honest critic have to say the reading breaks down — maybe in the way the film still needs a "real" original timeline for emotional stakes? I'd love specifc scenes to point to if I write this up for my film club, and whether a Madhyamaka philosopher would say the movie gets something right about suffering and attachment that analytic philosophy papers usually miss.
