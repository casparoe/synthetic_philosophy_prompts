I've recently become personally preoccupied with the scope of suffering after caring for a family member through severe neurodegeneration, and this has led me to take panpsychist arguments in fundamental physics more seriously than I once did. I'm drafting a proposal for a six-month independent research project—roughly equivalent to a long-form essay or white paper—on a specific ethical question at the intersection of panpsychism and physics. The precise question is this: if one adopts a Russellian-style panpsychism about quantum fields, treating fundamental physical properties as having proto-experiential or proto-sentient aspects, does the ethical principle of non-maleficence generate obligations regarding large-scale technological or cosmological interventions that alter vacuum states or quantum coherence structures? Existing discussions of panpsychist ethics tend to focus either on macroscopic organisms or on the implausibility of attributing rich consciousness to rocks and tables; they leave largely untouched whether there is graded ethical status attached to the maintenance of fundamental field configurations themselves. I want to construct a conceptual framework that distinguishes between (a) suffering as phenomenally rich experience, (b) proto-suffering as minimal negative valence at the fundamental level, and (c) the absence of either, and then ask whether interventions risking destabilization of (b) carry moral weight distinct from standard environmental or physical risk. The method would be philosophical rather than empirical: first, clarifying the physical ontology (what quantum field configurations are on the panpsychist interpretation), second, mapping the ethical distinctions onto that ontology, and third, testing the framework against two concrete cases—high-energy particle collisions that disrupt local coherence, and speculative cosmological engineering that alters vacuum expectation values—without requiring predictions beyond conceptual coherence. What I need is the proposal structure itself: the research question refined so it has a determinate yes/no or graded answer, why current literature in both philosophy of physics and ethics fails to settle it, the step-by-step approach across six months with milestones, what a successful deliverable looks like (for example, a decision framework usable by physicists or policymakers weighing intervention risks), an honest assessment of what would make the project fail, and a realistic judgment of whether this scope is achievable in the timeframe.
