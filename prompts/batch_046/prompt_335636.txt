I have been turning over in my mind, without much formal training, a puzzle about blame and hypocrisy that ordinary conversation never quite resolves. People constantly invoke phrases like “you have no right to blame me, you are just as bad,” yet I cannot tell whether that is a genuine moral argument or only a rhetorical deflection. I want you to draft the joint statement that two people could both sign after a long, good-faith adversarial collaboration: one who holds that hypocrisy always cancels a person’s standing to blame anyone for the same fault, and one who holds that a blamer’s own dirty hands never change whether the blamed act was actually wrong. First, provide a numbered section of agreed claims—about what blame is actually for, about whether hypocrisy is a single kind of failure or several different ones, about whether the blamer’s character changes the moral status of the blamed act, and about whether the same rule applies equally to strangers, to close friends, and to public figures. Second, present a disagreements table with two columns, one for the “standing-is-forfeited” camp and one for the “standing-is-independent” camp; each column must be written in wording that the holder of that view would genuinely accept, with no straw-manning. Third, lay out the cruxes—the precise sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side. I take it that two likely cruxes are whether moral authority works like a license that can be revoked by misconduct, or like a mirror that may be dirty yet still reflects correctly; and whether our intuitive judgments about hypocrisy are actually tracking logical consistency, or are instead a disguised mechanism for protecting people we like and attacking people we dislike. For each crux, supply one concrete situation that both sides agree is a fair test—perhaps a recovering addict urging a friend not to use, or a government that violates an international treaty while condemning another government for the identical violation—and record, in advance and in separate columns, exactly what each side predicts that situation demonstrates. The statement should be precise, formal, and built entirely from reasoning; do not include bibliographies, lists of philosophers, or citations. I want the disagreement mapped exactly, with its verbal parts separated from its genuine parts, so that I can see where the argument actually lives.
