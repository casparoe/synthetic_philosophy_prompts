I am a doctoral student in philosophy of science, examining Karl Popper's demarcation criteria as applied specifically to evolutionary and evolutionary-adjacent research programs. I want to test whether my reconstruction of his rulings is internally consistent and to extend it to a case he never discussed.

Here is the track record I take Popper to have established. First, on Darwinian evolutionary theory by natural selection, he initially judged the core framework to be a metaphysical research program rather than an empirically testable scientific theory, on the grounds that the principle of survival of the fittest risked tautology and that the theory could accommodate virtually any observed adaptation through post hoc narrative reconstruction, thereby lacking genuinely risky predictions. He later revised this judgment, granting Darwinism full scientific status because he came to see that it does generate precise, risky predictions—about phylogenetic tree structure, about specific adaptive mechanisms, and about morphological combinations that should not occur—which could have been empirically falsified. Second, on Marxist historical materialism, he judged it pseudoscientific: its predictions about class polarization and revolutionary inevitability were contradicted by events, yet defenders protected the core by introducing auxiliary hypotheses such as false consciousness or imperialist delay, rendering the theory compatible with any outcome. Third, on Freudian psychoanalysis, he made a parallel pseudoscience verdict, noting that the theory seemed capable of explaining any behavior—whether a man rescues a child or drowns one—as confirming the same underlying drives, leaving no observable counterexample. Fourth, on Einstein's general theory of relativity, he treated it as paradigmatic science because it risked a precise quantitative prediction—the gravitational deflection of starlight—such that a negative result would have seriously undermined the framework.

These four verdicts are jointly consistent with more than one reconstruction of Popper's rule. One reconstruction holds that a theory is scientific if and only if it makes precise, risky empirical predictions capable of falsifying its core; on this reading, Popper's shift on Darwinism reflects a corrected assessment of its predictive risk, Marxism and psychoanalysis fail because they avoid risk through immunization, and Einstein succeeds by embracing it. A second reconstruction emphasizes not risk per se but the absence of systematic immunization strategies: a program is scientific when its defenders do not routinely shield core claims with ad hoc adjustments. Here Darwinism initially resembled Marxism and psychoanalysis—capable of absorbing anomalies—until Popper recognized that its adjustments are constrained and genuinely vulnerable. A third reconstruction distinguishes a metaphysical core from a progressive scientific research program built around it: Darwinism's core principle might remain metaphysical, but the surrounding empirical program is scientific and progressive, suggesting that Popper's later recantation concerned the status of the broader program rather than the falsifiability of the core itself.

Now consider a new case not in Popper's texts. Call it the Epigenetic Landscape Constraint Model (ELCM), a research program in evolutionary developmental biology. ELCM proposes that heritable chromatin and cytoskeletal states create deep topographical constraints—valleys in phenotypic space—that make certain evolutionary transitions structurally inaccessible regardless of selection pressure. It predicts that specific trait combinations will never evolve unless an intermediate, transient relaxation of the chromatin scaffold occurs. For instance, it predicts that no vertebrate lineage will evolve high-metabolic-rate endothermic physiology combined with an ectothermic reproductive strategy, because the chromatin landscape linking metabolic regulation and reproductive mode is locked in a deep valley. When researchers recently documented an apparent counterexample—a small mammalian lineage with precisely this combination—ELCM's defenders responded by positing an unobserved, transient chromatin relaxation event that left no detectable molecular trace and that occurred only in the ancestral population.

Applying each reconstruction, what should a consistent Popperian say about ELCM? Is it scientific, pseudoscientific, or metaphysical?
