Two graduate students in philosophy, Alex and Blake, argued over coffee about the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem. (1) Alex said the classic case — a husband saving his wife out of moral duty rather than love — shows that certain reasons are structurally inappropriate for intimate practices, and he extended this: a musician playing solely for fame has a corrupted relationship to music, because the internal goods of the practice are displaced by external rewards. (2) Blake replied that this overgeneralizes: external reasons only become wrong-kind when they actually displace internal ones, not when they coexist. A musician who loves the music and also wants fame is not corrupted; reasons are not zero-sum, so the problem requires evidence of displacement, not mere presence. (3) Alex then proposed what he called a "reverse" wrong-kind-reasons case: a parent who acts solely from spontaneous affection, with no sense of moral obligation or long-term duty, might neglect necessary discipline. Here, the absence of external, duty-based reasons seems to corrupt parenting just as surely as their presence corrupts love. So wrong-kind-reasons should be symmetric — it is about mismatch between reason-type and practice, not just about external reasons intruding. (4) Blake countered that this collapses the concept: what Alex describes is practical weakness or insufficient reason, not the specific structural failure Williams identified. By making the problem symmetric, Alex turns it into generic bad reasoning and loses the distinctive claim that some reasons are inappropriate because they reveal a failure of the very attachment that makes the practice intelligible.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Also say whether Alex's "reverse" case genuinely extends the wrong-kind-reasons problem or changes its subject, and identify what both disputants are missing about how institutional or collective contexts might complicate their individual-level analysis.
