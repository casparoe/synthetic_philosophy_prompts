You serve as the senior review officer for an international arbitration panel that evaluates individual combatant status in hybrid conflicts. The panel operates under the principle of moral equality of combatants: in any armed conflict, lawful combatants on opposing sides possess identical moral permission to employ lethal force and identical immunity from prosecution for acts that comply with the laws of war, irrespective of whether their cause is just. You must apply this principle repeatedly—often under time pressure, with incomplete files, and without consulting moral philosophers—to determine whether a particular individual retains combatant-equality protection or should instead be classified as an unlawful participant subject to ordinary criminal jurisdiction.

Design a reusable decision procedure your office can complete in approximately thirty minutes per case. Present it first as an ordered checklist: the specific questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, and the precise conditions under which the procedure halts with a determination of protected status, halts with exclusion, or stops and hands the file to a human decision-maker because the remaining issue is one of values rather than verifiable fact. Then render the same logic as a flowchart in words, marking every branch point and every dead end. The procedure must preserve the core commitment of moral equality—that the justice of a cause does not alter a combatant’s permission or immunity—while accommodating three modern complications: fighters who belong simultaneously to state and non-state command structures; remote operators who direct lethal force without physical presence in the theater of conflict; and actors who observe some but not all reciprocity conditions.

Apply the procedure to three worked cases.

First, a uniformed state infantry officer who also exercises de facto command over an affiliated local militia that wears no fixed insignia but restricts its attacks to identifiable military targets.

Second, a civilian-contracted remote pilot who operates armed drones for a state air force from a facility outside the conflict zone, holds no military rank, and never enters the theater physically.

Third—this should yield an answer you will find unwelcome—compare two figures: a foreign volunteer fighting for an unjust cause who wears a recognizable uniform, never targets civilians or protected sites, and fully accepts the reciprocity conditions of the laws of war; and a uniformed state soldier fighting for a just cause who deliberately uses a protected civilian site as cover for a military strike. Show exactly how the procedure classifies each figure, identify the branch where your own moral intuition pulls sharply against the procedural result, and state whether the procedure allows you to override that intuition or requires you to record the outcome and dissent separately.

Conclude by specifying what the procedure cannot do: distinguish practical failures arising from missing information from structural limitations built into the equality principle itself, and flag any case type for which the checklist offers no determinate path at all. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the deliverable must be usable by an officer without access to philosophical literature.
