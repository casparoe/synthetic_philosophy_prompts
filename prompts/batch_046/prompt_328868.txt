(2) A viral video claims that all taxation is theft; I adopt that conviction without examining arguments. That feels like gullibility, not knowledge.

(3) My ethics teacher says consequentialism is the correct framework, and I accept it on her authority alone. I'm torn: broad theories might be okay to inherit, but I'm unsure.

(4) In my community, elders teach that hospitality to strangers is a sacred duty; I hold that belief mainly because they said so. That feels legitimate, even though I can't fully defend it philosophically.

The puzzle is that (1) and (2) both involve accepting a specific moral claim from someone else, yet one feels right and the other wrong. Meanwhile, (3) splits me—authority seems more acceptable for frameworks than for slogans, but I can't say exactly why.

Is there one principle—perhaps about whether I could eventually access the reasoning myself, or about the source's standing in my epistemic community—that captures all four? If not, which verdict should I abandon? Also apply your principle to a case I haven't listed: an unbiased AI tells me a hiring algorithm is unfair, and I accept that without checking its data.

Please lead with the principle in one sentence, then defend it in no more than 200 words. Be explicit about which of my four verdicts your account cannot keep.
