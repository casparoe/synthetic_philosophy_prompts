For a seminar I'm writing up my stance on public reason and religious arguments so folks can tear it apart. Here it is: I'm mostly Rawlsian—public reason should only govern constitutional essentials and basic justice, not every ordinary law; I dont think religious citizens should be forced to bracket their faith or translate convictions into secular language before voting, that feels like an asymmetrical burden; I still buy the proviso that religious reasoning in public debate is okay if proper public reasons are offered in due course; I think secular comprehensive doctrines like hard utilitarianism or deep-green ethics are just as sectarian from other citizens' viewpoints, so singling out religion for special treatment is arbitrary; but I also think appeals to revelation are structurally different since they invoke an authority that isnt revisable by shared evidence, which makes them especially hard to accommodate democratically; and I want courts to strike down laws justified solely by scripture even outside constitutional essentials, because that violates civic equality. Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in real tension, which ones just look tense but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real ones by severity with the cheapest fix for each. Then say which single revision would clean up the most.
