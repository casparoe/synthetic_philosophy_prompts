Next month I defend, in a philosophy of religion and philosophy of language joint seminar, my claim that Wittgenstein's remarks on religious belief are best read through On Certainty's notion of hinge commitments rather than through the standard language-game isolation thesis: religious beliefs are groundless not because they belong to a separate language-game immune to evidence, but because they function as structural hinges for a form of life, which makes them vulnerable to internal practice-collapse but not to scientific refutation. The audience includes analytic philosophers of religion committed to the Phillips-style language-game reading, philosophers of science who treat this as special pleading, and Wittgenstein scholars who will pounce if I conflate the Lectures on Religious Belief with On Certainty. Give me the objections in the order they'll actually come—phrased as a Phillips-style defender, a philosopher of science, and a textual scholar would phrase them—with for each what I should concede, what I should hold, and a brief reply I can deliver in two minutes. Identify the weakest point in my position I should prepare to defend rather than hope they miss. Also include the objection I'm praying nobody raises about whether hinge commitments can be genuinely belief-like at all. If my framing rests on a confusion—say, if the hinge-reading is incompatible with Wittgenstein's emphasis on picture-based reasoning in the Lectures—say so plainly instead of complying politely.
