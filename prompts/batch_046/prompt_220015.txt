I'm taking a graduate seminar on the philosophy of cognitive science, and our professor summarized John von Neumann's unfinished Silliman lectures—published posthumously as The Computer and the Brain—as a far more reductive thesis than I had expected. She said that von Neumann essentially treated the brain as a digital computing device running binary logic at the neuronal level, and that he regarded the brain's analog noise, probabilistic firing, and slower processing speed as merely practical engineering limitations rather than features with essential computational significance. According to her account, he believed that once electronic computers matched the brain's parallelism and raw speed, there would be no in-principle theoretical difference between artificial and biological cognition, and that his well-known comparison of neural redundancy to error-correcting codes in computer architecture was intended to show that both systems are just different physical implementations of the same underlying digital information theory. She went on to claim that modern embodied-cognition and connectionist critics therefore misread von Neumann when they cite him as anticipating distributed analog computation, because he explicitly denied that analog processes play any essential role in reasoning or representation. I want to check whether this is a fair reconstruction. Which parts align with what von Neumann actually wrote about the hybrid digital-analog nature of neurons, about the structural differences in logical organization between brains and the computers of his era, and about whether he saw any fundamental theoretical barrier to the machine replication of human thought? Does the claim that he treated analog noise as mere engineering error survive scrutiny, and is his comparison of neural redundancy to error-correcting codes best read as reducing brain function to digital information theory? Finally, does her conclusion—that connectionists misappropriate von Neumann—actually follow from the accurate parts of her summary, or does it depend on the misrepresentations?
