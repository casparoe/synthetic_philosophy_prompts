I was talking with my friend last night about this weird medical condition called pain asymbolia, where people can feel pain—like burning or sharpness—but they dont actually mind it, they dont avoid it or act like its bad. And I started wondering if that means pain isnt actually bad in itself, just our reaction is. So I built this argument but now it feels like it proves to much and I cant tell where the mistake is.

(1) Pain is bad because of its intrinsic feel—how it hurts.
(2) In pain asymbolia, people have the exact same sensory feelings but they say it doesnt hurt or bother them, and they dont try to stop it.
(3) So the badness cant be in the sensation itself; it has to be in the separate emotional aversion that usually comes with it.
(4) That means pain is really just a neutral physical feeling plus a negative reaction tacked on.
(5) So if we could block that reaction—through drugs or brain stuff—wed have intense sensations with no harm done.
(6) Which means causing those sensations to someone who lacks the reaction wouldnt actually be wrong or harmful.
(7) But then all our moral ideas about suffering, like why torture is bad or why we care about animal pain, fall apart, because they depend on the reaction being unavoidable.
(8) I guess the conclusion is that suffering is optional and we could just turn it off, but that seems totally wrong. I want to keep (2) since its a real medical thing, and (3) sounds logical to me. Where does this go wrong? Is there an equivocation or am I just wrong about one of these steps?
