(2) This comparison is entirely value-neutral: it measures physical or informational divergence in state configurations and requires no specification of which outcomes are good, bad, desirable, or harmful.

(3) RL side-effect penalties—such as relative reachability distances computed against a baseline state distribution—implement exactly this structural idea, so they constrain behavior without smuggling in human values.

(4) At the same time, selecting which state features to include, how to weight them, and which distance kernel to apply necessarily involves normative judgments about what counts as a meaningful disruption versus an irrelevant fluctuation.

(5) I want to reject any framework that embeds a full human-value specification into the impact measure, both to avoid misalignment and because I think any such specification is necessarily incomplete.

(6) The agent must still be permitted to cause large, transformative state changes when they are direct outputs of its assigned task, which means the impact measure must distinguish task-allowed deviations from unintended side effects.

Rank the genuine tensions by severity, specify for each what has to give and what the cheapest revision is, and identify which apparent conflicts dissolve once the distinctions are made precise—especially whether (2) and (4) are actually contradictory or whether they can be reconciled by separating explicit utility functions from implicit framing choices. Also determine whether the principle in (2) can deliver the task/side-effect distinction I need in (6) without tacitly relying on the kind of value specification I reject in (5).
