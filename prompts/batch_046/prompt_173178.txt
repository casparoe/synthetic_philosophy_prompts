Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for a graduate-level philosophy of value seminar that classifies positions on the wrong-kind-reasons problem without relying on the standard textbook labels (buck-passing, fitting-attitudes, realist, anti-realist). The tree should be built from the logical space of answers to concrete diagnostic questions, with positions mapped onto leaves only after the branching logic is fixed.

Design the tree around three branching nodes, each posed as a concrete case or claim that a student can answer without knowing philosophical taxonomy:

Node 1: In a case where an agent takes a pill that chemically induces cheerful belief about an injustice she has not examined, is the reason's wrongness best explained by (a) a category mistake between practical and constitutive norms, (b) a structural failure of transparency in the reason's relation to the mental state, or (c) an external functional constraint on what can properly cause that state?

Node 2: Consider a belief formed under credible threat that happens to be true but is held solely because of the threat, not evidence. Does the wrong-kind-reason objection apply here, and if so, does it vitiate (i) epistemic justification only, (ii) rational coherence only, or (iii) both?

Node 3: An expert admires a malicious strategic maneuver purely for its technical elegance, fully aware of its moral cost. Is the wrongness of this reason located in (A) its moral content, (B) the agent's second-order inaccessibility to the reason's full normative character, or (C) a frame-mismatch between the evaluative domain of admiration and that of moral appraisal?

Add a fourth cross-cutting branch on 'epistemic-rationality crossover': is the diagnosis of wrong-kind reasons for belief structurally isomorphic to that for desire, or does belief admit a separate mechanism? If this branch collapses into one of the others, flag the collapse explicitly.

Requirements for the deliverable:
- 6 to 8 leaves. Each leaf must carry either a recognized position name (e.g., 'functionalist separation view', 'transparency-based anti-buck-passing') or the label 'unnamed', followed by one sentence stating its most awkward commitment.
- For every combination of answers that yields no actual defender, briefly diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or being merely unexplored.
- Flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining why the branching logic fails to distinguish them.
- Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific page numbers. The taxonomy must be answerable by reasoning about the cases alone.
- Keep the audience in mind: graduate students who understand the problem but should not be able to answer by reciting a summary from an encyclopedia entry.
