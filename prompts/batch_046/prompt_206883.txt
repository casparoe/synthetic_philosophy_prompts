I am a retired physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of mind, and I have tried to work out a consistent position on the nature of emotion. Before presenting it in a reading group, I would like a consistency audit of the following commitments.

(1) Emotions are best explained by an interoceptive predictive framework: the brain generates top-down predictions of bodily state, and affective valence is the registration of allostatic error—essentially a discrepancy signal—rather than a judgment that an object possesses value.

(2) Despite using predictive language, I deny that these dynamics involve mental representations with propositional content; I regard them as sub-personal, embodied regulatory patterns, structurally analogous to homeostatic feedback loops rather than to beliefs or perceptions with full intentionality.

(3) Emotions are rationally assessable in a robust sense: it is genuinely irrational, not merely maladaptive, to feel intense fear toward a clearly harmless spider, because the predictive model is miscalibrated relative to available evidence.

(4) I apply a knowledge norm to emotional testimony: I should assert "I am afraid of X" only if I know that the state is occurring and that it is appropriately directed at X; if evolutionary misfire or miscalibration is likely, I ought to withhold or correct the report.

(5) Emotions evolved as rapid, non-conceptual fitness trackers in ancestral environments, which entails that in contemporary contexts they systematically misfire, generating bodily responses that are fitness-neutral or fitness-negative.

(6) The phenomenological character of emotion is irreducibly first-personal, but I maintain that a complete causal and predictive account of its neural dynamics leaves no unexplained residue—there is no "hard problem" specific to affective consciousness.

Do these positions form a coherent package? I want an audit rather than a lecture: rank any genuine tensions by severity, indicate for each what commitment would have to be revised and at what cost; also identify any pairs that only appear to conflict but can be reconciled without revision. Please do not provide a general taxonomy of emotion theories, but assess specifically whether this particular combination is jointly tenable.
