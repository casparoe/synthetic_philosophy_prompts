I'm in a poli sci seminar on conservative theory and we just covered Chestertons fence. There's this move I keep noticing in reform debates that feels related but weirder. Like, someone defends an old zoning board by saying don't tear it down until you know why it's there. But then if you propose a new environmental review layer, they say don't build fences you don't understand — except now the fence is the absence of the rule, so tearing it down means passing the law. Same with church-state separation: defend the current line as an evolved fence, but also defend not drawing a stricter line because that's tearing down tradition. It's not quite hypocrisy and not plain equivocation either; it's like the logic gets applied symetrically to both presence and absence just to defend whatever state exists. Is there an actual term for this double-use? Or if not, how would you distinguish it from the regular fence argument and from just being inconsistent?
