Albert Einstein died in 1955 with a well-known philosophical profile: a deterministic, Spinozistic view of nature in which human free will is an illusion arising from ignorance of causes; a "cosmic religious feeling" directed at the rational harmony of the universe rather than a personal deity; and a lifelong insistence that quantum mechanics must be incomplete because it abandons the determinism and separability he took to be prerequisites of physical reality. Two major intellectual developments occurred after his death that bear directly on these commitments. First, the neuroscientific study of voluntary action—exemplified by experiments in which measurable neural activity precedes the subjective awareness of having made a decision—seemed to many to provide empirical support for determinism about human choice. Second, in quantum foundations, the idea of 'superdeterminism' was revived: the denial of the statistical independence (often called 'measurement freedom') between hidden variables and the settings of measuring devices, proposed as a way to preserve full determinism even in the face of Bell-type correlations. Imagine Einstein had lived to engage with both developments. Predict his likely assessment of each. Specifically: (a) Would he have seen the neuroscience as a vindication of his determinism, or as conceptually confused because it retains a dualistic framing of mind and brain that his monistic naturalism rejects? (b) Would he have welcomed superdeterminism as the fulfillment of his EPR-style realism, or rejected it because it undermines the very freedom of the experimenter to choose settings independently—a freedom he regarded not as metaphysical liberty but as a necessary methodological condition for objective science? (c) How, if at all, would he have unified these judgments? Would he have argued that brain determinism and quantum determinism must both be subsumed under a single rational law of nature, or that they belong to incommensurable domains? Provide a coherent reconstruction of his probable position, and identify the point at which his reasoning is most vulnerable to a modern objection.
