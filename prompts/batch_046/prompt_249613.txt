I serve on the internal governance board of a mid-sized technology firm, and we are required to classify every system we deploy as either Tool AI or Agent AI for regulatory filings; yet the standard distinction—whether a system pursues goals autonomously—has collapsed in practice, since every language model pursues coherence and every calculator pursues accuracy. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in philosophy of action rather than marketing categories, that my non-philosopher colleagues can run in about thirty minutes. Specifically, operationalize the distinction between instruments that operate within a closed normative loop—where the system applies pre-authorized evaluative criteria that cannot be revised by the system’s own outputs without explicit human authorization—and those that operate within an open normative loop, where the system’s outputs can revise the criteria by which subsequent outputs are evaluated, without passing through an external authorization gate. The deliverable should be a step-by-step flowchart in words: the ordered questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the points where the procedure stops with a definitive classification, the points where it must halt and hand the decision to a human because the question is one of values rather than architecture, and an honest flagging of where the procedure is likely to misfire or disguise a judgment call as a technical check. Apply it to three worked cases: a spreadsheet macro that updates pricing formulas using live market data; a customer-service chatbot that revises its own escalation thresholds in response to aggregated satisfaction scores; and an autonomous research assistant that updates its internal rubric for evaluating scientific hypotheses after each round of literature synthesis. At least one case should yield a classification that conflicts with our firm’s commercial interest in labeling the system a mere tool. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading; the procedure must stand on its own conceptual architecture.
