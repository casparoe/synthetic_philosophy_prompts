I'm working through an argument from a metaphysics seminar that seems to collapse the distinction between fundamental and derivative levels, and I can't locate the precise slip. Here are the steps:

(1) Grounding is irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive; if A grounds B, then B is ontologically dependent on A and strictly less fundamental.
(2) Any derivative composite object O is fully grounded in the existence, intrinsic properties, and spatial relations of its fundamental parts P1...Pn.
(3) The specific structural configuration S in which those parts are arranged—their geometric and relational ordering—is not reducible to the parts taken individually or to their mereological sum; S is an additional fact that must figure in any complete ground of O.
(4) Therefore O is partly grounded in S.
(5) Now ask what grounds S. Since S is not identical to any single part, and since the parts' intrinsic properties alone do not entail any particular arrangement, no collection composed solely of parts and intrinsic properties can fully ground S.
(6) If S were derivative and grounded in O, that would be circular, since O is partly grounded in S. If it were grounded in further configurations S', S'', etc., we face an infinite regress of structural grounds.
(7) So S must be fundamental.
(8) But then every possible structural configuration among fundamental entities is itself fundamental. Given the combinatorial vastness of such configurations, the fundamental level is explosively populated rather than sparse—contradicting the standard parsimony motivation for fundamentality.
(9) Hence either transitivity fails for structural grounding, or structural configurations are not genuine grounds, or the idea of a sparse fundamental base must be abandoned.

The speaker concludes that (1) is false because transitivity doesn't hold across "structural" and "compositional" grounding relations. I think the error lies elsewhere—perhaps in the move from (3) to (5), where "not reducible to parts alone" is treated as "not groundable except by being fundamental"—but I want to be precise about what that costs. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd especially like to preserve (2) and the intuition that there is a sparse fundamental base, but I'm willing to surrender either if the price is clearly marked.
