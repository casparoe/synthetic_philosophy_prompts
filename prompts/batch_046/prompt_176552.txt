Moritz Schlick, working within the Vienna Circle, addresses the meaning of life by applying the verificationist criterion of cognitive significance. Because assertions about an ultimate cosmic purpose cannot be empirically verified, he regards the traditional question as a pseudo-problem that should be dissolved rather than answered. Once dissolved, Schlick replaces purpose with play: work is always instrumental, done for the sake of something else, whereas play is pursued for its own sake and therefore possesses intrinsic meaning. He points to youth and artistic creation as pure instances of play, arguing that these alone confer meaning upon life.
