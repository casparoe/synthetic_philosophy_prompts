In a discussion about AI governance and democratic autonomy, a colleague summarized Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor with the slogan that freedom is too heavy a burden and people will always surrender it for bread and security. He used this to argue that modern citizens will voluntarily trade democratic deliberation for algorithmic administration if the latter guarantees material comfort. I need that one-liner taken apart into its precise theses. Separate the psychological claim about revealed preferences, the normative claim about what human flourishing requires, the historical claim about welfare-state development, and any metaphysical claim about innate human weakness. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, an empirically defensible thesis, or false, and identify which—if any—actually licenses the inference from the parable to algorithmic governance. Do not lay out the menu neutrally: take a committed position on which interpretations are serious and which are sloppy analogies, defend your verdict with reasons drawn from the internal logic of the Inquisitor's argument rather than from external political theory, and keep the analysis grounded in the parable's actual structure rather than in generic commentary on authoritarianism.
