I need a poem to close our seminar on the regress problem — foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism — and I want the form to actually do some philosophical work, not just decorate the idea. I'm thinking a villanelle (19 lines, two refrains, five tercets, one quatrain at the end). One refrain should track the infinite demand for justification — beliefs asking for grounds that ask for grounds — and the other should track the circular loop where belief A supports B and B supports A. The rhyme scheme should feel like it's either sinking into a foundation that cracks, or spining in a circle, or stretching out past sight. I'd like the turn in the final quatrain to be the infinitist moment: the regress isn't vicious, it's just the honest shape of knowing, an endless chain that doesn't need an end. So the first tercets feel like laying bricks (foundationalism), then the circle closes (coherentism), then the ladder extends. Don't just name-drop philosophers — make the structure recognisable to someone who knows the debate, and still readable to someone who doesn't. No sermon, leave the tension open, thats where our discussion ended up. Can you write it?
