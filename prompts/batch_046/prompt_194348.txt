Please find below a summary that a colleague recently reconstructed for me from an essay on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. Their account is as follows. Illusionists hold that phenomenal consciousness — that is, what it is like to undergo an experience — does not exist in any form; it is a complete fiction comparable to the belief in phlogiston. According to them, introspection does not reveal raw qualitative feels but rather interprets neural signals through a conceptual framework that erroneously posits intrinsic phenomenal properties. More specifically, they claim that the illusion is generated within the prefrontal global workspace, where information is broadcast for verbal report, while sensory cortices contain no phenomenal properties whatsoever. From these premises, they draw the further conclusion that illusionism is fully compatible with physicalism but necessarily incompatible with panpsychism, on the grounds that the absence of phenomenal properties in the brain entails their absence everywhere. Finally, they reject the phenomenal concepts strategy employed by certain physicalists, maintaining that our concepts of experience are not direct recognitional capacities but theoretical constructions.
