Here are my judgments about freedom across several situations, and I want to know whether they point to a single principle or force a choice among competing conceptions.

A prisoner is unfree: that seems obvious, whatever liberty means.

A citizen lives under an absolute ruler who happens to be benevolent and never issues constraining orders, but retains the legal power to do so at whim. I feel this citizen is unfree in a serious sense, even though no actual interference occurs.

A democratic state imposes heavy progressive taxation to fund healthcare and education; citizens vote, and the law applies uniformly. I do not feel this is a freedom violation comparable to imprisonment or dictatorship, though it is coercive.

A population is subject to pervasive surveillance that never results in punishment, yet people widely self-censor because they know they are watched. I think freedom is genuinely diminished here, despite the absence of direct prohibition.

In a market economy, structural incentives make it practically impossible for most people to become artisans, though no law bars entry. I think negative liberty remains intact, but I am unsure whether another kind of freedom is affected.

Finally, imagine a society in which all resources are distributed by a transparent, appealable bureaucratic algorithm; no private ownership exists, and citizens must petition for goods, yet petitions are granted according to public rules. I feel that dependency here threatens freedom, even though arbitrary will is absent.

So my pattern seems to be: direct coercion is bad; arbitrary power is bad even when dormant; self-censorship induced by monitoring is bad; democratic coercion is not bad in the same way; structural economic barriers are not clearly freedom violations; but rule-bound dependency still feels like a loss of freedom.

Can these verdicts be unified under one account—perhaps a refined republican conception, or a hybrid of negative and republican liberty—or do they force us to abandon one verdict? State the principle precisely, indicate which case motivates each clause, predict what it says about an unconsidered case in which an AI system anticipates and fulfills citizens' preferences before they form them (thereby eliminating choice without coercion or surveillance), and be explicit about which of my judgments the principle must reject if it is to remain coherent.
