My philosophy tutor has been working through Quine's critique of the analytic-synthetic distinction, ruling case by case on particular statements. So far she has said that "all bachelors are unmarried" is analytic, but only relative to English convention; that "water is H2O" is synthetic, since identifying hydrogen and oxygen required empirical chemistry; that "gold has atomic number 79" is synthetic despite feeling necessary, because the nuclear identification was experimentally discovered; that arithmetic truths like "seven plus five equals twelve" are analytic inside formal logic yet sit so close to the center of the web of belief that Quine might reject labeling them analytic at all; and that "all whales are mammals" is synthetic, because biological taxonomy is always open to empirical revision. She must now rule on the claim that logical validity just is truth-preservation in every model of the premises, including non-standard arithmetic models that contain elements with no corresponding standard numeral. Will she call this claim analytic, synthetic, or reject the distinction altogether for it? Which rule is she implicitly running—conventional definition, Quinean centrality, or a Kripke-style separation of metaphysical necessity from epistemic analyticity? And which further case—say, a revised geometric axiom or a chemically redefined element—would best reveal which rule she actually holds?
