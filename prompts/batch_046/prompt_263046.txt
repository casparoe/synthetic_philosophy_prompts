I am a student in an applied ethics seminar, and I am interning with a university office that designs mandatory contribution requirements for scholarship recipients. The office operates on an explicit consequentialist mandate—to maximize welfare through institutional design—but my supervisor has asked me to account for the demandingness objection: that morality, and therefore policy, may not require individuals to bear costs disproportionate to their fair share of the total good. I need a reusable decision procedure that my supervisors could actually run, without philosophical training, on any proposed requirement.

Please construct an ordered procedural protocol. For each step, specify: the precise question the administrator asks; what counts as a sufficient answer to proceed; where the procedure issues a clear prohibition or a conditional approval; and where it must halt and refer the case to a values committee because the dispute is no longer about facts but about normative weights. The procedure must preserve the consequentialist commitment that welfare gains matter, while incorporating the demandingness constraint through a specific conceptual distinction you should build in: separate burdens that merely reduce welfare within an intact life-plan from burdens that fracture the life-plan itself—call this the integrity threshold. Require that any burden crossing that threshold be justified not merely by large welfare gains, but by a non-substitutable institutional necessity (that is, no less burdensome mechanism could achieve comparable results).

Then apply the procedure to two proposals. First, requiring recipients to donate five percent of their stipend, with full transparency, to an evidence-based global-health intervention; second, requiring them to provide ten hours per week of direct administrative labor for that same intervention, with scheduling flexibility but no opt-out except program withdrawal. Design the analysis so that the first comes out as permissible only under strict procedural conditions that may feel bureaucratically burdensome, and the second comes out as impermissible despite its direct welfare impact—specifically because it colonizes time essential to study and employment, thereby crossing the integrity threshold. In analyzing the second case, identify the exact step at which the procedure halts, and flag whether that halt point is genuinely factual or a judgment call about values disguised as procedure.

Finally, state two structural weaknesses of the protocol: one class of burdens it will systematically misclassify because the integrity-threshold test depends on a subjective report of life-plan centrality, and one point where a supervisor who defines "life-plan" more narrowly could reverse the outcome for the second proposal. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the deliverable should stand on its conceptual architecture alone.
