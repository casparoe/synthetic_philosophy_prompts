In animal-ethics discussions I keep seeing the 'lottery factory' analogy used for the insect-scale problem. The picture is that every insect is like a ticket with a tiny chance of being conscious — maybe one in a thousand — but with trillions of tickets sold the expected number of 'winners' (sentient sufferers) is massive, so we shouldn't dismiss them just because any individual one probably isn't sentient. Activists use it to push for changing agriculture and pest control; sceptics say it proves too much because you'd have to worry about soil bacteria and background server processes too, and they call this the 'background noise' objection. Someone also said the analogy quietly assumes suffering adds up linearly across entities, which is exactly what's contested.
