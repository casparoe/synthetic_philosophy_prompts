I am constructing a taxonomy for a philosophy-of-mind seminar on the paradox of phenomenal judgment under epiphenomenalism. Please design a decision tree — framed as a sequence of concrete yes/no questions rather than as a roster of named theorists — that classifies the logical responses to the following case: subject S reports being in sharp pain; the report and the associated withdrawal behaviour are fully determined by C-fibre activation, with no causal contribution from the qualitative feel of the pain itself.

The tree should begin from the root commitment that phenomenal properties are causally inert with respect to both behaviour and belief-formation. From there, branch through these specific diagnostic distinctions: (1) whether S could still have formed the belief 'I am in pain' if the qualitative feel were replaced by an empty functional duplicate with identical neural dynamics but no subjective character; (2) whether the accuracy of S's report requires the feel to play any causal role, or only requires that the brain state tracks the feel by a necessary correlation; (3) whether one accepts that a belief can be genuinely about phenomenal properties despite being produced entirely by physical mechanisms that are blind to those properties.

The tree must terminate in exactly six leaves. Four should correspond to identifiable positions: (a) the interactionist rejection, which takes the case to refute causal inertness; (b) the direct-acquaintance dissolution, which holds that phenomenal concepts are acquired through non-causal acquaintance and therefore need not be physically mediated; (c) the self-stultifying acceptance, which concedes that the view undermines its own epistemic justification but treats this as a bullet to bite; and (d) the misattribution or eliminativist response, which denies that there is any phenomenal property being judged and treats 'pain' talk as a misdescription of the physical state.

The two remaining leaves should represent logically possible but apparently unoccupied combinations. One should be the view that phenomenal properties are redundant but non-inert because they are overdetermined by the physical pathway — please flag whether this collapses back into interactionism or remains distinct. The other should be the view that judgments are causally disconnected from phenomenal properties yet guaranteed to be true by a brute necessary correlation between brain states and qualia — please flag whether this is coherent, incoherent, or merely unexplored, and identify its most awkward commitment.

Every node should be phrased as a question a student can answer without already knowing which philosophical label it leads to. Where two named leaves answer the same diagnostic question in identical ways, flag that your tree cannot separate them and explain why. For each unoccupied combination, provide a one-sentence diagnosis of whether it is unattractive, incoherent, or simply absent from the literature. Use British spelling and punctuation throughout, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations.
