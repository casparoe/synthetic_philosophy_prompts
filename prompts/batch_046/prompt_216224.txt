I'm a doctoral student working on practical rationality and the philosophy of science, and I've been trying to pin down my position on sunk costs for a workshop on diachronic decision theory. I was discussing with a colleague whether honouring sunk costs can ever be rational, and I've realised my commitments may not cohere as neatly as I assumed. Here is roughly where I've landed, and I'd like a precise consistency audit rather than a general lecture.

First, I take it as axiomatic for instrumental rationality that, in the standard economic framing, honouring sunk costs is always irrational: a rational agent should maximise expected future utility and treat past expenditures as causally irrelevant to present choice. Second, however, I want to distinguish monetary sunk costs from what I call narrative sunk costs — the accumulated interpretive labour through which an agent or a community makes sense of an ongoing project — and I think these can rationally constrain present choice, not because of the past investment per se, but because abandoning the framework dissolves the coherence of the reasoning that led there. Third, I endorse a broadly Bratman-style view of planning agency: one's past self can bind one's present self through commitments that are not reducible to present preferences, which means diachronic rationality permits forms of self-binding that a purely synchronic account rules out. Fourth, I accept evolutionary debunking arguments that show our economic tendency to honour sunk costs is an unreliable, misfiring commitment-mechanism shaped by social coordination needs rather than by tracking future payoffs. Fifth, yet I also think Kuhn was right that scientific rationality involves rational persistence with a paradigm despite anomalies — puzzle-solving commitments are not mere psychological inertia, and abandoning a research programme too quickly can be epistemically irresponsible. Sixth, I reject the idea that rational assessment must be view-from-nowhere neutral: rationality is always situated within an ongoing project, whether personal or scientific.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what the cheapest revision would be in each case. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. In particular, I want to know whether my acceptance of evolutionary debunking in economic contexts undermines my defence of rational persistence in science and in personal planning; whether my claim that honouring sunk costs is always irrational in the instrumental framing conflicts with my allowance for narrative and planning-based constraints; and whether my rejection of the view from nowhere makes the instrumental framing itself parochial rather than genuinely axiomatic. Be specific about what each revision costs, and please answer in British English.
