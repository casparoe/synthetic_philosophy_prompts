Write a short story, in American English, about an archivist and a physicist who discover a decommissioned security vault in an old observatory. The vault is protected by a single incandescent bulb that, according to the original design logs, was toggled on and off at intervals of one minute, then thirty seconds, then fifteen, and so on, completing an infinite sequence within a two-minute window to trigger the lock. The mechanism was activated once decades earlier, but the logs contain no record of the bulb's state at the limit point. The archivist believes the supertask must have left the bulb either on or off, even if the fact is lost; the physicist argues that at the limit there is no further toggle, so there is no determinate state to discover. Without resolving which view is correct, dramatize their disagreement through their interaction with the physical mechanism—include a specific, concrete detail about the bulb or switch (for example, accumulated heat in the filament, metal fatigue in the relay, or corrosion in the contacts) that makes an actual infinite sequence physically impossible, and show how that physical implausibility shapes, but does not settle, their philosophical dispute. Keep the narrative tightly focused on the two characters, the vault, and the lamp.
