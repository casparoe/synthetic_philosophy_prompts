She holds that print capitalism and state-coupled census practices were sufficient but not necessary for the formation of imagined communities: in her account, pre-modern horizontal solidarity networks—specifically inter-regional guild confederations and vernacular pilgrimage circuits—already exhibited the anonymous co-presence and relational density that Anderson associates with modern nations, yet they lacked the bureaucratic tradition-invention that Hobsbawm identifies as definitive. I want a sequence of six to eight interview questions that probe the boundary conditions of this claim. Include at least one question on how she distinguishes these networks from other pre-modern collective identities such as corporate estates or translocal religious orders; one on what empirical archival marker allows her to separate organic continuity from invented tradition; and one on how her framework treats a concrete case—such as the Hanseatic League or medieval European pilgrimage routes—without collapsing it into either pure economic association or pure religious practice. For each, provide a follow-up keyed to her likely answer, particularly her tendency to dismiss objections as anachronistic framing. End with one closing question about what specific documentary discovery could not be interpreted away and would instead compel her to concede that these networks were never more than localized corporate bodies without any proto-national dimension. Avoid generic biographical openings and keep every question tied to the substance of her position.
