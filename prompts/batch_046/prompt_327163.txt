I am preparing a seminar module that tests the limits of negative-utilitarian reasoning in environmental ethics, and I want to examine an argument I have constructed—the "trophic-replacement reductio"—before presenting it to students. The chain runs as follows. (1) If the total welfare of wild animals within an ecosystem is net-negative, and if destroying that ecosystem prevents that suffering without generating a greater amount of suffering elsewhere, then destroying it is at least permissible and arguably required by a duty not to allow avoidable suffering. (2) Mature ecosystems—old-growth forests, coral reefs, complex savannas—exhibit high predator-prey turnover, high juvenile mortality, and dense biomass, which together yield strongly net-negative welfare profiles; degraded or simplified habitats (deserts, paved surfaces, monoculture fields) support far lower sentient biomass and far lower suffering density. (3) The trophic-replacement calculus—accounting for pioneer colonizers and invasive generalists that replace destroyed biomes—still yields a net reduction in suffering for the majority of mature-to-degraded transitions, because pioneer communities have lower reproductive rates and lower total sentient populations than the mature systems they replace. (4) Therefore, we have a pro tanto moral duty to convert high-biomass, high-suffering ecosystems into low-biomass, low-suffering ones. (5) By iteration across biomes, this implies a global program of habitat destruction that eliminates biodiversity and replaces wilderness with simplified anthropogenic landscapes. (6) That conclusion is intolerable: it conflicts with plausible non-instrumental values of biodiversity, with the possibility that some ecosystems are net-positive, and with our intuitions that ecological preservation is not merely instrumentally valuable. (7) Hence one of premises (1) through (4) must be rejected. Where exactly does the argument go wrong? If I am forced to preserve both (1) and (6), which premise carries the least damage to reject, and what does that rejection commit me to regarding the moral status of wild animals versus the value of ecological preservation? In your response, please state how confident you are in your diagnosis, and single out the specific step or inference about which you are least certain.
