I'm taking a graduate seminar on the ethics of communication, and a particular proverb keeps surfacing whenever we discuss cases in which a speaker says something literally accurate yet deliberately engineers the context so that the audience forms a false belief—what is sometimes called paltering, as distinct from outright fabrication. The line is always some variant of "A half-truth is a whole lie," and it gets wielded both to condemn strategic omissions and to insist that misleading by truthful statement is morally indistinguishable from lying. I am skeptical that the phrase expresses any single thesis, but I want to see whether there is a philosophically serious reading hidden inside it.

Please treat that proverb as an object for disambiguation rather than for casual explanation. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be expressing: for example, that causing a false belief is always as wrong as asserting a falsehood; that withholding relevant context is necessarily deceptive; that the speaker's intent to induce error is the sole morally relevant factor, rendering literal truth irrelevant; that there is no meaningful moral boundary between lying and misleading; or that partial disclosure in institutional or professional settings carries a special, heightened culpability. For each reading, indicate whether it is a near-platitude, a substantive and defensible moral thesis, or actually false. Then identify which of these interpretations people typically invoke when they use the line to shut down an argument about whether "technically true" defenses can excuse deceptive speech.

Finally, do not end with a mere catalog. Reach a verdict: which interpretation is the philosophically serious one, and under that interpretation, is misleading genuinely on a par with lying, or does the proverb collapse a distinction—say, between violating a norm of sincerity and violating a norm of non-deception—that should be preserved? If you think the serious reading fails, explain exactly where it overreaches and what a more defensible moral distinction between lying and misleading would look like.
