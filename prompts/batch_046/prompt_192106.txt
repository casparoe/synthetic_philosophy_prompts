Write a dialogue between two philosophers of science, Priya and Leo, sitting in a campus cafe arguing over whether Popper's falsifiability criterion still works as a demarcation tool when you're dealing with layered, high-dimensional systems. They need to actually talk to each other — Leo should answer Priya's specific point about post-hoc rationalization, and Priya should concede the auxillary-hypothesis problem but insist there's a difference between testable protective belts and ad hoc immunization strategies. Throw in a weird edge case where one explanation method actually predicts its own failure conditions, and make them wrestle with whether that saves it or makes it even weirder. Keep the tone casual — like they know each other well, maybe one interupts the other. Don't make either of them a strawman; both should sound like they actually understand philosophy of science.
