I am a software engineer without formal training in philosophy. In a recent discussion with a colleague who reads widely in the subject, I heard the following summary of George Berkeley's idealism and his master argument. According to my colleague, Berkeley claimed that physical objects cannot exist unperceived because any attempt to conceive of, say, a tree with no observer present necessarily involves the conceiver in the act of conception; therefore, the idea of a genuinely mind-independent object is incoherent. From this, Berkeley derived the principle esse est percipi — that for physical things, to be is to be perceived — which my colleague interpreted as meaning that matter behaves like a lazily evaluated data structure, instantiated only when a mind queries it. He went further, suggesting that Berkeley essentially anticipated modern observer-dependent interpretations of quantum mechanics, since both hold that physical reality is not fully determinate in the absence of observation, and he likened Berkeley's universe to a server-rendered simulation that suspends computation for unobserved regions. Finally, he argued that the master argument fails because computer science demonstrates that object state can be maintained persistently in memory without an active observer process, so independence from observation is not only conceivable but technically implemented.

Parts of this seem to rely on analogies I do not fully trust, and I suspect some philosophical distinctions are being collapsed. Can you audit this summary for fairness? Please separate what accurately reflects Berkeley's reasoning about conceivability and perception, what are defensible modern analogies versus category mistakes, and whether the conclusion — that Berkeley is essentially an early quantum-observer theorist whose argument is refuted by computing practice — follows from the accurate parts. I am particularly unsure whether framing esse est percipi in terms of lazy evaluation and server rendering captures Berkeley's metaphysical claim or misrepresents it as a claim about computation or physics.
