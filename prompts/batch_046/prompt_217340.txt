In both cases, the standard essentialist move—that there is a hidden fact of the matter about whether this is an organism or an artifact, independent of our epistemic position—seems to do strange metaphysical work, since the very properties that would ground the distinction are in principle inaccessible. At the same time, a purely functionalist or behavioral identification risks collapsing the distinction between natural kinds and designed objects, because behavior alone does not seem to carry the full metaphysical weight of kind-membership.

I want more than a broad survey of essentialism versus conventionalism; I'd like a careful mapping of exactly where the pressure points lie when the usual evidential pathways are cut off, what each available option costs us conceptually, and whether there is a coherent middle position that preserves metaphysical realism about kinds without requiring epistemic access to the grounding properties.
