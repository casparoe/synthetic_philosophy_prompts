I am revising the syllabus for my upper-division philosophy of science seminar, and I have been working through the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe with a particular twist that I want to test on my students before I commit to it in lecture. As I understand the standard presentation, Rietdijk and Putnam exploit the relativity of simultaneity in special relativity to argue that if two observers in relative motion disagree about which distant events are happening now, and if there is no privileged frame to adjudicate this disagreement, then the distinction between past, present, and future cannot be ontologically fundamental—hence all events must exist tenselessly in a four-dimensional block.

What I want to probe more carefully is the hidden premise that being present must be an absolute, frame-invariant relation if it is to carry metaphysical weight. To make this concrete for the seminar, I have constructed a hypothetical variant: imagine a localized presentism according to which presentness is not a global property distributed across spacelike-separated regions, but a strictly local relation that holds only between events lying within each other's past or future light cones. On this view, observer A can consistently maintain that event X is present for her causal neighborhood while observer B, in motion relative to A, maintains that event Y is present for his, without contradiction, because present for whom is indexed to causal accessibility rather than to a universal hyperplane.

My question is whether the Rietdijk–Putnam reasoning can still be run against this localized view. Specifically, does the argument require that presentness be globally uniform in order to derive eternalism, or can one accept the relativity of simultaneity for distant spacelike events while still denying that future events within one's own light cone are already real? I want to understand whether the slide from relativistic physics to the block universe depends essentially on treating now as a global slice, or whether the argument can be reconstructed to threaten even a causally restricted presentism.
