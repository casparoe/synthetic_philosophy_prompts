I'm auditing a graduate seminar that has been working through Wittgenstein's On Certainty and the secondary literature on hinge epistemology. As I read and listen to arguments about scientific rationality and skeptical challenges, I've begun to notice a specific dialectical pattern that I cannot place in the existing taxonomy of epistemic moves, and I want to determine whether it is a recognized failure mode or something that still lacks a name.

The pattern runs as follows. A discussant treats a broad framework commitment—say, that inductive reasoning is generally trustworthy, or that our basic perceptual apparatus does not systematically deceive us—as functioning like a Wittgensteinian hinge: not itself justified within the system of beliefs, but rather the standing condition that makes particular justifications and doubts intelligible. They invoke this hinge-status to block a skeptical attack that targets particular claims within the framework. But then, in a different argumentative context—perhaps when defending a specific controversial theory that depends on that very commitment, or when criticizing an opponent who relies on a parallel framework belief—the same discussant treats that identical commitment as an ordinary empirical belief susceptible to counterevidence, statistical revision, or methodological correction. The proposition itself has not changed; only its assigned epistemic role has shifted, and the shift appears to track whether the belief is currently being shielded from attack or being used to attack something else. I am trying to distinguish this from legitimate contextual flexibility about how we weight our commitments, and also from straightforward equivocation.

Is there an established term in hinge epistemology—or in philosophy of science more broadly—for this selective, dialectically motivated toggling between hinge-status and ordinary empirical status applied to the very same proposition? If no standard label exists, can you define the phenomenon precisely, provide boundary conditions that separate it from reasonable contextual framing on the one hand and from simple equivocation or motte-and-bailey retreat on the other, and indicate what would count as a genuine misuse of the hinge concept rather than an appropriate accommodation of argumentative context?
