I am drafting a novel set in a post-institutional settlement where social coordination must emerge without legislative bodies or shared ritual backgrounds, and I have realized that rendering this convincingly requires more than a passing familiarity with philosophical accounts of convention. Rather than a summary of any particular author, I need the full specification—the complete desiderata—that a theory of convention and the evolution of norms must satisfy to count as adequate, especially in conditions that depart from the small-group, face-to-face examples that dominate the literature. Specifically, I want the requirements list organized around: (1) the phenomena it must explain, including so-called silent conventions that stabilize in large anonymous populations and the precise mechanism by which a descriptive regularity acquires prescriptive force without any explicit agreement; (2) the distinctions it must preserve—above all between convention and mere habit, between coordination problems and genuine cooperation problems, and between having normative status and possessing a particular degree of normative weight; (3) the results from evolutionary game theory and from studies of institutional emergence that the theory cannot contradict; and (4) the specific case or consideration that eliminates the largest number of popular theories, with an explanation of why it is fatal. For each requirement, please provide the motivating case and indicate whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or whether it reflects a prejudice of one philosophical camp—particularly regarding whether common knowledge of intentions is an indispensable condition or an artifact of over-reliance on intimate-group examples. Keep the conceptual weight on the criteria and their justification rather than on a comparative scorecard, though a brief assessment of how David Lewis's original account and one prominent evolutionary alternative fare against this list would be useful for my world-building. The goal is not to select a winning theory but to establish the conceptual boundaries within which any credible account must operate, so that the social dynamics in the narrative do not collapse into either pure strategic calculation or unexplained moral obligation.
