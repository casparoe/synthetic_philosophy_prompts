I'm preparing a feature on whether epistemic closure survives once knowledge is analysed through Nozick's tracking conditions rather than through justification. Please construct a realistic, ordinary-life scenario — no science fiction, lotteries, fake barns, demons, or perfect predictors — in which a subject knows that p, p strictly entails q, yet the subject does not know q, specifically because Nozick's sensitivity and adherence conditions are satisfied for p but violated for q, with the violation for q not inherited from any failure concerning p. For both propositions, state the belief, the method of acquisition, and the relevant nearby worlds; then show, condition by condition, why tracking delivers knowledge of p but withholds it for q. Pinpoint the single feature of the case that isolates this divergence, describe what happens to the verdicts if that feature is removed, and finish with the strongest reply available to a defender of closure — for example, that the method employed for q differs from that for p, or that the entailment is not transparent to the subject — together with an assessment of whether that reply succeeds.
