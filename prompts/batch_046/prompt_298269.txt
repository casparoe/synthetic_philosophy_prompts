Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of cultural causation must satisfy if it aims to rehabilitate Weber's core thesis—that Calvinist values were causally efficacious in the emergence of modern capitalism—without collapsing into historical materialism on one side or an idealism in which values float free of institutional and psychological constraints on the other. Specify the full list: the comparative-historical cases it must accommodate (the presence of capitalist development in Calvinist regions, its relative absence in Lutheran and Catholic contexts with comparable economic structures, and the persistence of capitalist behavior long after the religious beliefs that allegedly produced it have declined); the conceptual distinctions it must preserve intact (value-rational versus instrumentally rational action, elective affinity versus direct linear causation, culture as a selection environment versus culture as an independent agent, and the difference between having a value orientation and being caused by one); the methodological constraints it must respect (methodological individualism, the prohibition on treating collective values as agents with intentions, consistency with comparative historical sociology, and compatibility with the finding that identical economic incentives produce different behavioral patterns across cultural contexts); and the explanatory results it must reproduce (why the "spirit" of capitalism outlived its theological origins, why functionally similar religious values in other traditions failed to generate comparable economic dispositions, and why purely structural accounts of capital accumulation leave a residual behavioral pattern unexplained). For each desideratum, state the motivating objection or case behind it; flag which are genuinely non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences (materialist, interpretivist, rational-choice institutionalist); and identify whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable—especially whether maintaining methodological individualism while granting values independent causal weight generates a contradiction that forces a choice rather than a synthesis. Then briefly score Weber's original elective-affinity model, a rational-choice cultural-embeddedness account, and a practice-theory institutionalist account against the list, noting for each where it fails a hard requirement versus merely violating a preference, and indicate which single desideratum does the most damage to the greatest number of contenders.
