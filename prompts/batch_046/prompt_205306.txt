Compare Rorty's antirepresentationalist neopragmatism with a reconstructed position we can call calibrationist antirepresentationalism. On Rorty's view, propositional vocabularies are tools for coping rather than representations of an intrinsic reality, and objectivity is reduced to intersubjective agreement within a community of inquirers; there is no world-language relation that could make a vocabulary "true" in a representational sense, and predictive failure is ultimately a social or practical breakdown rather than a misalignment with how things stand independently. The calibrationist view accepts that propositional content is non-representational and vocabulary-bound, but adds that there exists a minimal, non-propositional indexical calibration between an organism and its environment—drawing on ecological psychology's notion of direct perception of affordances—that explains why some vocabularies systematically fail to support successful action. On this reconstructed view, calibration is causal and indexical rather than representational: it tracks environmental constraints without attributing an intrinsic nature to reality, and it offers a pre-propositional criterion for why some vocabularies are abandoned while others are retained.
