hey, ive been thinking about simultaneous invention - Newton and Leibniz, Darwin and Wallace - and there's this argumentative move I keep seeing but can't name. First people say the discovery was inevitable, the time was ripe, so fighting over priority is just ego. Then in the very next breath they say the specific winner changed everything - Newton's notation stifled british math for over a century, Darwin's long evidence-gathering made the theory stick where Wallace's sketch might have faded. They use inevitability to dismiss credit, but path-dependence to explain actual history, and they never say how both can be true at once, becuase they never specify what level each claim applies to. Is there an established term for this exact oscillation? I know about multiple discovery and about technological path dependence, but this feels like a specific reasoning failure in philosophy of science. If there's no name, how should we carve it apart from plain inconsistency?
