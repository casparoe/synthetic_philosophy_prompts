Please explain the conceptual tension at the heart of Gödel's argument that time is unreal in a rotating universe. Gödel observed that Einstein's field equations permit a cosmological model with rotation in which closed timelike curves exist, seemingly allowing an observer to return to their own past. From this he concluded that an objective passage of time cannot exist, since no globally consistent 'now' can be defined. However, the Gödel metric itself is expressed in a coordinate system with a global time parameter. Explain whether the presence of this global coordinate undermines Gödel's conclusion, or whether his argument depends on a deeper metaphysical claim—namely, that for time to be real there must be a universal linear ordering of events rather than merely a local causal structure. In particular, clarify how the existence of closed timelike curves affects the distinction between local and global temporal ordering, and whether this distinction is sufficient to rescue an objective flow of time within the model.
