Construct a thought experiment that isolates the mechanism by which consciousness might function as a user illusion rather than as a direct feature of cognitive architecture. Design two variants of an artificial system—Variant A and Variant B—situated in an ordinary clinical neuroengineering laboratory. Both variants must possess identical global workspaces, identical self-models, identical behavioural outputs, and identical capacities for verbal report; the only permitted difference is that Variant B accesses its internal states through a mediated representational layer that compresses those states into a simplified phenomenal narrative (the user-interface layer), whereas Variant A accesses the same states through direct, unmediated transparency with no intermediate representational compression. In your scenario, Variant B reports rich phenomenal experience, while Variant A denies having any, despite both passing every standard behavioural and functional test for consciousness. Keep the scenario to one paragraph, use no science-fictional elements, no perfect predictors, no omniscient observers, and no appeals to exotic physics. After presenting the scenario, provide the reasoning: state what the user-illusion theory predicts for each variant, contrast this with the verdict of a global-workspace functionalist, identify the precise structural feature that causes the theories to diverge, and explain what happens to the case if that feature is removed. Then include an honest assessment of how a functionalist would attack the scenario—specifically, whether they can argue that direct transparency in Variant A is not an absence of illusion but merely a different modality of phenomenal access—and say whether that defence collapses the distinction into a standard zombie argument. Please write in British English. If the task rests on a confused premise—for instance, if direct transparency is incoherent in a system that already possesses a self-model—say so plainly rather than forcing the scenario through.
