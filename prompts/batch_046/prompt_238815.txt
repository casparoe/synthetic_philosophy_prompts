I'm on the university's general-ed committee and we vote thursday on a mandatory first-year seminar called "Reasoning Without Foundations." The course teaches students that direct moral reasoning is unreliable, and instead they have to justify any normative claim through a three-stage filter: (1) translate it into a preference-order for an ideal observer with full information but no particular social identity; (2) run it through a "stability constraint" that rejects norms which, if adopted, would undermine the deliberative framework itself; (3) apply a "non-circularity check" that blocks using the framework's own outputs as inputs. The philosophy dept supports it; student government and two faculty from poli-sci say it's teaching kids to dodge real moral responsability by hiding behind hypothetical proceedures. I need a short memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions—something the committee can actually vote on. The memo should treat the student-gov objection seriously rather than just dismissing it, and it should explain what's at stake philosophically: the idea that norms get their force indirectly, through idealized deliberation, rather than from direct intuition. Structure it like this: Bottom line (2-3 sentences), What the course actually requires (short paragraph), The philosophical stakes—indirect normativity and why idealized deliberation isn't just evasion (couple paragraphs), The strongest case against (don't strawman—engage the worry about avoiding moral committment), Verdict with any conditions (short). Keep it around 800 words. No citations, no bibliographies, just the reasoning.
