I've been debating whether to join this biotech trial my lab is running—it's not treating a disease, it's a one-time gene edit that raises your hedonic floor so you literally can't experience despair, anxiety, or grief. They say you'll still understand that bad things happen, you just won't feel them in that gut-level way. My partner thinks this is obviously good and that anyone who hesitates is just attached to suffering for romatic reasons. But I'm stuck on whether you can still have genuine moral growth if you never actually suffer from your mistakes.

The protocol apparently doesn't just dull pain; it also dampens the prediction-error signals your brain uses to update beliefs when you're wrong, so you might keep making the same mistake without that sharp 'this was bad' feeling. I guess my question is whether there's a real philosophical difference between wanting to get rid of suffering and wanting to get rid of the learning mechanism that comes with it, or if I'm just rationalizing being scared of change. Also, if everyone eventually did this, would we still be capable of recognizing injustice, or would we just intellectually note it without being moved?
