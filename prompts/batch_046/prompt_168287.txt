I recently encountered an unnamed popular article summarizing current thinking on societal collapse. The author writes: "The Late Bronze Age crisis, the Classic Maya abandonment, and Easter Island's ecological crisis are essentially the same phenomenon—complex societies expand until they hit hard environmental limits, after which political structures dissolve rapidly. Joseph Tainter demonstrated that collapse is merely rapid simplification driven by declining marginal returns on social complexity, which makes it a kind of economic adjustment rather than a catastrophe. Jared Diamond presented Easter Island as the purest case of ecological suicide, claiming that the Rapa Nui deliberately destroyed their palm forests, ruined their agricultural base, and then collapsed into civil war and demographic freefall. More recent archaeology has overturned the collapse framework entirely for both the Maya and the Bronze Age: the southern Maya cities emptied but Maya civilization continued in the northern lowlands and highlands, while the eastern Mediterranean states simply decentralized into smaller Iron Age kingdoms. Therefore, the concept of collapse is largely a modern myth—societies do not truly fail, they restructure, and only Easter Island counts as a genuine collapse, which proves that environmental destruction is fatal solely for isolated societies lacking external trade partners."
