I am a doctoral candidate in archaeology and comparative historical sociology preparing a dissertation chapter on long-term societal transformations. Rather than adjudicating between frameworks prematurely, I want to formalize the disagreement between the collapse-as-catastrophic-failure tradition and the continuity-through-reconfiguration tradition regarding the Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, the Classic Maya lowlands, and Rapa Nui (Easter Island). Draft the adversarial-collaboration document these two camps should produce.

Structure it as follows:

1. Agreed empirical claims: List the specific archaeological, paleoecological, and settlement-pattern observations about all three cases that both sides can sign without reservation—e.g., trajectories in settlement density, monument-construction rates, exchange-network topology, and environmental-proxy data—phrased in vocabulary that does not presuppose either collapse or continuity.

2. Neutral statements of disagreement: State precisely where the camps diverge, with each side’s position rendered in wording its own proponents would accept. Distinguish at least: (a) whether demographic contraction necessarily implies societal failure; (b) whether political decentralization counts as collapse or as adaptive restructuring; and (c) whether the archaeological record’s silence about particular institutions is evidence of their disappearance or of a shift in material practices that renders them archaeologically invisible.

3. Cruxes: Identify the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp. I suspect two are particularly important: whether there exists a non-arbitrary threshold of institutional simplification that distinguishes collapse from ordinary change, and whether ecological stress functions as a primary cause, a concurrent correlate, or an ex post narrative framing device.

4. Shared evidentiary tests: For each crux, specify a concrete comparative pattern or dataset—drawn from the three regions mentioned or from an analogous context—that both camps agree bears on the issue, and record in advance what each camp predicts about that evidence. For example, if a region exhibits rapid depopulation without corresponding ecological degradation, state which interpretation each camp expects that pattern to support and why.

5. Residual verbal disputes: If any portion of the disagreement is merely terminological—e.g., whether society refers to a state apparatus, an ethnic network, or an economic integration—say so explicitly, and propose the disambiguated vocabulary that would leave only a substantive disagreement about the cases.

Keep the document free of bibliographic citations, reading lists, and appeals to authority; I need the conceptual architecture and the predictive commitments, not a literature survey. Also avoid framing either camp as denying the material record; both accept the archaeological evidence but interpret its significance differently.
