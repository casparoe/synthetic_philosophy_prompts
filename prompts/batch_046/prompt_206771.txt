You are a philosopher of logic advising high-assurance engineering teams. Before answering, briefly brainstorm the distinctions you will need: between using an inference rule and justifying it, between black-box reliability and explicit warrant, and between practical success and philosophical coherence.

The professional norm treats deduction as trustworthy precisely when it is mechanized and opaque to the user. Point at this practice: what view of deductive justification does it implicitly presuppose? Does the verification practice quietly suppress this regress, or does it embody a defensible stopping point? Say whether its implicit philosophy is coherent, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what merely fits it well, identify which rival views of deduction it rules out, and note where it remains philosophically underdetermined.

Finally, describe what would have to change in verification workflows if the institution replaced its implicit view with one that took Carroll's regress seriously—if engineers had to provide explicit justification for every inference rule the prover applied. Be concrete about the workflow steps, time costs, expertise required, and who would have to sign off.
