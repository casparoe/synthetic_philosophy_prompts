Before assessing any reconstruction of the Mingjia debate concerning whether a white horse is not a horse, I want an explicit specification of what such an interpretation must deliver. Write the desiderata that any adequate account of Gongsun Long's argument has to satisfy: the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must respect, the puzzles it must dissolve, and the results with which it must be consistent, with the consideration behind each requirement. Specifically address the following. First, the ontological separation of colour and form, and whether the argument presupposes that universals of different kinds do not intersect. Second, the logical form of the inference, and whether a reconstruction must invoke modern distinctions—such as those between intension and extension, or between predication and identity—without anachronistically imposing a Fregean or Russellian apparatus. Third, fidelity to the textual and inter-textual context, including the relation to Hui Shi's theses on similarity and difference, and whether the paradox targets a naive realism about shared properties. Fourth, consistency with the broader Mohist warning that linguistic distinctions ought not to distort practical ontology. Fifth, whether the argument must be read as a reductio of common-sense categorisation or can be taken as a positive thesis about the relativity of kind-membership. Sixth, the capacity to explain why the dialogue was received as genuinely paradoxical rather than as mere sophistry, including any rhetorical or pedagogical function within the School of Names. For each desideratum, indicate whether it is non-negotiable for philosophical seriousness or merely a preference of one camp—such as analytic philosophers of language versus historians of Chinese thought. Flag any pair of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable, particularly the tension between historical fidelity and formal rigour, and between treating the argument as ontologically serious versus treating it as performative rhetoric. You need not supply a full scorecard, but briefly note where the standard logical-reconstructionist approach, the contextual-historical approach, and any hybrid view stand against the list.
