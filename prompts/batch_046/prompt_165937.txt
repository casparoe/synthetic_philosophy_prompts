Audit the analogy, common in data-policy debates, that personal data functions like oil: extracted, refined, and fueling a digital economy, with individuals holding mineral-style property rights that can be licensed for royalties. Provide the correspondence table—extraction, refining, pipeline, depletion, and ownership mapped onto data collection, algorithmic processing, transmission, storage costs, and legal control—then identify the disanalogies that bear on the argument, particularly non-rivalry, the absence of a depletion curve, and the fact that disseminated data cannot be reclaimed in the way unburned fuel can. Finally, assess whether the policy conclusion—that personal data should be governed by exclusive, market-transferable property rights—survives these failures, or whether they instead support a commons, fiduciary-duty, or human-rights-based regulatory model.
