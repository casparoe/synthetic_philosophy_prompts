Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a spacetime substantivalist and a relationalist about the ontological status of space and motion, with Newton's bucket argument and its modern physical descendants as the central organizing case. The substantivalist maintains that spacetime is a substantial entity existing independently of material bodies, and that absolute rotational motion is instantiated in that substrate. The relationalist maintains that spacetime is reducible to relations among material bodies and events, and that rotational dynamics can be fully accounted for by relations to the total distribution of matter without positing an independent background.

Your deliverable is the document both parties could sign. Structure it as follows.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims that both sides accept. These should cover: (a) the empirical behavior of rotating fluid bodies, specifically that a bucket of water rotated about its axis develops a concave surface even when there is no observable motion relative to the bucket walls or nearby apparatus, and that analogous inertial effects (Coriolis and centrifugal phenomena) appear for bodies rotated in regions far from local matter; (b) the logical form of Newton's argument, paraphrased without quotation, that if rotational dynamics are physically real yet there is no relational motion to immediately adjacent bodies, then either an absolute space exists in which that rotation occurs or some non-local relational structure must carry the explanatory burden; (c) the formal structure of Newtonian dynamics, particularly that the equations distinguish rotational from inertial motion through terms that depend on an absolute or globally defined frame; (d) the status of general-relativistic dynamics, specifically that the metric field is dynamical, coupled to the stress-energy of matter, and capable of propagating perturbations through regions with negligible local matter density.

Second, a disagreements table with two columns, each column phrased in wording its own side accepts. The rows should address: (i) whether the curvature of water in a rotating bucket demonstrates motion relative to an absolute spatial substrate or motion relative to the distant cosmic distribution of matter; (ii) whether the metric field in general relativity should be interpreted as a substantial spacetime geometry or as a codification of relational correlations among material detectors and worldlines; (iii) whether rotational inertia is an intrinsic property of a body interacting with a background structure or an emergent property arising from dynamical interaction with all other matter in the universe.

Third, the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party. For each crux, provide: (a) a concrete case, argument, or empirical scenario both sides agree is relevant; (b) each side's explicit prediction or interpretation of that case, recorded in advance. The cruxes must include at least the following. One: whether a fully relational dynamics—defining inertial frames solely through instantaneous material configurations without boundary conditions at spatial infinity—can reproduce Newtonian rotational inertia without empirical divergence from observed dynamics. Two: whether gravitational perturbations propagating through vacuum regions require a substantial spacetime carrier or can be interpreted purely as relational correlations among distant detectors. Three: whether the concept of rotation remains physically well-defined in a hypothetical universe containing exactly one rotating body and nothing else.

Fourth, a brief concluding assessment stating which parts of the disagreement appear genuinely substantive and which, if any, appear partly verbal—for example, whether "absolute space" and "the total material distribution functioning as an inertial frame" are competing ontologies or differently named descriptions of the same explanatory structure. If you identify a verbal component, specify exactly which formulations map onto one another and why.

Finally, include a self-critical meta-note specifying: (a) what specific theoretical result or observation—such as a derivation of centrifugal forces from purely local relational interactions without cosmic boundary conditions, or an empirical divergence between Machian predictions and observed frame-dragging—would cause you to revise your characterization of the disagreement; and (b) the specific step in your reasoning, likely in the interpretation of the metric field or in the analysis of rotational inertia, that you regard as most vulnerable to error.

Write in a formal, precise tone appropriate to a philosophical-physics collaboration document. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote historical texts verbatim; paraphrase all reasoning without quotation marks.
