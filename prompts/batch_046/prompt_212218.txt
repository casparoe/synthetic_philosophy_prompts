As a journalist covering science and ideas, I also serve on the UK Climate Science Accreditation Panel. By 15 June the panel must issue a formal recommendation on whether the Treasury should adopt the Mercator Integrated Assessment Model for carbon-pricing forecasts. The model relies on deliberately unrealistic premises — homogeneous economic agents with perfect rationality and complete information — yet its architects invoke what philosophers of economics call the F-twist: that predictive success, rather than descriptive realism, is the proper criterion for accepting a model in policy work. The chair contends that such false assumptions undermine democratic legitimacy irrespective of back-testing performance. Please draft the panel’s decision memo: recommend endorsement with conditions, conditional endorsement, or rejection; specify a concrete timeline for review or implementation; treat the modelling team’s argument with intellectual seriousness; and engage honestly with the strongest reason for the opposing position. The memo should be accessible to Treasury officials without specialist philosophical training, and it should avoid bibliographic appeals or citation lists — articulate the conceptual tension directly.
