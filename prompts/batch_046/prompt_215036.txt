Rietdijk and Putnam both show that presentism can't work with special relativity. Rietdijk said that because simultaneity is relative, there is no universal 'now', so all events exist equally — that's eternalism. He used the Andromeda paradox: if I walk towards Andromeda my plane of simultaneity shifts, so an event that was in the future is suddenly in the past. That proves presentism is wrong because presentism says only present things exist, but here the existence of distant events depends on my walking speed. Putnam added that since there's no privileged frame, we can't pick one 'now' slice, so we must accept the block universe where past, present and future are all real.

As for conventionality, Reichenbach showed that simultaneity is just a convention about clock synchronisation. This means there's no physical fact about what's simultaneous, so the presentist can actually choose a conventional frame and declare that frame's 'now' to be the real present. Even with rotating frames, where the Sagnac effect complicates clock synchronisation, the presentist just picks the convention that suits their ontology. Therefore conventionality saves presentism rather than undermining the block-universe argument.

Also, Rietdijk's argument is basically the same as Putnam's: both say determinism follows from the four-dimensional spacetime manifold, which means eternalism is true.
