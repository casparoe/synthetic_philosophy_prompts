I'm trying to pin down a move I see in arguments about blame and moral responsibility. Someone insists they've genuinely moved past resentment — done the therapy, read the books, they're clean of it — but then they vigorously defend punishing the wrongdoer for the sake of 'social norms' or 'community defence'. They treat their own absence of resentment as a kind of moral maturity, yet they still want the full reactive machinery switched on by other people. It's not quite Strawson's objective attitude, since they still talk in terms of desert and blame rather than clinical management, and it's not plain vicarious resentment because they deny feeling anything on behalf of others. Does this split have an established name, and if not, how should we seperate it from nearby concepts? Reply with exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion.
