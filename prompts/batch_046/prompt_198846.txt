hey so ive been chewing on that old death in damascus story — the servant sees death surprised in the market, borrows a horse, rides to samarra, and death is like yep we had an appointment here tonight. my roommate thinks its just a spooky fairy tale but i think it actually breaks determinism if you follow it through, and now im stuck going in circles.

heres the chain:
1. the servant flees damascus specifically to avoid death.
2. that flight is literally the cause of him being in samarra.
3. so his attempt to prevent death was the direct cause of it happening.
4. now say determinism means every event is fixed in advance, including my actions. if i learn im fated to die in a plane crash tuesday, and i cancel my flight, determinism still has to fill in the outcome — maybe i die in a bus crash tuesday instead.
5. that means knowing your fate never lets you escape it; it just swaps the mechanism.
6. but if thats true, then doctors giving risk warnings is useless, because telling someone theyll get lung cancer just makes them switch to some other fatal thing.
7. yet clearly doctors save lives with warnings all the time.
8. so determinism must be false — otherwise predictive knowledge would be causally impotent.

i really want to hold onto 3 (the flight-cause thing feels rock solid from the story) and 7 (doctors obviously help). but i also feel like 4 is where i went off the rails, maybe im mixing up fate decides the ending with fate decides every detail. idk which link i should break and what it costs me?
