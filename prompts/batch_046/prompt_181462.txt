On my view, Machiavelli's river metaphor and his discussion of preparatory works imply that virtù functions less as personal heroism and more as an adaptive, institutional form of resilience that anticipates contingency. Furthermore, I proposed that Machiavelli's gendered imagery of fortune should not be read merely as a rhetorical device to be subdued, but rather as an early, pre-statistical recognition that outcomes are distributed across a field of risk that no single actor can fully dominate. We circled back repeatedly to the same question: can fortune genuinely be beaten, or can the prudent prince only alter the probabilities within an irreducible domain of chance?
