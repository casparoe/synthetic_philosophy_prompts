Two teachers in my dept got into it over lunch about LLM tutors in intro courses. They'd both been reading Whiteheads stuff on the rhythm of education -- romance, then precision, then generalization.

Maya said giving students an LLM to answer "why is this cool?" in week one is a category error. The romance stage needs direct, unmediated wonder; outsourcing curiosity to a tool that synthesizes secondhand explanations skips the personal encounter that makes a subject come alive. She said its like sending a proxy to a first date.

Jon said thats romanticizing romance -- literally. He argued an LLM can serve the romance stage if you prompt it for bizarre historical connections or weird analogies, not summaries. The problem isnt mediation, its using the tool for precision (drilling rules) before romance is established. He thinks Maya is treating "direct" as synonymous with "good," which Whitehead never claimed.

Maya came back that even if the LLM sparks wonder, the wonder isnt the students own -- its manufactured excitement. Whiteheads point was that education starts with the learners own excitement, not imported excitement.

Jon replied that all education is mediated: textbooks, teachers, language itself. The LLM is just another mediator, and if it helps a student find their own question, its serving romance, not undermining it. He also said if we reject every mediator except raw experience, we couldnt teach anyone who hadnt already had the experience.
