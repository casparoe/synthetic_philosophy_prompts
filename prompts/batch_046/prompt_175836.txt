Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a historical constructivist and a critical realist on whether historical accounts are predominantly shaped by the power of those who prevail.

The constructivist holds that historical narratives are principally formed by institutional control over archives, commemorative practices, and educational canons, with no neutral, power-independent access to past events; victory should be understood as scalar rather than binary, encompassing degrees of control over mnemonic institutions, and the absence of counter-narratives often reflects suppression rather than non-occurrence.

The critical realist holds that although power profoundly filters which records survive and which voices are preserved, material traces — archaeological, demographic, environmental — and structural constraints permit more or less accurate reconstruction of past events; some occurrences are highly resistant to narrative revision, and archival silence can sometimes indicate genuine absence rather than mere suppression.

Please produce:

- A numbered section of claims both can sign, covering archival formation, source selection, commemorative practice, and the epistemic limits of reconstruction. Each claim must be worded so that neither side feels it concedes its core position.

- The precise residual disagreements, phrased so that each side accepts the wording of its own view. Distinguish genuine disputes from verbal ones — for instance, whether victory should be treated as binary or scalar, and whether writing history denotes authoring narratives, curating archives, or both — and identify which parts are verbal and why.

- The cruxes: sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party. I suggest two: first, whether there exists an archival silence threshold — a point at which the sustained absence of counter-evidence becomes epistemically significant in itself, rather than being explicable solely by suppression; second, whether the counterfactual robustness of non-textual material traces can ever provide a check on narrative revision without relying on prior interpretive commitments that are themselves shaped by power.

- For each crux, specify a concrete case or category of evidence both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Choose cases that are conceptually clear and do not depend on obscure specialist knowledge.

- Format the output as a numbered agreed-claims section, followed by a disagreements table with columns for the issue, the constructivist formulation, the realist formulation, and whether the dispute is verbal. Then present the cruxes section with the shared framing, the relevant case or evidence type, and the divergent predictions.

Write in British English. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote specific texts verbatim; paraphrase well-known phrases without quotation marks. Ensure the prompt does not ask for information that requires web access to answer accurately.
