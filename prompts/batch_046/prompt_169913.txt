I'm a retired molecular geneticist, and since I stopped running the lab I've gotten weirdly into philoshophy of science. Next month I'm giving a talk at my old university's biology colloquium defending the idea that philosophy isn't dead weight for working scientists, despite Weinberg's dismissals and Hawking saying philosophy is dead and that Feynman comparison of philosophers to ornithologists. My actual thesis is narrower and weirder: I call it conceptual janitor work — philosophers don't discover genes or build detectors, but they clean hidden statistical assumptions out of our models, and I claim this has changed actual desing choices in three fields I know: population genetics, climate proxy reconstruction, and quantum measurement interpretation.

The audience will be mostly working biologists, two physicists who still roll their eyes at philosophy, and maybe one grad studen who reads philosophy blogs. I want a prep sheet for the Q&A — about eight objections I'll actually recieve, ordered by how much damage they'd do, phrased like a skeptical scientist would say them (blunt, not academic-polite). For each: a reply I can deliver in about two minutes, plus a note on where I'm being soft or hand-waving. And please include the one question I'm hoping nobody asks — probably something asking me to name one specific experiment that was redesigned solely because of a philosopher's paper.

Write it casually — I'm not submitting this to a journal, just trying not to get laughed out of the room.
