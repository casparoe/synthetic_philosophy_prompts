I'm drafting a unit on migration ethics and basicly every semester my students reach for the same analogy: a nation-state is like a condo association — residents pay dues, vote on rules, and can blacklist outsiders from the pool. They lean on it to defend a hard right to exclude, saying open borders violate collective property rights the way letting strangers crash the ameneties would. Can you audit how far this holds? I want the correspondence table — what plays the role of the board, the dues, the common pool, the blacklist — then the disanalogies that matter, especially birthright membership versus voluntary buy-in and the fact that states coerce non-citizens at borders in ways condo boards can't. Also check whether the analogy quietly imports a consumer-choice model of citizenship that republican liberty theorists reject. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then reasoning, no preamble and don't restate my question.
