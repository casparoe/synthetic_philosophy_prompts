Scholars have been fighting about democratic backsliding for over a decade now -- Hungary, Poland, Turkey, the US -- and despite mountains of data it feels like were running in circles and talking past eachother. My working theory is the stall isn't really empirical: we mostly agree courts got packed and media independence dropped. The split seems conceptual. One camp treats backsliding as a measurable decline on indices like V-Dem or Polity, basically continuous erosion of veto points. The other treats it as a normative breach -- specific institutional changes that violate democratic equality, regardless of where the country lands on a scale. So are they actually disagreeing about facts, or answering different questions under one label? Is there a hidden formal question doing the work, like whether democratic stability requires a specific threshold of institutional interdependence, or is the concept just too normatively loaded and partisan to be analytically useful? Give me a diagnosis with reasons, not a literature review: what does each side actually commit to, is this verbal or substantive, and could anything -- a new dataset, a formal model, a conceptual split -- actually move the needle? Or should we just drop the term?
