Because the prior penalises descriptive complexity exponentially, a world containing a vast, spatially distributed population of minimally sentient beings—each of which requires some independent specification within the world-description—receives far lower prior weight than a world containing a single, highly integrated but descriptively simple super-agent, even if the aggregate moral value of the distributed population is orders of magnitude greater. The consequentialist agent, who holds that moral weight scales with the number and quality of experiences rather than with compressibility, therefore discovers that her predictive framework systematically discounts precisely those high-cardinality futures her ethics demands she favour and preserve.
