Please argue that Leibniz's thesis that the actual world is the best of all possible worlds should be interpreted through the lens of what might be called compossibility saturation rather than through any aggregative measure of good over evil. On this reading, God does not maximize a global utility function but selects the unique maximal set of essences that can be jointly instantiated without contradiction, where the criterion of superiority is the structural density of genuinely compatible perfections—especially the realization of rational monads at every viable grade of perception—rather than the minimization of suffering or the maximization of happiness. In developing this argument, explain why a hypothetical world that eliminates a particular evil (for instance, the suffering of a specific class of sentient beings) by omitting one actual grade of monadic perception altogether is not, on this view, a better world, and defend the claim that saturation is not equivalent to the thesis that every existing thing is metaphysically necessary. You need not cite specific texts or provide bibliographic references; construct the position conceptually from the general Leibnizian framework of compossibility, essence, and divine choice.
