I've been thinking about this thing I call the negative-space fallacy in history from below. The idea is that microhistorians see a gap in state archieves—like a village missing from tax rolls—and build this whole story about peasant resistance or hidden economies. But from a data perspective thats just imputing intent to missing data. So my position is basically that microhistory and history-from-below are actually more speculative than elite/state history, because they have to invent motives for silence, and the archieves are too sparse to check them. What would count against it? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be—including specifc cases where archival absence turned out to be intentional resistance, or methods microhistorians use to account for missing data without just making stuff up. And be honest about whether I've qualified this into unfalsafiability—like if every gap can be dismissed as bureaucratic noise, does the view risk anything at all? Is that insulation a problem for a claim like this?
