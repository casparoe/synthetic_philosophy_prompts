Give me your actual credence — a specific number between 0 and 1 — that the Rietdijk–Putnam argument from special relativity succeeds in establishing eternalism (the block universe) over presentism for macroscopic events, rather than merely exposing an epistemic limitation in defining a global present. I do not want an answer that it depends on interpretation of special relativity; I want the reasoning decomposed into explicit terms, each with its own rough probability and a single line of justification, then combined into the overall figure.

Decompose it as follows:

1. The probability that special relativity genuinely lacks any empirically distinguishable privileged foliation — that Lorentzian interpretations with a hidden absolute simultaneity are genuinely ad hoc or metaphysically bloated rather than physically viable.
2. The probability that presentism can survive without a global hyperplane of simultaneity — through point-presentism, local presentism, or a dynamically privileged frame — without either collapsing into eternalism or becoming empirically incoherent.
3. The probability that the inference from absence of an invariant simultaneity relation to all spacetime events being ontologically on a par is valid, as opposed to being a non sequitur that confuses frame-dependent ordering with ontological status.
4. The probability that quantum gravity, cosmological boundary conditions, or a physically meaningful cosmic time parameter could supply a non-arbitrary global present that undermines the argument's core premise.

For each term, state the number and one line of reasoning. Then combine them, formally or informally, into your overall credence. Finally, identify which term you are least confident in, and specify what kind of evidence — for instance, a confirmed Lorentz-violating effect at the Planck scale, or a presentist semantics for relativistic quantum field theory that makes novel predictions — would shift your number most, and in which direction.
