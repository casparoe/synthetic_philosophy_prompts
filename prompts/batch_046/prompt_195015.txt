ok so i've been trying to map my gut reactions on private-language-style cases and i'm not sure they hang together. here's where i land:

1. a hermit names a private feeling "glorp" and tracks it in a mental diary over years. he thinks he remembers the original rule linking glorp to the feeling, but there's no external check. my take: not real language, because seeming-to-follow-the-rule is all there is, and there's no gap between that and actually following it.

2. same hermit, but now he writes explicit internal rules tying glorp to self-observable conditions (heart rate, posture). he applies them consistently and can catch himself breaking them. my take: this feels closer to language, but i still want to say it's parasitic on public-language concepts he learned before going hermit.

3. two hermits on seperate islands independantly invent identical private notations for internal states. they never meet, but if they did they'd match perfectly. my take: the correlation doesn't retroactively make their symbols public or shared, but it also feels different from case 1 — like there's some hidden structure — and i know i should reject that feeling but can't fully.

4. a locked-in patient builds a mental alphabet for her own cognitive states, using it to correct her internal monologue. she can misapply a symbol and fix it. my take: this is the strongest candidate for a real private language, yet i still think it's not genuinely private because the notion of "correction" smuggles in public norms.

5. an AI trained only on its own activation patterns assigns cluster labels and uses them in reasoning chains, with no human-readable mapping. my take: not a private language in Wittgenstein's sense, but honestly i can't say why without borrowing criteria that only apply to public-language users.

i want a principle (or maybe two competing ones) that captures 1-5, stated precisely, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it. it should predict what happens in a case i haven't listed — say, a hermit who invents a private calculus for shades of color only she can perceive, with explicit axioms and derivation rules. and i want an honest call on which of my verdicts, probably 2 or 4, has to be dropped if we keep the principle simple rather than saving every intuition at the cost of complexity.

please also seperate what's pretty settled in the Wittgenstein literature from what's genuinely contested among people who study this (like community-view vs individualist readings of rule-following), and don't present a disputed interpretation as if it's the consensus.
