I'm drafting a dissertation chapter on counterfactual reasoning in historical explanation, and I want to formalize the boundary between structural inevitability and path-dependent contingency. Below are my graded intuitions about six macro-historical outcomes, assessed for whether they were unavoidable regardless of perturbations to agents, mechanisms, or timing.

1. Imperial consolidation in Rome after Actium: Even had Octavian died, senatorial dysfunction and imperial scale made monarchical consolidation unavoidable. Verdict: the form was inevitable; the Augustan dynasty was not.

2. The fall of Constantinople in 1453: Without Mehmed II's siege cannon, the city might have held that year, yet Ottoman demographic and military pressure made Byzantine collapse unavoidable within a generation. Verdict: collapse was inevitable within a narrow modal window; the precise year and siege technology were contingent.

3. Standardized measurement in revolutionary France: Given local measure chaos and the state's rationalizing ideology, some national system was unavoidable, but the decimal meter anchored to the Paris meridian was a committee artifact. Verdict: standardization inevitable; metric/decimal form contingent.

4. The League of Nations' failure to restrain 1930s aggression: Even with US membership or without Wilson's stroke, the unanimity rule and absence of enforcement mechanisms made effective collective security structurally impossible against determined revisionist powers. Verdict: failure was inevitable; the specific sequence of aggressions (Manchuria, Ethiopia, Rhineland) was path-dependent.

5. Packet-switched networking by the mid-1970s: Had Cerf and Kahn never collaborated, ARPA funding priorities and parallel British research make some protocol architecture highly probable, yet TCP/IP was one convergent design among several. Verdict: network architecture inevitable; the specific protocol stack contingent.

6. Federal commercial authority in the US Constitution: Whatever the Convention's specific plan, economic chaos under the Articles made some interstate commerce power necessary, but Madison's precise wording reflects drafting compromise. Verdict: authority inevitable; textual form contingent.

I'm testing whether a principle can capture these—perhaps grading inevitability by the modal proximity of worlds in which all independent causal pathways are blocked, distinguishing institutional feedback loops (which generate convergence) from agent-level decision nodes (which generate branching). Yet cases 2 and 4 pull in opposite directions: in 2 I treat temporal precision as contingent despite structural pressure, while in 4 I treat the failure pathway as contingent but the outcome as locked. Meanwhile, case 5 raises whether technological convergence should count pathway independence differently than political convergence.

Can a unified account accommodate all six verdicts? If not, which should I abandon—the League's structural failure, or TCP/IP's contingency among convergent designs? Please apply the resulting principle also to an unlisted case: given late-Soviet economic stagnation and rising republican nationalism, was the USSR's dissolution in 1991 structurally inevitable, or was it contingent on Gorbachev's specific sequencing of glasnost and perestroika and his refusal to deploy force to preserve the union?
