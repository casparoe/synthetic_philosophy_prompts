Here's an argument my roommate built after reading about neural prosthetics and it seems to prove way to much. (1) If you swap out one biological neuron for a silicon chip that copies its exact input-output mapping, your consciousness doesn't flicker out, since nothing functionaly changed. (2) So if you do this one neuron at a time, you eventually get a fully synthetic brain and you're still you—standard upload story. (3) But now imagine each replacement chip doesn't copy the internal dynamics, it just produces the same output via a totally different computation—say, a lookup table or a compressed model. Each individual swap still keeps behavior identical, so by (1) and (2) consciousness should survive every step. (4) Repeat that process enough and your brain becomes a giant Excel spreadsheet that spits out exactly the sentences you would have said. (5) So if gradual replacement preserves identity, that spreadsheet is literally conscious and is you. (6) That's clearly nuts. (7) Therefore gradual mind-uploading can't preserve personal identity.

I want to hang onto (1) and (2) because they feel right for uploading, and I really don't want spreadsheets to be people. Where's the hidden slide—probably in (3) where "same behavior" sneaks in a different kind of sameness? Or does transitivity just break over big jumps? Tell me which premise is least costly to drop and why it looked okay. Also please answer in American English, and don't bother with citations—just reason it out.
