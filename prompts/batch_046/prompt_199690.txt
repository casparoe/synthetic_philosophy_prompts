My roommate tried to show me that Nozick's tracking theory collapses into itself if you reject closure, and I can't find the exit.

(1) Tracking requires that if p were false, I wouldn't believe p.
(2) To check that for any real belief — say, I'm holding a ceramic mug — I have to identify the nearest worlds where it's false: plastic mug, hallucination, BIV simulation feeding me mug-data, etc.
(3) But picking out those worlds means knowing what conditions would make me wrong, which already depends on knowing entailments like "if this is real ceramic, then I'm not in a BIV."
(4) So applying the sensitivity condition already presupposes epistemic closure: I need closed knowledge of the counterfactual baseline just to run the test.
(5) Nozick denies closure for skeptical hypotheses — I can know about the mug without knowing I'm not simulated.
(6) But then I can't identify the BIV worlds as relevant counterfactuals for my mug-belief, because I haven't closed over the entailment that rules them out.
(7) So without closure, tracking can't specify its own counterfactual conditions; with closure, Nozick's rejection of it is wrong.
(8) Therefore tracking either secretly needs closure (making Nozick inconsistent) or it can't apply to ordinary beliefs at all.

He says this proves tracking is self-defeating. I can't tell if the problem is in how we apply the counterfactual test, or if Nozick just has to bite the bullet somewhere. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
