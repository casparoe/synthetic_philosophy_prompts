hey im grading exams and need a second opinion. question: is nudging compatable with Mill's harm principal, and does libertarian paternalism avoid being hard paternalism? discuss with referance to opt-out organ donation and the green-energy default where opting out requires an in-person appointment.

student answer:
Mill's harm principle allows state interference only to prevent harm to others, not for one's own good, so hard paternalism violates it. Nudging keeps freedom intact by just changing the default, so it doesn't coerce. Thaler and Sunstein call this libertarian paternalism: paternalistic in goal but libertarian in method since people can opt out. So it's compatable with Mill since there's no force, just influence. In opt-out organ donation people still choose, so it's a nudge. The green-energy case also works because you can still switch, even if the opt-out is annoying. Critics like Conly say nudges manipulate autonomy behind the scenes, but that fails because the architecture is transparent, so it's really soft paternalism Mill would allow. Also, since the appointment barrier isn't a legal ban, it's not coercion, just friction.
