I'm prepping notes for my grad seminar on Dennett's real patterns idea and got stuck in an argument with a collegue over coffee. I want to make sure I'm not just being stubborn.

Me: The physical stance is ontologcaly complete, so design and intentional stances are just heurisitic shortcuts — good for prediction, but they don't pick out anything genuinely real in the world.

Her: No, the patterns are real in exactly the sense that centers of gravity are real. They're not fictional projections.

Me: Okay, but if they're real, either they reduce to physical patterns or they don't. If they reduce, physical stance covers them; if not, you're sneaking in some spooky emergent ontology that Dennett never wanted.

Her: That's a false dichotomy. Real patterns can have predictive autonomy without being reducible or spooky.

Me: Then take bacterial chemotaxis. You can predict nutrient-seeking behavior with design-stance talk far better than with quantum chemistry. That shows the design stance is indispensable.

Her: That's just computational tractability, not ontology. You're confusing what's useful with what's real.

Me: But in predicitive processing, the brain runs something like a design stance on its own dynamics — not an intentional stance. So maybe Dennett's taxonomy misses an intermediate layer that's actually doing the work.

Her: You're importing neuroscience that doesn't solve the metaphysics; it just relocates the problem.

Can you go through the exchange point by point and say who's right at each step, not just who wins overall? And are we both missing something about whether predicitive processing reveals a gap in Dennett's taxonomy — maybe a fourth stance, or just a deeper version of design? I feel like the argument touches on something neither of us named, especially when we think about artificial systems that seem to sit between design and intentional.
