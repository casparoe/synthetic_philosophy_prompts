Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on the difficulty of distinguishing moral preferences from cognitive biases, particularly in normative judgments about distributive justice and interspecies welfare. For my feature, I need a document both sides could sign.

Camp A, the Reflective Calibration camp, holds that moral preferences can be methodologically isolated from biases through structured reflective equilibrium combined with affective debiasing protocols, and that without this distinction normative ethics collapses into descriptive sociology.

Camp B, the Constitutive Entanglement camp, holds that biases are not distortions of an underlying pure preference but are structurally constitutive of value-formation itself; consequently, any procedure claiming to strip away bias is actually substituting one normative framework for another under the guise of neutrality.

The statement should follow this structure.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims—specifically: (i) the empirical findings on framing effects and cultural variation in fairness intuitions; (ii) the documented instability of first-order preferences under conditions of temporal discounting and epistemic scarcity; (iii) the limits of existing debiasing methods; (iv) what would count as evidence that a preference is genuinely pure rather than conditionally constructed; and (v) the practical domains where maintaining the distinction remains instrumentally valuable even if it is metaphysically imprecise.

Second, a disagreements table. Each disagreement must be phrased in wording its own camp accepts. I want at least three cruxes: whether moral judgment can ever achieve a fully perspective-independent standpoint; whether intersubjective convergence after debiasing tracks objective normative structure or merely reflects shared conditioning; and whether second-order preferences about one's first-order preferences can ever be free from the same entanglement. For each crux, provide a concrete case or experimental design both sides agree bears on it—such as a cross-cultural study of resource-allocation intuitions after controlled exposure to scarcity narratives—and record each camp's advance prediction about the outcome.

Third, a brief section identifying any part of the dispute that is verbal rather than substantive—specifically, whether bias is operating in a statistical-descriptive sense versus a normative-prescriptive sense, or whether preference is being used to denote revealed choice versus endorsed valuation.

Write in formal, polished prose appropriate for a long-form science and ideas publication. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for external verification; the prompt must stand on conceptual precision alone.
