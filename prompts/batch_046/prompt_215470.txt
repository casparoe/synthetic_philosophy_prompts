I've become conviced that the Anthropocene shouldn't be pinned to Watt's engine in 1784 or even the Trinity test, but to the plastic sediment horizon around 1950, and that the ones who ought to decide are not just ICS geologists but some kind of intergenerational council with proxy voices for future generations. What'd actually count against this view? I want more than the standard "stratigraphy needs a clear golden spike" objection. Which specific findings in core samples, which institutional realities about who controls geological naming, and which conceptual problems with letting unborn people vote on epoch boundaries, would be decisive? And which ones are just awkward? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely annoying, and say which ones a committed defender of this intergenerational-council idea would themselves admit hurt the case, versus which they'd dismiss as missing the point.
