I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind running a single 50-minute graduate section for an interdisciplinary seminar—mostly cognitive-science students who have taken one semester of philosophy of language but no metaethics. I need a teaching plan, not the explanation itself, for introducing the Frege-Geach problem specifically as it targets expressivism about moral discourse. Rather than treating it as a routine logic exercise, I want to present it as a structural crisis for compositionality in non-descriptive attitudes. The plan should cover: (1) the dependency order for introducing the components, with explicit justification for why Frege's compositionality principle must be formulated in a version that permits non-cognitive modes of combination before the embedding case is shown, and how this ordering makes the failure feel inevitable rather than contrived; (2) the single carrying example—ideally embedding a moral-expressive attitude inside a counterfactual conditional carrying an epistemic hedge (e.g., a case structured around "If lying is not wrong, then...")—specifying exactly what features it must possess and what escape routes it must block (particularly descriptivist reinterpretation and the separation of force from content); (3) the misconceptions the audience will form, especially the conflation of the problem with a mere failure of logical consistency rather than with a breakdown in semantic compositionality, together with the precise point in the sequence where each misconception should be intercepted; (4) one diagnostic check—perhaps a novel embedded case they must analyze—to distinguish genuine grasp of the structural tension from mere recall that expressivists "have trouble with conditionals"; and (5) what I should explicitly set aside at this level (for instance, canonical quasi-realist and norm-expressivist salvage strategies) and how to frame that omission so it reads as a boundary rather than an evasion. The students command basic propositional logic but are unfamiliar with moral semantics, and I want to avoid any request for further readings or bibliographic sources.
