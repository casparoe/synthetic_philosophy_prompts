Hey, I'm putting together my views for a philosophy of mind reading group and want someone to poke holes before I present. Here's where I stand on Avicenna's flying man and related stuff:

(a) I take the suspended-man thought experment to show that self-awareness is non-inferential and doesn't need sensory input or bodily awareness — you still know you're there even if you never had a body.

(b) But I also think in actual human life self-awareness is always kinda mediated by proprioceptive background noise; there's no "pure" floating consciousness in practice.

(c) I read the flying man not as describing a real metaphysical possibility, but as a conceptual isolation trick — like stripping away accidents to see the essence.

(d) Still, I think it succeeds in proving the soul is a substance, not an accident of the body, because the self is present even when every bodily feature is suspended.

(e) I'm also somewhat sympathetic to embodied cognition about perception and imagination, but I think the rational soul can think just fine once it's separated — the body is an instrument, not a substrate.

(f) And I hold that if the soul is genuinely independant for its essential being, then strong embodied-mind claims about all cognition have to be wrong, or at least limited to non-rational powers.

Can you go through these and tell me which pairs actually pull against each other? I don't want a lecture — just an audit: rank the tensions by severity, say what the cheapest fix is for each, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess. Also, is there an apparent tension that dissolves once you look closer?
