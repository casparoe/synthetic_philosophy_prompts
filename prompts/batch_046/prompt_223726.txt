I'm trying to work out my gut feelins about reduction and emergence in science, going by my verdicts on concrete cases.

Case 1: Temperature being mean kinetic energy. Verdict: clean reduction, nothing left over.

Case 2: Superconductivity explained by BCS theory — Cooper pairs and broken symmetry. The macro phenomenon (zero resistance) derives from quantum mechanics, yet the collective state has properties none of the parts have. Verdict: reduced in principle, but the broken-symmetry description is indispensible, so there's a weak emergence.

Case 3: Consciousness, specifically the subjective feel of seeing red. No neural map seems to close the explanatory gap. Verdict: strong emergence; not reducible even in principle.

Case 4: A starling murmuration — local interaction rules producing the swarm shape. Verdict: fully reducible; it's just aggregate causation, no downward causation.

Case 5: Critical phenomena near phase transitions. Magnets, fluids and superconductors all share the same scaling laws, explained by the renormalisation group showing micro-details get washed out. Verdict: this is genuine emergence — the macro-law has explanatory autonomy because the micro-physics is literally irrelevant.

Case 6: The heart's function of pumping blood. The mechanism is reducible to biochemistry and physics, but 'function' is a teleological concept that doesn't appear in physics. Verdict: mechanism reduced, function emergent.

The problem: case 2 and case 5 both involve macro-descriptions that are indispensible and micro-details that are present but don't determine the outcome uniquely. In case 2 I call it weak emergence with reduction succeeding; in case 5 I call it genuine emergence. That feels inconsistent — what's the real difference?

Also, I keep going back and forth on downward causation: if a flock changes local wind patterns that then affect the birds, is that just complex feedback, or is the swarm-level pattern genuinely causing changes at the particle level?

So: is there a single principle that captures cases 1–6, or do I need to split the concept of emergence? Which of my verdicts has to go?
