I'm working through my commitments for a metaethics seminar and want an internal consistency audit before I present. I hold that (1) moral realism is correct in a robust sense: there are mind-independent moral facts, and at least some of our judgments genuinely track them rather than merely expressing culturally variable preferences. I also accept (2) the empirical picture from cross-cultural moral psychology—particularly the WEIRD/non-WEIRD divergence in how populations weight harm, fairness, loyalty, authority, and purity, and the related finding that pre-theoretical moral intuitions cluster around Shweder-style ethics of autonomy, community, and divinity in systematically different ways across socio-ecological contexts. From this I draw (3): the best explanation for that divergence is that moral cognition functions largely as an adaptive cooperative toolkit shaped by local ecological demands, not as a dedicated faculty for detecting mind-independent moral properties. At the same time I maintain (4) that reflective equilibrium is still the right epistemic method for ethics, but it must be applied globally rather than parochially—seeking coherence across the full range of human moral data, not just within one's own cultural starting point. I reject evolutionary debunking in (5): the causal origin of a belief in group loyalty or purity norms does not by itself defeat its justification, so knowing that these norms evolved for coordination does not show they are unjustified. Finally, I hold (6) that despite deep and persistent cross-cultural disagreement, moral knowledge is achievable in principle, and there are objectively correct answers to at least some core questions—for instance, that torturing an innocent person for amusement is wrong regardless of cultural consensus.

Please audit these six commitments for joint tenability. Which pairs genuinely pull against each other, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled with modest adjustments, and how severe is each tension? Rank them by severity rather than treating them equally. For each real tension, specify what has to give—whether weakening the adaptive-toolkit claim in (3), restricting reflective equilibrium in (4), qualifying realism in (1), or revising the anti-debunking stance in (5)—and identify the cheapest available fix. Be explicit about whether (3) undermines (1) in a way that (5) cannot block, and whether (4) can actually deliver the knowledge claimed in (6) given the divergence documented in (2). Then tell me which single revision to my package would resolve the most tension at the lowest overall cost, and whether that revision forces me to abandon global reflective equilibrium or merely to reconceive how it operates across culturally divergent datasets. I am especially unsure whether my defense of global equilibrium is doing hidden work, or whether it is just a wishful bridge between the adaptive explanation and my realist ambitions.
