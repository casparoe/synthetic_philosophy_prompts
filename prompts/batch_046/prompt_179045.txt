Write an essay on whether moral particularism can accommodate structural moral regret—the sense that one has acted wrongly not because a principle was violated, but because the situational configuration itself made every available option harmful—without collapsing into a tacit generalism about harm. Weigh the particularist claim that moral reasoning is irreducibly context-sensitive against the objection that treating structural regret as rationally binding implicitly commits us to a general norm governing complex situations. Consider whether a particularist can consistently hold that recurring patterns of regret are merely heuristic guides rather than principles, and whether that distinction remains stable under pressure. Reach and defend a verdict: can particularism take structural moral regret seriously without conceding that at least some moral principles are unavoidable?
