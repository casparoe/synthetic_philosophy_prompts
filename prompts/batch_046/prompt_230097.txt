Write a villanelle in American English about Russell's theory of descriptions. Structure the two mandatory refrains so that one carries the existence condition and the other carries the uniqueness condition of Russell's analysis. Use the body of the poem to treat the familiar case of a present King of France being bald, then pivot—where the refrains intersect in the middle stanzas—to the embedded case inside a counterfactual: if a present King of France existed, he might be bald. Ask whether Russell's logical form demands a primary occurrence that denies existence outright in every world, or permits a secondary occurrence that lets the conditional survive. In the closing quatrain, state precisely which occurrence applies and what it means for the truth value of the whole claim. Keep the logic accurate enough that a reader of Russell would recognize the primary-versus-secondary distinction, but do not name specific texts, authors, or page numbers. Make the poem formally polished, with the rhyme scheme and refrain rotation echoing the rearrangement of surface grammar into quantified logical form, suitable for reading aloud at the end of a philosophy seminar.
