Please explain the following conceptual issue in Molinism. Standard analytic presentations hold that God has middle knowledge of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom—for instance, that if Peter were placed in circumstance C, he would freely deny Christ. This knowledge is supposed to be logically prior to any creative decree, giving God a menu of feasible worlds without God causing the free choices within them. Now consider the tension with a robust libertarian view of freedom, on which an agent who freely does A in circumstances C must genuinely have been able to do otherwise in those very same circumstances, with the same causal history and internal states up to the moment of choice. If that is right, then for any true counterfactual “Peter denies in C,” there must be a possible world with identical pre-choice conditions in which Peter affirms. But then it seems the truth of that counterfactual depends on which world is actualized, rather than being a fixed, pre-decretal fact God could consult when deciding which world to create. Some Molinists respond by treating these counterfactuals as “soft facts”—truths partly grounded in future contingent events—but that response seems to conflict with the claim that they are available to God prior to the decree for world-selection.

Your task is to explain, with conceptual precision, whether there is a coherent way for the Molinist to maintain both (a) that counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are pre-decretal and available for divine planning, and (b) that libertarian freedom requires same-circumstance alternative possibilities. In doing so, carefully distinguish the epistemic question—how God knows these counterfactuals—from the metaphysical question—what grounds their truth. Focus on the architecture of the view rather than on historical exegesis, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
