A colleague in science education frames Galileo’s hermeneutics with an analogy she finds clarifying: reading Scripture is like interpreting a legal contract that contains both binding operative clauses and non-binding preamble language adapted to the contracting parties’ limited vocabulary. On her account, the cosmological passages in Genesis are boilerplate preamble, accommodated to ancient audiences and carrying no scientific authority, while the soteriological core remains binding; from this she concludes that modern physics and biblical religion are non-competing because they address different contractual domains. I am not convinced the contract model captures Galileo’s own distinction between language that accommodates human weakness and truths that have been demonstrated by natural reason, particularly because Galileo assigned the Church, not the individual reader, the authority to determine when an accommodated passage must yield to a demonstrated conclusion about nature.
