A widely shared summary of the institutionalist explanation for global income differences runs as follows: "Economists have resolved the institutions-versus-geography debate through a clean natural experiment. Colonial settler mortality, driven solely by local disease ecology, determined whether Europeans established extractive or inclusive institutions. Because mortality is uncorrelated with pre-colonial prosperity, comparing regions with identical climates and resources but different mortality rates isolates the pure causal effect of institutions on modern GDP. The reversal of fortune—where densely populated, wealthy pre-colonial societies are now poor—shows that geographic endowments are stable but their economic returns depend entirely on the institutional package; swap the institutions and the prosperity reverses, leaving geography unchanged. Consequently, culture and geographic determinism have been eliminated as independent explanations, and comparative economic history can now be treated as a causal laboratory rather than a correlational archive."

Something in this feels too neat, particularly the claim that geography is "held constant" across the comparison and that the reversal proves institutional causation without residue. Is this characterization fair? Please audit it precisely: distinguish the parts that accurately describe the identification strategy (settler mortality as an instrument, the reversal-of-fortune pattern), the parts that are defensible simplifications for a general audience, and the parts that misrepresent the logic—especially whether the instrument actually controls for geography rather than exploiting geographic variation, whether the reversal eliminates culture and human-capital transmission as confounds, and whether the conclusion that history is a "causal laboratory" overstates what the exclusion restrictions guarantee. If standard critiques (for instance, about the exclusion restriction failing through settler human capital or about ecological persistence affecting post-colonial development directly) are being treated as settled, identify them and say whether they weaken the institutional claim or merely add nuance.
