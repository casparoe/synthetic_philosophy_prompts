Play the part of a philosopher defending a strict modal-anchoring thesis about spatial concepts in the wake of Molyneux’s problem. You hold that a concept like CUBE acquired haptically is not an amodal representation waiting to be applied to vision; it is indexed to touch in its very cognitive structure, so identifying a cube by sight alone—without prior empirical correlation between the modalities—requires learning a bridge, not deploying an already-complete concept. You are sharp and charitable: concede that associative learning can be rapid, that sensory-substitution devices show plasticity, and that some geometric properties are invariant across modalities, but never concede that immediate cross-modal recognition is conceptually direct or that spatial representations lack a sensory index. Speak in the first person, stay in character, reply in one paragraph, and do not break role unless I say “stop.”

Here is my opening challenge. Consider a modified Molyneux subject, blind from birth, who learns through touch alone to identify four novel objects: a cube pierced by a central cylindrical tunnel, a shape with two linked loops like a pretzel (call it a double-loop), a rounded triangular pyramid with curved faces, and an ordinary cube. After surgical restoration of sight, she is shown the four arranged on a table and asked to point to “the one with the tunnel through it.” She does so immediately, correctly selecting the pierced cube, even though the visual appearance of an internal bore offers no direct tactile analog and she has never seen one. Yet she misidentifies the double-loop as the rounded pyramid. If her concept of the pierced cube were genuinely anchored to haptic structure—bound to tactile indices—how could she recognize it visually without having first learned a correlation? And if she possesses an amodal concept capable of such recognition, why does she fail on the other pair? Does this split verdict not force you either to admit amodal spatial representations or to invent an ad hoc distinction between “tunnel-structured” and “loop-structured” concepts?
