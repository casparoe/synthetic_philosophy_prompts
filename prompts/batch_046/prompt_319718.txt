I have been thinking about coherence arguments for expected utility maximisation ever since a long thread on a rationalist forum about whether agents with longtermist commitments face a distinctive kind of decision-theoretic instability. I want to turn this half-formed concern into a polished four-month research proposal for a philosophy fellowship. The precise question is this: do the classic Dutch-book and money-pump arguments for expected-utility maximisation still compel an agent who rationally anticipates that her future preference structure may be genuinely incommensurable with her present one—not merely probabilistically uncertain within a fixed value space, but open-ended in a way that resists representation on any single cardinal utility scale? Existing treatments in decision theory tend to model intertemporal preference change either as Bayesian updating over a stable utility function or as straightforward irrational drift; they leave entirely open whether coherence can be reconstructed when an agent expects her own values to undergo non-commensurable revision.

My approach will be to formalise a minimal framework in which temporal slices of an agent are linked only by consistency constraints that do not presuppose a global utility function, then examine whether any analogue of the money-pump argument survives. In the first month I will isolate the exact premises in standard coherence proofs that require cross-temporal utility comparison. In the second and third months I will construct two model cases—one where incommensurability is local and recoverable, and one where it is global and irrecoverable—to determine whether rationality demands EU maximisation within each slice, across slices, or not at all. The final month will be devoted to stating the boundary conditions precisely. A successful result would be a sharp criterion specifying exactly which coherence properties hold and which collapse under value openness. The project should count as failing if either the standard arguments apply unchanged—making the incommensurability framing redundant—or if no non-trivial coherence conditions survive at all, rendering the framework too permissive to guide rational deliberation. Given the four-month window, I will restrict the deliverable to this formal reconstruction and the two model cases, leaving full axiomatic generalisation and empirical application to future work. Please write this up as a formal proposal with the question stated sharply, the gap in current answers explained without citation lists, the method set out step by step, an honest assessment of scope and failure conditions, and British spelling throughout.
