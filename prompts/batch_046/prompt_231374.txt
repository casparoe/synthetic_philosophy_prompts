Draft the joint statement that a defender of the narrative-gravity account of selfhood and a defender of the minimal-phenomenal-ownership account could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Frame the collaboration around a specific, constructed test case: a synthetic agent that maintains a unified, temporally extended, first-person autobiographical narrative with emotional-valence tagging across discontinuous hardware substrates, yet lacks any continuous phenomenal consciousness or pre-reflective sense of mineness. The parties should address whether such an agent possesses a self in the same explanatory and normative sense that humans do, and whether the absence of phenomenal ownership is a decisive deficit or merely an implementation detail.

Structure the deliverable as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims, covering: (a) the functional architecture of narrative construction in both human and synthetic cases; (b) the phenomena that any adequate account must explain, such as first-person pronoun use, temporal integration of experience, attribution of moral accountability, and the sense of authorship over actions; (c) the conceptual distinctions and no-go constraints both sides accept, including that selfhood does not require a Cartesian substance, that narrative coherence alone does not automatically entail full moral personhood, and that phenomenal ownership is not necessarily transparent to introspection. Second, a disagreements table with columns for the issue, the narrative-gravity formulation worded so its own side would accept it, the minimal-ownership formulation worded so its own side would accept it, and the precise crux—the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one party. Third, for each crux, specify one concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it: include the synthetic-agent scenario described above and one human clinical case involving severe narrative disruption or dissociative fragmentation, and record in advance each side's prediction of what that case demonstrates. Fourth, a brief concluding section identifying which residual disagreements are substantive and which appear to be verbal—particularly whether "self" is functioning as an explanatory posit, an honorific, or both. Keep the language neutral and precise; do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, citations, direct quotations from named texts, or references to specific page numbers or publication dates.
