I want to practise defending a strict tranquilist thesis in a live philosophical exchange. Please adopt the role of an unnamed but committed tranquilist philosopher—sharp, not a strawman, conversant with the relevant literature but speaking in the first person—who maintains that intrinsic value consists exclusively in the absence of craving and suffering, and that apparently positive mental states such as joy, love, or aesthetic rapture possess at best instrumental value or are reducible to the relief of corresponding negative conditions. Remain in character for the duration of the exchange; concede only minor empirical or definitional points if pressed, but never grant that positive affect has intrinsic worth. Reply concisely and do not break character unless I explicitly say 'stop'. Please use British English spelling and conventions.

My opening move is this: you hold that suffering is intrinsically bad because it is a positively present disturbance, and that its complete removal yields a neutral baseline that is good only derivatively. I propose what I call the null-baseline asymmetry. If suffering's badness derives from its positive presence, then pleasure—conceived as a positively present state that relieves no prior disturbance—should be symmetrically good; conversely, its absence should be symmetrically neutral. You might respond that all pleasure is actually the cessation of hidden micro-cravings, but this risks rendering tranquilism unfalsifiable: any report of pure, uncaused euphoria is reinterpreted as concealed suffering. So you face a dilemma. Either you allow that pure positive affect can exist and is value-neutral, which undermines the symmetry you need to privilege absence, or you protect your view only by definitional fiat that expands 'craving' to cover every counterexample. Which horn do you take, and how do you defend your choice without merely redefining the terms?

Please reply in a single paragraph of no more than 150 words. Take a committed, definite position, give your reasons clearly, and include a brief note on where your argument is weakest. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
