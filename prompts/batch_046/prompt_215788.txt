For an essay I am preparing for a science-and-ideas supplement on the philosophy of sleep, I need a diagnostic taxonomy—part decision tree, part sorting quiz—that maps the logical space of positions on whether dreams are genuinely experiential. The framing should centre on the Malcolm–Dennett dispute, but the branches must be built from concrete commitments rather than named schools, so that a reader can locate their own view without already knowing the labels.

Begin with a root question that separates sceptics from defenders of experience: if someone reports, upon waking, that they saw a crimson sphere floating in a grey room, is that report susceptible to the same kind of error—misperception, hallucination, false memory—as a comparable report made in waking life? From there, split through four further distinctions that actually divide the positions.

First, must phenomenal character be accompanied by concurrent access-consciousness, or can it exist in a phenomenally present but access-blocked state? Second, is the narrative coherence of a dream assembled retroactively upon awakening—as a reconstructed memory or story—or is it already structured during the sleep episode itself? Third, does the suppression of sensory precision-weighting during REM—treating dream imagery as high-level prediction with minimal bottom-up correction—place dreams in a separate ontological category: neither full-blown experience nor pure confabulation? Fourth, does the absence of behavioural criteria during sleep undermine any ascription of experience, regardless of what the subject reports afterwards?

The leaves must include: Malcolm’s sceptical denial that sleep offers adequate criteria for experience; Dennett’s anti-experientialist claim that the dream is a narrative or memory constructed on waking; the orthodox received view that dreams are degraded but real perceptual-like experiences; and at least one unnamed hybrid—specifically, the view that dreams possess phenomenal content but lack epistemic entitlement because global precision-weighting is suspended. Where the tree produces logical combinations that no philosopher occupies—for example, phenomenal character exists without access, is not retroactively constructed, yet is denied epistemic entitlement for non-predictive reasons—flag the gap and briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Please do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading; the instrument must be usable by someone without online access. Write in formal British English, keep every node phrased as a concrete claim a reader could agree or disagree with, and conclude by noting any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate.
