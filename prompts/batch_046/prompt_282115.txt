For a workshop on rationality and decision, I'm drafting my position and want an internal consistency audit before I present. Here are my commitments, in my own terms:

(1) I accept the synchronic Dutch-book argument for probabilism: if one's present credences violate the probability axioms, one is vulnerable to a guaranteed-loss betting arrangement.

(2) I reject diachronic Dutch-book arguments as rationality constraints. That a sequence of updates could have left me open to a sure loss over time does not, by itself, make my current refusal to update irrational.

(3) I endorse causal decision theory and reject evidential decision theory, on the grounds that rational choice should depend only on what I can causally influence, not on what my action evidences about states of the world I cannot affect.

(4) In Newcomb's problem, I one-box.

(5) In the counterfactual mugging scenario, once I learn that the coin came up heads, I should refuse to pay; the causal link between my payment and the reward has already been broken by the actual outcome.

(6) I hold that a correct decision theory must be invariant under what I call epistemic reframing: if the same physical situation is redescribed using different conceptual frames—say, describing the predictor's algorithmic assignment rather than the bare coin result—the recommendation should not change.

Please audit this package. I want to know: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear tense but dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension, how severe it is and what the least costly revision would be. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal. Be specific about which commitment pulls against which, and do not lecture me on decision theory in general—just trace what these particular commitments commit me to. Write your response in American English.
