Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the nature of corruption should have produced. Camp One—the Institutional Universalists—holds that corruption is essentially the subversion of impersonal, rule-governed public authority for private gain, and that Western-derived bureaucratic standards of neutrality and transparency provide the correct universal benchmark for identifying it. Camp Two—the Relational Contextualists—holds that corruption is essentially the betrayal of locally legitimate, thick obligations of reciprocity and care that constitute authority in a given social order, and that applying Western, atomistic standards to practices such as customary gift-giving or clientelism imposes an ethnocentric framework that misdescribes the moral logic of those practices.

For this exercise, both camps have agreed to adopt a provisional analytical distinction—between “thin-procedural corruption” (the diversion of institutional authority within an impersonal framework) and “thick-relational corruption” (the betrayal of embedded covenantal duties that make authority legitimate)—without yet agreeing on whether both categories are genuinely distinct, whether one reduces to the other, or whether one is the primary concept.

Your deliverable should be structured as follows.

First, a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, including: shared structural descriptions of clientelism and gift-giving; agreement on empirical patterns (for example, that patronage networks can stabilize governance in some contexts and destabilize it in others); logical points they both accept (for instance, that defining corruption purely by local approval risks circularity, and that defining it purely by formal deviation risks missing normative betrayals that formal rules do not capture); and explicit acknowledgment of the limits of both universalist and contextualist claims.

Second, a disagreements table. For each remaining dispute, state the issue in neutral wording, then present the formulation each camp accepts for its own position—so that the Universalist entry reads in terms they endorse, and the Contextualist entry reads in terms they endorse. The table should cover at least: whether bureaucratic neutrality is a culturally specific achievement or a universal moral requirement; whether gift-giving in administrative contexts is necessarily a subversion of public duty; and whether the provisional distinction between thin-procedural and thick-relational corruption captures a real division or is a false dichotomy.

Third, identify the cruxes—the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp closer to the other. For each crux, specify: a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree is relevant; and the prediction each camp makes about that case under the crux. One crux must address the practice of “pre-decisional hospitality”—where officials in customary tribunals receive hospitality from disputants before rendering judgments in kin-based disputes, a practice locally understood as a prerequisite for being heard rather than as payment for a favorable outcome. Another crux should address whether there exists any cross-contextual convergence on judgments about particular acts (for instance, whether the secret diversion of disaster-relief funds by an official is condemned across societies, and what that convergence proves or fails to prove).

Fourth, conclude with a brief jointly acknowledged open question—endorsed in substance by neither camp—about whether part of their dispute is verbal: specifically, whether they disagree about the extension of the term “corruption” itself, or about the normative evaluation of the same acts described in different vocabularies.

Write this as a polished, formal document suitable for publication in a collaborative report. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific authors or texts. Do not invent specific empirical studies with fabricated data; if you reference evidence, frame it as the kind of evidence both sides agree would be relevant, with predictions stated clearly.
