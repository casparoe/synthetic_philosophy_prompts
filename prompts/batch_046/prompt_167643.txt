I've adopted a hybrid position in my philosophy of language course: Russell's quantificational analysis holds for attributive definite descriptions, but for referential uses I accept Donnellan's distinction, and for descriptions that fail in fiction I treat them as domain-restricted to an empty or fictional set rather than as false existential claims. This avoids Strawson's presupposition-failure problem without committing to Meinongian objects. What would count against it? Please rank the defeaters by severity: formal issues with anaphoric linkage across fictional and actual domains, modal-scope ambiguities that blur the attributive-referential split, or intuitions about misdescription that defenders of pure Russellianism or pure Strawsonianism would themselves concede. I also want an honest assessment of whether allowing unrestricted domain restriction makes the view unfalsifiable, and whether that insulation is a vice for a theory of this kind.
