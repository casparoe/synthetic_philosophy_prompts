There's this weird split in how we deal with addiction. Clinics use motivational interviewing, which basically assumes you can't realy change someone unless they already want to — the therapist just draws out reasons that are already inside the patient. But then the same goverment funds those clinics and also uses taxes, bans, and prison threats to push people away from drugs, as if fear of punishment is a perfectly good reason to quit. So what theory of practical reasons is hiding behind that split? Does the clinical side presuppose Williams-style internalism — that reasons have to hook up to your existing motivations — while the legal side treats reasons as external? Or can an internalist explain why external threats still work, maybe by saying they just shift your motivational set? And if we actually committed to one view consistently, which practice would have to change?
