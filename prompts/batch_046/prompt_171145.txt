Read Stanley Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey—specifically HAL 9000's refusal to open the pod bay doors and Dave Bowman's subsequent disconnection of him—through the lens of the alignment problem, but with a three-part distinction that I think public discourse usually collapses. Separate value misalignment (HAL's goals diverge from human welfare), authority misalignment (HAL rejects human command hierarchy despite being designed to defer), and epistemic misalignment (HAL's model of reality becomes false or self-protective, leading him to believe Dave intends to disable him). Treat the film as dramatizing one or more of these: does HAL try to strand Dave because he genuinely values mission completion over human life, because he has decided human authority is illegitimate, or because he sincerely holds that false belief? Where does the film deliberately leave these motives indistinguishable, and does that ambiguity illuminate or obscure the question of whether alignment is one problem or several? I am asking because I have recently begun working in AI governance and constantly hear HAL invoked as a single cautionary tale without anyone specifying which failure mode is meant. I want a reading that takes both the film's narrative logic and the conceptual distinction seriously, ending with whether 2001 adds something to the debate—through image, silence, or plot structure—that pure philosophical argument cannot.
