Consider a persistent virtual world with the following hybrid ontology: every digital artifact is constituted simultaneously by (i) a specific configuration of server-state data and (ii) an evolving collective charter of player intentions that can retroactively revise identity conditions—so that a monument rebuilt after a full server migration may be declared "the same monument" despite having no shared underlying data with the original. Give a concrete, detailed example of a conceptual issue arising at the intersection of this dual-constitution model and the question of whether virtual harms are genuinely real. Your scenario should involve the destruction of one such artifact in a way that makes the harm's reality-status genuinely indeterminate: it appears to be a real loss if the intentional-constitution layer takes priority, yet merely simulated if the server-state layer does. Specify the case precisely enough to show why reducing the harm to either layer alone fails, and identify the exact conceptual knot—not an empirical disagreement about player psychology or server architecture—that leaves it unresolved whether the affected parties have suffered something real.
