Construct a formal taxonomy—structured as a branching decision tree—of philosophical positions on metaphysical grounding and fundamentality, intended for use in a graduate seminar on metaphysics. The taxonomy should be built from four sequential branch points that generate a logical space of up to sixteen combinations, with each combination either assigned to a named or clearly characterized position or marked as unoccupied and briefly diagnosed.

The first branch point asks whether there exists a most fundamental level of reality at all: answer patterns are (a) yes, there is a unique fundamental base; (b) no, reality descends infinitely without bottom; (c) no, reality is coherently structured with mutual dependence but no privileged base.

The second branch point, applicable only to path (a), asks whether that fundamental base is ontologically uniform (monism about the fundamental) or comprises irreducibly distinct kinds (pluralism).

The third branch point introduces a distinction—call it diagonal grounding—between vertical dependence (between hierarchical levels) and cross-categorial dependence mediated not by direct reduction but by structural correspondence (for instance, mental or normative properties standing in dependence relations to physical properties through organizational patterns rather than through identity or straightforward supervenience). The question is whether diagonal grounding is reducible to vertical grounding plus intra-level coherence, requires its own primitive relation, or is an illusion that collapses back into one of the other two.

The fourth branch point concerns negative existentials and absence facts: are they grounded in corresponding positive totality states of affairs, grounded directly in the fundamental base without mediation, or do they require a primitive absence-maker?

Every leaf must specify its answer pattern across all four branches, state the position's central claim in one sentence, and identify its most awkward theoretical commitment. For logical combinations that remain unoccupied, provide a brief diagnosis: is the combination incoherent, unstable under pressure, or merely unexplored? Where two positions share the same answer pattern but differ in motivation, flag them explicitly rather than merging them.

Phrase every branch node as a concrete claim that a student could accept or reject without knowing which position it yields—do not use philosopher names or school labels at the nodes. The audience should be able to locate themselves by answering the four questions, then read the matching leaf. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for further sources. Keep the tone formal and polished, and ensure the taxonomy does not depend on any obscure empirical fact or highly specialized technical result that would be inaccessible without external reference.
