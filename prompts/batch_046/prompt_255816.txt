I serve on the grant-allocation committee of a university interdisciplinary research institute. We must produce a collective preference ordering over three competing proposals for a single large award: a climate-adaptation engineering initiative, a digital-privacy governance project, and a comparative-cognition study. Our charter forbids the chair from deciding unilaterally and requires that every voter rank all three proposals without ties. One faction proposes a Borda-count aggregation with chair tie-breaking; a second faction advocates a sequential pairwise-elimination runoff that permits abstention at each round; a third faction suggests a hybrid in which voters assign cardinal intensity scores, which are then normalized and converted back into an ordinal ranking before aggregation. Several members cite Arrow's impossibility theorem to argue that any deterministic rule will violate at least one standard fairness condition. Draft a formal decision memo for the committee recommending which of these three mechanisms — the Borda system, the pairwise runoff with abstention, or the cardinal-normalization hybrid — should be adopted, or whether the charter itself should be amended. Your memo should explain, at an intuitive but technically precise level, how Arrow's result applies to each option, identify which of Arrow's conditions — unrestricted domain, Pareto efficiency, independence of irrelevant alternatives, or non-dictatorship — is violated or preserved in each case, and honestly address the strongest counter-argument to your recommendation — in particular, whether permitting abstention or injecting cardinal information genuinely escapes the impossibility result or merely displaces the failure onto a different condition.
