I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind and neuroethics, preparing material for a workshop on moral attribution to hybrid cognitive architectures. I need a precisely engineered thought experiment that isolates one underexplored variable: whether moral patienthood can be undermined by what I will call interstitial computation—cases in which a causally integrated computation is realized only across the interfaces between substrate-heterogeneous nodes, with no single biological or artificial system containing the complete functional loop.

Please construct a realistic, present-day-laboratory scenario—no teleportation, no perfect predictors, no speculative physics—involving exactly three coupled nodes: two human-derived cortical organoids in culture and one neuromorphic processor linked by a low-latency bidirectional interface. In this system, a specific recurrent computation—one that meets standard functional criteria for sentience, specifically recursive self-modeling coupled with affective predictive-error minimization—is distributed such that the full loop is only complete when all three nodes are synchronized. Any two nodes can maintain a degraded version that preserves lower-order predictive processing but lacks the recursive self-reference. The computation never resides wholly inside one organoid or inside the chip; it exists only in the interstitial dynamics of their coupling.

Then demonstrate that your case satisfies the following specifications:

1. State the precise attribution at issue (for example, that the tri-node system is a moral patient deserving of non-maleficence).

3. Specify the relevant nearby worlds—e.g., the identical loop instantiated entirely within one enlarged organoid; the loop split across three biological nodes with no silicon component; and the loop disrupted so that only isolated nodes remain active.

4. For the actual case and for at least one nearby world, show exactly why each theory delivers the verdict it does, referencing the specific structural feature (interstitial substrate-heterogeneity, homeostatic containment, or functional integration) that drives the judgment.

5. Give an honest assessment of which feature of the case does the philosophical work, explain what happens to both verdicts if that feature is removed, and identify the point at which the scenario collapses into a trivial agreement.

6. Choose the theory that comes out badly in your construction and defend it against your own case: present the specific objections its proponent would raise—not general skepticism, but targeted attacks on your design, such as claims about hidden internalization, about the chip functioning as mere peripheral equipment, or about the degraded two-node version revealing that the “full” loop was never genuinely distributed.

Finally, I want a committed response rather than a balanced survey. Take a definite position on whether interstitial, substrate-heterogeneous computation undermines moral attribution in this scenario; argue for that position with explicit reasons; and state which feature of the case you regard as genuinely decisive rather than merely illustrative. Write for an audience of philosophy graduate students who are conversant with basic philosophy of mind but not specialists in neuroethics.
