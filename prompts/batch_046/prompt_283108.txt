hey I'm drafting the final exam for my grad metaphysics of agency seminar and I'm sick of answers that just recite Strawson's Basic Argument like it's a grocery list. I need a take-home essay prompt that forces students to actually reconstruct the logic and then break it on something strange.

Write one exam question suitable for about 90 minutes of writing. The first part should ask students to lay out the Basic Argument precisely: true moral responsibility requires being causa sui, but since character is shaped by heredity and enviroment, and actions flow from character, the regress never bottoms out in something self-caused. The second part should inject a twist: imagine an artificial agent built with a recursve self-modification loop, where version V_n designs V_{n+1}'s core values, but V_0 was itself designed by V_{-1}, with no external originator. Ask whether this loop creates a genuinely self-caused agent, or whether it just distributes the same regress across iterations. They should have to say whether Strawson's argument proves too much by ruling out even loop-based agency, or whether the loop actually confirms the argument by showing that distributed causation still isn't self-causation.

Please include a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points adding up to 20 marks. For each point, specify the marks and add a one-line note on the classic mistake -- e.g., treating the loop as an empirical AI claim rather than a logical structure, or confusing "no external designer" with "self-caused." Don't ask for citations or a reading list; this should be doable by someone who understands the argument without hunting for a specific page. Keep it away from obscure recent papers; I want the difficulty to come from the reasoning, not from knowing niche literature.
