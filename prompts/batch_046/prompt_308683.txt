There's this debate about whether qualia are incoherent that started with Dennett's quining argument back in the day, and then critics like Chase and Sanborn said he was just talking past the defenders. Decades later it still feels like the same stalemate: eliminativists claim phenomenal properties are redundant theoretical posits, defenders say denying the feel of pain is self-defeating or conceptually confused. My hunch is it's not really about consciousness but about what counts as closing an explanatory gap — one side wants reduction, the other thinks reduction is definitionaly missing the point. But I also wonder if there's a hidden split about time: maybe the eliminativist is modeling a static functional architecture, while the defender is tracking a dynamic, first-person present-moment relation-to-self that isn't a property but a temporal mode. Is this impasse verbal, substantive, or are they answering different questions — one about cognitive architecture, one about phenomenology — under one label? Diagnose the disagreement using what each side actually says, and tell me what, if anything, could count as progress: a conceptual distinction, an empirical result about introspecion, or just accepting it's two fields talking past eachother?
