In our philosophy of biology seminar, a classmate kept invoking the slogan 'there are no skyhooks, only cranes' to dismiss evolutionary-psychological accounts of moral reasoning, claiming they either smuggle in a designer-like shortcut or fall into greedy reductionism by treating norms as mere adaptive heuristics. I have since heard the same phrase used to defend physicalism, to mock vitalism, and to suggest that any multi-level explanation is automatically suspicious. Could you disentangle the precise theses this slogan might express — distinguishing platitudes from substantive claims and false ones — and say which reading was actually being deployed in the seminar argument? In particular, does any defensible interpretation support the view that evolutionary explanations of normativity are cranes rather than disguised skyhooks, and where does greedy reductionism genuinely enter? Please separate what is broadly settled in philosophy of science from what remains genuinely contested, and avoid presenting a disputed reading as the consensus.
