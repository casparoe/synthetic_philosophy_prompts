Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps interpreting Douglas Hofstadter’s tripartite message framework—frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages—when applied to artificial symbol systems.

Camp A, the frame-constitutive theorists, holds that in any symbolic system, including formal representations processed by AI, the frame message does not merely guide interpretation but partially constitutes semantic content; without the correct framing, inner structures remain syntactically determinate yet semantically inert.

Camp B, the inner-structure essentialists, holds that meaning resides primarily in isomorphic correspondences within the inner message itself; frames and outer messages are epistemic scaffolds for human interpreters, but an entity that correctly processes internal structural relations grasps the meaning regardless of whether it possesses or applies the framing instructions.

Produce a document with: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims about the three-level distinction, about the formal possibility of misframing, and about conditions for correct interpretation; (2) a disagreements table stating the precise residual differences, each side’s wording phrased so that its own camp would accept it; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp—including whether an AI lacking meta-level framing can be said to understand an inner message, and whether outer messages are necessary for verification rather than merely heuristic; and (4) for each crux, one concrete hypothetical case or argument both camps agree bears on it, with each camp’s explicit prediction or verdict recorded in advance. If any portion of the dispute is partly verbal—about what “meaning” or “understanding” denotes—identify exactly which portion and explain why. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
