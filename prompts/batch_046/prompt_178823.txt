I'm a third-year on our philosophy of mind course and I also sit as the student rep on the uni's interdisciplinery research ethics and funding board. We've got this proposal for a public installation-slash-study called 'The Rewriting Room' that uses a modified colour-phi display — red dot, green dot, motion illusion in the middle — but with a cross-modal twist: there's a pitch-shifting tone playing at exactly the same interval, and participants are asked whether they 'remember' seeing the intermediate shade or only 'constructed' it afterwards. The researchers want to frame everything around the Orwellian vs Stalinesque revision debate — is consciousness rewriting its own past like an airbrushed photo, or is it falsifying the present display while the raw record stays hidden? — but they haven't said which interpretation they're backing, and they plan to run it without telling participants about the philosophical framing until after they've given all their temporal-binding reports.

Half the board thinks it's just standard perceptual psych with a fancy label, and that the deception is minimal since colour-phi is well-known. The other half (me included, tbh) thinks that by not disclosing the study is specifically testing illusionist vs realist accounts of phenomenal timing, they're manipulating first-person reports about the 'when' of experience without proper informed consent. There's also a worry that the stereo-sound binocular version they're proposing isn't supported by the pilot — the pilot only had n=12 with monocular presentation and no auditory cue, yet the grant asks for full binocular stereo with spatial audio to claim something about multimodal consciousness revision.

Can you draft a board memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions? It should actually engage with the Orwellian vs Stalinesque distinction and what it means for whether participants are reporting a genuinely experienced intermediate state or a retrospectively fabricated one, and it needs to treat the strongest argument from the losing side honestly. Please write it in British English, keep it readable for a student-led board, and don't worry about being overly formal — just make sure the reasoning is something the researchers could actually respond to. (Also, I keep writing 'recieve' instead of 'receive' so feel free to ignore my spelling.)
