I'm serving as a TA for a doctoral seminar in cooperative game theory, and I'm grading problem sets on bargaining solutions. The exam question given to students was:

"Consider a two-player bargaining problem with disagreement point d = (0,0) and feasible set S equal to the convex hull of (0,0), (4,0), (0,2), and (3,3). (a) Compute both the Nash bargaining solution and the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution, showing your work. (b) State the four axioms that distinguish the two solutions—Pareto efficiency, symmetry, independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA), and individual monotonicity—and indicate which solution satisfies each. (c) Demonstrate that removing (4,0) from S changes the Kalai-Smorodinsky outcome but leaves the Nash outcome unchanged, thereby illustrating KS's failure of IIA. In your answer, keep the distinction—stressed in this seminar—between invariance under scaling of the feasible set and invariance under independent affine transformations of the players' utility representations; do not treat these as interchangeable."

Below is one student's answer. Please grade it. Provide a letter grade (A through F) and specific, point-by-point comments that justify the grade. Focus on conceptual accuracy, correct computation, and logical coherence; do not correct spelling or grammar unless it affects meaning. The asker is grading another student's work and should not signal their own opinion of it.

Student answer:

"(a) The Nash solution maximizes the product of gains over d on the Pareto frontier. The frontier connects (4,0) to (3,3) to (0,2). Maximizing u1·u2 yields (3,3), so Nash selects (3,3).

For Kalai-Smorodinsky, we first identify the utopia point u, which is the point in S that is best for both players; here that is (3,3). Drawing the line from d = (0,0) through u gives u2 = u1. Its intersection with the Pareto frontier is (3,3) itself, so KS also selects (3,3).

(b) Both solutions satisfy Pareto efficiency and symmetry. Nash satisfies IIA and scale invariance. Kalai-Smorodinsky violates IIA but satisfies collective monotonicity: if the feasible sets of both players expand together, both receive higher payoffs. Individual monotonicity is just a weaker version of this that applies when only one player's options grow.

(c) Let S' be the convex hull of (0,0), (3,3), and (0,2), with (4,0) removed. The utopia point remains (3,3), so the line from d to u is unchanged, and KS still selects (3,3). For Nash, the new frontier runs from (3,3) to (0,2), and maximizing u1·u2 again gives (3,3). Thus both solutions stay at (3,3) after the removal, which shows that IIA is preserved in this case rather than violated."
