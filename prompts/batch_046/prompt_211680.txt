I was arguing with my roommate about whether consciousness is just a user illusion — like your brain serves up this slick interface so you don't have to deal with raw neurons. She said it's exactly like a computer desktop: you drag a file into the trash, but there's no trash can in the hardware, just magnetic states, and the icon is basically a lie that helps you get stuff done. From this she concluded that qualia — colors, pains, whatever — are just interface icons with no real neural correspondent, and anyone who thinks they're "real properties" is like someone who thinks there's literally a folder inside the laptop.

But then I read this weird comparison in a blog post (can't remember the author) that said consciousness isn't a GUI hiding the code, it's more like a compiled executable — the source code (neurons) gets translated into a totally different kind of object that runs on its own terms, not just hidden behind pictures. So now I'm wondering if the desktop analogy is even the right one, or if it's smuggling in the idea that there's always a "real" level underneath that we could peek at if we just had better tools. Can you map out the correspondence table — user, OS, icons, hardware, maybe the trash can — and then tell me where the desktop picture breaks, especially with this compiled-executable twist? Does the "qualia are just icons" conclusion survive once we stop treating consciousness as a hidden display and start treating it like translated code? And is their a hidden assumption in the analogy — like that interfaces are designed by someone — that makes it misleading when applied to evolution?
