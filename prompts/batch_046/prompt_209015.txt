Take teh Egyptian revolution and the three years after it (Mubarak out in 2011, Brotherhood wins in 2012, Sisi's coup in 2013, then back to military rule) and run it through Polybius's anacyclosis, Ibn Khaldun's asabiyyah cycle, and Spengler's culture-life cycle. I want the actual mapping, not a history recap.

For Polybius: map the constitutional stages onto 2011-2013 — monarchy under Mubarak, then the democratic interlude, then the slide. Does the 2013 coup count as a reset to monarchy, an aristocratic correction, or something the cycle doesn't have a box for? And if the cycle keeps running, where should Egypt go next?

For Khaldun: trace the decay of group solidarity in the old regime, then ask whether the Brotherhood ever built real fresh asabiyyah or just rode a temporary urban coalition. Does the speed of the turnaround — about three years — break Khaldun's usual dynasty-length predictions, or is Egypt just an accelerated case?

For Spengler: can his Winter/decline frame apply to a single state's rapid institutional collapse, or is the timescale totally wrong? Does Egypt in 2013 look like a culture entering Winter, or is that poetic overreach?

Then check Toynbee's challenge-and-response: does it explain the military's coherence and the Brotherhood's failure in a way the other two don't, or is it just a fancy relabeling?

Finally, be honest about where each lens is silent or wrong-footed. Polybius doesn't talk about foreign aid and Gulf money shaping institutions; Khaldun assumes a tribal basis for solidarity that urban Cairo politics might not have. Is this episode actually cyclical, or are we forcing a linear story of institutional failure into a circular mold? Don't paper over the gaps.
