Please explain the conceptual problem known as Bradley's regress and its specific bearing on the unity of the proposition. I want to understand whether the regress arises chiefly because we implicitly model relational combination on the pattern of object-property composition—treating a relation as if it were an additional entity that itself requires connecting to its terms—rather than on a pattern where relating is a primitive structural feature that does not enter the proposition as a further term. In your explanation, distinguish carefully between the claim that relations are external to their relata, in the sense that they leave the intrinsic nature of the terms unchanged, and the claim that they are internal, in the sense that the relata cannot be the particular entities they are without already standing in that relation. Then address this further issue: if one attempts to halt the regress by saying that the relation relates its terms necessarily, or by its very nature, does this genuinely solve the problem, or does it merely rename the binding power without explaining how one entity can be ontologically dependent on a multiplicity without generating a further multiplicity of dependencies? I am particularly interested in whether the regress is best understood as a problem about infinite ontological grounding, or rather as a problem about the coherence of treating logical syntax as if it were metaphysical structure, and what that distinction implies for whether relational properties can be primitive.
