I have recently been asked to help design an advanced undergraduate seminar module on authoritarian consolidation, and in preparing the syllabus I have realized that I cannot clearly map my own intuitions about counterfactual dependence in interwar Germany. I need a decision tree—phrased as concrete commitments a student could endorse without already knowing the historiographic labels—that classifies positions on whether a regime comparable to historical Nazi Germany would have emerged had Hitler never become chancellor, or had he died in 1923 rather than 1933. The tree should open with whether the Weimar structural crisis—reparations, hyperinflation, paramilitary violence, and conservative elite acquiescence—was sufficient to generate a radical right-wing authoritarian state regardless of who led the nationalist coalition. For respondents who deny that sufficiency, the next nodes should distinguish counterfactual removal in 1933 versus 1923 versus complete absence from politics, and then ask whether comparable regime requires specifically the Holocaust and the war of aggression, or merely a militarized racial state with revisionist foreign policy. Each leaf should carry either the name of an actual position or the label unnamed, together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment, such as the claim that genocide required Hitler’s particular obsessional drive, or conversely that any replacement chancellor drawn from the NSDAP senior ranks would have pursued identical policies because the party apparatus had reached what I am calling an institutional substitution threshold at which leadership was redundant for extremism but not for consolidation speed.

Please flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely cannot separate—for instance, structuralists who allow minor variation in atrocity scale versus those who deny all individual-level causal contribution—and note which combinations of answers leave a region of the logical space empty, such as a view holding that Hitler was necessary for the Holocaust yet that the party had redundant capacity for every other policy; for each empty region, diagnose whether it reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect in the literature. Finally, state explicitly what kind of consideration—perhaps archival evidence on conservative elites’ willingness to back a non-Hitler chancellor in 1933, or a formal causal model of authoritarian path dependence—would lead you to redraw the tree’s boundaries, and identify the specific reasoning step where your classification is most likely to fail.
