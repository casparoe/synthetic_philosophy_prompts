I am a student in an advanced philosophy seminar on practical reason, and I have been developing a position about incommensurable values that I want stress-tested. Here is the view I hold: when two options involve genuinely incommensurable values — say, a career in philosophy versus one in medicine — there is no hidden metric that could adjudicate them, yet rational choice is still possible through what I call 'authoritative self-constitution.' On this account, the agent resolves the hard choice not by weighing but by committing to one option in a way that partly reconstitutes her practical identity, and the commitment is rational (rather than arbitrary) only if it maintains 'directional continuity' — that is, alignment along at least one dimension with her prior practical commitments. The rationality is therefore ex post, grounded in the coherence of the identity produced, not ex ante comparative assessment. I also claim that this is not mere rationalization because the continuity constraint is objective and falsifiable: a commitment that severs all directional links to prior commitments is not a resolution but a rupture. What would count against this position? I want an honest assessment of its exposure, not a list of generic objections. Which specific arguments, psychological findings, conceptual distinctions, or possible cases would be decisive against it, and which would be merely awkward? What do defenders of this kind of view themselves concede would damage it, and what do they dismiss as based on a misunderstanding of ex post rationality? In particular, evaluate whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — if it can always reinterpret any commitment as exhibiting some hidden continuity — and whether that insulation is a problem for a normative theory of choice. Also address whether empirical work on post-decisional dissonance supports the identity-constitution claim or undermines it by showing that commitments are retroactive justifications rather than genuine constitutive acts. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and conclude with whether the position is well insulated and whether that is a virtue or a vice. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Please respond in American English.
