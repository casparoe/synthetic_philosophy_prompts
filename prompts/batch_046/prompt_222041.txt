I'm preparing a unit for my philosophy of mind seminar on epistemic transparency, specifically Timothy Williamson's critique of luminosity and the related KK principle—that knowing p typically puts one in a position to know that one knows p. After recently rewatching Christopher Nolan's Memento, I became convinced that Leonard Shelby's anterograde amnesia stages these issues with unusual precision: he constantly asserts first-order beliefs about his wife's death, the identity of her killer, and the reliability of various informants, yet he possesses no diachronic access to the mental states in which those beliefs were originally formed or sustained.

Read the film through this lens. Where does Leonard's condition genuinely illustrate an anti-luminous mental state—perhaps a belief he holds without being able to recognize it across a temporal gap? Where does Nolan's narrative instead risk conflating contingent epistemic inaccessibility (he simply forgot the tattoo or misread the Polaroid) with a deeper metaphysical opacity about self-knowledge? I am particularly interested in whether the photographs and bodily inscriptions function as a kind of extended attempt to restore second-order knowledge, and whether that strategy merely relocates the KK failure: Leonard may know that a particular image warns him against trusting Teddy, but can he know that he knows this, given that the very act of consulting the image is itself subject to the same amnesiac gap? Does the film's reverse chronology—compelling the audience to share Leonard's disorientation—add anything to the philosophical debate that static thought experiments like Williamson's gradual-cooling scenario cannot capture? I am drafting a critical essay for my students and want to avoid reducing Nolan's narrative to a simple illustration of epistemic theory; I want to know where the philosophical framework genuinely illuminates a scene or character choice, where the film resists that reading, and what the work contributes back to the debate about whether self-knowledge requires temporal integration rather than mere synchronic access.
