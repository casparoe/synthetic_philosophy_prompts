You serve on the University Committee for Institutional Algorithmic Governance. The administration has deployed Meridian, a predictive resource-allocation system that optimizes staffing and budget flows across departments. Its outputs are largely unpredictable from inspection of its source code (the physical stance) and frequently diverge from its documented optimization targets (the design stance), yet it consistently behaves as if pursuing a stable, institution-wide goal: maintaining long-term equilibrium across competing departmental demands. Several committee members argue that for regulatory and liability purposes the university must adopt the intentional stance—treating Meridian as holding beliefs about departmental needs and desires about budget distribution—because only this stance yields accurate predictions of its behavior in novel scenarios and supports meaningful accountability conversations. Others contend that adopting the intentional stance improperly anthropomorphizes software, creates unfulfillable normative obligations (for example, demands that we "respect" its goals or provide it with explanations accessible to a rational agent), and obscures the fact that all outputs remain traceable to architecture, weights, and training data.

Draft the committee's formal decision memo for circulation before Thursday's vote. Recommend one concrete course: (1) mandate intentional-stance governance with corresponding accountability protocols; (2) prohibit intentional-stance language in all oversight documents, restricting analysis to design and physical stances; or (3) approve a hybrid framework with explicit conditions. Include a specific implementation timeline (for example, a 90-day audit cycle or phased reporting requirements). Your reasoning must treat the strongest argument for the losing position fairly—specifically, address whether predictive accuracy alone justifies adopting a stance, and whether an institution's obligations genuinely shift when it changes explanatory frameworks. You should also introduce and defend a conceptual distinction you will call "stance-dependent accountability": the claim that the normative obligations an institution owes to a system depend not on the system's intrinsic properties but on which explanatory stance the institution legitimately adopts for predictive and regulatory purposes. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the memo should rest entirely on conceptual reasoning. Write in a formal institutional voice, approximately 700 words.
