I've been thinking about that gender-equality paradox thing — where countries with more equality and welfare actually end up with more gendered career choices, not less. People often cite it to say women just genuinly prefer caring work when structural barriers are gone. But I've noticed they never apply the same reasoning to other areas that also get more male-skewed in rich, free societies — like high-risk sports or competitive chess. It's always career choice that gets treated as this pure window into innate preferance, while other gaps are just ignored or explained away culturally. Is there an established term for this specific argumentative asymetry — using the paradox selectively to naturalise job splits but not other free-choice gaps? Or is it just a kind of motivated reasoning? I don't want the name of the paradox itself; I'm asking about the blind spot in how people deploy it.
