Im trying to figure out when its actually okay to feel real emotions about stuff thats made up, going by my gut on cases. Watching Romeo and Juliet die makes me genuinely sad and that feels right. Reading a news story that turns out to be a hoax makes me feel tricked and the sadness vanishes, which also feels correct. But in a video game when my character dies I feel bad for like ten minutes, and part of me thinks thats silly since its just pixels, though I dont think its wrong exactly. Heres one I keep going back and forth on: I read this AI-generated poem about a dying child and I cried, but then I learned no human wrote it and now I feel like my tears were misdirected, like I was mourning for nobody real. But I still feel sad for the child in the poem? Not sure. I also get scared during horror movies even knowing the monster isnt real, but I dont get scared reading about a mythological monster in a history textbook. And heres the pair that really bugs me: I feel genuine grief when a fictional dog dies in a movie, but when I read about an actual dog that died in a history book from 1800 I feel almost nothing, just "huh thats sad I guess." So is there one principle behind all this, or do I have to drop a verdict? Maybe its about whether Im being invited to imagine the situation as happening to me? Or maybe its about whether theres a real author behind it? What would that say about Titanic, where I feel more for Jack and Rose than for the actual passengers? And does the AI poem case mean feelings need a human mind somewhere in the chain, or can I keep feeling for the character and just feel weird about the source?
