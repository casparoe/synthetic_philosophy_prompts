Write an essay on whether emergent collective decision-making in autonomous drone swarms produces a responsibility gap that is structurally distinct from standard human-machine gaps. In conventional autonomous weapons, the gap arises because an operator lacks sufficient knowledge or control, or because the system is wrongly treated as a moral agent. In a swarm, however, lethal targeting can emerge from local interaction rules that no engineer fully predicted and no commander directly authorized. Defenders of institutional innovation argue that this requires a new framework of distributed structural accountability across design, training, and deployment; opponents contend that such emergent unpredictability is not a problem to be managed but a decisive reason to categorically prohibit lethal swarm deployment. Weigh the arguments carefully, but reach and defend a verdict: either that structural accountability can genuinely close this gap, or that emergent behavior makes the gap unclosable and therefore mandates prohibition. Do not end on a non-committal note.
