I'm a senior software engineer who runs design reviews for a platform's recommendation and moderation systems. Every sprint we face feature choices—explainability versus engagement, harm prevention versus open expression, data retention versus user autonomy—where the values in tension don't seem reducible to a single metric like latency or revenue. I need a reusable decision procedure my team can execute in thirty minutes without philosophy training. The procedure should operationalize three distinctions I keep encountering: standard tradeoffs (values share a scale), genuinely incommensurable conflicts (no common measure exists), and "hard choices" in the specific philosophical sense where options are "on a par"—not equal, not incomparable, but such that rational choice requires a non-arbitrary commitment. I also want an explicit check for what I'll call "non-compensable structural harm": a harm embedded in one option that cannot be offset by any quantity of benefit elsewhere, functioning like a lexical barrier within architecture decisions. For each category, specify the ordered questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with a negative decision, and where it must hand off to human judgment because the issue is normative rather than procedural. Apply the procedure to three cases: (1) adding an explainability dashboard that reduces engagement by 12 percent; (2) deploying a filter that eliminates harmful misinformation but also systematically suppresses a legitimate minority viewpoint, where the procedure should yield a recommendation I will find unacceptable; and (3) choosing between two data-retention architectures whose privacy-autonomy profiles appear genuinely on a par. Finish by stating exactly what the procedure cannot settle and where it is likely to misfire.
