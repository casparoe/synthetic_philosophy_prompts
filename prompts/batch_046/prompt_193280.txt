I've been thinking about sortition and term limits and I realy think career politicans are the root problem, not partisanship. My proposal: a hybrid legislature where half are citizens picked by lot for one non-renewable term, and the other half are elected but with hard caps and a lifetime lobbying ban after leaving. The idea is breaking the career-money feedback loop keeps corruption low without killing democratic input. But I want to know where this breaks. What institutional dynamics or historical cases would show that removing career incentives actually makes governance worse or just moves corruption elsewhere? And is there a real risk that the sortition half gets captured by staff or bureaucrats instead? I want the honest defeaters, not just "unelected elites are bad."
