I'm advising a small interdisciplinary group—philosophers and cognitive scientists—with three years of funding to work at the intersection of Ned Block's distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness and global workspace theory (GWT) as developed by Bernard Baars and later advanced by Stanislas Dehaene. Rather than a general survey, I want a defended strategic agenda: which open conceptual questions and empirical lines of work in this intersection should they prioritise, and in what order?

To prevent the framing from collapsing into well-worn rehearsal, consider this distinction I'm introducing for the exercise: separate "broadcast entry"—the moment information crosses a threshold into the global workspace—from "broadcast maintenance"—the sustained availability of that information for report, reasoning, and behavioural guidance over time. Block's arguments for phenomenal consciousness without access consciousness typically invoke cases where information seems present to experience yet unavailable for report, often because it decays too rapidly. Meanwhile, GWT treats consciousness as tied to widespread broadcasting, but it remains unclear whether broadcasting requires sustained maintenance or can be satisfied by transient entry alone.

Given this framing, please rank the following categories of open questions by how much effort the group should devote to them over three years. Defend the ordering with explicit criteria—upstream dependency on other questions, tractability for a team without massive empirical resources, potential to sharpen or dissolve the access/phenomenal distinction, and neglect in current literature. The categories are:

1. Whether phenomenal consciousness can occur with broadcast entry but without broadcast maintenance—i.e., a transient "flash" of experience that never becomes reportable, and whether such flashes are genuinely phenomenal rather than merely pre-conscious.
2. Whether access consciousness, properly understood, requires maintenance-level broadcasting or can be satisfied by entry-level broadcasting alone, and what either answer implies for the coherence of Block's original distinction.
3. Which neural architectures—such as recurrent processing in sensory cortices versus long-range fronto-parietal loops—are necessary for entry versus maintenance, and whether subcortical pathways could support phenomenal consciousness without either form of broadcasting.
4. The conceptual and clinical implications—particularly for dissociative or minimally conscious states—if we accept that phenomenal consciousness might be sustained by mechanisms that achieve entry or local recurrence without global maintenance.

For each rank, explain which other questions depend on its resolution, how tractable it is within the group's constraints, and what specific result over the next three years would cause you to reorder the priorities. The deliverable should be a strategic research agenda, not a literature review; please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Please write the answer in American English.
