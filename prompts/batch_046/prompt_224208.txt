The philosophical debate over whether virtual harms—psychological trauma, social exclusion, or economic injury experienced within persistent immersive worlds—are genuinely real has stalled in a peculiar way: all sides agree that suffering is bad and that digital environments can cause severe distress, yet they remain immobile about whether that distress counts as harm proper rather than as a mediated representation of some offline injury. Diagnose the impasse. Is it a hidden conceptual split between representational harm, where the virtual environment merely symbolizes real-world damage, and ontologically embedded harm, where the specific rules, persistence, and interactive structure of the virtual world itself constitute a distinct domain of injury? Or is it actually a masked disagreement about whether moral standing requires physical embodiment, with empirical examples being used to smuggle in an unargued metaphysical boundary? Give a committed diagnosis—choose the explanation you find most convincing, defend it with reasons, and do not merely survey the positions—together with what kind of conceptual distinction or argument, rather than further data, could actually resolve the disagreement, or an honest argument that it is in principle unresolvable and why.
