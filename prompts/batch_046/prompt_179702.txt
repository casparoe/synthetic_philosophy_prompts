Present the agreement as a single continuous legal text of approximately six hundred words, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, composed entirely in unbroken prose as though drafted by a meticulous corporate attorney. The text should contain four interwoven provisions: first, an affirmative user duty to evaluate any cause bearing a non-zero prior, however small, whenever its potential payoff exceeds a threshold scaled linearly to that prior; second, a continuous-consent clause stating that a user's refusal to fund a high-utility, low-probability scenario does not terminate the obligation but merely triggers a reframed prompt with adjusted framing; third, a limitation of liability absolving the foundation for any decision paralysis arising from the infinite regress of ever-smaller probabilities; and fourth, a governing-law clause specifying that disputes shall be resolved by comparing the expected value of arbitration against the expected value of the charitable outcome at stake, with the option possessing greater expectation binding regardless of procedural fairness or the magnitude of the legal costs involved. The satire should exaggerate the genuine structural features of Pascal-style reasoning—the linear aggregation of expectation across incommensurable scales, the absence of any principled cutoff for negligible probability, and the erosion of practical boundaries between action and inaction—so that a reader acquainted with the concept recognizes the parody as internally consistent rather than a straw man. Let the standard objections emerge solely from the deadpan extension of the platform's premises, never stating them explicitly. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or requests for further reading; do not name specific contemporary authors; and do not rely on any specialized notation beyond basic arithmetic and the language of probability.
