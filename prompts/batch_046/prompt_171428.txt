I'm writing this for my metaphysics seminar on personal identity, and I want to test whether an argument I sketched actually proves something or just sounds rigorous. The claim is that rejecting origin essentialism doesn't merely open a few nearby possible worlds; it collapses personal identity into complete modal thinness. Here are the steps, numbered precisely as I presented them in section:

(1) Origin essentialism is false: I am not essentially the child of the particular sperm and egg that actually produced me.

(2) Therefore there exists at least one possible world W in which I come from a different gamete pair.

(3) If there is one such world, then there is no metaphysical barrier preventing me from coming from any other biologically compatible pair: once the essentialist anchor is removed, modal accessibility is not selectively restricted by origin.

(4) Among those other pairs are ones that, through ordinary genetic variation or evolutionary divergence, would produce beings with radically different phenotypes, cognitive architectures, or even non-human physiology.

(5) By the transitivity of identity across worlds, if I am identical to the being in W, then I could have had those radically different properties.

(6) So if origin essentialism is false, I could have been non-human, non-conscious, or psychologically discontinuous with my actual self.

(7) But this applies symmetrically to every person: reject origin essentialism, and everyone loses all essential properties except bare self-identity.

(8) Psychological-continuity theorists reject origin essentialism yet still maintain that I am necessarily human and necessarily conscious; they do not embrace modal thinness.

(9) Therefore either psychological-continuity theory is incoherent, or one of the steps above fails—most likely (3), which looks like a quantifier shift disguised as a modal inference.

My suspicion is that the slide between (2) and (3) is the weak point: from "there exists a world with a different origin" to "all origins are modally available to me." But I am unsure what principle blocks that slide without smuggling origin essentialism back in under another name—say, by treating psychological-continuity conditions as de facto origin filters. If I reject (3), what do I pay? Must I accept haecceitism, or can an anti-haecceitist block the inference differently? And if I instead reject (5), am I giving up transworld identity altogether, or can a four-dimensionalist treat origins as spatiotemporal parts rather than modal anchors and still save continuity? I would like to know which premise is least costly to abandon, what each abandonment commits me to regarding essential properties, and whether the argument can be repaired into a valid but less extreme version without losing its critical force against the psychological-continuity view.
