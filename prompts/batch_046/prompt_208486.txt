A graduate student in my seminar summarized Hofstadter and Sander's thesis in Surfaces and Essences (2013) as follows: "Cognition is nothing but analogy-making; concepts are compressed analogies, and the mind contains no rules, representations, or innate structures. Because analogies depend on embodied, lived experience, a disembodied system—say, a contemporary large language model—cannot genuinely think, regardless of its fluency. Consequently, this view decisively refutes computational theories of mind, since computation is rule-manipulation and analogy is irreducibly non-computational." The student intends to use this to argue against functionalist accounts of intelligence. Before I allow it, I need an accuracy audit: which elements of this summary are faithful to Hofstadter and Sander's actual argument, where does it conflate their view with neighboring positions such as conceptual blending or embodied cognition, and does the anti-computational, anti-functional conclusion actually follow from their premises—or does it depend on an unstated equivocation about computation and representation? Please be precise about what survives correction and what must be withdrawn or qualified.
