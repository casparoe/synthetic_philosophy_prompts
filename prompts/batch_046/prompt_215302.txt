For our graduate seminar in formal epistemology and philosophy of science, please provide a rigorous but accessible explanation of how Solomonoff induction relates to Kolmogorov complexity and to philosophical formulations of Occam's razor. Your answer should address three interrelated points in detail. First, explain precisely how the universal prior—defined via prefix-free Kolmogorov complexity—assigns prior probabilities to hypotheses in a manner that formalizes the intuition that simpler theories ought to be preferred. Second, examine the objection that because Kolmogorov complexity is invariant only up to an additive constant that depends on the choice of universal Turing machine, the Solomonoff prior cannot provide an objectively correct ordering of competing hypotheses; assess whether this machine-dependence genuinely undermines the philosophical claim that Solomonoff induction captures an objective version of Occam's razor, or whether the constant can be philosophically marginalized without loss of normative force. Third, distinguish carefully between interpreting the convergence of the Solomonoff predictor to the true data-generating distribution as a normative justification for inductive inference and interpreting it as a purely descriptive mathematical result about asymptotic behavior. Write for an audience of advanced philosophy students who understand basic probability theory but have not worked through the technical literature in detail; avoid bibliographic references and focus on conceptual clarity and argumentative precision.
