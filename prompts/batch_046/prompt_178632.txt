I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind writing on collective cognition and the upward application of the problem of other minds. I want a disciplined case analysis that runs in both directions: using the collective-mind thesis—specifically the framing in Hofstadter’s Ant Fugue, where high-level colony-level patterns might feed back onto individual ant behavior in a non-reducible strange loop—to interpret an actual episode of collective decision-making, and using that episode to test whether the thesis explains anything or merely redescribes mechanistic processes in mentalistic vocabulary.

The episode: In the ant Temnothorax rugatulus, relocation to a new nest proceeds via individual scouts assessing candidate cavities and recruiting nestmates through tandem running. Recruitment accelerates at the superior site, and the colony moves only once the number of ants present there crosses a quorum threshold—roughly one-third of the colony. No individual ant compares all available options, maintains a global representation of “best site,” or acts as a central comparator; and there is no mechanism by which the colony represents itself as an agent with preferences.

Analyze this episode through the collective-mind lens. First, what does the thesis say the colony faces and “does”? Does the quorum threshold function analogously to belief-formation about nest quality, or is it purely statistical accumulation without semantic content? Second, address whether Hofstadter’s strange-loop model requires a form of self-reference—some minimal modeling of the colony-as-agent—that these insects appear to lack, and if so whether the model is misapplied here or reveals a necessary condition for emergent minds. Third, apply the other-minds problem upward: if we deny the colony a mind on the grounds that we lack heterophenomenological access to any “experience” it might have, are we relying on a criterion that, applied consistently, would undermine our ordinary attributions to other human beings?

Please keep the mapping precise—who plays which theoretical role, what counts as evidence, and where the theory is silent rather than forced to speak. Conclude by saying whether this episode confirms the collective-mind thesis, counts as an unanticipated anomaly, or shows that the thesis was never genuinely about mechanisms like this quorum system.
