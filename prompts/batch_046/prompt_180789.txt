I'm on the charter revision commitee for a mid-sized city, and we vote next month on a hybrid proposal: keep the elected council but impose hard lifetime term limits (two terms max, ever), and replace the budget and zoning committees with citizens drawn by lot for 18-month paid stints. The idea is to break the career pipeline where staffers become councilors and councilors become lobbyists, but half the commitee says randomly selected citizens lack a democratic mandate and can't be held acountable. Draft the memo I'll circulate: recommend adoption, rejection, or adoption with conditions, with a concrete pilot timeline — say one budget cycle — and deal seriously with the mandate objection rather than just waving it away with ancient Athens. The reasoning should address whether pairing sortition with term limits actually solves careerism or just splits the problem between two bodies.
