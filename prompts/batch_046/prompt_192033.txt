after spending months caring for my mum i've realised how much of who i am depends on being seen by people who actually know me, which makes ubuntu's idea that personhood is relational rather than something you carry inside suddenly feel urgent rather than academic. i need a workshop activity for about 12 people in one hour that makes them feel this, not just talk about it: design a role-play or game where each person starts with full voice and claim rights, but these can only be kept or lost through the collective recogntion of the group, with no individual appeals allowed once recognition is withdrawn. they should hit the crisis where losing recognition means losing the capacity to even argue for it, then a debrief that maps this onto relational identity, plus an honest note on what the simulation misses about real ubuntu ethics. present it in exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion, and don't ask for citations or reading lists.
