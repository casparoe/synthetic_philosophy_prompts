I have been thinking on my own about George Orwell's writing on language and politics — not through any formal study, just reading and reflecting — and I keep returning to what seems like a genuine split among people who take his arguments seriously. The language-first readers understand Orwell to be saying that the corruption of language is an independent cause of political corruption: when words lose precise meaning, the ability to think clearly and resist manipulation collapses, and this decay can happen even without a deliberate author behind it. The power-first readers understand him to be saying that language is primarily an instrument of domination: regimes corrupt vocabulary and grammar deliberately to maintain control, and treating language reform as a primary remedy mistakes the effect for the cause.
