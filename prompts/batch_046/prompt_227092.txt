I am preparing to moderate a colloquium Q&A with an unnamed philosopher who advances a particularist reading of Wittgenstein's family resemblance. She maintains that family resemblance governs concept-formation only within participatory language-games, not across all conceptual domains, and that this restriction resolves the tension between Wittgenstein's anti-essentialism and the apparently essentialist role of grammatical rules in the Philosophical Investigations. She is prone to deflect circularity objections by insisting that "the game defines the resemblance, not vice versa."

Draft eight to ten interview questions that avoid mere restatement of her position. Include at least one question that presses her on how she distinguishes participatory language-games from non-participatory conceptual practices without tacitly invoking the essentialist criteria she rejects; at least one question that asks whether her treatment of grammatical rules as non-essential constraints collapses into conventionalism and thereby loses the resources to distinguish conceptual error from mere deviation; and at least one question addressing how her view accommodates concepts—such as mathematical or logical ones—where family resemblance seems least applicable. For each of these lines, provide a follow-up keyed to her most probable reply, particularly anticipating her circularity deflection. Structure the sequence so that her standard response becomes progressively more difficult to maintain, and conclude with a question about what kind of textual or practical evidence could, in principle, undermine her claim that family resemblance is restricted to participatory contexts.
