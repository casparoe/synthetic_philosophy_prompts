I have recently been studying thermodynamic limits on computation in connection with a low-energy information-processing project, and I have come to hold a view stronger than the standard Landauer-resolution of Maxwell's demon. I think the demon was never a genuine paradox—not merely because erasure costs kT ln 2, but because information acquisition is itself a physical interaction that necessarily couples the demon's internal state to the bath, distributing entropy into correlations that are thermodynamically irrecoverable. On this view, there is no stage at which pure information can be separated from thermal cost, even before any erasure occurs. What would count against this? I want decisive objections, not just restatements of Landauer's principle: explicit physical models in which information is extracted without such coupling; quantum-measurement results that decouple acquisition from disturbance; or conceptual arguments that information and entropy remain categorically distinct. Rank them by how fatal they would be to my position. Which objections would a defender of this view have to concede, and which could be absorbed by refining what physical interaction requires? I also want an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that is a problem for a claim of this kind.
