People defending eliminative materialism always compare belief and desire to phlogiston or witchcraft — theoretical entities science eventually tossed out. My roommate says thats too quick: phlogiston got replaced by oxidation with clear predictive gains, but folk psychology hasn't got an equivalent replacement, and witchcraft was never a serious predictive framework to begin with. He pushes a different analogy: folk psychology is like a geocentric navigation app — it works fine for local directions even though the underlying ontology is wrong. Audit both analogies: map what plays the role of the eliminated entity, the replacement science, and the practical success in each. Where does the navigation-app picture break, especially around first-person authority we seem to have about are own beliefs? And do either analogies actually carry the eliminativist conclusion, or are they both just dressing up an intuition?
