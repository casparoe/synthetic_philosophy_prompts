I am preparing the interview guide for an upcoming episode of a philosophy-of-mathematics podcast I host for graduate students and faculty, and the guest will be a contemporary defender of strict finitism—someone who denies that there are any actual infinities in ontology, who treats Hilbert’s hotel not as a benign curiosity about the cardinalities of completed infinite sets but rather as a reductio ad absurdum of treating such collections as fully formed totalities, and who reads Cantor’s diagonal argument as establishing only that any enumerated list of real numbers can be extended by a constructive procedure, not as proving the existence of a completed uncountable actual infinity. I want eight to ten interview questions that do not ask him merely to restate this package, but that press where the literature actually presses: at least one question on whether Hilbert’s hotel reveals an internal inconsistency in actualism or merely an unintuitive feature that can be domesticated, with a follow-up keyed to the standard reply that “counterintuitive is not contradictory”; at least one on how exactly the finitist distinguishes the potential infinity of an unending process from the actual infinity Cantor is said to have demonstrated, with a follow-up anticipating his likely move to proceduralize the diagonal construction and then pressing whether that proceduralization collapses the argument’s force into a claim about human cognitive limits rather than about set existence; and at least one on whether his finitism is meant as an epistemic caution about the limits of mathematical cognition or as a straightforward metaphysical denial of infinite objects, with a follow-up addressing how he accounts for the apparent success and indispensability of classical analysis and measure theory in physical science. The remaining questions should probe further pressure points—such as whether a potentialist can make sense of quantification over “all real numbers” without tacitly presupposing a completed domain, whether the finitist can accept standard limits and continuity without smuggling in actual infinite structures, and what evidence or theoretical development could in principle weaken his commitment—each paired with a brief anticipation of his probable answer and a sharper follow-up designed to make deflection harder. Please keep the tone formally suited to an academic colloquium, avoid any request for bibliographies or citation lists, and frame every question around the substantive content of his position rather than biographical or generic prompts.
