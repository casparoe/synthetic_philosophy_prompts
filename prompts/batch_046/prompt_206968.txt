Dennett's rejection of the Cartesian theater in favor of a multiple-drafts model is usually taken to preserve the unity of consciousness by replacing a central observer with distributed processes that achieve temporary integration. But if there is genuinely no single finish line at which experience becomes present, and if integration is only a contingent result of which drafts achieve widespread influence in the brain, then the distinction between one person with fragmented awareness and several coexisting subjects within a single body seems to collapse. Would this not imply that dissociative conditions, rather than being failures of integration, are merely instances of the normal architecture of consciousness operating without the usual coalitional dominance? And if the self is indeed nothing more than a narrative center constructed from competing drafts, should legal and moral personhood be reconceived as a distributed property that a single organism might instantiate more than once?
