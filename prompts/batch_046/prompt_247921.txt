You are an expert in social choice theory, institutional design, and applied philosophical logic. After a long and somewhat heated argument last night with a close friend—who insisted that any committee facing inconsistent majority judgments should simply defer to the premises and derive the conclusion mechanically—I need your help drafting a formal recommendation memo. Please think carefully, work through the reasoning step by step, and begin with a brief internal brainstorm of the core tension between premise-based aggregation, conclusion-based aggregation, and hybrid rules in collective decision-making. Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific academic papers; I need reasoning I can present to non-specialists.

The context: I sit on a university funding committee for interdisciplinary ethics research. We must vote separately on three propositions regarding each proposal: (1) the empirical premises are sound; (2) the normative framework is appropriate; (3) the proposal should be funded. We have just encountered a discursive-dilemma structure: a majority endorses both (1) and (2) yet rejects (3); a different majority endorses (1) and (3) but rejects (2); and a third endorses (2) and (3) but rejects (1). No single conclusion commands a direct majority across all three pairwise combinations.

My friend proposes a confidence-weighted premise rule: members assign confidence scores to their premise judgments, we compute weighted aggregates, and we derive the conclusion mechanically from the weighted premises. I worry this merely relocates the inconsistency into the weighting scheme and masks genuine disagreement about the conclusion itself.

Your task: Draft a short policy memo (approximately 600–800 words, formal tone, actionable bottom line) addressed to the committee chair recommending which aggregation rule we should formally adopt—pure premise-based, pure conclusion-based, or a modified hybrid that incorporates one specific procedural innovation. To satisfy the competing demands of logical consistency and democratic legitimacy, inject into your proposal a concrete niche mechanism: a minority premise veto, whereby if at least three members reject a specific premise that the weighted majority accepts, that premise is flagged for open deliberation rather than being treated as settled, and the conclusion is suspended pending resolution.

The memo should:
- State the recommendation clearly in the first paragraph.
- Explain the discursive dilemma as it applies to our three-proposition voting structure, without assuming the reader has prior expertise.
- Compare the three aggregation approaches, giving each its strongest argument.
- Give an honest and substantive treatment of my friend’s confidence-weighted idea—acknowledge what it solves and where it fails—before explaining why your recommended rule, including the minority-premise-veto innovation, handles the problem better.
- Conclude with specific procedural language the committee could vote on.

Please write in a polished, professional voice suitable for an institutional document, and avoid any reference to obscure theoretical frameworks that depend on literature I have not mentioned.
