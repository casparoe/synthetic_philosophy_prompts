hey, trying to write up my stance on cosmological arguments for a seminar and want a consistency check before i embarrass myself. here's roughly where i land:

(1) i accept the kalam version: whatever begins to exist has a cause, the universe had a temporal begining, so there's a cause outside of time.
(2) i also accept the contingency version, but with a restricted PSR — every concrete contingent being needs an explanation that eventually bottoms out in something necessary, not in another contingent thing. so infinite explanatory chains are out.
(3) but i also think an infinite temporal past is perfectly coherent; there's no contradiction in an endless sequence of prior moments.
(4) i think God, if he exists, is timeless and necessary, so he doesn't begin and doesn't fall under either argument.
(5) i think modal collapse worries are overblown: God can be metaphysically necessary without every contingent thing he sustains being necessary too.
(6) and i think if there's a multiverse spitting out universes eternally, that multiverse is still just one big contingent thing that needs explaining.

my question: is this package actually consistent? specifically, does (2) clash with (3) — i reject infinite explanation but allow infinite time, and is that stable? does (1) sit ok with (6) if the multiverse never began? and is there a hidden tension between (5) and (2) about what 'necessary' does? please rank the tensions by severity, say which one's the fake tension that dissolves, and tell me the least costly revision if i have to drop something. also, what does my view imply about cyclical cosmological models?
