I sit on my city's Public Arts and Civic Space Committee, and we must vote next month on a proposed permanent installation for Riverside Park titled "The Labor We Don't See." The collective behind it wants to house a small group of goats and chickens in climate-controlled, automated enclosures with one-way viewing glass; machines would handle all feeding, cleaning, and temperature control, and veterinarians have certified that the setup eliminates physical pain, stress, and human handling. Visitors would listen to audio about hidden agricultural labor while observing the animals. The artists say the point is to make invisible human dependence visible, not to exploit the creatures.

I have no formal training in philosophy, but I've been reading on my own and keep coming back to Kant's idea that we don't have direct duties to animals—only indirect ones, since cruelty toward them hardens us against cruelty to other people. If that's right, the usual welfare objections seem to fall away here, because there's no suffering and no cruelty. Yet I've also been wondering whether there's a separate wrong in using living, sentient beings purely as props in a human narrative, even when every physical need is met automatically. It feels like treating them as mere instruments rather than as beings with any moral standing of their own, and I can't tell whether Kant's framework allows for that distinction or whether I'm just inventing an intuition that doesn't hold up.

Please draft the formal decision memo I will circulate before the meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—and specify a clear time frame, such as a deadline for compliance or for removal if conditions aren't met. The memo should take the Kantian indirect-duty view seriously, examine whether "instrumental use without cruelty" can be permitted within that view, and honestly present the strongest consideration on the losing side. Write in American English, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or requests for further reading; I need reasoning that stands entirely on its own.
