Write a villanelle that stages the disagreement between Dennett's two-bitser — the minimal system with only two bits of state, whose intentional content is wholly derived from a designer's intentions — and Searle's thesis that original intentionality is irreducibly intrinsic and that syntax is never sufficient for semantics. Let one refrain speak from the bitser's functional perspective, registering presence and absence as derived meanings fixed by design; let the other refrain speak from Searle's position, insisting that no symbol-shuffling without subjective consciousness yields genuine understanding. The villanelle's turning refrains should enact the way these two arguments circle each other without convergence, and the closing couplet should set them side by side so that the reader sees exactly why they do not resolve. The poem should be precise enough that a philosopher of mind recognizes the binary-state tracking of the bitser and the syntax-semantics gap of the Chinese Room, yet remain a coherent poem for a reader who does not know the debate.
