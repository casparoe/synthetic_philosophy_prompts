everyone defending big-history books like Harari or Diamond keeps saying writing them is like zooming out on google maps -- you lose street-level detail but suddenly see continent-shaped patterns you need for climate or pandemics. they use it to push back against critics who say local culture and agency just get erased. audit the analogy: whats playing the role of the map, the zoom, the satellite; where does the correspondence actually break; what does it quietly assume about whether local actors are just noise in a bigger signal; and does the defense survive once those breaks are on the table? then compare it to a different framing: is writing grand history more like averaging the colors of a pointillist painting and claiming thats the picture? say which analogy is fairer and whether either actually supports the conclusion that local detail doesnt matter.
