Construct a thought experiment that meets the following specifications and then demonstrate that it does so. Design a realistic, non-science-fictional scenario—no demons, perfect predictors, or technological fantasies, suitable for an undergraduate audience, and no longer than a single paragraph—in which endurantism and perdurantism deliver opposite verdicts about whether a particular human being, call her M, survives a sustained physical change, while psychological continuity, causal connectedness, social recognition, and first-person memory are all held constant across both theories. To avoid the well-worn Ship-of-Theseus and brain-transplant templates, inject this structural feature: the change must be interwoven rather than sequential. Specifically, M’s original biological tissue and cultivated biological replacement tissue must coexist in a functionally integrated state throughout her body for a prolonged interval, with the proportion of original tissue declining continuously and uniformly from one hundred percent to zero, so that there is never a discrete moment at which a specific original part is removed and replaced. State M’s belief about her own survival through this process, specify the method she uses to track her persistence, identify the relevant nearby worlds, and show precisely why an endurantist must deny that M survives while a perdurantist can affirm that she survives as a four-dimensional worm composed of distinct temporal parts. Then isolate the feature doing the metaphysical work: explain what happens to both verdicts if the interwoven feature is replaced by standard part-by-part sequential replacement, and present the strongest endurantist rebuttal—specifically, an argument that functional integration across a temporal interval does not entail identity over time, together with the charge that the case illegitimately smuggles four-dimensionalist intuitions into a three-dimensional ontology.
