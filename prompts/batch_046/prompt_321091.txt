2. Armenia: there's continuous cultural and religious identity going back millenia, but the modern republic's borders and national narrative were reconstructed after Soviet collapse, with a lot of retroactive myth-making about the 1918 republic. Verdict: partly old, partly invented, but the invention doesn't erase the old part.

3. The 'nation' of data scientists. No territory, but shared jargon, origin myths about the 2012 deep learning boom, and a sense of shared destiny. Verdict: imagined community in Anderson's sense, but not a nation, since there's no sovereignty claim.

4. The European Union. It has invented traditions like Europe Day and shared symbols, but people don't die for it. Verdict: imagined community trying to become a nation and failing because it lacks the sacrificial dimension.

Only about 400 people, but they've done this for 30 years. Is that a nation? My gut says no, because there's no intergenerational mass belief, but I'm unsure what separates them from the Zionist movement in the 1890s, which was also small and weird at first.
