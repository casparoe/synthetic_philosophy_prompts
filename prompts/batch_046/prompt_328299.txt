I am drafting an essay that revisits Jackson's knowledge argument through a modified scenario I call Synthetic Mary. In the canonical formulation, Mary knows all physical information about color vision but acquires something new upon seeing red. In my variant, Mary is confined to a monochrome environment and obtains, in addition to standard propositional physical knowledge, a complete structural-functional map of color qualia produced by running a high-fidelity neural simulation of herself in vitro. From this simulation she can predict, with perfect accuracy, every qualitative report that any subject will issue upon seeing red, without ever having undergone the experience herself. The essay contends that even this augmented epistemic position fails to close the explanatory gap, though not for Jackson's original reason. Rather than positing a novel phenomenal fact, I argue that the residual gap lies between third-person predictive mastery and first-person authoritative stance: Mary can anticipate what it is like for others without being in a position to avow it from her own point of view. I then examine whether this reformulation collapses into a version of the transparency thesis or instead preserves an anti-physicalist conclusion by relocating the missing element to a pragmatic deficit in self-knowledge rather than to an ontological shortfall.

I would welcome conceptual comments on this architecture. Specifically: (1) Does the Synthetic Mary case genuinely differ from the original knowledge argument, or does it merely redescribe the physicalist reply that Mary gains a new ability rather than a new fact? (2) Is the distinction between predictive mastery and authoritative stance philosophically robust, or does it conflate epistemic access with metaphysical constitution? (3) Are there hidden commitments—perhaps regarding the limits of simulation or the conditions for first-person authority—that undermine the argument's neutrality? I am especially interested in whether this framing avoids standard physicalist rebuttals, such as the ability hypothesis or the acquaintance-knowledge reply, or whether it inadvertently strengthens them by granting the physicalist a more powerful predictive apparatus. I am not seeking a bibliography or citation list; I am looking for pressure-testing of the argument's internal coherence and its susceptibility to existing counter-arguments.
