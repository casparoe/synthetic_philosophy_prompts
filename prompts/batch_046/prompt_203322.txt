For a reading group I've been asked to write down my philosphical profile on Zhuangzi's butterfly dream and dream scepticism so the others can poke at it. Here it is: I reckon Zhuangzi's story isn't mainly about scepticism but about the fluid boundry between self and other; I'm a direct realist about waking perception but representationalist about dreams, so there's an asymmetry in how we relate to the world; I also think there's no first-person epistemic marker that lets you tell which mode you're in; I'm an externalist about justification, so I can know I'm awake if my belief-forming processes are reliable even without introspective access; I think phenomenal concepts are private but not incorrigible; I deny there's anything it is like to be a butterfly, even in Zhuangzi's transformed state, because butterflies lack a unified phenomenal field; and I think the best answer to dream scepticism isn't Cartesian certainty but pragmatic encroachment — the practical stakes of being wrong about being awake are too low to require proof.
