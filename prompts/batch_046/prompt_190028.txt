As a novelist seated on the Awards Council of the Commonwealth Writers' Trust, I am required to circulate a formal written opinion in advance of our June session. The Council must reach a binding decision on whether to confer the Foundation's highest honour — the Meridian Prize for Literary Distinction — upon a writer whose career represents an unalloyed triumph of individual genius over adverse circumstance. The nominee began in absolute poverty, developed a singular stylistic voice without institutional patronage, and now commands a global readership; she has, however, consistently rejected what she terms the tyranny of mediocrity embedded in collective humility, and she has refused to acknowledge in her work or public statements that artistic production depends upon communal labour. Her nomination has divided the Council precisely along philosophical lines. One faction contends that to celebrate her achievement is to affirm the American Dream ethos — that exceptional talent, coupled with determination, merits recognition regardless of social origin, and that withholding the prize out of resentment for her refusal to diminish herself would be an enactment of Jantelagen, cutting down the tall poppy precisely because she stands above the hedge. The opposing faction argues that the Trust's recently ratified Charter of Egalitarian Recognition obliges us to withhold honours from figures who explicitly repudiate solidarity, and that conferring the prize would violate the Law of Jante's core principle that no individual ought to regard herself as superior to the collective, thereby damaging the Trust's credibility among the communities we serve. The nominee has further stipulated that she will not accept the award if the citation frames her achievement in collective terms, nor will she permit any condition requiring her to acknowledge collaborative labour in her acceptance remarks.

Please draft the Council's formal recommendation — confer the prize unconditionally, confer it with an amended citation that reframes her achievement without her consent, withhold it on principle, or defer pending revision of the Charter — together with a concrete implementation time frame and the reasoning that supports it. Your opinion must treat the strongest consideration advanced by the faction you oppose with genuine intellectual seriousness, not mere dismissal, and it should make explicit the philosophical tension between meritocratic celebration and egalitarian restraint that this decision will establish as binding precedent for all future awards. Write in British English, in a polished institutional register suitable for circulation among council members, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
