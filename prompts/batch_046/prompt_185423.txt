I am designing a seventy-five-minute session for second-year philosophy undergraduates who have just completed an introductory ethics course. The goal is to teach the structural problem in population axiology commonly called the repugnant conclusion, but I want to carry the entire explanation through an unconventional framing: treating axiological comparison as analogous to curating a permanent archive in which adding vast quantities of minimally valuable items can, under certain aggregation rules, diminish the overall worth of the collection. I do not want the explanation itself; I want a teaching plan that specifies the exact dependency order in which total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, and a critical-level adjustment must be introduced so that the paradox feels inevitable rather than contrived; the one concrete archive-curation scenario that should serve as the carrying example, including what formal properties it must possess (scalability without qualitative ceiling, clear separation between presence and absence of value) and what it must deliberately lack (explicit images of suffering, temporal urgency, or personal identity across generations); the predictable misconceptions students will bring or form—especially the conflation of "barely worth living" with "not worth living," the slide from logical aggregation to empirical predictions about overpopulation, and the belief that average utilitarianism resolves the difficulty without cost—and the precise point in the sequence where each should be intercepted; a brief diagnostic check I can administer near the end to confirm that students grasp the structural conflict rather than merely reacting with emotional revulsion; and an explicit statement of what must be left out at this level—such as the formal mathematics of variable-value theories or the parallel sadistic conclusion—together with the acknowledgment that these omissions are deliberate. The plan should assume the audience has no access to external readings or bibliographies, and should not ask them to consult any.
