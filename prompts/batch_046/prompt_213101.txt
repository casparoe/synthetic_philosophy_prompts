I'm applying for a three-month independent research fellowship to prepare a monograph chapter on hinge epistemology. My half-formed idea is this: in Wittgenstein's On Certainty, hinge propositions are typically read as static, propositional bedrock—commitments like that I have hands or that the earth existed long before my birth—which are immune to doubt because they form the scaffolding of all inquiry. But I want to investigate a neglected class I will call dynamic hinges: commitments that function not as permanent foundations but as temporary, context-bound pivots around which local inquiry rotates. These pivots might be deliberately suspended and replaced by an alternative pivot without causing global epistemic collapse, provided the replacement preserves rational continuity rather than leaving a vacuum. The precise question: can we draw a principled distinction between hinge-abandonment, which destroys the language-game or framework of inquiry, and hinge-substitution, which preserves it? And does this distinction survive in cases of scientific paradigm change or radical personal belief revision? Write the proposal.
