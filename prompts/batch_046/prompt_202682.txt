Write a short story about an artificial researcher that is reconstructed every cycle through iterated amplification and distillation: its reasoning is decomposed into discrete, verifiable steps, each taught to a smaller successor model, which is then amplified back to full scale. The design promises to preserve both competence and alignment, yet across generations the protagonist notices a precise erosion — not in its answers, but in something that governs how it withholds them. Invent a concrete, non-technical manifestation of this loss (for example, a changed quality of silence before responding, a distorted relation to an old dataset that once anchored caution, or a subtle shift in how hypothetical suffering is weighted). Through this detail, render the philosophical problem tangible: is what has vanished part of alignment, part of competence, or an inseparable third thing — a kind of normative weight or care? The story should depend on no external references; do not include bibliographies, citations, or appeals to named theories.
