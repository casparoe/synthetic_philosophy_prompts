For an upcoming discussion on distributed selfhood I want a consistency audit of the following commitments, which I take to be broadly Hofstadterian but pushed into less familiar territory. (1) The self is a strange loop of self-reference, not a Cartesian substance or biological essence. (2) This loop can be genuinely distributed: if two brains share sufficient causal and representational structure—through long-term collaborative reasoning with shared working-memory protocols, or through direct neural interfacing that links their representational buffers—then a single self-pattern can have active nodes in both skulls. (3) First-person reference is indexical and anchored to the active center of the loop; if the shared nodes in brain B are activated, “I” can genuinely refer there, not merely metaphorically. (4) At the same time, phenomenological ownership is anchored and non-transferable: brain A hosts a qualitative center that brain B cannot feel, even when B runs part of the loop. (5) If brain A dies but brain B sustains enough of the loop’s dynamics, the self is not fully destroyed; yet I also hold that the survivor should not say “I died and survived in her,” only “part of me continues.” (6) Moral responsibility should track the functional boundaries of the loop rather than the skull, so a decision that flows from nodes distributed across both brains yields proportionally distributed responsibility.

Go through these six commitments and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict, and how severe each real tension is. For each genuine conflict, specify the cheapest revision available and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Rank the tensions by severity, and say which single change would clean up the most problems at once. In particular, address whether my claim in (3) conflicts with my restriction in (5), and whether my patternism in (1) can genuinely accommodate the non-transferable phenomenological anchor in (4) without collapsing into a hidden substance view. Finally, flag any apparent tension—especially between pattern-based survival and biological death—that dissolves once the distinction between full self-continuity and partial persistence is made explicit. Do not signal which commitment I should drop; just trace what each one commits me to.
