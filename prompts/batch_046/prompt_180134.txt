I’m working on a novel where a charcter’s conciousness kind of leaks into other people through shared dream-implants, and my editor wants me to map out the philosphy behind it so the world feels grounded. I was talking with friends about Hofstadter’s idea that your self is like a pattern that can persist inside other people after you die—basically your soul is just the copy of you living in anyone who remembers you well—but we got stuck on whether that counts as YOU still being there, or just an echo in the chorus.

So I need a taxonomy, more like a decision tree, that sorts out positions on whether an 'I' can acutally be distributed across multple brains. Not a list of names people already use—something that sorts you by what you’d say about concrete cases. Start at the root: does being an 'I' require being tied to one specific living brain? From there branch on whether continuity is biological substrate or informational pattern. Then split on whether a distributed self is one subject with several nodes, or a cluster of overlapping subjects—like, is there one me running on three brains, or three mes that share memories?

Then include a branch about Hofstadter-style persistence: if your pattern lives on in others after your body dies, is that survival, or just influence? And another on real-time linkage—if two brains are wired together live, does that change the answer compared to posthumous persistence? I also want you to inject a distinction I made up for the book: call it the 'veto node problem.' If one brain in the network can override the others, is the self still genuinely distributed, or is it just one brain with remote sensors? Make that a branch point.

Each leaf should be a named position if one exists, or 'unnamed,' with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can’t seperate—maybe standard psychological continuity and Hofstadter-style persistence collapse into the same answers on every case here. Note any empty combinations too, like maybe nobody defends biological-only selfhood plus live distributed linkage because that combo is incoherent.

Write it so every node is a question someone can answer without knowing jargon—no asking 'are you an animalist?' just ask about the cases. Aim for eight to ten leaves. And please, no bibliographies or 'see Parfit 1984'—this is for my world-builidng notes, not a seminar paper. I probably misspelled a few things but you get the idea.
