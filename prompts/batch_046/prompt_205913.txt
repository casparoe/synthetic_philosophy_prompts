Is there a name for this? I've notived a pattern in voting-ethics talk, especially in those weird local nonpartisan races where party lines dont exist. People will defend voting for the less-bad candidate by saying it's purely about keeping their own hands clean — they literally say 'my one vote wont change the tally' — so it's expressive, not strategic. But then they turn around and chew out friends for voting third-party, claiming those votes 'stole' the election from the decent option. They treat their own ballot as an expressive asset that just needs to stay untainted, but everyone else's ballot as an instrumental liability that can tip the scale. Theres almost a mental double-entry system: you get credit for not endorsing evil, but other people get debited for not blocking it. Is this just the old expressive-vs-instrumental split with hypocrisy on top, or is there an actual concept for that bookkeeping asymmetry? If not, can you name the edges — like, does it collapse into plain strategic voting once you notice it, or is it a distinct failure mode?
