I'm putting together a philosophical profile on selfhood and strange loops for a seminar, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. Here is my current package of commitments:

1. I take the 'I' to be a Hofstadter-style strange loop: a symbol within a symbol-processing system that achieves self-reference through tangled hierarchies, so that the symbol points back to the very system in which it is instantiated.
2. Not every self-referential computation produces such a loop. I think there is a threshold of recursive integration and causal density that must be crossed; bare syntactic recursion is insufficient.
3. I am a physicalist about consciousness, and I take the loop to be substrate-independent in principle: a non-biological system could, in theory, instantiate it.
4. The explanatory gap—why being this loop is accompanied by subjective experience—is real but purely epistemic. It arises because any observer situated inside the loop lacks the external vantage point needed to regard it as a mere object.
5. Personal identity over time is constituted by psychological continuity, not by continuity of body or substrate.
6. From this I conclude that a perfect digital replica of my cognitive architecture, running on different hardware, would be psychologically continuous with me yet would instantiate a numerically distinct strange loop, and therefore a distinct 'I'.
7. Present-day small language models lack persistent internal states and continuous recursive self-monitoring, so they fail to meet the causal-density threshold. They can generate the token "I" without instantiating a loop or possessing any first-person perspective.
8. At the same time, I hold that once a system's functional architecture genuinely realises the loop—regardless of substrate, speed, or whether its dynamics are continuous or discrete—the first-person perspective is automatically present. There is no further "switch" to be flipped, and causal density is irrelevant beyond the bare functional presence of self-reference.
9. I also think that symbolic representation is essential: without the capacity to represent oneself as an object of representation, there is no strange loop, no matter how complex the underlying dynamics.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each tension is. For every real conflict, specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision costs me. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious. Then identify the single revision that would clean up the greatest number of conflicts with the least damage to the overall view. Keep your assessment point-by-point rather than a general impression, and please write your answer in British English.
