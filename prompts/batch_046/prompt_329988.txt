I sketched a short essay comparing counterfactual and interventionist theories, but I think I invented a third thing that doesn't quite fit either camp. I'm calling it "stabilization causation": in a system far from equilibrium, a factor counts as a cause not because flipping it changes the outcome (interventionist) or because its absence yields a different possible world (counterfactual), but because its presence actively dampens perturbations that would otherwise push the system into a different attractor. So the causal work is suppression of divergence, not production of change. I also claimed that Lewis-style counterfactual analysis fails here because the "nearest possible world" without wolves isn't a stable high-elk world but a collapsed one, which makes the counterfactual baseline ambiguous. Am I making a category error by treating system dynamics as causal relations? And is my objection to counterfactuals actually just confusion about ceteris paribus conditions?
