Exam question: Explain Bradley's regress argument against the reality of relations. Then discuss how the problem of the unity of the proposition relates to this regress—specifically, if a judgment is composed of discrete parts (subject, predicate, or concept and object), what binds them into a single thought? In your answer, evaluate whether Russell's distinction between internal and external relations, together with his theory of descriptions, can resolve the regress without collapsing into Bradley's monistic Absolute. Also address whether distributed representational architectures—where content is encoded across activation vectors rather than localized symbols—escape the binding problem by lacking discrete relata.

Student answer: Bradley argues that if relation R connects terms a and b, then further relations R1 and R2 are needed to connect R to a and to b, yielding a vicious infinite regress. From this he concludes that relations are unreal and that only the undifferentiated Absolute exists, in which all distinctions are internal. The problem of propositional unity raises the same difficulty: if a judgment like "Socrates is wise" is composed of discrete parts—the subject, the predicate, and perhaps a copula—then something must bind them, apparently requiring a relating relation and thus the regress. Russell avoids Bradley's monism by adopting internal relations in Bradley's sense: he holds that relations are not independent entities but are reducible to the intrinsic properties of the relata taken together, so that in a complex the terms are already essentially connected. At the same time, Russell's theory of descriptions shows that definite descriptions are not genuine singular terms but quantificational constructions. If we extend this to mental representation, then distributed activation patterns—where content is spread across vectors rather than localized in discrete symbols—do not contain parts that need binding, because every apparent part is already a quantificational structure internal to the whole pattern. So Russell's framework resolves the unity problem without an infinite hierarchy of binders.
