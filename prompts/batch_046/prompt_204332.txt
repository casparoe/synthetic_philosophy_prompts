I am taking a course on philosophy of religion and have been trying to work through Luis de Molina's doctrine of middle knowledge—the claim that God possesses knowledge of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom that is logically prior to any divine decree, and therefore does not causally determine human choices. In our seminar, a sharp disagreement has emerged: some students maintain that middle knowledge successfully reconciles exhaustive divine providence with a robust libertarian account of free will, while others argue that the well-known grounding objection undermines the coherence of such knowledge, since there appears to be nothing in the agent or in God that makes these counterfactuals true prior to the actual decree.

To test these intuitions, I want to introduce an unconventional but precise analogy. Imagine a technological system—call it a counterfactual oracle—that can compute, with perfect accuracy, what every genuinely libertarian-free agent would choose in every conceivable circumstance, yet exerts absolutely no causal influence over those choices. Proponents of the analogy suggest that this shows how perfect foreknowledge can be causally inert and therefore compatible with freedom; critics respond that if the oracle's outputs are fixed and infallible, then the agent's choices are predetermined in all but name, and the analogy actually deepens the problem for Molinism rather than solving it. Write an essay that uses this counterfactual-oracle framework to assess whether Molinism genuinely preserves both divine sovereignty and human libertarian freedom. Carefully weigh the Molinist defense—that middle knowledge is pre-volitional and therefore non-deterministic—against the grounding objection and its analogical extension, which questions whether counterfactuals of freedom have any adequate metaphysical basis. Reach and justify a definite conclusion: either Molinism coherently reconciles providence with libertarian freedom (perhaps strengthened by the oracle analogy), or the combined force of the grounding problem and its technological parallel demonstrates that the reconciliation collapses. Please compose your answer in American English, and do not provide a bibliography or citation list.
