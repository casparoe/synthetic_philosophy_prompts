I'm a PhD student in philosophy of economics, and I'm consulting for a small regional development office that reviews climate-adaptation proposals — shared irrigation, seed banks, early warning systems — in places where standard cost-benefit analysis collapses because people have wildly different risk attitudes and we can't run large surveys. I want to turn Harsanyi's aggregation theorem into a reusable decision procedre the team can actually run: ordered checks for whether individual preference orderings satisfy the independence conditions, rules for constructing a weighted social utility function when interpersonal cardinal comparisons are partly unknown, explicit stop points where the aggregation conditions fail and the answer is 'no,' and hand-off points where the weights depend on value judgments the procedure can't resolve. It needs a small battery of test questions a non-specialist can apply in ten minutes, should flag where substituting ordinal rankings for cardinal ones breaks the theorem's premises, and must include an epistemic veto step — if the team can't establish cardinal utility differences within a factor of three, it defaults to conservative ordinal comparison rather than forcing aggregation. Be explicit about misfires, especially when households aren't unitary or future generations are affected.

Then apply it to two proposals: distributing drought-resistant seeds across households with heterogenious risk profiles, and building communal water storage where benefits are collective but costs fall on individuals. Include one worked example where the procedure gives an answer the agency will probably reject, and say clearly what it can't settle — like whose utility counts inside multi-generational households or how to weight present versus future welfare. I was discussing this with my advsor and we realized nobody has actually operationalized Harsanyi for field-level NGOs, so I'm hoping to fill that gap. Please don't ask me for a bibliography or reading list — the practitioners I'm writing for won't have library access, and I don't want them guessing at citations.
