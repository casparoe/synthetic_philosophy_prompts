As a novelist assembling conceptual prompts, I request a deadpan parody of a federal “Moral Status Grading Directive” issued in the flat voice of bureaucratic compliance. The satire should exaggerate the genuine difficulties of a gradational view—drawing precise thresholds between insects and mammals, weighing a thermostat’s thermal preferences against a child’s, administering proportional rights—without inventing straw arguments. Present it as exactly five numbered regulatory clauses, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion; the clauses should progress from low-grade entities to high-grade ones so that the sustained irony exposes the administrative absurdity of continuous moral scaling. Target the practice of graded moral status itself, not any named philosopher, and keep the irony deadpan: the joke must come from the logic being followed correctly to an uncomfortable endpoint.
