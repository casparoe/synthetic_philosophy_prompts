I've been trying to work through epiphenomenalism—the view that conscious experiences, like feeling pain or seeing red, don't cause any physical effects. I understand the basic worry about behavior: if my pain doesn't cause my brain to make me wince, then my wince is just caused by brain chemistry. But I'm stuck on phenomenal judgment. If the physical brain processes that generate my belief "I am in pain" operate entirely without the actual feeling playing any causal part, then it seems a phenomenal zombie with identical brain activity could form the very same belief despite having no feeling at all. This makes me wonder: is the paradox really about consciousness being left out of the causal chain for self-knowledge, or does it come from assuming that knowing my own mind works like ordinary perception, where the object must cause the belief? Could there instead be a direct structural correlation between a brain state and a belief about that state that doesn't need the experience to push anything? Please explain, in American English, whether that kind of correlation solves the problem or just moves it somewhere else, and whether the core difficulty is that consciousness becomes causally irrelevant even to our knowledge of it.
