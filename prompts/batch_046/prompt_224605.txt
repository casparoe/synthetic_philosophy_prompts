I am currently preparing a seminar module on artificial cognition and have found myself returning repeatedly to the symbol grounding problem, not in its standard formulation but through a specific three-way distinction that does not map cleanly onto the usual internalist-externalist divide. For my purposes, I need to sort positions according to three diagnostic axes: whether grounding requires direct causal coupling with a referent or can be satisfied by functional isomorphism alone; whether social triangulation—other agents correcting misapplication—is constitutively necessary or merely pedagogically useful; and whether a system that has never had direct world-contact but achieves perfect predictive coherence with linguistic reports about that world counts as genuinely grounded or only as simulating grounding. Please construct a decision tree or branching taxonomy built from the logical space of answers to these three questions, not from a list of named philosophers. Each leaf should carry either a recognized label or the mark “unnamed,” followed by one sentence stating its most awkward commitment—specifically, what it must say about a concrete case, such as a language model trained solely on text that correctly predicts pain-reports, or a robot with tactile sensors but no linguistic community. Note any unoccupied combinations of answers and briefly diagnose whether each gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The tree should contain no more than eight leaves, and the branching questions must be phrased so that a respondent could answer them without knowing any philosophical terminology in advance.

To keep the exercise useful for students who will not have access to external sources, I ask that you do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; instead, ground the classification in conceptual distinctions and the concrete cases mentioned above. I am particularly interested in whether any view in this space can consistently deny that predictive coherence without causal coupling is sufficient for grounding, while still allowing that a socially isolated but causally coupled system—say, a single sensor-array in a void—achieves genuine reference. If the logical structure forces certain positions to collapse into one another, flag them explicitly rather than papering over the overlap. The result should function as a “which position do you actually hold?” instrument: someone should be able to trace their intuitive answers through the branches and arrive at a named or unnamed view with its characteristic awkwardness made explicit.
