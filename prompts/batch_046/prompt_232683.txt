Exam Question:
Origin essentialists claim that an individual could not have originated from a different sperm and egg. Critics often invoke monozygotic twinning: if the same fertilized egg can yield two people, then being tied to that particular egg is not sufficient for personal identity. In response, some philosophers have proposed what we might call developmental trajectory essentialism (DTE): the thesis that what is essential is not the genetic material but the particular continuous path of zygotic development and differentiation that produces one organism. Assess whether DTE survives counterexamples from chimerism (specifically tetragametic chimerism, where cells from two separately fertilized eggs contribute to one body), and identify any hidden assumptions in the move from genetic origin to developmental origin.

Student Answer:
The origin essentialist holds that you could not have come from a different sperm and egg. A simple genetic version says your DNA is essential, but this fails because monozygotic twins share DNA yet are distinct persons. So philosophers have moved to developmental trajectory essentialism (DTE): the view that what is essential is not the genetic code but the particular zygotic path of cell division that produces one continuous organism. Under DTE, each twin has a separate trajectory after the split, so the counterexample dissolves.

Chimerism, however, is fatal to DTE. In tetragametic chimerism, two fertilized eggs merge and one person develops with cells from both lineages. This individual therefore has two distinct zygotic trajectories in one body, yet remains a single person. So DTE allows one person to have two origins, breaking the one-to-one relation essentialism requires.

There is also a modal mistake in the original argument. Essentialists claim that if I came from different gametes, I would not exist. But existence is not tied to a particular causal chain; psychological continuity is what matters. A different sperm and egg could have produced a psychologically continuous me, so origin is at best a contingent feature, not an essential one.
