The recipient, Ms. Chen, was suspended for 90 days for missing three mandatory job-training sessions under the Mutual Obligation Agreement, which requires 20 hours weekly of approved activity—formal employment, training, or registered caregiving only. This triggered stricter verification rules, and the caseworker treated her absence explanation as presumptively unverified rather than assessing it on its merits.

The board must choose: uphold the suspension, overturn it outright, or overturn with conditions that allow verified informal care hours to count toward her obligation with a defined verification protocol and a 30-day implementation timeline. Draft the board’s decision memo recommending one concrete course of action. Engage seriously with the recipient’s claim that conditionality is arbitrarily enforced across care boundaries, and also treat the strongest opposing consideration—likely that relaxing verification undermines contractual reciprocity and fraud prevention—honestly rather than dismissively. Take a definite position rather than surveying options; defend your recommendation directly, and include a candid note on where your reasoning is most vulnerable if the risk-score mechanism or the formal-care boundary is defended on procedural-fairness or predictive-accuracy grounds.
