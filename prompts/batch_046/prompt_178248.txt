I am fluent in category theory and dependent type theory — I think naturally in objects, morphisms, universal properties, coherence conditions, and fiber bundles — but I have almost no background in political philosophy. Restate the populist thesis that democratic legitimacy requires a government to express a single, pre-institutional 'will of the people', together with its corollaries that pluralist mediation corrupts that will and that the people constitute a homogeneous body, using only that categorical vocabulary and nothing else. For each element give the honest translation, note where distortion is required, and identify what has no counterpart at all; then say whether the residue indicts the populist claim or reveals a limit of the mathematical framework. Present the result as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the will of the people rendered as a universal property or terminal object; (2) popular sovereignty rendered as an initial-object or uniqueness condition; (3) pluralist institutions rendered as failures of factorization or coherence violations; (4) the homogeneity of the people rendered as indecomposability or connectedness of the object; (5) the residue report stating what remains untranslatable and whether the gap lies in populism or in category theory.
