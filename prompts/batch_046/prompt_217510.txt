Over the past month I have been trying to decide between two postdoctoral positions that are identical in salary, research resources, and geographic desirability, and the experience has left me thinking about whether rational choice can tolerate true ties. Along the way I have also been reading about how artificial agents break symmetry in reward landscapes, which made me wonder whether my own philosophical commitments about decision and freedom are consistent.

Here is the profile I would like audited:
(1) I hold that rational choice requires a complete, strict preference ordering; if two options are genuinely equivalent in value, rationality provides no guidance, and paralysis—like Buridan's ass—is the appropriate result.
(2) I also hold that breaking a tie arbitrarily—by coin flip, alphabetic order, or random noise—is fully rational behavior, not a departure from rationality.
(3) I accept the principle of insufficient reason for epistemic states: symmetric evidence warrants equal credence. But I deny that this principle extends to practical reason; action needs a decision procedure, not a belief about equality.
(4) I am a physical determinist: every choice event, including the initiation of a tie-breaking procedure, is causally determined by prior physical states.
(5) I think libertarian freedom requires that an agent be able to choose between psychologically identical options without that choice being determined by prior differences in mental state.
(6) I think a reinforcement-learning system facing a symmetric reward function should inject stochastic noise to escape local equivalence, and that doing so is rational even though the system has no hidden preference for one branch over the other.
(7) I think the normative status of a decision rule should be invariant to the temporal point at which the agent considers the problem; framing effects that depend solely on when you think about the choice are errors, not rational revisions.

Please go through these commitments and identify which pairs pull against each other, ordering them by severity. For each genuine tension, explain what would have to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me. Identify at least one pair that looks tense but dissolves on closer inspection, explaining which distinction resolves it. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised, would resolve the largest number of conflicts, and write the response in American English.
