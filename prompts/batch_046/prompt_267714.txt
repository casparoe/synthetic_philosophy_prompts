I am a high school student preparing an interview for our school’s philosophy and physics club, and my guest is an unnamed philosopher who defends a strict version of the Rietdijk-Putnam argument for the block universe. Her view is not generic eternalism: she holds that the denial of absolute simultaneity in special relativity dissolves not only temporal becoming but also the explanatory asymmetry between past and future, so that backward-looking and forward-looking causal explanations should be structurally symmetric, with our preference for past-to-future narratives being merely an evolved cognitive bias. I want you to draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate eternalism. The questions should press where the literature actually presses—specifically, on whether the argument survives in general-relativistic spacetimes where global simultaneity surfaces do not exist, on how she responds to quantum entanglement correlations without appealing to a privileged foliation, and on whether the Andromeda-paradox scenario genuinely shows that distant future events are already physically present or only metaphysically indexed. For each major line, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, especially if she attempts to deflect with the claim that an objection is “just a research program, not a problem.” End with one question about what empirical finding—perhaps in quantum gravity or cosmological observation—could actually undermine her position rather than merely adjust its details. The tone should be intellectually serious but accessible to an informed high school audience, with brief annotations noting the anticipated answer and its weakest point. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
