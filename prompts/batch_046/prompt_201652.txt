I am drafting an upper-level exam in philosophy of science on logical empiricism. Write one 90-minute essay question on Otto Neurath's defense of a universal picture-language (Bildersprache) as an intersubjective protocol system. The question must be unanswerable by reciting Neurath's Isotype program or his anti-metaphysical slogans; instead, build in the following conceptual tension. Neurath holds that pictures are physical configurations immediately accessible to all observers, avoiding the privacy of verbal protocol sentences and the metaphysical balloon of correspondence theories. Yet any picture carries empirical content only through conventional choices—scaling, symbol-state mapping, and feature selection—that seem as interpretively loaded as verbal conventions. Ask students to assess whether this conventional dimension undermines Neurath's claim that picture-languages provide a direct, metaphysics-free empirical basis, and require them to contrast his position with Carnap's later syntactic-physicalist treatment of protocol sentences, explaining which view better accommodates convention without collapsing into coherence or correspondence. Provide a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit conceptual moves (with point values totaling 25), specifying for each what an A-level execution looks like, what a B-level execution misses, and a one-line note on the typical student error. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the evaluation should rest on conceptual argument alone.
