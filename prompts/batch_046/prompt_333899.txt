I am redesigning the final problem-set for my graduate philosophy-of-biology seminar, and I want to push past the familiar gene-versus-individual-versus-group framing. I need you to rank the following five proposed units of selection on a single dimension: the strength of their claim to irreducible causal efficacy, measured against the risk of what I will call the aggregation fallacy—treating a statistical summary of lower-level interactions as if it were an agent with independent evolutionary interests.

The candidates are: (1) the selfish replicator at the gene level; (2) the phenotypic interactor (individual or group); (3) the holobiont, conceived as a host-microbiome system; (4) the epigenetically transmitted lineage; and (5) evolutionary selection construed structurally, without discrete unit-boundaries.

Please assign ranks 1 (strongest claim, least fallacy risk) through 5 (weakest claim, greatest fallacy risk). For each, provide a single concise sentence that applies the aggregation-fallacy criterion directly to justify its placement, rather than reciting the unit's standard literature defense. Precision on the conceptual distinction is essential, since I will use these responses to calibrate the grading rubric.
