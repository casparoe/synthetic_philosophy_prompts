As a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, I have been reading about virtue ethics and the situationist critique, and I keep running into an analogy that people on both sides seem to lean on: moral character is said to be like a craft skill—specifically, like developing proficiency in software engineering, where deliberate repetition, mentorship, and code review build stable dispositions that carry across different projects, languages, and teams. Virtue ethicists appear to use this to license the idea that moral education should work like an apprenticeship, and that we can legitimately praise or blame someone for being “skilled” or “unskilled” at virtue regardless of the particular situation they face. Situationists, in turn, cite experimental evidence that minor contextual shifts—time pressure, ambient noise, the presence of bystanders, or even the smell of fresh cookies—drastically alter moral behavior, and they argue this proves character is not like a stable craft skill at all.
