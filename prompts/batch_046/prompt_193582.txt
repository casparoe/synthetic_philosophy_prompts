Please grade the following exam answer. The prompt given to students was: Moral particularists, following Jonathan Dancy, deny that moral reasons behave atomistically: a feature that counts in favor in one case may count against, or be silent, in another. Critics charge that this holism of reasons either collapses into moral relativism or destroys moral justification, since without invariant principles reasoning becomes arbitrary. In about 300 words, (a) explain the holism of reasons with an original example, (b) distinguish particularism precisely from relativism, and (c) evaluate whether particularism undermines justification or merely relocates it.

Here is the student's answer:

(a) Holism means that a feature's moral force changes with context. For instance, causing pain usually counts against an action, but in a dental procedure it is unavoidable and not wrong-making; in warning someone with a painful truth it may even contribute to the rightness of the act. So reasons are not invariant weights—they can switch direction entirely.

(b) Particularism is not relativism. Relativism holds that moral truth varies by culture or framework, allowing contradictory judgments. Particularism allows no such contradiction: within any shared framework, cruelty is always wrong. The particularist just denies that this follows from a master principle. Instead, in each case cruelty functions as a reason against because of the overall shape of the situation. Thus particularism concerns the logic of reasons, not cultural disagreement.

(c) Critics object that without principles, justification collapses into arbitrary intuition. Particularists reply that generalists also depend on judgment to apply principles, so both sides need discernment. Principles are therefore only post-hoc summaries of particular judgments. This shows particularism does not eliminate justification; it relocates it in the agent's attentive perception of each situation's morally relevant features. The critics mistakenly assume that only rule-following counts as reasoning.
