The metaphysics literature on grounding and fundamentality has been stuck for over a decade on whether "grounding" denotes one unified relation of ontological dependence or a family of irreducibly distinct ones—metaphysical, normative, logical, and, more recently proposed, structural. Unity advocates cite shared formal properties (irreflexivity, asymmetry, restricted transitivity) and canonical cases: mental states grounding behavioral dispositions, sets grounding members, parts grounding wholes. Pluralists reply that normative dependence—e.g., an act's wrongness being grounded in its causing suffering—operates with a different explanatory register, and they invoke "structural grounding" as an additional kind: the symmetry properties of a physical system depend on relational structure rather than on any particular entities, yet this dependence does not reduce to standard entity-to-entity grounding. Monists absorb this as grounding with abstract relata; pluralists treat it as a distinct joint. Both sides accept the same formal toolkit and cite overlapping examples; neither has persuaded the other, and the literature circulates between taxonomy and defense without resolution.

My working hypothesis is that the stall is not substantive but structural: "grounding" is being used once as an ontological priority relation and once as an explanation-type classifier, so the sides talk past each other under one label. But I want a diagnosis, not adjudication.
