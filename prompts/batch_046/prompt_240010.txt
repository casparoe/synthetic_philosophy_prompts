I have no formal training in philosophy, but for the past year I have been thinking through an ethical idea on my own that I now understand is close to what some call tranquilism: that the only things which genuinely matter in evaluating any practice or institution are the durable absence of suffering and the durable absence of craving — not happiness, not growth, not social connection for its own sake, and certainly not achievement. I serve as the program director for a small community resource center, and every quarter I must recommend which of our eight programs to maintain, redesign, or discontinue. The board expects a reusable procedure rather than a case-by-case intuition, and I have about thirty minutes and limited data for each evaluation. I want you to translate this absence-first principle into an operational decision procedure I can apply repeatedly: ordered questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, the precise point where the procedure yields a keep, redesign, or discontinue, and the explicit points where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be marked as such. Because I am working with limited information, I also need the procedure to distinguish between what I will call surface absence — participants report feeling okay during and shortly after the program — and structural absence — the program does not generate new cravings, comparisons, or dependencies that persist or intensify after participation ends. Please include a small set of practical tests I can run without specialized training, specify the hard cases this distinction creates, and apply the full procedure to three programs: a grief-support circle, a competitive job-skills bootcamp that participants praise highly, and a popular social club that I suspect breeds status comparison despite its cheerful atmosphere. One of these three should produce a recommendation that conflicts with my own emotional attachment or with the board's likely preference, and you should flag it. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do — which kinds of absence it will miss, where it is vulnerable to my own biases as the person running it, and what kinds of decisions it must refuse to make.
