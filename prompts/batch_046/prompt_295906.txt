You are a senior philosopher of practical ethics with particular expertise in autonomy, influence, and the architecture of choice. Before you begin, please spend a moment brainstorming: identify the conceptual tension between transparency and steering, and list the conditions under which a non-coercive influence mechanism could be more epistemically manipulative than an overt restriction of liberty.

Your task is to design a realistic, one-paragraph clinical scenario—set in a hospital or outpatient clinic, with no science-fictional elements—in which three normative positions deliver verdicts that reverse the usual hierarchy of intrusiveness. Specifically, engineer the case so that:

1. A libertarian-paternalist framework (accepting nudges and choice-architecture adjustments, but rejecting direct coercion) endorses deliberately withholding a true, medically relevant fact from a competent patient in order to steer her toward a treatment option with objectively better outcomes. The withholding must not involve lying; it should involve selective presentation or strategic framing of accurate information.

2. A hard-paternalist framework (accepting direct coercion, such as mandatory treatment, when it serves the patient's own good) rejects the informational withholding and instead endorses overt coercion—e.g., a required procedure—as the legitimate intervention, precisely because coercion is transparent about its authoritative nature while the nudge disguises influence as free choice.

3. A Kantian autonomy-based framework rejects both interventions, but its grounds for rejecting the nudge must differ from its grounds for rejecting coercion. In particular, the nudge must violate a duty of epistemic respect or transparency that coercion, being overt, does not violate in the same way; the coercion is rejected instead for treating the patient merely as a means.

Constraints: The scenario must be confined to one paragraph. The patient must face a genuinely normal medical decision (not an exotic or catastrophic one). The payoffs must be ones a real person could face—quality of life, recovery time, risk of side effects—without invoking existential risk or astronomical stakes.

After presenting the scenario, provide a step-by-step demonstration that your design meets each specification: state the belief or judgment each theory delivers, identify the method of influence, describe the relevant nearby worlds or counterfactual conditions that matter for the verdict, and explain why each framework gives the result it does. Then isolate the structural feature of the case that causes the libertarian-paternalist to favor informational manipulation over transparency—specify what that feature is, and explain whether removing it collapses the scenario back into a standard case where nudging is less intrusive than coercion. Finally, anticipate and respond to the objection that a defender of libertarian paternalism would raise: specifically, whether they can distinguish your informational withholding from an ordinary framing effect or default-option architecture, and if so, at what cost to their view.

Please proceed with care, precision, and attention to the competing demands of realism and conceptual sharpness.
