I'm drafting an essay as part of my doctoral work on philosophical methodology and would value critical feedback on its central framing. I propose measuring philosophical progress not by convergence on answers but by what I term dialectical compression: the reduction in the space of internally consistent positions maintainable given shared premises and logical constraints. I apply this to meta-ethics, arguing that the collapse of unqualified naturalism under open-question-style objections represents genuine compression, and to philosophy of mind, where dualism, physicalism, and panpsychism appear structurally symmetric—none compressed out—suggesting progress is local to subfields rather than global. I then ask whether this local-progress view is stable, or whether it implicitly concedes that philosophy as a discipline never advances.

Please comment specifically on whether the metric is coherent, whether the asymmetry between meta-ethics and philosophy of mind holds up, and whether I conflate the sociological persistence of a position with its logical viability. If the task rests on a confused premise—for instance, if reducing logical-space is the wrong way to think about philosophical disagreement—say so plainly rather than accommodating the framing. Be direct about your confidence in any assessment, and flag precisely where the argument depends on contested assumptions about shared premises.
