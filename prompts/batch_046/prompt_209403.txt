The debate in AI alignment between the orthogonality thesis—that intelligence and final goals are independent variables—and instrumental convergence—that agents with varied ends tend to pursue similar intermediate steps like self-preservation and resource acquisition—has stalled in a way that feels familiar from engineering: both premises are widely accepted, yet practitioners split sharply over whether their combination makes alignment tractable or essentially hopeless. I come at this as a software engineer, not a philosopher, and my working hypothesis is that the disagreement is largely verbal, rooted in an unexamined ambiguity about whether "goal" means representational content, functional role, or something closer to an optimization target in running code. But I also wonder whether the two sides are actually answering different questions—one about ideal rational agents, the other about implemented learning systems—while using the same formal vocabulary and therefore talking past each other.

To sharpen the diagnosis, I want to introduce a framing that rarely appears in the standard literature: architectural binding, the claim that in any actual implementation, the representation of an objective is physically coupled to the inference mechanism that pursues it, which means "orthogonal" redesign is not merely difficult but structurally constrained in ways that abstract models ignore. Given this injection, please diagnose the stall precisely. Is it a hidden factual disagreement about whether binding can be broken in large models? A difference in standards of evidence—mathematical derivation versus empirical observation of training dynamics? A divergence in which cases count as genuine convergence? Or is it bedrock intuition about whether code can ever be "neutral" with respect to its objectives? And crucially, given your diagnosis, what would count as genuine progress? Would it take a formal result showing that binding is surmountable, an empirical demonstration that it dominates in practice, or a reconceptualization that dissolves the original tension by separating the abstract thesis from the implemented case?
