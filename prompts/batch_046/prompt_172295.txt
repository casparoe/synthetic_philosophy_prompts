hey so i'm trying to get my thoughts straight on price controls for a seminar i'm running and i want a consistance check before i open my mouth. here's where i stand, more or less:

(1) price controls are only ethically okay when they fix a coordination failure that stops people from reaching a deal they'd both want — pure redistribution through price-fixing violates the autonomy of the parties. (2) minimum wage is defensable because labor markets have monopsony power and search frictions that suppress wages below the cooperative surplus, so the floor actually helps more exchanges happen, not fewer. (3) rent control isn't defensible the same way because housing supply is inelastic in the short run and there's no analogous coordination failure; the cap just moves surplus from landlords to tenants. (4) i take deadweight loss seriously when it blocks transactions both sides want, but in labor markets i don't think the "blocked jobs" (where wage > productivity) are real losses — the worker's reservation wage is distorted by desperation, not autonomous preference, so those aren't exchanges we should mourn. (5) but i also reject the idea that desperation invalidates consent in general: if someone takes a low-wage job out of desperation, that's still a valid contract, and the state shouldn't second-guess it unless there's actual coercion. (6) i do support soft rent stabilization (tying increases to inflation) but not hard caps, because stabilization regulates an ongoing dependency relationship rather than setting a spot-market price — there's a temporal assymetry in reservation prices when the contract is relational rather than one-off.

i also have this side idea about the spot-versus-relational distinction: that once a contract is running, the weaker party gets locked into a dependency that makes their reservation price artificially low, which justifies regulating the terms but not the initial price. i think that applies to tenants but not to workers because employment is always at-will and therefore never relational in the same way.
