Provide your credence, as a specific number between 0 and 1, that the Epicurean argument succeeds in showing death is not a harm to the one who dies, evaluated under a non-standard framing in which the usual condition that there is no subject present to suffer harm is replaced by a diachronic interest condition. By this I mean the thesis that a person at time t holds a non-experiential, trajectory-based interest in the continuation of their psychological life—an interest not reducible to present welfare states, not requiring metaphysical identity with a future self, but grounded instead in the normative continuity of a constructed narrative. Decompose your reasoning into four parts with approximate probabilities: (a) that the standard Epicurean objection succeeds absent this framing; (b) that the diachronic interest condition is metaphysically coherent and genuinely applies to the deceased; (c) that, if (b) holds, the frustration of such an interest counts as a harm to the deceased rather than only as a loss for survivors; and (d) that any harm of this kind, should it exist, is comparable enough to experiential harms to undermine the Epicurean conclusion that death is of no concern to the deceased. State the aggregate credence with brief justification for each term, indicate how they combine, identify which term your final number is most sensitive to, and specify what argument or piece of evidence would most move that term.
