I am currently completing a senior-level philosophy elective at my high school, where our instructor has structured the final quarter around comparative moral responsibility. We have spent several weeks working through translated primary texts—without access to original Sanskrit or Pali sources, only classroom translations—on the classical Indian conceptions of karma. Through these discussions I have developed the view that karma, particularly in its Abhidharmic elaboration, is not best read as a bare causal law of action and consequence, but rather as a structured theory of moral desert: the qualitative character of an agent's intentions is preserved in some form of karmic potential and eventually distributed as proportionate outcomes, independently of any theistic administrator. For my capstone presentation I need to defend this desert interpretation against the competing naturalistic reading, which treats karma as an entirely impersonal mechanism with no normative tracking of merit or blame.

Rather than delivering a conventional argumentative essay, my instructor has asked me to prepare as though I were mediating a good-faith adversarial collaboration between the Desert Camp and the Naturalist Camp. I want you to draft the joint statement they could both sign. Please structure it in three parts: first, a numbered section of agreed claims about karma's basic architecture in classical Abhidharmic thought, especially concerning the storage of intention-quality and the phenomenon of delayed fruition; second, a disagreements table that formulates each camp's position using wording that its own members would accept without feeling misrepresented; and third, a precise statement of the central crux—whether the delay between action and result, what I will call karmic latency, undermines the claim that karma genuinely tracks desert rather than mere causation. For that crux, include a shared test case that both sides agree is relevant, such as an agent who performs a clearly virtuous act but suffers immediate misfortune, with the corresponding karmic result said to ripen only in a subsequent life, and record each side's predicted interpretation of that case in advance.

Please do not include any bibliography, reading list, or references to specific modern scholars I should consult, since I am preparing this without university database access and want the reasoning to rest entirely on conceptual analysis. Also, please phrase the objections and formulations exactly as members of each camp would phrase them, not as caricatures, and keep the tone formal and suitable for presentation to my instructor and peers.
