i've been messing with the socialist calculation debate and i think i found a weird middle ground that doesn't get talked about. my position: hayek's right that central planners can't hold dispersed local knowledge in a form they can compute with, but lange's trial-and-error model doesn't need them to if we stop treating prices as information signals and start treating them as stability basins. planners just run a revision protocol — cut price when surplus accumulates — and let the system converge to an attractor. the distributed knowledge is in the trajectory, not in anyone's head.

but here's my injected twist: call it epistemic closure under revision. the idea is that once a price vector survives N iterations with no exogenous shock, it implicitly contains every local adjustment that was available, so the planner can treat the result as authoritative without ever extracting the underlying preferences. this blocks hayek's claim that planners lack the knowledge to compute prices, because they never compute them; they just stabilize centraly.
