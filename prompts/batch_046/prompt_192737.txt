I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind drafting an essay on self-reference and explanatory hierarchy. I take Escher’s Drawing Hands not merely as an illustration of strange loops but as a visual argument that certain hierarchical structures can be co-constituted without an inviolate base level. To develop this, I distinguish three varieties of tangled hierarchy: representational (the image depicts its own creation), causal (reciprocal feedback in dynamical systems), and normative (standards of correctness that apply to a system evaluating itself). I propose that Drawing Hands exemplifies primarily the representational kind, whereas conscious self-modeling involves all three intertwined in what I call “mutual ontogenesis”—a localized region where vehicle and content, or substrate and representation, are reciprocally determinative rather than unidirectionally grounded.

In the essay’s central section I apply this framework to mental representation. If a high-level self-model and its low-level neural substrate are locked in a loop of reciprocal determination, then the standard vehicle/content distinction collapses locally without collapsing globally; other mental contents might remain hierarchically flat while the self-model forms a tangled knot. I then address a physicalist objection: that neurons firing still constitute an inviolate physical level grounding the loop. I attempt to defuse this by distinguishing “grounding in” (the substrate in which realization occurs) from “grounding for” (epistemic or normative priority). The neurons are the medium in which the loop is realized, I argue, but they are not conceptually or normatively prior to the self-model they help constitute.

My draft notes are currently rough, especially the middle argument for localization—claiming that tangling is confined to self-representation and does not spread to all cognition. I would value comments on the coherence of the three-way distinction; whether “mutual ontogenesis” genuinely advances the discussion or merely rebrands familiar emergence talk; whether the “grounding in / grounding for” response addresses the physicalist worry or just shifts vocabulary; and whether my localization strategy succeeds or invites a slippery slope in which every mental state eventually becomes tangled. I am also unsure whether the move from a visual artwork to neural dynamics is analogically sound or risks overextending the image’s conceptual purchase.
