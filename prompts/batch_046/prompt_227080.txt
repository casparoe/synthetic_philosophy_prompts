I've recently gone mostly plant-based and now I cant stop thinking about whether eating meat is wrong when my one meal obviosly makes no difference to factory farming. I think the right view is that contributing to collective harm is wrong not because of causal efficacy but because you're opting into a harmful cooperative practice — like joining a crowd that's pushing a boulder, even if your own shove is redundant and the boulder rolls either way. But I want to know what would actually count against this. What if there's no shared intention behind factory farming, just dispersed consumers and producers? Could unavoidable participation excuse it? And is there a danger this makes every tiny purchase morally loaded in a paralysing way? I'd like an honest assessment: which objections are fatal, which are merely awkward, whether defenders of this view should concede anything, and whether I've just insulated myself by redefining harm rather than facing the inefficacy objection head on.
