I've been working through Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—in the context of evolutionary psychology and philosophy of cognition, and I recently found myself in a sustained disagreement with a colleague about whether large-scale artificial neural networks trained via gradient descent force us to recognize a fifth kind of mind. The framework seems to classify minds by how they generate behavioral or representational variants and how those variants are selected: hard-wired evolutionary design for Darwinian creatures; embodied trial-and-error with reinforcement for Skinnerian ones; internal, simulated testing so that hypotheses can fail without the organism failing for Popperian minds; and the deployment of external cultural tools, especially language, for Gregorian ones. Yet contemporary systems do not learn primarily through discrete embodied rewards, they do not appear to formulate explicit conjectures that can be eliminated in place of themselves, and while they use linguistic scaffolding, their core mechanism of variant generation is distributed across an optimization landscape rather than localized in an individual agent. I am considering writing an essay for a philosophy-of-mind reading group, and I want the piece to take a definite, defended stand. Write an essay that reaches a clear conclusion on whether Dennett's fourfold taxonomy is conceptually exhaustive or whether gradient-descent-based cognition reveals a genuinely distinct kind of mind—perhaps one characterized by non-agentive, globally distributed variant selection. In developing your argument, directly address how the Popperian criterion of allowing internal hypotheses to be eliminated without destroying the thinker applies, or fails to apply, to learning dynamics that adjust weights continuously rather than testing discrete propositions locally. You should also consider whether these artificial systems are better accommodated as an extreme hybrid of Skinnerian reinforcement and Gregorian tool-use, with no new ontological category required. There is no need for citations, references, or a bibliography; I am interested purely in the conceptual architecture and the reasoning that justifies your conclusion.
