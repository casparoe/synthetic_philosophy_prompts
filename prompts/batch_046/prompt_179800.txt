I'm helping design an interdisciplinary seminar on philosophy of psychiatry, and we've been discussing a practice I noticed at a regional mental-health consortium: for legal and administrative determinations—such as involuntary-commitment hearings or disability-benefit appeals—the consortium relies strictly on DSM categorical codes, treating these as if they pick out stable, pre-existing conditions independent of the patient's self-interpretation. Yet within the same clinics, the standard of care now requires clinicians to disclose the diagnosis to the patient, explain that it is provisional, and invite the patient to accept, resist, or reframe the label in an ongoing diagnostic dialogue. The patient's responses—whether they internalize the category, reject it, or reshape their behavior around it—are documented in a separate, evolving narrative field in the electronic record, but they never alter the static administrative code used for legal and billing purposes. The result is a bifurcated record: one layer treated as a mind-independent classification for the state, another treated as an interactive, looping construct within the therapeutic relationship.

This arrangement puzzles me. It seems to presuppose that psychiatric kinds can be both natural kinds for the law and interactive kinds in Hacking's sense for the clinic, without explaining how the same institution can endorse both ontologies simultaneously. What theory of psychiatric classification does this dual-track practice actually presuppose? Must it assume that looping effects are real but confined to the clinical sphere, leaving administrative reality untouched? Or can it be reconstructed as a pragmatic division of labor that does not commit to any deep ontology at all? I want you to reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely fits it comfortably, and identify where the practice is philosophically underdetermined—such that rival views, say a thoroughgoing social constructionism or a robust biological realism, could each license some version of it. Finally, tell me what would have to change in the institution's record-keeping, legal reporting, or clinical protocol if we replaced its implicit view with one that treated looping effects as penetrating the administrative layer itself, or conversely with one that denied them any clinical relevance.
