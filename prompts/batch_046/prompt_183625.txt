Write a villanelle about the metaphysics of persistence—endurantism versus perdurantism—using the two refrains “I am wholly present now” and “I am the sum of stages spread through time.” Structure the five tercets so that the first introduces the endurantist claim that an object exists in its entirety at each moment it occupies; the second and third tercets should present the problem of temporary intrinsics (for instance, being bent at one time and straight at another while remaining numerically the same) and the perdurantist reply that incompatible properties belong to distinct temporal parts, borne only derivatively by the four-dimensional whole. In the fourth tercet, inject the stage-theoretic complication—if only instantaneous stages are fundamental, then the ordinary “I” may denote nothing more than a present stage, with diachronic identity reconstructed via counterpart relations among stages rather than by a continuing subject—before the closing quatrain brings both refrains into direct collision without resolving them. Let the villanelle’s repeating A1 and A2 lines do philosophical work: each return should feel like the same claim reasserting itself against its rival, and the rhyme scheme should make the two descriptions interlock rather than harmonize. Make the poem accurate enough that a metaphysician of persistence recognizes the standard machinery—wholly present entities, temporal parts, derivative property-bearing, relational versus intrinsic accounts of change, and the stage view—rather than treating these as mere labels. After the poem, include a brief note separating what is broadly settled in the literature (that endurantism denies temporal parts and takes persistence to involve strict identity over time; that perdurantism analyzes persistence analogously to spatial extension via parts) from what remains genuinely contested (whether exdurantism is a form of perdurantism or a separate position; whether relational strategies for temporary intrinsics are adequate or ad hoc; whether the stage view eliminates or merely relocates the need for diachronic identity). Do not present any contested interpretation—such as that endurantism is incoherent, that perdurantism is the default contemporary view, or that the stage view solves all problems of change—as the consensus position.
