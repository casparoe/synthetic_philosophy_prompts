I have been thinking about Dewey's claim that democracy is essentially a mode of social inquiry rather than a mechanism for aggregating preferences. If that is correct, it seems democratic institutions ought to mirror the logic of scientific investigation: policy proposals should be treated as working hypotheses, citizens ought to function as peer reviewers who can block adoption on evidentiary grounds, and there should be something like an epistemic escrow—a mandatory period of suspended judgment—until objections have been registered and addressed. But this makes me uneasy. In science, peer review often ends in persistent disagreement without a binding verdict, whereas democracy must eventually authorize action. So am I wrong to think that Dewey wants democratic decisions to be falsifiable in the same way scientific claims are? Or is the confusion elsewhere—perhaps in treating cooperative problem-solving as if it required the same kind of evidentiary gatekeeping as a laboratory?
