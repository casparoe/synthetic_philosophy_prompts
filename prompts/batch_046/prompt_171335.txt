Here are notes for a short essay on Molyneux’s problem. The standard framing asks whether a person born blind, who learns to distinguish a cube from a sphere by touch, could immediately do so by sight once cured. I want to argue that this framing conceals a deeper issue about whether geometric concepts are modality-independent but format-bound. On my view, the blind subject does possess a genuine geometric concept of cubeness—one that is not merely tactile but genuinely spatial—yet this concept is indexed to a proprioceptive coordinate frame. When sight is restored, the retinal image delivers geometric information in an egocentric visual space that has not yet been calibrated against his existing spatial schema. So his initial failure to recognize the shapes is not evidence that his concepts are modality-specific, but rather that cross-modal recognition requires a translation layer that takes time to establish. I further suggest that work on sensory substitution—where tactile arrays are mapped onto visual cortex—supports this interpretation: subjects initially fail to interpret the substituted input geometrically, but gradually develop calibrated spatial concepts that bridge the modalities. I am unsure whether this genuinely resolves Molyneux’s problem or merely relocates it: does eventual success show that the original concept was always cross-modal, or that a new, visually indexed concept was constructed?

Please comment on whether this reframing is philosophically coherent and whether it misrepresents what is known about sensory substitution and cross-modal plasticity. In particular, please separate what is well-established—such as the basic mechanisms of cortical remapping—from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers of perception and cognitive scientists, especially regarding whether geometric concepts can be format-independent. Do not present any contested interpretation, such as the claim that sensory substitution demonstrates unified amodal spatial representations, as the consensus view. Also indicate whether the argument is interesting, and point to any place where I seem to assume without argument that a proprioceptive frame is conceptually prior to a visual one.
