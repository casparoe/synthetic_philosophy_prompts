I've come round to the view that Bertrand's paradox doesn't really hurt the principle of indifference, so long as we tie the paramater space to the physical process that actually generates the chord—say, throwing a stick onto a circle—so the right distribution is just 1/3 and the other answers come from mixing up causally different setups. What'd actually count against this? I want the evidence and arguments that should shift me, sorted by how decisive they'd be—including whether there's a formal result showing you can't uniquely pick the generating process from symmetry alone, or cases where one physical setup genuinely supports two different paramaterisations. Has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability—if every counter-example gets dismissed as the wrong causal story, is that a problem? And be honest about whether defenders of this line would concede anything as a real defeater, not just what critics say.
