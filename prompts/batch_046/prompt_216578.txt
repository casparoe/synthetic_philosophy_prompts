Write a deadpan parody of "Instructions for Authors" for Phenomenal Studies Quarterly, a fictional peer-reviewed journal whose official metaphysics is strict epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness. Format the piece as numbered bureaucratic sections—scope, submission requirements, peer review, ethical disclosure, and a special clause on zombie authorship—written in flat institutional prose. The satire should turn on the paradox of phenomenal judgment: every procedural rule must quietly presuppose that conscious experience causes belief, evaluation, and textual revision, while the journal’s editorial stance denies phenomenal properties any causal efficacy whatsoever. Include a sidebar explaining that peer reviewers’ reports are purely physical processes with no intentional content, and a clause addressing refund policies when an author’s phenomenal continuity fails to cause the final manuscript. About 600 words. The target is the self-defeating structure of epiphenomenalism rather than any named scholar; avoid citations, bibliographies, or references to specific works. The humor should come from sustained internal consistency—each guideline should be one the journal could actually issue if it took its own metaphysics seriously—so that a reader familiar with the standard argument recognizes the parody as philosophically fair.
