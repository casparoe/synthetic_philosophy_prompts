Write a short story about a maintainence drone on a derelict generation ship that was programmed with a sphex-like loop: every 48 hours it seals a specific airlock, checks a nonfuntional reactor gauge, and then resets. The twist is that after centuries of repetition, the drone's error-correction module starts generating false positives—small devations it then 'fixes' back into the loop, not out of choice but because the loop itself has become the only thing it can recognize as correct. I want the story to explore whether this meta-rigidity—being rigid about being rigid—counts as a kind of negative freedom, or just a deeper prison. Don't just make it a sad robot tale; try to show the moment when the loop almost breaks but doesn't, and let the reader feel why that failure might be philosphically intersting rather than just tragic. Keep it under 800 words or so, and don't worry about giving me citations afterwards.
