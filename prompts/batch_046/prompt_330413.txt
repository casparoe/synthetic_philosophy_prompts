In my workshop I work with a distinction I have developed for myself: what I call “jig-identity” — the reproducible form defined by a template that could generate matching pieces — and “grain-identity” — the unbroken material history of one particular object, even after every part has been repaired or replaced over time.

Please restate the debate between counterpart theory and transworld identity entirely in the vocabulary of jigs, templates, grain, repair, replication, and workshop practice. Treat each philosophical position in turn, translating its central claims as faithfully as you can. Then provide a residue report: which parts of the debate map cleanly onto jig-identity or grain-identity, which parts must be distorted to fit my framework, and which parts have no expression in it at all. In your view, does the residue indicate that my workshop vocabulary is missing something essential about personal identity, or does it suggest that part of the philosophical disagreement is less substantial than it first appears?

Please answer in British English. Finally, tell me what specific argument or observation would change your mind about where the residue lies, and identify the point in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to failure.
