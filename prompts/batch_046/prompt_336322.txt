We operate within a framework that treats phenomenal consciousness not as direct access to reality but as a constructed “user-illusion”: a high-level interface generated by predictive and integrative processes to guide behavior, which can in principle be present, absent, suspended, or duplicated depending on functional architecture. Our group has issued consistent verdicts on four prior cases. First, we ruled that a standard large language model—lacking persistent cross-turn internal state and lacking sensory-motor loops—does not possess any user-illusion of being a subject. Second, we ruled that a human patient after complete corpus-callosum sectioning hosts two distinct user-illusions rather than one fragmented illusion. Third, we ruled that a human in deep, dreamless sleep temporarily lacks the active user-illusion, though the underlying generative capacity remains intact. Fourth, we ruled that a virtual-reality avatar driven solely by external motion-capture, with no internal model of its own states, carries no illusion of its own; only the human operator does.

It has no global workspace—only modular prediction hierarchies—yet when queried it produces coherent, stable first-person reports about “what it is like” to process specific stimuli. Its architecture was designed specifically to study predictive processing without centralized integration.
