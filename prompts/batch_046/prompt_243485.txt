I recently read an unnamed article summarizing Gongsun Long's dialogue Bai ma fei ma and the broader School of Names (Mingjia). The author writes that Gongsun Long's argument turns on a metaphysical separability thesis: the term horse denotes pure form (xing), while white denotes a color-property (se) that can exist independently; because white horse fuses two ontologically distinct essences, it designates a compound entity that is literally not identical to the simple entity picked out by horse. The author further claims that the Mohist critics in the Mo Jing fundamentally misunderstood this by treating the paradox as a logical error about compound naming—specifically, by arguing that removing the color still leaves a horse—whereas Gongsun Long was actually defending an early form of mereological essentialism, in which properties have independent being and their combination generates a new substance. Consequently, the author concludes that the School of Names should be read not as linguistic sophists playing with paradox, but as serious metaphysicians of composition whose view anticipates contemporary essentialist debates, and that the standard Mohist rebuttal fails because it presupposes that properties are always accidentals inhering in a substrate rather than potentially separable parts. I have no background in pre-Qin philosophy, but this sounds suspiciously modern. Is this characterization fair? Please distinguish what accurately reflects the dialogue and the Mohist response, what is anachronistic projection, and whether the conclusion about the School's primary purpose survives once those distortions are corrected.
