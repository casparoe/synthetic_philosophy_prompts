I am preparing a podcast interview with an unnamed contemporary metaphysician—call her Dr. A. Voss—who has developed what she terms “framework-recursive Madhyamaka.” On her view, Nāgārjuna’s catuskoti, the fourfold negation of being, non-being, both, and neither, should not be read primarily as an ontological claim about the absence of svabhāva in particular entities. Instead, she treats it as a second-order structural constraint: any conceptual framework that attempts to stabilize a distinction between conventional designation (samvṛti) and ultimate reality (paramārtha) will, upon recursive self-application, undermine the very coherence conditions it needs to stand. Emptiness, she argues, is therefore not a property discovered in things but a feature of framework stabilization itself—what she calls “emptiness of the frame.” The audience is philosophically literate but not specialist in Buddhist philosophy or formal metaphysics.

Draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that avoid biography, broad summaries, or requests for reading lists. I want each question to turn on actual content and to anticipate the reply she is most likely to give, with an immediate follow-up keyed to that reply. Specifically: include at least one question pressing the self-refutation worry—that if all framing collapses recursively, then her own framework-recursive thesis should collapse too—followed by the follow-up she will likely deploy (perhaps applying the conventional/ultimate distinction to itself) and then the counter-follow-up that exposes the tension in that maneuver. Include at least one question on conventional truth: if conventional designations are dependently originated yet not merely erroneous, how does her view preserve the normative force of ordinary discourse without smuggling in a hidden svabhāva of language? Anticipate her probable answer—that conventional truths are pragmatically warranted but ontologically ungrounded—and craft the immediate follow-up. Also include a comparison with the classical prasanga method: does treating the tetralemma as a framework constraint rather than as a dialectical reductio alter the logical status of Nāgārjuna’s negations? Provide the follow-up keyed to her likely defense that prasanga itself presupposes the very framework stability her view denies. Conclude with a question about vulnerability: since she denies that emptiness is an empirical claim about entities, is there any development in formal semantics, modal logic, or conceptual analysis that could genuinely threaten her position, or has she rendered framework-recursive Madhyamaka immune to counterexample by definitional fiat? Each question should be substantive, sequential, and designed for an interview setting rather than an exam or lecture.
