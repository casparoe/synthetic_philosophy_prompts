There's an analogy floating round some econ-philosophy threads that Aristotle's theory of exchange is basically like a potluck: each household brings what it naturally produces (bread, shoes, whatever) and swaps till needs are covered, but no one shows up with an empty bowl just to hoard other people's dishes. People use it to claim modern investment banking is just that empty-bowl move, and therefore Aristotle would see it as unnatural chrematistike rather than proper oikonomike. How far does the potluck picture actually hold? Lay out the correspondance table - host, dishes, empty bowl, leftovers, the rule against hoarding - then the breaks, especially where money's sterility and the idea of need-based exchange stop matching. Does the 'banking is category error' conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the anology smuggling in the anti-usury sentiment all along?
