I was discussing Popper’s philosophy of science with a colleague who offered the following summary of his views on Darwinism, and I want to check whether it holds together. She claimed that Popper began by treating Darwin’s theory of natural selection as a purely logical tautology—defining fitness circularly through survival—thereby denying evolutionary biology any empirical content and excluding it from genuine science altogether. On her account, Popper maintained this exclusion consistently until he encountered the modern evolutionary synthesis, at which point he recognized that Mendelian genetics and population genetics had supplied an independent mechanistic framework that made selection predictions genuinely risky and falsifiable; this, she said, led him to fully retract his tautology objection and welcome Darwinism back into empirical science, essentially conceding that his original critique had been a product of pre-genetic ignorance. She added a further twist: she suggested that part of Popper’s initial hostility came from treating evolutionary narratives as a form of biological historicism—an attempt to derive unconditional prophecies about the future course of species—which he rejected in The Poverty of Historicism for the same reasons he rejected Marxist historical laws.

The summary sounds tidy, but I worry it may conflate Popper’s actual epistemic categorization with a simpler science-versus-non-science binary, and that it may overstate both the completeness of his reversal and the relevance of his anti-historicist writings to biology. Could you evaluate this characterization claim by claim? Specifically, I want to know: whether Popper ever truly “excluded” evolutionary biology from science or rather assigned it a different status (such as a metaphysical research programme); whether his later remarks about tautology amount to a full retraction or only a qualified correction; whether genetics actually played the decisive role in his change of view that the summary implies; and whether the connection to historicism is a legitimate extension of his philosophy or an imported framing that the texts do not support. Please distinguish accurate representation, common simplification, and misrepresentation, and tell me whether the summary’s conclusion—that Popper’s critique is now a resolved dead issue—follows from the corrected parts. If my request assumes a cleaner narrative arc in Popper’s writings than actually exists, or if auditing a second-hand summary requires more precision about sources than I have provided, please say so plainly rather than accommodating the framing.
