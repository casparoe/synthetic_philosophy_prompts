I teach an upper-level philosophy of history and comparative sociology seminar, and I am constructing the final examination. I need a single essay question—designed for 45 minutes, with an expected response of roughly 800 words—that interrogates whether the contrastive formulation "Why Britain and not China?" is conceptually well posed in explaining the Great Divergence. The question must not be answerable by reciting standard summaries of the Needham question or by listing familiar empirical factors (coal, colonies, institutions). Instead, it should force students to isolate a specific logical or methodological defect in macro-historical contrastive explanation—such as the implicit teleological commitment that treats industrialization as a predetermined endpoint rather than a contingent branching path—and then apply that defect to an analysis of two institutional mechanisms: the Qing civil-service examination system's allocation of elite human capital, and the British patent system's incentive structure for incremental innovation. The question should further ask students to evaluate whether introducing a geographical counterfactual (e.g., relocating Britain's coal deposits) resolves the framing defect or merely displaces it onto a different contingent variable. Please provide: (1) the exam question itself; (2) a model answer of approximately 600 words that demonstrates the required conceptual identification, applies it to both mechanisms, and explains why the counterfactual does not salvage the original framing; and (3) a marking scheme out of 20 points that distinguishes an A-range answer—which must explicitly name the teleology/contingency problem, use both institutional examples correctly, and diagnose the counterfactual's limitations—from a B-range answer—which describes the mechanisms accurately but treats the framing issue as purely empirical rather than conceptual.
