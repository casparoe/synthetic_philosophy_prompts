Two people at my local rationalist meetup got stuck arguing about Dennett's three stances, specifically whether the design stance is genuinely independent or just a watered-down intentional stance for artifacts. (1) Jordan said that if a pattern is real enough to justify treating a heart or thermostat as having a purpose, it's already real enough to justify treating it as having beliefs about its environment — just coarser ones. He said the only difference is the complexity of the compression scheme, not the kind of pattern, so the stances are really points on a spectrum. (2) Riley pushed back: the design stance works because we know the etiology (someone built it for X), not because internal states have aboutness. She said treating a thermostat as "wanting" 72 degrees requires attributing representational content that its bimetallic strip lacks, and without that aboutness structure you're not in the intentional stance at all, just using functional shorthand. (3) Jordan came back with a niche idea he called "counterfactual compression density" — he said if you can predict the thermostat's behavior across novel failures using only its functional spec but not using any model of its "beliefs" about temperature, then the pattern you're tracking isn't the kind Dennett requires for intentionality. He claimed this proves the design stance tracks its own kind of real pattern. (4) Riley replied that this just means the intentional stance fails for the thermostat, not that the design stance succeeds independently; it's just physical-stance prediction plus our knowledge of the builder's intent. She added that if Jordan is right and design patterns are weak intentional patterns, then biology collapses into psychology — we'd have to say the heart literally has beliefs about pumping blood, just simple ones. Jordan said that's not a collapse but recognizing functional attribution comes in degrees. They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't.
