There's this old debate about Tolstoys What Is Art? that just keeps going in circles — is he giving a real definition of art or just preaching about good Christian art? One camp reads the infection idea literally: art is emotional transmission, full stop, and thats why he lists jokes and church services alongside paintings. The other camp says he's smuggling in radical Christian ethics so he can trash Shakespeare, Wagner, and most of his own novels. I feel like nobody moves because theyre answering different questions but using the same word art. Heres a twist I want you to inject: the distinction between horizontal infection, where feeling moves peer-to-peer in collective participation, and vertical infection, where an artist broadcasts to passive consumers. Nobody in the Tolstoy literature really uses that framing, but it might explain why he praises folk songs and religious icons while dismissing opera and symponies. So diagnose it properly: is this verbal, substantive, or are there hidden empirical claims about emotional spread? Are the sides really doing ontology versus cultural hygiene? And what would count as progress — a new conceptual distinction, a look at his 1890s drafts, or is the whole thing ill-posed? I want the disagreement mapped with what each side actually says, not just another literature survey.
