Please provide a concrete scenario—perhaps involving a character who exhibits spontaneous compassion in one context but sustained cruelty in another—where the two frameworks yield not merely different moral evaluations, but different ontological descriptions of what is happening inside the agent. The example should turn on a distinction that is genuinely in the weeds: not the familiar caricature of “nature is good versus nature is bad,” but something closer to whether moral failure represents a stunted growth that remains latently present within the agent, or an absence of imposed form that renders the agent structurally incomplete. I want the scenario rendered with enough philosophical precision that a reader could see why one framework describes the agent as wounded and the other as unfinished, without either side collapsing into psychological intuitionism or behaviorist conditioning.
