Restate the core commitments of Kelly betting and geometric rationality—specifically, the argument that rational agents should maximize the geometric mean of returns, the defense of log-optimal fractional betting, and the claim that arithmetic-mean maximization leads to near-certain ruin—using only the vocabulary of structural engineering. Treat available capital as total load-bearing capacity, individual wagers as load allocations distributed across redundant members, probabilistic outcomes as either successful load transfer or localized member failure, and long-run growth as the cumulative structural integrity of the system over successive loading cycles. For each translated claim, report precisely what survives intact, what must be distorted to fit (for instance, whether subjective probabilities can be mapped onto deterministic load factors without losing normative force), and what has no structural counterpart at all—paying special attention to whether the distinction between myopic single-period optimization and intertemporal planning in the Kelly literature expresses anything that redundancy modeling can capture. Then assess the residue: does the untranslatable remainder show that geometric rationality is essentially structural intuition dressed in mathematical notation, or does it point to a genuinely normative, quantitative claim about belief and action that engineering vocabulary is structurally unable to express?
