Hey, I'm sketcing an essay on Nagarjuna's catuskoti and could use some comments. My idea is that the tetralemma gets misread as a dialetheist logic that just tolerates contradictions, when it's really an ontological deflation of svabhava — intrinsic nature. So I'm applying it to causal powers: realists say yes, eliminativists no, process folks both, quietists neither. Nagarjuna rejects all four, but emptiness isn't a hidden fifth view or super-property. It's just that all four depend on conventional designation within pratityasamutpada. So causal powers aren't real or unreal in themselves, just conventionally designated, though still constrained by dependant origination. I want to say this doesn't collapse into anti-realism that kills science, since conventional designation tracks regularities. But I'm worried I'm confalting epistemic humility with metaphysical emptiness, and I don't know whether emptiness of emptiness saves the view from nihilism or just makes it self-refuting. Does this framing hold water? Where am I likely going wrong? Is my line between conventional designation and mere subjective projection clear enough?
