Write a short story riffing on Death in Damascus, but twist it through an epistemic-closure angle. The protagonist — call him J — gets a warning in Damascus that he's going to be 'deleted' in Samarra by a system that tracks counterfactualy beliefs. He books it to Samarra thinking he's broken the chain, but the system admin tells him she was always going to meet him there, because in every nearby world where he correctly tracks the warning and flees, the tracking conditions actually close on Samarra being true. Have J realize his belief back in Damascus rested on a false intermediate step — he thought the server was local, but it was already mirrored there — so his escape was never safely grounded and the false lemma undermined the whole tracking chain. Keep it casual, like you're explaining a weird idea over coffee, and don't polish it too much; maybe write that he runned to the train.
