I am preparing a dissertation prospectus in formal epistemology, and I want to build my project around self-locating belief rather than treating it as a peripheral puzzle. The canonical entry point is the Sleeping Beauty problem: Beauty is put to sleep Sunday after a fair coin is flipped; if heads, she is awakened once on Monday; if tails, she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday, with her Monday memories erased, and she is interviewed upon each awakening. When she finds herself awake and is asked for her credence that the coin landed heads, the literature splits between halfers, who hold her uncentered credence should remain one-half, and thirders, who argue that finding herself awake provides evidence for duplication and should push her credence to one-third. I understand the dialectic well enough, but I want to move past the stalemate into the underlying structure of indexical reasoning—specifically, how an agent should update when she knows her reasoning apparatus may be instantiated multiple times across a branching possibility space, and whether each awakening provides independent evidential weight for duplication or is merely indexically transparent, revealing her location in a structure she already fully knew.

Within this cluster, I see four live but unevenly developed lines of work, and I need to know which deserve the bulk of a three-year project. First, whether Bayesian frameworks for centered worlds can be made precise without either collapsing into arbitrary prior selection or rendering duplication evidence trivial. Second, how self-locating belief should constrain rational betting: if Beauty is offered a wager that pays out differently on Monday versus Tuesday, does her updated credence in being in a tail-world alter her willingness to accept, and does this depend on whether she treats her Tuesday-self as a separate beneficiary? Third, whether anthropic reasoning in cosmology—particularly arguments involving Boltzmann brains or multiverse distributions—depends on the same indexical updating rules, or whether physical duplication introduces evidential considerations absent in purely mental duplication. Fourth, whether there is a principled formal distinction between self-locating belief formed through amnesia-induced repetition and belief formed through spatiotemporal travel or quantum branching, and if so, whether the analogy between Sleeping Beauty cases and Everettian branching survives rigorous scrutiny.

Rather than a survey, I want a defended agenda. Assuming I have about three years, a background in logic and decision theory, and no need to run experiments, which two or three of these questions are genuinely upstream of the others? For each, explain the tractability criterion—why a philosopher with formal tools can make progress on it now—what makes it more important than the alternatives you are deprioritizing, and what specific argument or model result over the next year would cause you to reorder the ranking. I am especially interested in hidden dependencies: for instance, whether resolving the betting-behavior question requires first fixing the representation of centered worlds, or whether the cosmological analogies are premature until the mental-duplication case is settled. Please rank them with explicit trade-offs between importance, tractability, and neglectedness, not as a list of interesting topics.
