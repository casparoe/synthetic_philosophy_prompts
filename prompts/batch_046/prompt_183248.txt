In consciousness studies people have been arguing about Dennett's heterophenomenology for decades — whether treating first-person reports as fictional narratives about a subject really gets us a science of consciousness or just sweeps the hard part under the carpet. My reading-group theory is that nobody's actually changing their mind because the two sides arent disagreeing about consciousness at all; they're disagreeing about what counts as a scientific explanation of a mental state. One camp thinks explaining the report-mechanism is explaining the phenomenon itself; the other thinks that's like explaining a thermometer without explaining heat. Is that right? Or is there a hidden empirical disagreement — say, about whether intersubjective calibraton of first-person reports is even possible without assuming the reality of the reported state? And if it is just a methodological standoff, could anything actually resolve it — a new neuroimaging result, a conceptual distinction between phenomenal content and heterophenomenological content, or is the question just ill-posed from the start? I want a diagnosis, not a survey, and an honest call on whether progress is possible here.
