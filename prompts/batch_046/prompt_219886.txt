I'm building a decision-tree instrument for a philosophy-of-science seminar on whether old evidence can confirm a newly proposed theory. The root commitment is: "Evidence E was already part of the background knowledge of the scientific community when theory T was formulated, and T was constructed in part to accommodate E." From there, the tree should sort respondents into named or unnamed positions by asking concrete diagnostic questions they can answer without knowing philosophical labels.

Branch nodes (each phrased as a claim a student can endorse or reject):

A. Is the relevant kind of novelty logical — E was not entailed by the prior belief set — or temporal — E was not known at the time T was formulated?

B. If T was built to accommodate E, does that automatically block confirmation (the bootstrapping objection), or can construction-history be separated from evidential support?

C. Is the comparison class for evaluating T's success the specific rival R that also accommodates E, or the full prior distribution over all possible theories?

D. Can explanatory coherence — T providing a unified mechanism that makes E expected rather than merely permitted — substitute for predictive novelty in generating confirmation?

E. Can old evidence confirm T if T assigns E a different structural or probabilistic role (e.g., E is a boundary condition in one framework but a derived consequence in another), even when every rival also accommodates E? Treat this "structural-reassignment" criterion as a distinct node.

Leaves: For each combination of answers, assign the name of the position that occupies it (e.g., Glymour-style bootstrapping defense, Howson-style skepticism, the logical-novelty-only view, the explanatory-unification view, the comparative-rival view, the structural-reassignment view) or label it "unnamed" and provide one sentence diagnosing the vacancy — incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored. Where two named positions land on the same leaf, flag them explicitly and state what further distinction (outside these five nodes) separates them.

Requirements: The questions must be phrased as claims a student could endorse or reject without recognizing the view they lead to. The tree must be built from the logical space of answers first, then mapped onto actual positions, with unoccupied combinations noted. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not depend on specific publication dates or titles.
