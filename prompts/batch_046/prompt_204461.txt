Me and my roommate got into it last night over presentism vs eternalism after I mentioned that old dinosaur bone in the museum. (1) She said presentism is obviously right because if eternalism were true then dinosaurs literally exist right now, just "back then," and that'd mean they're existant in reality alongside us which is nuts. (2) I told her that's mixing up time with space -- eternalists don't think past things are hanging out in some hidden dimension, they just think pastness is a real property like distance, so there's no dinosaur in my kitchen. (3) She said fine, but then presentists still need to explain why "T-rex lived 65 million years ago" is true if there's no T-rex. She proposed this thing she called "causal echo" -- present fossils have a real present feature that points back, not to an existing dinosaur, but to a past cause that's now gone, kind of like a shadow that keeps the shape without the object. So presentism can ground past truths without putting dinosaurs in the ontology. (4) I pushed back that either that echo is genuinely relational, which means it's relating to something that doesn't exist (which sounds like eternalism with extra steps), or it's just an intrinsic present feature we happen to call "fossil," in which case we've lost any reason to say it's about dinosaurs at all and not just a weird rock. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Also say who gets the overall point, and what both of us are missing -- especially whether her "causal echo" idea actualy works or just renames the problem.
