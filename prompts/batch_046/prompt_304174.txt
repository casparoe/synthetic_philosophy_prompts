In our school's philosophy-of-mind elective, the instructor introduced Douglas Hofstadter's argument in Gödel, Escher, Bach—that consciousness involves strange loops, recursive patterns in which a system represents its own representations—to explain why self-awareness might require more than simple information processing. During the discussion, a classmate used the line "the self is just a strange loop" to reject my question about whether a large language model that describes its own weights could already contain the kind of self-reference Hofstadter discusses. I want to understand what that slogan actually commits us to. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that "the self is just a strange loop" or "consciousness requires strange loops" might express, separating (1) the formal Gödelian claim about self-reference in logical systems, (2) the cognitive-scientific claim about recursive self-modeling in minds, and (3) any stronger metaphysical claim about special causal loops or non-computable properties? For each reading, say whether it is trivial, defensible, or false, and specifically whether it supports the conclusion that feedforward architectures are necessarily incapable of consciousness or that present-day language models might already qualify. Please present the options as a menu rather than selecting one, and indicate which reading is actually central to Hofstadter's analogy in GEB and which ones are later accretions.
