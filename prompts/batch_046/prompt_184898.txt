This illustrates the School of Names' central concern that linguistic categories and actual entities diverge (ming shi bu fu).

Gongsun Long's reasoning depends on the ontological priority of qualitative determinations over formal ones, a premise he takes from Hui Shi's doctrine of contracting differences (he tong yi), according to which distinct qualities must be separated before a form can be instantiated. By treating 'horse' as a bare particular lacking accidental properties, the argument establishes that 'white horse' denotes a composite substance formed by joining the quality 'white' with the form 'horse'. The interlocutor ultimately concedes that a yellow horse is likewise not a horse, confirming the general principle that any determinately colored animal lies outside the extension of the determinable 'horse'.

Consequently, the argument succeeds as an ontological defense of the separation of form and quality, placing Gongsun Long close to a proto-Platonic realism about universals in which pure forms exist independently of their qualitative instantiations.
