Here are my intuitive verdicts on what belongs inside or outside the Stoic dichotomy of control in modern life, and I want to know whether one principle can organize them all, or whether I must abandon some. I think my anxiety upon seeing a news headline is within my control, since I can choose not to open the feed, even though the event reported is not. When my employer installs keystroke-monitoring software, I think my resentment is within my control but the surveillance itself is not; yet this feels different from complaining about rain, because the system was designed by people I could in principle organize against, which places it in some middle zone I cannot clearly name. Practicing negative visualization about my child’s future feels fully within my control and healthy, but when I visualize climate collapse in detail and become paralyzed, I think I have misused the exercise, which suggests there is a hidden boundary about the content of what I visualize, not merely about the act of visualizing itself. I think casting a vote is within my control in a way the election result is not, but I also feel responsible for the result if I abstained, though not if I voted for the loser; that asymmetry seems to treat abstention as a kind of indirect causation I am answerable for in a way that active opposition is not. If I miss a flight because of traffic, I do not hold myself responsible, even though I chose my departure time, because traffic feels like an ambient condition I navigate rather than cause; but if I miss it because I stayed up late gaming, I do hold myself responsible, even though my sleep and morning alertness feel partly outside direct volition. So my cases appear to distinguish direct actions, collective or institutional influence, ambient conditions, and mental habits I can train but not command instantly. Is there a coherent Stoic-inspired principle that delivers all of these verdicts, including the hard asymmetry between abstaining and voting for the loser? If not, which verdict should I overturn? Please also say what your principle predicts for a case I have not listed: whether I bear responsibility for the emotional climate of a moderated group chat, where my rules shape the tone but every individual message comes from someone else. Respond in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one might write in a letter or email.
