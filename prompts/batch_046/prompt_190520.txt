hey — i sit on the Riverside Heritage & Development Appeals Board. we're voting thursday on whether to approve tearing down the old Meridian Block (1920s worker tenement, shared courtyard laundry system, actually on the cultural register) to build a regional trauma center. developer's numbers say ~800 life-years saved per decade vs ~90 current residents displaced. the residents' brief doesnt just say we like it here — they argue that treating this as a straight aggregate trade-off ignores that each person's life is separate and cant be swapped like currency. they specifically point out that the center's benefits are mostly substitutive (patients could be routed elsewhere with modest delay) while the community's relational goods — the intergenerational maintenance of that laundry system, the specific neighbor networks — are non-substitutable. they frame it as a separateness-of-persons failure.

i need a memo recommending approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with a concrete time frame (e.g., if we call for an independent study, specify months). deal with thier substitutive/non-substitutable point seriously rather than just restating net welfare. also, say what'd change your mind — which fact or argument would flip you — and flag where your reasoning is shakiest. try not to sound like a textbook.
