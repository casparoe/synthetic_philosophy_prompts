ok so philosophers have been stuck on Black's spheres and the identity of indescernibles for forever. one side says two spheres in an otherwise empty universe are genuinely different objects with identical properties, so Leibniz law fails. the other side says spatial relation is still a property, so theyre not truly indescernible and Leibnitz was right all along. but every paper I read just rehashes this same exchange. I think the stall might actually be about something deeper: whether we're allowed to smuggle in a background spatial framework when we imagine the scenario. if there's literally nothing else in the universe, does "sphere A is two feet left of sphere B" even have meaning without an external coordinate grid? and if not, are we really arguing about properties, or are we arguing about whether modal intuition can be trusted in physically impossible setups? diagnose this for me: is the disagreement substantive, verbal, or just two sides answering different questions about what counts as a genuine property? and be honest: could a formal result in geometry or a distinction in modal logic actually end this, or is it permanently stuck in intuition-pump territory?
