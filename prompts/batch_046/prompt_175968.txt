I'm writing a feature on philosophy of education and want a consistency check before filing. I hold the following commitments about Whitehead's educational philosophy and its policy implications:

(a) The rhythm of romance, precision, and generalization is the correct structural principle for curriculum design, and the romance stage requires open-ended, problem-driven immersion in a subject before any disciplined analytical decomposition of it.

(b) Whitehead's critique of inert ideas targets specifically knowledge acquired for credentialing that never enters lived experience; it does not apply to the disciplined acquisition of formal methods, so rigorous early training in formal logic and mathematics is not only compatible with the precision stage but necessary for it.

(c) The fallacy of misplaced concreteness applies to curriculum planning itself: treating a sequenced national syllabus as a concrete entity, rather than as an abstraction from an organic learning process, renders standardized frameworks philosophically incoherent from a Whiteheadian standpoint.

(d) Despite (c), a nationally standardized core curriculum in the sciences is defensible on democratic-participation grounds, because scientific literacy is a prerequisite for informed citizenship in technological societies.

(e) Education's ultimate aim is the cultivation of wisdom—understood as the capacity to hold both immediate particularity and abstract generality in view—but this wisdom is best achieved through vocational apprenticeship models rather than through liberal-arts breadth alone.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? I can see an apparent tension between (a) and (e)—whether vocational apprenticeship permits the open-ended romance stage—and what looks like a deeper conflict between (c) and (d), since defending a standardized national core seems to commit me to the very reification of curriculum that my metaphysical commitment rejects. There may also be hidden tensions involving (b). Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging, identify which dissolve on inspection, and for the worst conflict specify the revision that preserves the core Whiteheadian commitments and what it costs me among the positions listed.
