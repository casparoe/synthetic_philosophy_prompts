I'm on the municipal arts funding board and we're deadlocked over a proposal for a permanent installation called the 'Null Room' — basically an empty, climate-controlled concrete chamber with no lights, sounds, textures, or objects, designed so visitors sit in a state of total absence of craving and suffering. The artists call themselves tranquilists, and they argue that only the absence of craving and suffering has real value; pleasure, beauty, and narrative meaning are just temporary distractions that actually stimulate new cravings and therefore harm people. They claim traditional art violates ethics by feeding those cravings. One faction says funding this respects a serious philosophical view and our charter allows experimental work; the other says our mandate is to support art that actively enriches human lives, and funding pure emptiness is a betrayal of the public trust. Draft the board's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with reasoning that both the artists and the dissenting board members could actually respond to, and include the strongest argument for the side that loses. Please write in American English, and dont include citations or reading lists since no one here has time to verify them. I write novels for a living so im used to wondering whether emptiness can carry a story, but keep the memo practical and actionable.
