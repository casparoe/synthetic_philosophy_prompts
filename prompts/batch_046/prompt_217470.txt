A person born with no pain sensors gets hurt constantly but feels totally fine — I still say that's a disease, like thier alarm system is broken. But my cousin is colorblind and lives perfectly normal, and I don't think he's sick at all, even though doctors call it a deficency. Then there's my friend with winter depression: every year she can't work or socialize for months, and that feels like real illness to me, but mostly because it stops her living the way she wants. Meanwhile my uncle drinks every day, his liver is literally damaged, but he funcions okay and feels good — I still think that's sick, because an organ is breaking. So am I using two different rules? There's also this concept I ran into, allostatic overload, where your body adapts to stress so well it wears out silently — you pass every test, feel fine, but you're slowly breaking down with no single broken part. If that's legit, is someone with high allostatic load sick, or not sick until something actually fails? I need to know: is there one principle that gets all these verdicts, including the deafness thing I keep wobbling on — being born deaf seems like just a different way of being, but going deaf from an infection feels like a disease? Or do I have to drop one of my calls to keep it simple? And what does whatever rule you find say about a case I haven't listed, like someone who chooses to stay in constant low-level stress because it helps them work?
