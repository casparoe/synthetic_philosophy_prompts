I sit on our university's evolutionary biology curriculum committee. Next thursday we vote on whether Bio 101 should be required to include a module on non-adaptive architecture—spandrels, exaptation, evolutionary constraint—rather than framing every trait as an optimized adaptation. The chair (who taught from Dawkins texts in the 90s) think's this is sociology of science diluting hard biology; the grad students say leaving it out makes the course methodologically obsolete. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate: recomend either adopting the module with a concrete implementation timeline, or rejecting it with conditions for future review. Take the "this is just politics" objection seriously—show why someone smart could hold it—not just dismiss it. And please say exactly what evidence or argument would change your mind, plus point out the weakest link in your own reasoning.
