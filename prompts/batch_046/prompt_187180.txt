I've been analyzing rationalizability in two-player games where players can mix freely over pure strategies. I understand that a strategy survives iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies exactly when it is a best response to some belief supported on the opponent's surviving set—what Pearce characterized for finite games. Applying this to matching pennies, I see that no mixture is ever strictly dominated, since any p ≠ 1/2 does better against one opponent action and worse against the other, and linearity of expectation means mixtures of opponent strategies preserve this ordering. So the rationalizable set should be the full simplex. Yet discussions of matching pennies often present the 50/50 mixture as the outcome forced by common knowledge of rationality, as if dominance reasoning alone should eventually converge to it. Is there a hidden, stronger dominance concept—perhaps defined over correlated beliefs or convex belief sets—that can shrink the rationalizable set to the unique Nash equilibrium? Or is the gap between rationalizability and equilibrium genuinely unbridgeable without importing an explicit equilibrium assumption?
