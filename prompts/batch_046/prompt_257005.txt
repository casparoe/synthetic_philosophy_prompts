Imagine Karl Marx were alive today and presented with a detailed account of contemporary AI training pipelines: distributed workers, often in the Global South, perform fragmented micro-tasks—image labelling, sentiment verification, and content moderation—mediated by algorithmic platforms that calibrate remuneration in real time according to behavioural metrics. These workers never see the assembled dataset or the model it trains; their labour is dissolved into statistical patterns that become proprietary fixed capital. Drawing solely on Marx’s own conceptual apparatus—exploitation, alienation, real and formal subsumption, and his observations on machinery and the general intellect—how would he diagnose this situation? In particular, does the extraction of value here depend on surplus labour in the classical sense, or does capital now appropriate something closer to cognitive surplus? How does alienation operate when the worker is separated not from a tangible commodity but from the very patterns of judgement they contribute? And would he regard this as a qualitative extension of capitalist exploitation, or as a transformation that renders some of his categories obsolete? Please develop the analysis strictly in Marx’s own terms, without invoking later commentators.
