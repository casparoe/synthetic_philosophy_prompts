I am preparing interview questions for a podcast episode featuring an unnamed historian who defends a view she labels calibrated presentism. Her core claim is that historians should treat present-day moral frameworks not merely as an unavoidable lens but as an active evidentiary filter: sources originating within what she calls structurally unjust epistemic environments—social orders where institutional power systematically distorts testimony—ought to carry reduced default credibility unless corroborated by contemporaneous subaltern records. She insists this is a methodological correction for archival distortion by power rather than anachronistic projection.

Draft eight to ten questions that treat this position as live and specific, not as a strawman. I need at least one question pressing how she identifies a structurally unjust epistemic environment without smuggling in her own era's norms; include a follow-up that anticipates her appeal to cross-cultural human-rights benchmarks and asks how she avoids circular calibration. Add a question probing whether the rule applies to societies like democratic Athens or Ming-era China, where modern liberal categories are anachronistic yet structural domination clearly existed—anticipate her likely distinction between local injustice and global normative access, then push the follow-up on who authorizes that distinction. Include a question addressing the standard anti-presentist reply that all historiography is already value-laden, but force her to specify what positive evidentiary standard survives once calibration is applied—what, concretely, makes a corroborated subaltern source more trustworthy than an official record she has downgraded? Conclude with a question asking what empirical result—say, a documented case where subaltern testimony in a given archive proves systematically less reliable than official records—would weaken or overturn her rule. Avoid generic questions about her career or about defining presentism; each question should assume familiarity with the presentism literature and should make her defend the mechanics of calibrated presentism itself.
