I am writing a chapter for my dissertation on non-standard sensory modalities and their implications for the metaphysics of consciousness. I need you to design a thought experiment to precise specifications and present the analysis in American English.

Construct a realistic, single-paragraph scenario—no zombies, no brains in vats, no perfect predictors, and no exotic physics—in which a human subject acquires magnetoreceptive awareness solely through direct cortical stimulation that bypasses all peripheral sensory organs. The case must be engineered so that both a phenomenal-concept strategist and a representationalist illusionist predict that the subject will sincerely report that there is something it is like to be in this state, yet the two theories deliver opposite verdicts on whether a hard problem of consciousness remains for this particular state.

After describing the scenario, please do the following. First, identify the specific belief or report, the stimulation mechanism, and the relevant counterfactual conditions or nearby worlds that each theory treats as decisive. Second, explain why the phenomenal-concept strategist holds that the hard problem is dissolved for familiar sensory modalities but persists here: specifically, because the subject has not undergone the ordinary perceptual-learning trajectory required to form the appropriate phenomenal concept for this quality, leaving a genuine explanatory gap between the physical stimulation and the first-person report. Third, explain why the illusionist denies that any hard problem exists at all, treating the report as a misattribution of representational content about environmental magnetic fields rather than as evidence of irreducible phenomenal properties. Fourth, isolate the structural feature that drives the divergence—namely, the absence of peripheral receptor mediation and the corresponding lack of a standard sensory-learning pathway—and explain what happens to both verdicts if that feature is removed, for instance if the magnetoreception is delivered through a conventional peripheral implant with normal adaptive learning. Fifth, conclude with the strongest objection a defender of the view that fares worse in this case—whichever view must treat the novel report as either conceptually empty or metaphysically mysterious—could raise against the scenario, and assess whether that objection undermines the case.

Please avoid bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific page numbers; do not invoke highly technical formalisms or obscure sub-literatures unfamiliar to philosophers of mind; and keep the focus on the conceptual architecture of the case rather than on empirical neuroscience details.
