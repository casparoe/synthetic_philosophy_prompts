Here is my opening challenge. I accept that for a single, homogeneous epistemic subject your argument shows that knowledge of one's own mental state can drift away without notice. But I want to press a hierarchical variant. Imagine a distributed system of nested epistemic agents—call them L1 through Ln—where each layer Li can access and verify the mental states of Li-1, but no layer can introspect its own states directly. The top-level agent Ln attributes knowledge to the system only after cross-layer calibration corrects for gradual drift detected at lower levels. In such an architecture, the collective subject seems able to know that it knows—thereby satisfying the KK principle—while the sorites trap is avoided because the drift is caught externally rather than internally. Where in your argument does this distributed, self-correcting structure fail? Does your rejection of luminosity depend essentially on a Cartesian, unitary self, or does it still block hierarchical knowledge of knowledge?
