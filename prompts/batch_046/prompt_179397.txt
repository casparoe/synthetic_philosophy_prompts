If hierarchical predictive models in neuroscience can implement both the design stance and the intentional stance within a single architecture—treating beliefs and desires as high-level predictions—then when a clinician explains a patient's refusal of treatment by attributing the belief that doctors are untrustworthy, while a lower-level model explains the same refusal through prediction-error dynamics, do the two explanations identify distinct real patterns? Or does their predictive equivalence show that the intentional stance is only a lossy compression of the design stance, lacking independent ontological weight? Given Dennett's claim that real patterns exist wherever there are counterfactual-invariant predictions that rival descriptions miss, doesn't this imply that wherever predictive modeling succeeds, the intentional stance is necessarily heuristic rather than genuinely explanatory? What exactly is wrong with this line of reasoning?
