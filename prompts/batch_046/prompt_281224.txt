Compare Neurath’s international picture-language project (ISOTYPE, or Bildersprache) with his doctrine of protocol sentences (Protokollsätze) as developed within his physicalist epistemology. Specifically, examine whether the holistic, system-dependent revisability that Neurath assigns to protocol sentences—denying them any privileged, theory-independent foundational status—carries over into the design logic of ISOTYPE, or whether pictographic communication implicitly relies on a form of semantic atomism: discrete, stable icons whose meanings are fixed independently of the broader representational system in which they appear. To what extent does Neurath’s commitment to intersubjective verification, statistical normalization, and redundant pictorial encoding resolve, or merely obscure, this tension? In your answer, distinguish the anti-metaphysical motivations shared by both projects from the structural differences in how each handles compositionality, translation failure between symbol systems, and the possibility that a single pictogram might be interpreted differently across distinct theoretical contexts.
