I've been trying to pin down the full specification for an adequate theory of self-locating belief—specifically one that can adjudicate the Sleeping Beauty problem without hand-waving—before I commit to being a half-er, a thirder, or something more exotic. I want the desiderata, not another round of intuition-pumping. Give me the requirements list: what any such theory has to be able to deliver, with the concrete case or conceptual pressure behind each, flagging which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps (e.g., preserving standard Bayesian conditioning for uncentered evidence, avoiding the Doomsday conclusion, maintaining first-person symmetry across awakening instances). If any subset is jointly unsatisfiable—say, yielding the canonical one-third credence in heads while keeping non-indexical updating intact and blocking anthropic explosion—say so explicitly; that tension is the part I most want mapped.

Specifically, the list should cover:

- A definite, principled verdict in the canonical protocol: fair coin, Monday awakening if tails, Monday and Tuesday awakenings if heads, full amnesia between them, asked upon each awakening for the credence in heads. The theory must explain why its answer isn't just a normalization convention.
- A clean separation between centered updating (learning "it is now Tuesday and I am awake") and uncentered updating (learning "the coin landed heads"), with an account of how they interact when both are present.
- Handling multiplicity without arbitrary counting: heads produces two centered-world instances of the same agent, tails one; the theory must justify weighting them, or justify not weighting them, rather than treating experience-counting as primitive.
- Compatibility with ordinary Bayesian conditioning outside the indexical domain. The motivating case: learning my lottery number lost should drive my credence near zero in the standard way; the indexical machinery must not corrupt this.
- A consistent stance on anthropic reasoning. The case: if self-locating belief makes me think humanity is likely small just because I am an early human, that needs principled explanation or dissolution, not ad hoc suppression.
- First-person symmetry: no metaphysically privileged "actual" awakening or hidden center that the agent could identify but doesn't. The theory should work entirely from the agent's centered perspective.
- Handling asymmetric epistemic access between centered worlds. Here is the injected case I want included: modify the protocol so that, on Mondays only, Beauty is shown a hidden card (red or black, uncorrelated with the coin) after her credence is elicited but before she is put back to sleep; on Tuesdays she is shown nothing and knows she wouldn't have had the opportunity. She knows the full protocol. Upon waking—before seeing any card—she is asked her credence. The theory must say whether and how the absence of a card-opportunity in Tuesday-centered worlds affects her prior self-location, without collapsing the absence of information into positive evidence.

Then, briefly and with minimal scoring, how do the halfing view, the thirder view, and an SSA/SIA-flavored approach fare against this list? Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating tensions—especially whether the asymmetric-access case breaks any of them—rather than on a full scorecard. If accommodating all seven requires dropping one, say which one you'd drop and why.
