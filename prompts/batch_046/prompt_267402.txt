I'm working out my position on biological function for a philosophy-of-biology seminar and need a consistency audit before I present. Here are my commitments:

(1) I hold a pure causal-role account: the function of a trait is nothing more than its present contribution to some system-level capacity in the organism. The heart pumps blood; that is its function now, and its evolutionary past is irrelevant to what that function is.

(2) At the same time, I think evolutionary selection history is indispensable for drawing the line between a genuine function and an accidental by-product. Without reference to what was selected for, I have no principled way to say that circulating blood is the heart's function while generating audible vibrations is not.

(3) I take malfunction to be objective and trajectory-relative: a trait is malfunctioning when it departs from the causal profile that the organism's own developmental program is organized to stabilize and maintain, even if it is currently contributing to some other capacity. A heart with a damaged valve is malfunctioning with respect to circulation regardless of whether it happens to assist respiration in some incidental way.

(4) I apply function talk uniformly across biological and artificial domains—dialysis machines, synthetic enzymes, prosthetic valves—without treating artifacts as a different metaphysical kind, and I do so without invoking designer intentions, manufacturing history, or any etiological story.

(5) I reject proper-function metaphysics altogether: treating functions as purposes that depend on an item's origin strikes me as smuggling teleology into natural science.
