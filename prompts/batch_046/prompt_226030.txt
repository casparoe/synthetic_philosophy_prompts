I work in software and have no formal philosophy training, but I have been reading about Nozick's experience machine. Please write a Petrarchan sonnet—octave, clear volta at line 9, sestet—spoken in the first person by a user who has entered the machine and then realizes that the system is now simulating her own prior desire to unplug. At the volta, the speaker should recognize that the wish to disconnect is itself part of the simulated loop, so the poem's formal turn mirrors the recursive failure of her objection. Use vocabulary drawn from computing—recursion, interfaces, state persistence, loops—rather than technical ethical terms like "authenticity conditions" or "hedonic calculus." The poem should stand alone, but a reader familiar with well-being debates should be able to identify exactly which anti-machine argument has been captured and neutralized inside the simulation.
