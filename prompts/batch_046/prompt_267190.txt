Rank the following five interpretations of causation associated with al-Ghazālī's occasionalist framework—(1) the denial of all secondary causal efficacy in created things, (2) the habit or custom view that treats regular succession as a divinely instituted pattern without metaphysical necessity, (3) the epistemic-habit view that grants scientific prediction reliability while withholding ontological causal powers from creatures, (4) the concurrentist revision that allows created powers only as instruments continuously sustained by direct divine action, and (5) a constructed interventionist occasionalism that translates occasionalist commitments into modern causal-modeling terms by treating every causal relation as a mediated divine intervention constrained only by statistical regularities—according to how successfully each preserves both (a) an unrestricted doctrine of divine omnipotence and (b) the practical possibility of counterfactual scientific reasoning about interrupted regularities.

Assign each interpretation a tier—Top, Middle, or Bottom—based on its performance across both criteria together. For each, provide a single sentence explaining why it occupies that tier, focusing specifically on whether its account of interrupted regularities collapses into determinism, renders science purely descriptive, or sustains predictive counterfactuals without attributing independent powers to nature. Present the result as an ordered list from best to worst overall reconciliation, with the tier noted beside each entry. No citations or bibliographies are required.
