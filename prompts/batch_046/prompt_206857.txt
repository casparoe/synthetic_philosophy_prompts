I'm a PhD student in value theory and I'm trying to think through the heterogeniety problem for hedonism, but not in the usual Mill-versus-Sidgwick way that every textbook repeats. Most accounts just list different pleasures — eating ice cream, solving a proof, hearing a chord resolve — and ask what they share besides being called pleasant. I'm trying to figure out whether the objection is really about ontology, that there's literally no common property, or about epistemic access, that we just can't introspect it.

So ice-cream pleasure and proof-solving pleasure share the gloss, but the underlying states are completely different. Does this save hedonism, or does it just collapse into a kind of desire-satisfaction theory? And does quantitative hedonism fare better here than qualitative hedonism, or is it the other way round?

Could you explain the difference between an ontological and an epistemic reading of heterogeniety, and whether the gloss move actually solves anything or just shifts the problem?
