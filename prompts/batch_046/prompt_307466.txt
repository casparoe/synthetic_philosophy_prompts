hey, i'm working on illusionism for my diss and i keep getting stuck on where the line actually is -- my intuitions feel like they should hang together but they probably don't. here's four cases where i think i know what's going on, but i'm not sure there's one principle behind them:

1. when i stare at a red apple, it genuinely feels like there's something it's like for me to see that redness -- not just data processing, but actual phenominal presence. i don't want to call that an illusion.

2. in those old sperling partial-report experiments, subjects swear they saw the whole grid before the cue even though they can only report half. that "feeling of seeing everything" strikes me as a clear phenominal illusion -- there's no real overflow, just a confabulated sense of richness.

3. with split-brain patients who point correctly to left-field objects but report seeing nothing there, i think we have access without phenomenal consciousness. the absence of report tracks something real.

4. then there's the meditator describing "pure awareness with no content whatsoever." i tend to read that as a mischaracterization of intense attentional focus, not a genuine state of contentless phenomenology.

the tension is between 1 and 4: if i trust my own introspective report about redness, why dismiss the meditator's? and 2 makes phenomenal reports seem unreliable, but 3 treats missing reports as authoritative. what's the principle? is it global workspace integration? consumption by a self-model? availability for verbal report? i want it stated precisely, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it, predictions for a case i haven't listed (say, a blindsight patient who learns to guess shapes with above-chance accuracy but still denies seeing anything), and an honest statement of which of my four verdicts it has to overturn if it can't save them all. if saving every verdict requires a baroque taxonomy, say so and pick the simpler account. don't just lay out Dennett's view, Frankish's view, and leave me hanging -- take a definite stance on which principle is right and defend it, even if that means my intuition about the apple is the one that goes. and please, no bibliographies or page numbers, just the argument. oh, and seperate from all that, tell me where your account thinks the fridge-light illusion applies to consciousness, if at all.
