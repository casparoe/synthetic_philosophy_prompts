I'm a high school student drafting an essay on dream skepticism, and I've noticed a recurring structure I can't name. In Zhuangzi's butterfly dream, he questions whether he is a man dreaming he is a butterfly or vice versa, relying on memory and narrative coherence to frame the doubt. In Descartes's dream argument, he uses reasoning about the coherence of present experience versus remembered waking states to motivate skepticism. But in both cases, the cognitive tools used to formulate the skeptical challenge—reasoning, memory comparison, analogical judgment—are themselves capacities that a dream might replicate or fabricate. This creates what seems like a reflexive trap: if the present state really is a dream, then the very argument that reveals this might be part of the dream's internal logic. I can see how this relates to Cartesian circularity and to the problem of the criterion, yet those usually address general justification rather than this specific loop, where the skeptical hypothesis undermines the reasoning employed to state it. Does this configuration—where dream skepticism reflexively destabilizes its own argumentative machinery—have an established term in epistemology or philosophy of mind? If there is no precise label, what is the nearest concept, how should its boundaries be drawn against ordinary circular reasoning, and what would be the clearest way to describe the point at which the reflexive entrapment begins and ends?
