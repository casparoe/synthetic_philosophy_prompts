I am a retired natural scientist who has recently begun reading philosophy of mind. In Dennett's well-known discussion of Sphex ichneumoneus, the wasp's invariant burrow-checking loop is offered as a paradigm of mechanical rigidity: if the burrow is displaced during inspection, the wasp repeats the sequence exactly. I understand Dennett to be arguing that such rigidity excludes free will, and that human freedom therefore consists in possessing what I shall call deep sphexishness—deterministic behavioral loops with extensive internal branching that can accommodate varied inputs. If this is right, then free will seems reducible to high-dimensional determinism. Yet I see a difficulty: every internal branch remains part of the loop, so increasing complexity only widens the loop rather than escaping it. Is the only remaining option for genuine freedom a meta-sphex interruption that is not itself embedded in any deterministic branch? And if so, by what conceptual criterion could we identify such an interruption in human cognition without presupposing the very freedom we seek to explain?
