I sit on my university's institutional animal care and ethics board. We have received a neuroscience protocol proposing invasive but non-lethal cognitive tests on six macaques. The study is designed to assess what the investigators call "threshold moral agency"—a partial capacity for preference-revision that falls short of full Kantian autonomy but exceeds standard animal cognition. They invoke Nozick's distinction between utilitarian consideration for animals and side-constraint protection for persons to argue that these primates, lacking full self-ownership, may be subjected to procedures impermissible on humans so long as suffering is minimized and the knowledge gain is substantial. Board opponents respond that Nozick's framework is binary rather than gradated, and that inventing a "threshold" category risks licensing the very instrumentalization his side-constraint view was meant to prevent; they further argue that without a predefined suffering ceiling the protocol fails even a utilitarian calculus. Draft the board's decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—recommending a concrete course of action with a specific time frame for implementation or response. The memo should open with its bottom line in the first sentence, followed by reasoning with no preamble and no restatement of the prompt. It should treat both the researchers' framing and Nozick's actual position with philosophical seriousness, include an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side, and avoid treating either view as a strawman.
