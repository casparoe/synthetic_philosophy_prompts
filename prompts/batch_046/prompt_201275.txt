I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. I need to deliver the opening remarks at a retirement workshop honoring my former manager, who spent thirty years in site-reliability engineering. The room will be split between engineers who think in root-cause narratives and family members who do not. I want a five-minute speech that uses the distinction between inside-view mechanistic reasoning and outside-view statistical forecasting as its actual structure, not ornament. The backbone should be her practice of withholding final approval from any post-mortem until its causal story had been checked against the fleet's historical failure-rate baseline, and the argument should frame that habit as epistemic modesty: she treated every detailed explanation as provisional because the reference class could always reveal the event was not unique. The talk must be warm, free of named philosophers and citations, intelligible without technical jargon, and should show why holding both perspectives was for her a moral commitment as much as a technical one.
