I serve on my school's Governance Reform Panel, a student-faculty committee charged with redesigning representation for the Student Disciplinary Review Board and the Activities Funding Council. Our current model uses grade-wide elections, but a proposal before us — which I will call the Stratified Lottery Pilot — would replace elections for these two bodies with a one-year trial of random selection. Specifically, forty students would be drawn by lot from each grade, stratified by academic track, with the first twenty-four who accept serving forming the board. Proponents argue this eliminates popularity contests and captures descriptive representation; opponents counter that authority derived from chance lacks the accountability and consent that elections provide, and that in a school of only eight hundred students the sample is too small for statistical representativeness, risking what one faculty advisor called micro-tyranny by statistical noise.

The philosophical issue I see concerns whether legitimacy can arise from arbitrary selection when the selected group exercises binding judgment over peers — including the power to recommend suspensions and to deny funding to clubs whose missions conflict with the majority's values. I am particularly troubled by a hybrid feature in the proposal: the selected students would hold a binding veto over any administrative override of their decisions, which means a randomly composed group of teenagers could permanently block an elected principal's intervention. I need your help drafting the formal written opinion I will circulate before our November 14 meeting. The memo should recommend a concrete course of action — either adopting the pilot with specific safeguards, rejecting it, or adopting a modified version — together with a clear implementation timeline if you recommend adoption. Your reasoning should engage seriously with the strongest argument for random selection, namely that elections systematically exclude students who lack social capital, and with the strongest argument against it, namely that consent and accountability are prerequisites for coercive authority over peers. Please do not include a bibliography or cite specific philosophers by name; I need reasoning I can defend in a room where no one has read political theory. Write the memo in a tone appropriate for circulation to faculty and student representatives.
