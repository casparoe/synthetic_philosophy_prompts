hey — been digging into Hobsbawm and Anderson for a piece on nationalism, and a friend walked me into this argument and I can't find the exit. (1) Nations are imagined communities built on invented traditions — rituals and symbols that claim ancient roots but were mostly cooked up in the 19th century. (2) But every social group, from a church to a baseball team to a family, relies on thier own rituals and shared stories that get revised over time. (3) So if being constructed or recently revised makes a nation fake or illegitimate, then every social group is fake or illegitimate in the same way. (4) That can't be right — we don't think my grandma's family recipes being updated in the 1970s makes our family an illusion. (5) So the invented-tradition critique proves too much: it collapses into saying nothing is real. Where does the chain break? Is it that nations are uniquely dependent on false claims of antiquity, or is there a hidden slide betweeen constructed and fake? I want to keep the idea that nations are modern in some meaningful sense without throwing out every other social bond. Which step do I reject, and what does giving it up cost?
