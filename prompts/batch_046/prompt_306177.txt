My partner and I are in the middle of IVF treatment at a clinic that uses time-lapse morphokinetic grading. The embryologists explained that several embryos may come from the same sperm and egg batch, and that embryos with genetically identical origins sometimes receive different grades—say, 5A versus 4B—based solely on minute differences in the timing of cell-division events. Only the highest-graded embryo will be implanted; the others may be frozen, donated, or discarded.

This has left me with two disquieting thoughts that feel philosophical rather than clinical. First, if embryo A (graded 5A) is implanted and becomes our child, while embryo B—formed from the exact same sperm and egg, but graded 4B because its second cleavage was slightly delayed—is never used, was B in some sense the same potential child? Would implanting B instead have produced the very same person? Second, if a laboratory error led to the implantation of embryo C, formed from a different sperm sample (still biologically mine, but collected on a separate occasion) and a different egg, would that child still be "ours" in the same deep sense?

I am not asking about legal parenthood, emotional bonding, or the ethics of embryo donation per se. What I want is clarity about whether the particular gametes that initiate development are essential to the identity of the person who results. If they are essential, then the embryos we do not implant include not merely "possible children" but specifically "our child, had circumstances differed." If they are not essential, then my sense that embryo B is a replaceable version of embryo A is probably a mistake, and I should think differently about what it means to discard or donate the remaining embryos. How should I sort this out?
