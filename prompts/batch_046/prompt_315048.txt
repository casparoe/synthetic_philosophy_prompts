Having retired from physical oceanography and recently turned to philosophy, I am troubled by an old commitment whose binding force I can no longer locate. In the 1980s, I conducted thermohaline studies in a remote coastal basin with the guidance of an elder from a local fishing collective who served as an informal living archive of seasonal currents; in exchange, I promised that any results bearing on their harvest patterns would be shown to him before wider publication. The collective dissolved in the 1990s, the elder died without heirs, and the only record of our agreement is a handwritten calibration log I still possess; I am now preparing a retrospective synthesis for a general audience. Although no identifiable recipient remains and the practical conditions that made disclosure meaningful have vanished, I feel an enduring pull to honor the word I gave. What, if anything, still binds me, and why should a promise persist when both its recipient and its original purpose have disappeared? Please respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
