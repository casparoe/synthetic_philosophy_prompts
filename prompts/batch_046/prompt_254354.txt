I have been thinking on my own for some time about whether there is a genuine asymmetry in the reasons we have for and against bringing people into existence. The view I have arrived at is this: if a particular child will live a life of serious suffering, then there is a real reason not to create that child; but if a particular child would live a genuinely happy life, there is no corresponding reason to create them, because there is no specific person who is deprived of that happiness by our failure to act. I do not take this as a bare intuition. I take it to track something structural about harm and benefit: both seem to require an actual subject who is made worse or better off, and a merely potential person is not the kind of thing that can be wronged by omission.

I want an honest assessment of what it would actually take for this position to be wrong—not just that someone finds it emotionally unsatisfying, but which arguments, observations, or formal results would genuinely count against it. For instance, is there a defensible form of impersonal value according to which the world is made worse by the absence of happy lives, even when no specific individual is harmed? Could the asymmetry collapse once we move from individual reproductive choices to collective or institutional decisions about population size, or to long-range obligations such as those raised in climate ethics regarding future generations? Could work on the logic of obligations to merely possible beings show that my claim about "no subject, no harm" is unstable?

Please distinguish between what would be decisive and what would merely be awkward. Which of these potential defeaters would a committed defender of the asymmetry concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss as missing the point? I also want to know whether the view is well-insulated: have its defenders qualified it so thoroughly that almost nothing could touch it, or does it make concrete predictions that rival accounts avoid? If you think the position risks little, say so plainly; if you think it is exposed in specific places, name them. And please say what you personally find most persuasive among the possible defeaters, even if a defender could resist it.
