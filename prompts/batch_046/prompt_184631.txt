I want to apply this distinction to the philosophy of temporal consciousness, specifically to interpretations of the color phi phenomenon and to the broader problem of the timing of experience relative to neural events. Please compare the Orwellian and Stalinesque analogies as explanatory models: how would each account for the apparent anticipation of color change in phi, and for the reported latency between stimulus onset and conscious report? In doing so, evaluate which analogy better captures the idea that conscious content is postdictively integrated rather than continuously updated, and identify at least one point where the historiographical parallel breaks down for each model. I am not looking for a literature review or citation list; please construct the argument conceptually from first principles.
