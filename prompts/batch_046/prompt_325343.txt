(2) That makes the present value of saving a billion lives in 500 years basically zero. (3) So pure time preference licenses ignoring long-run existential risk. (4) But apply the same rate intrapersonally: my welfare in ten years gets discounted exactly like a stranger's in 500. (5) So I should be almost indiffernt between eating well now and starving in a decade, which is nuts — planning is clearly rational. (6) So the rate can't apply to my own future self. (7) But there's no non-arbitrary line between "me in ten years" and "someone in 500"; both are just future utility streams. (8) So either r=0 everywhere (which means I should be indiffernt between saving one life now and saving infinitely many later — also nuts), or r>0 everywhere and rational planning is impossible. Where's the slip? I really want to keep (5), but I also want to avoid biting the bullet on (8). Is the mistake in (6), (7), or the hidden assumption that the same rate has to govern both?
