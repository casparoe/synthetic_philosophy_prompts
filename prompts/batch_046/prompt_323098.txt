Me and my philosophy of emotion group have been going round in circles for months about guilt versus shame. We all agree shame is more social and guilt more internal, and we've read the same ethnographies about cultures running mainly on one or the other. But then we hit a wall: half think guilt is the better moral emotion because it preserves individual autonomy, the other half think shame is better because it repairs social bonds, and nobody can agree whether a culture could actualy choose to swop one for the other without wrecking its affective infrastructure. I keep wondering if the stall isn't about which emotion is best, but about two hidden disagreements—one about whether emotions are plastcity enough to be culturally engineered, and one about whether 'better' means better for autonomy or for cohesion. Is that the right diagnosis, or are we just stuck on bedrock intuitions about personhood that neither side can argue for? And if it is the double-meaning problem, what distinction or piece of evidence would actualy move us?
