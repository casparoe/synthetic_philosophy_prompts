Since a close friendship ended unexpectedly last year and I have since been trying to determine whether relationships have value only for the feelings they generate or for something inherent in the connection itself, I have been studying G. E. Moore's discussion of friendship and aesthetic appreciation in Principia Ethica. A summary in some lecture notes I came across presents his view as follows: Moore holds that friendship and the contemplation of beauty are the two supreme intrinsic goods, and he analyzes both through his principle of organic unities. Specifically, he claims that friendship derives its value entirely from being a conscious state in which one takes pleasure in the existence and qualities of another person, combined with an appropriate cognitive awareness of those qualities. Because these are the only constituent elements, Moore applies the principle of organic unities to show that the value of friendship is strictly additive—it equals the value of the pleasure plus the value of the cognition taken separately. Consequently, he is committed to the view that a solitary individual who could reproduce the same pleasure and the same cognitive appreciation through imagination or art would possess an ethically equivalent state, so that actual mutual presence adds nothing further. The notes conclude that for Moore friendship is therefore reducible to a particularly complex form of aesthetic enjoyment, and that this makes his ethics ultimately solipsistic despite its emphasis on other people.

This strikes me as suspiciously neat, but I am not well enough versed in Moore to identify where the distortion lies. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what Moore actually claimed about the intrinsic value of friendship and beauty, what is a defensible simplification, and what the summary gets wrong about the principle of organic unities—particularly whether value in this case is additive or non-additive. Also, assess whether the conclusion that Moore's position is solipsistic or reducible to aesthetic enjoyment follows from the accurate parts, or whether it depends on the misrepresentations.
