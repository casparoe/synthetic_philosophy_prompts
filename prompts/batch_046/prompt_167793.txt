Write the desiderata that an adequate normative theory of obligations to the real dead in historical fiction and biopic dramatization must meet. Specify what the theory has to deliver: the cases it must adjudicate correctly—including, for instance, a case where documented actions are preserved but the subject’s own written account of their motives is overridden for dramatic arc, and a case where the deceased left explicit posthumous instructions about representation—together with the distinctions it must not collapse, such as that between wronging living survivors and harming the dead themselves, and that between factual misrepresentation ("representational harm") and the subordination of the subject’s self-interpretation to present narrative needs ("narrative colonization"). Include the constraints it must remain consistent with: the legal non-personhood of the dead in standard jurisdictions, the autonomy of fictional art, and the epistemic limits of posthumous testimony. For each requirement, give the consideration that makes it non-optional, flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify which single requirement does the most damage to the popular positions in this space. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than on scoring named theories.
