I am preparing a position paper on causation and want a consistency audit of the package I am defending before presenting it. My commitments are these: (1) causal relations are irreducibly modal and cannot be reduced to patterns of actual occurrence; (2) the interventionist framework—structural equations coupled with do-operators—captures this modality precisely, because it defines causation in terms of invariant relationships under surgical manipulation of variables; (3) counterfactual analyses in the Lewis-Stalnaker tradition are ultimately reducible to interventionist representations, with similarity orderings over possible worlds serving as informal heuristics for the structural constraints that causal graphs encode formally; (4) in deterministic cyclic systems—such as climate feedback loops in which temperature and ice-albedo depend on each other—counterfactual dependence delivers contradictory verdicts about whether an intervention on X produces Y, because backtracking similarity conditions and forward-tracking conditions pull in opposite directions, whereas the interventionist account resolves this by holding exogenous terms fixed and computing the equilibrium shift; (5) despite (3) and (4), I maintain that the metaphysical ground of causation is still counterfactual dependence, and that structural equations are merely descriptive instruments that summarize those underlying modal facts rather than constituting them; (6) I reject any view that requires actual physical interventions to occur, so causal claims about untouched natural systems remain fully meaningful and contentful.

Please audit this set for joint tenability. Does (3) conflict with (5)—if counterfactuals reduce to structural representations, can I consistently claim that structures are only descriptive of counterfactual facts? Does (4) support (3), or does it instead show that counterfactual dependence is too unstable in cyclic cases to serve as the metaphysical ground, thereby pushing me toward a pure structuralism? Is there a hidden tension between (2), which appeals to surgical manipulation, and (6), which denies that manipulation must actually take place? Rank any genuine tensions by severity, identify which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and state what the least costly revision would be—whether abandoning the reduction claim in (3), weakening the metaphysical priority claim in (5), or conceding that deterministic cyclic cases compel a fully interventionist ontology.
