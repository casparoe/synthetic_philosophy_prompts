Write a sestina about Wittgenstein's notion of family resemblance. Choose these six end-words—thread, rope, fiber, knot, fray, hold—and rotate them through the six stanzas according to the standard sestina pattern (1-2-3-4-5-6, then 6-1-5-2-4-3, and so on). In each stanza, alter the grammatical function or semantic domain of every word so that none retains an identical role across the poem, modeling the idea that category members share only overlapping similarities rather than a single essential feature. The envoi should present all six words together in the order 2-4-6-5-1-3 without resolving them into a definition. The form should do the philosophical work: a reader familiar with the rope-and-fiber discussion in Philosophical Investigations should recognize the structure, not merely the vocabulary.
