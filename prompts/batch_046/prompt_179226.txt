Next thursday I'm presenting to our metaphysics and philosophy of mind reading group—maybe eight people, mostly analytic philosophers who work on mental representation and philosophy of language, plus one visiting logician who thinks anything with Hegelian vibes is automatically confused, and a couple grad students who are deep in the grounding literature and keep trying to reduce every relation to fundamentality. I'm defending a view I call "unity-first representationalism": I take Bradley's regress seriously not just as a technical problem for relational theories of the proposition, but as evidence that the whole "structured proposition" framework—constituents plus a syntactic glue relation—is the wrong model for mental content altogether. Instead I think propositional attitudes are primitive holistic states that have internal articulation without being literally composed of parts plus a unifier. I borrow Bradley's idea that relations are "unreal" apart from the concrete whole they belong to, but I want to keep a representationalist story about belief and desire, so I end up saying mental content is differentiated internally rather than syntactically composed. My argument targets both the standard Russellian reply—that relations just relate thier terms without needing a further relator—and the modern grounding-inspired picture where propositions are grounded in arranged parts. Include the question I'm praying nobody asks, probably something about whether this collapses into mystical monism or makes belief attribution impossible.
