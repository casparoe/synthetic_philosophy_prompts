I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm pitching a small foundation for a four-month fellowship to develop a long-form essay on embedded agency — but not the usual AI-alignment version. I keep running into this idea that because any real agent is physically and informationally inside the world it's reasoning about, there just isn't an "outside" vantage point from which to verify its own reasoning is correct. Most people treat this as either an engineering headache (how do you model yourself without infinite regress?) or as a vague metaphor about human finitude. I think both miss something sharper.

The proposal needs to ask whether embeddedness is only a practical limit on self-modeling, or whether it conceptually undermines the idea of rational self-correction itself — can an agent that is fully inside the causal loop ever have a genuinely non-ciruclar reason to revise its own goals? It should say why the decision-theory camp (which assumes an external evaluator) and the enactivist camp (which rejects representation altogether) don't settle this. The method should be conceptual, probably working through reflexive decision procedures in toy models rather than empirical work. I want to know what success looks like — maybe a framework for "local" rational revision that doesn't need an outside view — and what failure looks like, like if every version of self-correction collapses back into circularity. Keep the scope honest for four months, and please don't include a bibliography or citation list since I'll fill those in myself.
