If you're allocating a three-year conceptual research budget—say, one senior philosopher and two postdocs—to work on Scanlon's contractualism and its aggregation problem, which open directions should take priority, and in what order? I'm not asking for a literature survey; I want a defended agenda that says, for each line, why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers working without empirical tools, and what kind of result over the next few years would either elevate it or kill it.

Specifically, consider these four competing fronts. First, the collective-agent extension: can contractualism apply to institutions or artificial systems as non-aggregated agents that owe justification directly to stakeholders, or does any institutional framing inevitably collapse back into individual-level reasoning? Second, the structural-harm threshold: in cases where individual burdens fall below any plausible wronging-threshold but the collective effect is severe—systemic risk, climate externalities—does contractualism need a new conceptual category, call it justificatory overhang, to avoid either permitting catastrophic outcomes or smuggling aggregation back in through the rear door? Third, formal scaling of justification: is there a tractable conceptual framework—not an ML implementation—for reasoning about what can be justified to each affected party in large-scale, partial-information interactions without aggregating reasons across persons, and does building this depend on resolving the aggregation problem first? Fourth, alignment-by-justification: if we reject aggregative welfare maximization in AI alignment, can contractualist reasoning provide a principled way to weigh harms across heterogeneous user populations, or is this practically downstream of (1) and (2) to the point of being premature?

For each, defend your ranking with explicit criteria—importance, tractability, dependence on other results, and what evidence or conceptual breakthrough over a three-year horizon would change your view. Which of these is the real crux that reshapes the framework, which are valuable but secondary, and which sound important but are actually red herrings that don't shift the underlying logic of what we owe to each other?
