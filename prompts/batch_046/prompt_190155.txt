Next thursday we vote on whether to approve funding for an interactive exibit called "The Phenomenal Gap," which presents illusionism about consciousness—no inner movie, just representational processes that misrepresent themselves as having qualitative feel. The exibit includes "qualia calibration booths" where visitors match their color reports to spectral data, and the aggregate divergence is displayed as evidence that first-person reports are unreliable instruments rather than windows onto intrinsic properties. Half the board says this is cutting-edge science communication about cognitive error; the other half, led by our education director, says it's irresponsible to present a contested metaphysical view as settled neuroscience, especially since telling visitors their joy and suffering are "illusory" could undermine public trust.

Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before the meeting: it should recomend a concrete course of action with a clear time frame, take the illusionist position seriously without strawmanning it, and honestly address the strongest worry from the education director's side—that we're conflating measurement disagreement with ontological absence. Don't ask for citations or a reading list; I just need the document itself, with reasoning both sides could actually respond to.
