Write the full specification — the desiderata — that any theory treating phenomenal properties (qualia) as real, intrinsic, and non-functional must satisfy to avoid being incoherent, in the tradition that treats such properties as a conceptually confused posit. Your list should cover four families of requirements.

First, the cases the theory must correctly classify: inverted-spectrum possibilities; absent-qualia scenarios (e.g., zombies or functionally identical subjects lacking phenomenal consciousness); the transparency of experience (the claim that attending to an experience reveals only its object, not a mediating phenomenal vehicle); and a blindsight-style case in which a subject retains full behavioral and cognitive functional equivalence with sighted subjects yet reports no visual phenomenal access. For each case, state why correct classification is non-negotiable and what mistake (collapse into behaviorism, over-attribution, or mysteriousness) results from getting it wrong.

Second, the distinctions the theory must preserve without collapse: between phenomenal character and intentional/representational content; between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness; between the determinacy of phenomenal content (whether there is a definite fact about whether an experience is reddish) and its determinability (whether the conditions for being reddish can be specified without circular appeal to the very experience being explained). Also include the distinction between intrinsic properties of experience and relational properties constituted by subject-object interaction.

Third, the formal and empirical constraints: consistency with established psychophysical correlation laws; compatibility with the causal closure of the physical domain; avoidance of causal overdetermination if phenomenal properties are granted efficacy; and non-violation of the principle that a theory must not presuppose the very acquaintance with qualia it seeks to explain. Flag each as either non-negotiable across camps or as the preference of a particular camp (physicalist, dualist, representationalist).

Fourth, identify any jointly unsatisfiable subset of your desiderata. In particular, examine whether the triad of (i) intrinsicness of qualia, (ii) causal efficacy, and (iii) causal closure of physics is impossible to satisfy together, and whether transparency and first-person introspective acquaintance generate a separate tension. If you find an impossible subset, say so explicitly — that is the central result requested.

Then, briefly score four positions against your list: a Dennett-style eliminativism that rejects the posit altogether; a representationalist reduction of phenomenal properties to representational content; a property-dualist view that grants qualia causal autonomy; and a relationalist account that treats phenomenal properties as constituted by subject-object relations. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; the scorecard should be brief.

Conclude by stating how confident you are in your identification of jointly unsatisfiable requirements, and single out the specific claim or distinction about which you are least sure.
