Write a proposal for a philosophy dissertation chapter, approximately 8,000 words, to be completed within one semester, that examines whether the problem of transformative experience—choices that are epistemically transformative (the agent cannot know what the experience is like without undergoing it) and personally transformative (they alter core preferences or values)—can be coherently extended from individual first-person decision-making to collective institutional agents. Concentrate on democratic legislatures or constitutional assemblies that make binding, long-horizon commitments—such as irreversible climate-policy frameworks or intergenerational resource allocations—on behalf of future citizens who are not present to deliberate. State the precise question: does the absence of a unified collective phenomenology and collective personal identity dissolve the transformative-experience objection at the institutional level, or does the problem re-emerge in distributed form across representatives, affected populations, and successive generations? Explain why the existing philosophical literature, which treats the problem almost exclusively in individual terms, leaves this institutional extension open. Outline the approach: first, distinguish epistemic, preferential, and identity-based transformation at the collective level; second, evaluate whether representative proxy reasoning or deliberative mechanisms can substitute for the first-person knowledge that transformative choices normally require; third, apply the analysis to one concrete institutional mechanism rather than remaining at an abstract level. Identify the two principal possible conclusions—either that collective transformative commitments are rationally defensible through suitably structured proxy reasoning, or that they suffer from a compounded epistemic deficit that undermines standard expected-utility justification—and specify which considerations (for example, the possibility of distributed phenomenology, the reversibility of collective commitments, or the asymmetry between positively and negatively valenced transformations) would adjudicate between them. Conclude with an honest scope assessment: state explicitly whether resolving general debates about group agency and collective consciousness is a necessary prerequisite, or whether those debates should be bracketed to keep the project feasible for a single chapter. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or specific textual references; describe positions and arguments in your own terms, and indicate that formal citations will be supplied separately.
