Draft the joint statement that an Orthodox Dichotomist and a Revisionist Continuist could both sign after a sustained adversarial collaboration on the role of Epictetus’s control distinction in modern Stoic practice. The Orthodox Dichotomist maintains that the sharp boundary between what is in our power—judgments, intentions, and assent—and what is not—body, property, reputation, and external events—is both descriptively accurate and ethically indispensable; without it, Stoic therapy collapses into either passive resignation or anxious overreach. The Revisionist Continuist holds that this binary misrepresents human agency, which operates along gradients of influence, and that retaining it forces modern Stoics to treat partially controllable domains—career trajectories, relational dynamics, health behaviors, and collective outcomes—as mere indifferents, thereby undermining practical engagement.

Produce a document with four parts. First, a numbered list of claims both sides accept, covering: the historical origin of the dichotomy in the texts attributed to Epictetus; the core therapeutic goal of reducing suffering through corrected judgment; the fact that modern Stoicism has gained significant popular and clinical traction in the past two decades; and the agreement that not every external event should be treated as a direct object of moral concern.

Second, a disagreements table. Each row should state one precise point of divergence, phrased in wording that the named side would accept for its own position and that the opposing side recognizes as an accurate statement of that position rather than a strawman. Include at least: whether the internal/external boundary is categorical or admits of intermediate zones; whether treating health or relationships as indifferents is a therapeutic strength or a motivational liability; and whether Stoic ethics requires the dichotomy as a foundational premise or can survive on a revised account of graded responsibility.

Third, identify the cruxes—those sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would move at least one party significantly closer to the other. For each crux, specify: a concrete case or empirical finding both sides agree is relevant (for instance, clinical outcomes from cognitive-behavioral protocols that either isolate or integrate behavioral control beliefs; or a shared scenario such as a physician-patient negotiating end-of-life care where influence is partial but not absent); and, for each side, the specific prediction or interpretation they would make about that case.

Fourth, conclude with a brief, jointly authored assessment of whether any portion of their dispute is merely verbal—specifically, whether disagreements about control dissolve once both parties agree on a functional definition of agency—versus genuinely substantive disagreements about normative commitment or psychological mechanism. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke obscure scholarly controversies that depend on access to specialized literature; rely instead on conceptual distinctions and shared examples that can be evaluated from first principles.
