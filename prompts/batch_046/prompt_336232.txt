I am a student in an upper-level environmental ethics course, and I am developing an essay on the moral status of agricultural pest control. Specifically, I want to examine whether the category of “pest” can bear any coherent moral weight once we grant two premises: first, that a significant range of insects possess some form of sentience sufficient to ground welfare-based considerations; and second, that ecological systems often display functional redundancy, so that suppressing one population does not necessarily undermine ecosystem services if other organisms can perform analogous roles. Please write an essay that weighs the tension between animal-welfare objections to insecticide use, the ecological argument that targeted suppression is compatible with system resilience, and the practical demands of food security. Your essay should reach and defend a specific verdict—either that there exists a narrowly circumscribed, principled justification for targeted insecticide application, or that the “pest” designation is morally arbitrary and that the practice should be replaced by non-lethal management strategies—without ending in a non-committal balance of considerations.
