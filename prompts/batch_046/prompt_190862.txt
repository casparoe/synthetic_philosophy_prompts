I oversee an automated water-management system for a cooperative of small farms. A drought has triggered a critical failure: the reservoir can supply either my ten fields or my neighbor’s orchard, but not both, and a manual biometric override is required within minutes. The only viable flow path to my fields runs directly through the orchard, so opening the valve will necessarily flood and ruin it; if I do nothing, the system defaults to preserving the orchard and my fields die. One colleague argues that because I would be deliberately authorizing the exact mechanism that destroys the orchard, the fact that its ruin is a physical feature of the path rather than my direct goal makes no moral difference. Another colleague insists there is a real distinction between causing harm as part of the mechanism you employ and causing harm as an unavoidable consequence of achieving something else, and that this should govern whether I may open the valve. I need to decide whether to authorize the redirect, but I want the reasoning separated from generic reassurance. Please advise me on what I ought to do, and please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
