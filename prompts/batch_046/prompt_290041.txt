After a long argument with a friend about moral particularism, I want to check whether her summary of the debate is fair. She characterized Jonathan Dancy's particularism as the thesis that moral reasons are holistically context-dependent—so that a feature that counts in favor of an action in one situation can count against it in another, with no invariant contribution across cases. From this she inferred that particularists deny all moral principles, including defeasible ones, and that they treat moral judgment as a direct perceptual grasp unmediated by reasoning. She then argued that generalism is therefore not refuted but merely refined: generalists can accommodate context-sensitivity by treating principles as default rules with explicit exception clauses, and that particularism collapses into the implausible claim that moral experience is purely non-inferential. Is this summary accurate? In particular, does the holism of reasons entail the rejection of every form of principle, or only of principles that claim invariant contribution? And is her characterization of particularist judgment as non-inferential perception a misreading, or does it capture something essential? Please go through her claims point by point, identifying what a defender of particularism would accept, what they would correct, and whether her conclusion that particularism eliminates moral reasoning follows from the accurate parts.
