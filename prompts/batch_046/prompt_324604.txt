Rough notes: Ryle splits knowing-that (propositional) from knowing-how (practical), using examples like knowing that bikes have gears versus knowing how to ride. The inmates learn by ear and feel; they can't articulate rules, and there's no propositional content they could write down. But calling it pure knowing-how ignores that the cello itself "remembers" tension, the room's acoustics shape what's heard, and the group corrects mistakes without ever naming them. "Tacit orchestration" tries to name this in-between space—it's not knowing-that, but it's also not private knowing-how stored in muscle memmory. The objection I can't shake is that maybe this is just knowing-how with extra steps, or knowing-that disguised as bodily skill.
