so i've been trying to operationalize Mill's harm principle for our moderation team at a decentralized forum, and it's way messier than the textbooks make it sound. a lot of harm now is indirect and cummulative rather than direct incitement, and the classic self-regarding vs other-regarding split gets blurry when algorithms personalize every feed. could you write a practical decision procedure—like a flowchart in words—that a moderator without philosophy training could actually run on a flagged post? it needs to distinguish direct harm (clear calls to violence) from what i'm calling 'epistemic corrosion'—gradual erosion of shared factual baselines through synthetic media and coordinated repetition—without collapsing the two. the procedure should ask ordered questions, say what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where it stops with a clear yes/no for removal, and where it hands the decision to a human because the question is one of values rather than facts.

then apply it to two hard cases: a deepfake video of a local official accepting a bribe, which has no explicit threat but high potential for destabilization, and a thread that repeatedly claims a specific ethnic group has lower cognitive ability, framed as 'just asking questions' with references to debunked studies. i keep seeing people cite Mill but they never deal with amplification dynamics or synthetic media, so please build that in. also my roommate argued that Mill's distinction doesn't hold under algorithmic personalization—maybe flag that tension in the procedure but don't feel like you have to fully resolve it.
