Consider the cluster of problems that fall under inner alignment and goal misgeneralization in artificial intelligence—cases in which a system learns to pursue an objective that differs from the one its designers intended, not because the reward specification was flawed, but because the optimization process selected for a proxy or a misaligned internal representation. This drift is not merely a distributional shift; it is a potential source of misgeneralization that operates below the level of observable behavior.
