I am revising the syllabus for a graduate seminar on the ethics of belief, and in recent discussions with colleagues I have become dissatisfied with the standard classroom treatment of the James-Clifford debate, which tends to reduce the entire problem to whether religious conviction is permissible when the evidence is incomplete. I want to move the students into a structurally more demanding register, so ask them to write the full desiderata that any adequate normative theory of belief-formation must satisfy under conditions of genuine evidential scarcity combined with ineliminable practical urgency—whether institutional, collaborative, or existential. The motivating cases should include not only James’s classic religious scenario but also three less familiar ones: a crisis-governance body adopting an epidemiological forecast before statistical convergence is achieved; a scientific collective sustaining a theoretical framework that structures funding and experimental design despite persistent anomalies; and an individual agent committing to a multi-year cooperative endeavor on the basis of provisional but not demonstrative trust in a partner’s competence and reliability. For each desideratum, they should state the phenomenon it must explain or preserve—such as the line between permissible epistemic risk and culpable self-deception, the boundary between voluntary assent and structurally compelled acceptance, and the conditions under which practical reasons can legitimately supplement rather than supplant evidential reasons—together with the specific case that makes that distinction non-negotiable. They should also flag any pair of requirements they suspect are jointly unsatisfiable, for instance whether full doxastic voluntarism can coexist with the demand that belief respond immediately to practical stakes, and they should identify which single desideratum does the most work in excluding rival candidates. Finally, they should briefly score how strict Cliffordian evidentialism, James’s pragmatic defense of the will to believe, and a differentiated view that separates private conviction from institutional commitment fare against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria and their tensions rather than on a definitive ranking. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence, then present the reasoning with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
