Write a short story about a archivist in a post-conflict museum who has to arrange teh "Hall of Measured Harm" — an exhibit that literally ranks historical atrocities on a single linear scale from least to worst. The twist: her institution uses a niche ethical framework she half-invented in grad school, "dimensional incommensurability," which says some evils can't be plotted on the same axis — like the erasure of a language versus the starvation of a population — but the funding board wants one number for each event so they can build the physical corridor with exactly that many steps between displays. She has to defend her refusal to rank the Rwandan genocide lower than the burning of the Library of Alexandria just because the death toll was smaller, but she also realizes her own framework might be a cop-out. Don't make it preachy; show her arguing with a board member who thinks comparing is obscene, and with a historian who thinks not comparing is cowardice. Include one small detail about how the physical space of the museum (the length of the hallway) forces a false precision.
