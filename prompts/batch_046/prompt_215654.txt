I defend the view that Bertrand's paradox is not a counterexample to the principle of indifference, but a symptom of physical underspecification. On my account, selecting a chord “at random” must be modeled as a stochastic generating process—specifically, a midpoint distributed inside the circle according to a Brownian motion with a defined drift vector and diffusion tensor, together with an orientation distribution governed by the same dynamics—rather than as a direct uniform draw over geometric outcomes. Under this framing, the three classic answers—probability 1/3, 1/2, and 1/4—do not conflict; each corresponds to a different dynamical regime (uniform drift, radial drift, and isotropic orientation without positional drift, respectively). The principle of indifference applies correctly within each regime to the invariant measure of the specified process, not to an abstract, mechanism-independent set of chords. Consequently, the paradox dissolves into a taxonomy of generating mechanisms, and pure symmetry-based resolutions fail because they implicitly assume compact parameter spaces that do not hold for all physical realizations.
