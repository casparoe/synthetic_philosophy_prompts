I'm the chair of a hospital clinical ethics committee that reviews staff ethical lapses—protocol violations, disrespectful conduct, refusal to follow orders—approximately twice a month. Each review is constrained to forty-five minutes, must yield a written recommendation co-signed by risk management and the relevant department head, and must decide whether to pursue individual character-focused remediation or system-level situational redesign. I need a reusable procedural framework—ordered tests, a decision flow in words, explicit judgment nodes—translating virtue ethics and the situationist critique into operational terms for this role. The procedure must incorporate the distinction between local trait attribution and global trait attribution, and between trait-expressive and merely trait-relevant behavior, without collapsing these distinctions into common-sense intuition. It should include a consistency-band assessment across contexts, a situational-load scoring step, and clear markers for where quantitative judgment is impossible and qualitative assessment takes over. Provide worked applications to three cases: first, a veteran clinician with an otherwise unblemished record who commits a serious breach during an acute emergency; second, a trainee who repeatedly makes minor but similar ethical errors across different wards and supervisors; third, a staff member whose refusal to carry out an order is ethically justified resistance that has been misfiled as insubordination. Include at least one case where the procedure recommends system redesign when individual blame seems warranted, or individual remediation when the context seems overwhelming—an answer I am likely to resist. State explicitly what the procedure cannot accomplish, such as diagnosing personality structures or guaranteeing predictive accuracy. Separate settled points—such as the influence of both dispositional and contextual factors on moral behavior—from genuinely contested issues, including whether robust global traits exist, whether practical wisdom can be cultivated without restructuring situational pressures, and how to weight cross-domain consistency against local contextual variance. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; present no contested philosophical position as if it were the consensus view. The output should be formal, suitable for committee documentation, and honest about its own misfire points.
