I'm trying to work out what I actually think about how beauty and friendship interact, using Moore's basic idea that both are intrinisc goods but don't reduce to each other. Going by gut on cases. Telling my best friend her sculpture is beautiful when it's actually clunky and overworked: not fine, feels like I'm corrupting honest aestetic judgment for social comfort. Telling a stranger at an opening I love their painting when I only feel mild interest: also not fine, but lighter, more like social grease than betrayal. Staying close friends with someone whose appearance I genuinely don't find attractive: totally fine, friendship stands alone. Feeling aestetic rapture at a portrait of my friend, then realizing I'd feel the same if it were a stranger's face: that undermines something, maybe the idea that my feeling is tied to the friendship at all. Telling a dying friend they still look beautiful, even when they don't: fine, maybe required, because the claim is about them not just visual form. Writing in a card that our friendship is the most beautiful thing in my life: okay even if metaphorical. But here's the clash: lying about office art to get a job vs lying to my partner about the same art to spare their feelings. Both are beauty-lies for social gain, but the partner one feels way worse, not just scaled differently. And the case I keep flip-floping on: my kid shows me an ugly clay pot and asks if it's beautiful. I want to say yes because I love them, but I also want to keep some separation between loving them and falsifying aestetic judgment. Is there a principle that gets all these, maybe about whether the beauty-claim is functioning as a proxy for the friendship-value or being isolated? Or do I have to drop one verdict? What does it say about the kid case?
