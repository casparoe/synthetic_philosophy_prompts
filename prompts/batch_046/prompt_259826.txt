But I feel like everyone just repeats the same tired back-and-forth and nobody gets into the weeds. The proposal should state the question so it has a determinate answer, explain why the standard "philosophy is useful" responses don't actually settle it, lay out the method — probably tracing two case studies like quantum mechanics and statistical mechanics where philosophers reshuffled explanatory standards without making predictions — and argue why this historical-comparative approach beats just analyzing the critics' texts in isolation. It needs to say what a positive result looks like, what counts as failure (maybe if the heuristic distinction dissolves under pressure), and be brutally honest about scope since it's only four months.
