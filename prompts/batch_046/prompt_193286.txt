As a retired ecologist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mind, I wish to understand how teleosemantic accounts ground mental content in biological function rather than in correlation or syntax alone. For a reading group of fellow scientists, please construct a diagnostic taxonomy—a decision tree built from logical distinctions rather than from a list of named theories—that classifies the positions on whether evolutionary history can supply the norms of representation.

Please use four binary branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete question about a biological case that a reader could answer without knowing the philosophical label it leads to. The four distinctions should be:

1. Whether mental content requires an evolutionary selection history, or whether a system with the correct current functional organisation but no evolutionary past—think of an accidental 'swamp' organism—can possess genuine representational states.
2. If evolutionary history is required, whether the norm for correct representation is fixed by what the producing mechanism was selected to do, or by how the consuming system currently uses the representation to guide behaviour—using, for illustration, a mutated photoreceptor that misfires in a novel light environment.
3. Whether misrepresentation in derived representational functions—such as inter-organismal signals, for example symbiotic microbial metabolites that regulate host behaviour—can be explained within the same evolutionary framework as direct sensory representations, or whether such cases fall outside the scope of organism-bounded content.
4. Whether the theory allows that representational content can be fully determined for propositional attitudes alone, or whether it must also accommodate perceptual content, and whether any residual indeterminacy is resolved by the consumer's current practice or left as an open theoretical problem.

At each leaf, identify the named position that occupies that combination—Millikan's biosemantics, Papineau's evolutionary success semantics, an enactivist or anti-representationalist stance, Dretske's informational account (included as a non-teleological comparator), Fodor's asymmetric dependence theory, and any other view that genuinely resides there. For every logical combination that lacks a named occupant, mark it 'unoccupied' and briefly diagnose whether the vacancy arises from incoherence, from an unattractive consequence, or from the combination being unexplored—paying particular attention to any position that accepts evolutionary norms for direct functions but denies them for derived inter-organismal signals.

Please flag explicitly any pair of leaves that the tree cannot separate, and state what further distinction would be required to divide them. The taxonomy should be suitable for an audience without philosophical training, so do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Please present the answer in British English, in a formal but accessible style.
