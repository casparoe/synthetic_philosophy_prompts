hey, i'm a phd student in enviromental philosphy (mostly climate ethics) and i've run into this weird design quirk in my university's travel booking system that i can't stop thinking about. so when you reserve a flight the portal runs a carbon footprint calc and displays the full estimated climate damage in dollars—like it literally says this trip causes $420 in social cost—but then the suggested offset donation is always capped at $30, regardless of flight length or emissions. you can manually enter more, but the default is locked at that flat ceiling.

i'm writing about demandingness objections to consequentialism, and this feels like it preseupposes something specific about how much sacrifice morality can demand. on the one hand they quantify the harm precisely, which looks consequentialist; on the other they refuse to ask for the full amount, which looks like they're accepting a hard limit on individual obligation. is the portal implicitly committed to a threshold view of moral demandingness? or could a consequentialist justify the cap purely as a behavoral nudge without conceding that outcomes stop mattering past $30?

and there's this tension with our research office's grant policy, which requires full-cost carbon accounting with no donation cap at all—just internalize the externality. so the same institution seems to preseuppose one theory of responsibility in travel booking and a different one in grant administration. does that mean the portal's cap is just pragmatics, or does it genuinely rule out strong consequentialism? what would have to change in the interface if we rejected the idea that there's a ceiling on what morality can ask?
