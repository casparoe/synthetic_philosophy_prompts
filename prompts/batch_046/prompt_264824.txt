Write a deadpan internal corporate memo, approximately 550 words, from the Chief Ethics Compliance Officer of a fictional multinational firm that has adopted strict aggregate welfare maximization as its governing personnel policy. The memo should announce the rollout of new “Inter-Subject Utility Consolidation Protocols”: employees may now file “cross-personal hedonic debit” requests, by which one worker’s reduced workload or bonus is justified by another’s documented stress, on the grounds that suffering and satisfaction are fungible across staff and that “there is no metaphysically privileged boundary between welfare experienced by Subject A and Subject B.” Include invented bureaucratic instruments—e.g., “Moral Boundary Dissolution Forms,” “Unit-Level Pain Pooling,” “Consent-Waiver for Aggregate Optimization”—and present them as routine HR compliance. The satire should stay entirely in voice: never break character to explain the joke. It should quietly reproduce the separateness-of-persons objection—burdens are redistributed without regard to who bears them, individual consent is waived because aggregate welfare is “prior to personal boundary,” and the ethics board treats persons as interchangeable containers rather than distinct loci of moral claim—so that a reader familiar with the Rawlsian critique recognizes precise echoes (the denial of separate “containers” of utility, the fungibility of suffering across individuals, the subordination of distributive fairness to total sum) while a general reader still grasps that the system is monstrous. Maintain formal corporate diction throughout; do not use quotation marks around any real philosophical text.
