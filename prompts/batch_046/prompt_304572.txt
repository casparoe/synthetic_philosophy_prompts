Please argue that Descartes's cogito, properly interpreted, proves only the occurrence of a present cognitive event and not the existence of a persisting substantial self, and that the inference to being a thinking thing smuggles in an unexamined premise about diachronic identity. In particular, defend the view that the first-person certainty of the act of thinking establishes nothing more than an indexical position—what we might call an epistemic subject-point—without licensing any claim about the temporal extension or metaphysical kind of whatever occupies that position. To make your case, distinguish sharply between token reflexivity (the unavoidable self-reference of the act of doubting itself) and type persistence (the existence of a continuing thinker), and show that Descartes conflates the immediate givenness of the former with the substantial reality of the latter. You should also address the objection that even a momentary event requires a substrate, explaining why that objection either begs the question against the event-ontology you are proposing or can be answered without positing a Cartesian res cogitans.
