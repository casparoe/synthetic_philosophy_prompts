For a seminar on the philosophy of artificial and human agency, write the list of desiderata that an adequate conceptual taxonomy must meet to distinguish approval-directed agents—those whose action-selection is structured around securing a positive evaluative response from an observer, system, or social environment—from goal-directed agents—those whose action-selection is structured around bringing about specific states of affairs in the world—with particular attention to cases where the distinction blurs: for instance, when an approval-seeking agent develops internal predictive models, call them shadow goals, that function instrumentally like world-models in service of securing approval, or when a goal-directed agent systematically anticipates and internalizes evaluative feedback so that its goal-pursuit becomes behaviorally indistinguishable from approval-optimization. For each desideratum, state the conceptual consideration or edge case behind it, mark which are non-negotiable (e.g., preserving the distinction under conditions of motivational opacity, accounting for instrumental convergence in approval-seeking) and which are preferences of particular theoretical camps (e.g., behaviorist, intentionalist, functionalist), and flag any subset of requirements you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—especially if distinguishing the two architectures requires both a clean motivational separation and full behavioral equivalence in some domains. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing frameworks, though you may briefly indicate how a behaviorist, an intentionalist, and a functionalist account fare against the list.
