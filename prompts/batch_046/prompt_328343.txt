I'm presenting a chunk of my thesis at our interdiscplinary aesthetics workshop tommorow, and I'm trying to prep for the q&a without just re-reading the standard catharsis papers for the millionth time. The position I'm defending is that the old paradox of tragedy -- why we seek out art that makes us sad -- dissolves if you stop treating enjoyment as one uniform feeling and instead think about what I call 'affective escrow.' The idea is that tragic art works like a temporary holding account for grief or dread we haven't processed in real life. We don't exactly 'enjoy' the sadness; we enjoy the relief of experiencing heavy affect inside a frame where it doesn't come with obligations -- no one to comfort, no decision to make, no social performance required. The artwork holds the feeling, lets us sit with it, and then returns it not resolved but validated. So the pleasure is specifically in the mismatch between the narrative's emotional closure and our own lack of it.

The audience is gonna be a mix. There's Prof. Chen, who writes on Hume's conversion of passions and thinks talk of 'enjoying sadness' is usually a category mistake. There's Dr. Patel, an emotion-regulation psychologist who'll ask for mechanism and will probably say my escrow metaphor is just rebranded psychological distancing. Mira, a grad student in comparative literature, keeps bringing up mono no aware and will want to know why this isn't just that. Then there are two analytic philosophers who know the Levinson-style objections inside out and will hammer me on whether I'm smuggling catharsis back in through the back door. I need a prep sheet: the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased like each person would actually say them, with what I should concede, what I should hold, the weakest point in my own view that I should actually defend instead of hoping they miss, and the one question I'm dreading -- probably whether my account accidentally implies we ought to seek out more real suffering just to build up emotional debt to deposit. No citations or reading lists needed, just the sheet.
