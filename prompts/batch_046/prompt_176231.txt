In my graduate seminar on historiography, we have spent the term examining the dispute between Leopold von Ranke’s objectivist aspiration—to recover and present the past precisely as it actually occurred—and the sustained scepticism advanced by E.H. Carr and Hayden White. Carr insists that historical facts are never raw data but are always selected and constituted by the historian’s present concerns, while White contends that every historical account depends on pre-linguistic tropes and emplotment structures that render pure objectivity impossible. Despite decades of rehearsal, the exchange feels entirely static: the objectivists reply that selection does not entail fabrication, the sceptics reply that selection is already interpretation, and neither camp registers the other’s reply as more than a restatement of first principles. My working hypothesis is that this is not a single disagreement but a cluster of disputes running under one banner—perhaps partly verbal, over whether “objectivity” means intersubjective agreement, correspondence to past events, or freedom from presentist moral framing; partly about different disciplinary aims, such as explanation versus narrative coherence; and partly about a hidden normative question regarding whether the historian owes fidelity to the dead or to the living. I want you to diagnose the stall using what each side actually says, not by surveying secondary literature. Is there a substantive disagreement about the ontology of historical facts, or are the sides answering different questions—one epistemological, one literary, one ethical—under a single heading? If part of the dispute is verbal, identify which term is doing the hidden work and how disambiguating it would change the landscape. Given your diagnosis, tell me what, if anything, would count as genuine progress: a conceptual distinction both sides could accept, a formal argument about narrative structure, an empirical study of historiographical practice, or should we conclude that the question is ill-posed and abandon it?
