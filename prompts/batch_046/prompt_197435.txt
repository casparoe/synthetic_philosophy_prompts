The slogan is doing far too much argumentative work. I need a precise disambiguation.

Please lay out, as a numbered menu, the distinct precise theses this proverb could be expressing in this specific domain. Among them, distinguish at least: (i) a purely descriptive or predictive claim about social coordination; (ii) a prudential claim about avoiding sanctions or social friction; (iii) a deontological claim about respecting local autonomy or authority; (iv) a consequentialist claim about maximizing welfare within the local system; and (v) a stronger meta-ethical claim—perhaps a form of conventionalism or moral locality—according to which the moral status of an act is fixed by local convention itself.

For each reading, specify: (a) whether it is trivial or substantive; (b) whether it is defensible or false, with brief reasoning; and (c) whether it actually yields a genuine moral obligation for the visitor (as opposed to a reason of etiquette, prudence, or preference) specifically with respect to tipping, queuing, or jaywalking. Where a reading fails to generate a moral "ought," say exactly where the inference breaks—for instance, whether it confuses conventional regularity with normative authority, or whether it illegitimately derives an individual duty from a collective pattern.
