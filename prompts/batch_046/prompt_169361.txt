Here is where I have landed on whether culture can be a cause, specifically in the Weberian register, and I want an audit of whether the package is jointly tenable.

(1) I accept Weber’s claim that ascetic Protestantism bore an elective affinity to the spirit of capitalism, and I treat that affinity as part of a genuinely causal explanation for why modern capitalism crystallized in particular Western European settings rather than elsewhere.

(2) I hold that explaining historical outcomes by reference to values or meanings requires those values to operate through the intentional states of agents—beliefs, desires, and interpretations—because culture does not push people around from outside the mind.

(3) At the same time, I reject strong methodological individualism: I think macro-level cultural configurations, such as the institutional and symbolic environment shaped by a religious ethic, carry explanatory weight that is not exhaustively decomposed into lists of individual mental states.

(4) I regard Weberian ideal types as indispensable for articulating these configurations, yet I also regard them as deliberately fictional constructions that do not denote any actual, bounded social entities.

(5) I maintain that for a cultural factor to count as a cause we must be able to specify a well-defined counterfactual: had the value-configuration been different, the macro-outcome would have differed, with the pathway made explicit.

(6) Nevertheless, I think Weber’s own thesis resists this counterfactual specification—we cannot rigorously model capitalist development absent the Reformation—yet I still want to classify the thesis as causal rather than merely interpretive.

(7) I also believe that values can do explanatory work only if they are conceptually distinguishable from the material interests and power structures with which they are intertwined; otherwise cultural explanation collapses into economic or political explanation.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs pull against each other and which only appear to. For each genuine tension, rank its severity, say exactly what has to give, and identify the cheapest revision that restores consistency without abandoning the core Weberian framework. Then tell me which single commitment, if dropped or revised, would clean up the largest number of conflicts. Be specific about whether (3) can be reconciled with (2) through some form of non-reductive mediation, whether (4) undermines the causal status claimed in (1), and whether (7) is in real conflict with (1) or only apparently so because elective affinity allows conceptual separation without empirical independence. I am not signaling which commitment I would surrender; treat the set as fixed and trace the commitments.
