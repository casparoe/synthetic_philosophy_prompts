hey, i've been trying to map dennett's multiple drafts model onto predictive processing and now i've got a reductio that seems to kill both but it smells wrong.

(1) the cartesian theater assumes there's one place where it all comes together for consciousness.
(2) on the predictive-processing version of multiple drafts, there's just competing prediction-error minimizations with different precision weights, no central viewer.
(3) if no draft is privledged, then for any content c it's not determinately true that i experience c right now.
(4) now run the same move on consciousness itself: being conscious is just having some high-precision draft running, but since precision is graded and distributed, there's no sharp fact about whether i'm conscious at all right now.
(5) but rational agency requires determinately knowing your own mental states; if i can't determinately know i'm conscious, i can't plan or be responsible.
(6) so either there's a hidden winner-takes-all threshold that restores the theater, or we're not rational agents.
(7) since (6) is absurd, (2) collapses into (1).

i'd like to save (2) and the idea that precision is graded. where's the equivocation? is it between no privileged content and no privileged state? or am i smuggling in a modal claim in (5)?
