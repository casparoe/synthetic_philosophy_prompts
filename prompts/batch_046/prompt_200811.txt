I have been reading about panpsychism on my own — the view that consciousness or experience is fundamental and present even in very basic physical entities — and I keep returning to what philosophers call the combination problem. As I understand it, the problem is this: if every tiny physical constituent has its own minute experience, how do those countless micro-experiences come together to form the single, unified stream of consciousness that I have when I look at a sunset? Over recent decades, defenders of panpsychism — thinkers like Philip Goff and others in that camp — have responded with ideas like "phenomenal bonding," special "co-consciousness" relations, or even cosmopsychism, in which one universal consciousness divides into smaller ones. Meanwhile, critics — including figures like David Chalmers — argue that these responses mostly rename the mystery rather than solving it, and that panpsychism has not actually explained how combination works any better than physicalism explains emergence.

What strikes me, though, is that the debate never seems to move. Both sides agree on what needs explaining — a unified macro-subject with rich qualitative content — and both sides keep refining the same two moves: either propose a special combining relation, or deny that any special relation is needed. I have begun to suspect that the stalemate might depend on a hidden assumption neither side makes explicit: namely, that macro-consciousness must be literally composed out of micro-consciousness in the same part-whole way that a table is composed of atoms. If one side accepts that compositional model and the other rejects it, they may be answering different questions — one asking for a mechanism of aggregation, the other asking how a single subject can have parts with perspectives. Perhaps there really is a substantive disagreement about whether experiential properties can be summed, fused, or integrated, and perhaps the impasse simply reflects that our current empirical and conceptual tools cannot decide between these options.
