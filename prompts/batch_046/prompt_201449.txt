I have just come out of a long, circular argument with a friend about Zhuangzi’s reaction to his wife’s death—the drumming on the tub, the refusal to mourn, the claim that she is now part of the seasonal transformation of qi. We kept disagreeing about whether this is a genuinely metaphysical insight or a rhetorical dismissal of attachment, and whether the “four seasons” analogy really supports treating a particular person as interchangeable with autumn leaves. Rather than continue the debate, I want you to extend the logic to an institutional extreme.

Write a deadpan satirical piece, approximately 600 words, in the form of a corporate human-resources memorandum and an attached “Wellness Program Terms of Service” issued by the “Institute for Seasonal Qi Transformation,” an organization that has adopted Zhuangzian metaphysics as its official operating philosophy. The memo should announce a new “End-of-Life Transition Protocol” for employees and their dependents, treating death as a routine change in qi-state comparable to seasonal decay. Include standard bureaucratic elements: a compliance checklist, a grievance procedure (which must be shown to be incoherent within the system’s own logic), a sidebar defining “Improper Attachment” as a policy violation, and a brief FAQ addressing whether bereavement leave violates seasonal harmony. The voice must be relentlessly deadpan; the satire should land the real objections—that this collapses particular persons into undifferentiated process, that the four-seasons analogy smuggles in a false equivalence, and that Zhuangzi’s response might have been situational rather than universal—without ever stating them outright. Push these familiar ideas into a framework they have not inhabited before: a fully bureaucratized metaphysics of death.

After the piece, append a brief self-critical note of about 150 words identifying exactly where the satire takes liberties with Zhuangzi’s text—for example, whether his philosophy actually requires the abolition of personal grief, or whether contested readings treat his response to his wife as exceptional rather than normative—and state clearly what argument or textual correction would change your mind about the fairness of the parody. Specifically: if a particular feature of Zhuangzi’s actual view were shown to be central rather than peripheral, which feature would cause the satirical corporate-Zhuangzian position to collapse?
