As an undergraduate taking a course on comparative ethics and the philosophy of religion, I have been struck by a structural pattern in karma-based theories of moral desert that I cannot place within the literature we have covered. I want to know whether this pattern has an established name, or whether it remains unnamed and requires a fresh conceptual carving.

The pattern is this. A karma framework holds that wrongful actions generate moral debts that must be repaid through suffering, often in a subsequent existence. The repayment is said to be perfectly just because it tracks the original wrongdoer. But the being who actually undergoes the suffering is, by the framework's own metaphysical commitments, not psychologically continuous with the original agent: there is no memory of the deed, no shared character, and in many interpretations not even strict numerical identity. Thus the mechanism that makes the accounting work—the distribution of consequence across lives—is exactly what severs the links that ordinarily justify punishment: the sufferer cannot learn from the experience as a correction of their own past choice, observers cannot use it to deter the specific agent, and the sufferer themselves experiences the harm as unmotivated misfortune rather than as the working-out of their own agency. In ordinary secular theories of punishment we treat such a disconnect as a failure of justification; in karma it is treated as the very architecture of justice. I have noticed a parallel in some discussions of intergenerational collective guilt, where descendants suffer for ancestral wrongs despite lacking involvement, but the karma case seems distinct because the framework explicitly denies that the recipient is the same person in any meaningful sense, yet insists more strongly that the suffering is deserved.

Is there an established concept—perhaps in philosophy of religion, theories of punishment, or debates about personal identity—for this precise configuration, where moral desert is maintained only by distributing it across subjects who are metaphysically linked but psychologically and agentially discontinuous? If the nearest terms are things like vicarious punishment, transgenerational trauma, or puzzles about diachronic identity in retributivism, I would like to know exactly how this differs from each, and which if any captures the core of the pattern. If there is no established name, please coin one with a clear definition and boundary conditions, specifying whether secular analogues like inherited national guilt belong inside or outside the category. I will not be satisfied with a bare label; I want to understand what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges fall.
