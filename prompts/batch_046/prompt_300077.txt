I've been trying to work out my own stance on collective harm and the inefficacy objection—partly for a discussion group, partly because I keep finding myself defending incompatible intuitions across climate, diet, and voting. Here is roughly where I've landed, and I want to know whether the package actually hangs together.

First, on climate: I think the inefficacy objection fails because carbon emissions are linearly additive. My emissions are a definite increment to an aggregate pool that crosses harmful thresholds, so I have a distributed obligation not to contribute, even though no specific disaster can be traced back to me alone and my absence wouldn't change the total in any identifiable way.

Second, on meat: I reject replaceability arguments. I think refusing to purchase animal products marginally lowers demand, and that causal pathway—however small—is enough to ground a personal obligation to abstain.

Third, I also hold that if replaceability were actually true—if my refusal changed nothing about how many animals suffered—I would still have reason to abstain, but for a different reason: participating in an institution of systematic harm expresses a kind of structural complicity that is wrong independently of my causal footprint.

Fourth, on voting: I don't vote, and I don't think I'm failing a duty. The causal chain from my ballot to policy is so mediated by other agents that my expected contribution is effectively zero, and democratic legitimacy doesn't depend on universal participation.

Fifth, I think collective moral obligations must be grounded in either a real expected causal contribution or in a direct institutional role that makes me answerable for outcomes; pure expressive or symbolic considerations alone cannot ground a binding duty.

Sixth, I treat climate mitigation as a baseline obligation rather than heroic supererogation, but I also accept a "reasonable limits" principle that prevents me from being required to spend all disposable income on carbon offsets or effective climate charities—there is a boundary where personal projects become permissible.

I'm worried that the reasoning I use to defend climate action and vegetarianism pulls in directions that undermine my abstention from voting, and that my appeal to expressive complicity in the meat case conflicts with my rejection of symbolism as a sufficient ground for obligation. But I also think there might be an apparent tension between my climate and voting stances that dissolves once you distinguish continuous additive harm from discrete pivotal events. Could you audit this set for me? Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how badly, which ones only look tense but come apart on inspection, and for each real conflict say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision—giving up one claim or altering one principle—would clean up the most tension at the least cost. Please write this as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write an email or a letter.
