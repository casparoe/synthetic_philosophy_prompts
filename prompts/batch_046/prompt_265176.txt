My friend and I just finished a long, circular argument about Gödel’s rotating-universe metric—specifically whether the closed timelike curves are meant as a live cosmological possibility or merely as a reductio showing that Einstein’s field equations, coupled with a global time coordinate, fail to capture the reality of temporal passage. To settle my nerves, I want you to produce a piece of sustained satire.

Adopt the role of an expert philosopher of physics with a deadpan sense of humor. Before drafting, take a moment to brainstorm the relevant conceptual landscape: Gödel’s metric and its rotational parameter, the absence of global Cauchy surfaces, the distinction between local causality and global chronological ordering, Gödel’s own ambivalence about whether the universe actually rotates, and the standard objection that if closed timelike curves exist then the distinction between past and future collapses globally.

Then write a parody of a corporate employee handbook—approximately 600 words, formatted as numbered sections with HR-style subheadings—for a multinational firm that has relocated its headquarters to a Gödel rotating universe. The voice should be that of an overly cheerful but legally cautious human-resources director addressing new hires. Treat the philosophical issues as mundane workplace policies: scheduling software that cannot resolve meetings occurring in your own past, the anti-nepotism clause that prohibits hiring ancestors you have not yet met, mental-health coverage for “chronological disorientation” caused by the absence of a globally defined present, and a liability waiver stating that the company assumes no responsibility for paradoxes arising from violations of global hyperbolicity. The satire should exaggerate real features of Gödel’s argument—his use of the metric to challenge the objectivity of time in general relativity, the fact that every worldline eventually returns to its origin, and the debate over whether this is physics or philosophy—so that a reader familiar with Gödel recognizes the accuracy beneath the absurdity, while a general reader can follow the joke. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; do not ask the reader to verify sources. Keep the irony sustained and deadpan: never wink at the reader, but make sure the philosophical target is unmistakable.
