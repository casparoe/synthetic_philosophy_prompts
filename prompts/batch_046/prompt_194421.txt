A philosophy podcast summarizes P.F. Strawson's argument in "Freedom and Resentment" as follows. Strawson holds that moral responsibility is grounded entirely in reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, and indignation—which are natural emotional responses to the perceived goodwill or ill will of others. He claims that if determinism were true, rational consistency would force us to adopt the "objective attitude" toward all people, treating them as objects of management or therapy rather than as targets of moral emotion. Since we find it psychologically impossible to suppress resentment toward a wrongdoer even after learning he was fully determined, this proves determinism false and establishes that moral responsibility requires libertarian free will. Consequently, Strawson thinks the compatibilist/incompatibilist debate is a red herring: responsibility is not about causation but about whether our emotional practices withstand rational scrutiny, and since they do, we are justified in holding people responsible regardless of metaphysics. I suspect this gets Strawson backward on determinism and misuses the objective attitude, but I am not sure exactly where the errors lie. Which parts of this summary are accurate to Strawson's view, which are subtle misrepresentations or overstatements, and does the podcast's conclusion—that Strawson is secretly a libertarian who dissolves the free-will debate—actually follow from the accurate parts?
