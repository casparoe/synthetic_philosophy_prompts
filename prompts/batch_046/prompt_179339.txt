I am working through a course on the foundations of mathematics and have been discussing with friends whether Russell's paradox should be treated as a localized technical failure of naive comprehension or as symptomatic of a deeper tension in the ontology of logical objects. To clarify my own thinking, I have been trying to parse Frege's response in the appendix to the second volume of his Grundgesetze der Arithmetik (1903), where he confronts the contradiction Russell communicated to him. In the passage, Frege explains that the concept not falling under itself generates an impossible result when applied to its own value-range: should that value-range fall under the concept in question, then by the very definition of the concept it does not; and should it fail to fall under it, then it necessarily does. He admits that this strikes at the root of his logical system, especially Basic Law V, though he expresses hope that he might repair the damage without demolishing the entire structure he has constructed.

What I find difficult is determining the precise scope of Frege's crisis. Does he regard the contradiction as damaging only the specific law governing value-ranges, or does he recognize that it also undermines his broader commitment to the idea that every well-formed concept determinately fixes a complete, fully determinate totality? I am also wondering whether the passage permits a more radical interpretation—one that reads Frege's difficulty not merely as the presence of an erroneous axiom but as an implicit acknowledgment that the relation of falling under cannot consistently function as a first-level relation that applies to entities which are themselves constituted through that very relation. Might the text be read as dimly anticipating later concerns about impredicative definition and the indefinite extensibility of the concept of set, or is such a reading entirely anachronistic? I would appreciate help in clarifying exactly what commitments the passage reveals, particularly with respect to whether Frege distinguishes between repairing a single logical law and reconceiving the ontology of logical objects altogether.
