I'm running a small podcast Q&A next week with a political theorist who just put out a book called The Thin Contract, and I'm trying to prep questions that actually dig into her argument instead of letting her do the usual tour. Her core claim is that workfare and benefit conditionality don't fail because they're too harsh — they fail because they're "moraly thin." By that she means modern welfare strips away the old symbolic rituals that made poor relief feel like reciprosity (the parish overseer's visit, the workhouse intake interview) and replaces them with pure economic incentives, so recipients experience support as an unearned handout rather than part of a mutual obligation. She also thinks "benefit fraud" is basically a symbolic panic with almost no real fiscal bite, and that the Scandanavian model only works because high social trust acts like hidden moral screening — it doesn't abolish the "undeserving poor" category, it just makes it invisible.

I need about eight or nine questions, with real follow-ups keyed to the answers she'll probably give. At least one should press whether she's romanticising Victorian poor relief; another should ask what her view says about people who genuinely can't contribute, not just people who refuse to; another should probe whether her "hidden screening" story about Nordic welfare is empirically grounded or just a convenient narrative. For instance, if she argues that anonymity in universal programs proves moral screening isn't needed, follow up by asking why fraud and non-take-up stay low even where bureaucrats have no personal knowledge of claimants. Don't ask for bibliographies or citation lists — I just want the questions written out in sequence. And please keep them specific enough that she can't just say "that's a research program, not an objection" and move on.
