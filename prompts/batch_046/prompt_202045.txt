i'm giving a guest session in a friends highschool debate club on genocide as a legal and moral category, focusing on Lemkin's original broad framing versus the narrower UN Convention definition and the politics of why states resist the name. the kids come in thinking genocide just means the worst kind of mass killing and that labeling an atrocity is purely moral, not strategic. plan the explanatory arc: what idea needs to be in place before they can even see why the legal definition excludes cultural destruction, the one carrying example to build around (maybe a hypothetical government that systematically destroys a minority's language and religious archives but kills very few people — does that count?), the objections they'll raise in order and how the sequence should absorb each, the check that shows they've grasped that naming is politically loaded rather than just descriptive, and what to leave out at this level and say so explicitly. also flag the misconceptions they'll form, like thinking that if it isn't genocide then it isn't serious, or that cultural genocide is just a bonus harm. keep it casual, theres probably a typo somewhere.
