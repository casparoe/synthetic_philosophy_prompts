I'm working through a draft essay on Richard Rorty's antirepresentationalism for a graduate seminar, and I would value your critical comments on both its interpretive accuracy and its philosophical coherence. The essay argues that Rorty's rejection of the mirror-of-nature metaphor does more than dissolve the correspondence theory of truth; it also destabilizes the standard pragmatist defense of scientific justification through practical success, since identifying an experiment as failing already presupposes a minimal representational relation between belief and the causal constraints of the world. I try to show that if vocabularies are genuinely tools for coping rather than copies of reality, then the distinction between successful and failed scientific predictions risks collapsing into mere social consensus about descriptive utility, leaving no independent check on inquiry. From there, I suggest that this pushes Rorty closer to a linguistic idealism regarding scientific practice than he typically acknowledges, though I also consider whether he might reply by treating resistance and failure as internal vocabulary adjustments rather than external constraints.

My specific concerns are twofold. First, am I mischaracterizing Rorty's treatment of scientific inquiry in works such as Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature and Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity, where he seems to allow that the world can cause beliefs without thereby validating representational accuracy? Second, does my argument illegitimately conflate causal interaction with representational correspondence, or is it a defensible extension of antirepresentationalism to argue that practical success cannot serve as a non-circular criterion of justification? I would particularly welcome suggestions on sharpening the distinction between causal resistance and representational fidelity, correcting any misreadings of Rorty's texts, and assessing whether the essay's conclusion—that antirepresentationalism risks rendering scientific justification entirely circular—is a fair critique or a straw man.
