I just finished a long, somewhat circular argument with a close friend over whether the Kripkean necessary a posteriori genuinely undermines the analytic-synthetic distinction or merely relocates contingency onto the causal-historical chain of baptism. We kept returning to the same tension: if 'water' functions as a rigid designator, then in every possible world in which it exists it picks out H2O, yet our knowledge of that identity was entirely empirical. What finally unsettled me was an edge case we only touched on—what happens to rigidity when the baptismal chain is counterfactually disrupted? Imagine a Twin Earth scenario in which the phoneme 'water' is anchored not to H2O but to XYZ, yet speakers continue to deploy the word with apparent semantic stability; does the name still travel across worlds unchanged, or does descriptive fit quietly take over?

Given all that, I would like you to write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a closing quatrain, using only two rhymes and two refrains that must remain verbatim in every stanza—about rigid designators and the necessary a posteriori. Treat the two refrains themselves as rigid designators: they should hold constant like a name that does not vary with descriptive content, while the rotating rhyme-words and interior lines shift to represent different modal contexts—our world, Twin Earth, a disrupted baptism, and a counterfactual seminar. Please embed, within this rotating structure, the specific Twin Earth thought experiment: a speaker insists, through one refrain, that "We named it water," even where the causal chain points to XYZ, while a second refrain—perhaps tracking the origin—holds steady like "The chain was broken there." The poem should be philosophically precise enough that a specialist in metaphysics and philosophy of language would recognize the distinction between de re and de dicto necessity, and it should avoid treating "water is H2O" as analytic. Use the closing quatrain to bring both refrains together so that the structural repetition itself demonstrates what must be abandoned if we reject rigidity: not the empirical discovery, but the very idea that reference can survive modal travel unchanged. I am looking for something formal and measured—verse one might actually read at the close of a graduate seminar.
