Consider Benacerraf's dilemma: mathematical objects, if they exist, lie outside space-time, yet we take ourselves to have mathematical knowledge, and causal theories of knowledge require spatiotemporal interaction. Rather than surveying every response, identify the three most promising live strategies that attempt to preserve both a form of mathematical realism and a broadly causal epistemology—without simply dismissing one horn. Rank them by expected philosophical and cognitive-scientific yield over the next five years. For each, state the central conceptual move, the single most important open question that remains, and what kind of result—conceptual, empirical, or formal—would most change its prospects. Defend the ranking with explicit criteria (tractability, how many adjacent epistemological problems it resolves, risk of collapsing into a familiar position). Finally, flag which strategy relies on a non-standard or technically narrow conception of causation that generalist researchers should approach cautiously.
