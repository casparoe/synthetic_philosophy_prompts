I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about merit and desert, going by my gut on cases. I was arguing with my roommate about this last night and realized my intuitions are all over the place, so I want to see if there's a principle hiding in them or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Here are my verdicts. A surgeon who trains for fifteen years and saves lives deserves a high salary, even in a mostly socialized system. A child prodigy pianist who barely practices but plays beautifully deserves the applause, but I don't think she deserves a bigger cash prize than someone who grinded for decades and plays almost as well. A factory worker who shows up every day and does honest work deserves a living wage, but if the factory closes because of some global trade shift, I don't think she deserves a government bailout just because she tried hard. Someone born filthy rich who then starts a company and works eighty-hour weeks - I think they deserve the profits they actually generated, but they don't deserve the head start the inheritance gave them. A student whose apartment burns down the night before an exam should get a do-over, but I wouldn't say she deserves the actual A she might have gotten. Then there's the poet vs consultant thing: same IQ, same upbringing, one writes poems for peanuts and one advises corporations for millions. I don't think the poet deserves to be poor, but I also don't think the consultant deserves every extra dollar just because the market happens to value consulting more.

So there's this tension between effort, contribution, and luck. Is there one principle that captures all that, or do I need two? My roommate says since talent is just luck, only effort should count for desert - but then what about people with disabilities who literally can't put in the same hours? Saying they deserve less feels awful, so maybe effort can't be the whole story. I'm also stuck on whether "deserve" here is about rewards, about treatment, or about something else entirely. Can you lay out a principle or a small set of candidates that gets close? Say exactly which clause handles which case, what it predicts for the case I keep going back and forth on - a CEO who works insanely hard but makes terrible strategic calls that tank the company, versus a passive investor who does nothing and just gets lucky with the stock - and be honest about which of my verdicts it forces me to drop.
