There's this thing called the discursive dillema in judgment aggregation—like when a group votes on premises and then on a conclusion and gets contradictory results even though everyone is consistent. People like List and Pettit have been arguing for years about whether premise-based rules actually solve it or just smuggle in a hidden priority ordering. My hunch is that the stalemate isn't about formal results (we already have the theorems) but about whether rational group agency is even a coherent target—like, are we projecting personhood onto committees? I want a diagnosis: is this a verbal disagreement about what rationality requires, a hidden normative clash about democratic legitimacy, or is there actually an open formal question nobody's answered? Don't survey every view; tell me what kind of disagreement this is and what would actually break the deadlock—another theorem, a conceptual distinction, or just admitting the question is malformed?
