You are invited to deliver the opening address at the graduation ceremony of the Institute for Public Governance. The audience is mixed: the graduates themselves — mostly mid-career civil servants, policy advisors, and local officials — sit alongside family members, academic mentors, and a small group of investigative journalists invited to observe. The philosophical idea that must structure your remarks is the problem of dirty hands in politics: the claim that those who govern well may sometimes have to do what is ordinarily wrong (lie, break promises, authorise harm) and that this leaves a real moral cost that success does not wash away.

Please write a speech of roughly five minutes that does not merely decorate itself with the idea but lets it shape what you can and cannot say. Specifically: do not offer false reassurance that integrity is always compatible with effective governance; do not glorify compromise as heroic; and do not advise the graduates to retreat into purity and leave politics to others. Instead, use the dirty-hands framework to articulate a specific tension between democratic accountability — the expectation that officials explain themselves to the public — and the practical necessity of secrecy and strategic deception in negotiation or crisis. You should inject a somewhat niche conceptual distinction: distinguish between taint, a moral cost that genuinely attaches to the agent and demands acknowledgement, and stain, a damage to reputation or record that can be managed, concealed, or polished away. Argue that democratic culture too often treats dirty hands as a stain rather than a taint, and that this confusion encourages either hypocrisy or withdrawal from public life.

Write the address in British English. After the speech, include a brief reflective note stating which part of your argument relies on the most contested interpretation of dirty hands — for instance, whether moral remainder genuinely survives justified wrongdoing — and identify what evidence or argument would lead you to abandon that interpretation. Also indicate clearly where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
