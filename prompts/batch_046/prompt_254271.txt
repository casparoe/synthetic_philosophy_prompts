The peer added that Fischer's critics only contested the archival weight of his documents, not his underlying assumption that a state can bear guilt analogously to an individual, and therefore concluded that asking whether 'Germany' is to blame is purely an empirical matter of weighing evidence rather than a conceptual problem about group agency. Please audit this characterisation: which parts align with Fischer's continuity thesis regarding pre-war Weltpolitik, which conflate causal responsibility lodged in state decision-making structures with distributed moral blame across a population, and does the conclusion survive once one distinguishes institutional agency from the collective intentionality required for genuine national guilt? Respond in one paragraph, no more than 200 words.
