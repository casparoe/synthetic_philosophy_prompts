I'm working through a position paper on anattā and personal identity for a philosophy-of-mind seminar, and I'd like an audit of whether my commitments actually cohere before I present them. Here is where I've landed: (1) I take the Buddhist anattā thesis to deny not just a permanent soul but any metaphysical subject of experience—what philosophers sometimes call "phenomenal ownership" is, on my view, a constructed relation rather than a primitive one. (2) At the same time, I think diachronic personal identity is best explained by narrative-psychological continuity: what makes me the same person over time is the coherence of autobiographical memory and projected practical intention, not bodily continuity or a Cartesian ego. (3) I want to say that moral responsibility survives the absence of a metaphysical self, provided there is sufficient psychological continuity between the agent-stage that acted and the accountability-stage now assessing it. (4) I also hold that, taken rigorously, first-person pronouns lack a genuine referent under anattā, though they remain indispensable pragmatic devices in ordinary life. (5) Finally, I think the phenomenology of being an "I" is better explained by contemporary predictive-processing models—temporary "centers of gravity" formed by real-time interoceptive-exteroceptive binding—than by any enduring narrative thread.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what the least costly revision would be in each case. Rank the tensions by severity, and for each say whether the fix requires dropping a substantive commitment or just refining how I state it. I am especially unsure whether (2) and (5) can sit together: if identity is narrative coherence over months or years, but the felt sense of self is just a momentary binding event, do I have two incompatible explanations of the same phenomenon? But I also wonder if (3) and (4) pull apart, since holding someone accountable seems to require referring to them, yet I claim that "I" and "you" have no rigorous referent. If my framing rests on a confused premise—say, if predictive-processing accounts actually presuppose the very kind of ownership relation I reject in (1)—please say so plainly rather than forcing consistency. Be direct about where the package is weakest and what the single most damaging revision would be if I had to choose one.
