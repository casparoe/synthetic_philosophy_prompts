I'm preparing a feature for a science-and-ideas section on whether the Anthropocene is a measurable geological boundary or a moral verdict disguised as stratigraphy, and I need an opening poem that performs that tension structurally rather than merely illustrating it. Write a poem in three stanzas of blank verse—each stanza exactly twelve lines, unrhymed iambic pentameter—each stanza devoted to one physical trace of a proposed epochal start: the first to coal, steam, and the sustained combustion of fossil carbon; the second to plutonium isotopes and the global fallout layer of the mid-twentieth century; the third to the transoceanic mixing of biota, silver, and pathogens following sustained contact between the Eastern and Western Hemispheres, rendered without naming any year. Between the stanzas, place a single-line refrain that begins "Who drives the spike?" and completes differently each time: first identifying the stratigraphers who seek a formal Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point; second identifying the societies that produced the layer being measured; third leaving the agent grammatically unfinished so the authority remains suspended. The poem must treat the golden-spike procedure as a real scientific practice—recognizable to a geologist—while using it to ask whether naming an epoch is a descriptive act or an admission of collective culpability. Do not resolve the question. End with the image of an unlabelled core sample whose layers are unmistakable but whose official name is still in dispute, so that the form itself—three competing origins, a mutating refrain, an absent label—mirrors the conceptual problem: that the power to periodize history is inseparable from the power to assign blame for it.
