I have been participating in a philosophy seminar where discussion of Jackson's knowledge argument—the case of Mary, the neuroscientist who possesses complete physical knowledge of color vision but has never seen color—has reached a frustrating standstill. The same repertoire of physicalist replies keeps circulating: appeals to ability, to phenomenal concepts, to acquaintance, to the limits of a priori derivation. Meanwhile, the anti-physicalist insistence that something non-physical remains unaccounted for never yields ground. Rather than asking you to adjudicate the argument itself, I want a diagnosis of the stall. Is this a substantive metaphysical disagreement about whether phenomenal properties reduce to physical ones, or is it partly verbal—a dispute about what "knowing" and "explanation" must include, with each side answering a different question under the shared label of physicalism? Could there be a hidden empirical assumption, for instance about whether phenomenal character is functionally definable, that both camps take for granted but never defend?

To make the diagnosis concrete, consider a modified case. Suppose Mary not only commands all physical facts but can also run a complete predictive simulation of her own future brain states, giving her perfect anticipatory access to what her color experiences will feel like before they occur—without ever actually undergoing them. If the debate were fundamentally about reducibility, this predictive capacity ought to dissolve the problem; if it were about an irreducible first-person mode of access, the simulation should still leave a gap. Does this thought-experiment help reveal what is actually at stake?

Please lead with your bottom-line diagnosis in the first sentence—substantive, verbal, methodological, or a mixture—then provide the reasoning with reference to what each side actually maintains, and indicate what, if anything, could count as progress: a conceptual distinction, an empirical result, or an argument that the question is ill-posed. Do not include a preamble or restate my question.
