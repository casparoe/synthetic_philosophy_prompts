As a journalist covering science and ideas, I am commissioning a formal adversarial-collaboration document on a persistent philosophical question: do the sciences carve nature at its joints? I want two opposing camps—those I will call the Joint-Carving Realist and the Taxonomic Constructivist—to produce the statement they could both sign.

The Joint-Carving Realist holds that successful scientific taxonomies track mind-independent structures. These need not be classical intrinsic essences; they may include relational, homeostatic, or mechanistic clusters that exist whether or not we classify them. The Taxonomic Constructivist holds that scientific kinds are robust, intersubjective tools shaped by predictive success, institutional practice, and intervention goals, with no corresponding "joints" in nature independent of our classificatory activities.

Please draft the joint statement in British English. Structure it in three parts.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims. These should cover: the formal requirements of kind-terms (extension, intension, projectibility); the historical fact that taxonomies in microbiology have been repeatedly revised as methods shifted from morphology to genetics to broader genomic and ecological analysis; the observation that psychiatric nosology has moved toward dimensional models for several conditions, reducing sharp categorical boundaries; the working status of exoplanetary categories such as "hot Jupiters" and "super-Earths," which are observationally anchored but theoretically provisional; and the shared recognition that some classifications survive deep theoretical change while others collapse.

Second, a disagreements table. Each row should present one side's position in wording its own proponents would accept, followed by the other side's position in its own wording. The Realist must state precisely what "tracking a joint" entails—whether heterogeneous mechanisms can still form a kind, whether relational structures qualify, and how predictive success relates to ontological reality. The Constructivist must state precisely what "projected kind" entails—whether this implies arbitrariness, how it accounts for interventional success, and whether some projections are "better" in a non-arbitrary sense.

Third, identify the cruxes: sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party. I anticipate two central cruxes. Crux one: is the predictive and clinical success of dimensional psychiatric models (for example, in assessing neurodevelopmental variation) better explained by mapping continuous real structure, or by serving institutional and therapeutic needs? Crux two: does the revision or collapse of a kind—such as the reclassification of Pluto in astronomy, or the abandonment of "race" as a biological category in anthropology—demonstrate that the kind was never real, or that science corrected an earlier misapplication of a genuinely real structure?

For each crux, specify a concrete case or line of evidence both sides agree is relevant, and record each side's prediction in advance. For crux one, both might examine whether dimensional models improve long-term intervention outcomes specifically because they align with neurological continuity, or specifically because they reduce stigma and expand access. For crux two, both might examine whether Pluto's reclassification was driven by the discovery of similar bodies in the Kuiper belt (suggesting a corrected category boundary) or by a conventional decision to maintain a neat taxonomy (suggesting a constructed boundary).

Finally, provide an honest assessment of whether part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive. If so, identify which part—perhaps the disagreement over "real" dissolves once "corresponds to an intrinsic essence" is separated from "supports reliable predictions across interventions"—and explain why. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Write for a knowledgeable reader without web access, describing positions conceptually rather than by obscure technical names. Use British spelling and formal register throughout.
