I work in relativistic physics and think naturally in terms of world-lines, world-tubes, spacetime slices, Lorentz frames, and invariant intervals, but I have no background in metaphysics. Restate the debate between endurantism and perdurantism—whether a persisting object is wholly present at each time or is instead a four-dimensional spacetime worm composed of temporal parts—entirely within that physical vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report: which philosophical claims translate cleanly into the geometry of world-tubes, which must be distorted to fit (for example, assertions about "the same object" across slices that are not simultaneous in any frame, or about the ontological privilege of "now"), and whether any part of the metaphysical disagreement has no counterpart in how physicists describe extended objects in Minkowski spacetime. In particular, address whether Lorentz invariance creates trouble for one view, and whether the residue reveals a genuine metaphysical distinction that physics omits or merely shows that part of the debate concerns distinctions the physical vocabulary was never designed to express.
