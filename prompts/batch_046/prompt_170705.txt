I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm constructing an analytical case study for a long-form feature on how public institutions navigate philosophical controversies that sit at the boundary of settled science and conceptual explanation. I need you to produce a formal decision memo — not for publication, but as a rigorous standalone document — addressing the following institutional case.

The case: The Public Content Oversight Committee of the National Museum of Cognitive Science must render a decision on a proposed permanent installation, the "Explanatory Gap Gallery." The gallery is built around the meta-problem of consciousness — the challenge of explaining why, even if physicalist neuroscience fully maps the neural correlates of subjective experience, people continue to feel that something about consciousness remains unaccounted for. The installation includes interactive stations comparing third-person neural reports with first-person phenomenological descriptions, and a final room presenting the meta-problem as an open conceptual task rather than a dissolved illusion.

The committee is split. One faction, composed primarily of neuroscientists and museum scientists, contends that framing the meta-problem as an unresolved philosophical wound misrepresents scientific consensus, risks misleading the public into thinking dualism remains viable, and violates the museum's charter to distinguish empirically settled mechanisms from speculative metaphysics. The opposing faction, comprising philosophers of science and science-communication specialists, argues that suppressing the meta-problem produces a sanitized, historically misleading narrative; they maintain that explaining why consciousness seems hard is itself part of the scientific story, and that the museum's credibility depends on showing precisely where mechanistic explanation ends and conceptual explanation begins.

Your deliverable: A formal recommendation memo addressed to the museum director, recommending approval, rejection, or approval with specific conditions. The memo must contain a clear actionable bottom line; reasoning that both factions could recognize as engaging with their core concerns; an explicit and honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side; and a conclusion that the committee could actually defend to its stakeholders.

Before drafting the memo, please think carefully and step-by-step: first, briefly map the conceptual terrain by distinguishing the meta-problem from the hard problem and clarifying the role of phenomenal concepts; second, evaluate the museum's institutional obligations in light of those distinctions; third, apply the specific conceptual framing of "mechanistic transparency" (the state in which a system's operational pathways are fully mapped) versus "phenomenological residue" (the persistent intuition that subjective experience contains an element not captured by such mapping) to assess whether the gallery's design respects that distinction; and only then construct your recommendation.

You should adopt the role of a senior advisor with joint expertise in philosophy of mind and museum studies — someone accustomed to writing institutional documents rather than op-eds. Write with formal precision.

Crucially, please include an explicit epistemic self-assessment: state exactly what argument or finding would change your recommendation. Identify the specific point in your reasoning that is most vulnerable — for example, whether empirical evidence about visitor comprehension of physicalism, or a particular philosophical resolution of the meta-problem, would overturn your conclusion — and explain why.

Please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote any source verbatim; paraphrase freely. Do not invent specific scholars, publication titles, or institutional rules beyond what is required to establish the case. Keep the analysis self-contained, rigorous, and accessible to an intelligent non-specialist audience that includes both scientists and humanities scholars on the committee.
