Here are my gut reactions on inheritence taxes and equality of oppertunity, and I can't tell if there's one principle behind them or I'm just being inconsistent. Case one: a tech billionaire dies leaving 40B to his kid; I think that's wrong for equal opportunity, even if he earned it fair and square and wants his kid to recieve it all. Case two: a farmer leaves the family farm to her daughter, and a heavy estate tax forces a sale to agribusiness; that feels like a different kind of wrong, not just standard tax pain. Case three: some country lets you pass unlimited cash but has massive public education and healthcare, so poor kids still have real shots; I think inheritance tax matters less there, but I still feel uneasy about one family controlling a media empire for generations. Case four: a dynasty inherits mostly board seats and social connections rather than liquid assets, and uses those to block housing development near their estates; that seems worse than case one somehow, even though the dollar value is smaller. Is my principle just "tax liquid wealth but protect role-bound assets"? Or is it about whether the inheritance creates competitive advantage vs mere comfort? And what about someone who inherits a patent portfolio and licenses it aggressively, crushing small competitors—where does that land? I want a principle that captures why case two and case four feel different from case one, but I'm willing to drop one verdict if it buys simplicity. State it precisely, say which clause each case motivates, predict on the patent case, and tell me honestly which of my four reactions it can't save.
