I'm preparing to moderate a colloquium Q&A with a historian who has just published a monograph defending what she calls contingent institutional decay as the primary driver of the French Revolution. She rejects both Marxist class-struggle narratives and ideological accounts that treat Enlightenment ideas as causally determinative, arguing instead that fiscal-military overload, provincial administrative fragmentation, and a sequence of uncoordinated local breakdowns produced the crisis of 1789 without any necessary trajectory. She further claims that the Revolution does not, in any meaningful sense, consume its own agents: the Terror and the Thermidorian Reaction are not successive phases of a single revolutionary logic eliminating its own leaders, but rather unrelated regimes of violence generated by the same underlying institutional vacuum, each with distinct local causes. I want a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that go beyond restating her thesis. At least one should press on how she explains the organized, ideologically articulated demands of the sans-culottes and the sectional assemblies—phenomena that appear to exemplify class mobilization or ideological commitment in action. Another should ask how she distinguishes contingent institutional decay from older revisionist emphases on contingency, and whether her framework can account for the absence of comparable revolutionary outcomes in other eighteenth-century states that faced similar fiscal stress. At least one should target her claim about revolutions not consuming their own agents: ask whether she denies that there is a structural pattern linking revolutionary radicalization to the elimination of earlier leaders, or whether she merely redescribes that pattern in institutional terms, and then include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer—specifically, that the fall of figures such as Robespierre was administratively opportunistic rather than ideologically necessary. For each question, briefly indicate the anticipated answer and a sharper follow-up.
