I've come around to thinking Moore's proof of an external world is basically sound, but for a weird reason that isn't in the textbooks. Most people say he's just begging the question — he assumes he knows he has hands to prove there's an external world. I think that's a misreading. The proof actually works as an indexical demonstration: it's not deriving existence from premeises, it's showing that skeptical doubt about your own hands is performativly unstable in the first-person present, kind of like how "I don't exist" collapses when you utter it. This saves Moore from the circularity charge, but it opens up a scope problem. If the mechinism is this first-person present-tense presence, then the proof should only cover what's immediately present, yet Moore clearly wants it to license belief in past objects and other minds too. So there's a tension between the local indexical mechanism and the global anti-skeptical conclusion.

What would count against this view? I want the defeaters ranked — from fatal to just awkward. Not just the standard "you begged the question" line, but specifically: could someone coherently maintain skeptical doubt in the first-person present without contradiction? Could the indexical mechanism apply to hallucinated hands or false memories, undermining its reliability? Is there a reconstruction of Moore's argument that does the same anti-skeptical work with zero indexical machinery? And honestly, have I just qualified the view into unfalsifiability — is that a problem for an argument like this, or is it okay if it's just describing a structural feature of doubt?
