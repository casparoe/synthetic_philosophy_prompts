ok so i've been thinking about this weird split in tax practice. wages are taxed every april, but if you inherit stock your parents held, stepped-up basis wipes out all the capital gains—like the appreciation never happened. at the same time, the rhetoric around inheritence tax is all about equal opportunity, breaking up dynasy wealth so everyone starts from the same place. yet the code lets family foundations and dynasty trusts hold capital for generations with minimal tax if they spend a small slice on charity. what philosophical commitments are actually driving this? does it treat labor income and inherited capital as falling under different moral categories—maybe one is public and one is private entitlement? and here's a niche angle: if you apply the Lockean proviso intergenerationaly—each generation must leave enough and as good for the next—does the system fail not because estate taxes are too low, but because it lets families lock up appreciating assets in structures that grow faster than the economy, effectively monopolizing future opportunity? in other words, is equal opportunity being interpreted as just a snapshot at birth, rather than a dynamic constraint on capital accumulation? and if you replaced that with a view that treats long-held family capital as a kind of intergenerational commons, what exactly in the tax code would have to change—would stepped-up basis have to go, or just the trusts, or both?
