People in my game theory class keep comparing backward induction in the centipede game to pulling a thread on a sweater -- once the last player sees the payoff for defecting, the rationality unravels backward and the whole cooperative structure falls apart. They use this to argue that rational cooperation is structurally doomed, not just empirically hard. My roommate in CS says it's actually closer to a recursive call with a bad base case: if your termination condition hard-codes perfect rationality, the stack collapses all the way to move one, but tweak the base case -- bounded reasoning depth, noisy beliefs about the opponent's type -- and the recursion might halt differently.
