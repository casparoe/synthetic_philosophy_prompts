Design a decision-tree taxonomy for a philosophy-of-mind workshop that sorts contemporary responses to the poverty-of-the-stimulus argument in first-language acquisition. Begin at the root with the claim that the linguistic input available to the child is genuinely impoverished relative to the grammatical competence eventually achieved—answer yes or no. From the yes branch, split on the source of compensatory structure: domain-specific innate representations, domain-general statistical learning mechanisms, or cross-domain analogical scaffolding in which non-linguistic representational formats constrain the syntactic hypothesis space. From each of those, split again on whether acquisition requires explicit negative evidence or can proceed through implicit error-correction signals alone. At every leaf, provide the standard name if one exists (e.g., classical nativism, pure statistical empiricism, usage-based constructivism) or mark it unnamed; then state in one sentence the position's most awkward empirical commitment, paying special attention to any hybrid leaf that pairs analogical scaffolding with implicit error correction. Next, map the full logical space of answer combinations: for each unoccupied branch, briefly diagnose whether the gap is conceptually incoherent or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of leaves that the tree fails to genuinely separate and explain why the distinction collapses. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the instrument should be navigable using only the conceptual distinctions given.
