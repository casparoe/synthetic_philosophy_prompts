Putnam's Twin Earth case demonstrates that semantic content for natural kind terms is not determined internally. Oscar and Twin Oscar are psychologial duplicates, yet Oscar's term 'water' denotes H2O and Twin Oscar's denotes XYZ. Therefore, reference depends on environmental causation rather than mental representation.

This reasoning extends naturally to propositional attitudes. If Oscar believes that water is wet, the content of his belief involves H2O, whereas Twin Oscar's corresponding belief involves XYZ. Hence belief content is also broad and externally fixed. Putnam consequently rejected the existence of narrow content altogether — he maintained that there are no shared psychological states between the twins that determine reference. This distinguishes his view from Burge's social externalism, which allows that some mental content remains narrow. Furthermore, the same mechanism applies to indexicals: when Twin Oscar thinks 'I am thirsty', the term 'I' refers to Twin Oscar not because of any internal feature, but because the social-linguistic environment assigns it, exactly parallel to how 'water' gets assigned H2O or XYZ.
