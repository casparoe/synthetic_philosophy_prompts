Write a dialogue between a proponent of David Pearce’s hedonistic imperative—the view that biotechnology should abolish suffering and replace it with gradients of well-being—and a critic who accepts the abolition of involuntary suffering but rejects what they term “decoupled euphoria”: a hypothetical neurobiological state in which intense positive affect is engineered to carry zero motivational salience, zero behavioral reinforcement, and zero cognitive integration, leaving pleasure phenomenologically present but functionally inert. The critic should argue that this decoupling erodes the architecture of agency, meaning, and value that makes well-being worth pursuing, while the proponent should defend the imperative either by denying that decoupled euphoria is the actual endpoint of the program or by maintaining that such a state is still preferable to suffering even if it transforms motivation. Both interlocutors should be equally competent and well-informed; neither should be a strawman. The exchange should contain genuine back-and-forth, with each character responding directly to the other’s specific points rather than talking past them. Do not include requests for citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or references to specific page numbers or editions.
