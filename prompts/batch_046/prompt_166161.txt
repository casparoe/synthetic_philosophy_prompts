Essay notes: “The Authority Vacuum in Recursive Amendment”

The standard treatment of self-amending legal rules—exemplified by the classic Scandinavian paradox in which a constitutional provision sets out the procedure for amending the constitution, including that very provision—usually frames the difficulty as a logical contradiction or an infinite regress. The usual conclusion is that any legal system containing such a loop must either collapse into arbitrariness or resolve the loop by implicit appeal to an extra-legal source of authority.

I want to argue that this framing misses a functional possibility. Instead of viewing the loop as a failure of legal coherence, we should recognize that it generates what I call an “authority vacuum”: a precisely defined interval during which no institution within the system possesses unambiguous jurisdiction to validate or invalidate the proposed change. This vacuum is not an absence of law but a structural feature—an intentional “reboot window” in which the system temporarily suspends its own continuity conditions in order to reconstitute them. On this view, a meta-rule that authorizes its own suspension does not contradict itself; it creates a provisional state of “jurisdictional superposition,” where the old constitutional order and the not-yet-constituted order coexist without either having final authority.

The essay develops three claims: (1) that authority vacuums are formally distinguishable from mere legal gaps or contradictions; (2) that they can be designed into a system through “recursive entrenchment protocols,” where an amendment rule is protected by a higher-order rule that is itself amendable only under conditions that trigger the vacuum; and (3) that such protocols are not pathological but serve a stabilizing function, allowing a legal system to adapt its foundational identity without requiring an external sovereign.

I am especially interested in whether there are hidden premises in the move from (1) to (2)—for instance, whether assuming that a system can “design” its own discontinuity already presupposes a designer outside the loop.
