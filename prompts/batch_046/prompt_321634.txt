I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about advance directives and whether the earlier self gets to boss the later one around, going by my gut on cases. Here are my verdicts: If someone writes a living will refusing feeding tubes if they ever get severe dementia, and then they do get it and seem genuinely happy eating pudding with a spoon, I still think we should follow the directive. The earlier self called the shots. But if the directive was scribbled at 25 right after watching a sad documentary about nursing homes, and now at 75 the person has lived a full life and clearly says they want everything done, I think we should ignore the old paper. It was based on bad information. If a person is in a minimaly conscious state, occasionally smiles at music, has no idea who they are, but their directive said 'no heroics if I'm not me anymore,' I think it's okay to withhold. The 'me' they meant is gone. But if someone has a psychotic break and demands we stop treating their cancer because they think they're some historical figure who never had cancer, I think we treat them anyway, even though they currently have strong preferences. So what's the principle? Is it about autenticity, narrative continuity, or just how well-informed the preference was? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on: a person with advanced Alzheimer's who never wrote anything down, but their adult child insists 'Mom always said she wouldn't want this,' yet Mom now seems content and doesn't resist care?
