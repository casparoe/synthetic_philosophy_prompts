I've been working through arguments in the vein of Chase and Sanborn's claim that qualia are an incoherent posit, and I constructed a version for a philosophy-of-mind seminar that now strikes me as suspiciously destructive. Here is the chain: (1) Qualia are defined as whatever remains in experience after we subtract all functional, relational, and structural properties—the residue left when we "quine" the explainable parts. (2) If that residue is genuinely present, it must be accessible through a form of direct acquaintance that does not depend on conceptual mediation, behavioral comparison, or inferential correction. (3) Now imagine a "phenomenal calibration protocol" in which two subjects attempt to align their reports about a shared stimulus using only first-person alignment, with no feedback loops, linguistic correction, or functional comparison permitted. (4) Under that protocol, any disagreement about whether the two subjects share the same quale is not merely hidden from observers but structurally unadjudicable—there is no mechanism, even in principle, that could settle it. (5) But if ascriptions of a property lack conditions of correctness and error altogether, then the property does not function as a genuine concept; it is an incoherent posit rather than a mysterious but real feature. (6) Therefore, if qualia are the residue from (1), they are incoherent. (7) Yet the same reasoning applies to direct-acquaintance models of aesthetic judgment, introspective self-knowledge, and even basic proprioceptive awareness, which suggests the argument proves too much and threatens domains we are not prepared to abandon. (8) So either (1) mischaracterizes the residue, (5) overstates what incoherence requires, or (2) wrongly assumes that direct acquaintance must be calibration-independent.

I'd prefer to keep (1) and (5) if possible, because giving up the quining move or the incoherence criterion seems to cost the core motivation for eliminating qualia, but I am unsure whether (2) or (4) is the weakest link. Where exactly does this argument slip—does the unadjudicability in (4) genuinely entail incoherence in (5), or is there a way for direct acquaintance to carry normative conditions without becoming functional? Give me a dependency-aware diagnosis: which step, if rejected, preserves the most of the anti-qualia position while blocking the overgeneralization to self-knowledge and aesthetic judgment? Also, please say explicitly what would change your verdict—whether a convincing model of non-functional but correctable acquaintance, or a demonstration that the calibration protocol in (3) already smuggles in functionalist assumptions. I want to know where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
