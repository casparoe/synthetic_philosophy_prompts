In analyses of hierarchical systems that allow self-reference—such as those illustrated by Escher’s Drawing Hands—philosophers often posit an inviolate level: a foundational stratum that initiates the hierarchy but is never itself constituted by the levels above it. Without such a ground, it is claimed, any strange loop collapses into incoherence, because there is no stable origin to anchor the feedback. Applying this framework to Drawing Hands, in which each hand appears simultaneously to draw and to be drawn by the other, one must identify which of the two hands serves as this necessary inviolate base. Which hand occupies the foundational position, and what does its status as the un-drawn drawer imply about the ontology of artistic agency in the image?
