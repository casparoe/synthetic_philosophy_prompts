A colleague who studies evolutionary anthropology summarized the Darwinian account of culture—encompassing both Dawkins's notion of memes and the subsequent dual-inheritance models associated with researchers like Boyd and Richerson—to me in the following terms: "Cultural items are replicators transmitted through imitation and teaching, functioning analogously to genes. Because cultural transmission maintains far higher fidelity than genetic mutation, complex adaptive traits can accumulate culturally at rates far exceeding genetic evolution. As a result, cultural dynamics are largely decoupled from genetic fitness: a meme may proliferate widely even if it consistently lowers the reproductive success of its carriers, provided only that its transmission efficiency remains high. On this view, human rationality should be interpreted not as an independent causal capacity but as an epiphenomenal byproduct—what we call reasoning is simply the behavioral output of whichever memeplexes have achieved dominance within a given cognitive environment." I have the sense that this characterization blends accurate theoretical claims with subtle overstatements and category mistakes, but I lack the background to pinpoint them. Could you audit the summary claim by claim: identifying which statements correspond to actual commitments in memetics or dual-inheritance theory, which conflate the two frameworks, which misrepresent the fidelity and adaptiveness of cultural transmission, and whether the reduction of rationality to memeplex dominance follows from the preceding premises? Please also clarify whether serious proponents of these theories genuinely treat human agents as passive vehicles for replicators, or whether that framing is an unjustified popular extrapolation.
