I am a retired field geologist who, since leaving the laboratory, has begun reading metaphysics with the same attention I once gave to stratigraphy. The problem of persistence—whether a thing that changes through time is wholly present at each moment, as endurantism claims, or is rather a four-dimensional worm of successive temporal parts, as perdurantism holds—strikes me as structurally similar to asking whether a glacier is one enduring body or only a sequence of ice-stages. For a closing session of my philosophy discussion group, I would like a poem that makes this parallel do real work rather than serve as ornament. Please write a villanelle: nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a quatrain, with the standard ABA rhyme scheme and two refrains that alternate and finally converge in the last two lines. One refrain must articulate, in precise philosophical language, the endurantist position: that the object is entirely present now and entirely present then, despite apparently bearing contradictory properties. The other refrain must articulate the perdurantist position: that the object is an extended series of stages, each possessing one property in turn, with no single stage bearing the contradiction. The governing image should be that glacier—calving, advancing, retaining its mapped name—so that the reader must decide whether the image resolves the metaphysical dispute or merely reproduces it. The form itself should track the argument: the refrains return unchanged, as the two theories keep reasserting themselves, and only in the final quatrain should they appear together, unresolved. The poem must be conceptually accurate enough that a specialist in the metaphysics of time recognizes the specific difficulty with temporary intrinsics, yet it should require no citations, bibliographies, or references to particular texts. Write in a formal, measured tone, appropriate for an audience that understands the distinction between three-dimensional and four-dimensional ontologies but that also expects the verse to stand on its own.
