I was talking with my roommate about verbal disputes and metaontology and we got into this weird corner. So Carnap's idea is that ontological debates are just about picking a linguistic framework, right? Once you're inside the framework, asking "do numbers exist?" is just like asking "are there prime numbers greater than 100?" - it's internal. But I think this misses something important that doesn't get discussed enough in the standard literature: the problem of explanatory export.

Here's what I mean. Suppose you're a nominalist about properties. You reject universals, but physics keeps explaining things by citing properties like charge or mass. The deflationist says "just translate those explanations into your nominalist framework." But what if the translation doesn't preserve the explanatory structure? Not just that it's clunky, but that it introduces new primitive relations that do the same work as the properties you rejected? Then you've accumulated what I'd call "explanatory debt" - you've pushed the ontology around but haven't eliminated it.

Could you argue that metaontological deflationism fails specifically in cases where explanatory export is asymetric? Like, the realist can absorb nominalist explanations without loss, but the nominalist can't absorb realist ones without either losing explanatory power or sneaking in property-like entities through the back door? I want you to apply this specifically to the properties debate and maybe touch on modal realism too - does Lewis's modal realism have the same export problem, or is it different because the worlds are causally isolated? Don't need citations or a reading list, I just want the argument spelled out with the explanatory-debt idea made precise.
