I serve as the senior review editor for an interdisciplinary journal that publishes applied work in AI safety auditing, clinical diagnostics, and intelligence assessment—fields where authors routinely extend a localized knowledge claim into a high-stakes conclusion by entailment. Over the past year, my board has repeatedly encountered submissions that treat Nozick’s tracking conditions—truth, belief, sensitivity to falsehood in nearby worlds, and adherence across nearby worlds where the claim remains true—as automatically licensing any proposition those premises entail, ignoring that tracking can fail independently for the derived claim. I need a reusable editorial procedure, expressible as an ordered checklist of tests with explicit stopping points, that my referees can run in thirty minutes on any manuscript making an inferential knowledge claim. At each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer, flag where judgment about modal proximity is unavoidable rather than mechanizable, and mark where the procedure must halt with a rejection or defer to human expertise because the issue is one of values or domain-specific risk tolerance rather than formal tracking. Then apply the procedure to three submissions: first, an AI safety report arguing that because engineers track the architecture’s conformance to its design specification, they therefore know the deployed system will not cause catastrophic misalignment, despite the long chain of social and technical premises linking architecture to deployment behavior; second, a clinical study claiming that because physicians track the presence of a biomarker, they know the patient has the associated disease, even though in nearby worlds with the biomarker present but different etiologies the belief in the disease would fail adherence; third, the skeptical derivation that because I track that I have hands in nearby accident worlds but do not track that I am not a brain in a vat in far-away skeptical worlds, closure should still transmit knowledge across the entailment. Include one verdict that the board is likely to resist—specifically, a case where practical urgency pushes toward accepting the conclusion even though the procedure rejects it— and be explicit about exactly which tracking condition fails and why closure does not salvage the claim.
