Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two historians: one committed to Ranke’s objectivist ideal of reconstructing past events as they actually occurred, and one working in the tradition of E.H. Carr and Hayden White, who holds that historical narratives are necessarily constructed and that the selection and emplotment of evidence undermines any claim to neutral reconstruction. Their collaboration addresses a problem that has become concrete only with recent technology: generative-AI systems now produce synthetic primary sources—fabricated correspondence, forged census entries, and generated photographic records—that are being discovered intermingled with authentic documents in digital archives.

The deliverable should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can sign, covering the mechanics of synthetic archival injection, the epistemic risks it poses to both objectivist and constructivist methods, and the shared standards of evidence both accept; (2) a disagreements table stating, in wording each camp would endorse for its own position, exactly where they diverge—specifically on whether undetectable synthetic sources strengthen the sceptic’s case, undermine the objectivist project, or leave the philosophical dispute unchanged; (3) the cruxes, identified as sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one party; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or class of evidence both agree is relevant, paired with each side’s explicit prediction of what that case or evidence would demonstrate. The statement should avoid straw-man formulations, distinguish substantive disagreements from merely verbal ones that dissolve once the synthetic-source problem is made explicit, and remain intelligible without reference to specialized literature beyond the well-known positions named.
