hey, software dev with basically no philosophy background except podcasts and reddit threads. lately i've been thinking about how people decide what's okay to ignore or delete -- animals, AI, even smart devices -- and i keep seeing the same argument pattern but i don't have a word for it.

it goes like this: someone starts with a hard line. either a thing can suffer and has moral status, or it's just machinery/nature and doesn't count. then someone brings up a middle case -- like an ant that pulls away from heat, a brain-dead patient who still reacts, a language model that writes 'that hurts' when you delete its weights, or a thermostat that adjusts to cold -- and the hard-line people don't abandon the line. instead they say it's 'partially sentient' or 'a 0.3 on the scale' or 'pseudoconscious'. but the number always feels made up after the fact to protect the original yes/no gut feeling. every weird case gets its own custom grade, with no actual ruler connecting them.

i see this in animal ethics forums, AI safety debates, old medical ethics papers about consciousness. is there a real concept for this specific move? not just 'things exist on a spectrum' -- i mean the retreat from a clean boundary to a handmade gradent just to save intuition about the clear cases. how's it different from the usual 'where do you draw the line' problem? if philosophers don't have a name, what should we call it? and i want the edges: when is grading something legitimate, and when is it exactly this post-hoc save?
