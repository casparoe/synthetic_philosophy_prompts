I'm a software engineer without any formal philosophy training, but over the past year I've been trying to apply Stoic frameworks—specifically Epictetus's dichotomy of control—to how I think about system reliability, team dynamics, and personal stress management. In the modern revival, particularly in tech and productivity culture, the dichotomy is presented as a sharp binary: my judgments, intentions, and efforts are fully within my control, while outcomes, market conditions, and other people's behavior are not. The practical advice that follows is usually a kind of emotional firewall: detach from results, optimize only what you can directly manipulate.

Meanwhile, critics in philosophy and psychology have been arguing for years that this framing is either incoherent or ethically corrosive. They claim that control is not binary but graded, that modern popularizers have stripped away the Stoic commitment to cosmopolitan virtue and social duty, and that the framework collapses into moral disengagement—treating systemic problems as personal attitude failures. What puzzles me is that this debate never advances. Both sides invoke the same texts—the opening of the Enchiridion, the Discourses on what lies within our power—and neither moves the other. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is mostly verbal: popularizers are using control in an engineering sense (direct manipulability within a system), while critics are using it in a normative or metaphysical sense (the conditions for moral responsibility). But I worry there might be a hidden substantive disagreement—perhaps about moral psychology or about whether emotional detachment is adaptive—doing the real work beneath the vocabulary clash.

Can you diagnose the actual structure of this impasse? I want a committed answer, not a balanced survey: is this disagreement verbal, substantive, or a case of two sides answering different questions under one name? Ground your diagnosis in what each side actually says about the dichotomy and its modern application, and tell me honestly whether any conceptual distinction, empirical finding about emotional regulation, or formal model of agency could actually resolve it—or whether the stall indicates that the underlying question is ill-posed. If you conclude the disagreement is verbal, specify what distinction should replace the binary and whether the popular framing should be abandoned; if you conclude it is substantive, say which side is closer to being right and why.
