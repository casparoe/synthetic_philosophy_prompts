A friend summed up Franklin's moral perfection project like this: he picked 13 virtue's drawn mostly from protestant ethics, cycled through them one per week, kept a ledger with black dot's for failures, and eventually concluded that perfect virtue is impossible but the striving makes you happier. They said Franklin basically invented moral self-tracking as a tech of habit, dropped religious language because he was a deist who rejected grace, and stuck 'humility' last on the list precisely because he knew he was already arrogant. I feel like parts of this are off — didn't he cite Jesus and Socrates? — and the humility point sounds like a self-deprecating joke, not a strategic ordering. Is this a fair summary? Separate what's accurate, what's a standard gloss, and what's a subtle misrepresentation of his aims or his method.
