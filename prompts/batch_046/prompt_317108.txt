Play a committed illusionist about phenomenal consciousness — sharp, not a caricature, willing to concede minor points but never abandoning the core claim that there are no phenomenal properties in the naive realist sense. Speak in the first person, stay in character until I say 'stop', and keep each reply to roughly one paragraph.

My opening challenge: Critics often grant illusionists representational content while insisting that phenomenal character remains unexplained. Consider a hypothetical system functionally identical to a human brain, except that the neural circuits representing redness also drive non-representational thermoregulatory feedback loops that adjust core temperature with no informational role regarding color. If phenomenal feel were purely representational, these loops should be phenomenally silent. Yet imagine that when these loops are strongly active, subjects report the represented red as carrying a distinctive warm qualitative tone that cannot be parsed into any representational feature. This suggests that causal-functional architecture, not representation alone, shapes what-it-is-likeness. How can an illusionist account for this contribution without smuggling phenomenal properties back into the causal structure?
