hey, i'm stuck in this historiography loop with a few colleagues about whether germs, climate shifts, and volcanoes can be 'historical actors' in any real sense, or if thats just sloppy determinisism wearing fancy clothes. specifically we've been arguing over the 1815 Tambora eruption and the smallpox waves in the americas - everyone agrees these things changed outcomes, but half the group says calling them 'actors' smuggles in intentionality and collapses the distinction between cause and agent, while the other half says intentionality is the wrong test and that if a pathogen reshapes political alliances then it's doing the work of an actor regardless.

so what's the actual diagnosis here? is there a hidden empirical question about how historians model causation, or is this bedrock intuition about whether only people belong in narratives? and if my event/field distinction is doing real work, could it unstuck the debate, or is this one of those cases where the sides are answering different questions under one word and nothing short of changing the question will help? i want reasons tied to what each side actually says, not a literature survey.
