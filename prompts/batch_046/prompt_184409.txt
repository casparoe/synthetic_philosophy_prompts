A philosophy tutor has ruled on several arguments for the block universe. Her track record: she accepts Rietdijk's claim that for spacelike-separated events there is no observer-independent fact of the matter about whether they are present; she rejects Putnam's inference that this renders determinism unavoidable; she treats the twin paradox as illustrating time dilation rather than eternalism; she dismisses appeals to quantum non-locality as supporting the block view; and she regards the velocity-dependence of simultaneity as a reductio ad absurdum of presentism. Predict her verdict on this argument, identify the competing principles that could underlie her prior rulings—whether she requires only frame-relative simultaneity, the global failure of present slices, or full ontological parity among all spacetime points—and specify which further case, such as one turning on the conventionality of simultaneity or on a cosmological model with a privileged frame, would best tell those principles apart.
