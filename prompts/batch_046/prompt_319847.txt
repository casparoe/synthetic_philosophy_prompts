I've been working through the grandfather paradox in my own thinking, and it seems to me that the standard discussion treats it as if it were only a physical or causal puzzle—something like violating energy conservation—when in fact it looks structurally identical to the liar paradox. If the time traveler succeeds in preventing his own birth, then he was never born to make the journey, which means the journey never occurred, which means he was born after all. That is not merely an unlikely loop; it appears to be a direct contradiction, with the very same state of affairs being both true and false at once. Because contradictions of that kind are impossible in every possible world, not merely in ours, shouldn't backward time travel be metaphysically impossible in the same sense as a round square, rather than just technologically unrealistic? I suspect I am conflating causal consistency with logical consistency here, but I cannot locate the exact mistake. What hidden assumption allows philosophers to treat this scenario as a problem about narrative coherence or physics, rather than as a straightforward demonstration that time travel violates the law of non-contradiction?
