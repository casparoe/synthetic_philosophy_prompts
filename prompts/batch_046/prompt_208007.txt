My current position is that both paradoxes are artifacts of forcing graded credence into an all-or-nothing category: just as I can rationally assign 99.9% per-label accuracy to a classifier without trusting its joint prediction across a thousand independent labels, I can rationally hold near-certainty for each lottery ticket losing while withholding belief from the conjunction, with different action thresholds governing betting behavior versus assertion. What would genuinely count against this dissolution? I am not asking for the standard objection that rational belief should be closed under conjunction; I want to know which formal coherence constraints—such as Dutch-book vulnerability under graded stakes, or results about the aggregation of partial beliefs—would be fatal, and which would be merely awkward adjustments to the framework. Which intuitions about knowledge, assertion, or practical reasoning would defenders of this view concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss as category errors imported from binary logic? Does the view risk collapsing into simple probabilism, and if so, is that a substantive loss or just a clarification? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and indicate whether any of them are already anticipated and neutralized within a threshold-based account of belief.
