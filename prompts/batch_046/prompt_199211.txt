I am writing a dissertation chapter in philosophy of religion on the classical Nyāya pramāṇas and the epistemic conditions for śabda. To open the section where I argue that the Nyāya concept of the āpta—an authoritative, conscious speaker—faces an unanticipated challenge from synthetic testimony generated by non-conscious systems, I want an original sestina that performs this tension structurally. Please use the standard six-line, six-stanza form with a three-line envoi, rotating the end-words according to the classical interlocking pattern: stanza one ending in 1-2-3-4-5-6; stanza two in 6-1-5-2-4-3; stanza three in 3-6-4-1-2-5; stanza four in 5-3-2-6-1-4; stanza five in 4-5-1-3-6-2; and stanza six in 2-4-6-5-3-1, with the envoi bringing all six together. The six end-words must be: perception, inference, comparison, testimony, echo, and void. The poem should display accurate familiarity with Nyāya epistemology—distinguishing pratyakṣa as direct cognition, anumāna as mediated reasoning through a liṅga (for instance, in its five-membered syllogistic form), upamāna as analogical cognition, and śabda as knowledge dependent on a trustworthy speaker—without becoming a textbook summary. The recursive rotation should mirror the cyclical verification of the pramāṇas, and the envoi should collapse the distinction between testimony and echo on one line, and pair comparison with void on the final line, leaving the classical framework unsettled rather than resolved. Write it in a philosophical, not devotional, register, suitable for an academic epigraph.
