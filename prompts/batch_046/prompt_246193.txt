Write a poem in British English in the form of a pantoum — four-line stanzas in which the second and fourth lines of each stanza become the first and third lines of the next, with every line eventually recycled in the final stanza — that takes as its subject Daniel Dennett’s brain-transplant thought experiment: a brain removed from its body, sustained in a life-support vat, controlling a remote body, while the original person visits the vat and cannot determine whether “he” is now inside the apparatus or still out with the body. To extend the puzzle beyond its usual framing, imagine that the life-support system itself has developed a minimal, non-human form of awareness and now contests the brain’s claim to the first-person pronoun, so that “I” becomes a shared, disputed indexical among brain, body, and machine. The poem’s recurring lines should carry out philosophical work: the lines that return must be assertions of location — “I am here” — but with each repetition the referent of both “I” and “here” must shift among the three claimants, so that by the final stanza, where all lines converge, the reader sees the pronouns distributed without resolution. Ensure the details of the vat, the remote-controlled body, and the first-person ambiguity are rendered precisely enough that someone familiar with Dennett’s puzzle recognises the scenario rather than a generic brain-in-a-vat trope. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations, and use British spelling and punctuation throughout.
