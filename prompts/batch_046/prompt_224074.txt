I want them to avoid producing yet another literature review of subsystem independence or Cartesian boundary-drawing. Instead, I need a defended prioritisation of the open conceptual questions and lines of work that would genuinely change how we think about building safe, goal-directed systems.

Please identify the three or four most important open problems in this area, rank them, and explain your reasoning with precision. By "important," I mean upstream of other results: resolving or clarifying this problem should unlock progress on several others. By "tractable," I mean something philosophers or formal theorists could meaningfully advance without running experiments on large-scale models. I am especially interested in a tension that receives far less attention than the causal and decision-theoretic literature: if the boundary of the agent is not merely a causal or informational fact but is partly constituted by the agent's own normative commitments—i.e., which parts of the environment the agent treats as "outside" its value boundary—then embeddedness raises a problem of normative closure. Can an embedded agent coherently optimise its environment without either dissolving its own boundary or arbitrarily fixing it? How does this intersect with decision theory?

For each item in your ranking, explain: why it comes before the others, what a concrete success would look like within three years, what evidence or argument would cause you to demote it, and which other questions depend on its resolution. I am not asking for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations—only conceptual reasoning. Please write in British English, and keep the tone formal and precise, as if addressing a grant committee familiar with philosophy but not steeped in AI alignment jargon.
