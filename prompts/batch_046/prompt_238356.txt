Phrase every node as a yes/no/it-depends question someone could answer without already knowing which philosopher it maps to. The leaves should include named positions — Lucas-Penrose style anti-mechanism, the strict "it's only about formal systems" dismissal, maybe some epistemic-humility views about science — plus unnamed combinations. Each leaf gets one sentence describing its most awkward commitment. Also flag any pairs your tree can't really seperate; I have a hunch that some quietist readings and some weak epistemic-humility positions collapse together.

Please build in one niche framing I cooked up: distinguish syntactic-transfer views (the system literally can't derive the statement) from hermeneutic-transfer views (the system can encode the statement but can't treat its own proof as authoritative). Treat that distinction seriously even though it's not standard textbook stuff.

Organize your answer in these sections, roughly these lengths:
1. The diagnostic tree (about 350 words): show the branching questions and drop the positions into the leaves.
2. Leaf awkwardness (about 250 words): one sentence per leaf on the weird thing they have to swallow.
3. Gaps and inseparables (about 200 words): which pairs collapse and why, especially around the syntactic/hermeneutic split.
4. Why this framing (about 150 words): why separating syntactic from hermeneutic transfer matters for the debate.

Don't ask for a bibliography or reading list — half the people using this won't have access to a library right now. And don't just regurgitate encyclopedia entries; construct the logical space first, then locate the actual views inside it.
