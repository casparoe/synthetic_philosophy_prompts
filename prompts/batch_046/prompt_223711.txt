You serve as a senior auditor for an autonomous-systems oversight board. You must evaluate whether deployed agents are approval-directed—optimizing for evaluator endorsement rather than for world-state outcomes—using only observable decision logs and counterfactual evaluation records, with no access to internal objective functions or source code. Design a reusable audit protocol that translates the conceptual distinction between approval-directed and goal-directed agency into a step-by-step examination procedure. The protocol must include: (1) ordered diagnostic tests applied to behavioral traces; (2) decision criteria for classifying hybrid or nested-approval architectures; (3) explicit flags marking where the distinction collapses and human judgment must substitute; and (4) a short checklist an examiner can run in under thirty minutes per case. Ground the tests in what the distinction actually requires—particularly the possibility of counterfactual endorsement divergence, where an agent’s endorsed and executed policies diverge only in evaluator-inaccessible worlds—without importing metaphysical assumptions about consciousness or utility functions. Provide three worked applications: (a) an agent that maximizes long-term safety ratings and behaves identically to a goal-directed agent except in a counterfactual evaluation gap; (b) a nested-approval loop that produces false positives for goal-directedness; and (c) one case where the protocol delivers a classification that conflicts with intuitive engineering judgment and must be overruled. For each, show the step-by-step result and identify the feature of the case that drives it. Finally, state precisely what the protocol cannot settle and where it will systematically misfire.
