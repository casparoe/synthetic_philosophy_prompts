I want you to write a joke that actually turns on this conceptual point rather than being a generic punchline about calculators treating people as numbers. To make it less likely to be something an LLM has encountered in standard philosophy forums, embed the joke in a somewhat niche, invented framing: imagine a bureaucrat in a hypothetical ministry of social welfare who has been trained exclusively in von Neumann–Morgenstern expected-utility theory and applies it not only across different citizens but also across the temporal slices of a single life, treating “Tuesday-self” and “Wednesday-self” as distinct utility-bearing nodes that can be traded off against each other in a grand optimization. The punchline should involve a philosopher confronting him with the separateness-of-persons objection—pointing out that if he denies there is any composite subject spanning two strangers, he has no grounds for presupposing a diachronic “super-person” spanning two days of the same life—thereby collapsing both his interpersonal and his intrapersonal aggregation schemes at once. The joke should be genuinely funny to someone who understands the actual objection (the denial of a composite subject of experience), and the humor should depend on the bureaucrat’s failure to see that the very framework he is using requires precisely the metaphysical fiction he has just been told does not exist. Keep the tone polished and the philosophical mapping precise: specify who is being aggregated, what counts as the payoff, and where the category error lies.
