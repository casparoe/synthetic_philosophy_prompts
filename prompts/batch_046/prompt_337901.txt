Consider the present research landscape concerning the underdetermination of theory by evidence, especially in contexts where predictive equivalence extends across entire modeling architectures rather than isolated observational outcomes—situations in which structurally distinct representations generate identical forecast distributions over all measurable variables. Rather than requesting a survey of well-known historical positions, I want a defended research agenda: if you were directing a small philosophy-of-science group with three years of funding and no obligation to cover canonical figures, which open questions or lines of work should command the bulk of your effort?

In your response, adhere to the following sections in order, with approximate lengths as indicated.

1. Framing and Prioritization Criteria (around 150 words). Define what it means for a question to matter most for this specific group—whether measured by tractability with current formal tools, dependency relations among debates, practical payoff for working scientists, or degree of neglect. 2. The Defended Agenda (around 300 words). Rank three to four concrete open questions or research directions, not broad thematic headings.
