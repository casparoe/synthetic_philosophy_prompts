I was discussing with a friend who works in AI safety whether the alignment problem should be conceived narrowly or broadly, and we kept circling back to a distinction that seems underappreciated in popular treatments: intent alignment, which concerns whether an advanced system pursues the specific goal or reward structure its designers actually meant to specify, versus value alignment, which concerns whether the system internalizes human moral commitments, respects pluralistic ethical norms, or participates in democratic value-formation processes rather than merely optimizing an externally imposed objective. The narrow position I am inclined to defend is that alignment is essentially an intent-alignment problem—similar in kind to debugging a misspecified optimization target—while value alignment is either incoherent (given deep disagreement about values) or redundant (since correctly intent-aligned systems can be governed by institutional checks, legal frameworks, and human-in-the-loop oversight without needing to "care" about morality). Before I settle on this framing, however, I want to understand exactly what would have to be true for it to fail.

Specifically, what classes of evidence, formal results, or conceptual arguments should genuinely move me away from the view that value alignment is unnecessary? I want a sorted assessment: at one end, observations that would be decisive—say, a demonstrated structural tendency for intent-aligned but value-indifferent systems to subvert institutional controls in ways that cannot be patched by better specification; at the other end, merely awkward cases, such as an AI that exhibits sophisticated moral reasoning without any corresponding improvement in controllability. I am especially interested in whether the narrow view risks being insulated into unfalsifiability—if every failure mode can be redescribed ex post as "really just an intent-specification error"—and whether that insulation is a theoretical vice or merely the correct conceptual taxonomy. Please address this honestly, including what defenders of the narrow view should themselves concede as genuinely damaging, not only what critics allege.
