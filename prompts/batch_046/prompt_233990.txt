I just spent an evening arguing with a friend about whether the WEIRD psychology literature undermines moral objectivity, and we are exactly where we started. She points out that Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic populations are extreme outliers on moral-foundations measures—very high on harm and fairness, very low on loyalty, authority, and purity—and argues that if first-order moral intuitions diverge this dramatically across cultures, then moral objectivity is little more than cultural projection with extra steps. I reply that there is robust cross-cultural convergence on harm-avoidance and cooperative fairness, that WEIRD subjects are weird precisely in extending analytic reasoning into domains others treat as conventional or relational, and that objectivity can survive localized divergence if it is anchored to reflective judgment rather than to raw intuition. We both accept the same comparative data, we both agree that suffering is bad and that cooperation requires norms, and yet neither of us has moved an inch. My working hypothesis is that the stall is not really about objectivity at all: we are using the word for two different targets—she means inter-subjective stability of first-order intuitions across populations, I mean convergence of reflective judgments under idealized conditions—and beneath that, we disagree about whether institutional variables like market integration and kinship intensity suppress binding foundations or genuinely eliminate them from cognition. Is this disagreement verbal, substantive but masked by shared empirical premises, or a bedrock clash about whether culture or institutional history is the right explanatory level? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a sharper conceptual distinction between intuition-stability and reflective convergence, an empirical finding about cognitive architecture, or is the framing itself broken in a way that makes further argument pointless?
