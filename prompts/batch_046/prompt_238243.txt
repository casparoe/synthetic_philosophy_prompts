I run a forecasting workshop for grad students — about 10 people, 90 min. I want a game that makes them feel why the outside view of empires is so hard to hold onto once inside details start screaming. Invent a fictional empire ("The Hexarchate") and build reference-class cards with rough base-rate lifespans for continental, maritime, and tributary systems. At minute 30, hand them a "classified briefing" with very specific internal crises — a mad heir, a currency collapse — but these details secretly belong to a different reference class than the one they initially bet on. They wager points (fake money) on collapse year. Design rules, materials, payoff scheme, timeline, and a debrief that maps each stage onto the reference-class problem and outside-view failure. Be frank about which forecasting feature the activity can't reproduce. Also say what will predicably go wrong first run. Lead with the bottom line — core mechanic and debrief insight in one paragraph — then the details. Max 450 words, no citations or reading lists.
