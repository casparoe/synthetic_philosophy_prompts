I'm a high school senior writing an independent research paper for my advanced European history seminar on Machiavelli's paired concepts of fortuna and virtù. Recently, I've been examining how modern states use predictive algorithms and large-scale behavioral analytics to anticipate and preempt collective political action—treating potential unrest as a forecasting problem managed through automated risk-scoring and preemptive resource allocation rather than through direct personal leadership. If Machiavelli were observing a regime that governs in this way, how do you think he would assess whether its rulers are genuinely exercising virtù? In particular, would he regard algorithmic anticipation as an extension of a prince's power over fortuna, or would he instead see it as proof that the rulers have become passive dependents on an artificial, technologically mediated form of fortuna—one they neither create nor truly command? I want to understand whether his framework can distinguish between mastering chance through human boldness and merely outsourcing chance to an opaque system of computation.
