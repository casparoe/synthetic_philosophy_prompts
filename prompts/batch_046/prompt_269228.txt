Write a short dialogue between two people — call them A and B, both sharp, neither a strawman — arguing about simultaneous invention, using Newton/Leibniz and Darwin/Wallace as the main examples. Don't just rehash the usual "great man vs inevitable progress" line; instead, have them work through whether near-simultaneous arrival at calculus or natural selection means individual credit is mostly a legal fiction, or whether thier different conceptual routes (fluxions vs differentials, Darwin's domestic-breeding analogy vs Wallace's biogeographic spike) show that "the same discovery" was actually two distinct achievements that happen to overlap. They should reply to each other's actual points rather than talking past one another. Use British English throughout. At the very end, include a line on its own that starts with "Verdict:" giving a one-sentence summary of the tension they leave unresolved.
