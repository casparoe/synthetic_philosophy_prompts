Write a satirical, deadpan operating manual for a fictional management-consulting firm called Virtù Partners, presented as a glossy internal guide for tech executives. The manual should parody the practice of translating Machiavelli's political concepts—specifically that virtù is the adaptive capacity to shape fortuna rather than conventional moral virtue, that fortune remains partly uncontrollable and demands bold, timely action, and that effective rule often requires accepting moral ambiguity—into neutral corporate metrics and leadership frameworks. Format it with numbered sections, bullet points, and a brief "FAQ for Founders." Invent specific tools that exaggerate these ideas: for example, a Virtù Index that rewards strategic inconsistency, a Fortuna Hedge for market volatility, and a "Borgia Launch Protocol" case study that applies Machiavelli's reasoning to a modern product rollout, pushing the logic to a reductio that makes the ethical cost explicit. Keep the voice entirely deadpan—like a McKinsey-style report—so that a specialist recognizes the satire as fair and a non-specialist can still read it as a strange but coherent business document. The piece should be about 600 words. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or direct quotations from Machiavelli's texts; paraphrase the relevant ideas in your own words. Target the style of discourse that strips political realism of its danger and repackages it as value-neutral management science.
