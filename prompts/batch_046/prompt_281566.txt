I have been thinking on my own about whether properties such as being a contract, being obsolete, or being a sovereign authority share the same kind of reality as redness or circularity. Most discussions I encounter treat properties as a single class, but it seems to me that those rooted in human practices might require a different metaphysical account. If a person without academic affiliation or research funding—just sustained time to think—wished to make real progress on the problem of universals over the next several years, how should they allocate their effort? I want three open lines of inquiry ranked in order of priority: first, whether the traditional realism-versus-nominalism division is still the most productive axis, or whether the deeper fault line concerns how practice-dependent properties relate to practice-independent ones; second, whether resolving that fault line carries any weight outside metaphysics—specifically for how we classify legal statuses, diseases, or technological functions; and third, whether a solitary researcher could develop a coherent intermediate view, one that treats certain universals as framework-relative rather than fully mind-independent or merely nominal, without requiring empirical laboratory work or archival access. For each position in the ranking, defend it with explicit criteria: importance relative to the others, tractability for an isolated thinker, the number of downstream questions that depend on it, and what specific counterexample or conceptual clarification in the near term would cause you to reorder them. This is for my own long-form planning, so deliver a defended agenda with reasoning, not a survey of authors or a list of texts.
