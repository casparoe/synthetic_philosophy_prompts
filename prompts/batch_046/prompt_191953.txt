I recently became the primary caregiver for a parent with progressive dementia, and the experience has made me acutely aware that standard Confucian accounts of filial duty assume a reciprocity—mutual recognition, shared ritual context, emotional attunement—that no longer exists in our case, yet the obligations do not feel dissolved. I want a proposal for a one-semester philosophical project of roughly eight thousand words that asks whether Confucian role ethics can generate normative guidance for what I will call interstitial care obligations: duties that persist in the gap between a formally intact social role (the filial child) and the collapse of the relational conditions usually taken to ground that role’s authority, without importing universalist or consequentialist frameworks.

State the question precisely and with a determinate negative option: do the classical dynamics of remonstration, the distinction between serving parents and blindly obeying them, and the idea of extending care outward from the family contain an implicit separation between role-obligation and role-performance that survives cognitive asymmetry, or do they necessarily depend on a reciprocity condition that dementia violates? Explain why existing reconstructions leave this open: contemporary interpretations tend to treat roles as constituted by reciprocal practices and shared meanings, which makes them illuminating for healthy family dynamics but silent on technologically mediated, long-distance, or cognitively asymmetric care. Note why the obvious alternative—treating ren as a universal virtue that simply applies regardless of role—collapses role ethics into virtue ethics and abandons the specific normative structure the project seeks to preserve.

Propose a method of conceptual reconstruction rather than empirical study: isolate the logical structure of role-obligation in the classical texts, determine which conditions are presented as necessary versus merely facilitating, and test whether mutual recognition belongs to the first category. A successful result should deliver a taxonomy of three modes—fully reciprocal, asymmetrically sustained, and role-transitional—with criteria for legitimate action in each. A clear failure condition should also be specified: if the texts consistently tie filial duty to conditions that cognitive decline necessarily destroys, then the project must conclude that Confucian role ethics offers no internal guidance here, which is itself a determinate philosophical outcome.

Keep the scope realistic: restrict analysis to the parent-child relation and to cognitive asymmetry alone, avoid primary-language philology, state what will be treated as given, and do not include a bibliography or citation list, as I will supply references separately. The deliverable should be a polished two-page prospectus suitable for a graduate seminar, arguing both that the work is worth doing and that it can be completed within one semester.
