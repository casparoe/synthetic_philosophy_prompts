You serve as the permanent methodological referee for an international arbitration tribunal that adjudicates causal-responsibility disputes in complex adaptive infrastructures—systems that learn, update, or self-modify and therefore cannot be experimentally reset to test counterfactual histories. The tribunal receives cases on a recurring basis: within seventy-two hours of filing, you must issue a standardized determination form, co-signed by a technical reviewer and a legal officer who lack philosophical training, stating whether a specified design parameter, policy threshold, or algorithmic weight counts as a cause of a documented systemic failure, or of its absence, for liability and remediation purposes. The stakes involve binding financial obligations and operational mandates, but direct experimental replication is impossible; your procedure must therefore function using only observational traces, structural models, and documented intervention points.

Construct a reusable decision procedure—formatted as an ordered checklist with explicit decision nodes and a word-described flowchart—that integrates counterfactual-dependency reasoning, which evaluates whether an outcome depends on the factor across relevant possible worlds governed by the actual laws, with interventionist-manipulation reasoning, which evaluates whether a feasible intervention on that factor within the system's actual design space would alter the outcome. The procedure must explicitly resolve conflicts between the two tracks, including cases where a parameter is technically adjustable in the design specification but was never actually modified in world history, and cases where a factor exhibits strong counterfactual dependency yet lies outside any institutionally or physically feasible intervention pathway. At each conflict node, specify whether the procedure defaults to one track, demands supplementary evidence, or flags the dispute for unavoidable human judgment.

Provide three worked applications: first, a case in which both tracks converge and the procedure returns an unambiguous positive determination; second, a case in which the tracks diverge and the procedure mandates a negative liability finding despite robust counterfactual dependency—an outcome that your legal reviewers will find counterintuitive and resist; and third, a case in which the procedure returns an indeterminate result, together with precise instructions on what additional structural or historical information would be required to resolve the ambiguity.

Conclude by delineating the procedure's hard limits: identify which philosophical distinctions it suppresses for operational usability, specify the categories of causal claim that fall permanently outside its scope, and mark every point at which a referee's discretionary judgment cannot be eliminated.
