I am taking a graduate seminar on philosophy of physics, and our recent discussion turned to whether panpsychism, if correct, creates an overwhelming ethical crisis—since fundamental fields and particles might possess some form of sentience, multiplying moral patients to an astronomical scale. To avoid this, one of my colleagues proposed what she called the symmetry-coherence exclusion principle: the idea that phenomenal properties are physically instantiated only where thermodynamic irreversibility breaks local gauge symmetry, which means sentience is restricted to macroscopic dissipative structures rather than distributed uniformly across quantum fields. I am skeptical that this boundary is anything more than an ad hoc rescue, but I also worry that rejecting it commits us to the view that every vacuum fluctuation is a site of potential suffering. Write an essay that evaluates whether panpsychism genuinely entails a moral emergency about the fabric of physics, treating the symmetry-coherence exclusion principle as a central test case. Weigh the arguments that all pan-sentient substrates deserve direct moral consideration against the arguments that coherence and symmetry suppress phenomenal integration at microscopic scales, and reach a justified conclusion about whether we should embrace an ethical crisis, reject panpsychism on normative grounds, or adopt a constrained version like the proposed exclusion principle. Lead with your bottom-line conclusion in the first sentence, then develop the reasoning with no preamble and without restating the question.
