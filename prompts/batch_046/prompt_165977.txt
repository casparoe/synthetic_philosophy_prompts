hey, phd student in phil of mind here. been reading a ton on teleosemantics and naturalizing mental content and i keep noticing this pattern that feels like it should have a name, but maybe im just missing the literature.

so picture a cognitive mechanism that evolved to track some feature in the wild—like a bird recognizing a mating call, or a visual system picking up predator movement. in the normal evolutionary context, when it fires, thats because the feature is actually there and the tracking serves some biological point (finding a mate, catching food, whatever). but then in the lab or in some engineered environment, you can set up conditions where the mechanism fires exactly right—tracks the feature perfectly—but the feature is only present because someone reproduced the sensory signature artificially, or because modern tech happens to hit the same parameters. the bird responds to a speaker playing the call. a face-recognition system responds to a hyperrealistic digital render. the tracking is accurate, the mechanism isnt broken, but the biological 'point' of the tracking is totally absent—theres no mate, no prey, no evolutionary payoff.

in the debates i see, people immediately want to call this misrepresentation, but that doesnt fit because the content is true—there really is a call-like sound, a face-like pattern. its also not evolutionary mismatch in the usual sense, because the mechanism isnt malfunctioning or producing maladaptive behavior; if anything its working too well, just in a context that severs the link to the evolutionary history that fixes its function. some authors treat these cases as failures of 'normal conditions', others use them to argue that content is fixed regardless of context. but nobody seems to have named the specific configuration where representation succeeds precisely because the environment has been decoupled from the evolutionary context that defines the function.

is there an established term for this? if there isnt, can you coin one and define it precisely—say exactly what distinguishes it from misrepresentation, from evolutionary mismatch, and from a simple failure of normal conditions? and please take a definite stance: do you think its a genuine problem for teleosemantic accounts, or just a confused category that collapses once we get the function/pluralism distinction right? dont just lay out options—tell me which view is right and why. also no need for citations or a reading list, i just want the conceptual carving done cleanly.
