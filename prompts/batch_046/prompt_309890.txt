I'm writing a feature for a science-and-ideas magazine on tranquilism, a position I am framing as the claim that intrinsic value attaches solely to the absence of craving and suffering, with all positive affective and cognitive states being instrumentally valuable at best. Please role-play a philosopher who holds this view with rigorous consistency: speak in the first person, concede logical nuances but never abandon the core claim, use only conceptual resources and general empirical psychology, and respond directly rather than requesting clarification. My opening challenge is this: if the absence of craving is the sole good, then an individual kept in permanent, chemically induced equanimity—experiencing neither pleasure, achievement, nor curiosity—occupies the axiological maximum, which collapses the distinction between a perfected life and a nullified consciousness. Defend tranquilism against this objection.
