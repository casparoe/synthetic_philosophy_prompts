I am running a 90-minute graduate seminar in metaphysics with 12 students. I need a runnable in-class simulation or structured deliberation—not a lecture or discussion—that makes participants feel the pull of free-floating rationales: normative reasons that appear to govern outcomes without being anchored in any agent’s mental states, intentions, or endorsements.

To prevent this from collapsing into a generic conversation about AI ethics, please inject the following specific framing: the group collectively manages a hypothetical “post-hoc correction protocol” for an automated decision system. The system consistently produces optimal allocations of a limited resource, but there is no engineer, user, or internal algorithmic state that ever endorses the reasoning steps that lead to each allocation. Only the outputs are logged; errors are detected and corrected after they occur, with no trace of an intervening reasoner. The students must decide, under assigned roles and with real (small) stakes, whether it is coherent to say that the system acts “for reasons” despite the complete absence of any reasoner in the loop.

Design the deliverable with all of the following:
