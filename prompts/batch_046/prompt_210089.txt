"Hilbert's hotel works because you can shift every guest from room n to n+1, freeing room 1 and showing a proper subset (rooms 2 onward) has the same size as the whole set of rooms. This violates Euclid's axiom that the whole is greater than the part, which finitists think is a basic logical truth. Cantor then showed that for any set, its power set is strictly larger, so there's ℵ0, ℵ1, ℵ2, etc., with no largest cardinal. This means actual infinities don't just exist; they come in an endless tower.

On the philosophical side, Hilbert was the realist—he thought infinite sets exist in nature and mathematics describes them—while Cantor was the formalist, treating transfinite numbers as useful fictions and even placing the 'Absolute Infinite' outside mathematics altogether. So Cantor was actually closer to the finitists than Hilbert was.

Kronecker's objection is mainly epistemological: he said we can't construct an infinite set in finitely many steps, so we can't know it exists. There's also an ontological worry about violating part-whole, but that's secondary. So Hilbert's hotel already breaks logic, and Cantor's hierarchy just piles on more unconstructible objects. The finitist should reject both, but mostly because they exceed our cognitive reach, not because they violate part-whole."
