I'm a PhD student working on epistemology in philosophy of mathematics, and I've noticed a recurring argumentative move that I can't quite place in the literature. Someone starts with a well-known challenge: if the objects mathematics studies—numbers, sets, functions—are not part of the physical world and can't affect our senses, then it's puzzling how we could ever know anything about them. To get around this, a philosopher might say that mathematics isn't really about independent objects at all; it's about patterns of relations, like positions in a network defined only by how they connect to one another. But then a second problem seems to appear. If those relational patterns are themselves still outside the physical world—abstract blueprints that exist independently—then we haven't solved the original puzzle; we've just moved it from the objects to the patterns. But if those patterns only ever exist inside concrete things—like brain states, written symbols, or computer representations—then mathematical truths start to look like they depend on accidental features of the physical world, which contradicts the usual idea that mathematics is necessarily true everywhere and always. So the attempt to avoid mysterious abstract objects seems to land in a dilemma: either the mystery of knowledge returns at the level of patterns, or mathematics loses its necessity. I know this is in the neighborhood of Benacerraf's problem and of debates about whether mathematical structures exist on their own or only inside other things. But I'm asking specifically about this two-step failure pattern—escaping object-commitment only to face either a relocated knowledge-gap or a collapse into physical contingency. Does this configuration have an established name of its own? If it is just an internal objection within one of those broader positions, which one, and where exactly is the boundary? And if it doesn't have a standard label, how should its edges be drawn—especially regarding whether the contingency worry is an essential part of the pattern or only a possible misstep?
