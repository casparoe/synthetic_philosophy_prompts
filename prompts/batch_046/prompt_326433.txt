hey so im writing this up for my philosophy elective and i need a sanity check. weve been talking about tranquilism — the idea that only getting rid of craving and suffering actually counts, and all the positive stuff like joy or love is just neutral, not actually good in itself. my study partner came up with this argument that it proves way too much and i cant figure out where the leak is.

(1) Hard tranquilism says absence of craving and suffering is the sole intrinsic good, and presence of other mental states adds zero value.
(2) If absence of X is the only good, then any situation with zero X and nothing else bad is maximally good regardless of whats missing.
(3) A brain-dead patient with no awareness has zero craving and zero suffering.
(4) So that patient is in a maximally good state.
(5) But a rock also has zero craving and zero suffering, so the rock is in the exact same maximally good state.
(6) By the same token, non-existence has zero craving and zero suffering, so death or literal nothingness is maximally good too.
(7) Therefore hard tranquilism cant distinguish between an enlightened monk, a corpse, a rock, and literal nothingness — theyre all tied for best possible state.
(8) Any view that cant rank an enlightened life above death or a rock is broken.
(9) So we have to reject one of (1)-(6).

i feel like maybe (2) is doing something sneaky with "state" — like its treating absence as a property of the world instead of something that needs a subject? but then if i drop (2) im not sure what tranquilists can still say about why suffering matters. also my partner mentioned something like a scope shift between absence-in-a-mind vs absence-in-general? can you tell me exactly which step is wrong, why it looks convincing, and what tranquilists have to pay if they fix it? like do they end up having to say rocks have welfare? i kinda want to keep (1) because it feels right that suffering is the only real evil, but im okay with losing (7) if thats the price.
