As a retired microbiologist who has recently taken up philosophy of mind in earnest, I have drafted the following brief argument and should be grateful for precise critical comments—especially on conceptual coherence, not on bibliographic completeness. The draft concerns Dennett’s distinction between original and derived intentionality, centered on his wandering two-bitser: a simple register-shifting device whose aboutness is conferred by an interpretive system rather than being intrinsic. I set this against Searle’s biological naturalism, which holds that all intentionality is ultimately intrinsic and biologically grounded. Rather than rehearse the familiar exchange, I propose to test the distinction using a synthetic biological case. Imagine an engineered bacterial strain that detects and responds to a synthetic quorum-sensing molecule that has never occurred in nature; the detection pathway is designed de novo, lacking any evolutionary history, and the metabolic response serves as a functional registration of an environmental state that can be interpreted—by another engineered organism or by an observer—as indicating that state. The question is whether this loop possesses derived intentionality in Dennett’s sense, since its aboutness would be conferred by design and interpretive context, without possessing original intentionality in Searle’s sense, given the absence of evolutionary origin or intrinsic mental state. I am concerned that Searle might object that the design process itself imports original intentionality from the human engineers, rendering the case circular. Conversely, I wonder whether Dennett’s account requires that the interpretive chain eventually terminate in a system with original intentionality, or whether interpretation can remain purely functional and recursive without a biological terminus. Could you comment specifically on whether this synthetic scenario genuinely isolates derived intentionality, or whether it collapses either into eliminativism about bacterial aboutness or into a hidden reliance on designer intentionality? I am particularly interested in whether there is a principled distinction between mere functional registration—the causal correlation between signal and state—and derived aboutness, and whether my framing obscures that boundary. Please treat this as an exercise in conceptual engineering rather than in scholarship; I seek to know whether the argument holds together, not whether it cites the correct page numbers.
