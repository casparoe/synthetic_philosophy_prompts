Me and two friends are drafting rules for a cooperative bakery we're starting, but we haven't assigned jobs yet—who bakes, who delivers, who might have to drop out if they get injured. One friend says we should lock in pay splits now, before anyone knows their role, and that it's okay if the head baker earns more only if that extra money goes into a fund that buys the worst-paid worker out of the worst shifts, like cleaning ovens, rather than just giving them cash. Another friend says that's just rationalizing inequality and we should split everything equal, bad shifts included. I feel like there's a real philosophical issue here about fairness when you dont know where you'll land, and whether some gaps are okay if they specifically lift the bottom in a non-monetary way. Is the first argument actually principled, or is it a rationalization?
