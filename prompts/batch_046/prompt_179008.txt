I'm giving the opening talk at a new memorial center that puts three different atrocities in adjoing rooms -- not on a wall of numbers, just physically next to each other so you can't avoid the scale. Half the audience are historians and philosophers who argue about whether ranking historical evils is possible or obscene; the other half are survivors' relatives and local students who need this to feel like remembrance, not a debate tournament. The philosophical question has to do real structural work: I need to say something about comparison that lets me acknowledge magnitude without claiming one horror "wins," and that tension has to determine which words I can actually use up there. Write me about a ten-minute address. Don't make it a bibliography -- I'll fill in refs myself. And avoid the easy line that all suffering is incomparable; instead, build it around one specific, slightly strange comparative observation -- maybe about how different atrocities leave different kinds of archival gaps -- that makes the point without turning grief into arithmetic.
