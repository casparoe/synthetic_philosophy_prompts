Plan a ninety-minute teaching session for a philosophy-of-science graduate reading group—students familiar with natural kinds and social-construction debates but with no clinical training—on Ian Hacking's looping effects of human kinds in psychiatric classification. The session must explain how diagnosing people can change the people diagnosed, without collapsing the idea into labels cause behavior or psychiatric categories are purely constructed.

Carry the explanation with a single paired case: the absorption of Asperger's disorder into autism spectrum disorder in DSM-5, and the subsequent defense of Asperger's as a social identity by neurodiversity advocates who maintain the category in self-description and community practice despite its removal from the manual. This pairing should illustrate both the standard loop—official classification reshaping self-understanding and clinical presentation—and a resistant or reverse loop—collective identity work altering the social extension and meaning of the category from below.

Set out the exact order of introduction and the dependency relations: begin with the distinction between indifferent kinds (e.g., chemical elements, whose properties do not shift when classified) and interactive kinds (e.g., refugee status, where being classified changes the subject's possibilities and self-conception); then introduce psychiatric diagnosis as a paradigmatic interactive kind; only then present the paired Asperger's/ASD case. Specify what the carrying case must possess—a clear clinical presentation prior to widespread labeling, a visible shift in patient self-narrative and symptom reporting after the diagnostic change, and an organized community response that explicitly rejects the merged category—and what it must lack, namely any stable biological marker that would render the kind indifferent regardless of classification.

Map the misconceptions students will predictably form at each stage: treating looping as a placebo-like expectation effect rather than a change in the kind's causal properties and social possibilities; inferring that looping proves the condition is unreal or purely discursive; and assuming the loop is always top-down from manual to patient, missing the bottom-up resistance shown in the paired case. For each, state precisely where in the sequence—before introducing the case, during the first loop, or during the resistant loop—to intervene and how.

Provide one check-for-understanding question that reveals whether students grasp that looping alters the extension and causal profile of the kind itself, not merely its social reputation.

Finally, state explicitly what should be omitted at this level—such as the full Sybil/MPD media history, cross-manual divergences in other classification systems, or Hacking's later historical-ontology framework—and explain why leaving each out protects the dependency chain you have built.

Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not explain the concept directly; deliver only the teaching architecture.
