A fellow student in a philosophy of religion seminar summarised Molinism for me as follows: Molina held that God possesses three kinds of knowledge. First, natural knowledge of all necessary truths and possibilities. Second, middle knowledge — knowledge of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom, specifying what every possible free agent would freely do in every possible circumstance. These counterfactuals are true independently of God’s will; He discovers them rather than decreeing them. Third, free knowledge of the actual world He creates. Because the counterfactuals are fixed prior to any creative decree, God can only actualise worlds consistent with them; He therefore surveys the full set of feasible worlds and selects the one that best achieves His purposes. This preserves libertarian freedom, since agents in the actual world still choose freely, and it protects God from being the author of evil, because any sin that occurs is already embedded in the counterfactuals that God merely permits by choosing that particular world. The student added that Molinism necessarily entails transworld depravity — for each possible free person there is some world in which they freely sin — which means God could never have created free beings without allowing at least some evil.

How fair is this summary? I should like an accuracy audit that proceeds claim by claim. Please distinguish parts that are essentially correct, parts that are standard pedagogical simplifications, and parts that subtly misrepresent Molina’s view — particularly regarding the logical ordering of the decrees, the metaphysical status of the counterfactuals (whether describing God as ‘discovering’ them imports an anachronistic modal-realism), and the relation between Molinism and Plantinga’s transworld depravity problem. Does the framing of divine choice as surveying a menu of feasible worlds capture Molina’s own account, or does it distort the nature of the creative decree? And is the student right that Molinism entails God could not have created a world with free creatures and no sin at all?
