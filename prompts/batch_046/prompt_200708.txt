I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, preparing interview questions for a podcast episode with a political philosopher who defends an organizational reading of Machiavelli. On her view, virtù is not individual moral excellence but an institutional capacity for adaptive restructuring—something like a system’s ability to revise its own rules in response to stochastic shocks—while fortune is not an external enemy to be dominated once and for all but a persistent load that only continuously adaptive structures can redistribute rather than eliminate. She claims that Machiavelli’s image of fortune as a woman who must be beaten applies only to static hierarchies, not to institutions that learn.

Please draft six focused interview questions that stay on the philosophical mechanics of this position rather than on biography. At least two should press the distinction between this collective, adaptive virtù and the Aristotelian notion of hexis (a stable individual disposition), anticipating her likely reply that organizational habits are not reducible to the character traits of members, with a follow-up asking whether this risks collapsing political judgment into a form of systems engineering. Include one question about whether her framework can accommodate Machiavelli’s praise of founding violence—such as Romulus’s fratricide—without treating such violence as merely a one-time “system initialization” that adaptive institutions must eventually transcend, plus a follow-up keyed to her probable appeal to exceptional founding moments that break old rules in order to establish new ones. End with a question asking what kind of historical or contemporary institutional failure could genuinely count as evidence against her claim, specifically probing whether an adaptive system’s collapse would show that it never truly mastered fortune or only that it misallocated risk to more vulnerable nodes.
