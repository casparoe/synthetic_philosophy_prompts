Hey, long time lurker here, been reading rationalist forums since before the AI boom but never post much. I keep noticing this pattern whenever people try to design governance for some group - whether it's an AI alignment collective, a DAO, or just a discord server with moderation rules. They want to be democratic, so they say "everyone affectd should vote," but then you imediately need a rule for who counts as "everyone," and that rule has to be decided by... someone. Usually the people who were already inside when the server started. If you try to fix it by letting people vote on the membership rules, you're just pushing the problem back one step, because now you need to know who gets to vote on *that* vote.

I know this is in the neighborhood of the democratic boundary problem - like, who belongs to the demos - but the specific loop I'm tracking feels different. Every attempt to formalize the constituency just embeds the previous constituency as a hidden premise. People call it circularity or infinite regress, but those feel too generic; there's something specific about the constituency-definition version. Is there an established name for this exact bootstrap loop, and how does it differ from the standard boundary problem or from the non-identity problem? If there's no specific term, what should we call it, and where exactly are its edges - like, does it apply to corporate boards, or only to genuinely democratic groups?
