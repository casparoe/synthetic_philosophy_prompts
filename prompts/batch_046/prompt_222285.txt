hey — i sit on our university's grad wellbeing advsory board (the committee that signs off on mental-health programming for phd students). there's a proposal from a very rationalist-leaning student collective to make their "Dichotomy Workshop" mandatory for all incoming grads. it's built on the old stoic idea that some things are up to us — judgments, impulses, effort — and others just aren't — advisor moods, funding cuts, job-market outcomes, publication luck — but they've fused it with modern CBT-style reframing and some LessWrong mental-model language.

the pitch is that burnout comes from treating those externals as if we control them, so the workshop trains students to draw the line sharply and stop emotionally investing in outcomes they can't directly steer. supporters say it's practical stoicism updated for academia. critics on the board say institutionalizing this is just victim-blaming with a philosophical gloss: if we mandate it, we're telling students that toxic labs, funding collapses, and bad mentorship are just framing errors they need to fix inside their own heads. there's also a concrete pilot case that worries me — one participant used the framework to justify not filing a lab-safety report, reasoning that "the equipment hazard is external and not my problem, but my anxiety about reporting is internal and I should just reframe it away." the collective says that's a misunderstanding, but they haven't actually mapped where institutional responsibility ends and personal framing begins.

i need a memo for thursday's vote that picks a clear course — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with reasoning the collective can genuinely respond to, not just "stoicism is toxic." it should treat the victim-blaming worry seriously, deal with that safety-reporting misapplication head-on rather than waving it off, and say something about whether the modern stoic revival works as an institutional intervention versus just a private philosophical practice. write it formal-memo-style but keep the tone direct. and please don't ask for citations or a reading list — i want reasoning a model can actually produce without searching the web.
