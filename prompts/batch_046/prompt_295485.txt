For a philosophy-of-mind discussion written from a rationalist-epistemic standpoint, set out the desiderata that any adequate theory of phenomenal consciousness distribution across non-human animals must satisfy. I want a precise specification of what such a theory has to be able to do: the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must preserve, the empirical constraints it must observe, and the conceptual tensions it must navigate, with the motivating case behind each criterion and a clear mark of which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps.

Specifically, include requirements addressing: (1) evolutionary continuity without collapsing into panpsychism or human exceptionalism; (2) the separation of nociceptive reflex processing from affective phenomenal experience in invertebrates; (3) treatment of distributed or decentralised neural architectures (for example, cephalopod ganglionic processing) relative to centralised ones; (4) whether consciousness is graded across species or requires a sharp threshold, and what principle could justify either; (5) how behavioural and functional markers relate to phenomenal presence without reducing the latter to them; and (6) consistency with the intuition that simple reflex arcs lack consciousness despite being evolutionarily continuous with mammalian nervous systems.

For each desideratum, state the consideration that motivates it, indicate whether it is genuinely mandatory or camp-specific (e.g., biologists favouring continuity versus phenomenologists insisting on qualitative discontinuity), and flag any jointly unsatisfiable subsets — in particular, whether demanding both graded distribution and a hard boundary against attributing consciousness to bacteria or plants forces an unstable compromise. I am especially interested in whether the requirement of not attributing consciousness to simple reflex arcs can be reconciled with evolutionary continuity, or whether one must be surrendered.

Please write in British English, keep the response concise and formal, avoid bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and weight the criteria and their internal tensions more heavily than any brief scoring of existing frameworks.
