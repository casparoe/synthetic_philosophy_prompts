I'm a high school senior preparing a taxonomy handout for my philosophy club's unit on value theory, and I want to move beyond the standard textbook framing. We've been discussing the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—whether pleasures as different as solving a difficult logic proof and feeling the warmth of a bath share anything that makes them comparable—especially in cross-modal cases where the intentional objects and sensory modalities diverge entirely. Rather than asking people which philosopher they agree with, I want a decision tree that sorts respondents by what they actually believe about comparison, homogeneity, and asymmetry.

Please construct a taxonomy tree with the following architecture. The root should present a concrete comparison case: a respondent is asked whether they can rank the pleasure of completing an abstract mathematical proof against the pleasure of a warm bath, and if so, on what basis.

From there, the first-level branches should separate four strategies: (i) there is a shared phenomenal feel or qualitative "tone"; (ii) there is a shared intentional structure—what the pleasure is directed at or about; (iii) there is a shared functional role in motivation and choice-behavior; or (iv) direct comparison is incoherent because the pleasures are incommensurable.

The second-level branches should ask, for each strategy except (iv), whether the respondent accepts an asymmetry thesis: that pain exhibits a relatively unified phenomenal character across contexts, which makes negative hedonism more defensible, while pleasure lacks such unity, which undermines positive hedonism. This should split each branch into symmetric and asymmetric versions.

The leaves should be named positions where they exist—such as classic qualitative hedonism, attitudinal hedonism, functionalist hedonism, pluralist incommensurability, negative hedonism, or symmetric unified hedonism—and marked "unnamed / unoccupied" for logical combinations that have no clear defender. For each empty combination, include one sentence diagnosing whether it seems incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Every leaf must also carry one sentence stating its most awkward practical commitment, especially regarding how it justifies choosing between the proof-pleasure and the bath-pleasure without smuggling in a non-hedonic criterion.

Please include a brief scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the leaves, not onto self-chosen labels. Flag any pair of leaves that these questions cannot genuinely separate. The handout should be formal, suitable for an advanced high school audience, written in American English, and should not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts.
