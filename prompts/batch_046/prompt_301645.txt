You chair your university’s Institutional Precommitment Review Board. Your track record: you approved a ten-year locked research endowment that included a hardship-release clause; you rejected a faculty resolution attempting to bind all future senates to a fixed curriculum with no sunset or override mechanism; you approved, with conditions, a student-government constitutional amendment that required both a supermajority and external arbitration to amend; and you rejected an administrative rule that delegated budget authority to an automated system lacking any human-override pathway. The board now faces a proposal for a nested Ulysses contract: the current trustees seek to constitutionally entrench an ethical-investment mandate that binds not only themselves but every successor board, paired with an escape clause permitting unbinding only if a future board demonstrates “epistemic humility”—specifically, that it has formally deliberated under at least three mutually incompatible normative frameworks, documented why each is insufficient, and then voted to repeal. Draft the board’s decision memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Identify the closest analog among your precedents; analyze whether the escape clause genuinely protects future autonomy or merely adds procedural theater; state the concrete principle you would be adopting with each possible verdict; and propose a time-bound course of action. Structure your response as follows: (1) Executive Summary (approx. 100 words); (2) Precedent Analysis (approx. 150 words); (3) The Case For Approval and The Case Against Approval (approx. 150 words each); (4) Recommendation with Concrete Conditions (approx. 100 words); (5) Principle Adopted Either Way (approx. 75 words).
