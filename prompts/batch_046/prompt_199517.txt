I am designing a graduate seminar session on the metaphysics of propositional unity, and I would like you to write a comparative analysis for it. Compare F. H. Bradley’s regress argument against relational accounts of propositional unity with the structuralist resolution that treats unity as a primitive feature of propositional structure rather than as mediated by an external relating relation. To prevent a generic rehearsal of textbook positions, frame the comparison around singular propositions expressed by demonstrative and indexical constructions—specifically, a proposition of the form “This very concrete particular is now decaying,” in which a unique, unrepeatable object is picked out by a spatial demonstrative and anchored to a single temporal index.

For Bradley’s side: explain whether achieving the unity of this proposition requires an infinite hierarchy of relating relations that connect the particular, the property of decaying, the spatial index, and the temporal index; and determine whether his rejection of external relations forces him to collapse the entire content into an undifferentiated absolute, thereby losing the distinctness of the constituent roles.

For the structuralist side: explain how primitive structural roles—subject-role, predicate-role, and indexical-role—allow the same concrete particular to occupy the subject-position across different propositions without being reduced to a bare particular, and show precisely how this avoids Bradley’s regress.

Then evaluate whether the structuralist resolution genuinely dissolves the regress or merely relocates it into the ontology of structural roles themselves. Conclude by defending one of the two views as offering the more coherent account of singular propositional unity, and identify the exact point at which the structuralist must either accept something like Bradley’s absolute or concede that structural roles are relational entities subject to the same regress. Write in a formal, argumentative style appropriate for a metaphysics seminar; do not provide citations, reading lists, references to specific page numbers, or appeals to literature you could not be expected to have memorized.
