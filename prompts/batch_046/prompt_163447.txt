Hey, I'm writing my chapter on archival power and I'm stuck on this tagging protocol our library rolled out. Every digitized manuscript gets indexed by named author, institutional affliation, and citation links, but if a doc has no single author — collective labor reports, anonynmous protest leaflets — it gets dumped in 'miscellaneous' and isn't in the searchable tree. The archivists say it's practicaly just logistics, but it feels like it commits them to some view of who counts as a knower and what counts as a record. What philosophical commitments does that tagging practice presuppose? Can it survive on purely logistical grounds, or does it need a full theory of authorship? And if we rebuilt the system to index collective and anonymous production equally, what would have to shift in how we think about intellectual property and historical responsibility?
