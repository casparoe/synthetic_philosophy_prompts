hey, i'm a retired biophysicist who picked up philosophy books after i stopped working in the lab, and i've been trying to wrap my head around this eliminative materialism stuff. back when i was studying ion channels we used the Hodgkin-Huxley model with those gating variables m, n, and h — everybody knew they weren't literal physical gates sitting in the membrane, they were just interveening variables that let us predict action potentials with crazy accuracy. we never talked about "eliminating" them just because they don't correspond to discrete molecular parts; they stayed in the equations because they work. so now i'm reading Churchland and he says beliefs and desires literally don't exist, mostly because folk psychology doesn't integrete well with neuroscience and hasn't made real progress. but that sounds to me like complaining that our behavioral model is incomplete, not that the entities inside it are fictional like phlogiston. if beliefs are functioning like those gating variables — useful predictive constructs that might not map one-to-one onto specific synaptic circuits — then shouldn't we keep them in our model of human behavior the same way we kept m and h in electrophysiology? or is there some hidden criterion that makes mental states different from other theoretical intermediates? i feel like i'm probably mixing up reduction with elimination, but i can't see why we'd declare victory and toss out belief-talk before we have a complete predictive replacement that actually does the job better in everyday contexts, not just in the lab.
