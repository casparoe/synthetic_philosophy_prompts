I've been working through the Rietdijk-Putnam line for the block universe, but I think I've driven it past its legitimate stopping point into a result about agency that feels absurd, and I want to know exactly where the overreach occurs. Here is the chain as I see it. (1) In special relativity, for any two spacelike-separated events A and B, there are inertial frames in which they are simultaneous, frames in which A precedes B, and frames in which B precedes A. (2) If reality is three-dimensional and only present-tense events exist, then whether B exists "now" varies with frame choice. (3) But there is no physically privileged inertial frame; all are dynamically equivalent. (4) So either existence is frame-relative, violating the intuition that what exists should not depend on coordinate convention, or all events—past, present, and future—exist tenselessly within a four-dimensional manifold. (5) The first horn is incoherent, so the block universe follows: future events are as real as present ones.

Now I extend this with what seems like a straightforward corollary, but it produces a contradiction I can't resolve. (6) If future events exist tenselessly with definite properties, then any decision I will make tomorrow is already inscribed at its spacetime location. (7) Yet from the first-person standpoint, deliberation between genuine alternatives is real: it is not experienced as consulting a pre-written script but as an open process that determines which option becomes actual. (8) So we seem committed both to a fixed future content in the block and to an open selection process that fixes that content. (9) If the selection process is real, it must either alter the content of the future event—which contradicts its being fully present in the manifold—or be a mere epiphenomenon, which undermines the reality of agency. (10) Therefore either the block universe eliminates genuine deliberation, or one of the premises leading to (5) must be rejected.

I'd prefer to preserve both the frame-independence argument in (3) and the reality of deliberation in (7). Is the slide hidden in the move from physical equivalence of frames to metaphysical parity in (4), perhaps by smuggling in a principle that "no coordinate dependence" applies to ontology rather than just to law? Or is the error in (6), where I assume that tenseless existence entails determinate content, when one might hold that future events exist but lack determinate properties until they enter the present light cone? If I reject (6) in that way, does it collapse back into presentism, or can it be reconciled with a four-dimensional ontology? And if the flaw is instead in (8)—maybe deliberation is compatible with a fixed block because the "openness" is indexical, like being ignorant of which branch one is on—what does that cost me in terms of explaining why deliberation feels causally efficacious rather than merely epistemic? Please diagnose which step is the weakest link, what rejecting it requires, and whether any repair lets me keep both the Rietdijk-Putnam conclusion and a robust account of first-person agency.
