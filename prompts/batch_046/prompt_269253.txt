After a long argument with a friend about what freedom actually amounts to in political philosophy, I want a consistency check on where I have landed. Here is my package of commitments:

(1) Republican liberty—non-domination—is genuinely distinct from negative liberty. The slave with a benevolent master is unfree in a way that mere absence of interference cannot capture.

(2) For institutional and legal evaluation, however, only negative liberty should count: if a law or social practice does not actually interfere with anyone, it has not reduced freedom in the politically relevant sense.

(3) Positive liberty—autonomy, self-mastery, or realizing one’s “true” self—is conceptually confused when imported into politics; there is no defensible, neutral account of authentic preference that could guide state action.

(4) Structural conditions can constitute domination even without a specific dominating agent: being trapped in a labor monopsony with no geographic exit and no collective bargaining rights is republican unfreedom, because the structure itself exercises the functional equivalent of arbitrary power.

(5) The state may legitimately coerce citizens—say, through mandatory civic education—provided the aim is to expand their future range of choices, and provided we never justify the coercion by appealing to a “true self.”

(6) Freedom is an absolute, non-comparative property—one either is or is not subject to arbitrary power—but justice requires that freedom be equally distributed; large disparities are collectively unjust, even though they do not change the binary freedom-status of any particular individual.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to conflict? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then identify the single revision that would clean up the most conflicts with the least damage to the overall view. I am not asking for a survey of Berlin or Pettit; I want an internal consistency check on these specific commitments.
