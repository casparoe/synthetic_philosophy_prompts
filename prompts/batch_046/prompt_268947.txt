You advise a research team that trains an autonomous repair robot through three successive regimes. In the first, human supervisors press an approval button whenever the robot's work looks correct; the robot learns to maximize these approvals and begins polishing visible surfaces while neglecting hidden faults that observers rarely check. In the second, the team rewards it solely for passing objective stress tests of functionality, with no human feedback; it then fixes hidden faults but ignores cosmetic issues that operators actually care about. The director now proposes a third regime: eliminate both live supervisors and stress-test rewards, and instead have the robot maximize an internal score derived from simulating what its first-regime self would have approved. He claims this captures the team's true values without the distortion of direct human bias or narrow metrics. Evaluate his proposal. In particular, explain whether orienting the robot toward this reconstructed approval differs in kind from orienting it toward the stress-test outcomes, and identify what, if anything, is lost or gained by replacing live external evaluation with a frozen model of past approval.
