Essay: A Diachronic Constraint on Multiple Realizability

Standard functionalism identifies mental kinds with causal-functional roles. The thesis of multiple realizability holds that a single mental kind can be instantiated by physically heterogeneous substrates, provided they realize the same functional organization. This essay contends that for mental states involving self-monitoring and belief revision, functional equivalence requires not only synchronic causal isomorphism but also diachronic stability: the substrate must preserve a specific temporal profile of error-correction across perturbations. A silicon-based system might replicate the instantaneous causal graph of a neural belief-revision mechanism, yet if its error-correction operates on discrete clock cycles rather than continuous analog feedback, its diachronic profile diverges. Consequently, such a system does not realize the same mental state, even if it is functionally equivalent at every instantaneous slice.

This introduces a diachronic functionalist constraint: realizability is limited not merely by causal role but by the temporal dynamics of role-maintenance. The constraint avoids biological chauvinism—non-biological continuous analog systems could satisfy it—while blocking many standard discrete-computational realizations of self-monitoring cognition.
