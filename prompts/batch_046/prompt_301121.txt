I've come to accept a particular middle-ground view in the debate over whether large language models understand language. The view holds that LLMs do possess a genuine but deflationary form of semantic understanding—what might be called structural-compositional understanding. On this account, understanding is not defined by consciousness, intentionality, or direct causal grounding in the world, but by the presence of internally structured representations that encode compositional mappings between linguistic form and logical-inferential relations, supporting counterfactual and analogical manipulation within the model's activation geometry. The model understands not by referring to objects in the world, but by implementing a compressed structural model of the generative grammar and reasoning patterns present in its training distribution. This is meant to be more than statistical correlation—what critics dismiss as stochastic parroting—because the representations are systematically organized and support novel combinations that go beyond memorized templates. At the same time, it is less than full human understanding because it lacks intentional directedness and embodied interaction.
