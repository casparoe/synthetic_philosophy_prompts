Right, I've been chewing over this argument about Glaucon's ring and now I'm convinced it's broken but I can't see where. The idea is: if the only reason to be just is avoiding punishment and keeping a good reputation, then giving someone the ring of Gyges (full invisability) should make them completely unjust. So far so standard. But here's where I take it further, and probably mess up.

(1) Justice is only instrumentally valuable — it's good because of its social consequences, not in itself.

(2) The ring removes all social consequences, since no one can see what you do.

(3) Therefore, with the ring, there's literally zero reason to act justly.

(4) Now here's the move: mental states are also invisible. Even without the ring, nobody can directly observe your intentions, your secret resentments, or your private fantasies.

(5) So for mental life, everyone already wears the ring.

(6) If (1) and (3) are right, then for mental life there's also zero reason to be just — no reason to avoid malicious thoughts, secret betrayal, or cultivating virtue in private.

(7) But virtue just is a disposition of the mind and heart, not just outward behavour.

(8) So if there's no reason to have just mental states, there's no reason to be a just person at all, even in public.

(9) That means Glaucon's challenge doesn't just show that private injustice is rational; it shows that the very concept of being a good person collapses into pure acting.

(10) But that's absurd — we clearly think there's a difference between a genuinely kind person and a skilled performer of kindness.

(11) Therefore (1) is false, and justice must have some intrinsic value.

But wait, I feel like I'm cheating somewhere between (4) and (6). Is it that mental states aren't subject to moral evaluation? Or is there a hidden scope shift? I really want to keep (1) as a live option — I think Glaucon might actually be right about conventional justice — but I also want to keep (7), that virtue involves mental states. So where's the leak? Is (5) wrong because intentions can still be revealed by behavour? Or is (6) a non sequitur?
