I'm writing to settle a disagreement I keep having with my study partner about a clinical trial we're both enrolled in, and I'd like to check whether my reasoning is actually coherent or just sophistical.

The protocol is straightforward. Twice a week we take an experimental sleep compound and are monitored overnight. Before each session, the staff flip a fair coin in another room. If it lands tails, the compound fails: we are woken once the next morning, interviewed for fifteen minutes about our subjective state, and then discharged with our memories intact. If it lands heads, the compound works: we are woken on two consecutive mornings for identical interviews, but between them we receive an amnesic agent that wipes all memory of the prior awakening. From the inside, each awakening feels like the first.

We are now awake in the interview room. The researcher asks each of us to state, on a 0-to-1 scale, our current credence that the coin came up heads—that is, that there is a twin interview happening or about to happen. Our compensation is tied to a proper scoring rule applied per awakening, summed across all awakenings we actually experience. I argued that I should report 1/3, because there are three equiprobable first-person situations—this morning with failure, this morning with success and a twin tomorrow, and tomorrow morning with success feeling exactly like now—and I am equally likely to occupy any one of them. My partner insists on 1/2, since the coin is unbiased and I have gained no new evidence about its outcome; he says treating my own temporal position within the protocol as if it were evidence is a category error.

The twist that makes this harder is that the study also employs an external observer—an unblinded coordinator who sees every awakening and assigns a single collective calibration score to our pair's reports. We are asked to write down not only our personal credence, but also the score we believe the observer should assign to the aggregated set of reports from both of us. I think the observer should use 1/2, because from her view there are only two runs (a tails-run producing one report, and a heads-run producing two reports), and she weights by run frequency rather than by first-person possibility. My partner thinks the observer should also use 1/3, because she should adopt our first-person framing rather than treating the awakenings as interchangeable tokens.

I want to know three things. First, is my 1/3 answer defensible without committing me to some bizarre metaphysics of personal identity across the amnesic gap, or is the 1/2 response actually the only rational one once we separate evidential updates from framing effects? Second, does the scoring-rule structure change anything—specifically, should I report the credence I actually hold, or the one that maximizes my expected payout given that, under heads, there are two copies of me answering independently? Third, is there a principled way to separate what I should report for myself from what I should recommend to the external observer, and does that separation correspond to a real distinction in how beliefs anchored to "this particular awakening" ought to be treated?

I'm not asking for a bibliography or a history of the debate; I just want to know whether there's a consistent position that lets me keep the 1/3 personal report, the 1/2 observer recommendation, and avoid being accused of double-counting myself just because I want to have it both ways.
