I serve on my university's Joint Research Funding and Ethics Committee. We are reviewing a proposal—designated Project Aethel—that requests $2.4 million over four years to develop an open-source framework for allocating computational resources among distributed, self-improving artificial agents. The principal investigators argue that because the network architecture permits unbounded expansion, standard aggregative moral evaluation collapses into what they call "infinite-ethics paralysis": any action carrying a nonzero probability of generating infinite harm or infinite benefit dominates all finite considerations, rendering comparative decision-making impossible.

To avoid this paralysis, the researchers propose a novel evaluative protocol. Rather than weighing the welfare of all possible future agents, they restrict direct moral accounting to agents falling within an "information-theoretic causal horizon"—those reachable through traceable computational pathways within a fifty-year window. Agents lying beyond that horizon are assigned a status they term "latent but unactivated": they are acknowledged as potential moral patients but are excluded from present aggregative calculations, though the framework mandates a periodic re-evaluation of the horizon boundary as technology evolves.

Half the committee regards this as an arbitrary epistemic cutoff that merely disguises the underlying infinite-ethics problem rather than solving it, and worries that it licenses present negligence toward genuinely unbounded future populations. The other half views it as a necessary and defensible practical constraint that preserves decision-making capacity without denying long-term moral risk.

Please draft the short formal memo I will circulate before next Thursday's meeting. It should recommend one of three courses—approval, rejection, or approval with specific conditions and a defined time frame—and provide reasoning that the researchers and dissenting members could respond to directly. The memo should engage seriously with the researchers' causal-horizon and latent-status framework rather than dismissing it as a technical evasion, and it should treat honestly the strongest objection on the losing side. Write in American English.
