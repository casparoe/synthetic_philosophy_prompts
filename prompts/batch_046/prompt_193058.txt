I have been following a mental discipline described in a contemporary popular-philosophy book: divide every concern into two categories. The first contains only what lies entirely within my own agency—my intentions, my efforts, my judgments, and the manner in which I treat others. The second contains everything that does not—other people's choices, economic conditions, institutional rulings, and outcomes that depend on fortune. The author advises cultivating total commitment to the first category and complete equanimity toward the second.

Recently I became the unpaid maintainer of an open-source software library that hospitals use for patient scheduling. Within my direct agency, I can write clean code, maintain documentation, respond respectfully to bug reports, and set my own working hours. Outside it lie whether a hospital system actually adopts the update, whether a tech corporation offers sponsorship, whether regulators alter compliance requirements, and whether a security flaw is discovered by someone else.

The difficulty is that sustaining the library requires actions that feel like attempts to influence the second category. I write grant applications, draft responses to regulatory consultations, and reach out to potential corporate backers. These are my actions, yet their purpose is precisely to shape outcomes—funding approvals, policy rulings, sponsorship agreements—that the framework tells me to treat as indifferent. If I genuinely regard a grant rejection or a lost sponsor as outside my concern, I find it hard to muster the motivation to apply or to advocate persuasively; but if I treat those outcomes as within my domain of responsibility, I seem to betray the core distinction. Is my framework being misapplied, or does maintaining critical public infrastructure expose a genuine limitation in the two-category model? In particular, how should I understand activities like fundraising and institutional advocacy—are they part of my proper domain of effort, or are they attempts to command what is not mine to command?
