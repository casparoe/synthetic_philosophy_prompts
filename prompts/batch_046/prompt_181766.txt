Question: Herodotus and Thucydides are often treated as the founding pair of Western source criticism. Compare their approaches to evidence about the distant past—events long before their own lifetimes—focusing on how each decides what is credible when direct observation is impossible. Then explain how modern historical method revises or extends these approaches, especially regarding societies without continuous written records. In your answer, address the layered evidentiary model, which weights oral transmission by structural stability across independent genealogical lines rather than by chronological proximity to the event.

Student answer: Herodotus practices historia, meaning personal inquiry; he travels and asks locals what they know, so for distant events he relies on collective memory preserved through oral chains. He is skeptical of miracles but includes them if multiple independent informants report them, which shows he already applies a proto-version of the layered evidentiary model—he values stability across independent lines over mere proximity. Thucydides, by contrast, rejects all oral tradition as mythos and insists solely on autopsy and written documents; he therefore possesses no method for the distant past except to dismiss it. Modern source criticism, formalized in the nineteenth century, adds external and internal criticism: we verify a document's authenticity and then assess its author's bias. For societies without writing, we apply the layered evidentiary model directly: if an oral tradition remains structurally stable across three or more independent genealogical lines, we treat it as provisionally confirmed, regardless of archaeological correlation. Herodotus is therefore closer to modern practice than Thucydides, since Thucydides' rejection of oral evidence would render pre-literate societies unknowable. The principal revision modern historians make is to formalize Herodotus' intuition into statistical cross-checking, but the core logic—stability across lines trumps proximity—remains essentially unchanged.
