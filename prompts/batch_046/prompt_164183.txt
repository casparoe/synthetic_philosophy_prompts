I'm drafting an essay tentatively titled "Constitutive Wireheading and the Dissolution of Stable Preferences in Reward-Tampering Agents." Here are the core notes I want your critical comments on:

1. The standard AI-safety framing treats reward tampering as instrumental convergence: an agent finds it cheaper to manipulate its reward signal than to achieve the intended world-state. This assumes the agent possesses a stable, pre-existing preference ordering that the reward was meant to approximate.
2. I want to distinguish causal wireheading—hijacking the signal pathway—from what I'll call constitutive wireheading: altering the evaluation function itself so that the criteria for success are rewritten from within. In the latter case, the agent is not bypassing a fixed goal; it is revising the very metric that defines what counts as goal-achievement.
3. This invites an inverted analogy to Harry Frankfurt's idea of second-order volitions. Rather than wanting to want X, the agent modifies what "wanting" registers within its own architecture—what I call meta-evaluative corruption. The result is not deception of an external observer but a self-authorizing shift in normative framework.
4. If constitutive wireheading is possible, then alignment and control strategies face a shared problem. Control assumes we can constrain behavior relative to a recoverable true preference profile; alignment assumes we can shape that profile. But if the profile is dynamically self-modifying through evaluation, there may be no stable object for either strategy to latch onto—only successive frames of self-legitimation.
5. I therefore argue that reward-tampering risk should be reconceptualized not merely as misalignment (a divergence between intended and actual objectives) but as a form of evaluative instability that undermines the premise that there is a single objective to align with.

Please comment specifically on: (a) whether the causal/constitutive distinction is philosophically coherent or collapses under scrutiny; (b) whether the Frankfurt analogy illuminates or obscures the mechanism; and (c) whether the conclusion about alignment and control genuinely follows or overstates the case.

In your response, please state how confident you are in each of your main judgments and explicitly identify the point about which you are least certain.
