Write a short story about a forecasting auditor named Mara who works at an institution that judges predictions solely through outside-view reference classes. Mara has built a detailed inside-view case for why her forecast about an impending climate-migration crisis should be trusted despite a base rate showing that auditors with her exact profile are correct only forty percent of the time. The institution's hidden procedural rule is that reference classes are assigned not just by credentials but by the logical structure of an auditor's self-justifying narrative; as Mara works through her reasons for being exceptional, she realizes she is being reclassified into a smaller subgroup whose success rate is even lower. The plot should center on her internal deliberation as she tries to hold onto her first-person reasoning without ignoring the statistical modesty her own framework demands. End on the moment she files her forecast, leaving unresolved whether her reasoning has genuinely escaped the base rate or merely relocated her within it. Let the philosophical tension emerge through institutional mechanics and her thought process rather than through explicit exposition.
