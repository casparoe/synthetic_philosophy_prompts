I'm taking a graduate seminar on comparative moral philosophy and have become genuinely puzzled by Confucian role ethics, particularly the doctrine of zhengming—usually rendered as the rectification of names. In our last session, the discussion turned to a somewhat speculative case: whether an advanced caregiving algorithm, embedded in a domestic robot and performing all the practical functions of a filial child—monitoring health, offering companionship, coordinating elder care—could be said to occupy the Confucian role of son or daughter if the family explicitly named it as such. One student argued that the Confucian slogan means precisely that the label creates the obligation: name the robot a child, and the relational duties attach automatically. Another insisted on the reverse reading, that zhengming requires the performance to earn the name, so the robot's functional adequacy is irrelevant unless it participates in the reciprocal, affective structure that constitutes the role. Both seemed to treat the phrase as self-evident, yet they drew opposite conclusions about synthetic kinship, and neither explained what philosophical work the slogan was actually doing.

Can you disambiguate rectification of names—or the common one-liner that when names are not correct, language is not in accordance with things and affairs cannot be carried out—by laying out the precise, distinct theses it might express? I would like a menu rather than a single interpretation: for instance, a semantic or conventionalist claim about linguistic accuracy; a normative-functional claim about roles being constituted by their obligations; an ontological claim about social kinds having essential natures; a political claim about authority fixing social reality; and any other reading that does serious philosophical work within role ethics. For each, indicate whether it is trivial, whether it is defensible specifically within a Confucian framework, and whether it actually supports either of the two positions in our robot debate. Please do not provide a bibliography, cite specific texts by chapter and verse, or list secondary readings; I am trying to understand the conceptual architecture well enough to think through the case on my own.
