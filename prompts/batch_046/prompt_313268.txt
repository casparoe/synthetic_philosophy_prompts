My friend and I just concluded a long argument about superrationality, specifically Douglas Hofstadter's Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery that Scientific American conducted in the 1980s. Throughout the debate she insisted that superrational agents are best understood as two execution threads spawned from a single source-code repository: they share an identical reasoning architecture, so coordination requires no external signal, only an internal recognition of shared identity. She deployed this analogy to defend the superrational prescription in Platonia — that each of the twenty participants should send a telegram with independent probability one in twenty — and to explain away the Luring Lottery's collapse, in which contestants submitted astronomically large entry numbers rather than each rolling a virtual die with sides equal to the expected number of respondents. In her view, the lottery failed not because superrationality is conceptually unsound, but because the contestants violated the identity condition: they possessed divergent reasoning processes, distinct risk preferences, and asymmetric information, so they were never true copies to begin with. I want a rigorous audit of this comparison. Please provide the explicit correspondence table: what in the dilemma corresponds to the source code, the execution thread, the shared memory space, the random-number seed, and the hardware separation? Then isolate the disanalogies that bear specifically on her conclusion — especially whether shared reasoning architecture in human agents actually entails the deterministic or probabilistic synchronization she assumes, whether the absence of a genuinely shared random seed undermines the one-in-twenty prescription, and whether the runaway inflation of entries in the lottery is better explained by a breakdown of agent-identity or by structural properties of the payoff matrix that would persist even among perfect copies. Finally, assess whether her conclusion — that superrationality remains intact and only its preconditions were unmet — survives once these disanalogies are exposed, or whether the source-code analogy was doing the essential argumentative work all along.
