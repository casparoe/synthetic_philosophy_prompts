I am convening an advanced seminar on path dependence and the logic of counterfactual reasoning in institutional development. One of my doctoral students keeps repeating that 'history rhymes' whenever I press her on whether a particular legal or technological trajectory was genuinely contingent, or whether an equivalent institutional form would have arrived via a different causal sequence. Before I reply, I want to be rigorous about the work that sentence is actually doing. Could you disambiguate the slogan into a menu of precise theses — ranging from the trivial observation that patterns display family resemblances across time, through substantive causal claims about lock-in and increasing returns to adoption, to stronger metaphysical assertions that temporal ordering itself constrains the modal space of branching trajectories? For each reading, indicate whether it is a platitude, defensible but contentious, or plainly false, and tell me which version the student is likely invoking when she uses the phrase to dismiss counterfactual questions about whether the same outcome would have emerged had an earlier contingency resolved differently. I am especially keen to know whether any coherent reading supports dismissing such counterfactuals without collapsing either into hard determinism or into the uninteresting observation that causes have effects.
