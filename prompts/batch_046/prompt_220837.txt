I'm grading exams for a course on formal epistemology and self-locating belief. Here is the question I set and one student's answer.

Question: Judy belongs to one of two companies, Red or Blue, with prior credence 1/2 each. Within Red she is uniformly distributed over Battalions 1 and 2; within Blue she is uniformly distributed over Battalions 1, 2, and 3. Her commander states: "If you are in Red, you are not in Battalion 3; if you are in Blue, you face no such restriction." Judy then learns she is in Battalion 2. Some philosophers argue her credence in Red should update to 3/5; others maintain it should stay at 1/2. Analyze this dispute, with particular attention to whether the commander's claim should be modeled as a material conditional, an indicative conditional describing actual frequencies, or a counterfactual constraint on nearby possible worlds. Discuss whether either interpretation licenses an update away from the prior, and take care not to conflate this case with temporal updating puzzles like Sleeping Beauty.

Student answer: The student formalizes the prior correctly: P(R) = P(B) = 1/2, with P(B2|R) = 1/2 and P(B2|B) = 1/3. Applying Bayes' rule yields P(R|B2) = (1/4)/(5/12) = 3/5, which they present as the correct thirder result. They then correctly observe that if the commander's claim is read as a material conditional (R → ¬B3), it is satisfied in every world where Judy is not in Red, so the conditional is evidentially inert and the prior remains 1/2. Next they argue that the proper treatment is counterfactual: the commander's statement restricts the sphere of Red-worlds closest to actuality, so that within the relevant modal neighborhood Judy can only occupy B1 or B2 if she is Red. From this they infer that Judy should apply an "imaging" update that redistributes weight from eliminated worlds to retained ones, yielding 3/5. They claim this refutes the halfer, who allegedly assumes that company labels are arbitrarily assigned and therefore that being in B2 is evidentially symmetric across companies. The student concludes that the halfer confuses epistemic symmetry with evidential symmetry, and that once the counterfactual structure is recognized the update to 3/5 is unavoidable.

Please assign a letter grade and provide specific, point-by-point comments that justify it. Identify which parts of the reasoning are correct, which contain subtle mistakes—such as a conflation, a misapplied view, or a step that does not actually follow—and whether the conclusion is supported by the premises given. Do not signal your own view of the correct philosophical answer; assess the work as presented.
