Please restate the essentialist position and its principal objections entirely in the vocabulary of genetics, embryology, and developmental biology: speak of gametes, zygotes, meiotic recombination, genomic identity, mitochondrial lineages, and developmental trajectories, and avoid philosophical jargon wherever you can. Then give me the residue report: indicate precisely which elements of the position translate cleanly into biological terms, which must be distorted to fit (for instance, treating a gamete as an individual with essential properties rather than as one stochastic product of crossing-over and mutation), and which claims have no counterpart at all in how biologists understand origin. I am especially interested in whether the biological fact that every sperm and egg is genetically unique because of meiotic shuffling undermines the very idea of identifying a specific origin pair, or whether that uniqueness instead reinforces the essentialist intuition. Please respond in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as you might in a formal letter.
