Below are notes and a partial outline for an essay I am drafting. I propose extending Daniel Dennett’s heterophenomenological method—originally developed to treat first-person reports of conscious experience as texts describing a fictional world that may or may not correspond to an underlying reality—into the domain of mechanistic interpretability for large neural networks. Specifically, I argue that when researchers use probing and feature-visualization techniques to attribute representational content to specific model circuits, they are producing reports that are structurally analogous to heterophenomenological texts: they describe what the system appears to represent without committing to the claim that the system literally possesses beliefs, intentions, or phenomenal states. I then examine whether this analogy fails because human verbal reports are authored by conscious agents, whereas interpretability outputs are causally generated by statistical mechanisms with no authorial subject. I tentatively respond that heterophenomenology already brackets questions of authorship and intentional agency in favor of treating reports as data to be interpreted functionally, so the absence of a conscious author in the AI case does not undermine the comparison but rather highlights that the method was always functionalist in orientation. The essay then asks whether this reveals heterophenomenology to be less a neutral descriptive framework for consciousness science and more a specific form of interpretive functionalism that applies wherever a system produces structured, interpretable outputs. I am particularly concerned that I may be conflating Dennett’s epistemic agnosticism about qualia with a broader methodological license to interpret any complex system as if it had an inner world. Could you comment on the coherence of the analogy, on whether I am misrepresenting heterophenomenology as functionalist rather than methodologically neutral, and on whether the lack of an authorial agent in neural networks creates a disanalogy that undermines the extension? I would also appreciate comments on whether the essay should address the objection that heterophenomenology depends on social and linguistic practices embedded in human forms of life, which artificial systems lack, and whether that objection limits the analogy or collapses it entirely.
