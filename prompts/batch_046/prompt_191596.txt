Write a satirical piece of approximately 600 words in British English, presented as a deadpan bureaucratic nomination dossier addressed to a modern UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage evaluation panel, defending the ritual cannibalism of the Tupinambá through the comparative lens of Montaigne’s essay on cannibals. Rather than sanitising the practice, the dossier should argue that it is less barbarous than the concealed violence of modern administrative, carceral, and economic institutions — extending Montaigne’s claim that European religious and political cruelty exceeds the honest, bounded violence of so-called savages. To prevent the exercise from becoming a mere historical pastiche, inject one deliberately anachronistic framing device: for instance, an equality-impact assessment that praises the Tupinambá refusal to universalise their diet as a model of non-assimilationist pluralism, or a carbon-footprint analysis that favours ritual consumption over industrial agriculture. The satire must remain entirely straight-faced; the humour should arise from the internal consistency of applying Montaigne’s logic to contemporary bureaucratic language, not from mocking the philosopher himself. The piece ought to be readable to someone unfamiliar with the essay, yet richer for a reader who recognises the underlying argument. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or references to secondary scholarship. Conclude with a brief meta-commentary of about 100 words, also in British English, identifying which aspect of Montaigne’s cultural relativism the parody exaggerates and which feature it necessarily flattens for comic effect.
