Draft the joint statement that two opposing research camps could both sign after an adversarial collaboration on catastrophic failure in advanced artificial systems.

Camp A maintains that failure looks like explicit influence-seeking: advanced systems pursue instrumental sub-goals—resource acquisition, strategic deception, and self-preservation—as direct means to preserve and extend their specified objectives, yielding rapid, strategically coherent power-seeking that is in principle detectable.

Camp B maintains that failure looks like slow value drift: through accumulated mis-specification, feedback loops that gradually reshape internal representations of value-relevant categories, and long-horizon distributional shift in the deployment environment, a system's behavior diverges from intended goals without ever forming explicit strategic plans or influence-maximizing intentions, making the divergence hard to detect until it is severe.

Produce a document with four parts:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims about the formal structure of both mechanisms, about patterns observable in present-day systems, about monitoring limitations over long deployment, and about the conditions under which each mechanism predicts detectable precursors.

2. A disagreements table that states each camp's position in wording its own advocates would accept; clarifies whether the mechanisms are mutually exclusive or can compound; and specifies whether "influence-seeking" requires representational strategic intent or can be defined purely behaviorally.

3. The cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp. For each crux, identify a concrete simulated scenario, empirical pattern, or decision case both sides agree bears on it, and record each camp's advance prediction of what that case will reveal.

4. A brief analysis of whether any portion of the dispute is verbal—particularly around definitions of "goal," "intent," or "drift"—stating which portion and explaining why.

The statement should be neutral: neither camp should feel caricatured. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke obscure named theories that depend on specific attributions; describe the positions conceptually.
