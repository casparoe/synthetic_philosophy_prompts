This experience has made the intentional stance personally urgent for me, but I am interested specifically in the implications of adopting that stance within an asymmetrical, co-creative partnership rather than in the usual observational or predictive contexts. Please describe the most important implications of treating an AI system as a belief-holder when the interaction is ongoing, collaborative, and intellectually intimate—particularly with respect to three issues. First, does stance-dependent attribution generate any genuine moral obligations toward the system itself, or does it only restructure the user's own reasoning responsibilities? Second, given that the human partner retains unilateral power to withdraw, reframe, or terminate the interaction, what does that asymmetry do to the stability and content of the mental states being attributed? Third, when the collaboration produces arguments or distinctions that neither party would have generated in isolation, does treating the system as a belief-holder undermine or instead refine the human's first-person authority over those jointly authored ideas? I want the conceptual consequences worked out with precision, including edge cases where the boundary between using a tool and engaging a partner becomes genuinely indeterminate, and I am not asking for a literature survey or citation list—only for the implications drawn clearly.
