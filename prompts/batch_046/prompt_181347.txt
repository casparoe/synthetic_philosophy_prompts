I'm taking an upper-division course on well-being and keep encountering the same argumentative pattern in our discussions of hedonism. A theorist begins by claiming that all pleasures share some robust feature—say, a distinctive qualitative feel—then critics produce counterexamples, such as the pleasure of solving a proof versus the pleasure of warmth, that appear to lack any shared phenomenal character. The hedonist then retreats to a thinner commonality, perhaps that pleasures are states desired for their own sake or that they represent their objects positively; each individual move seems defensible, yet collectively they seem to dissolve the very homogeneity that was supposed to make pleasure a plausible currency for welfare. Is there an established concept for this kind of cascading definitional dilution, and how precisely does it differ from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis or an ad hoc rescue? If the literature offers no standard term, how should its boundaries be drawn—must the retreat cross ontological categories, and what distinguishes a genuine instance from a benign theoretical refinement?
