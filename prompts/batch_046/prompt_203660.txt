I sit on our university's Committee on Institutional Speech, and my colleague Professor Elena Voss has adjudicated the following cases concerning whether an utterance in a governance context counts as a binding performative. First, the provost pronounced the prescribed formula appointing Dr. Chen to a chairship, but the official record misspelled the appointee's name; Voss held the appointment valid, treating the error as clerical rather than conventional. Second, the student government president declared the ratification of a constitution with correct quorum and wording, though she acted under severe coercion and lacked sincere commitment; Voss held the ratification performed but classified the failure as an abuse of the procedure rather than a misfire. Third, the dean pronounced the revocation of tenure with correct form and apparent authority, yet unknown to her the faculty handbook had been amended that morning stripping her of that power; Voss held the revocation invalid. Fourth, the chancellor declared the opening of a research center with full authority and correct formula, though the municipal occupancy permit had not yet been issued; Voss held the opening valid, treating the missing permit as an external regulatory failure. Now consider a new proposal: a trustee authorized under her charter employs the exact prescribed formula to establish a scholarship fund, with correct quorum, sincere intent, and full procedural compliance, but she does so through a biometric-verified voice synthesis system triggered by her deputy holding her authorization token, in accordance with a recently adopted institutional protocol permitting distributed presence; immediately afterward, the board of regents issues a resolution denying that distributed presence satisfies the charter's personal-presence requirement and refusing to recognize the act. Predict Voss's verdict; identify the closest analog among the four precedents; state whether she is implicitly running a conventionalist, an uptake-dependent, or another rule; specify the competing rule that fits her track record equally well; and name the single further case that would force a choice between them. Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the verdict on the new case; (2) the closest analog; (3) the primary rule; (4) the competing rule; (5) the distinguishing further case.
