Write a dialogue between two philosophers of science, Alex and Jordan, debating whether Richard Rorty's antirepresentationalist neopragmatism can genuinely account for scientific error-correction without covertly relying on a correspondence theory of truth.

Alex defends a Rortyan position: scientific vocabularies are tools for coping with the world, not representations that mirror an independent reality. Jordan holds a pragmatic representationalist view: we can reject the mirror metaphor and still maintain that beliefs possess minimal accuracy conditions that explain why some vocabularies fail.

Their disagreement should turn on a specific invented case. In 2018, a materials-science group proposed that a particular crystalline lattice exhibits coherent resonance — a stable oscillatory pattern that they took to be an intrinsic structural property. By 2023, further experiments showed the pattern vanishes when the measurement apparatus is calibrated differently; it is now widely regarded as an artifact of the detector's feedback loop. The original group concedes the mistake and abandons the term.

Alex argues that describing the 2018 claim as false is merely a retrospective evaluation that the vocabulary of coherent resonance no longer serves predictive and practical goals; there is no deeper representational failure, only a shift in what works. Jordan counters that this explanation is incomplete: to distinguish the 2018 mistake from a mere change in fashion, we need to say that the belief was inaccurate — that it misrepresented the lattice's actual behavior — without committing to any mirror-of-nature metaphysics. Jordan proposes a minimal accuracy condition: a belief is inaccurate if it fails to hold under the conditions that the best available practices specify as correct for testing it.

Alex responds that Jordan's minimal accuracy condition is just a normative rule about intersubjective robustness dressed up as metaphysics, and asks Jordan to explain, without using representational language, why the 2023 calibration should be preferred over the 2018 one except that it is more useful. Jordan offers an explanation based on robustness across independent measurement traditions, but admits that this robustness is itself evaluated by its contribution to broader practical projects.

The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth: each character should respond to the other's actual points rather than talk past each other. Both should be equally competent — neither should be a strawman. The dialogue should conclude without a neat resolution, but with a clearly articulated remaining disagreement about whether error-correction requires representational accuracy conditions or can be fully explained by shifts in vocabulary's practical utility.

Write in a formal, polished style. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not ask for direct quotations from Rorty's works.
