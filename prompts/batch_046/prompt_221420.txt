I'm presenting a position I'm calling developmental-niche teleosemantics at next week's philosophy-of-mind colloquium. The thesis is that mental representational content should be naturalized not through standard evolutionary selection histories (as in Millikan-style biosemantics) but through the contribution of a neural mechanism to constructing and maintaining the selective environment—its developmental niche—that reciprocally stabilizes that mechanism's activity patterns. I argue this solves two familiar problems at once: it gives a non-ad-hoc account of representations of non-actual or novel states, and it dissolves the disjunction problem by defining malfunction relative to a currently maintained niche rather than to an ancestral selection environment, since the niche-construction loop itself can fail.

My argument sketch is: (1) pure selection-history accounts cannot assign content to representations of states that never contributed to fitness in evolutionary history without invoking derived proper functions that multiply entities implausibly; (2) niche-construction dynamics provide a present-tense stabilization criterion that does not depend on ancestral tracking; (3) misrepresentation occurs when the mechanism's activity no longer sustains the niche conditions that stabilize it, which is empirically detectable via developmental disruption rather than via counterfactual evolutionary history.

The audience is a mixed philosophy-of-mind and philosophy-of-biology group with no decision theorists and almost no familiarity with teleosemantics. Specifically: two philosophers of biology who accept evolutionary explanation in general but think mental content requires an intentionality that biological function cannot supply; one committed enactivist who rejects subpersonal representational vehicles altogether; three cognitive-science graduate students who know predictive-processing frameworks well but have not read the teleosemantics literature; and one evolutionary psychologist serving as external commentator, who is sympathetic to niche-construction theory in ecology but thinks applying it to individual neural mechanisms is a category error.
