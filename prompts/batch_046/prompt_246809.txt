Hey, I'm drafting a take-home exam for a grad seminar on bioethics and personal identity. Need an exam question plus model answer and marking scheme. Constraints: 48 hours, about 2000 words, 30 marks total. Level is MA/PhD comprehensive.

Topic: advance directives and authority of the earlier self. But don't just ask for Dworkin vs Buchanan/Brock summaries — that's to easy and students will just regurgitate. I want them to work with a synthetic idea I'm injecting: the 'hinge-commitment model' of directive authority. The idea is that an advance directive isn't an empirical preference about the future (like Dworkin's critical interests) nor a best-interests calculation, but functions like a Wittgensteinian hinge in On Certainty — a practical commitment that makes the patient's other value-judgments possible and coherent. Once dementia dissolves the framework that holds that hinge, the directive doesn't bind like an order from a past self; it becomes structurally inert, not violated but inapplicable.

Case: Mr. L wrote a directive refusing antibiotics for pneumonia if he ever reached advanced Alzheimer's where he no longer recognized family. He now has advanced Alzheimer's, seems generally content, enjoys food and music, recognizes no one. He gets pneumonia. Family cites the directive; care team argues he's now a different subject with different interests.

The exam should ask students to do three things: (1) reconstruct the hinge-commitment model and show precisely how it differs from both precedent-autonomy and current-best-interests accounts; (2) apply it to Mr. L — should antibiotics be withheld?; (3) identify and respond to one objection that is internal to the hinge framing itself — not the usual 'he's happy now' but something about hinges (e.g., that hinges are immune to rational revision, so treating a directive as a hinge makes it unrevisable even when clearly mistaken, or that if the framework is dissolved we can't even identify the patient as the same practical agent).

Model answer needs to hit: the hinge vs empirical belief distinction from On Certainty; the point that authority here is structural not diachronic; the application arguing that since Mr. L's value-framework is dissolved, the directive is inert so withholding isn't required (though not necessarily wrong); and a response to the hinge-specific objection.

Marking scheme: 30 marks total. Seperate them out — maybe 6 for reconstruction, 6 for distinctions from standard views, 9 for application to case, 6 for the hinge-internal objection, 3 for argumentation/clarity. For each section list the specific error that costs marks: conflating hinge-commitment with 'just a really strong preference'; treating the model as Dworkin with fancy language; missing that on this model the directive loses authority precisely because psychological continuity is broken; or bringing in the standard happiness objection instead of one about hinge properties.

Keep it casual — it's going in a dataset — and no citations or reading lists, since this should be answerable without looking stuff up. Maybe include a typo or two like 'recieve' or 'to' instead of 'too'.
