I am a doctoral student in moral philosophy with a focus on Scottish sentimentalism, and I am preparing a conference presentation on Adam Smith's theory of sympathy. My argument turns on a somewhat neglected structural feature of his view: sympathy is not mere emotional contagion but a judgment of propriety, in which the spectator asks whether the agent's sentiment is proportionate to its cause; and within this framework, Smith treats sorrow and joy asymmetrically, since grief typically invites fellow-feeling without stringent conditions, whereas joy must pass a stricter test of desert before the spectator approves. I also discuss how the internalized spectator—the figure Smith describes as residing within one's own breast—can be more lenient or more severe than an actual bystander, precisely because he judges from the inside the fit between feeling and occasion rather than from the outside the appearance of the feeling. For a brief interlude in my talk, I would like an original joke that exploits one of these conceptual points: for instance, a scenario in which an internal spectator approves of an excessive emotional response because he correctly grasps the cause, while an external spectator condemns it for being disproportionate in appearance; or a punchline that depends on the idea that sympathy with joy is harder to earn than sympathy with sorrow because the impartial spectator demands evidence that the happiness is deserved. The joke should make sense to listeners who are generally familiar with Smith's moral philosophy but should not require knowledge of specific editions, correspondence, or recent secondary literature. Please do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to particular scholars. The tone should be suitable for an academic audience but structurally concise—something that could be delivered in under thirty seconds.
