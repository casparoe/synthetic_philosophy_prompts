Present an argument about self-locating belief that seems to prove too much and ask where it fails. (1) In the Sleeping Beauty problem, if centered worlds are the objects of credence, Beauty should distribute her credence equally over all centered worlds compatible with her evidence—Monday-Heads, Monday-Tails, and Tuesday-Tails—yielding 1/3 for Heads. (2) Now consider a nested protocol: if Tails, Beauty is woken on Tuesday with a hidden counter showing how many prior Tuesday awakenings have occurred in this branch; her memory is then wiped and she is re-woken with the counter incremented, repeating up to an unknown maximum N. (3) Each Tuesday awakening with a distinct counter value is a different centered world, so the Tails branch contains 1 + N worlds while Heads contains one; by the same rule her credence in Heads should be 1/(2+N). (4) Because N is unknown and arbitrarily large, her rational credence in Heads can be driven near zero by a structural parameter she cannot observe, violating the principle that belief should be stable under ignorance of hidden framing conditions. (5) Hence the centered-worlds rule must be rejected, and self-locating belief should always be governed by standard uncentered conditioning, giving 1/2 in every variant. Which step is wrong? Explain why it seemed plausible and what abandoning it costs for the framework of self-locating credence. Respond as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
