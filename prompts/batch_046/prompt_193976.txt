hey, i'm putting together my commitments on the self for a seminar and want a consistency check before i present. here's where i stand, roughly:

a) the self isn't a cartesian thing or hidden essence—just a center of narrative gravity, a useful fiction we tell to track intentions and actions over time.

b) but first-person authority about my present mental states is direct and real. when i say i'm in pain i'm not interpreting a story; there's immediate access.

c) personal identity over time is narrative continuity—psychological continuity shaped into an ongoing autobiograpical thread, not just bare memory or character.

d) these narratives aren't brain-bound. they extend into my close relationships and digital archives, and in principle the pattern could be mirrored or distributed elsewhere.

e) self-deception is just narrative incoherance—suppressed chapters, rewritten parts—not some true self hiding behind the curtain.

f) there's also a minimal pre-narrative embodied agency, like being the bare subject of action, that grounds the narrative but isn't part of it itself.

g) and i reject any idea of a true self behind the narrative; authenticity is just high coherence in the story.

so which ones actually clash, which only look like they do, and what's the least costly revision? rank the tensions by severity—don't just list them as equally bad. i want an audit, not a lecture: where do i have to give something up, and what does it cost? also, is there an apparent contradiction that dissolves once you look closer?
