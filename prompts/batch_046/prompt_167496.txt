So I was working on a piece about how old philosophical arguments keep popping up in modern science debates, and I got into a long thread with a historians friend about this medieval thinker—Al-Ghazali I think?—who said fire doesn't actually burn cotton, God just creates the burning every single time the two get close. The wild part isn't even the theology; it's that he doesn't deny fire is usually followed by burning. He just says that's a custom or habit God keeps up, not a real necessary link in nature.

That got me thinking about a pattern I see in other contexts too, not just theology. You have someone who rejects that A really causes B at the metaphysical level—maybe they say correlation isn't causation, or that laws are just descriptions—but then they immediately turn around and build an entire predicitive framework that treats A and B as if they're locked together, just under a different name like regularity or stable disposition. The science works fine, the predictions are identical, but technically they've denied causation and replaced it with something that behaves exactly like causation without the commitment. I've seen this in occasionalist debates, but also in some modern philosophy of science discussions where people want to be anti-realist about laws but still do physics.

My question is: is there an established term for this specific maneuver—the structural move of stripping away necessary causal connections and then re-importing them functionally through a renamed mechanism like habit, custom, or regularity? I don't mean occasionalism broadly, and I don't mean Hume's constant conjunction exactly, though it's in the neighborhood. I'm asking about the rhetorical or conceptual architecture of denying-then-smuggling-back. Does that have a precise name, and what distinguishes it from just being a cautious empiricist who never claimed there was metaphysical glue in the first place? I'm trying to map where the edges are—when is it a genuine denial of causation with a workaround, versus just refusing to talk about metaphysics?
