I run a philosophy-of-tech seminar, about 8 postgrads, 75 minutes. I want an in-class game that makes them feel why defining "avoid side effects" for a low-impact AI collapses unless you've already smuggled in a full value specification.

Don't use the standard cleaning-robot-breaks-vase setup. Instead, bake in this twist: the problem isn't just physical interference, it's counterfactual baselines and indexical harm. The group has to decide what counts as the "unchanged world" that side-effects are measured against — e.g., the world as it was 30 seconds ago, vs the world as it would have been without this agent at all — and they should discover that picking that baseline already requires deciding which changes are worth tracking, which is a value call disguised as measurement.

Split them into two roles: half are "engineers" who must write a formal, value-free constraint (no words like good, bad, harm, preference) that an AI should obey to avoid side effects; half are "auditors" who get hidden scenario cards and have to apply the constraint literally. Use reputation points, not cash — engineers lose points when auditors show their rule either permits something awful or blocks something obviously benign. Then swap roles with a harder scenario involving a farming drone that must avoid altering soil chemistry, but the hidden card reveals that "no change from baseline" depends entirely on which baseline you picked.

The debrief should map each failure back onto the philosophical problem: counterfactual stability without normative deviation, the baseline-selection problem, and why low-impact framing can't escape it. Be honest about what the game can't reproduce — probably the full specification problem itself, since we're stuck with toy scenarios — and tell me what's going to go wrong the first time, like one group cheating by using common-sense values without noticing.

Please lead with the bottom line — the single thing the activity proves — in the first sentence, then lay out the rules, roles, hidden cards, point system, debrief timeline, and the honest limitation, with no preamble and no restating the question. And please, dont use real citations or reading lists — the model wont have them.
