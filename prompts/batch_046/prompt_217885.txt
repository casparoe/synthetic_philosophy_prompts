I am a senior undergraduate in a seminar on practical rationality and longtermist ethics, and for our final project I am organizing a recorded interview with an unnamed visiting scholar who defends a modified cause-prioritization framework. Rather than treating importance, tractability, and neglectedness as multiplicative factors that yield a linear expected-value score, she proposes that when an intervention addresses risks with catastrophic tail outcomes the standard formula should be replaced by a risk-weighted expected-utility procedure: the importance term becomes convex in the severity of worst-case scenarios rather than linear in lives affected, while tractability is discounted by an epistemic-uncertainty factor that scales with the institutional distance between current capacity and the scale of intervention required. I want eight to ten interview questions that press her on the mechanics of this hybrid view, not on general effective altruism. Specifically, I need at least one question on why convex weighting of catastrophic importance does not collapse into simple maximin reasoning; one on how she measures institutional distance without circular reliance on the tractability score itself; and one on what the framework prescribes for causes that score high on conventional importance but have near-zero tractability because the relevant actors are not influenceable—does the convexity term still recommend investment in advocacy, or is it nullified by the institutional-distance penalty? For each of these three, please include a sharp follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—for instance, if she appeals to a threshold of sufficient institutional proximity, ask what sets that threshold and whether it is endogenous to the risk-weighting; if she distinguishes advocacy from direct intervention, ask whether that distinction survives once importance is weighted convexly across catastrophic scenarios. I also want a closing question that applies the framework to a concrete tension: comparing near-term wild-animal suffering reduction, which offers high tractability but linear importance, against long-term AI safety research, which carries extreme convex importance but institutional distance so large that tractability approaches zero—does her procedure yield a determinate ranking, or does it hand the decision back to an explicit judgment call about risk tolerance? The tone should be rigorous but accessible to philosophy undergraduates who have read basic decision theory but not the specialist literature on existential-risk prioritization.
