Write a deadpan internal corporate memo, approximately 500 words, from the HR Department of the fictional firm Geist Consulting, announcing the company-wide adoption of Recognition-First Management (RFM)—an organizational framework explicitly modeled on Hegel's master-slave dialectic. The memo should lay out the new hierarchy: Executive Lords who depend on recognition from subordinate Bondsman Divisions; the quarterly Recognition Audit that reveals Lord-level self-consciousness is structurally hollow because it is conferred by consciousnesses that are not independent; and the Bondsman professional-development track, in which true self-certainty is achieved through direct labor upon material workflow and through the formative experience of existential risk (corporate restructuring). Include a brief sidebar proposing Mutual Recognition Workshops intended to establish equality between Lords and Bondsmen, and explain in flat bureaucratic prose why these workshops must necessarily fail: equality would dissolve the very hierarchical conditions that make recognition possible. The satire should exaggerate but not misrepresent the dialectic's core dynamics—the asymmetry of recognition, the slave's labor and fear of death, and the reversal that grants the bondsman the more complete form of self-consciousness—so that a reader familiar with the Phenomenology recognizes the logic being carried to its administrative extreme. Target the structural tension of the dialectic itself rather than any named individual. Maintain a consistently deadpan, ironic corporate voice throughout, and do not request citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts.
