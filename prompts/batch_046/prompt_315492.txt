I have come to hold a fairly strict view about what heritability coefficients do and do not license. Specifically, I take it that broad-sense heritability (H²) is nothing more than a population-level ratio describing the proportion of phenotypic variance associated with genetic variance under a specific, observed distribution of environments; it carries no information about the causal mechanisms linking particular genotypes to phenotypes, and — crucially — it is formally silent on what would happen if the environmental variance structure were deliberately altered. I therefore think that arguments moving from high heritability estimates (say, for educational attainment or personality dimensions) to the conclusion that environmental interventions must be limited or futile commit a specific category mistake: they treat a static, descriptive variance decomposition as if it were a counterfactual-stability claim about environmental invariance. The position is that this is not just a common misinterpretation but a conceptual error that collapses the distinction between "genetic variance explains X% of observed differences" and "genetic factors constrain the range of possible outcomes under changed conditions."

What I want is an honest map of this view's exposure. What would genuinely count against it? I am looking for evidence or arguments that should move someone holding this position — not merely rhetorical objections — sorted from decisive to merely awkward. For instance, are there formal results showing that heritability estimates can be legitimately extended to counterfactual regimes under specific conditions? Are there empirical cases where high-heritability traits showed unexpectedly large environmental malleability, or conversely, cases where low heritability co-occurred with intervention resistance? I also want to know what defenders of the standard interpretation would concede as genuinely damaging versus what they would dismiss as confusion about the definition. Finally, I would like an assessment of whether my view risks being insulated into unfalsifiability: if every successful intervention is explained away by "that changed the environmental variance structure, so heritability no longer applies," has the position become a tautology, and is that a problem for a conceptual claim of this kind?
