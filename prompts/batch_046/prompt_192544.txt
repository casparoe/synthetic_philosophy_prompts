hey so in my lit class we just read parts of this book called Le Ton beau de Marot where this guy Hofstadter compares like a dozen english translations of some old poem by Marot about a sick girl. i noticed this weird pattern that keeps happening: the translators always keep the rhyme scheme and the syllable count perfect, but to do that they swap out really specific images for vaguer emotional words. like in one version theres a concrete flower or body part in the french and in the english it just becomes "my heart" or "sadness" because thats easier to rhyme. and then people defend it saying the "music" is still there even though the picture totally changed. is there a specific name for this exact thing? not just "free translation" or "poetic license" but the pattern where rhyme and meter force concrete stuff to become abstract and everyone treats that as preserving form? and how is it different from just paraphrasing? i feel like its its own failure mode with its own rules but i dont know the word for it.
