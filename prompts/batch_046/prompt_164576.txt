In my graduate philosophy of science seminar we have settled into what I have started calling a projectibility deadlock, and after a year of circular discussion I am less interested in adjudicating the dispute than in diagnosing its anatomy. The focus is the classic paradox involving the predicate grue — green before some future date, blue thereafter — and the shared premise that inductive inference must treat some predicates as projectible and others as not. The natural-kind camp argues that the privilege tracks real structural divisions in nature: green belongs to a lawlike kind, whereas grue is gerrymandered. The pragmatic camp counters that projectibility is nothing but entrenchment in our linguistic practice and predictive success, and that appeals to naturalness merely rationalize that history. Every participant accepts the formal symmetry of the two predicates, agrees that either framework can accommodate any observation by adjusting auxiliary assumptions, and can reproduce the standard examples without error; nevertheless, no one has revised their position, and our meetings now feel like a ritual repetition of opening moves.

I want to understand what kind of disagreement this is. Is there a hidden empirical question — about the cognitive architecture of human categorization, about the formal properties of predictive algorithms, or about the actual history of scientific theory change — that could in principle break the stalemate? Or do the sides differ not about facts but about standards of explanation: one demands that induction be justified by ontology, the other by practical success, with neither standard reducible to the other? Could it be that they are answering different questions under one name — one asking what makes induction rational, the other asking what makes it psychologically available — so that they talk past each other without contradiction? Or is the impasse a sign that the intuition about naturalness is genuinely bedrock, that the question is ill-posed, or that both camps are implicitly relying on a distinction — perhaps between descriptive and normative projectibility — that neither has made explicit?

Given whatever diagnosis you think best fits this pattern, I also want to know what progress would look like. Would it require a formal result about the convergence of prediction rules, an empirical finding about concept learning in children or machines, a conceptual distinction that separates the rational from the psychological question, or a demonstration that no neutral arbiter exists? Or should we expect this particular stall to be permanent, and if so, why?
