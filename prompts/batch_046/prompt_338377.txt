For a final exam in an upper-level undergraduate course on value theory and rational choice—allocate 90 minutes for this section, 30 marks out of 100, and expect roughly 800–1000 words—design an essay question that asks students to evaluate whether the relation “better than” is transitive, using a spectrum argument constructed specifically for this problem. To avoid answers that merely recite textbook summaries of the sorites paradox, inject the conceptual device of a continuous “improvement ladder” comprising one thousand lives, L0 through L999, where each successive life differs from its predecessor only by an imperceptible increment of one good (for instance, a marginal gain in aesthetic comprehension), yet L0 is a life of bare survival with minimal pleasure and L999 is one of extraordinary flourishing. Require students to defend one of two positions: (A) that “better than” is fully transitive, which commits us to saying L999 is astronomically better than L0 despite the imperceptibility of each step, or (B) that transitivity fails globally because of “threshold incommensurability”—the claim that the metric of evaluation shifts across the ladder (say, from hedonic to eudaimonic) at certain points, yielding homogeneous value-regions within which local transitivity holds but across which comparison is either undefined or non-transitive, producing only a partial order. They must address the standard money-pump objection to intransitive preference and carefully distinguish a metaphysical failure of value-transitivity from mere epistemic indeterminacy about where the thresholds lie.

Provide a model answer—about 400 words—that takes position B: it should reconstruct the ladder precisely, defend local transitivity within uniform regions, explain why the money-pump objection targets rational preference rather than objective value-relations, and identify the specific error of treating the spectrum as a purely linguistic vagueness problem rather than as a structural feature of plural, non-commensurable values. Then supply a marking scheme that awards 6 marks for accurate reconstruction of the spectrum argument and ladder; 6 marks for correct use of threshold incommensurability and regional partial ordering; 6 marks for handling the money-pump objection together with the preference/value distinction; 6 marks for clarity on epistemic versus metaphysical failure of transitivity; and 6 marks for argument structure, precision, and avoidance of conflation with language-based sorites solutions. Specify that answers which assert transitivity “by definition” without engaging the ladder should receive no more than 10 marks total, and that answers which confuse intransitivity of value with irrationality of choice should lose 4 marks.
