My verdicts on when an LLM has genuinely induced a concept in the Bongard sense, and whether there's a principle behind them. When a model classifies a novel Bongard problem after seeing only two examples with no visible chain-of-thought, I call that real induction. When it solves the same problem only after being given an explicit symbolic rule, I say that's not induction but instruction-following. When it succeeds only on figures rendered in the original Bongard visual style and fails with geometrically identical shapes in a different format, I take that as tracking surface statistics rather than abstract relations. And when a human child transfers a structurally identical distinction to a social domain without training, I treat that as the benchmark that models haven't met. But I'm unsure whether cross-domain transfer should be part of the criterion, or whether consistent success across visual styles is enough. Is there a coherent account of Bongard-style induction that preserves my first three verdicts, and what does it say about whether the fourth case overturns my judgment about the first? If the principle requires cross-domain transfer, then my first verdict was too generous; if it doesn't, then I need to explain why human social induction is irrelevant to the machine case without making the criterion ad hoc. What further case—say, a model that induces correctly across styles but only within the geometric domain—would force me to revise?
