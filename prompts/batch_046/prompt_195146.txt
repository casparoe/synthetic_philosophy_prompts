I'm in a graduate seminar on metaphysics where we've been working through the standard literature on dispositions, finks, and masks—particularly the idea that a disposition can be present but prevented from manifesting by an interfering factor, or hidden behind a counterfactual blocker. Lately, though, I've noticed a recurring pattern that doesn't quite fit either category, and I'm wondering whether there's an established term for it, or at least a recognized distinction in the literature.

Consider two concrete cases. First, take a researcher who genuinely possesses the intellectual virtue of openness to counterevidence. When an anomalous result appears, she doesn't suppress it (that would be a mask) and she doesn't lack the virtue altogether. Instead, her openness is consistently redirected: she absorbs the anomaly into an elaborate auxiliary framework that preserves her core commitments, and the very act of accommodating the data feels to her like a triumph of open-mindedness. The disposition is fully active, but its typical manifestation—revision or abandonment of belief—is systematically replaced by a different outcome that serves the same psychological role. Second, imagine an engineered material that is genuinely brittle, but embedded in it is a responsive lattice that, upon stress, doesn't prevent fracture but transforms the fracture into a highly ordered, structurally useful pattern. The brittleness isn't masked or finked away; it expresses itself, yet the expression is always converted into something that functions as its opposite.

In both instances, there's an underlying disposition that remains intact and causally active, yet there's a stable, predictable mechanism—call it a converter or redirector—that doesn't block the manifestation but transforms it into an outcome that undermines the usual functional significance of the disposition. It seems different from a simple fink, because the disposition does manifest; different from a mask, because there's no hidden underlying state that would manifest if the mask were removed; and different from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis, because the mechanism isn't an ad hoc add-on but an integral part of the system's architecture. Does this configuration have a name in the philosophy of dispositions or in adjacent literature? How precisely does it relate to the fink/mask distinction, and if there is no established label, how should we characterize its boundaries—particularly the line between a redirective mechanism and a case where the original disposition has simply been replaced by a different one?
