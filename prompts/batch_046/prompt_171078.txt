I am a novelist preparing to lead a three-hour MFA workshop on the mechanics of creative revision. My students hold a binary view: either art emerges from inexplicable inspiration or it is the disciplined execution of craft. I want to introduce them to Douglas Hofstadter's model of creativity as variation-on-a-theme—systematic adjustment of conceptual parameters, the metaphor often rendered as knob-twiddling—to show that discovery can be systematic without being mechanical. I am not asking for an exposition of Hofstadter; I need a pedagogical plan for this specific audience. Lay out the dependency order of the ideas so that parameter-variation arrives as a description of artistic discovery rather than engineering; identify the single carrying example—drawn from fiction revision, such as adjusting a protagonist's relationship to silence across successive drafts—that must possess and lack certain properties to bear the weight; anticipate the misconceptions they will generate, particularly that knob-twiddling is random trial-and-error, that it eliminates authorial agency, or that the underlying theme is fixed rather than revealed through variation; specify the precise point in the sequence where each misconception should be intercepted; propose one check for understanding that distinguishes genuine comprehension from mere acquiescence; and state what part of Hofstadter's broader cognitive framework should be explicitly left out at this level. Please answer in American English.
