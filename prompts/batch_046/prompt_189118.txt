Write a short story set in a fully occupied Hilbert-style hotel with a countably infinite number of rooms, but frame the narrative around a night manager who is a strict finitist: she denies actual infinities and holds that any plurality is either genuinely finite or only potentially unending, never completed as an infinite totality. The plot should center on an overbooking emergency in which she must relocate every current guest to the room numbered twice their current room, thereby freeing all the odd-numbered rooms for an infinite bus of new arrivals. Dramatize her internal resistance as she executes the pairing, especially at the moment she recognizes that the subset of even-numbered rooms appears equipotent with the entire hotel—something her finitism should reject as a completed comparison between two infinite totalities. Weave in concrete sensory details: the brass numbering on the doors, the echo of announcements in an endless corridor, the physical exhaustion of a shift that never reaches a final room. Let her attempt to rebut Cantor-style reasoning about one-to-one correspondence by appealing to potentiality rather than actual enumeration, but have the practical logic of the hotel force her to act as if the correspondence were real. Avoid explicit moralizing or allegorical conclusions; the tension should remain unresolved, lived through her actions and thoughts rather than summarized. The story should be intelligible and conceptually rich to a reader familiar with Hilbert's hotel and Cantor's arguments, without requiring citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts.
