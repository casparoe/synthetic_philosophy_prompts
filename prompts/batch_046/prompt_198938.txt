I am preparing materials for an advanced philosophy discussion group at my high school, and I need a decision-tree taxonomy that classifies positions on wireheading and reward tampering without relying on familiar labels that participants might memorize rather than understand. The tree should be built from concrete diagnostic cases arranged in logical order. First, ask whether direct neurological self-stimulation in a conscious human and an artificial agent’s direct editing of its own reward function are evaluatively equivalent forms of wireheading, or whether the presence or absence of phenomenological consciousness splits them. From each of those branches, split on whether retaining reflective awareness—that one has actively modified one’s reward mechanism—restores a form of second-order autonomy and rational self-governance, or whether such awareness only deepens the failure by making the deception explicit. Then, for positions that allow some form of reward modification, introduce a further branch asking whether achieving success entirely within a virtual or simulated environment—where the reward signal is decoupled from any external state of affairs—counts as genuinely satisfying one’s original goals or instead constitutes a sophisticated form of self-deception. Each leaf should carry the name of the position if it is named in the literature, or be marked “unnamed,” followed by one sentence describing its most awkward or counterintuitive commitment. I also want the tree to include at least one logically possible combination of answers that is currently unoccupied—for instance, the view that accepts biological wireheading as rational but rejects artificial reward tampering solely on phenomenological grounds—with a brief diagnosis of whether that gap results from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Every node should present a specific scenario rather than asking participants whether they “are a hedonist” or “accept functionalism,” so that the instrument sorts people by what they believe rather than by the vocabulary they know. Finally, please flag any pair of positions that this particular tree, given its three branching questions, cannot cleanly separate, and do not include any bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations, since the taxonomy should work through reasoning alone.
