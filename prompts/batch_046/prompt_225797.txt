I've come around to thinking that pain asymbolia doesn't just show affect can split off from nociception -- it shows the badness of pain is entirely in that affective gap, and that pure sensory pain is basically value-neutral, like seeing red without caring. So my view is: pain is bad solely because of its aversive emotional coloring, and asymbolic patients prove you can have the full sensory profile with zero badness. What would actually count against this? Not the usual "but they still pull their hand back" unless it's genuinely decisive. I'm looking for the real defeatters: evidence that asymbolics have hidden negative affect they just can't report; arguments that the sensory quality itself carries intrinsic disvalue regardless of affect; maybe a functionalist point that the two dimensions are too entangled to separate cleanly. And what aboutt the hypothetical "reverse asymbolia" case -- someone with full suffering behavior but who insists there's no sensory quality at all -- does that break my separation or just muddle the taxonomy? Rank these from fatal to merely awkward. Also be honest: has this view been insulated into unfalsifiability by reclassifying any sensory-pain-without-suffering as "not real pain"? If so, is that insulation a real problem for a view of this kind, or just decent taxonomy?
