I'm constructing an instrument for an advanced philosophy-of-biology seminar that sorts positions on biological function without relying on the familiar etiological-versus-causal-role dichotomy students can recite from memory. Please write a decision tree—presented as a nested taxonomy with three binary split nodes—that takes a respondent from a starting commitment to one of eight logical leaves. The root split (Q1) should ask whether evolutionary selection history is necessary for a trait to possess a genuine biological function. The left branch should then split (Q2) on whether artificial selection or rational design counts as an equally qualifying history. The right branch should split on whether current causal contribution to the organism's survival, reproduction, or systemic robustness is sufficient on its own.

Each leaf must carry either the name of an actual position or the label 'unnamed,' followed by exactly one sentence describing its most awkward commitment with respect to at least one of these concrete cases: (i) a synthetic cardiac tissue patch engineered to contract in response to light rather than electrical signaling, implanted in a mammal, contributing to circulation, but lacking any evolutionary history; (ii) a naturally evolved vestigial structure maintained by clear past selection but now making no causal contribution; (iii) a rationally designed synthetic gene circuit built to produce insulin that has been deactivated and now produces nothing. Identify any logically unoccupied combinations among the eight and diagnose the vacancy—whether it stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or being merely unexplored. Also flag any pairs of leaves that standard named accounts fail to separate cleanly, explaining precisely where the ambiguity lies (for example, whether an etiological defender who accepts synthetic biology lands on the natural-only or the design-inclusive branch).

The diagnostic questions at each node should be phrased as concrete claims or case judgments that a student can answer without already knowing which philosophical label they point toward. The tree should be built from the logical space first, with positions then located within it. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and present the response in American English.
