As a student in our philosophy of language seminar, I have been trying to articulate a pattern I keep encountering in discussions of semantic externalism that does not match the standard Twin Earth thought experiment in any straightforward way. Here is the configuration: a natural kind term, for example jade, has long been used by lay speakers with a particular mental representation and a deferential intention toward whatever local experts identify. The experts then discover that the term actually covers two chemically distinct minerals, jadeite and nephrite, and revise their taxonomy accordingly. At that point, the causal-historical chain that fixes reference for the expert community splits, so that according to an externalist account the experts now refer to two different kinds. Yet for a substantial period the lay community continues to use the term as a single unified kind; their mental states and social practices have not yet adjusted, and they still defer, but now to an expert community that is itself divided. The result is a kind of reference inertia: the external environment and expert practice have shifted, but lay usage remains suspended in an intermediate state, neither fully anchored to the old unified kind nor fully split along the new scientific lines. I see analogous dynamics with morphological versus phylogenetic classifications of species, or with revisions to the chemical characterization of water under extreme conditions, where expert reference changes but pre-theoretic usage lags behind.

Is there an established concept in the literature on the division of linguistic labor or on reference change for this specific phenomenon—the resistance or lag of lay deferential reference after an expert-driven split in the underlying kind? If such a concept exists, how precisely does it differ from ordinary reference shift, from simple ignorance of scientific revision, or from straightforward polysemy? If there is no dedicated term, what distinguishes this intermediate state from both standard externalist reference-fixing and from purely internalist meaning? In particular, does the existence of such a lag constitute a genuine difficulty for semantic externalism, or can it be treated as a purely temporal or sociological delay without semantic consequences? I am not looking for a bare label; I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its boundaries lie—under what conditions the inertia collapses into reference ambiguity, which cases merely look similar but involve gradual linguistic evolution rather than expert-driven revision, and whether the carving applies only to natural kind terms or extends to other deferential domains as well.
