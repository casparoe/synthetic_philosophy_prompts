heres the student answer (im paraphrasing from the exam booklet):

The student says Sartre thinks freedom means we assign meaning to our facticity, which is basically right. Facticity is the raw material—like being born in 1985 or having damaged ears—while freedom is the interpretation layered on top. The pianist is in bad faith because she treats her despair as mechanically caused by her ears, like one billiard ball hitting another. For Sartre, emotions are never caused; consciousness is transparent, so she is fully responsible for every emotional state she lives through.

But then they slip in two places. First, they claim facticity is purely external to consciousness, like a rock sitting outside a window that we then observe, missing that facticity is always already given within the field of consciousness and is assumed rather than encountered as pure exteriority. Second, they conflate Sartre's fundamental project—which is global and pre-reflective—with moment-to-moment emotional selection, saying she should just choose a different feeling now, like switching radio stations. This makes Sartre sound like he advocates Stoic detachment from suffering, but actually he thinks suffering is fully assumed and lived through, not detached from. They get the rejection of Freudian determinism right, but overstate transparency by ignoring that bad faith itself involves a kind of self-deceptive opacity.
