I am preparing a six-month independent research proposal for a foundation grant in moral philosophy and the history of ethics. The project concerns G. E. Moore's treatment of beauty and friendship as intrinsic goods in Principia Ethica, specifically whether his doctrine of organic unities—according to which the value of a whole need not equal the sum of the values of its parts—applies consistently across both domains. The standard commentary treats Moore's aesthetics and his ethics of personal affection as separate chapters: organic unity explains why a beautiful object placed in an ugly context can diminish the value of the overall scene, but scholars rarely ask whether friendship itself, as a complex mental and relational state, should be reconstructed as an organic unity in this same technical sense. I want the proposal to formulate the precise research question as follows: if friendship is analyzed as an organic unity composed of mental states (affection, consciousness of reciprocity, pleasure) together with relational properties, does Moore's framework yield the non-additive result that introducing instrumental goods—practical support, companionship, shared projects—can either raise or lower the friendship's intrinsic value in ways parallel to aesthetic cases? Or does the relational and temporal structure of friendship break the part-whole analogy, thereby undermining the consistency of Moore's pluralism about goods? The proposal should include: a precise statement of the question and why existing scholarship leaves it open (the separation of aesthetic and ethical analysis in the Moore literature); the methodological approach—close logical reconstruction of Moore's arguments about intrinsic value in the relevant chapters, combined with a formal model of organic-unity judgments applied to interpersonal relations rather than perceptual scenes; what a successful result would look like (for example, a reconstructed account of friendship-value that either integrates with aesthetic organic unities or diverges from them, with explicit consequences for whether Moore can consistently maintain that both beauty and friendship are fundamental goods); the conditions under which the project fails (such as finding that friendship lacks the requisite part-whole structure, or that Moore's text implicitly restricts organic-unity judgments to perceptual complexes); and an honest assessment of feasibility for a six-month, non-archival project, including the risk that the comparison collapses into a general defense or critique of Moore's value theory rather than the targeted cross-domain analysis. The intended deliverable is a 6,000-word paper suitable for a specialist journal in the history of ethics. Please do not include a bibliography, literature review organized by citation, or reading list; I will supply all references myself. Write the proposal in formal academic prose appropriate for a review panel with philosophical literacy but no specialist expertise in Moore.
