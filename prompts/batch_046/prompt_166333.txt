Please argue that negative utilitarianism ought to reject the procreation asymmetry—not because suffering and pleasure carry symmetric weight, but because, on a four-dimensionalist account of personal persistence, the suffering of a merely potential person is metaphysically continuous with that of an actual person. From this, defend the claim that bringing a being into existence with a non-trivial risk of severe suffering is structurally analogous to extending the suffering of an existing being, and therefore that suffering-focused ethics should treat non-creation and the cessation of suffering as governed by a single principle rather than by asymmetrical obligations. Address the charge that this collapses negative utilitarianism into an anti-natalism vulnerable to the non-identity problem, and show why the four-dimensionalist framing either dissolves that problem or renders it irrelevant to the moral calculus of suffering.
