I've got a presentation next tuesday in our philosophy dept's grad seminar and I'm definatly not ready for the Q&A. I'm defending a weird take on the Meno problem — not the usual "knowledge is justified true belief plus reliability" line. My claim is that knowledge is better than true belief because it comes with what I'm calling "epistemic custodianship": when you actually know something, you're bound to maintain it, defend it against counter-evidence, and integrate it with your other beliefs in a way that makes you genuinely responsible for its downstream effects. True belief — especially the kind people get from algorithms, experts, or lucky guesses — lacks that binding, so it's not action-guiding in the same way. I'm especially interested in cases where someone outsources a belief to an AI: they might end up with a true belief, but since they don't hold custodianship, they don't have knowledge.
