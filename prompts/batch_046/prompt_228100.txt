I'm in this undergrad seminar on democratic ethics and I've been trying to nail down my own position, but I'm not sure it all fits together. Here's roughly where I've landed: (1) voting is basically an expressive act — you're putting your judgment on record, not pulling a lever to cause an outcome. (2) There's still a moral duty to vote when the stakes are high and the cost is low, but that duty comes from a general civic obligation to help legitimate democratic authority, not from any expected utility of my single ballot. (3) So if there's a clear lesser-evil among the actually viable candidates, I should vote for them even if I think both are pretty bad, because registering that this one does less harm is part of the expressive duty in (2). (4) But I also think it's totally okay to vote for a third-party candidate with zero chance of winning if that's where your real judgment lies, even in high-stakes races, because suppressing your true view just to avoid wasting the vote turns the ballot into an instrumental calcuation, which contradicts (1). (5) At the same time, I think that civic obligation in (2) isn't unique to voting — jury duty or serving on a local board can discharge it — so skipping an election isn't automatically a moral failure if you're engaged in other ways. (6) Finally, I think abstaining is the right move when you're genuinely indifferent between the viable options, but wrong when you have a real preference, since a forced vote expresses a false judgment.

Do these hang together? I'm especially worried about (3) and (4) pulling in opposite directions — one seems to demand strategic expression for the least bad, the other forbids strategic suppression of your real preference. And (2) with (5) feels like it might collapse into voting is optional as long as you do something civic, which undermines the specific duty I wanted. But maybe some of these tensions are only surface-level? I want an audit: rank the real conflicts by severity, say which ones dissolve on closer look, and tell me the least costly revision to make the package consistent without just abandoning the core idea that voting is mainly about expressing judgment rather than causing outcomes. Don't worry about giving me a reading list — I just want to know if I'm being coherent.
