I am preparing the final examination for my graduate seminar on anthropic reasoning and cosmological philosophy. I need one essay question that tests whether students genuinely understand the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and the self-indication assumption (SIA), together with the so-called presumptuous-philosopher objection, rather than simply reproducing Bostrom’s taxonomy from memory. To prevent recitation, I want the problem embedded in the following niche framing that I have devised for this exam: the Nested Archival Hypothesis.

In this scenario, conscious observers arise only inside discrete “archives”—localized spacetime regions of uniform internal observer-density—scattered throughout an otherwise empty multiverse. Archive-type A contains approximately 10^12 observers; archive-type B contains approximately 10. A rational agent within any archive possesses only local phenomenological evidence that reveals nothing about the archive’s size. A cosmologist proposes that, on independent physical grounds, the prior probability of an A-type archive is astronomically lower than that of a B-type archive, because the physical conditions required to generate 10^12 observers are far more specific and unstable.

Your task is to construct the exam question, model answer, and marking scheme according to these specifications.

The exam question itself should ask students to do four things: first, state SSA and SIA precisely as they apply to selecting one’s reference class within this nested archival structure; second, derive the contrasting probabilistic verdicts that SSA and SIA yield about whether one is in an A-type or B-type archive; third, explain whether the presumptuous-philosopher objection applies in this setting, with particular attention to whether SIA illegitimately allows anthropic weighting to swamp the low physical prior for A-type archives, and whether SSA avoids the objection only by rendering anthropic reasoning inert; fourth, defend one assumption over the other, or propose a constrained synthesis, addressing specifically whether anthropic reasoning can ever legitimately override strong physical priors without collapsing into presumption.

The question should be calibrated for a 75-minute written response at the advanced graduate level.

Please provide:
- The exam question, written in clear, formal prose;
- A model answer of approximately 750 words that demonstrates the specific conceptual moves required, including the formal contrast between random selection within a reference class and existence-weighted updating across reference classes;
- A marking scheme out of 20 points, built around five must-hit analytical moves, with marks allocated to each, and a one-line note on how candidates typically fail that particular point;
- The scheme should explicitly distinguish an A-grade answer—which must engage with the tension between physical priors and anthropic weighting, and must address why the nested structure does not automatically dissolve the presumptuous-philosopher worry—from a B-grade answer, which merely describes the two assumptions and rehearses the standard objection without applying it to the archival scenario.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation prompts. The answer should depend only on reasoning that a well-prepared student could produce without external reference materials.
