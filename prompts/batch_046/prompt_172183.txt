"The Humean view, like Lewis's best-system account, holds that laws are the regularities that figure in the simplest, strongest, most balanced axiomatization of all particular facts. But I think this reply fails. Once you build the law into the property, you're just describing a regularity in how properties are distributed across the mosaic. That means the law supervenes on the particular facts, which is exactly Humean supervenience. So essentialism collapses into Humeanism. Also, Humeans can accept that properties have essences; they just deny those essences do any extra governing work. So the anti-Humean hasn't gained anything."
