I run the review panel for our city’s small arts grant fund. We recieve about forty applications a month, each reviewer only gets like twenty minutes per file, and then we have to sign off on a scorecard that the council actually uses to cut checks. Our charter says we score artistic merit only, but people keep sneaking in reasons like "this painter is houseless," "this dance piece will make the council look progressive," or "the theme is politically important right now." I want a reusable decision procedre my panel can run on every app — ordered tests, with what counts as passing each, where it stops with a hard no, and where it has to hand the call to a person because it’s values not facts. Ground it in the wrong-kind-of-reasons idea, but make it practical: reviewers need to seperate reasons that bear on whether the work itself is good (right kind for the evaluative attitude) from reasons that just make funding it attractive for unrelated causes (wrong kind for that attitude, even if they’re real considerations for the funding decision). Give them actual sentence-level tests they can apply — maybe a counterfactual biography check and an attitude-action split with two columns. Include three worked cases: one where the artist’s hardship story is compelling but the work is mediocre; one where the project has a clear social benefit that has nothing to do with its aesthetic value; and one where the procedre gives an answer the reviewer will hate — like a technically brilliant piece that promotes a message the board personally dislikes. Flag the exact spots where judgment can’t be eliminated, say what the procedre can’t do (for instance, resolving whether political content ever counts as part of artistic merit), and keep it something a non-philosopher can use in ten minutes. No reading lists or citations, please — just the checklist and the examples.
