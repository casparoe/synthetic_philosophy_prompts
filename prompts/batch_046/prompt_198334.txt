Plan the explanatory arc: the exact order in which the components must be introduced so that the parallel feels inevitable rather than contrived; the one concrete carrying scenario that should bear the weight of the explanation (it must contain both a large set of individually well-supported items and an explicit institutional admission that at least one is flawed—specify what properties the scenario needs and what it must lack so it does not collapse into illustrating only one paradox); the misconceptions the audience will form—I'm anticipating "just lower the probability threshold," "the preface is only about epistemic humility rather than logic," and "this is a statistical problem about large numbers rather than a tension within belief norms"—and the precise point in the sequence where each misconception should be intercepted; one diagnostic question at the end that reveals whether they grasp the shared aggregation structure rather than merely recalling the two puzzles separately; and what should be explicitly left out at this level and acknowledged as such.
