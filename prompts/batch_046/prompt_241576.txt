I am drafting an adversarial-collaboration framework for a philosophy-of-logic seminar on whether Frege's late political diary bears on his philosophical system. The two parties are integrationists—who argue that the diary's exclusionary, pan-Germanist rhetoric is structurally continuous with Frege's logicist separation of objective Gedanke from subjective Vorstellung, and therefore bears on the normative architecture of his philosophy—and separationists—who maintain that the diary records private prejudice with no analog in his published logic, semantics, or philosophy of mathematics.

Please produce the joint statement. Structure it as follows. (1) Agreed claims: list propositions both sides accept about the diary's actual contents (without verbatim quotation) and about Frege's core anti-psychologistic and logicist commitments. (2) Residual disagreements: state each camp's position in wording it would endorse, precisely locating where they diverge on whether the objective/subjective distinction carries exclusionary normative weight and whether rhetorical patterns in the diary mirror distinctions drawn in his logical writings. (3) Cruxes: identify the two decisive sub-questions—for example, whether anti-psychologism implies a hierarchy of rational agents, and whether Frege's appeal to objectivity in logic parallels his appeal to cultural purity in the diary—then for each propose one concrete textual comparison that both sides agree is probative, recording in advance what each camp predicts that comparison will show. (4) Verbal disputes: state whether any disagreement dissolves upon clarification and, if so, which. (5) Settling evidence: name the single hypothetical discovery—such as an unpublished lecture note linking logical objectivity to ethnic exclusion—that both sides agree would be genuinely decisive, and say in which direction it would push.

Write concisely, avoid bibliographies or citation lists, and frame the dispute so that it can be assessed without external sources.
