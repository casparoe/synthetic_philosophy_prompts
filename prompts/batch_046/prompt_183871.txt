ok I'm trying to write out my welfare-state commitments for a discussuon group and want a consistency check before I present. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Every adult should recieve an unconditional baseline — need shouldn't depend on labor-market luck, and means-testing beyond income verification is humiliating.
2. I also support activation programs with sanctions for people who refuse suitable work, but only if structual barriers (childcare, transport, disability) are actually addressed first.
3. I reject the "undeserving poor" framing completely — it's empirically false and morally toxic. Poverty is structurally mediated; individual blame is almost always misplaced.
4. But I do think there's a real distinction between distributive desert (which doesn't exist) and contributory reciprocity (which does). You don't deserve poverty for not working, but solidarity requires some expectation of contribution where capacity genuinely exists.
5. I think "benefit fraud" is wildly overstated by politicians, but deliberate misrepresentation of income or job-search status should still be prosecuted seriously — not because fraudsters are morally bad, but because it undermines trust in universal programs.
6. The state has no business judging anyone's moral worth, but assessing functional capacity and contribution is a totally different thing.

Do these hang together? I'm especially worried that (1) kills (2): if the baseline is unconditional, why do I care about contribution for supplements? And is (3) even compatible with (2) — if poverty is always structurally mediated, is "refusing suitable work" a coherent category or am I just smuggling in blame? Also, does (5) reintroduce the moralized undeserving category I reject in (3), or can I defend it purely on system-integrity grounds? And what about (4) vs (6) — is "contributory reciprocity" just moral worth in functional clothing?

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what's the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. If I have to drop one commitment to clean up the mess, which one goes?
