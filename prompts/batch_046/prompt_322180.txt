I have been working through the modal metaphysics literature and find that debates between counterpart theorists and defenders of transworld identity too often recycle the same monadic examples—whether Humphrey might have won—without addressing how the dispute plays out for relational properties whose modal profile depends on contingent co-instantiation. For a workshop I am organizing, I want a document that forces the issue onto structural roles: cases like being the unique maximal element in a contingent ordering (say, the tallest building in a skyline that could have included different structures), or being the root node in a causal network whose membership is not modally fixed. Could you draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed Lewis-style counterpart theorist and a committed Kripke-style transworld-identity theorist, with the discussion restricted to these relational, co-instantiation-dependent properties?

The statement should include: a numbered section of claims both sides can sign, including about the formal semantics of quantified modal logic and about what needs explaining (e.g., why it seems true that a particular building could have failed to be the tallest without being a different building altogether); a disagreements table in which each side’s position is phrased in wording it would accept for itself; the precise cruxes, especially whether assigning counterpart relations to relational roles is necessarily arbitrary unless one already presupposes transworld identity; and, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, together with each side’s predicted outcome. I also want you to flag any subset of the requirements for an adequate modal theory of relational properties that you think is jointly unsatisfiable—say, if maintaining both non-arbitrary counterpart selection and the denial of transworld identity for concrete particulars turns out to be impossible. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cruxes rather than on a scorecard of who wins.

Please write without bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, since I want conceptual clarity testable without reference access. At the end, include a brief self-critical note: identify exactly what kind of argument or piece of evidence—perhaps a formal result about the uniqueness of counterpart relations in relational structures, or a counterexample involving abstract artifacts—would cause you to revise your characterization of either camp’s position, and point out where your reasoning is most likely to fail or to lean on a contested interpretation of one view.
