Imagine Einstein’s brain was preserved and interviewed in the manner of the dialogue in Hofstadter and Dennett’s The Mind’s I, and that the interviewer pressed him specifically on whether the physical book containing that very dialogue—if instantiated with the appropriate organizational dynamics—could itself be conscious. Drawing on Einstein’s determinism, his field-theoretic physicalism, his Spinozist conception of mind-body unity, and his well-known skepticism toward quantum-mechanical subjectivism, predict his response to the claim that consciousness is substrate-independent. Would he deny book-consciousness on the grounds that gravitational and electromagnetic fields lack the specific organizational closure characteristic of neural tissue, or might his commitment to a unified physical description push him toward a functionalist line? In particular, address whether he would regard the philosophical-zombie scenario as relevant to this case, and whether he would hold that a completed unified field theory could close the explanatory gap between physical process and subjective experience, or that such a gap remains principled regardless of theoretical completion.
