A close colleague recently confided that she has begun working independently in the sex industry, and the conversation has compelled me to move beyond the abstract policy debates I once followed at a distance. She is a citizen with full labor rights, works without a brothel or agency, and lives in a jurisdiction where landlords may still evict tenants for moral turpitude and banks routinely deny accounts to known sex workers. I want an evaluation—not of abstract morality, but of practical institutional protection—of the three dominant regulatory architectures: Sweden-style asymmetric criminalization (the Nordic model), state-licensed legalization (Germany and the Netherlands), and full decriminalization (New Zealand).

Please rate each framework on five dimensions, using a scale from 0 to 1: (1) direct physical safety from clients and third-party coercion; (2) economic autonomy, including the enforceability of contracts and freedom from managerial exploitation; (3) protection from state violence, arrest, or compulsory health and registration measures; (4) resilience against private-sector discrimination in housing, banking, and healthcare access; and (5) scalability and equity for non-citizen or undocumented workers. Assign explicit weights that reflect her specific profile—an independent, citizen worker facing private-sector exclusion rather than state criminalization—compute a weighted overall score for each model, and present the results clearly.

Crucially, I want you to inject a conceptual distinction that standard comparative analyses usually collapse: the gap between formal legal status and what I will call infrastructural decriminalization, meaning the presence or absence of coupled anti-discrimination provisions in housing, finance, and health access that render statutory rights practically exercisable. Rate each model not only on its formal rules but on its typical or predictable infrastructural gap within the jurisdictions named. Then identify the single dimension that carries the greatest weight in separating your highest-rated framework from your lowest, and specify whether that separation is driven primarily by empirical institutional patterns observed in those countries or by the normative weights you assigned. Keep your reasoning fully explicit, do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and avoid relying on highly specific statistical claims that could only be verified by current data retrieval; I need your structured reasoning, not a research plan.
