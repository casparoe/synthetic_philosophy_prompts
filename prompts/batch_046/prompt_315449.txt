I'm preparing interview questions for a podcast episode with an unnamed philosopher of mathematics who defends a strict mental-constructivist interpretation of intuitionistic logic. She holds that mathematical objects exist only as completed mental constructions; that the law of excluded middle fails for infinite domains because those domains are ontologically indeterminate rather than merely unknown; and that the BHK interpretation should be read in terms of finished mental acts rather than potential algorithms. She accepts Markov's principle for decidable properties but denies this makes her view epistemic. I want eight to ten questions that go beyond restating her position.

Please include at least one question on each of the following, with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give:

First, whether non-canonical proof terms—those that establish existence without yielding a specific witness—count as legitimate constructions under her mental-act reading of BHK; follow up by anticipating her appeal to normalization and asking whether normalization guarantees ontological determinacy rather than mere syntactic regularity.

Second, how her ontological reading of LEM failure distinguishes her from epistemic interpretations, especially given her acceptance of Markov's principle for decidable predicates; follow up by anticipating her distinction between epistemic access and ontological structure and pressing whether that distinction can be maintained without smuggling in a hidden classical ontology.

Third, what her view says about classical mathematics—specifically whether real analysis can be reconstructed constructively without being treated as a useful fiction; follow up by anticipating her reference to Bishop-style constructive analysis and asking whether the necessary abandonment of arbitrary choice in reconstruction shows that classical results were never genuinely about the real numbers she accepts.
