I am a retired experimental physicist who has only recently turned to philosophy in earnest, and I find myself returning to the regress problem in epistemology: foundationalism demands beliefs that need no further justification, coherentism makes justification circular among interdependent beliefs, and infinitism accepts an endless chain with no ultimate ground. The parallel with my former laboratory work strikes me forcefully—every measuring standard requires calibration against a prior standard, and there is no final, uncalibrated ruler that escapes the dependency.

Please write a villanelle—nineteen lines, iambic pentameter, ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains—that takes this calibration parallel as its governing image. The first refrain should voice the foundationalist demand for a self-certifying ground that needs no borrowed authority; the second should voice the coherentist claim that instruments, like beliefs, hold one another up only through mutual adjustment. The form’s own structure—the refrains taking turns and finally meeting in the closing couplet—should enact the way these two positions keep passing each other without resolution, while the poem’s refusal ever to step outside its own circular refrain-system should quietly suggest the infinitist alternative: an open-ended chain with no external termination. Render the three positions with enough philosophical precision that a specialist recognises them in the imagery and logic rather than in named labels.

Please begin with the poem itself, with no preface or commentary.
