Design an in-class game based on the dollar auction, but with a twist: every player gets two "exit chips" they can cash in to leave with half their sunk costs, and there's a hidden "escalation mirror" that copies the highest current bid into a shared pool visible only after someone exits. I want participants to feel commitment escalation not just as sunk-cost blindness but as social entrapment — leaving feels like conceding ground to whoever stays. Give me rules, a point-based payoff scheme (no real money), a rough timeline, and a debrief that maps the chip-burning moments onto different theories of rational exit. Also be honest about what predictably go wrong the first time, and note which feature of the classic dollar-auction paradox this version fails to reproduce.
