Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when Tolstoy's late account of art—on which a work qualifies as genuine only if it transmits feeling sincerely and directly so as to foster universal brotherhood—is tested against a novelistic technique that produces its intended emotional effect solely through sustained narrative unreliability, such that the reader's final feeling of compassionate union is inseparable from recognizing that every preceding emotional cue was deliberately mediated by deception. Writing from the perspective of a novelist who intends to employ this technique, specify the precise philosophical tension: does the necessary indirectness of the transmission violate Tolstoy's requirement of unmediated sincerity, or can the reader's synthesized, retrospective feeling be counted as direct? Identify the exact narrative juncture—such as the moment of revelatory reversal—where this conceptual distinction becomes decisive rather than merely formal.
