Here's an argument I cooked up for a discussin and now find suspicious because the conclusion is absurd. (1) Rand defines altruism not as kindness but as the moral duty to sacrifice your own values for others. (2) She also uses the idea of the sanction of the victim—voluntary self-sacrifice still counts as treating your life as expendable. (3) Now take honesty: if I refuse to lie for a friend, I give up an immediate social gain to stick with reality. (4) That foregone gain is a sacrifice of immediate self-interest, so by (1) and (2) it looks like I'm practicing altruism, sacrificing my comfort for someone else's expectation of integrity. (5) More broadly, any principled choice requires passing up some payoff, which is structurally the same as the sacrifice Rand condemns. (6) So her ethics should condemn all principled action, including the virtue of honesty she praises. (7) Therefore her distinction between selfishness and altruism collapses. Where does this slip? I feel like (3) or (4) sneaks in a different meening of sacrifice, but I want to know exactly which step is rotten and what fixing it costs her attack on altruism.
