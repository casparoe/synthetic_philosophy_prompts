I'm a software engineer working on event-sourced distributed systems, and I've encountered a recurring structural pattern that feels conceptually deeper than a routine engineering issue, though I have no formal background in philosophy.

Here are two concrete instances.

First, in our warehouse-inventory service, a robot relocates an item and we log a single event. Downstream read models must preserve everything that hasn't changed—weight, temperature history, ownership chain—yet different services care about different subsets of these survivors. An auditing service needs the ownership chain intact; a climate monitor needs the temperature history. If I enumerate every invariant explicitly, the event schema becomes unwieldy; if I remain silent, a consumer may incorrectly treat unmentioned properties as erased. The boundary between what is updated and what persists seems to depend on the interpretive purpose of the reader rather than on any feature of the event itself.

Second, in our access-control module, revoking a permission leaves past actions in the historical log, but whether those actions still count as authorized depends on whether one is running a compliance audit or a live security check. The underlying records do not change; what shifts is which facts are treated as persistent authorization states versus revoked ones, reconstructed according to the query context.

Both cases share the following feature: an action alters part of a system state, and the remainder must be carried forward implicitly, but the correct remainder is not fixed by the data model or the event. Instead, it is reconstructed differently by different observers, even though the system requires a single canonical history.

This feels analogous to discussions I've heard about in artificial intelligence—specifically, the difficulty of specifying all the things that stay the same when an action occurs—yet my cases involve observer-dependent reconstruction in audit logs rather than physical actions in a world. Does this configuration have an established philosophical or AI-theoretic name? If it does not map cleanly, please say so and propose a precise definition with clear boundary cases that distinguish it from its nearest neighbors.

Please also indicate what would change your mind: for example, if the observer-dependence turned out to be fully eliminable through a more complete data model, or if the core difficulty is really just database normalization with no conceptual analog. Finally, please point out where your reasoning is most likely to fail—especially if you are relying on an analogy between software event logs and physical-world actions that does not hold up under scrutiny.
