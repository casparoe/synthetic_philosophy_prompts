I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind serving on my university hospital's neuroethics review panel. We are evaluating a proposed clinical trial in which 0.1 percent of a consenting patient's cortical synapses are replaced weekly with functionally identical synthetic analogs over twenty months, with continuous subjective reporting required; upon completion, approximately 85 percent of the original neural substrate will have been substituted. The researchers seek approval to treat the post-substitution subject as retaining the original patient's legal identity, informed consent authority, and rights to accumulated clinical data, but panel members are divided: some hold that gradual functional and phenomenological continuity suffices for personal persistence, while others contend that quantitative substrate replacement severs identity regardless of reported experience. Draft the panel's decision memo—approve with conditions, suspend pending an intermediate identity review, or reject—providing actionable reasoning that the researchers can directly address, and giving an honest account of the strongest consideration favoring the position you do not adopt. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
