I spent thirty years running reaction-rate experiments in a chemistry lab, and since I retired I've been trying to read actualy philosophy instead of just tinkering with my garden. Lately I've been obsessed with Borges — there's this essay where he denies that time exists, and I think it's titled A New Refutation of Time, though I might have that slighlty wrong. Anyway, in my little philosophy meet-up we've been arguing about it for months without moving an inch.

One camp reads Borges as doing straight metaphysics. They say he's defending something like an eternalist block universe — past and future are equally real, duration is an illusion — kind of like the four-dimensional spacetime I used to encounter in physics textbooks, except without any equations. The other camp says he's not talking about physics or ontology at all. They think he's describing how memory collapses temporal distance, so when he denies time he's really denying that duration has psychological reality, not physical reality. We all agree on the examples in the text, nobody denies he uses the river example, and yet the debate is totally frozen. Is this a verbal disagreement about what time means? Is there a hidden factual question about whether Borges actually knew relativity theory well enough to be influenced by it? Or are we stuck because the metaphysicians think a literary essay can't be serious unless it aims at literal truth, while the phenomenologists think that framing misses the point entirely?

I want an honest diagnosis of the disagreement — is it about evidence standards, bedrock intuitions about naturalness, incommensurable frameworks, or just that Borges is deliberately playing both sides? And if you diagnose it, tell me whether anything could actually break the deadlock — maybe a sharp distinction between clock-time and lived duration, or a formal point about identity over time — or whether I should just accept that the question is ill-posed and go back to gardening.
