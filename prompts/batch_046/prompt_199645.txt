I now want to apply this framework to two technological possibilities that emerged after Nozick's death: first, the use of gene-editing techniques to produce farm animals that are neurologically incapable of suffering, thereby permitting intensive confinement without welfare costs; and second, the development of cultivated meat that eliminates animal slaughter altogether.

Please predict how Nozick would likely have evaluated each development. Would he have regarded the genetic removal of sentience as a permissible form of instrumental use, consistent with his denial of full rights to animals, or would he have seen it as violating a side-constraint—perhaps an implicit prohibition against altering the nature of a being solely to facilitate exploitation? And how would he have weighed cultivated meat, given that it avoids both suffering and killing but also removes animals from the moral equation entirely?

In your response, please separate what is firmly grounded in Nozick's explicit texts from what requires speculative extrapolation.
