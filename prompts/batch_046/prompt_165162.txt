Construct a thought experiment engineered to separate Molinism, Open Theism, and a simple-foreknowledge account of divine providence. Design a single possible-world scenario in which God considers actualizing a world containing exactly one human free decision—morally neutral in itself but causally decisive for whether a specific, serious harm befalls an innocent third party. Specify the counterfactual of freedom that the Molinist claims God knows via middle knowledge, the actual choice the agent makes, and the outcome God wishes to secure without causally determining the agent's will.

Then demonstrate that the three views deliver distinct verdicts: (1) the Molinist says God can select the world precisely because middle knowledge reveals which choice the agent would freely make in that circumstance; (2) the simple-foreknowledge view says God knows the actual future choice but lacks knowledge of what the agent would do in counterfactual circumstances, so God can only secure the outcome if the actual world happens to align; (3) Open Theism says God does not know the future free choice at all, so God cannot guarantee the outcome in advance and must respond after the fact.

Next, identify the structural feature—counterfactual knowledge of freedom versus actualist knowledge versus ignorance of the future—that drives the divergence. Show what happens if that feature is removed: does the case collapse into a standard Newcomb-style problem, or does it reduce to a deterministic choice? Finally, present the strongest Open-Theist objection that the scenario illicitly assumes God needs exhaustive foreknowledge to achieve providential goals, and say whether that objection succeeds in dissolving the case or merely shifts the burden.
