I've been trying to apply Sartre's idea of the original choise to long-term commitments and I think I've backed myslef into a corner. Here goes:

(1) For Sartre, consciousness is never identical with its facticity; we always transcend our past through the original project.
(2) Bad faith is treating that project as if it were a fixed nature—like saying "I'm just not the kind of person who quits."
(3) But to actually pursue any project over time, I have to treat it as stable enough to guide future action; otherwise every moment I'm just reinventing myself with no continuity.
(4) So treating my project as stable for practical reasoning is treating it as necessary, which is bad faith.
(5) Therefore every sustained commitment involves bad faith.
(6) But Sartre clearly thinks authentic commitments are possible—he talks about pure reflection owning the contingency of the project without abandoning it.
(7) So either (4) is wrong, or Sartre's account of authenticity collapses into contradiction.

Which step should I reject, and what's the cost? I really want to keep (3) because without some practical stability life seems impossible, but I'm also attached to Sartre's claim that we can be authentic about our choices. Where's the slip?
