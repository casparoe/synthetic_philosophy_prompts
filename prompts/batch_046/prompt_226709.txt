I am not a philosopher by training, but I have spent considerable time thinking about how beliefs are justified. The classic problem seems to be that if every belief requires a reason, then either the reasons form an infinite chain, loop back in a circle, or rest on beliefs that need no reason—the three options usually called infinitism, coherentism, and foundationalism. In my own reflection, however, I notice that I frequently stop asking for reasons not because I have reached something self-evident, not because I have circled back, and not because I intend to continue forever, but because I encounter a direct experience or a trusted source where further questioning feels practically idle rather than intellectually illegitimate. This halt does not seem to possess the special "basic" status that foundationalists assign, since I could still supply further reasons if pressed; yet it genuinely ends my inquiry.

Given this pattern, I want a decomposed credence on the following claim: that the standard trilemma is genuinely exhaustive of the rational structures for resolving the regress, and that my kind of "practical halt" is not evidence of a missing fourth option—perhaps a view in which justification terminates not in epistemic privilege but in contextually enforced stopping rules, which we might call pragmatic terminationism. Please provide:

- P(A): the probability that every actual termination point in reasoning is correctly analyzed as a foundational basic belief, despite not feeling self-evident.
- P(B): the probability that coherentist circularity can fully absorb these practical halts without requiring an independent category.
- P(C): the probability that infinitism can explain why reasoners feel legitimately satisfied stopping, even if the chain technically continues.
- P(D): the probability that there is no structurally distinct fourth option deserving independent philosophical status.

Give each a value between 0 and 1 with one line of justification, then combine them into an overall credence that the trilemma is exhaustive. After that, identify which single term does the most work in your final number, and describe what observation about ordinary reasoning—something that could be noticed without formal study—would shift that term the most.
