I am drafting the conceptual framework for a new graduate seminar in philosophy of biology, and in recent discussions with colleagues I have noticed a persistent gap: my students can enumerate the biological, phylogenetic, morphological, and ecological species concepts with ease, yet they consistently fail to distinguish the unresolved questions that are genuinely upstream of scientific and legal practice from those that amount to taxonomic housekeeping. If you were advising a small interdisciplinary research group—say, three philosophers of science and two evolutionary biologists—granted four years of funding with no empirical data-collection mandate but with a strict expectation that the work should be capable of shifting how biologists or conservation lawyers actually operate, which two or three open questions within the species problem would you direct them to pursue, and which prominent debates should they actively deprioritize?

Please defend your ordering with explicit criteria: for each prioritized question, assess its tractability given present conceptual and formal tools; identify the downstream debates in conservation policy, ecological forecasting, or microbiology that depend on its resolution; and explain whether answering it would alter institutional practice or merely refine an internal vocabulary. In particular, I want you to weigh a strategic division that I think is under-discussed: should the group defend a pluralist framework in which species boundaries are legitimately context-relative—permitting morphological criteria for macrofauna and genetic-cluster criteria for microbes—or should it instead pursue a unified individuation criterion robust enough to survive cross-domain application despite extensive hybridization and horizontal gene transfer? Rank these strategic orientations, articulate the trade-offs between conceptual coherence and practical applicability, and specify what kind of result—whether a formal model, a conceptual argument, or a coordinated empirical-philosophical case study—would cause you to revise your ranking within the next few years.
