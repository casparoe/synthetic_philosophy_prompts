In a recent seminar discussing whether generative-model weights should fall under patent law, an interlocutor repeatedly invoked the slogan 'ideas want to be free' to reject any form of proprietary protection for algorithmic outputs. She appeared to be trading on the phrase to do several different jobs at once: describing the physics of information replication, asserting a moral right of universal access, denying that abstract objects can be owned, and predicting that enclosure would stifle innovation. Could you lay out the precise, distinct theses that this slogan could be expressing — separating descriptive, normative, metaphysical, and economic readings — and for each indicate whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, plainly false, or simply a non sequitur with respect to the patentability of model weights? I would like the answer structured as a menu of interpretations rather than a single resolution, written in formal British English, so that I can use it to disentangle the arguments in the seminar without appealing to authority.
