Imprecise probabilities mean you replace a single point value with a range or set of probabilities, so instead of saying "the chance is 30%" you say "its somewhere between 15% and 45%." Precise Bayesians have to commit to one prior and update with Bayes rule, but imprecise people keep a family of priors and update each one. In the climate case, if ten models disagree, you can represent that disagreement as an imprecise credence—its basically second-order probabilities, where you assign weights to each model being right. This lets the policymaker avoid ambiguity aversion because she doesnt have to choose one model; she can stay indecisive inside the range. So yes, it solves the ambiguity problem that Ellsberg talked about. The best decision rule here is to take the midpoint of the interval and treat that as your precise belief, then act on it.
