I’m prepping my Q&A for a metaethics workshop next month where I’m defending my thesis chapter. The core claim is Quinean but pushed into moral values: I say moral properties sit in our total web of belief right alongside electrons or numbers, so the realism versus anti-realism debate is basically a false dichotomy — both camps wrongly assume morality needs some special metaphysical license. To make it concrete I use this “moral center-of-mass” idea: just like physics treats center of mass as real but non-fundamental, moral values are real inside the regimented language of practical reasoning, not because they correspond to spooky non-natural entities. The audience is mostly analytic metaethicists — two realists who love the open question arguement, one expressivist, and a Quine specialist who knows translation indeterminancy cold. They care whether Quine’s criterion of ontological commitement even applies to normative discourse, and they think I’m smuggling realism in through the back door.

Give me the objections in the order they’ll actually come, phrased like they’d phrase them. For each I need: what to concede, what to hold, and where my center-of-mass analogy is weakest. Order them by damage potential. Keep replies tight — I’ve got twenty minutes plus ten for Q&A.
