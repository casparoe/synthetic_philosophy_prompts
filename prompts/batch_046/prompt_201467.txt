I'm putting together a taxonomy handout for a philosophy of physics seminar on Einstein's hole argument, but I want it to actually sort people by what they commit to rather than by which textbook summary they've half-memorisied. Could you build a decision tree with about eight nodes? Start with whether the hole construction reveals genuine physical indeterminacy in GR, or just a redundancy in how we describe the same situation. Next branch on whether spacetime points have primitive identity independant of the metric and matter fields. Then split on whether diffeomorphism invariance is best treated as a gauge symmetry (like in electromagnetism) or as something physically deeper about spacetime structure. After that, include a niche split: do you think the argument survives in quantum gravity or discrete spacetime where point-identity might not exist at all? The leaves should include naive manifold substantivalism, sophisticated substantivalism, relationalism, structural realism about spacetime, and at least one unnamed/unoccupied combination. For any empty branch, briefly say if it looks incoherent or just unexplored. Every node must be phrased as a concrete claim or case — not 'are you a relationalist?' — so someone can answer without knowing the labels. Keep the audience in mind: it's a casual reading group, not a journal article. Flag any pair of named positions that land on the same leaf. At the end, put a one-sentence summary of the tree's logic on its own line.
