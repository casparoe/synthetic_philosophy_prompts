I'm on my university's research ethics bord and we vote thursday on a neuroprosthetic trial. The study implants a cortical array in congenitally blind adults that bypasses the retina and feeds geometric patterns straight into V1. The PIs argue that if subjects immediately distinguish cubes from spheres without prior haptic training, it resolves Molyneux negatively—spatial form isn't learned cross-modally. Half the board thinks this is just sensory substitution with extra wiring, not vision, so the study mislabels its outcome and risks overstating benefit in the consent form. The other half thinks subjective report is enough.

Please draft our decision memo: recommend approve with conditions, reject, or approve outright, with reasoning the PIs could actually respond to. Take the modality-conflation objection seriously—especially the worry that we're treating an amodal geometric prior as if it proves visual experience. Also include a paragraph on informed consent, since subjects are effectively testing a philosophical claim about whether they have 'visual' experience without being told thats the study's aim. Keep it focused; no bibliography needed.
