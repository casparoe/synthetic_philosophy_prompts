I built the following argument for a seminar on collective cognition, and now it reads like a reductio I cannot quite locate. (1) If attributing mental states to a system is genuinely indispensable for explaining its adaptive, goal-directed behavior — not merely a heuristic shortcut — then those mental states are literally present in that system. (2) The ant colony conventionally called Aunt Hillary makes adaptive foraging decisions — selecting between food sources of unequal quality, reallocating castes in response to colony-level stress — that are not predictable from the behavior of any individual ant; explaining its path-selection requires attributing to the colony beliefs about food richness and a desire for colony survival. (3) So Aunt Hillary literally has beliefs and desires. (4) The same explanatory standard applies upward to human organizations: a corporation’s strategic pivot is best explained by attributing to it beliefs about market conditions and an intention to maximize long-term value, not merely by aggregating the mental states of its employees. (5) Therefore corporations literally have minds with those contents. (6) Applying the standard downward inside the brain, specific subcortical and prefrontal networks display adaptive, goal-directed activity that is best explained by attributing executive intentions and evaluative beliefs to those regions themselves, rather than only to the organism as a whole. (7) We are therefore committed either to an implausible proliferation of nested minds within every human brain, or to the claim that explanatory indispensability never justifies literal mental attribution after all. (8) But if we reject (1), then we forfeit the best reason for thinking Aunt Hillary has a mind in the first place, and collective cognition collapses into instrumental fiction. I am inclined to keep (1) and (3). Which step should I reject, and what does that rejection actually cost me? Give me a committed answer — take a definite position on where the argument slips, explain why that step looked plausible, and price the alternatives clearly rather than laying out every option and leaving me to pick.
