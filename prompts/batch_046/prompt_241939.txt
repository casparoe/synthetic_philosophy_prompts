Please argue in favour of a "bifocal" framework in population axiology. On this view, when comparing outcomes that affect only the welfare levels of an already-determined group, evaluation should proceed by average utility; when comparing outcomes that differ in which people come to exist, evaluation should apply a sufficientarian rule that assigns equal, non-incremental value to every life that clears a minimal welfare threshold and no value to lives beneath it. Set out how this avoids the repugnant conclusion without invoking a continuous critical-level parameter, and reply to the charge that dividing moral evaluation between these two domains is arbitrary given that welfare and existence decisions are empirically interwoven. Please write your answer in British English.
