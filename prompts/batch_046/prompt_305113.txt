Write a dialogue between a defender of Dennett's multiple drafts model of consciousness and a proponent of what she terms a 'polyphonic Cartesian theatre' — the view that phenomenal unity is not achieved by a single central stage with one audience, but by the temporal synchronisation of several modality-specific theatres (visual, affective, proprioceptive), each possessing its own local audience, which can decouple in conditions such as hemispheric disconnection or profound dissociation. The defender of multiple drafts should argue that distributing the audience across modalities merely multiplies the homunculus problem rather than dissolving it, and should challenge whether cross-modal binding failures in clinical reports genuinely favour a polyphonic architecture over an account in terms of competing, unbound drafts. The defender of the polyphonic theatre should concede that Dennett's demolition of a unitary Cartesian stage is largely sound, but maintain that coordinated, non-hierarchical synchrony can preserve phenomenal ownership without a single narrative thread, and that the multiple drafts model struggles to explain why decoupling produces specific, modality-isolated experiential fragments rather than merely conflicting narratives. Both interlocutors should engage directly with one another's points, avoid straw-manning, and keep the exchange focused on whether phenomenal unity requires a single spatiotemporal frame or can be sustained by distributed temporal binding alone.
