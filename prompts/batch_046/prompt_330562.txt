Its been bugging me that the bootstrapping debate in epistemology never seems to go anywhere. You know the setup - using perception to verify perception, then claiming knowledge. People split between saying thats vicious circularity and saying its harmless coherence-building. But I think the real split is between linear bootstrapping, where one method checks itself, and network bootstrapping, where a web of methods cross-check each other. The first camp says network cases are just circularity with extra steps, the second says no single node is self-validating so the structure is genuinely different. Is the stall actually about whether circularity is a logical property or a pragmatic one about cognitive load? Or are they answering different questions - one about formal validity, one about how minds actually work? Give me a diagnosis, not a survey: verbal disagreement, hidden empirical question, or genuinely hard conceptual knot? And what would actually break it - a formal model of interdependence, a psychological study, or just a cleaner distinction?
