My sister was recently diagnosed with a severe genetic disorder, and thinking about her suffering has made Leibniz’s claim that this is the best of all possible worlds feel personally urgent rather than abstract. Here is an argument that seems to show the claim is either false or empty, and I cannot locate the weak link. (1) If this world is best, then for any actual evil there is no compossible world that contains the same goods with that evil removed. (2) I can conceive a world W identical to ours except that the mutation causing my sister’s condition never occurred; in W she is healthy, and every other person and relationship remains. (3) So W contains the same goods with less suffering, violating (1) unless W is impossible or harbors hidden compensating evils. (4) I can iterate this reasoning, removing one suffering after another, generating a sequence that should terminate in a suffering-free world containing all the same people. (5) No such world is actual, so either God failed to maximize perfection, or changing one detail changes the complete concept of every interacting substance, meaning W never contains my sister but only a counterpart. (6) But if every local improvement alters all complete concepts, then “best” becomes incomparable across worlds and the thesis is unfalsifiable. Where does this argument fail? I would prefer to keep (2) and (4), but I will surrender either if the cost is made clear. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
