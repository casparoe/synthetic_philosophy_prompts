In a graduate seminar on comparative metaphysics, a colleague repeatedly invoked the line form is emptiness, emptiness is form whenever I pressed her on whether śūnyatā is best understood as a negative property, a universal substrate, or a purely methodological stance. She treated the formula as self-evidently dissolving the question rather than answering it. I want to bring analytical precision to this exchange. Please lay out, as a structured menu rather than a single favored reading, the distinct precise philosophical claims that this slogan could be expressing within Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka framework, especially in relation to the tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi). For each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or genuinely paradoxical; specify which readings would actually license dismissing an ontological question about emptiness itself; and trace how each reading fares when the fourfold negation is applied reflexively—not merely to tables and persons, but to the thesis of emptiness itself (asking whether emptiness is, is not, both, or neither). Additionally, consider whether the most radical interpretation of the slogan collapses into a form of ontological quietism analogous to certain readings of Wittgenstein's Tractatus, and whether such quietism can be reconciled with Nāgārjuna's explicit use of reductio-style reasoning in his root verses. Please state how confident you are in each disambiguation, and explicitly identify the point—particularly concerning the reflexive application of the tetralemma—about which you are least sure.
