The concept I must explain is subsidiarity—not the popular gloss of “decisions should be made locally,” but the specific normative-empirical compound claim that authority ought to reside at the lowest competent level, combined with the institutional problem of how that competence is recognized without circular appeal to the very authority being distributed. I need a teaching plan, not the explanation itself.

Plan the explanatory arc for this audience. First, specify the dependency order: which idea must be in place before subsidiarity can even be distinguished from simple decentralization or libertarian anti-statism, and which distinction must come before I introduce the carrying example? Second, identify the one concrete comparative scenario that should carry the entire session—perhaps the divergence between German Länder autonomy, Swiss cantonal conferral, and the EU’s principle of conferred powers in environmental regulation—and specify exactly what this comparison must include (a case where the higher level demonstrably possesses technical competence yet should still defer) and what it must exclude (a purely cultural or linguistic dispute that would muddy the normative structure). Third, map the predictable misconceptions: I expect them to arrive thinking “local is always more democratic” and to conflate subsidiarity with federalism simpliciter; tell me at which point in the sequence each misconception should be headed off, and what specific phrase or distinction should be used to defuse it without making the audience defensive. Fourth, propose one check-for-understanding question I can ask at the end that would reveal whether they have grasped the competence-plus-legitimacy structure rather than merely memorized a slogan about “lower levels.” Fifth, state explicitly what I should leave out and acknowledge as omitted—specifically, the Catholic social-teaching genealogy, which without historical context sounds like theological intrusion rather than conceptual origin—and say whether I should name the omission openly or simply avoid the territory.

Finally, I want to inject an analogy from my own domain: comparing subsidiarity to graceful degradation in distributed architectures, where local nodes absorb failures without central coordination but remain subordinate to a shared protocol. I need to know whether this analogy should serve as the opening intuition, as a mid-session consolidation tool, or be avoided altogether because it risks importing assumptions about “system efficiency” that distort the normative core of subsidiarity. If it belongs in the plan, specify precisely where and with what qualification.
