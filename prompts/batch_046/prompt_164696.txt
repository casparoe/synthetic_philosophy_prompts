There's this norm in science that if your name's on a paper you're on the hook for the whole thing, even parts you didn't write and maybe barely understand. But in jury trials we say each juror's only responsible for their own vote, not the reasoning the others used. What does the coauthorship rule presuppose about joint action? Does it need a real collective we-intention, or can it be explained just by overlapping individual commitments? And why do we treat these two cases so different—does the legal system implicitly deny that groups can have intentions in the way academia seems to assume they can?
