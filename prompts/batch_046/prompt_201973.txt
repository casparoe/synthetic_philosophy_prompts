Write an essay on whether sustained prosperity and public order can genuinely justify authoritarian rule, with China and Singapore as your central cases. Don't just replay the standard 'Asian values' vs liberal democracy script. Instead, work with what I'd call the feedback-loop problem: when a regime supresses dissent and controls information, the prosperity and stability it points to become partly self-certifying, because citizens (and outsiders) can't reliably measure hidden costs or compare counterfactual trajectories. Does that corruption of the evidentiary base kill the moral force of performance legitimacy, or can we still evaluate outcomes from outside in a way that saves the argument? Weigh both sides hard, but don't end on a fence -- take a definite position and defend it. If you think performance can justify authoritarianism, specify exactly which conditions survive the feedback-loop critique; if you think it can't, say why economic results are always the wrong kind of reason to override procedural rights. I want this committed, not a balanced survey.
