I've been trying to understand whether nature can genuinely steer history, or whether we only blame it for changes that were already inevitable. Compare two frameworks: one that grants germs, climate disruptions, and volcanic eruptions a kind of causal sovereignty, treating events like the Tambora eruption or the spread of plague as historical actors that can redirect societies regardless of their institutions; and another that sees them only as conditional accelerants, forces that reveal or deepen fractures already built into social structures. Which view better avoids environmental determinism without denying that non-human causes genuinely matter? And if similar volcanic winters or pandemics produced radically different outcomes in different societies, does that variation favor the accelerant account, or can the sovereignty view explain it without collapsing into determinism?
