i sit on the state education equity board and we have to vote thursday on whether to fund a pilot "counter-typical nudge" program in high school career counseling. the background brief notes the gender-equality paradox: in countries like sweden and norway with the strongest equality policies, occupational gender segregation is actually higher than in places like italy or the US. half the board thinks this means egalitarian conditions reveal authentic, uncoerced preferences and we should stop pushing students away from gender-typical choices. the other half thinks the paradox just shows that structural bias has gone underground — that when economic coercion is removed, gendered socialization becomes a luxury good for identity signaling, and justice still requires intervention. i need a memo recommending approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions for the pilot, with concrete conditions if thats the call. take the "authentic preference" argument seriously — dont just dismiss it — but also address whether justice can demand reshaping preferences that formed without direct coercion. inject the idea that removing material constraints might amplify rather than reduce gendered choice by turning career into a domain of self-expression. no citations or reading lists please, and dont lean on obscure theorists — write it so the board can actually act on it. include a honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side.
