I'm on an ethics review board for a wildlife research station that works with corvids, cephalapods, and a couple mammal species. Every month we get proposals for cognitive enrichment or welfare interventions and I need a reusable decision procedre I can run in about 15 minutes per proposal to classify what kind of mind the target animal has -- Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, or Gregorian, following Dennett's taxonomy -- and then match the intervention to that cognitive architecture. The procedure should list the tests or questions in order, say what counts as enough evidence for each category, flag the transitional cases (like a raven that sometimes seems to model outcomes before acting), and clearly mark where the decision is a value judgment about welfare versus autonomy rather than a factual call. Then apply it to three proposals: teaching ravens a multi-step tool sequence, giving octopuses puzzle-feeders that only work by trial-and-error, and running intensive language-training drills with African grey parrots. Include at least one case where the procedre gives an answer the board probably won't like, and be honest about what it just can't settle.
