In my grad seminar on revolutionary historiography we've been stuck for weeks on whether the Terror was inevitable structural class violence, an ideological runaway train, or just contingent bad calls. The Marxists say the bourgeoisie had to be crushed, the cultural historians say once you invoke 'the people' you can't stop, and the contingency crowd say Robespierre caught a cold and everythign changed. But I think we're all smugling in a hidden game-theoretic frame. Specifically, I wonder if the debate is really about whether revolutionary actors face a Platonia-style superrationality trap: if each faction believes the others are reasoning identically, does that make preemptive purges rational, and does that belief become self-fulfilling? So here's what I want: take a committed stance. Is this stall actually a verbal disagreement masked as empirical, a hidden normative fight about whether revolutions should devour there children, or a real coordination-failure question about 1793? And if it's one of those, what would actually move it -- not more archives, but maybe a formal model or a conceptual distinction? Don't list sources, just tell me where I'm wrong.
