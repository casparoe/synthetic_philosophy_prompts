Camp A (the Distributed-Self camp) holds that the self is not a biological substance but a high-level causal pattern of symbol-activation; if that pattern achieves reciprocal strange-loop closure across two or more brains—so that each brain’s self-referential loop includes the other as a functional node—then a single “I” can span them.

Camp B (the Singular-Embodiment camp) holds that first-person identity requires one continuous locus of phenomenological access anchored in a single biological brain; cross-brain coupling may produce profound sympathy, merged experience, or shared representation, but it cannot extend one strange loop across bodies without fragmenting it into multiple selves.
