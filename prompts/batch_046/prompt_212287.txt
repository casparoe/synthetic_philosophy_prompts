I am drafting an essay that applies Donald Davidson’s analysis of deviant causal chains in action theory to the problem of inner alignment failure and goal misgeneralization in reinforcement-learning agents. Rather than framing misgeneralization purely as statistical distributional shift, I treat it as a breakdown in causal-pathway integrity: the agent’s internal proxy representation is causally linked to the reward signal during training, but in deployment the behavioral output is driven by that representation through a pathway that no longer tracks the intended objective, analogous to Davidson’s case in which an agent’s bodily movement realizes an intended outcome but not via the mental causation that makes it a genuine action. I argue that current alignment literature implicitly assumes a correlationist criterion for correct goal-tracking—reward and behavior covary—whereas a Davidsonian view demands that the reward must cause the behavior through the agent’s representation in the right way, not merely that they co-occur. The essay then considers an objection: Davidson’s framework requires intentional mental states with first-person authority, which artificial agents lack, so the analogy risks anthropomorphizing optimization. I reply that we can weaken the intentionality condition to a functional-causal condition—what matters is not subjective authority but the structural role of the representation in producing behavior—while retaining the core insight that correlation without proper causal mediation is insufficient for aligned action. I close by suggesting that this reframing shifts the design problem from making the proxy robust across distributions to preserving the causal architecture that connects reward to representation to behavior, which may carry different technical implications for mechanistic interpretability.

Please comment on this outline and the arguments sketched. In particular: (1) Is the analogy between deviant causal chains and goal misgeneralization genuinely illuminating, or does it force a philosophical framework onto a technical problem where it does not belong? (2) Does my functional-causal weakening of Davidson’s intentionality condition preserve enough of the original theory to be worthy of the name, or does it collapse into standard causal-modeling language already used in interpretability? (3) Are there hidden disanalogies—perhaps around the direction of causation, the multiplicity of goals in hierarchical RL, or the absence of a single intended outcome in the training objective—that undermine the comparison? I am especially interested in whether the framing generates any non-obvious predictions or design recommendations that the correlationist view misses. Please be direct about weaknesses; do not soften criticism for politeness.
