I'm trying to stabilize my views on linguistic sexism, satire, and institutional reform, partly in dialogue with Douglas Hofstadter's satirical essay "A Person Paper on Purity in Language." Before I present this in a reading group, I want a consistency audit of the following commitments. I do not want a summary of Hofstadter's argument; I want to know whether my own package holds together.

Here is where I stand:

(1) The essay's satirical force derives from transposing gendered linguistic structures onto racial categories, which makes visible that gendered defaults feel "natural" mainly through habituation and social convention rather than through grammatical necessity.

(2) Nevertheless, I think gender pronouns and generic masculine forms in English are genuinely grammatically unmarked: they participate in agreement chains and anaphora resolution in structurally embedded ways that racial terms never replicate, so the analogy is syntactically imperfect.

(3) Even so, I hold that grammatically neutral forms can carry normative weight. The routine deployment of masculine generics in legal, scientific, and institutional discourse produces an associative default linking authority with maleness, and grammatical neutrality does not cancel that cognitive effect.

(4) I believe satire is well suited to shifting individual intuitions about hidden conventions, but it is largely ineffective at producing institutional change, because institutions respond to codified rules and incentives rather than to altered feelings.

(5) For that reason, I think the appropriate remedy is top-down institutional standardization—revising editorial style guides, legal drafting norms, and educational materials—rather than bottom-up campaigns to police individual speech habits.

(6) I also think that attempts to introduce entirely novel pronoun coinages (outside of singular "they") generally fail not because of ideological resistance, but because pronouns are subject to severe constraints of phonological brevity and morphological integration that invented forms typically violate.

Please audit this set. Specifically: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; rank the genuine tensions by severity; for each, specify what the cheapest consistent revision would be and what it costs; and tell me which single revision to one commitment would resolve the most tension overall. Do not treat all conflicts as equally serious, and do not recommend that I abandon the whole framework—work within the set.
