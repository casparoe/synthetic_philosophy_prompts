Write a deadpan FAQ page for "OntoStack™" — a fictional B2B metaphysics-as-a-service platform that helps enterprise clients figure out whether scattered parts actually compose one object. Voice should be pure corporate tech support: chipper, acronym-heavy, relentlessly literal, like a Zendesk page that accidentally became ontology. The target is restricted-composition views, especially the ones that smuggle in "life," "fuctional unity," or "causal integration" as the magic boundary that turns parts into wholes. The satire should come from pushing those criteria into mundane business scenarios so the arbitrariness shows: a logistics manager asks if her distributed warehouses count as one inventory-object, an engineer asks whether a swarm of delivery drones is a single drone, and HR asks if a decomposing employee is still the same organism for payroll purposes. Inject at least one genuinely niche twist — apply mereological essentialism to corporate mergers (if Company A acquires Subsidiary B, does A survive the part-change?) or let the "problem of the many" corrupt the SKU database so that 47 slightly-overlapping warehouse-objects are all technically correct. Include a sidebar on pricing tiers: Universalist (everything is one object, flat annual fee), Nihilist (no objects exist, therefore no invoice), and Restricted (consultation required, billed per successfully unified part, with a surcharge for vague boundaries). Keep it around 500-600 words. Don't ask for citations, reading lists, or named living philosophers — invent the founders if you need them. Write casually; a typo or two like "recieve" or "fuctional" is totally fine.
