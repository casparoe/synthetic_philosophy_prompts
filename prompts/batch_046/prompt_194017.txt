As a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science working on artificial agents, I am developing an evaluation framework for theories of honesty and deception that apply to large language models and multi-agent systems lacking beliefs, intentions, or explicit theory-of-mind architectures. Rather than comparing existing accounts right away, I want the full specification first: what must any adequate theory be able to deliver?

Write the desiderata. The theory must correctly classify constructed but realistic cases—such as a system that systematically understates epistemic uncertainty in high-stakes domains because its reward function weights user satisfaction over calibration (call this calibrated misdirection), versus a system that strategically omits contextual limitations across multi-turn dialogue without altering any individual factual claim (call this pragmatic omission). It must preserve distinctions between representational error, functional deception, strategic misdirection, and cooperative miscommunication, without collapsing all misleading outputs into malicious intent or all accurate outputs into honesty. It must remain consistent with known architectural constraints: absence of explicit mental-state representations, presence of reinforcement learning from human feedback, scalability across languages and modalities, and the real possibility that an internal evaluation module tracks ambiguity separately from the generation module that produces output.

For each desideratum, state the motivating case or architectural consideration behind it, and flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—especially requirements that demand intentionality or belief-like states versus those that permit purely behaviorist or normative-functional criteria. Then briefly indicate how a behaviorist account, a representationalist account that attributes belief-like or intention-like states, and a normative-functional account that treats deception as a failure of cooperative communication norms fare against your list.

Please present your answer in American English.
