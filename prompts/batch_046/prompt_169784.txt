Frege's puzzle about identity is usually illustrated with Hesperus is Phosphorus. Some philosophers have tried to extend the puzzle to what they call ostensive-fixing identity — cases where two names are introduced by pointing at the same object in different perceptual contexts, but without any descriptive backing. Explain whether Frege's distinction between sense and reference can be applied here, and whether the informativeness of the identity statement requires that the senses differ even when the fixing acts are purely demonstrative.

and heres one students answer (grade it like a TA who actually read Frege):

Frege said that if a equals b is informative then a and b must have different senses but same reference. In the ostensive-fixing case, you point at Venus in the morning and call it Hesperus and in the evening and call it Phosphorus. Even though there's no description, the sense is still different because the mode of presentation is tied to the perceptual context — morning vs evening appearance. So Frege's framework applies directly: the reference is Venus, but the senses are different because the ostensive acts occur in different experiential frames. This is why learning Hesperus is Phosphorus is informative. However, Frege also said that sense determines reference, but here the reference is fixed by the pointing, not by the sense. So there's a tension: if sense doesnt determine reference in ostensive cases, then Frege's principle that sense determines reference is violated, which means the puzzle doesnt really apply.
