I've followed the hole-argument debates in physics and philosophy-of-physics circles long enough to see the same moves recycled: Einstein's original worry about determinism in general relativity, the standard reply that diffeomorphism invariance strips spacetime points of independent physical identity, and then the same three camps repeating themselves—substantivalists enriching point structure, relationalists celebrating the vindication, and a minority suggesting the whole framing confuses gauge redundancy with ontological indeterminacy. The mathematics of quotienting the metric space by diffeomorphisms has been settled for decades, yet the philosophical disagreement hasn't moved. My working hypothesis is that the stalemate isn't formal or empirical but concerns what counts as a legitimate ontology when the theory's observable content lives in equivalence classes: essentially a hidden disagreement about the criteria for scientific realism applied to gauge symmetries, not about spacetime itself. Is that diagnosis correct, or is there a genuinely open formal question—perhaps about whether fiber-bundle formulations restore determinism at the level of physical states, or about whether structuralist interpretations can be made precise without circularity—that is keeping the debate alive? I want an honest structural diagnosis rather than a survey of interpretations: tell me whether the sides are talking past each other on metaphysical standards, whether they are implicitly answering different questions about explanation versus prediction, or whether a specific conceptual distinction—say, between descriptive redundancy and ontological redundancy—could actually resolve it. And if the answer is that no progress should be expected, I want to know exactly why: is the question ill-posed, or is it just that the relevant standards are irreducibly prior commitments that no theorem can adjudicate?
