I'm working out my own view of when hypocrisy undermines standing to blame, and I'd like to see whether my intuitions across cases hang together. Here are my verdicts.

A parent who regularly lies to her child about trivial matters ("the shop is closed") blames the child for lying about homework. I think she lacks standing entirely.

A climate activist whose research requires air travel blames an airline for lobbying to expand carbon-intensive routes. I think she retains full standing; her travel is structurally compelled and negligible against institutional scale.

A tax preparer who underreports her own income blames a wealthy client for offshore tax avoidance. I think she lacks standing, even though the client's harm is larger.

A recovered alcoholic, sober for a decade but still in recovery, blames a friend for driving while intoxicated. I think he retains standing, despite the shared domain.

A politician who publicly supports environmental regulation but privately holds fossil-fuel stock blames ordinary citizens for not recycling. I think she lacks standing, though here the hypocrisy is concealed and the blame is downward, which feels different from the tax case.

A marriage therapist who is emotionally neglectful with her own partner advises a friend to leave a toxic relationship and blames him for staying. I'm genuinely torn: her lived hypocrisy seems to undermine standing to blame for weakness, yet her expertise might preserve a limited form of it.

What principle, if any, unifies these? I'm especially interested in whether standing is undermined by domain-matched hypocrisy, by downward-direction blame, or by a combination, and whether structurally compelled personal failures should be treated differently from voluntary ones. If no single principle saves every verdict, present the two strongest candidates, specify exactly which case each must overturn, and apply both to one I haven't listed: a physician who smokes blames a patient for refusing life-saving surgery on the grounds that the patient "doesn't value health."
