I've been going through some stuff lately—my dad was just diagnosed with dementia and suddenly I'm obsessed with whether I can even trust my own memories of growing up. That led me to Wittgenstein's hinge ideas, and I think I've landed on a view that's maybe not the standard reading. I think hinges aren't just static background conditions but are more like social contracts we maintain through who we recieve corrections from. So the idea that the earth has existed for ages isn't just a hinge I hold individually; it's sustained because I defer to geologists and historians I trust, and if that whole trust network collapsed, the hinge could shift without anyone ever presenting direct counterevidence. I definatly think this makes hinge epistemology more dynamic and less individualistic than people usually say. But I also worry I'm just copeing with family stuff and projecting. So what would actually count against this social-maintenance view? Not just the usual objection that hinges aren't beliefs at all—I know that one. I want to know which observations, arguments, or results in other areas (say, testimony theory or collective belief revision) would really damage it. Which ones would defenders concede are fatal versus ones they'd dismiss as missing the point? And is there a risk this view just collapses hinges into ordinary beliefs about authority? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward if you can.
