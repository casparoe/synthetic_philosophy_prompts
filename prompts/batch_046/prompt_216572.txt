For a graduate-level reading group on decision theory and long-run governance—about ten people, ninety minutes, no computers—design a tabletop simulation that makes the singleton hypothesis feel like an immediate strategic pressure rather than a distant cosmological speculation. Divide participants into four nested “decision nodes” (individual, institutional, planetary, and interstellar), each holding private information about a shared pool of “counterfactual commitment tokens”: paper chits representing binding promises to take actions in worlds the agent will never observe. Each round, nodes may spend tokens to improve local payoff or to merge authority with another node; merging raises joint payoff but eliminates independent choice. The rules should be arranged so that locally rational play—maximizing one’s own token yield without explicit negotiation—gradually collapses the four nodes into a single authority, yet participants should not be told this is the design goal until after round three.
