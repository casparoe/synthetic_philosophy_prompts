My study group got into it over Gongsun Long's white horse argument. Maya said the claim that a white horse is not a horse is an ontological argument about compound natures: horse refers to shape or form, white refers to color, so putting them together makes a different kind of thing, not just a horse that happens to be white. Jonas said she's overreading — it's a language trick about intension vs extention. The phrases have different meanings, so they can't be swapped everywhere, but they still refer to the same animals. He said Maya is smuggling modern metaphysics into a School of Names paradox that was really meant to show language doesn't match reality. Maya answered that if it's just subsets aren't identical to sets then it's trivial, but Hui Shi and the other Mingjia thinkers treated it as a deep challenge to classification, so Jonas's linguistic reading makes it toothless. Jonas replied that the only alternative is saying whiteness literally changes the animal's kind, which is crazy, so the linguistic reading is the only sane one. They asked me to call it.
