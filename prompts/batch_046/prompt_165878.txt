Construct a laboratory-feasible thought experiment built around a modified color-phi display that forces a divergence between an Orwellian revisionist account, a Stalinesque revisionist account, and a direct-realist non-revisionist account of the timing of conscious experience. The scenario must be presented in a single paragraph, must involve no science-fictional devices, no perfect predictors, and no demons, and must generate—not stipulate—the specified verdicts.

Specify the scenario as follows: a subject views a red dot at position A for 40 ms, immediately followed by a chromatic backward mask at position A lasting 120 ms (suppressing reportability while leaving early sensory processing intact). After a 60 ms inter-stimulus interval, a green dot appears at position B for 40 ms, yielding the standard color-phi illusion of a yellowish dot in motion between the locations. At 300 ms after the onset of the green dot, an auditory retro-cue asks the subject to report, on a graded confidence scale, specifically what was experienced at position A during the first 80 ms—before the motion percept could be fully constructed.

Then demonstrate that the design works. For each account—Orwellian, Stalinesque, and direct-realist—state: (i) the specific experiential content attributed to the subject during the initial interval and at the moment of the retro-cue; (ii) the psychological or neural mechanism responsible for that content or for its revision; (iii) the conditions (mask presence, cue latency, ISI duration) that fix the verdict; and (iv) why nearby worlds with altered conditions might change the result. Identify the single feature that does the explanatory work—namely, the interaction between backward masking, which prevents report of an original draft, and the delayed retro-cue, which tests whether such a draft can be retrieved after revision—and explain exactly how the divergence collapses if that feature is removed (for example, if the mask is absent or the cue is delivered immediately). Finally, explain how a defender of the account that comes out worst—likely the direct-realist—would attack the case, such as by denying that retro-cues access original drafts or by arguing that the mask eliminates consciousness rather than merely blocking its report.

End by stating explicitly how confident you are in the analysis you provide, and single out the specific point—whether concerning the interpretation of backward masking, the temporal dynamics of revision, or the reliability of retro-cue retrieval—about which you are least sure.
