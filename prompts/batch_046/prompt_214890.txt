I have recently taken up the post of constitutional review adviser to a national parliamentary committee on European affairs, appointed in the aftermath of a domestic referendum that rejected a treaty revision establishing a joint fiscal capacity, and I now face a recurring practical duty: every fortnight my small team must assess proposals for deeper integration — whether in defence procurement, banking supervision, or climate funding — and advise whether they violate our constitutional identity or create an irremediable democratic deficit. I need a reusable decision procedure, expressed entirely in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables and written in British English, that translates the theoretical distinction between pooled sovereignty, which preserves democratic authorisation chains, and delegated sovereignty, which severs them, together with a remediability filter that asks whether any democratic deficit can be corrected through existing European Parliament mechanisms or requires direct national democratic input. The procedure should set out the questions to ask in strict order, specify what counts as a sufficient factual answer to each, identify the exact point where the procedure terminates with a recommendation to reject on constitutional grounds, and mark precisely where judgment must be handed back to elected representatives because the issue is one of values rather than facts. Please apply it to three proposals: first, a scheme for common EU debt issuance to finance cross-border climate infrastructure; second, a directive harmonising corporate tax bases across member states; and third, a defence cooperation framework that I personally favour on strategic grounds but that I suspect will fail the democratic-authorisation test, so that I can see how the procedure handles an unwelcome result. In addition, state clearly what the procedure is unable to do, particularly when confronted with ambiguous constitutional identity clauses or contested interpretations of democratic legitimacy, and ensure that the entire response remains in continuous prose, avoiding any structured formatting, with British spelling and usage throughout.
