I am preparing a conceptual framework for a municipal heritage board that must adjudicate conflicts between institutional archives and sustained community testimony. The case in question concerns a nineteenth-century territorial agreement: the official record presents it as a voluntary exchange with documented signatures, while the descendants' collective memory—preserved through intergenerational oral narrative, ritual commemoration, and localized toponymy—holds that the agreement was extracted under coercive conditions and should therefore be regarded as morally void. Rather than resolving the empirical dispute, I wish to examine a normative stance I am calling mnemonic precedence: the principle that, when a community's living collective memory conflicts with the historical record, the memory ought to carry authoritative weight in domains of identity formation, moral reckoning, and institutional repair, irrespective of the documentary contradiction. Please describe the most important implications of adopting mnemonic precedence as a governing framework for public institutions.

In particular, I am interested in three dimensions. First, the epistemic implications: how should archives, historians, and educators treat documentary evidence if community testimony is granted normative priority—does this require a revision of evidentiary standards, or does it create separate spheres of authority? Second, the ethical and political implications: what obligations of recognition or reparation follow for institutions that have long relied on the archival account, and how should public commemoration be redesigned when the authoritative narrative shifts? Third, the institutional and conflict-resolution implications: how can a framework of mnemonic precedence accommodate situations in which different communities maintain incompatible mnemonic traditions about the same event, and what safeguards prevent the principle from being instrumentalized by dominant groups to suppress dissenting archives? Please treat this as a conceptual exercise rather than a request for historical verification; I do not need citations, bibliographies, or references to specific scholarly literatures, only a rigorous analysis of the principle's internal logic and practical consequences.
