ok so here's where i've landed on dewey-style democracy and inquiry and i'm not sure it hangs together: (1) democracy is basically an experiemental method where policies are hypotheses tested by lived social outcomes, not just majority voting. (2) inquiry is cooperative and fallible, so no authority — scientists, majorities, anyone — gets final say; warranted assertibility is always provisional. (3) still, in practice i think citizens should largely defer to climate experts and treat policy as technical execution of their findings. (4) but if a democratic community consistently rejects those findings after open deliberation, that rejection should count as disconfirming experiential data that forces experts to revise their models, not just dismiss the public as ignorant. (5) i reject the idea that democracy needs shared metaphysical foundations or a single conception of the good life. (6) yet i think democratic inquiry requires everyone to share a basic commitment to empirical methods and to belief-revison in light of consequences, which sounds like it demands a shared foundation. is this package consistent? which pairs actually conflict, which tensions dissolve on inspection, and what's the cheapest way to fix whatever's broken? lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then reasoning, no preamble, and don't restate the question.
