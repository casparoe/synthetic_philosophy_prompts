I write for a science-and-ideas desk and am commissioning an essay on the philosophical dispute over biological function. Focus on the clash between etiological accounts, which tie function to selection history, and causal-role accounts, which tie it to present contribution to system capacity. To avoid a well-worn survey, ground your argument in a specific, under-explored case: structures in synthetic or radically engineered organisms—such as minimal cells with reconstructed metabolic pathways or gene circuits lacking any evolutionary lineage—where historical and present-role ascriptions sharply diverge. Weigh both sides seriously, but reach and justify a definite verdict on which framework, or what revised hybrid, ought to govern functional ascription in these contexts. Please write in British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
