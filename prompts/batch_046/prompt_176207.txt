I'm running a seminar on consciousness and ethics, and we've been stuck for weeks on what panpsychism actually commits us to morally. One camp — let's call them the aggregationists — says if every fermion carries a flicker of proto-experience, and some of those flickers have negative valence, then the universe is basically a horror show of suffering, and we have a cosmic-scale ethical problem. The other camp — the integrationists — say that's nonsense, suffering isn't just negative valence multiplied; it needs a unified subject with behavioural integration, a boundary, a narrative self, and a quark has none of that, so there's literally nothing it is like to be in pain for a quark.

We've read a bit of Strawson and Chalmers, but the argument just circles. The agregationists keep saying 'if it's bad for me, it's bad full stop, scale doesn't matter'; the integrationists keep saying 'you're applying the concept of suffering outside its conditions of applicability'. Nobody moves. I think the stall might come from them answering different questions — one about whether value aggregates linearly across subjects, one about whether a subject exists at all — but I'm not sure if that's right or if I'm just trying to be clever.

There's also this idea I've been playing with, maybe it's rubbish: the 'symmetry-breaking threshold' where distributed proto-valence crystallises into something harm-able only when decoherance produces an integrated information boundary. If that distinction holds, then the debate isn't about ethics or metaphysics but about where the boundary falls. But maybe I'm just inventing a fake problem to avoid admitting the question is ill-posed.

So diagnose it for me: is this verbal, substantive, or two questions wearing one coat? And if the symmetry-breaking idea is actually useful, what kind of progress would settle it — a formal result, a phenomenological argument, or should we accept no progress is possible? Answer in British English, please — and don't bother with citations, I just want your read.
