Hey, I've been trying to formalize an evolutionary debunking argument for a seminar and it keeps swallowing it's own tail. Here's the chain I built:

(1) If belief-type B is selected purely for inclusive-fitness benefits and selection is blind to B's truth, then B is unjustified unless we have an independent, non-circular check.
(2) Our belief that causing gratuitous harm is categorically wrong — the harm norm — fits this exactly: kin selection and reciprocal altruism explain it without assuming any mind-independent moral fact.
(3) So the harm norm is debunked.
(4) But (1) itself is a belief about causal explanation and epistemic reliability that was shaped by the same evolutionary pressures — brains that seek tidy causal narratives and signal intellectual rigor do better socially.
(5) There's no independent check for (1) that doesn't already depend on the reasoning faculties we're trying to validate.
(6) So by (1), (1) debunks itself.
(7) Once (1) collapses, (3) collapses with it.
(8) Therefore evolutionary debunking either takes down morality and its own reasoning together, or it applies to neither.

I think there's a scope shift or maybe I'm conflating "selection explains origin" with "selection undermines reliability" but I can't name the slip. Which step do you reject, and what does giving it up cost? I'd like to keep (2) — the evolutionary story about harm norms feels solid — and I'd also like to keep that debunking works for at least some belief-types.
