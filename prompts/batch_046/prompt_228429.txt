Please argue that the institutional purpose of marriage is best understood neither as reproductive complementarity nor as the formalization of romantic attachment, but as domestic co-authorship: the mutual authorization of life plans that generates a shared practical identity through the irreversible interweaving of economic, custodial, and bodily vulnerability. From this premise, defend the claim that extending marriage to same-sex couples strengthens rather than dilutes the institution, because the symmetrical institutional capacities of both partners allow the authorization structure to be realized more fully than within asymmetrical gender-role frameworks. In developing your argument, address the objection that this reconception collapses marriage into deep friendship or contractual partnership, and explain why the state retains a distinctive interest in recognizing domestic co-authorship specifically when it involves this kind of vulnerability-interweaving rather than mere affective or economic coordination. Your reasoning should proceed by conceptual analysis of authorization, practical identity, and institutional recognition, without appealing to empirical sociological data about marriage outcomes or to the authority of specific legal judgments.
