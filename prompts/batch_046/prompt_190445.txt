I've been working on a side project about moral revolutions — you know, the Appiah-style story about dueling and foot-binding ending because honor codes flipped, versus the more materialist line that slavery collpased once it stopped being profitable. But I've come to hold a somewhat weirder hybrid view, and I want to stress-test it. Basically: I think practices like dueling or slavery end not when people are persuaded they're wrong, nor purely when interests shift, but when the 'performative reciprocity' that sustained them breaks. By that I mean the implicit deal where refusing to duel or owning slaves was punished socially, but participating earned you future reciprocity within a closed network. Once the state monopolized violence (for dueling) or global markets made slave labor redundant (for slavery), that reciprocity mechanism collapsed — not because anyone argued, but because the social accounting that made the practice rational for individuals just stopped balancing. The moral arguments, I think, came in afterwards to save face and reframe the shift as principled.

What would actually count against this? I'm not asking for a bibliography — I know I can't check sources offline — but I want a map of the exposure. Which historical details about dueling's decline or abolition's timeline would be fatal versus just awkward? Would a defender of this view concede that, say, explicit moral campaigns (like the British abolitionist literature) actually changed minds before economic incentives shifted? Or is that something we'd dismiss as post-hoc rationalization? And is there a risk my view has been qualified into unfalsifability — if any moral argument can be written off as face-saving, then haven't I insulated myself? I want an honest ranking of defeaters, from 'this kills the reciprocity story entirely' to 'this is annoying but I can absorb it by tweaking the network conditions.' Also, which rival accounts (pure honor, pure interest, pure reason) does my hybrid actually risk collapsing into if I try to defend against these hits?
