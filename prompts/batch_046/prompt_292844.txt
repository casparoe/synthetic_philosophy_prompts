I am a doctoral researcher in moral philosophy, and next week I am leading a single fifty-minute workshop for second-year philosophy undergraduates who have completed an introductory ethics course but have no training in empirical psychology. I want a teaching plan rather than an explanation: the precise order in which I should introduce the components of the argument that cross-cultural variation in folk morality—specifically the documented divergence between WEIRD populations and small-scale societies in the weighting of harm, fairness, loyalty, authority, and purity—does not by itself adjudicate between moral objectivism and anti-objectivism, but rather exposes a prior, often hidden commitment about what kind of thing moral disagreement is (a conflict of beliefs, a conflict of sensibilities, or a conflict of social functions). I need the dependency structure made explicit: which conceptual distinction must be in place before the empirical data can be introduced without being misread, and why. For the carrying example, I had considered contrasting Western trolley-problem responses with the moral evaluations observed in pastoralist communities where direct physical interference is treated as categorically different from indirect harm; however, I worry this risks reducing those communities to an exotic counter-example. Please advise whether this case can carry the conceptual load if framed correctly—specifying exactly what it must possess and what it must avoid—or propose an alternative single case that would serve better. I also need a misconception map: I expect the students will slide immediately from "cultures disagree about morality" to "therefore moral relativism is true," and separately that they will treat psychological findings as if they were normative premises. At which point in the sequence should each be intercepted, and with what move? Include a final check question that tests whether they understand the meta-ethical dependency rather than merely recalling empirical patterns, and state explicitly what should be left out at this level—for instance, evolutionary debunking arguments, the methodological controversies in cross-cultural survey design, or the specific literature on conceptual relativism. Please present the plan in British English.
