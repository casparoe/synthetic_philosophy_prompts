I want to tackle conceivability as a guide to possibility, but not the usual replay of Chalmers versus Hume. My half-baked idea: everyone treats can-I-imagine-it as one uniform mental act, but I think we should split it into passive conceivability - you sit back and the scenario comes to mind without resistance - and adversarial conceivability, where you deliberately try to destabilize your own conceptual scheme and check whether the scenario still coheres. The open question is whether adversarial failure carries different modal weight than passive failure. If I can build paired cases where one succeeds and the other fails, does that tell us something real about possibility, or is it just psychological noise? The standard literature leaves this open because it either collapses both into a single prima facie notion or treats any failure of imagination as mere ignorance.
