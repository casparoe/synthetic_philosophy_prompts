A popular philosophy blog summarized eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires like this: Paul and Patricia Churchland hold that beliefs and desires are theoretical posits of folk psychology, comparable to phlogiston in pre-modern chemistry. Because contemporary neuroscience already explains human behavior using only neural mechanisms—without ever invoking beliefs or desires—those mental states are obsolete fictions. They also claim that folk psychology has made no genuine theoretical progress in over two millennia, since we still use the same belief-desire framework the ancient Greeks used. Therefore, eliminativists conclude that no one literally has beliefs or desires, and that once neuroscience matures we should abandon all mentalistic vocabulary and speak only of brain states.

How fair is this summary? Go through it carefully: which claims would defenders of eliminativism accept, which are overstated or subtly wrong, and what important distinctions does it blur—particularly about whether the view targets all mental talk or only propositional attitudes, and about whether neuroscience has already replaced folk psychology or only promises to do so? Finally, does the conclusion that we should drop all mentalistic language actually follow from the premises given, or does it depend on the misrepresentations?
