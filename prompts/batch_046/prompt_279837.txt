Exam question: In Zhuangzi's treatment of death — especially the tub-drumming episode after his wife's death and the butterfly dream — he seems to treat personal extinction as no big deal. One line of interpreters (the 'process-qi' crowd) says he isn't really denying selfhood, just relocating it: the self is a temporary pattern in qi, like a whirlpool in a river, so death is transformation rather than annihilation. Another line says that's a misreading and that he actually pushes a stronger skeptical claim: we can't even know whether death is like waking up or falling asleep, so grief is rooted in false certainty. Which reading is closer to the text, and does the butterfly dream support the process-qi view or undermine it?

Student answer:
Zhuangzi clearly thinks death is transformation, not annihilation. The tub-drumming story shows he accepted his wife's death because he saw her as part of the cycle of qi — like ice turning to water, her form just changed. This is the process-qi view. The butterfly dream supports this too: just as Chuang Tzu doesn't know if he's the butterfly or the man, we don't know if we're alive or dead, so the boundary between them is fake. Therefore there's no real self to lose, which explains why he could drum and sing — he wasn't suppressing emotion like the Stoics do (Stoics think you should kill passion, but Zhuangzi just sees there's no one there to feel it). So the skeptical reading is wrong because Zhuangzi gives us a positive metaphysics of qi, not just doubt.
