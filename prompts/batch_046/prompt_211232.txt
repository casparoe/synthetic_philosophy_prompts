I keep seeing people invoke Godel's second theorem to argue that AI can never fully verify its own reasoning—like, they say an LLM is basically a formal system, so it can't prove its own consistency, meaning there's always true stuff about its reasoning it just can't recognize. The analogy they lean on is a student grading their own exam with the same answer key they used to write it: eventually you need an outside grader. But I'm not convinced the mapping holds. Can you lay out what corresponds to what between Godel's arithmetic system and an LLM's reasoning architecure? Where does the analogy break—does an LLM even have a fixed axiomatization, or is the "system" constantly shifting with weights and context? Separate the disanalogies that actually matter for the self-verifcation conclusion from the ones that are just cosmetic. And if the serious disanalogies are on the table, does the conclusion—that AI must always have unrecognizable true beliefs about itself—survive, or does it collapse into something way weaker? What's a better analogy for the limits of self-checking that doesn't smuggle in fixed-rule assumptions?
