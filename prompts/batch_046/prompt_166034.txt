I have recently been appointed to a working group tasked with drafting the normative framework for a multi-stakeholder reasoning system — essentially a shared deliberative AI intended for policy support across jurisdictions that endorse incompatible comprehensive doctrines, ranging from strong anthropocentric liberalism to deep-ecological holism. The group is divided between proponents of liberal neutrality, who hold that the system must avoid embedding any thick conception of the good and should instead provide only procedural scaffolding for democratic deliberation, and proponents of explicit value aggregation, who argue that any functional alignment mechanism necessarily presupposes a substantive metric for weighing competing claims. Rather than asking for a survey of the literature, I want to know how I should direct my limited research and advocacy efforts over the next eighteen months: among the genuinely open conceptual questions in this space — for instance, whether neutral procedural architectures secretly encode aggregative commitments; whether incommensurable axiological frameworks can be mapped onto a shared decision-theoretic structure without violence to their internal logic; whether the authority to align should be distributed across institutional nodes rather than resolved philosophically; and whether empirical study of value revision under human-AI interaction should take precedence over formal analysis — which two or three should I treat as upstream of the others?

I am asking for a defended agenda, not a reading list: rank these lines of work according to their tractability for philosophical rather than empirical methods, the number of downstream design choices that depend on resolving each, and what specific form of evidence or argument over the next year would cause you to reorder them. In particular, I want to understand which crux, if settled the wrong way, would render the other efforts moot regardless of their internal success, and where a small research investment now would most change the practical options available to the consortium. Please justify your ordering with explicit criteria — importance, tractability, neglectedness, and dependence relations among the questions — rather than treating all four as equally urgent.
