Read The Matrix (the 1999 one, not the sequels) thru the lens of virtual-world ontology: are virtual objects and virtual harms genuinely real? I want you to work with a framing I'm calling "ontological parity of suffering" — suffering counts the same regardless of whether its cause is virtual or physical, but only if the sufferer can't tell the difference. The movie clearly treats being stuck in the simulation as a harm, but is that harm the deception itself, the missed physical-world opportunities, or something deeper about the ontology of simulated pain? Look at the steak scene, the construct program, and Cypher's betrayal: where does the film support parity, and where does it smuggle in the idea that physical reality just has intrinsic value no simulation can match? Don't reduce the movie to an illustration; say where it actually complicates the philosophy, and what the film adds to the debate that pure argument alone misses.
