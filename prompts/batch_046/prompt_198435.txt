As I draft the central philosophical dialogue for my novel—a scene in which a theologian steeped in apophatic tradition debates a philosopher of religion who argues that divine hiddenness undermines theism—I require a precise specification of the desiderata that any adequate account must satisfy. I do not seek a bibliography or a catalog of named positions; I need the logical architecture of adequacy itself: the phenomena that must be explained, the distinctions that must be preserved without collapse, the findings from cognitive science of religion and comparative theology that must be respected, and the particular cases that serve as stress tests. To keep the framework from being generic, I ask that it address three modalities of hiddenness my characters treat as irreducible—epistemic hiddenness (the absence or inaccessibility of evidence), ontological hiddenness (the divine as not belonging to the category of beings that could be manifest), and relational hiddenness (a presence withheld within a structure of covenantal rupture)—as well as the niche problem of distributed hiddenness, in which the same putative reality is manifest within one communal-cognitive framework and hidden within another, without allowing the theory to dissolve into cultural relativism. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or conceptual pressure, classify it as non-negotiable or as a preference specific to one camp, and identify which single requirement, if enforced, eliminates the greatest number of popular responses. If any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, flag the tension explicitly. Where possible, locate actual positions—evidential responses in the style of Schellenberg, skeptical-theist defenses, apophatic theologies, process-theological accounts—within the space defined by the criteria, noting any combinations that remain unoccupied and diagnosing whether the vacancy results from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. A table is welcome, but the emphasis should rest on the criteria and their interactions rather than on a scorecard.
