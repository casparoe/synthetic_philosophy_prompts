I am serving as a teaching assistant for an undergraduate philosophy-of-mind course, and I have been asked to help grade the midterm responses on consciousness and sleep. One exam question asked students to reconstruct Norman Malcolm's argument that dreams are not experiences—particularly his claim that applying the concept of experience to sleep involves a conceptual mistake about verification and retrospective reporting—before evaluating Daniel Dennett's reply. The student was expected to explain Dennett's cassette model and to assess whether it rescues the received view that dreams are concurrent conscious experiences.

The answer I am grading begins well: it correctly identifies Malcolm's core premise that first-person reports upon waking do not establish prior experiential content during an unobservable state, and it distinguishes this conceptual point from a crude behaviorist denial of all mental life. The student then introduces Dennett's cassette theory, describing it as a neural mechanism that loads narrative content into waking memory at the moment of arousal. At this point, however, the answer slips: it treats the cassette model as a defense of the received view, suggesting that Dennett uses it to show how REM-related experiences occur during sleep but become accessible only afterward. In fact, the cassette model is intended as a rival to the received view, since it denies that the narrative is experienced during sleep at all. The student also appeals to REM physiology as if Dennett takes such correlations to empirically prove concurrent consciousness, when Dennett's actual position is more agnostic—the REM data are meant to be neutral between the received view and the cassette alternative. There is an additional subtle confusion in the student's framing of Malcolm: they write as though Malcolm denies the possibility of false dream reports altogether, whereas his concern is specifically about the conceptual conditions for verifying prior experiential content, not about the reliability of memory.

Please grade this answer with a specific letter grade and provide a point-by-point assessment. Identify the accurate reconstruction of Malcolm, flag the mischaracterization of Dennett's use of the cassette theory, correct the misreading of Dennett's stance on REM evidence, and note the smaller conflation regarding false reports versus conceptual verification. Do not offer your own theoretical opinion on whether dreams are experiences; keep the evaluation focused on fidelity to the texts, logical coherence, and conceptual precision.
