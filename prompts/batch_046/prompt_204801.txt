As a doctoral researcher in moral philosophy and welfare axiology, I am preparing materials for an adversarial collaboration between two camps that have argued past each other in recent seminars on suffering and value. Camp A defends lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism: they hold that pleasure and pain are commensurable along a single cardinal scale of phenomenal intensity, and that suffering acquires lexical priority once it exceeds a context-sensitive threshold. Camp B comprises pluralist critics who deny any shared phenomenal currency between pleasure and pain; they treat welfare as vector-valued, with suffering and pleasure occupying incommensurable dimensions, and they regard lexical-threshold claims as artifacts of an illegitimate unidimensional compression.

Please produce the joint statement document these camps should have co-authored. Structure it precisely as follows:

1. Numbered agreed-claims: formal and empirical propositions both sides can sign, including shared commitments about the reality of conscious experience, the fallibility of introspective reports, and methodological constraints on welfare comparison. Keep these substantive, not vacuous.

2. Disagreements table: state the residual disputes in wording that each camp accepts for its own position and, where possible, for the opponent's position, so that the formulation is not slanted. Explicitly flag any disagreement that appears to be merely verbal and propose a neutral reconstrual.

3. Cruxes: identify the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. I anticipate two primary cruxes: (i) whether the denial of commensurability is a conceptual claim about phenomenal kinds or only a practical claim about our current measurement capacities; and (ii) whether a lexical threshold can be sustained on purely ordinal welfare rankings or necessarily presupposes a continuous cardinal scale with discontinuities.

4. Tests and predictions: for each crux, specify one concrete hypothetical case—not dependent on citation or historical authority—that both sides agree bears on it. Include, for example, a scenario involving a calibrated "phenomenal exchange-rate" thought experiment or a "bivariate welfare matrix" comparing chronic low-intensity dysphoria against brief high-intensity aesthetic pleasure. Record in advance each camp's prediction about how that case should be evaluated.

Please construct the arguments and cases from first principles; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts. The framing should be in the weeds: inject at least one novel conceptual device (e.g., the bivariate welfare matrix or a cross-modal intensity calibration protocol) that is not a standard named theory, but that serves to expose the boundary between the camps without requiring external literature.

Finally, append a brief reflexive note stating: (a) for each reconstructed camp, what specific argument or empirical finding (such as a demonstrated failure of cross-modal calibration or a proof linking ordinal rankings to continuous scales) would cause you to revise your characterization; and (b) the precise point in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to failure—whether in attributing a commitment to continuous scales, in interpreting the pluralist's vector-valued model, or elsewhere.
