Write a sonnet in strict Shakespearean form (ABAB CDCD EFEF GG) on the Humean versus anti-Humean debate about laws of nature. In the octave, present the regularity view through concrete imagery: laws as observed patterns, constant conjunction without hidden compulsion, and the difficulty of separating true laws from accidental generalizations. At the volta, precisely line 9, shift to the anti-Humean charge: that a law must govern its instances, carrying modal force that explains why the pattern holds rather than merely recording that it does. In the sestet, do not adjudicate; instead, use structural repetition—parallel clauses in lines 11 and 13—to show that both theories must speak of "all cases" and "what must be," so that the rhyme pairing of "govern" in line 10 with "given" in line 12 suggests the anti-Humean's necessity is borrowed from the Humean's data. Close with a couplet asking whether the difference between law and accident is a feature of the world or of the language we use to systematize it. The poem should be philosophically precise: someone familiar with the debate should recognize the regularity theory, the governing conception, and the specific asymmetry that Humeans can explain lawhood but not modal force, while anti-Humeans can explain modal force but face an identification problem.
