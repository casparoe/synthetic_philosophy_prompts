Having spent my career in evolutionary ecology and only recently begun studying philosophy, I have come to think that Isaiah Berlin's well-known distinction between negative and positive liberty leaves out something important that my scientific background makes visible. Please argue that republican liberty—understood as freedom from arbitrary domination—should not be classified as a third independent category of liberty, but rather explained as a social mechanism operating on the same logic as the handicap principle in biological signaling. More specifically, defend the view that institutional checks on power are sustained not chiefly because they expand individual choice-sets or foster self-mastery, but because they impose visible, unrecoverable costs on potential dominators, thereby making commitments to equality credible and legible within a group. In your argument, address why the procedural waste built into redundant safeguards is not an inefficiency to be eliminated, but precisely the feature that renders the signal honest—analogous to the way a peacock's tail is not a failure of energy budgeting but its evolutionary purpose.
