I have recently been trying to understand Max Weber’s claim that the Protestant work ethic played a causal role in the rise of modern capitalism, yet I keep returning to the same difficulty: can culture genuinely cause anything, or do we only invoke it when we have already lost track of the material forces at work? In conversation with a friend who studies history, she pointed out that Weber himself seemed uneasy about explaining historical change through values, since values appear to be reasons people hold rather than forces that push them. This distinction has led me to sketch a rough separation between what I call ‘push-culture’—where shared norms actively drive behaviour in something like a mechanistic fashion—and ‘pull-culture’, where material conditions first shift and then draw particular values into prominence as post-hoc justifications. If Weber’s thesis is correct, it seems to require push-culture; but I wonder whether the evidence might instead support pull-culture, with Protestant ethics becoming attractive only after economic structures had already begun to change.
