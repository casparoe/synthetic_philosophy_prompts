As a doctoral candidate in EU constitutional law assisting a national parliamentary scrutiny unit, I am designing a reusable decision procedure to operationalize the theoretical conflict between member-state sovereignty, the democratic deficit, and the commitment to ever-closer union—specifically for assessing proposed EU measures that rely on enhanced cooperation or differentiated integration. The unit receives proposals weekly, must deliver preliminary assessments within forty-eight hours, and needs a flowchart-in-words that non-specialist legal staff can apply consistently, with sign-off required from a senior constitutional counsel only when the procedure explicitly defers. Please construct the procedure by translating the relevant distinctions—conferral versus implied powers, pooling versus delegation, input versus output legitimacy, and direct versus indirect democratic authorization—into ordered verification steps: what institutional and procedural facts must be confirmed in sequence, what counts as a sufficient authorization chain at each node, where the procedure issues a clear rejection, conditional approval, or approval, and where it must halt and flag a value judgment that theory cannot settle. Include explicit misfire warnings—scenarios in which the checklist would approve a measure that genuinely undermines democratic sovereignty or reject one that does not—and state which theoretical distinctions are retained in the simplified form and which are dropped for usability. Then apply the procedure to three hypothetical proposals: (1) a directive adopted via enhanced cooperation by twelve member states harmonizing corporate-tax bases, leaving the others outside; (2) a regulation granting the Commission direct enforcement authority over a carbon-border adjustment mechanism without national implementing legislation; (3) an emergency framework decision allowing temporary suspension of national social-protection standards during macroeconomic shocks. For each case, show the step-by-step path, identify the point—if any—where the procedure yields a verdict that conflicts with a plausible sovereignty-based objection, and specify what further factual or normative determination would be needed to resolve the ambiguity. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure cannot do, particularly regarding judgments about the legitimacy of deeper integration itself.
