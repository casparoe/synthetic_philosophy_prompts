I can tell that the phrase is doing several different kinds of work at once, but I cannot disentangle them precisely enough to see where the objection actually bites.

Could you lay out the distinct, precise theses that that one-liner might be expressing — ranging from the purely logical claim that the terms contradict one another, through empirical claims about the inevitability of coercion in any nudge, to normative claims about autonomy that might be violated even when choice is technically preserved? For each reading, please indicate whether it is trivially true, substantively defensible, or false; and say which of those readings is actually doing the argumentative labour when the slogan is used to dismiss a specific policy such as automatic enrolment with an opt-out clause. I am especially interested in whether the objection turns on a negative-liberty conception of freedom or on a thicker, perhaps relational or deliberative, conception of autonomy, and whether either conception genuinely supports the dismissal in that context.
