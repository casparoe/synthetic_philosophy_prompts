Write a joke that applies Simpson's paradox to a debate about the metaphysics of composition. In the setup, a philosopher argues that if a treatment is harmful within every demographic subgroup, it must be harmful for the population as a whole; an interlocutor counters with a hypothetical study in which the treatment is beneficial in every age-stratified subgroup yet harmful in aggregate, because older, higher-risk patients are disproportionately untreated. The punchline should turn specifically on the confounding mechanism—the interaction between subgroup base rates and unequal group sizes that reverses the association—rather than on generic statistical bewilderment. The joke should make sense to someone familiar with both Simpson's paradox and basic part-whole ontology, and it must not reference any real-world study, citation, or named scholar.
