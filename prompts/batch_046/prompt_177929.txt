For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it: the distinctions it must preserve (such as between moral desert and mere dangerousness, between punishment and therapeutic intervention, or between accountability for an act and accountability for a character), the phenomena it must explain (including punishment across radical personal transformation, collective harm without clear individual intent, and diminished-but-not-absent agency), and the institutional or psychological results with which it must remain consistent. Identify which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable and which reflect the partisan commitments of particular camps—for instance, whether determinism must be false, whether libertarian free will is essential to desert, or whether a compatibilist reconstruction can survive. Then, briefly, assess how Dennett's evolutionary-functional, compatibilist account and Caruso's hard-incompatibilist, abolitionist stance each fare against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria rather than on the scorecard. Most importantly, flag whether any subset of these requirements appears jointly unsatisfiable, and identify which single desideratum eliminates the largest number of popular theories. Keep the framing concrete enough that I can translate it into narrative conflict, but do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
