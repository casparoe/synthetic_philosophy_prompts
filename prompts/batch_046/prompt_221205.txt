hey, i just started working with a partner org in southeast asia after spending my whole career in the US, and i keep running into this thing i dont have words for. back home corruption is basically a transaction: you pay, you get the permit. here, people bring really expensive gifts — like, sponsorship for a local official's kids school fees, or these huge holiday hampers — but not when they need something specific. they do it constantly, just to stay inside the circle. if you refuse, nobody thinks youre honest, they just act like you dont exist. you cant get a meeting, not because youre blacklisted, but because you never entered the relation in the first place.

i know about clientelism, and i know about normal social gift-giving, but this feels different. its not vote-buying, and its not friendship. its more like... preemptive reciprocity? youre building a reservoir of obligation without ever naming the debt. western compliance manuals call it all facilitation payments or bribery, but applying that label seems to miss that the gift isnt tied to a specific outcome — its tied to your continued existance in the network.

does this specific configuration have a name in anthropology or political theory? im not asking for a reading list, i just want to know if theres an established concept that captures the relationship seeding aspect without reducing it to either pure corruption or pure culture. and if there isnt, what would be the right way to draw the line between this and actual bribery, given that the line cant just be did a gift change hands? also, is there a term for the mistake of judging thier practices by whether they look transactional from the outside, when the local logic is relational? i feel like im seeing something real but im probably reinventing the wheel.
