I'm a journalist writing about why stoicism blew up in tech and therapy circles lately, and I got stuck on an argument a philosopher friend sent me. It seems to prove modern stoics are hypocrites. Here goes:

1. The classic dichotomy of control — the one Epictetus builds on determinist cosmology where externals are just indifferents with preferred and dispreferred flavors — says you should only care about what's up to you: your judgments, impulses, and choices. Everything else (other people's actions, the weather, election results) is outside your sphere and shouldn't disturb your equanimity.

2. If that's right, then caring deeply about social justice, climate outcomes, or even your partner's happiness is a catagory mistake: you're treating externals as if they were internal goods.

3. Modern stoic revivalists — the ones writing the bestselling workbooks and running resilience programs — consistently tell people to stay engaged with politics, relationships, and long-term projects. They don't tell you to stop voting or break up with everyone.

4. So either they're betraying the core doctrine, or they've silently redefinded control to include anything you can influence, which makes the dichotomy useless because now everything is partly up to you.

5. Therefore the modern revival is either incoherent or empty.

I feel like step 2 or 4 is where the sleight of hand is, but I can't pin it. Which premise should I drop if I want to defend the idea that stoicism can genuinely motivate activism without collapsing into just control your attitude? And what does giving up that step actually cost — do I have to swallow full-blown determinism, or can I keep some middle ground?
