I want a sorting instrument—either a decision tree or a taxonomy with a scoring key—that maps a respondent's answers onto specific theoretical commitments rather than onto philosopher names, for an audience of philosophy graduate students familiar with basic philosophy of mind but not necessarily specialists in cognitive science.

Please construct a taxonomy with exactly six terminal leaves, built from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of names. The leaves must include: (1) a strict Cartesian Theater view requiring both spatial convergence and a singular temporal "now"; (2) Global Workspace Theory interpreted as a singular broadcast without an internal observer; (3) Dennett's Multiple Drafts Model with distributed editorial processes and no canonical stream; (4) a Higher-Order Thought view that avoids spatial convergence through distributed meta-representation but retains a unitary "point of view" for each conscious state; (5) a Predictive Processing account treating consciousness as a hierarchical best-guess but denying any literal broadcast stage; and (6) a fragmentation-tolerant position accepting split-brain and blindsight phenomena as showing non-unitary consciousness but rejecting Dennett's editorial metaphor for parallel, non-competitive drafts that never merge. For each branch, locate the six leaves in the answer-space, flag any unoccupied combinations with a brief diagnosis of incoherence or unexplored status, and avoid any question that asks directly "which philosopher do you agree with?"

Finally, please say what would change your mind about the tree: which empirical finding or philosophical argument would collapse two branches into one, and where the taxonomy is most likely to misclassify answers near a boundary. Also explicitly flag any interpretation of Dennett or of Global Workspace Theory on which your reasoning depends and that specialists might contest.
