I'm trying to lock down my view on actual infinities for a paper and need a consistency audit before I present. I hold: (a) strict finitism about the concrete world—no actual infinite collections of physical things, and any talk of infinity in computation is just shorthand for an extendable finite rule; (b) Cantor's diagonal argument and Hilbert's hotel are fully sound, but only as theorems about formal structures, not discoveries of real infinite totalities; (c) reflective equilibrium is the right method in philosophy of math, so if our best physics pulls one way and our metaphysics pulls another, we revise the metaphysics; (d) when a mathematical framework scores robust novel predictions—like infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces in quantum mechanics—that gives defeasible reason to think its ontology is at least approximately correct; (e) there's no Platonic realm, so Cantor basically found a consistency condition on notation rather than mapping a domain of infinite sets. Which commitments genuinely conflict, and which only look like they do? I can see a seeming tension between (b) and (a), but I think it dissolves once you keep formal results seperate from physical ontology. The deeper problem feels like (c) and (d) pushing against (a) and (e), but I'm not sure how severe it is or which revision costs least. Rank the tensions by severity, say exactly what has to give in each case, and don't treat them all as equally bad. I'm not saying I'd drop (e) first, but I'm not ruling it out either.
