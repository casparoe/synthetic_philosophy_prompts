During our prep discussion they kept reducing the issue to “we just need better forecasting,” and I want them to see that the obstacle is structural: we face compound ignorance about how values, institutions, and technologies co-evolve over long horizons, not merely missing probabilities or ordinary risk.

Plan the explanatory arc: the dependency order in which the pieces must arrive so the conclusion feels like a tightening knot rather than a skeptical shrug; the one concrete carrying scenario—genuinely ambiguous in its long-run ripples, explicitly not the standard charity-donation example, and not collapsible into risk-aversion—that should anchor the session; the misconceptions they will form or bring (that improved forecasting solves it, that it applies only to grand policy choices, that it is just extreme uncertainty) together with the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off; the single check-for-understanding question that reveals whether they have grasped the structural nature of the problem rather than just feeling uneasy; and what I should openly refuse to explain at this level, with a brief justification. Please present the plan as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
