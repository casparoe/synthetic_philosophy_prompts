Write a villanelle about sphexishness—the rigid, stereotyped burrow-checking loop of the Sphex wasp, which repeats identically even when experimenters have moved its prey or altered the burrow entrance, and the philosophical question of whether such mechanical inflexibility marks the absence of free will or only a different kind of agency. Use the two refrains to enact the loop structurally: one refrain should state the unchanging sequence (check, enter, drag, seal), the other should pose the question that the loop never answers (whether deviation is possible, whether the loop knows it is looping). In the five tercets, vary the external conditions—the prey displaced, the tunnel blocked, the light changed—while keeping both refrains word-for-word identical, so that the poem's form demonstrates the very rigidity it describes. In the closing quatrain, bring the refrains together without resolving them: do not conclude that the wasp is mindless, and do not grant it hidden freedom. The poem should be precise enough that someone familiar with the behavioral studies recognizes the specific pattern, yet it should stand as poetry for a reader who has never heard of the wasp.
