I am a retired field ecologist who, since leaving active research, have been trying to organize my philosophical thoughts on the nature of biological function. I find myself holding the following cluster of commitments, and I wish to know whether they form a coherent position or whether some must be abandoned. First, I take the etiological view seriously: for a trait to genuinely have a biological function—say, the heart’s contribution to circulation—it is not sufficient that it currently plays that causal role; ancestral versions of the trait must have been selected for precisely that contribution. Second, I am inclined to allow that evolutionary novelty can generate functions immediately: if an engineered symbiont or a de novo mutation begins contributing to osmotic regulation within an organism, we can legitimately ascribe a causal-role function to it even in the complete absence of a selection history. Third, I hold that malfunction ought to be understood as deviation from an evolutionary design specification rather than mere statistical abnormality; a heart that pumps weakly is defective because it fails to perform the role for which hearts were selected, not simply because it diverges from the current population mean. Fourth, I maintain that practicing biologists actually identify functions mechanistically—by tracing present causal contributions within systems—and that evolutionary narratives are typically retrospective glosses rather than operational constraints on functional ascription. Fifth, I want to deny that there is a single master concept of function; I believe etiological and causal-role accounts answer genuinely different explanatory questions, and a trait can possess one kind of function without possessing the other.

Please examine these commitments pair by pair. Which combinations are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe is each real tension? For every genuine conflict, indicate what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost in terms of my other commitments. Finally, tell me which single change to my profile would eliminate the most tension with the least damage to the overall view, and whether the remaining position is best characterized as a hybrid account, a purified etiological view, or something else entirely. I am not looking for bare labels or bibliographic pointers; I want an audit of exactly what commits me to what, and where the edges of consistency lie.
