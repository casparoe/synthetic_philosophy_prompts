The Frege–Geach problem has been pressed against moral and normative expressivism since Geach’s original formulation: if “lying is wrong” expresses a non-cognitive attitude rather than stating a fact, then in a conditional such as “If lying is wrong, then teaching children to lie is wrong,” the embedded clause seems to contribute propositional content, not an attitude, which appears to collapse expressivism into descriptivism. Over subsequent decades, critics have sharpened the objection to cover modal, epistemic, and quantified embeddings, while expressivists—Blackburn with quasi-realist “commitment” semantics, Gibbard with norm-acceptance frameworks, and more recent hybridists—have replied that embedding can be handled pragmatically or that the embedded occurrences are “parasitic” on base expressive acts.
