A colleague who has been reading about formal epistemology summarized what they call the "Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents" as follows. Gödel's second incompleteness theorem establishes that any formal system powerful enough to encode basic arithmetic cannot prove its own consistency. If a rational agent is modeled as such a system—specifically, one that can represent its own inference rules and form beliefs about them—then the agent cannot consistently derive a proof that it is sound. The colleague then extends this in three directions. First, they claim the obstacle applies not only to purely deductive agents but also to any system with a "reflective endorsement loop": a mechanism by which the agent updates its credences by treating the outputs of its own reasoning process as authoritative, since such a loop allows the agent to embed representations of its own consistency within its belief dynamics. Second, they assert that the obstacle is unavoidable for any agent with sufficient logical strength: to escape it one must either restrict reasoning to a logic too weak for arithmetic or permanently withhold full endorsement of one's own soundness, which they describe as an irreducible epistemic humility. Third, they conclude that recursive self-improvement in artificial agents is therefore bounded in principle, because a system capable of redesigning its own reasoning is capable of representing that reasoning, hence hits the obstacle and can never fully trust its successor version without contradiction. Finally, they remark that present large language models avoid this problem only because they lack the formal machinery to represent provability predicates, but that any future system combining logical rigor with self-modification will necessarily face this ceiling.
