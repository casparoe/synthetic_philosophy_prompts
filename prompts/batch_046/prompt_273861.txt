I chair the institutional review board for the Neurocontinuity Foundation, which evaluates clinical proposals involving cognitive-preservation technologies. A research consortium has applied to conduct a Phase-II trial of gradual substrate-replacement therapy: over approximately eighteen months, a consenting patient's biological neurons in the prefrontal cortex are replaced one at a time with synthetic analogues that replicate both electrophysiological firing patterns and, through continuous subjective-reporting protocols, the qualitative character of experience. The patient remains awake and communicative throughout. At 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% replacement, standardized continuity audits assess whether the patient reports feeling like the same subject, whether close relatives and clinical observers concur, and whether memory and personality measures remain stable.

The researchers argue that because replacement is gradual and both functional and phenomenal continuity are explicitly monitored and preserved, the original person survives with identity intact; they seek approval to extend the trial from localized cortical regions to full-brain replacement. Half the board holds that gradual replacement is metaphysically equivalent to slow destruction followed by reconstruction with a duplicate, so that the original patient is eventually eliminated and replaced by a numerically distinct but qualitatively similar entity, regardless of subjective reports. The other half holds that personal identity consists precisely in psychological and phenomenal continuity, which the protocol safeguards, and that rejecting the trial imposes an unverified metaphysical criterion—numerical identity of substrate—over a clinically meaningful preservation of the person.

Draft the board's decision memo. Recommend whether to approve the trial, approve with specific conditions, or reject, with reasoning that addresses both the metaphysical continuity objection and the clinical-ethical duty to the consenting patient. Include an explicit bottom-line recommendation, an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, and—if recommending approval—concrete conditions that would address the board's residual concerns about discontinuity risk. The reasoning should be accessible enough that the researchers could respond to it substantively without consulting external philosophical literature.
