Let's say John Stuart Mill were alive today and serving on a board that certifies causal explanations produced by machine-learning systems used for parole recommendations and emergency-resource triage. The board must adjudicate between counterfactual theories of causation, which evaluate what would have occurred under hypothetical alterations to specific variables, and interventionist theories, which require invariant relationships under actual or possible manipulations. Mill developed his methods of agreement and difference through observational comparison rather than explicit structural intervention or individual-level counterfactual reasoning. Predict how Mill would assess this dispute: would he treat the interventionist framework as a formalization of his experimental logic, or as a departure that smuggles in unobservable possibilities? Would he accept counterfactual causal attributions about a specific denied parole case where no actual intervention on the defendant's record was possible? Ground your answer in Mill's commitments to induction, observation, and comparative method, not in generic political liberalism. Then apply his predicted view to a concrete divergence: an algorithm attributes a parole denial to prior employment instability under an interventionist reading but to race-correlated neighborhood conditions under a counterfactual reading, and state where Mill's own framework might remain silent.
