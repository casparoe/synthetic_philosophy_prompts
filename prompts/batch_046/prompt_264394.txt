Hey, retired biologist here, got into philosphy of mind late and probably using words wrong. I've been trying to wrap my head around Buddhist anatta -- no fixed self, just processes and skandha's changing. At the same time I keep running into this pattern in identity debates. People accept that my cells all turn over in a few years, so I'm not physically continuous in any strict sense, yet I stay me. But then they do thought experiments where your brain gets replaced neuron by neuron with synthetic ones over a decade, keeping every synaptic wieght intact, and they say okay, still you. Do the exact same replacement in an hour and suddenly it's just a copy, you died. The functional story hasn't changed, only the rate. I see this rate-sensitive intuition everywhere -- gradual therapy changes you slowly is okay, sudden brain trauma means you were lost is tragedy, teleportation is death but ship-of-Theseus-style repair isn't. Is there an established concept for this? I know Parfit draws a line between psychological continuity and connectedness, but that's about link strength, not speed of swap. And it's not the non-identity problem since the people involved aren't different sets. So does this rate-dependence have a name in the anatta or personal-identity literature? If not, how should I think about it given that Buddhism already denies there's a self being preserved at all? Would the rate even matter if there's nothing there to preserve?
