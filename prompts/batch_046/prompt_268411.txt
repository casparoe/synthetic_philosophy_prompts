ok so im writing a piece on why some philosophy debates just... sit there. take Sartre's idea of radical freedom and bad faith—there's this endless back-and-forth between people who read him as saying we're literally free to choose anything regardless of circumstance, and people who say he's only describing the burden of choice within social structures. its been going since like the 70s and nobody budges. my hunch is its partly verbal—like 'freedom' means metaphysical capacity to one side and phenomenological experience to the other—but maybe there's also a hidden empirical question about how people actually make decisions under constraint? i dont want a literature review, just a diagnosis: is this substantive, verbal, or are they answering seperate questions (maybe one about ethics, one about metaphysics) under the same heading? and specifically, could the stall come from both sides assuming bad faith is basically self-deception—like lying to yourself—when Sartre might mean something more like a performative social choreography you dont own? if thats the hidden disagreement, what would progress even look like? a conceptual distinction? an admission that the question is ill-posed? im curious what actually breaks the deadlock here, or if nothing should.
