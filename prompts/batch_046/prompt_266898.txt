I am on the verge of signing a ten-year professional contract with substantial exit penalties, and I am considering whether to purchase an expensive predictive assessment of my long-term suitability for the field. A colleague maintains that acquiring further information can never leave me worse off, so long as I remain free to revise my decision afterwards; on that view, I should buy the assessment whenever its expected benefit exceeds its cost. I am troubled, however, by the possibility that receiving the result might not merely guide my choice but could reshape the very preferences with which I evaluate outcomes—for instance, altering how I weigh security against ambition, or even changing which outcomes I regard as desirable. Does this prospect of endogenous preference change defeat my colleague's reasoning? And in practical terms, should I still commission the assessment?
