Compose a villanelle addressing the paradox of deontology—specifically the coexistence within agent-centered morality of both a restriction against harming an innocent party to prevent greater harm and a prerogative that permits an agent to refrain from saving multiple innocent parties at comparable or lesser personal cost. Employ two refrains that rotate through the five tercets: the first should voice the prohibition on direct harm even for optimal outcomes (for instance, refusing to sacrifice one to save five), and the second should voice the absence of a duty to rescue (for instance, allowing five to perish when saving them demands only a slight burden). In the opening tercets, establish the shared root—respect for the separateness of persons or the inviolability of the agent’s own moral boundaries—so that both refrains appear to grow from a single intuition. In the middle tercets, stage the divergence: show how the same boundary that blocks the agent from using one body as a shield also blocks any claim that the agent must become a shield for others. In the final quatrain, let both refrains meet in adjacent lines without synthesis, preserving the tension that the same principle seems to license both excessive forbearance and excessive license. Use concrete, non-technical imagery—perhaps a bridge, a lever, a bystander’s unmoving hands—so that the philosophical structure is visible to a specialist but the poem remains legible to a general reader. The villanelle’s circular form should do conceptual work: the returning lines should feel like arguments that keep coming back, each repetition deepening the contradiction rather than resolving it.
