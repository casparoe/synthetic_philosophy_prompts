Write a deadpan, sustained-irony satirical job advertisement in the precise bureaucratic register of a contemporary research-university human-resources office, announcing an open tenure-track position in Geometric Civil Philosophy at the fictional Institute for Demonstrative Governance. The advertisement should treat Thomas Hobbes's actual methodological commitments—his belief that civil philosophy can achieve the demonstrative certainty of geometry, his synthetic procedure that derives political conclusions from rigorously defined terms, and his dismissal of empirical observation and historical example as merely hypothetical and therefore unscientific—as literal hiring criteria. Structure the posting with conventional sections: position summary, required qualifications, core teaching and service duties, a diversity-and-inclusion statement, and institutional resources. In each section, translate standard academic expectations into geometric analogues: candidates must demonstrate that their publications are constructed more geometrico without appeal to inductive evidence; the teaching load involves instructing students in synthetic deduction from first principles rather than case-method analysis; the service requirement includes adjudicating disputes over the quadrature of the circle and the proper definition of commonwealth with the same rigor Hobbes applied in his controversies with Oxford mathematicians; and the benefits package substitutes absolute demonstrative certainty for conventional health coverage. The satire should land the standard objections—circularity in defining sovereign power, the gap between necessary geometric truths and contingent human passions, and the historical failure of geometric politics to predict actual state behavior—without ever naming them. Keep the tone completely deadpan: no winking, no explicit acknowledgment that the position is absurd. The target is Hobbes's geometric model of politics itself, not any named individual. Length: approximately 600 words. The reader should be able to tell exactly what is being ironized, yet the text should read as a plausible, if extreme, administrative document.
