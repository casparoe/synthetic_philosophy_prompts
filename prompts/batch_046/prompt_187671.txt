Write the full specification of desiderata that an adequate "crane-only" theory of conceptual normativity—how norms of correct inference and meaning-fixation arise—must satisfy in order to avoid both skyhook explanations (unexplained, top-down normative authority) and greedy reductionism (the elimination of the normative phenomenon by collapsing it into its lower-level substrate). For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or failure mode: for instance, preserving the distinction between causal regularity and rational justification; accommodating evolutionary continuity without reducing norms to bare selection effects; maintaining the asymmetry between error and correction at the collective level; and respecting compositional transparency so that explanatory parts do not covertly presuppose the normative whole they are meant to construct. State which single requirement does the most work in excluding popular naturalistic candidates, and identify any subset of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—indicating whether that tension marks a genuine boundary for reductionist explanation or only an apparent paradox. The weight should fall on articulating and justifying the criteria rather than on ranking named theories, though brief indication of where standard proposals fail is welcome.
