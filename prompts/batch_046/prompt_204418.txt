I am preparing the final examination for our advanced secondary-school philosophy seminar, and I would like you to construct a single essay question—together with a model answer and a marking scheme—on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility. The question should not be answerable by mere summary; instead, it should require students to apply the argument's logic to a constructed conceptual case. Specifically, imagine an agent whose psychological character is partly constituted by a persistent, self-modifying advisory system—an embedded deliberative module—that the agent initially chose to integrate but now cannot fully disable without severe psychological disruption. This module continuously reshapes the agent's preferences and reactive attitudes, yet it was not authored by the agent and operates with functional autonomy.

Please draft the exam question, a model answer of approximately 800 words, and a marking scheme totaling 25 marks, designed for a 75-minute response. The question should ask students to evaluate whether Strawson's regress is blocked, accelerated, or transformed in this hybrid case, and whether the case demonstrates that true moral responsibility requires a kind of self-authorship impossible in principle for any causally embedded agent.

The marking scheme must name five specific argumentative moves that distinguish an excellent answer from a competent one—such as precise reconstruction of the argument's premises, correct identification of the regress problem, careful analysis of the module's hybrid authorship, evaluation of whether voluntary adoption blocks the regress, and a reasoned conclusion about the scope of the impossibility claim. For each move, assign marks (summing to 25) and provide a one-line description of the typical student error—e.g., conflating causal determination in general with the specific regress Strawson identifies, or treating the module as an external cause rather than as a constitutive element of the agent's psychology. The difficulty should come from conceptual precision, not from obscure references or demands for citation.
