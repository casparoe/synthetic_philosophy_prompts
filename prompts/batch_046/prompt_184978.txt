I want to defend the stronger interpretive claim that this separation functions not as a psychological caution about overconfidence, but as a logical constraint that blocks standard Bayesian conditionalization whenever evidence is ambiguous or incomplete; on this view, rational agents facing radical uncertainty should adopt a posture of structured agnosticism rather than assign subjective priors and update them, because the absence of a comparable probability relation prevents belief revision rather than licensing a default expectation.

What would count against this position? Please present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
