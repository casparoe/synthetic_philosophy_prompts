After a long argument with a friend about whether compatriots carry special moral weight, I want a precisely engineered case that isolates that claim from every confounding factor. Please construct a thought experiment that meets the specifications below exactly.

Set the scenario in a realistic, non-fantastical setting — a hospital ward or a municipal office — in a single paragraph. A decision-maker must allocate one remaining life-saving resource (for instance, a specialised dialysis filter) between two individuals with identical medical need and prognosis. One is a citizen with full political membership; the other is a long-term resident who has contributed equally to the local community for fifteen years but lacks citizenship. Both the nationalist and the cosmopolitan agree on all empirical facts, on the equal intrinsic worth of both persons, and on the tragedy of either death. The nationalist must favour the compatriot, but not for reasons of reciprocity, efficiency, or proximity; the preference must flow solely from shared political membership treated as a non-instrumental relational good. Build in a moral-remainder condition: whichever individual is not saved, the agent incurs a specific, non-compensable moral residue that the opposing framework does not recognise in the same form, so that giving priority to the compatriot is not presented as cost-free.

Then demonstrate that the design succeeds. State the principle each theory applies and walk through the reasoning that produces its verdict. Identify the single structural feature that drives the divergence — the non-derivative normative weight assigned to membership — and explain what happens if that feature is removed: does the scenario collapse into an ordinary problem of impartial allocation? Finally, say how a cosmopolitan critic would attack the case, particularly whether the moral-remainder element is engineered to make membership-based choice appear more burdensome than it genuinely is.

The aim is to test whether compatriot status can generate an independent claim in concrete decision-making, not to illustrate general benevolence. Keep it concise, avoid bibliographies or citations, and do not invoke perfect predictors, exotic physics, or obscure theoretical apparatus.
