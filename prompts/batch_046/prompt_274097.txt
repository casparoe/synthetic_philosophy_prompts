I am writing an essay for a course on moral psychology and the Holocaust, and I would like to read Bernhard Schlink’s The Reader through the lens of the debate between Christopher Browning and Daniel Goldhagen over whether perpetrators were ordinary men shaped by situation and culture or willing executioners animated by deep ideological hatred. Rather than treating the novel as a simple illustration, I want to examine how its retrospective narrative structure and Hannah’s illiteracy function as philosophical metaphors for moral ignorance and epistemic distance. Which scenes best illuminate whether the text supports a situational or character-based explanation of perpetrator behaviour? Does the novel’s refusal to grant Hannah full psychological interiority ultimately vindicate Browning’s emphasis on bureaucratic role morality, or does it implicitly concede Goldhagen’s claim that even ‘ordinary’ perpetrators required a specific cultural framework to act? Please point out where the novel resists either reading and what it adds to the philosophical debate that abstract argument alone cannot capture.
