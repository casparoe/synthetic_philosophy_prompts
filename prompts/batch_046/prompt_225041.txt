After a long and somewhat heated argument last night with a friend who works on predictive-processing models of cognition and insists that metaphysics of the self is now an empirical dead end, I am sharpening a seminar presentation for next Tuesday. I need a Q&A prep sheet.

My thesis is that Buddhist anattā, when interpreted through a Prāsaṅgika-Madhyamaka lens rather than through the Theravāda Abhidharma mereological reduction, does not eliminate diachronic personal identity altogether but reconceives it as what I call “borrowed coherence”: a functional, relational continuity among mental states that lacks any proprietary owner (svāmin) yet retains enough structural integration to ground both karmic accountability and minimal moral responsibility. This is meant to occupy a narrow middle ground between Western narrative-identity theories and the standard Buddhist no-self reduction, and I am not defending a substantial ātman in disguise.

The audience is a philosophy-department seminar with three distinct clusters. First, analytic metaphysicians and philosophers of mind who care about persistence conditions, diachronic identity, and whether any account can support moral responsibility without a substantial self. Third, my friend, who will press on whether “borrowed coherence” predicts anything empirically distinguishable from standard predictive-processing self-models and will treat purely conceptual moves as question-begging.
