hey im grading a take-home for a grad seminar on nyāya epistemology and need another pair of eyes. the prompt was: "Explain the Nyāya account of śabda-pramāṇa (testimony) and why Naiyāyikas think it can't be reduced to anumāna. Then discuss one problem with treating upamāna (comparison/analogy) as an independent pramāṇa rather than a subspecies of anumāna."

student answer, paraphrased:

In Nyāya there are four pramāṇas: perception, inference, comparison, and verbal testimony. Śabda works through an authoritative speaker (āpta-vācana). It can't be inference because inference needs a previously observed universal connection (vyāpti) between middle term and predicate, but in testimony we establish the speaker's authority inductively from past truthful speech—there's no direct observation of the link between this utterance and the state of affairs described. So śabda relies on inductive trust rather than syllogistic correlation.

On upamāna: knowing a wild cow (gavaya) by comparing it to a domestic cow uses direct resemblance (sādṛśya). The independence view says this isn't inference because there's no syllogistic form and no vyāpti applied—just seeing likeness. One problem is circularity: recognizing resemblance requires already knowing the properties being compared, and that prior knowledge comes from inference, so analogy secretly depends on the process it claims to replace.
