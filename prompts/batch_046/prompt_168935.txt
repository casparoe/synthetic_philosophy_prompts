In recent work on panpsychism and fundamental physics, a dispute about ethical obligations has stalled: one side holds that if negative valence is intrinsic to quantum fields or spacetime geometry, then diffuse “micro-suffering” generates real moral patients and our ethics must expand to account for them; the other side replies that suffering requires bounded, integrated subjects, which fields do not form, and that treating diffuse phenomenal character as harm is a category mistake rather than a moral oversight. Both camps share the same physical formalisms and the same macro-level phenomenology, yet neither has moved—no concession on whether moral status requires individuation, and no agreement on whether “suffering” here denotes a thin phenomenal property or a thick functional state. Is the stall verbal (a dispute about the word “subject”), substantive but masked by a hidden empirical claim about which structures realize integration, or a bedrock normative disagreement about whether ethical salience requires boundedness? Diagnose the disagreement by engaging what each side actually commits to, and say what—an analytical distinction in integration, a formal result about field-level information structure, or a reframing of the ethical question—would count as progress, or argue that no progress should be expected and why.
