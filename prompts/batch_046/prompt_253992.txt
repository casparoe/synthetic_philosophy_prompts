I'm a doctoral student in metaphysics working on restricted theories of mereological composition, and I've been trying to formalize a view I'm tentatively calling cross-modal functional integrationism. On this view, parts compose a genuine whole just in case they are jointly disposed, across the nearest possible worlds in which they all exist, to participate in a unified causal-functional loop that cannot be decomposed into independent sub-loops without loss of systemic properties. Applying this criterion, it seems clear that there are organisms and artifacts—since their parts display exactly this kind of cross-modal integration—but there are no arbitrary undetached composites like my left ear together with the Eiffel Tower. So far, this feels like a substantive answer to the special composition question: composition is restricted precisely to those parts that satisfy the integration condition.

But I'm now worried that I haven't actually addressed special composition at all, and that I've merely relocated the problem. Specifically, if functional integration is defined in terms of the behavior of a composite system—say, the homeostatic loop that characterizes an organism—then don't I need to already identify that system as a single object in order to measure its integration? If so, my criterion appears circular: it uses the existence of the composite to ground the conditions under which parts compose it. Furthermore, my advisor suggested I'm conflating special composition with the problem of material constitution—perhaps because I'm treating the question as being about which parts belong to which object (the body versus the person, for instance) rather than about whether parts ever form a whole in the first place. Could you help me see whether cross-modal functional integrationism genuinely resolves when parts compose a whole, or whether it secretly presupposes an answer to that very question and merely repackages it as an empirical issue about functional organization? More generally, is there a principled distinction between answering the special composition question and solving the constitution problem in cases where the parts themselves have complex modal profiles?
