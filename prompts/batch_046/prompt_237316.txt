Design a final-exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy-of-mind seminar on Dennett’s distinction between Orwellian and Stalinesque revisions of conscious experience, centered specifically on the color phi phenomenon and the problem of temporal ordering in experience. The question must be unanswerable by merely defining the two revision-types and labeling phi as one or the other; it should require students to adjudicate whether phi supports revising the content of an earlier experiential state (Orwellian) or revising the historical record of what was experienced when (Stalinesque), especially given a hypothetical neuroimaging result—introduced in the prompt itself—that cortical registration of the second color actually precedes the conscious report of the first, reversing the standard post-hoc construction narrative. Provide three parts: (1) the exam question, framed for a 45-minute, approximately 1000-word essay response; (2) a model answer of about 600 words that takes a definite, argued position and executes the required adjudication; and (3) a marking scheme that separates an A answer from a B answer by naming the specific philosophical moves each must contain (for example, distinguishing narrative construction from perceptual binding, addressing the reversed-latency hypothetical, and avoiding the fallacy of identifying report-time with experience-time) and the specific mistakes that should cost marks (for example, conflating the two revision-types, treating phi as a pure motion illusion, or ignoring the latency reversal entirely). Present the marking scheme first, then the model answer, then the exam question. Keep the entire response under 900 words. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or requests for quotes from specific texts.
