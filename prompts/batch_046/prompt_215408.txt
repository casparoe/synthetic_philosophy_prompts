I'm defending what I call the scaffolding view of meaning under absurdity: local practices—craft, care, inquiry—generate internal norms of excellence that do not depend on cosmic justification. These norms are stabilized not by a hierarchical foundation but by functional interdependence across domains, which I term lateral justification, and they remain genuinely normative precisely because the overall framework is acknowledged as revisable and provisional. I am presenting to a philosophy of value seminar: mostly analytic philosophers of well-being and meaning (familiar with objective-list and hybrid subjective-state accounts, skeptical of metaphysical pathos about "the human condition"), plus two existentialism scholars and a psychologist studying self-authorship. They care about whether this collapses into subjectivism, whether lateral justification is just value-coherentism with extra steps, and whether provisionality undermines normative force.

My argument, briefly: (1) cosmic-scale justification is neither available nor necessary for local normative structures; (2) these structures are not mere preferences because they have internal criteria of success and failure; (3) their interdependence supplies stability without foundationalism; (4) explicit awareness of contingency is not self-undermining but a condition of authenticity.
