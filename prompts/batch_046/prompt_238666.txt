I'm preparing an interview for my science-and-ideas podcast with Dr. Elena Vasquez, a decision theorist who argues that Good's theorem on the value of information carries an underappreciated normative corollary: institutions ought never to withhold freely available predictive data from decision-makers, even when recipients claim that knowing would weaken their commitment to a prior course. I want eight precise interview questions that avoid having her merely restate the theorem. Specifically, include: one on how she distinguishes ex ante expected value from ex post regret in cases of what she calls "corrupting information," where knowledge degrades rather than improves an agent's capacity to maintain prior commitments; one on whether her view can accommodate Ulysses-contract-style self-binding, in which an agent rationally restricts her own future information precisely to preserve a commitment she anticipates being corrupted by; and one on whether collective bodies such as juries or funding boards face a different calculus under the theorem than individual agents do. For each of these three, provide a sharp follow-up that anticipates her most likely defense. The remaining questions should press on institutional design and on whether there is any coherent normative exception to disclosure. Keep the tone accessible but rigorous, and do not include generic biographical openers or requests for bibliographies or citations.
