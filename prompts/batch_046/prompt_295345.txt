Draft a 90-minute essay question for postgraduate candidates who know Weber's argument in outline but must analyse, not summarise. The question should require students to distinguish whether values operate as a triggering mechanism, a structural precondition, or an epiphenomenal overlay, and to judge whether any of these roles allows culture genuine causal autonomy without reducing it to individual psychology or material base.

Please supply:
- the exam question, clearly framed;
- a model answer of roughly 650–750 words that executes the required conceptual moves;
- a marking scheme (20 marks total) built around five essential analytical points, each with its mark allocation, a one-line description of the specific mistake that typically loses marks on that point, and brief guidance on what separates a distinction-level answer from a competent one.

The prompt should avoid soliciting bibliographies, named secondary texts, or citations; it should test conceptual precision rather than recall. Write in polished British English, maintaining the formal register of a journalist writing for an educated general audience on science and ideas.
