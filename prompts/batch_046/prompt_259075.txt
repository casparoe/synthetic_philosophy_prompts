I am trying to determine what distinguishes a system that genuinely misrepresents the world from one that merely behaves in ways that look like error, and I have formed the following judgments about concrete architectures. A simple bimetallic thermostat that shuts off heating when the strip bends: I judge that it does not misrepresent temperature, only undergoes mechanical failure. A pigeon shaped by Skinnerian reinforcement to peck red circles, which then pecks orange shapes in dim light: I judge that this is not a false belief about redness, but a conditioned response extending past its training boundary. A rat with an internal cognitive map predicting cheese at location A when it is actually at B: I judge this is genuine representational error, because the map can be disconfirmed and corrected without altering basic conditioning weights. A human child who asks whether the moon is made of green cheese: I judge the error belongs to the child, though her representational capacity is scaffolded by the linguistic tools she has inherited. A large neural network trained on image classification that consistently misclassifies adversarial perturbations as pandas: I judge this is closer to the pigeon—there is no internal model being falsified, only feature-space exploitation. Finally, a hypothetical Gregorian system—an LLM that uses internal scratchpads and tool-use loops to verify claims against an external database, yet outputs a false historical claim after misinterpreting the retrieved record: I judge this is closer to the rat or child, because the error survives an explicit verification loop and can be corrected by revising the interpretation rule rather than by retraining weights; yet I also feel tension, since the system lacks any evolutionary history of risk or reward. Is there one principle—perhaps concerning whether the system deploys an explicit, revisable model for action-selection, independent of how its weights were originally shaped—that captures all six verdicts? If not, which verdict should be overturned, and why? Please state any principle you propose precisely, indicate which of my judgments it cannot accommodate, and then predict its verdict for a case I have not listed: a decentralized swarm-robotics system in which no individual robot maintains an internal model, but the collective maintains a distributed resource-map that can be globally wrong and then corrected by inter-robot consensus, without any single agent ever representing the error.
