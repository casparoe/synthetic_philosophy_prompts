I'm drafting a novel about a distributed planetary consciousness, and I'm returning to Lem's Solaris—both the novel and Tarkovsky's adaptation—as a structural reference. Read the work through the lens of panpsychism and the combination problem. The ocean reads as a single macro-subject, yet its substrate is planetary-scale and its manifestations—the Visitors—seem to carry discrete, private phenomenologies that the ocean cannot fully integrate or understand. Does Solaris dramatize the combination problem by depicting a macro-conscious entity that fails to achieve phenomenal unity with its own parts? Consider whether the Visitors function like micro-subjects that resist aggregation: the resurrected partner possesses suffering and self-awareness that appear genuinely hers rather than borrowed from the ocean's macro-experience, despite being physically constituted by its matter. Where does Lem's premise align with or resist distinctions between phenomenal blending and functional aggregation? I want a reading faithful in both directions—one that doesn't reduce the ocean to a metaphor for collective mind, but asks whether the fiction exposes something about failed integration that analytic arguments about subject-summing miss, especially regarding the emotional cost when parts cannot be summed into a whole.
