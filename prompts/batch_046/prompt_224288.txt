As a journalist covering philosophy and conceptual science, I am preparing a feature on modal metaphysics and need an original poetic piece to run alongside the analytical prose—something that captures the live tension between David Lewis’s counterpart theory and the transworld-identity view without reducing either position to a popular-science cartoon. I was discussing this with colleagues over coffee and realized that most treatments simply ask whether “you” could have been a philosopher in another world; they miss the deeper structural problem about whether the relation linking you to that figure is numerical identity, filtered by essential properties, or qualitative resemblance arranged along graded dimensions of similarity that never quite reach sameness. I want you to write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain with the classic A1-b-A2 rhyme scheme—whose two refrains are the competing claims: one asserting that the self travels intact across worlds, the other insisting that every cross-world traveler is only a counterpart, never the original. Before composing, please think carefully about the modal logic involved: under counterpart theory there is no transworld identity, only counterpart relations graded by intrinsic and extrinsic resemblance; under the rival view identity holds but is constrained by essential properties that may leave most “possible versions” of you outside your modal reach. Start by brainstorming how the villanelle’s compulsory repetition can mirror the way counterpart relations repeat properties without repeating identity, then draft the poem so that the refrains take turns through the tercets and alter slightly with each return—perhaps shifting a single word such as same to similar, or I to she—to show how similarity shifts under pressure. In the final quatrain the two refrains must appear together, side by side, without resolution. The poem should stand as literature for readers who know nothing of metaphysics, yet contain enough conceptual precision—references to haecceity, qualitative similarity, or essential properties woven naturally into the lines—that a specialist in modality recognizes the argument being made in form rather than only in content. Treat the assignment as if you are both a professional philosopher and a poet tasked with making a live metaphysical dispute audible; do not include any bibliography, citation, or reading list, and do not quote any existing philosophical text verbatim—if you invoke a specific formulation, paraphrase it in your own lines. I need the result polished, formally composed, and suitable for publication alongside serious analytical writing.
