I'm trying to identify whether the following philosophical stance has an established name. A thinker accepts the anti-luminosity argument — the idea that being in a mental state (for instance, gradually feeling colder) does not guarantee that one knows one is in it, since there is no sharp boundary one can reliably detect — and takes this to undermine any unrestricted principle that knowing p entails knowing that one knows p. At the same time, however, they maintain that phenomenal consciousness is an exception: if one is genuinely experiencing pain, one is not merely in a position to know it, but necessarily in a position to know that one knows it, not via an inference but because the state is self-transparent. The result is a selective rejection of the KK principle for propositional knowledge coupled with the retention of something structurally identical to it, but confined to phenomenal states. Does this specific combination — global anti-luminosity paired with local phenomenal KK — have a recognised label in the literature? If not, how should one distinguish it from standard transparency accounts of consciousness, and from the view that phenomenal knowledge is non-propositional and therefore outside the scope of KK altogether?
