hey, i've been trying to pin down my actual stance on work and automation for a paper and want a consistency check before i present it. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) automation is good even when it destroys jobs — unemployment is a distribution problem, not a technological one, and if we decouple income and healthcare from work, losing a job to a machine is a net win.

(2) but there's a real harm in losing work that goes beyond money: i call it "teleological unemployment," where people lose the structured directedness and social recognition that comes from having a defined cooperative role.

(3) the fix should be unconditional material security plus freedom to opt out — forcing people into jobs just to preserve "directedness" is paternalistic.

(4) still, if people opt out and consistently report feeling adrift, i take that as real evidence that directing human effort toward shared ends has a non-instrumental value i shouldn't just dismiss as false consciousness.

(5) i reject the protestant work ethic and think any claim that labor is intrinsically dignified is usually ideological mystification.

i think (2) and (4) probably conflict with (1) and (3), and (5) clashes with (4), but maybe some of these are only surface tensions. can you audit this: go pair by pair, rank the conflicts by severity, say which commitment has to give if any and what the cheapest revision would be, and point out which tensions dissolve once you look closer? also please seperate whats settled from whats genuinely contested among people who study this, and dont present a contested view as if its the consensus.
