Consider the conceptual pattern in which sophisticated adaptive competence arises in the absence of the explicit reasoning, understanding, or intentional design that we ordinarily treat as its prerequisite. Darwin's evolutionary mechanisms produce biological design without a designer's comprehension, and Turing's formal computation produces correct, complex outputs without the machine's grasp of meaning. Rather than treating these cases as familiar analogies already settled in the literature, treat them as generating an unfinished, interlocking agenda spanning philosophy of biology, philosophy of mind, and the philosophy of artificial intelligence.

Your task is not to survey the field but to deliver a defended prioritization: which two or three open questions or downstream lines of work within this inversion framework deserve the most sustained philosophical attention, and which should be actively deprioritized? Among the live issues are whether evolutionary competence can be fully decoupled from any form of representational content; whether Turing-style computation forces a reconception of understanding as observer-relative rather than intrinsic to the system; how these historical inversions should inform our evaluation of present-day artificial systems that exhibit high functional competence without transparent reasoning processes; and whether a genuinely unified account of biological and artificial competence is achievable, or whether the two cases are analogically misleading in ways that block unification.

Please defend your ordering by specifying the criteria you are using — for example, tractability with purely conceptual tools, how many other questions depend on resolving this one, the degree of neglect by current research, and the practical stakes for assessing non-human or artificial agency. Indicate which issues are upstream versus downstream, and identify at least one prominent line of inquiry that you regard as overrated or largely verbal, explaining why its resolution would not substantially restructure the broader framework. I am asking for an agenda calibrated for long-term philosophical work, not for a reading list, bibliography, or citation-based review; the deliverable should be usable by someone working without access to specialized empirical literatures.
