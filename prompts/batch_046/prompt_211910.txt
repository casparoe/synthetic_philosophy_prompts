Okay so my friend is arguing that Appiah-style honor explanations actually swallow everything, and I think he's wrong but the steps feel slippery.

(1) Take any norm that dies—slavery, dueling, footbinding, whatever.
(2) If people still genuinely honored it, they'd keep practicing it; the fact they stopped means honor withdrew.
(3) Now 'interests' just means people acting to protect or gain social standing, and 'moral argument' only moves people when it already tracks shifts in who's respected.
(4) So whenever interests or arguments seem to cause change, honor dynamics are really doing the work behind the curtain.
(5) That means for every dead norm, the real cause was honor loss, and citing interests or arguments is just describing the same event twice with different words.
(6) But then there framework isn't one explanation among three—it's the only one, and the other two are literally empty categories.
(7) My friend says this is a feature, not a bug, but it feels like he proved honor explains everything, which can't be right because then we couldn't explain a norm dying via pure economic pressure with no prestige shift at all. I think there's an equivocation in step 3 between 'interests involve honor sometimes' and 'interests are just honor,' but I can't quite name it. Which premise should I give up, and what does rejecting it cost for the broader debate about moral revolutions?
