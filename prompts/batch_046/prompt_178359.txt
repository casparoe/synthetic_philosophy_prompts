I'm organizing the final meeting of a graduate reading group on skepticism and common sense, and we want to close with something that treats G.E. Moore's proof of an external world as an argument with real structure rather than as a philosophical punchline. I'd like you to write a villanelle—nineteen lines total, five tercets followed by one quatrain, with two repeating refrains in an ABA rhyme scheme—where the form itself enacts the logic of the proof. The two refrains should be Here is one hand and Here is another; they must return in the standard villanelle pattern and remain essentially unchanged, representing the stubborn immediacy of the demonstration. The A-rhymes should cluster around words of presence, holding, or bodily display—held, grasped, shown—while the B-rhymes should cluster around concealment, absence, or withdrawal—hidden, withdrawn, unseen—so that the sound-structure maps the gap between holding something in view and establishing that it belongs to an external world.

The voice should be restrained and clinical, almost like an analytic philosopher delivering a lecture in verse, accurate enough that someone familiar with Moore's argument recognizes the steps: the direct ostension of the hands, the inference to at least two external objects, the further claim that an external world exists, and—crucially—the standard objection that the argument begs the question by assuming precisely what the skeptic denies. The volta in the final quatrain should arrive exactly at the point where this circularity is pressed, but the poem must not resolve the tension; it should leave the reader suspended between the force of the demonstration and the charge of question-begging. I want no sermon about humility and no shrug about the circle of life—just the open tension where our discussion actually ended.

As an additional formal constraint to ground the abstraction in sensory particularity without breaking the clinical tone, please include in each B-rhyme line exactly one word naming a tactile or visual quality—bone, wax, grey, red, cold, smooth—so that the skeptical withdrawal is always anchored to a specific texture. No citations, bibliographies, or reading lists are needed; the deliverable is only the poem.
