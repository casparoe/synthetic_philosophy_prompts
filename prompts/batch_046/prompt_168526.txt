I recently encountered an essay summarizing the School of Names (Mingjia) and Gongsun Long's white-horse argument in terms that blend classical Chinese logic with modern analytic metaphysics. The summary runs as follows. Gongsun Long's claim that a white horse is not a horse is reconstructed not as a paradox about color but as an argument about sortal identity: the bare kind-term horse denotes the pure form (xing) without accidental properties, whereas white horse denotes a compound descriptor that already includes a specific determination. On this reading, adding the color predicate does not merely describe the kind differently; it shifts the term from kind-reference to a restricted subset, so the compound can never strictly denote the original kind. The summary then links this to Hui Shi's well-known theses—especially the idea that universal love requires treating all things as a single whole—claiming that Hui Shi rejected Gongsun Long's move because for him distinctions like color are purely perspectival. From a broad enough standpoint, the white horse and the horse are identical, and all differentiation collapses into an undifferentiated continuum. The essay therefore divides the Mingjia into separators, who treat linguistic distinctions as carving reality at the joints, and uniters, who see them as conventional partitions. It concludes that modern formal logic sides with the uniters, since Gongsun Long's reasoning depends on treating predicative modification as kind-exclusion, which is a straightforward category error.

How fair is this characterization? I want to know whether the sortal-identity framing is a legitimate reconstruction or an anachronistic import, whether Hui Shi's theses actually function as a direct reply to the white-horse argument, and whether describing the school as split between separators and uniters misrepresents the historical relationship between these thinkers. Please separate what is accurate, what is a standard simplification, and what is subtly wrong, and tell me whether the conclusion about modern logic follows from the corrected premises.
