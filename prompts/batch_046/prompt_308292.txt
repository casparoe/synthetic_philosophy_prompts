I serve as the scientific review officer for a conservation grant program that receives frequent proposals justifying ecological interventions by appealing to evolutionary dynamics — for example, gene-drive modifications in wild populations, vaccination of individuals, or culling to protect group-level fitness. I need a reusable evaluation procedure, expressed as an ordered sequence of decision questions with stopping rules, that translates the units-of-selection debate into operational criteria my panel can apply in under thirty minutes per proposal. The procedure must distinguish selection on replicators from selection on interactors, must explicitly handle cases where selection appears to operate on a hologenome or an extended phenotype rather than on a discrete organism or gene, and must flag where the determination requires empirical data we do not have versus where it requires a normative judgment about whose welfare counts. For each step, provide a brief test or question that a non-specialist reviewer can apply, indicate the point at which the procedure yields a clear level-of-intervention recommendation, and mark the nodes where the answer depends on contested evolutionary theory rather than observable evidence. Apply the procedure to three proposals: (1) releasing gene-edited mosquitoes that suppress reproduction at the population level; (2) vaccinating individual badgers against tuberculosis when transmission dynamics depend on social-group structure; and (3) culling deer to prevent winter starvation, where the proponents claim group-level selection favors reduced population density but critics argue this confuses demographic regulation with evolutionary selection. At least one of these applications must produce a recommendation that conflicts with the intuitive ethical response of the reviewer, and you must explain why the procedure produces that result. Finally, state explicitly what kinds of proposals lie outside the procedure's scope — for instance, those invoking cultural evolution or multi-level selection across non-biological entities — and why.
