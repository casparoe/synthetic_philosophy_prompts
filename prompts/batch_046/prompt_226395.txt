Conservation ethics treats marble statues and clay vessels differently when parts are missing: for marble, restorers use visibly distinct, removable replacements that preserve the boundary between original and addition; for porous clay, they often apply chemically identical, irreversible fills that erase that boundary. The same institution thus seems to treat an artifact's identity as tied to its continuous material in one case but to its formal integrity in the other. What metaphysics of material constitution does that differential practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory of the relation between object and matter, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, say whether the two protocols are philosophically consistent, and indicate what would have to change in restoration standards if we adopted the view that a statue is nothing over and above the sum of its clay parts at any given time.
