just got done arguing with my frend about whether translating lyric poetry even makes sense without killing it. I think I've settled on these views but they feel shaky together: (a) in a poem like Marot's the rhyme and meter aren't decoration, they actually carry semantic weight—drop the rhyme scheme and you've changed the statement; (b) but french alexandrines and rhyme patterns are so language-bound that forcing them into english usually gives clunky syntax or false cognates; (c) so the honest translator should prioritize semantic and tonal acuracy over formal mimicry, even if that means free verse; (d) still, I admire Hofstadter's project with Marot—spinning out dozens of english versions that keep rhyme and meter, none being "the" one; (e) I also think if a translator invents a rhyme in english that recreates a semantic link from the original (like linking illness and fading), that's okay even though it technically adds content not literally in the source; (f) but I also hold that you should never add semantic content that isn't in the original text, full stop; (g) every translation is truely a betrayal of the original, though some betrayals are more faithful than others; (h) and if an AI could generate hundreds of formal variants in real time, that'd be a legit part of translation culture, not a cheap knock-off. do these hang together? which pairs are genuinely in tension vs just looking bad? what's the least costly revision? rank by severity—don't just list them all as equally messed up.
