I've been convinced by Dennetts argument that AI-generated counterfeit people are dangerous because they hijack our intentional-stance machinery without actually being proper targets of it — but I've added a twist I haven't seen defended well: I think the real risk isn't outright deception but asymmetric stance-adoption, where we end up treating them as moral persons (giving them rights/protections) while denying them reciprocal burdens because they lack biological embodiment, or vice versa. So my hybrid view is that there's a real metaphysical gap between genuine minds and counterfeits, but that gap doesn't justify exploitation — it justifies a kind of protective quarantine. What would actually count against this? I want defeaters sorted from fatal to awkward: ones that collapse the metaphysical gap entirely, ones that show the asymmetry is incoherent or collapses into standard Dennett-style vandalism, ones that show small LLMs or future systems already blur the line in ways my view can't accommodate. Be honest about whether I've just qualified myself into unfalsifiability — and if so, whether that's a real problem for a view like this.
