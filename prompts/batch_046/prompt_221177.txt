I've been reading about the positive vs negative vs republican liberty thing - Berlin, Pettit, all that - and honestly it feels like the debate has been stuck in the same loop since the nineties. Republicans say non-domination is not just absence of interference, negative-liberty folks say yes it basically is, and the positive-liberty camp says both miss self-mastery. Nobody moves.

But I started wondering if the stall is actually about liberty at all, or if its about people measuring totally different things and calling them all "freedom." So I cooked up a weird case to test it: imagine a society with a transparent algorithm that calculates every citizen's "domination score" - tracking how vulnerable they are to arbitrary power - but the algorithm never actually stops anyone from doing anything. People just see their score and adjust preemptivly, avoiding risky choices. No direct interference happens, but everyone knows they are being monitored and ranked.

Now, is this a loss of republican liberty? Negative liberty? Both? Or neither, since no one was actually blocked? And does the fact that people change their behavior without being forced mean positive liberty is also missing?

More generally, is the three-way debate stalled because each side is answering a different question - one about legal obstacles, one about psychological autonomy, one about social relations - and we keep pretending its one debate? Or is there a real substantive disagreement hiding underneath? Diagnose it using what each side actually says, and tell me what progress would even look like here - a unified definition, an empirical study, admitting they're different concepts, or something else?
