1. i'm a physicalist - i think phenomenal and access consciousness are both ultimately brain processes.
2. i accept Block's distinction: phenomenal consciousness is what it's like, access consciousness is availability for reasoning and behavioural report.
3. i think global workspace theory gets access consciousness right - it's broadcasting to consumer systems.
4. but i think phenomenal consciousness is realised by local recurrent processing in sensory cortices, not by the global broadcast itself.
5. i take Chalmers' zombie argument seriously as showing a conceptual gap, but i deny it proves dualism - i treat it as an epistemic limitation.
6. i think it's conceptually possible to have full global workspace broadcasting with zero local recurrence - a kind of 'broadcast zombie' with access but no feel.
7. in actual humans these two are never dissasociated - you can't have one without the other.
8. i reject the idea that phenomenal concepts involve special direct acquaintance; the explanatory gap is just hard neuroscience, not a metaphysical mystery.

do these hang together? specifically i'm worried that (4) and (6) sit awkwardly with (1) and (5): if phenomenal consciousness is just local recurrence, then my 'broadcast zombie' isn't physically identical to a conscious person, which means i'm smuggling in the zombie framing while denying its metaphysical bite. and is (7) compatible with (2) - if they're never dissasociated in practice, why treat them as genuinely distinct properties rather than two descriptions of the same mechanism?
