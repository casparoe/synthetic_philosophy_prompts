Schopenhauer famously distinguishes music from all other arts by arguing that it does not represent Platonic Ideas mediated through phenomenal form, but rather expresses the Will itself immediately and non-representationally. Consider a hypothetical technology developed after Schopenhauer's death—direct cortical sonification—in which structured auditory experience is induced entirely through calibrated transcranial neural stimulation, producing the phenomenology of harmony, melody, and rhythm without any acoustic vibration, physical instrument, or bodily resonance. Given Schopenhauer's metaphysical commitment that the Will objectifies itself through material grades of nature, and given his particular emphasis on the physicality of sound as a manifestation of that objectification, how might he evaluate music experienced through this disembodied, purely cortical pathway? Would he regard it as a more direct revelation of the Will because it eliminates phenomenal mediation, or would he reject it as deficient precisely because it severs the necessary material link between inner Will and outer representation?
