A mate in my seminar sumed up Parfit's hitchhiker argument like this: rational agents ought to cultivate a disposition to pay the driver after reaching their destination, even though once they arrive the payment is purely costly, because drivers only stop for people they expect to pay. He says this is a case where it is rational to cause yourself to have what looks like an irational behaviour — breaking the usual link between present interest and action — but he claims Parfit sneaks this in by relying on a backtracking counterfactual that treats your present commitment as altering the causal history of the ride, which only makes sense if you buy modal realism about possible worlds. From this he concludes that the whole argument is just Newcomb's problem in disguise, and that it does not show rational irrationality is genuinely possible, only that people confuse evidential reasoning with causal reasoning. Is this fair? Separate what is accurate about Parfit's view from what is distorted, say whether the modal-realist claim is something defenders actually need, and tell me if the comparison to Newcomb's problem is illuminating or just muddying the waters.
