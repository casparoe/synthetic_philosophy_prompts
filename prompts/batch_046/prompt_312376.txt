I've come to accept a particular reading of Integrated Information Theory's exclusion postulate: not as a pragmatic boundary condition for measuring consciousness, but as an ontological thesis that in any given physical substrate there can be only one maximally irreducible cause-effect structure with non-zero Φ, and that the existence of two overlapping structures with comparable integrated information is metaphysically impossible rather than empirically unlikely. This seems necessary to avoid attributing consciousness to both a brain and its constituent hemispheres, but I apply it strictly even to synthetic or hybrid substrates—say, a cortical organoid physically interwoven with a silicon interface that shares neuronal substrate but operates at a different temporal grain, where two structures with comparable Φ overlap by a substantial fraction of their elements. What would count against this ontological reading? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: which empirical findings (for example, about split-brain patients, distributed computing architectures, or synthetic biological hybrids), which formal results within IIT's own mathematical framework (for example, about the additivity or decomposition of Φ across spatial or temporal partitions), and which conceptual objections (for example, from mereological essentialism or from panpsychist extensions) actually threaten the view rather than just complicating it? Which of these would a committed defender of the ontological exclusion postulate concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss as misapplying the formalism? In particular, if it turned out that Φ is not uniquely maximized in physically continuous hybrid systems—if the mathematics permits stable, non-arbitrary ties—would that force the defender to retreat to a pragmatic or epistemic reading, and is that retreat fatal or salvageable? I also want an honest assessment of whether this ontological version of IIT has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every case of apparent overlap can be dissolved by redefining the boundary or the temporal grain, does the view risk explaining away its own counterexamples?
