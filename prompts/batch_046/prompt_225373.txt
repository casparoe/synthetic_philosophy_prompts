I have been working through Douglas Hofstadter's framework in which the self is not anchored to particular brain tissue but is rather a distributed symbolic pattern—a strange loop of self-reference that can, in principle, be instantiated across different substrates and even shared among minds. In discussing this with a colleague, Dr. Elena Voss, who applies the framework to edge cases in philosophy of mind, I have recorded her verdicts on several scenarios and now wish to anticipate her judgment on a case she has not yet addressed. She maintains that gradual neuronal replacement with synthetic equivalents preserves the same self without interruption; that a static digital archive of one's complete brain state is merely a dead record, not part of the self; that a lifelong intimate partner can come to literally embody part of one's self through sustained reciprocal emotional and cognitive integration; that a seminar group collectively reproducing one's reasoning without intimate emotional bonds does not constitute a distributed self but only influence; and that a brain-computer interface continuously mirroring one's neural patterns into an external processor remains a cognitive tool rather than an extension of the self, even when actively used.

The new case is this: a complete functional copy of Dr. Voss's cognitive architecture is instantiated in an isolated synthetic system. The system actively reasons, updates beliefs, and produces philosophical writing recognizably in her style, but it exists in total isolation—no social interaction, no emotional reciprocity, no mutual recognition with any conscious being. Meanwhile, her original biological brain is preserved in a minimally conscious state with virtually no meaningful cognitive activity. Given her earlier verdicts, predict whether she will judge that her self now resides primarily in the synthetic copy, has been lost or severely diminished, or is split in some qualified manner. In your answer, articulate at least two distinct rules that are jointly consistent with her five prior judgments—one that might emphasize substrate-independent active pattern instantiation and another that might emphasize reciprocal social-emotional embodiment—specify what each predicts about this isolated copy, offer your best-guess verdict with reasoning, and propose a single further case that would most clearly distinguish these two rules from one another.
