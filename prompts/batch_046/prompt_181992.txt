Construct a thought experiment engineered to isolate verbal from substantive disagreement in metaontology, using an injected feature—nested provenance—that is not a standard textbook case. Scenario (one paragraph, realistic setting only): two materialist museum conservators in a working studio examine a single gallery installation called the Cascade. The Cascade is physically assembled entirely from fragments of previously destroyed sculptures; each fragment retains its original catalog number and artistic attribution, arranged so that no original material survives intact and every constituent part is simultaneously part of multiple prior artworks. One conservator says, "The Cascade is not a sculpture; it is a graveyard of sculptures." The other says, "The Cascade is one sculpture, composed of parts that happen to have prior histories." They agree on every physical fact, every causal history, and every institutional record. After presenting the scene, show exactly why a deflationist about ontological disputes should diagnose this as verbal—identify the hidden ambiguity in a term like "sculpture" or "part" and explain why no ontological commitment differs—then show why a heavyweight realist should treat it as substantive, specifying what fact about artistic identity or composition is genuinely at stake. Next, identify which feature of the Cascade—its nested provenance—does the isolating work, and explain what happens if that feature is removed (e.g., all fragments come from unremarkable raw materials): does the dispute collapse, or revert to a standard ontological disagreement? Finally, state the weakest point in the deflationist's diagnosis and how the realist could press it. Constraints: no exotic physics, no demons or perfect predictors, no bibliographies or citation requests, and do not frame the dispute as merely about word-meaning without addressing ontological commitment.
