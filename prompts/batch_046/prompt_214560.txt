I'm in a political philosophy reading group and we've been stuck for months on the democratic boundary problem — who actually belongs to the demos. The usual answers keep recyling: all affected interests, all subjected to coercion, all stakeholders. Nobody moves. My hunch is that the stall isn't about values but about a hidden assumption that membership has to be all-or-nothing, like citizenship is a binary switch. If we treated belonging as scalar or role-relative — someone can be partly in the demos for some decisions but not others — does that dissolve the disagreement, or does it just relocate it? I want a diagnosis: is this a verbal dispute about 'demos', a substantive disagreement about the ontology of collective agency, or are the sides answering different questions (one normative, one descriptive) under one label? And be honest: is there anything — a conceptual distinction, an institutional desgin, or a formal model — that could actually break the deadlock, or is this the kind of question that stays open because the answers depend on bedrock intuitions about equality and coercion?
