A colleague summarized a recent position in the philosophy of mind and computation for me as follows: the careenium is the layer of formal symbolic structures, while the simmballs constitute the underlying physical substrate that instantiates them. The argument runs that because symbols belong to the careenium, they possess no intrinsic causal efficacy; they are merely patterns of organization. Consequently, the careenium can never literally push the simmballs around—only the simmballs' own physical dynamics do causal work. From this, the colleague concludes that any view holding that symbolic representations play a direct causal role in behavior—such as strong versions of embodied cognition—commits a category error, and that the correct ontology treats the careenium as epiphenomenal with respect to substrate-level mechanics. I think this summary may be unfair to the embodied-cognition side, and I worry it smuggles in an unstated dualism, but I am not sure where the missteps lie. Which parts of this characterization would a proponent of symbol-substrate interaction accept? Where does it conflate representational dependence with causal inertness, overstate the separation between careenium and simmballs, or present a standard objection as a refutation? And does the conclusion that embodied cognition is undermined actually follow from the accurate parts?
