I have been thinking, without any formal philosophical training, about those acts that are genuinely good yet seem to lie beyond what anyone can strictly demand of another person — volunteering for a cause, helping a stranger with heavy luggage, or spending an afternoon cleaning a shared space. I understand there is a term for this, supererogation, but I am approaching it only through ordinary experience. What puzzles me is the strange social alchemy by which an action initially praised as exceptional gradually becomes treated as the bare minimum; once enough people perform the "optional" good, anyone who stops is quietly blamed for falling short.
