I'm putting together a book club for my philospohy of food ethics class and we're doing Charlotte's Web next month. I want a reading that takes the novel seriously alongside the argument that crop cultivation kills far more wild animals per calorie than beef does, and that chicken is usually worse than beef on a suffering-per-meal basis because of numbers and conditions. The story saves one named pig, Wilbur, but the farm still slaughters other pigs and keeps chickens and cows in the background. Does the novel's emotional investment in Wilbur depend on him being named and visible, and does that expose a bias that makes us ignore the wild mice and insects killed growing the corn that feeds him? Point to specific scenes — maybe the fair, the converstaion with Charlotte about death, Fern's dad almost killing the runt — and ask what they reveal about whether suffering only matters when it's narrativized. Don't just say the book is pro-vegetarian; ask whether White accidentally makes a case for beef over chicken, or for the idea that saving one charismatic animal is ethically hollow if the feed crops kill dozens of wild ones. I want a real reading that doesn't reduce the book to an illustration and also doesn't distort the ethics to fit.
