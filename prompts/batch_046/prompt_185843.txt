Please write the opening remarks—about seven minutes, for an audience of roughly forty people—at an interdisciplinary workshop titled "The Weight of One," on individual action and collective harm. Half the room are moral philosophers familiar with the inefficacy objection in climate and consumption ethics; the other half are climate scientists and policy analysts with no background in that debate. The remarks should establish the workshop's governing tension: not whether one person's carbon footprint is causally negligible, which is common ground, but whether there exists a distinct, non-causal form of moral involvement in collective harm that persists even when one's causal contribution is effectively zero. Do not name this position, cite its originators, or provide a bibliography. The philosophical framing should do structural work: you must not resolve the tension by reassuring listeners that virtue still matters, nor by declaring individual choice morally irrelevant, because both moves collapse the problem the workshop exists to explore. Instead, navigate the constraint openly, speaking in terms accessible to the scientists—perhaps through a concrete decision about a single unnecessary flight—while sharpening the philosophical stakes for the experts. End not with an answer but with a precisely framed question that leaves both halves of the room in the same unresolved tension, ready to work.
