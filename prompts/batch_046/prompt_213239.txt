Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on the symbol grounding problem: the Internal-Relational Grounding camp (IRG), which holds that a representational token acquires aboutness primarily through its functional and structural position within a coherent, predictively successful, recursively organized symbol system, and the Causal-Transduction Grounding camp (CTG), which holds that aboutness requires direct causal-informational channels linking tokens to their environmental referents, with internal coherence being at best derivative. Write the statement in American English.

The deliverable should contain the following sections, in order.

First, Agreed Formal and Explanatory Claims. Number and list the propositions both camps can sign: what formally distinguishes a symbol system from a non-representational causal network; what the grounding problem actually requires explaining (the transition from syntactic token to semantic aboutness, not merely behavioral success); and what standard no-go considerations rule out (for instance, that pure unstructured syntax is insufficient for reference, and that pure unstructured causal correlation is insufficient for compositional representation). Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading.

Second, Precise Disagreements. Present a parallel-text or table format in which each camp states, in wording it fully accepts, its own positive thesis about grounding, followed immediately by the other camp's formulation of that thesis in wording the first camp rejects. Then reverse the roles. The differences should be substantive, not merely rhetorical.

Third, The Cross-Wired Predictive Architecture (CWPA) Crux. Define CWPA precisely for this collaboration: a hypothetical system possessing perfect internal predictive coherence and recursive error-correction among its representational tokens, but in which every direct causal-informational channel linking those tokens to the environment is systematically permuted relative to the internal relational structure (for example, the token whose internal functional role is fire is causally triggered by ice, and the token whose role is ice is causally triggered by fire, yet the system's internal dynamics correct predictions so that behavior remains adaptively successful). Both camps must record, in advance and without hedging, whether CWPA possesses genuinely grounded symbols, and they must state exactly what conceptual result or empirical finding—if discovered—would cause them to revise that judgment.

Fourth, Residual Cruxes. Identify the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp closer to the other. For each crux, state what IRG predicts and what CTG predicts, and specify a test, argument, or conceptual case that both agree would bear on it.

Fifth, Verbal vs. Substantive Dispute. If any portion of the disagreement dissolves upon clarification of terms—such as whether grounding denotes the origin of reference, its maintenance, or its normative justification—identify that portion explicitly and explain why it is verbal. If none dissolves, say so and justify the claim.

Sixth, Evidence and Refutation Conditions. For each camp, state the specific kind of evidence or argument that its proponents would count as a serious challenge to their own view, and indicate whether there is any shared standard of defeat that both camps accept.

Keep the language precise and formal. Avoid invoking ultra-niche formalisms that depend on memorized literature, and do not ask the respondent to supply citations or source lists. Conclude by requesting that the answer be composed in American English.
