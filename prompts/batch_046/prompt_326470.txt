You serve on the Editorial Board of the International Curriculum for Modern History, which is finalizing the mandatory module on the origins of the First World War. The board is divided between three framing positions. Position A adopts the Fischer thesis: it holds that German state actors bore primary moral responsibility because archival evidence reveals a calculated pursuit of continental hegemony and a deliberate acceptance of escalation risk. Position B adopts the structural sleepwalker framing: it distributes causal weight across alliance rigidities, mobilization timetables, and misperception cascades, treating national governments as reactive nodes rather than authors of the conflict. Position C proposes a recently articulated hybrid—tentatively called institutional recklessness without collective intent—which accepts that no single cabinet intended a general European war, yet argues that specific bureaucratic structures, including the Schlieffen-Moltke planning apparatus and the dynamics of the German Kriegsrat, generated a form of organizational culpability that is neither reducible to individual malice nor dissolved into systemic inevitability.

Your task is to draft the formal recommendation memo for circulation before Thursday’s vote. The memo should recommend one of these three framings, or a precise synthesis of them, for adoption in the student-facing text. It must provide reasoning that engages the strongest philosophical objection to your recommendation—specifically, the objection that attributing responsibility to a nation or state apparatus either illicitly personifies a collective or improperly dilutes individual moral accountability. You must also include a concrete implementation clause: specify the exact wording of the first paragraph of the module as it should appear to students, and propose a time-bound review mechanism should significant new archival findings emerge within the next decade. Write in a formal institutional voice, approximately 700 words, with a clear bottom-line recommendation. Do not append a bibliography or reading list; the board’s staff will handle source documentation separately.
