Here is my philosophical profile on adaptationism and the spandrels critique, for a consistency check before I present it: (1) I accept the spandrel argument in its architectural form—many phenotypic traits are necessary byproducts of selection on other structures, analogous to the triangular spaces that arise between arches (whether true spandrels in rectangular bays or pendentives supporting circular domes like that of San Marco), filled with functional significance only after the fact rather than designed for it. (2) I defend evolutionary psychology's adaptationist methodology for complex cognitive capacities—language, face recognition, theory of mind—holding that their functional specificity and cross-cultural stability are best explained by direct selection for those roles, not by spandrel origin. (3) I maintain that developmental and genetic constraints are themselves evolutionary products—either prior adaptations or spandrels of earlier adaptive architectures—so they do not constitute an independent, non-adaptive source of biological form. (4) I take exaptation to be widespread: a spandrel can be co-opted for a new function and subsequently refined by selection, and once co-opted, adaptationist analysis applies legitimately to that new functional role, regardless of the trait's original non-adaptive origin. (5) I deny that there is a deep, ontologically fixed distinction between adaptations and spandrels; the boundary is pragmatic, time-relative, and shifts with which functional description is currently under selective scrutiny, meaning any trait can in principle be redescribed across the divide.
