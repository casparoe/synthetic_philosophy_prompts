i keep seeing people compare the thucydides trap to two cars at an intersection with broken lights -- both drivers can brake or speed up, crash is worst case, so conflict is inevitable unless one swerves. they use this to argue US-China rivalry is structurally doomed unless one side "yields". i want to audit it properly: what's the correspondance table? drivers = states? broken lights = absence of global authority? braking = strategic restraint? acceleration = arms buildup? crash = hot war? then where does the analogy break -- are states unitary drivers, is there a hidden traffic cop (alliances, institutions?), does it assume perfect information about intentions? and what false premises does the car-image quietly import about rationality and visibility? does the inevitability conclusion survive once those are on the table, or is the analogy just dressing up a standard security-dilemma model in mechanical clothing? also compare briefly to the Munich analogy people deploy for the same rivalry -- does the intersection framing hide different things than Munich hides, and is one of them doing more dishonest work?
