Design a thought experiment set in an ordinary workplace or social setting—no drugs, brain stimulation, futuristic technology, or clinical conditions—engineered so that qualitative hedonism and attitudinal hedonism deliver opposite verdicts on two mental states. Specify State A as the warm, expansive affective tone that accompanies the sudden realization that a deeply held professional belief is false, experienced by a subject who actively resists the realization, seeks to suppress it, and refuses to welcome or pursue the feeling; specify State B as the cool, sustained, effortful satisfaction of methodically correcting a trivial data entry, entirely lacking that warm affective tone, yet deliberately sought, maintained, and welcomed by the subject. Present the narrative in one paragraph. Then provide the check: for each theory, state its verdict on State A and State B; identify the comparison class or nearby worlds each theory uses; and show precisely why qualitative hedonism classifies State A as pleasure and State B as not-pleasure, while attitudinal hedonism reverses both. Isolate the structural feature forcing the divergence—the decoupling of phenomenal character from pro-attitude—and explain whether removing that decoupling collapses the case back into agreement. Include defensive moves for both sides: show how a qualitative hedonist can argue State A is derivative or masked phenomenology rather than genuine pleasure, and how an attitudinal hedonist can argue State B is mere satisfaction or approval rather than pleasure proper. In your response, clearly separate what is settled about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism from what remains genuinely contested among specialists in philosophy of mind and value theory, and do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
