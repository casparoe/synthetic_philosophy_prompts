I'm preparing the interview guide for a philosophy-of-social-science podcast. Next week's guest is an unnamed defender of the view that generational cohorts—Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z—constitute real ontological kinds, specifically "demographic-structural kinds" constituted by shared exposure to a singular demographic-transition regime during an impressionable window, rather than being merely pragmatic age-period brackets or marketing constructs. She frequently deflects methodological objections by insisting that measurement difficulty is not metaphysical failure, and she tends to treat cross-national variation in cohort effects as noise rather than as potential falsification.

Please draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that press on the actual content of her position and make that deflection progressively harder to maintain. I want at least one question addressing the age-period-cohort identification problem and whether cohort parameters can ever be fixed without arbitrary constraints; one on the impressionable-years hypothesis and whether formative events actually generate shared dispositional structures rather than just shared retrospective narratives; one on whether her ontology commits her to essentialist membership conditions for generations; and one specifying what empirical result—say, a sustained null in rigorously controlled cross-national cohort-comparison studies—would genuinely undermine the reality of these kinds rather than merely indict our instruments. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer.

Present the questions in numbered order, with each follow-up clearly paired and indented beneath its question. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or bibliographic references. At the very end, provide a one-sentence summary of the overall interview strategy as the final line.
