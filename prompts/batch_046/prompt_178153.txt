ok so here's roughly where i stand on the metaphysics of propositions and i want a consistency audit before i present it to the grad reading group. tell me if it hangs together or if i'm helping myself to incompatible stuff.

(1) i think bradley's regress is genuinely fatal for any ontology where relations are full-blooded entities that also need to be related to their relata — you end up with an infinite stack of relating-relations, and that's a reductio.

(2) i think russell's external-relations reply technically avoids the regress, but it feels like a philosophical cop-out: it stops the infinite stack by just refusing to ask how R actually connects a and b, which leaves the unity of the proposition completely unexplained.

(3) so i think we need a real account of propositional unity. i'm drawn to frege's idea that the predicate is unsaturated — it's not a complete object like the subject, and that asymmetry is what binds the terms into one thought without any extra glue.

(4) but i also think there's no deep ontologial distinction between objects and properties/relations — they're all just entities that can be values of bound variables, and calling predicates unsaturated is mostly a grammatical metaphor that shouldn't be read into the ontology.

(5) and i don't think there's any extra binding fact or state-of-affairs togetherness (like armstrong's) over and above the constituents themselves — if you've got a, R, and b, that's all there is to the fact.
