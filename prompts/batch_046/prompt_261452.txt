ok so i'm trying to map out what i actually think about privacy by running gut-check cases, and my verdicts are all over the place. here's the list.

a govt database that pulls together every public record—tax, property, marriage, court filings—into one searchable profile. every fact was already public, but the aggregation still feels like a privacy breach to me.

a neighbor flying a drone over your fence to film a backyard bbq. also feels like a breach, but a different kind—more like trespass?

my employer makes me wear a biometric band at work that only tracks heart rate, not location, and they anonymize after 30 days. i don't think that's a privacy issue; it's more autonomy or annoyance.

a friend who remembers every embarassing story you told them and repeats it at parties. not a privacy violation, just them being a jerk.

now the pair that confuses me: an AI trained on all my public blog posts that generates convincing imitations of my writing style, without asking. that feels like a privacy breach, even though the data was fully public. but a human writer who reads all my posts and manually writes in my style? that feels like plagiarism or flattery, not privacy. they look almost identical but my gut puts them in seperate categories.

then there's these memory broker firms i just read about—companies that package your public social media history into personality profiles and sell them to employers. feels wrong, but is it wrong like the drone or wrong like the AI?

last case i haven't decided: a neural interface used in public transit that logs your emotional reaction to billboards, but deletes the video feed immediately and only keeps aggregate stats. privacy issue or not?

so is there one principle—maybe about informational norms, control over context, or synthesis versus access—that gets all these? in particular, what's the real difference between case 5 and 6? why does aggregating public records (case 1) feel different from my friend repeating stories (case 4)? apply whatever principle you come up with to the neural interface case, predict what it says about a public library tracking borrow patterns and selling them to advertisers (case i haven't listed), and tell me honestly which of my verdicts i have to drop if i want something coherent and not too baroque.
