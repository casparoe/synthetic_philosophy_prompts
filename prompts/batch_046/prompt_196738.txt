I was reading an essay in an AI-alignment discussion forum that frames what it calls the "Löbian obstacle" to self-modifying rational agents, and I am trying to determine how much of its argument survives careful scrutiny. The author summarizes the problem like this: Löb's theorem establishes that in any formal system strong enough to represent arithmetic, if the system can prove that provability implies truth for a given statement, then it proves that statement outright regardless of its actual content. From this, the author draws a direct parallel to artificial agents: if an agent has full access to its own source code and can verify that its future self will only take safe actions, then by Löbian reasoning it has already verified its own safety independent of the inspection, which collapses the distinction between evidence and conclusion. Consequently, the essay claims, a fully rational agent that understands its own architecture can never genuinely accumulate evidence that its future versions will remain aligned; it can either treat its safety as an unexamined axiom, or inspect itself but thereby forfeit any rational basis for trusting the result. The author concludes that this makes robust self-trust structurally impossible for any agent with logical strength comparable to Peano arithmetic, and that proposed remedies—such as deliberately restricted architectures or protocols that permanently obscure parts of one's own reasoning—are not genuine workarounds but tacit admissions that the obstacle is insurmountable.

This sounds mostly coherent to me, though I have heard that Löb's theorem is frequently conflated with Gödel's second incompleteness theorem, and that applying formal provability results to practical belief and behavioral alignment involves premises that are not in the theorem itself. I am inclined to think the author overstates how directly the logical result constrains an agent's evidential relationship to its future actions. Could you audit this summary? Specifically: which parts correctly capture Löb's theorem and its standard interpretation in the logic of provability, and where does the summary slide into Gödelian territory or confuse consistency proofs with predictions about behavior? Are the premises about "inspecting source code" and "accumulating evidence" genuinely part of the Löbian obstacle as discussed in the literature on reflective agents, or are they imported assumptions about epistemic access? Finally, does the conclusion that self-trust is structurally impossible actually follow from the formal result, or does it depend on misrepresenting what a rational agent needs in order to trust its future self—particularly if we distinguish between proving one's own consistency and having defeasible, probabilistic confidence in one's continued alignment?
