I'm a high school senior who has been asked to deliver remarks at the retirement ceremony for Dr. Elena Vasquez, who taught philosophy and logic at my school for thirty years. The audience will be sharply divided: her family and many current students have never encountered decision theory, while several former students in the room are now professional philosophers and rational-choice theorists who will notice every imprecision. I want you to write a speech for me, strictly no more than 400 words, that uses the absent-minded driver problem as its actual structural skeleton rather than as a decorative analogy. Work specifically with a modified version of the puzzle: the driver knows he will suffer partial amnesia about which highway exit he has already passed, but he has also left himself a handwritten note in the glove compartment whose authorship he can no longer verify—he cannot tell whether it was written by his earlier, more cautious self or by his later, more reckless self. At the first intersection he must therefore decide whether to take the exit now—accepting retirement with an incomplete record—or continue driving toward the second intersection, where the payoff is larger but where he will have lost even the capacity to read the note with any confidence. The speech should apply this backward-reasoning logic to honor Dr. Vasquez's choice to retire now rather than wait for one final, perfect proof of her work's value. You must explain the induction step clearly enough that a parent in the audience can follow the reasoning, yet precisely enough that an expert will not wince at a misstatement. Do not cite specific papers, invoke named theorems beyond the problem itself, ask for further reading, or refer to obscure formalisms; let the philosophical idea do all the structural work of showing why taking the exit at this moment is rational rather than premature. Conclude by addressing Dr. Vasquez directly, resolving the paradox in her favor: the exit she takes now is the one her past self, writing in good faith and with full recall, intended for her to take.
