I'm an ombuds for a small disability-services nonprofit and I have to mediate about two disputes a month between clients (adults with cognitive disabilities) and thier family caregivers over whether the client should do something they resist — like going to a day program, taking meds, or letting a new aide into thier home. I want a reusable decision procedure grounded in care ethics, but I need it to deal with what I'm calling "attentional bleed": the caregiver's burnout or anxiety makes them misread the client's resistance as pure danger, and care ethics usually doesn't tell you how to correct for that distortion in real time. Write me a 20-minute procedure with ordered questions, clear stop-points where the answer is "back off / no coercion," and explicit flags for where I'm just substituting my own values for care. Run it on three cases: (1) a flu-shot refusal where the caregiver is terrified of illness, (2) skipping a mandatory social program the parent calls "for your own good," and (3) a burned-out caregiver pushing for more support than the client wants. Make sure one case comes back with "do nothing" even though my gut says intervene, and say outright where the procedure will misfire — especially when the caregiver is articulate and the client communicates nonverbally. No bibliography needed, just the steps and the worked cases.
