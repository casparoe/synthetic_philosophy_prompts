hey so i've been obsessing over buridan's ass - the donkey that starves between two equal hay bales because it can't find a reason to pick one. i tried to formalize it and now it feels like it destroys free will for every random choise we make.

(1) if two options are in perfect equillibrium - same value, same cost, literally every factor tied - then there's zero rational basis to prefer either.

(2) but if there's zero basis, then picking one can't be an act of will. it has to be a random glitch, like a coin flip in your brain.

(3) buridan's ass dies because it waits for a reason that never arrives. so a rational agent should also freeze when facing true ties.

(4) now say i actually do pick the left bale, or i decide to think about apples instead of oranges even though both are equally interesting to me right now.

(5) if i made a pick, then either i had a hidden reason (so they weren't really tied) or it was just that random glitch (so i didn't freely choose).

(6) so "free choice between equals" is impossible: either perfect equality is fake or freedom is fake for arbitrary decisions.

(7) but i do this constantly - i pick a checkout line, i start walking with my left foot. it feels free.

(8) therefore either free will is an illusion for all small arbitrary choices, or perfect mental equilibrium never actually happens in real brains.

this proves way too much, right? i want to keep (1) and (7) - we really do face tied options and we really do choose freely. but (5) feels like a trap i'm falling into. which step is the weak link? and if i reject it, am i forced to say randomness can be part of will, or that hidden reasons are always lurking?
