Philosophers of biology have argued for decades over Karl Popper’s claim that Darwinian natural selection risks tautology—since fitness seems defined by survival, and survival by fitness—without reaching resolution. One camp holds that Popper correctly exposed a logical circularity in adaptationist reasoning that can immunize evolutionary explanations against empirical refutation by allowing post-hoc trait attribution; the other camp replies that fitness is operationalized through heritable variation and differential reproductive success measured independently of particular outcomes, generating predictions that are genuinely risky, and that Popper’s subsequent recantation—reclassifying Darwinism as a metaphysical research programme rather than a scientific theory—merely relocated the problem rather than solving it. Despite this, the debate has not advanced: the same examples (industrial melanism, antibiotic resistance, neutral drift) are rehearsed, and neither side revises its core judgment. My working hypothesis is that the stall is not primarily about evolutionary biology but about a deeper mismatch: one side treats evolutionary explanations as law-like mechanisms subject to universal falsification conditions, while the other treats them as singular historical reconstructions whose evidential standards are closer to those of geology or cosmology, with Popper’s demarcation criterion—designed for physics—applied unevenly across these categories. Diagnose the disagreement precisely: is it verbal (a dispute over the terms tautology, fitness, or scientific), substantive (a real conflict over what counts as empirical risk in historical explanation), or a case of two groups answering different questions—one about logical form, one about evidential practice—under the single heading of whether Darwinism is scientific? If there is a hidden disagreement about whether falsifiability requires predictive novelty, logical consistency with counterfactual outcomes, or independence from metaphysical framing, make that explicit. Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress? Would it take a formal analysis of testability in evolutionary models, a conceptual distinction between a metaphysical framework and the falsifiable theories it generates, a clarification of how demarcation criteria apply to contingent historical narratives versus universal laws, or should we conclude that the debate is structurally stalled because Popper’s framework was never meant to evaluate sciences of this kind?
