Write a dialogue between two philosophers—Mara and Lin—who disagree over whether Gödel's rotating-universe metric genuinely supports his conclusion that objective time is unreal. Mara argues that closed timelike curves in such a universe allow an observer to revisit an earlier point in their own world-line, which demonstrates that temporal order is path-dependent rather than globally fixed, and therefore undermines any absolute distinction between past and future. Lin counters that local temporal orientation remains invariant along every world-line, so the absence of a global time coordinate is fully compatible with a local reality of becoming; she accuses Gödel of sliding illegitimately from the failure of global synchronization to the non-existence of time itself. Press the disagreement further on a specific, less-discussed point: whether an observer who completes such a loop could accumulate distinguishable evidence of having returned, and whether that evidence creates an internal tension—since the observer's mental states at the point of return must be causally continuous with their departure states, suggesting a deterministic closure that sits uneasily with Gödel's rationalist belief that the universe is fully intelligible and contains no brute contingency. Require genuine back-and-forth: each speaker must respond directly to the other's preceding objection, and neither should be reduced to a strawman. The dialogue should stand alone without citations, bibliographies, or references to specific editions of Gödel's writings.
