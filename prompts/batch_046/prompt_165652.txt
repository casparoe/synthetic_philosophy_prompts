I've been chewing on this idea about "one thought too many" that I read about in some philosophy thing—basically that sometimes you should just do something because it's who you are, not because you ran the numbers. Here are my gut reactions, and I can't figure out the pattern for the life of me.

If my partner and a stranger are both drowning, I save my partner, no question. But if I literally paused to think "well my partner's life has more value to me," that'd feel creepy—like I'm treating her as a data point. So far so good.

But then: I decided not to have kids partly because I did back-of-envelope math on carbon and suffering. And that felt... okay? Not wierd at all. But when I chose to stay near my mom instead of taking a better job across the country, I didn't do any math, I just said "she's my mom." That also felt right. So why is calculus okay for big life choices but weird for split-second ones?

Another one: I lied to my friend that her painting was good. I did it because I thought "if I'm honest now she'll be crushed and never paint again, but if I lie she keeps going and maybe improves." That reasoning felt totally normal, not like one thought too many. But if I'd lied because I calculated "the social cost of her sadness exceeds the value of honesty," that'd feel wrong somehow, even though it's the same result.

Then there's my warehouse job. I refused to rat out a coworker for stealing small stuff. I just felt like "we're in this together." But my boss said I should have weighed the harm to the company. Doing that weighing—would that be the extra thought? And here's the part that confuses me: my neighborhood fought a development project not because anyone calculated property values, but because "this is our place." That felt like integrity. But then we hired a lawyer who did exactly that calculation, and that was fine too—just practical. So is the extra thought only bad when it's in your own head?

Can you tell me what principle, if there is one, gets all these? Or do I just have to drop one? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on—whether I should donate a chunk of my savings to malaria nets. Part of me says "do it, the math is clear," part of me says "no, I'm saving for my sister's education, and running that comparison is exactly the wrong way to think about family." Which part is right, and what would your principle predict if I told you I'm also considering adopting a dog mainly because "it'd complete my life project" versus "it'd reduce net suffering"? Definately need help here.
