You serve on your university’s autonomous-systems oversight committee. A robotics group requests approval to deploy an autonomous laboratory assistant, System Kappa, which addresses the frame problem not by enumerating all action effects but by maintaining a dynamic registry of “negative entitlements”—context-sensitive licenses to treat specified state-changes as inert without computing them. The registry updates via non-monotonic rules triggered when causal-salience thresholds are crossed. Proponents argue this implements a philosophically defensible boundary between relevant and irrelevant consequences; critics warn it is merely an opaque heuristic that could license catastrophic omissions if an unregistered interaction crosses a hidden threshold. Draft the committee’s formal decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—recommending a concrete course of action with an enforceable timeline and safeguards appropriate to a shared research environment. The memo should treat the frame-problem reasoning seriously, include the strongest consideration from the opposing side, and specify conditions reviewers and operators could actually verify without relying on proprietary black-box audits.
