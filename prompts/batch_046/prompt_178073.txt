Write a joke about Moore's paradox, but skip the tired "it's raining and I don't believe it" opener. Instead, build the punchline around the omissive form — p and I don't believe p — versus the commissive form, p and I believe not-p. The joke should land only if the listener sees that the commissive version isn't paradoxical at all; it's just being flat wrong about reality, whereas the omissive one is the genuinely weird self-undermining assertion. Have your character confuse the two and try to use them interchangable like logical cheat codes — maybe to weasel out of a bet or avoid updating their beliefs after losing an argument. Keep it casual, like you're pitching it to grad students who've just done the Wittgenstein reading.
