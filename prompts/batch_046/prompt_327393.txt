The analogy Douglas Hofstadter draws in Gödel, Escherich, Bach between Gödel's self-referential arithmetical formulas and the emergence of self-aware minds has been contested since the book appeared, yet the dispute seems to reproduce the same moves without convergence. One camp treats the strange loop as a structural homology: just as a formal system can encode statements about its own provability through Gödel numbering, a sufficiently complex symbol-manipulating substrate can generate an I through hierarchical self-modeling, with consciousness emerging as an epiphenomenal pattern rather than a biological primitive. The opposing camp regards this as a category mistake—formal self-reference operates on syntactic strings via arbitrary numerical encoding, lacking intentionality, phenomenal content, and first-person access, so that mapping it onto mental self-awareness confuses representation with representation-of, or syntax with semantics. After decades of repetition, the exchange rarely advances beyond these two postures. Diagnose the stall: is the disagreement substantive—about whether substrate-independent formal patterns can explain phenomenal consciousness—or is it verbal, turning on incompatible uses of self-reference, emergence, and explanation? Might the sides be answering different questions—one about logical possibility, the other about biological implementation—under a single heading? Or does a hidden empirical premise about neural symbol-processing do the work that neither side argues for, and do both camps share an unstated representationalist framework that makes the dispute circular? Present your diagnosis by engaging what each camp actually commits to, then specify what would count as progress given that diagnosis: a formal result separating syntactic from semantic self-reference, an empirical finding about neural self-modeling, a conceptual distinction that dissolves the analogy, or an argument that the question is ill-posed because it presupposes consciousness needs an explanation of the kind formal analogies can provide.
