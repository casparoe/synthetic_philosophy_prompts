Write the dedication address for the reopening of the former Meridian State Psychiatric Hospital, now converted into the Meridian Archive and Testimony Center. You are speaking to a deliberately mixed audience: former residents and their families, psychiatrists and medical historians, local policymakers, and community organizers who have long contested the institution's legacy. Take a committed, definite position—do not present the conflict between clinical records and lived testimony as a debate to be balanced, and do not fall back on platitudes about multiple truths or healing through dialogue alone. Instead, argue that the hospital's clinical archive operates as an instrument of hermeneutical capture: its diagnostic categories—treatment resistance, non-compliance, chronic deterioration—were constructed to make institutional harm epistemically invisible, and they continue to function that way whenever testimony is forced into their vocabulary. Introduce the idea of testimonial friction: the necessary resistance that occurs when a speaker refuses to translate experience into the listener's ready-made categories, even at the cost of being dismissed as incoherent or uncooperative. Use this framework to structure the speech: explain why the testimony center must not be treated as a human supplement to the archive, but as a site that exposes the conditions under which the archive acquired authority. Include one concrete, invented comparative example—such as a clinical entry describing a patient's refusal of sedation as agitated behavior requiring restraint versus that same refusal understood as an act of bodily self-preservation—without naming real individuals or citing specific case files. The speech must be rigorous enough that historians and clinicians in the room recognize the philosophical stakes, but spoken in language accessible to family members with no academic background. End with a specific, non-generic institutional commitment—for instance, that all oral testimonies in the center will be catalogued using the speakers' own descriptive terms rather than the hospital's original diagnostic taxonomy, and that researchers accessing the clinical records must complete a guided orientation on hermeneutical capture before receiving access. Take a definite stand: the purpose of this center is not reconciliation between record and memory, but the exposure of the power that made one count as knowledge and the other as noise.
