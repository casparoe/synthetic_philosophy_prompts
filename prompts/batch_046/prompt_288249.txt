A friend has constructed an argument about dispositions that seems to prove that nothing can be genuinely disposed to do anything, and I cannot locate the misstep. The argument proceeds as follows. (1) On the counterfactual analysis, O has disposition D (manifestation M in conditions C) only if, holding fixed the actual laws and O's intrinsic categorical basis B, O manifests M in all nearby worlds where C obtains and no interfering factors are present. (2) For any proposed B, one can construct what she calls a "counter-dispositional matrix": a law-governed, physically possible structural embedding of B (within O or its immediate environment) that requires no alteration to B's intrinsic nature yet systematically prevents M in C. (3) The world in which B is present inside this matrix is modally closer to actuality than any world in which B is absent, since the difference lies only in the arrangement of parts that do not constitute B itself. (4) Therefore, for every B there exists a closer world in which C holds, B is present, and M fails. (5) So B never supports the counterfactual required for D, and since B was arbitrary, no intrinsic categorical property grounds any disposition. (6) Consequently, scientific appeals to solubility, fragility, or charge are either false or not grounded in objects' intrinsic properties, leaving only an untenable eliminativism. She concludes that the counterfactual analysis, combined with the physical possibility of embedding matrices, refutes itself. I suspect the error lies between the modal comparison in (3) and the grounding failure in (4)—perhaps an illegitimate slide from "a closer interfering world exists" to "the basis fails to ground the disposition"—but I cannot articulate the cost of blocking that slide. Which step should be rejected? Does saving dispositions require revising the modal similarity metric so that worlds containing ad hoc blocking structures count as more distant, or does it require treating the matrix as itself an interfering factor that must be excluded from conditions C, and if so, does that render the analysis circular by defining dispositions in terms of the absence of precisely those conditions that falsify them?
