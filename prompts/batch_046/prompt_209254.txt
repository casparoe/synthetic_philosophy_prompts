A correspondent sent me this reductio, which injects the notion of doxastic dormancy: a belief can remain held while its justifying account lies unaccessed, even offloaded to an external notebook. (1) For Socrates, knowledge is true belief tethered by an account (logos); true belief lacks this tether and can flee away. (2) To actually tether a belief, the account must actively constrain it, not merely exist in principle. (3) If the belief is dormant—held in sleep, or stored externally—the account is not actively constraining it, so in that state it is only true belief. (4) Therefore every belief spends most of its life as mere true belief, with knowledge flickering only during moments of active recall. (5) So knowledge is not stably superior; it is a brief interruption in a continuous sea of true belief. (6) This contradicts the Meno intuition that knowledge is a durable, distinctly valuable condition. I think the error is between (2) and (3), but I want a precise diagnosis: which step should be rejected, and what does giving it up cost?
