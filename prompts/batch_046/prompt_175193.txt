So I spent thirty years running a chemistry lab and now I'm poking around in philosphy of science, and I'm stuck on whether emergence is real or just a fancy word for "too complicated to calculate."

Here are my gut verdicts on cases, and I want to know if there's a principle behind them or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Water boiling: it's the average kinetic energy of molecules, so totally reducible, no emergence at all.

Superconductivity: you can't derive the Cooper pairing from a single-electron Hamiltonian, so I call that genuinely emergent, with new causal powers at the macro level.

Fitness of an organism: it's defined by relation to environment and reproductive success, not present in the DNA sequence alone, so emergent at the organism level.

Benzene's delocalized pi system: it's collective quantum behavior across the ring, but the Schrodinger equation for the whole molecule gets you there, so reducible, just hard math, not true emergence.

Consciousness arising from neurons: I feel there's an explanitory gap, but I don't want to invoke magic, so I put it as weak emergence at best, and I keep waffling.

Downward causation in allosteric enzyme regulation: the overall folded shape changes the chemical potential at the active site. I want to say that's emergence with downward causation, but another part of me says it's just feedback in a reducable system. This is the one I flip-flop on.

The pair that really bugs me: benzene and superconductivity are both collective quantum phenomena, yet I call benzene reducible and superconductivity emergent. What's the real difference? I tried a principle: emergence only counts when the macro-property has causal powers that no subset of the micro-constituents can possess, even with complete information about them. But then benzene's delocalization isn't in any subset of atoms either, so that principle might make benzene emergent too, which clashes with my verdict.

Can you lay out one or more precise principles that capture my pattern? For each clause, point to the case that motivates it. Then tell me what it says about a case I haven't listed -- say, whether the critical point in a ferromagnetic phase transition counts as emergent. And be honest: which of my verdicts have to be thrown overboard? Especially that allosteric flip-flop and the benzene-superconductivity tension.

I'm not looking for a reading list or citations, just the conceptual work. I want to think it through myself.
