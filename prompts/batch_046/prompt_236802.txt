I defend a non-standard resolution to the Bayesian problem of old evidence. Rather than treating old evidence as either already absorbed into the prior or as confirming through an increase in explanatory coherence, I hold that a new theory can restructure the evidential partition itself: previously known facts become evidentially active within a representational framework they could not have occupied before the theory was articulated. What would genuinely count against this position? I want an honest exposure map. Which formal results would show that partition restructuring cannot alter confirmation relations without violating Bayes's theorem? Are there scientific cases where old evidence supports a theory despite no representational shift occurring? Could the view collapse into standard subjectivism, or into an unfalsifiable appeal to framework change? Does it risk insulating itself by allowing restructuring to be postulated whenever old evidence appears to confirm? Distinguish decisive defeaters from merely awkward ones, and indicate whether defenders of this approach should concede that the restructuring move renders the view too cheap to be informative.
