I'm prepping questions for a podcast Q&A with a philosopher who's pushing the Dennett-style line that the self is just a center of narrative gravity — not a soul, just the point where our stories happen to converge. I want to make their usual "that's a feature, not a bug" deflecion actually work for them, round by round.

Start with split-brain cases: left hemisphere spins one story about why the hand reached, right hemisphere spins a different one, and there's no master narrator sitting above them. Ask whether that means there's literally two centers of gravity in one skull, and if they say bodily integration keeps it unified, press them on what "integration" amounts to — because if it's just behavioral coherence and shared logs, then a distributed AI running on separate servers with one autobiographical feed would also count as one self, which feels wrong.

Then go after the gravity metaphor itself. If the self is literally like a center of mass, it's an abstraction calculated from other parts, not a thing in its own right. So ask: is the self just a statistical cluster? They might reply that patterns can be real. Follow up by asking why that pattern should carry moral weight — we don't think a center of mass has rights or deserves blame, so why should a narrative cluster?

Third, hit narrative revision hard. People recover false memories, or therapy totally rewrites their autobiography. If the self is the center of that narrative, then radical editing doesn't update the self — it replaces it. Ask whether the pre-therapy person dies, and if they say continuity lives in the editing process itself, ask what stops that process from being totally arbitrary. Couldn't I deliberately edit my story to become a completely different character and still claim it's me?

Finally, end with a defeat-condition question, not career advice. Ask what actual finding — maybe a neuropsychological case where narrative coherence is intact but selfhood is clearly absent, or an AI that generates flawless first-person narratives with no consciousness — would make them abandon the view entirely, rather than just relabeling it an unfinished research program?

Write this like I'm talking to a colleague over coffee — casual, maybe misspell "autobiograpical" or "phenomological" somewhere. No generic "what drew you to this" stuff.
