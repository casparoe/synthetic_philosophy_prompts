I'm running a workshop for about 14 grad students in AI ethics and philosophy, 90 minutes, and I want an activity that makes them actually feel the tension inside coherent extrapolated volition rather than just nodding at the concept. The idea should hit them in the mechanics, not in my explanation afterwards.

Design a structured deliberation game with three rounds and hidden information. Start them in four small groups with concrete, somewhat selfish preferences about splitting a communal research budget. Round one: each group writes their raw allocation; then they recieve hidden consequence cards (who actually benefits, long-term effects) and must revise — this is 'knowing more.' Round two: groups are paired and must produce a single joint proposal without averaging or voting, only reasoning together — this is coherence. Round three: each group sends one member to a 'future-self' panel, but here's the twist — the panelists are actually other participants who recieved secret instructions to push for logical consistency with the group's original values, not for moral improvement or wisdom. The group must defend their revised budget to this panel, and usually they discover that coherence pulls them back toward rationalizing their initial bias rather than transcending it.

Give me the full setup: materials (index cards, consequence envelopes), timeline broken into 10-minute chunks, social-stakes payoff (the group whose proposal survives the panel gets to actually decide a small real decision for the workshop, like the snack budget), rules that prevent simple compromise, and a debrief mapping each round onto parts of CEV — especially the gap between 'more informed' and 'more who we wish we were.' Tell me what will predictably go wrong: someone will reject the hidden cards, someone will game the coherence round by dominating the partner group. And be honest about which feature of CEV the activity can't reproduce — probably the full computation across humanity — and how I'll supply that verbally. Keep it runnable with no outside readings, no citations, no bibliography.
