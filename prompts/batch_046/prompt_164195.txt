ok so i've been trying to square some stuff about decision-making and i think i'm being inconsistent but can't tell where the leak is.

(1) i'm pretty convinced by Simon-style satisficing: we don't have the compute to maximize expected utility over all options, so we set aspiration levels and stop at good enough. that's not just a practical limitation, it's rational design.

(2) but in forecasting tournaments i act like approximating true probabilities is the whole point, and i spend way more compute on calibration than any pure satisficer should.

(3) i also think bounded rationality means we should use simple heuristcs (like take-the-best) even where we know they violate coherance, because the error-cost of complexity is real.

(4) yet i reject a sharp line between rational and boundd rational — all rationality is bounded, just with different bounds.

(5) here's the weird one: aspiration levels should be set dynamically based on search costs, but there's also an objective fact about whether you've hit the level, independent of your mental state. so i'm not a pure subjectivist about good enough.

do these hang together? specifically, does (2) conflict with (1) and (3), or is there a version of satisficing that allows intense optimization inside a constrained search space? and is (5) actually incoherent — can aspiration levels be both dynamically cost-sensitive and objectively verifiable?

not asking for citations or a reading list, just whether this package is stable or if one of these has to go.
