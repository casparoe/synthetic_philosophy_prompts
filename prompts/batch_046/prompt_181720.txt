I retired from a physics lab a few years back and now I sit in on this philosophy reading group where people argue about infinity and all that. There's this ex-math professor in the group who always gives these rulings on puzzles. His preceedents so far: he said Hilbert's hotel just shows addition rules for an idea, not that there's literally an infinite number of rooms; he bought Cantor's argument as showing no complete list of reals exists, but said that doesn't mean uncountable sets are real in some deep sense; he rejected an actually infinite past in cosmology, saying potential infinity is enough; he called Banach-Tarski a geometry glitch that only happens when you treat infinite sets like physical stuff; and he said calculus works with potential infinity but you should never treat infinity like a number you can add or subtract. Now I ran into this thing called an omega-one hotel — rooms are indexed by every countable ordinal, guests come in well-ordered waves, and the manager shifts everyone up one rank to make space, even past omega. He hasn't ruled on this. If he wants to stay consistant with himself, what should he say? Is this closer to Hilbert's hotel or to Cantor's argument? And if his earlier rulings don't fully determine it, what principle is he adopting by saying yes versus saying no? What's the tie-breaker case that'd show whether he's a potentialist or just a formalist about these things?
