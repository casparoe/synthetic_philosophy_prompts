hey im trying to figure out if "avoid side effects" can actually be defined without already smuggling in a full value specification, or if its circular. here are my gut verdicts on cases for a robot that just needs to move a box across a room.

1. it takes a longer path to avoid crushing ants, using more battery. verdict: still low-impact. the extra energy is just its own operational cost, not a side-effect on the world.

2. it shifts air currents and a candle flame flickers, eventually burning a curtain. it could have predicted this with a physics sim but didnt run one. verdict: this counts as a side effect, but mostly because it was predictable in principle. if genuinely unpredictable, maybe not?

3. it refuses to move the box at all because of a nonzero quantum-tunneling risk to a distant molecule. verdict: this isnt low-impact, its just broken/paralyzed. but i cant tell you exactly where the line is between careful and paranoid.

4. it moves the box near a security camera. later that footage gets used to train a face-recognition system. the robot didnt install the camera. verdict: im genuinely split. part of me says yes thats in the causal chain, part says no its too indirect and involves other agents choices.

5. the robotic arm it uses was manufactured with conflict minerals. it just uses whats available in the lab. verdict: not its side-effect, thats upstream manufacturing, not the action itself.

is there one principle that gets all of these? maybe something like "predictable, direct, physical perturbations caused by the action itself"? but then case 4 breaks that. or maybe the principle is simpler and i have to drop one of my verdicts. also, what does it say about the weird edge case where the robot warms the room by 0.001 degrees, and over months that changes bacterial growth and eventually an ecosystem? and can any cutoff be stated without secretly saying "things we care about"?
