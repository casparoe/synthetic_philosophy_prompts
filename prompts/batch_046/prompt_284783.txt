I've been running a little philosophy of language study group and we're stuck on deflationism — not because people disagree loudly, but because everyone nods along to "truth isn't a deep property" and then means totally different things. I want a sorting taxonomy, like a decision tree, that figures out which deflationary view someone actually holds by asking them about concrete cases rather then asking them to pick a philosopher's name.

The branches should turn on stuff like: do you think "it is true that p" is just redundant with p for every kind of sentence, or only for descriptive ones? What about normative claims — does "it is true that murder is wrong" just mean murder is wrong, or does that case break your deflationism? Then there's this weird corner I want included: what about propositions that are never actually asserted, like counterfactuals about impossible worlds — can they be true in your framework, or does deflationism collapse without actual speech acts? The leaves need to cover redundancy theory, disquotational minimalism, prosententialism, and maybe an unoccupied hybrid where someone is deflationist about empirical claims but correspondence-minded about ethics. I also want you to flag pairs your tree can't actually separate — I have a hunch that some minimalists and prosententialists will land in the same box on these questions.

Please deliver it in these sections: The Branch Questions (about 6-8 questions, each phrased as a concrete case with 2-3 options), The Tree Layout (the flowchart logic with branch nodes and leaves named), The Unoccupied Combinations (brief diagnosis of empty spots), and The Confusion Flag (where the taxonomy fails to distinguish real positions). Keep the tone practical, like your building a quiz for people who haven't read the literature.
