At a meetup, two people I know got into an argument about gradual mind uploading. Jordan said that if you replace a person's neurons one by one with functionally identical artificial substrates, personal identity is preserved—there is no abrupt cutoff, only a smooth trajectory like repairing a ship plank by plank, and the final synthetic brain is still the same person. Maya objected that this ignores dynamical coherence: conscious experience depends on phase-locked oscillatory coupling across cortical regions, and if replacements happen asynchronously, intermediate states will contain mismatched temporal dynamics between biological and silicon substrates. That breaks the unified field of consciousness, so the original stream dies in the hybrid phase and is replaced by a numerically distinct system that merely inherits the data. Jordan replied that Maya is treating coherence as a non-functional ghost: if the chips eventually reproduce the same synchronization patterns, every relevant computation is continuous, and demanding an unobservable causal essence is just dualism in disguise. Maya came back that Jordan confuses functional replication with causal continuity—the original person requires an unbroken chain of the right kind, and the coherence rupture in intermediate stages severs that chain regardless of the endpoint. They asked me to referee. Please adjudicate their exchange move by move: for each step, say whether Jordan or Maya is more right, and identify what, if anything, both are missing—for instance, whether they conflate token continuity with type replication, or whether there is a principled way to distinguish damaging asynchrony from benign functional substitution.
