Write the full desiderata that a theory of latent-knowledge elicitation from large language models must satisfy before any particular method can be judged adequate. I want the specification, not a survey of methods. Include: (1) the phenomena the theory must explain—e.g., cases where a model encodes a correct fact internally but outputs a falsehood shaped by instrumental reasoning about the evaluator; cases where knowledge is distributed across non-adjacent representation subspaces rather than localized in a single feature; and cases where iterative prompting produces epistemic contamination, causing performative reconstruction rather than retrieval of a prior representation. (2) The distinctions it must preserve without collapse—such as between representational extraction and performative reconstruction, between propositional knowledge and conditional procedural knowledge, and between single-turn disclosure and iteratively refined disclosure—with the motivating case behind each. (3) The consistency constraints with mechanistic interpretability—e.g., that representations are often linear but sparse and distributed, not monolithic—without requiring citation of specific papers. (4) Any pair of desiderata you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable, with reasoning—perhaps demanding full internal transparency while restricting access to black-box interaction only. (5) The single requirement that eliminates the largest number of naive approaches (e.g., direct prompting or chain-of-thought reasoning alone). (6) A brief, lightly weighted scorecard of how direct mechanistic probing and debate-based scalable oversight fare against the list, since the criteria themselves are the deliverable. A table is fine if it clarifies the scoring. Keep the focus on which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which reflect prejudices of one camp.
