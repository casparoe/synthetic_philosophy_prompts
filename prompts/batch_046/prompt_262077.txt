I am drafting the final examination for a graduate seminar I have been convening—part fiction workshop, part philosophical inquiry—on the architecture of constraint in language and form. I need one essay prompt, together with a model answer of approximately seven hundred words and a marking scheme, that addresses translation under formal constraint through the conceptual framework Douglas Hofstadter develops in Le Ton beau de Marot around Clément Marot's rondeaux. The prompt must not be answerable by reciting a summary of Hofstadter's familiar distinctions between literal and structural fidelity; instead, it should require students to adjudicate a specific constructed case. Introduce the notion of a "refrain shadow": a translation strategy in which the rondeau's repeated refrain lines are rendered not with identical wording but with phonemically analogous phrases that preserve syllabic stress and vowel coloring while altering lexical meaning—so that the ear recognizes the architecture but the mind receives a different emotional register. Ask students whether this practice honors or betrays the formal contract of the rondeau, whether it can be accommodated within Hofstadter's distinction between surface fidelity and structural fidelity, and what it reveals about whether form is primarily a phonetic, syntactic, or semantic object. The model answer should take a clear position, distinguish among phonemic, syntactic, and semantic preservation, anticipate a serious objection—perhaps that altering the refrain's meaning collapses the rondeau's logic of cumulative variation—and resolve it without recourse to bibliographic citation. The marking scheme should total twenty-five points, divided among five must-hit conceptual moves, each with its point value and a brief note on the typical failure mode. The level should be that of an advanced graduate student in comparative literature or philosophy of art; do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, since the examination tests conceptual command rather than archival recall.
