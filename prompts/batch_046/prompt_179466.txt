Write an ironic op-ed of approximately 500 words, in the deadpan voice of a contemporary philosopher writing for a serious intellectual quarterly. The author is a rigorous defender of Montaigne-style cultural relativism, specifically as articulated in "Of Cannibals," and is exasperated that colleagues keep praising him for being tolerant and enlightened about the Tupinambá. Every argument must remain internally consistent: he may not invoke universal human dignity, objective moral progress, cross-cultural rational standards, or any normative claim that transcends local custom, for doing so would undermine his position. The piece should push Montaigne's actual comparison—between ritual cannibalism and European religious warfare, including judicial torture and execution—to its logical extreme, so that the author cannot condemn colonial violence except as an expression of European taste, cannot accept praise for open-mindedness except as a tribal preference, and cannot distinguish describing a practice from endorsing it without smuggling in a universalist framework. By the conclusion, the reader should feel the genuine philosophical pressure of self-refutation: if all moral values are indexed to cultural custom, then the value placed on relativism itself collapses into merely another local habit. Include at least one moment where the author applies this logic to a modern institutional practice—such as human-rights advocacy, decolonial pedagogy, or academic land acknowledgments—to show that by his own lights he can neither endorse nor reject it, only report it. The tone should be weary, pedantic, and never winking; the satire lands only if the internal consistency is perfect. Do not let the author slip into implicit universalism, compatibilism, or any concession that some values might be rationally justified across cultures.
