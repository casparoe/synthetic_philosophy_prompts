Rank the following four stances on the moral relation between suffering and well-being according to how coherently they resolve the tension between incommensurability and trade-off: (A) classical hedonistic utilitarianism, (B) pure lexical negative utilitarianism, (C) threshold lexical negative utilitarianism with a discrete cutoff, and (D) the gradient incommensurability view — on which suffering and pleasure are evaluated along separate axes that permit local trade-offs only within bounded intensity bands but become strictly non-substitutable once either crosses a critical magnitude. For each stance, assign a tier (S, A, B, or C) and justify the placement in one or two sentences, specifically addressing whether the view can explain why a small reduction in severe suffering should outweigh an enormous increase in mild contentment without rendering all pleasure morally irrelevant.
