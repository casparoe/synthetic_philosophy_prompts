student answer:

Quining is stronger than eliminativism. Churchlands-style eliminativists say neuroscience won't find qualia, but Chase and Sanborn say the concept is self-contradictory, like a round square. Qualia are defined as intrinsic - no relational structure - yet we compare them, imagine inverted spectra, and report them. That needs relational access, so the idea collapses on itself.

This dissolves the hard problem. Chalmers asks why physical processes produce experience, but if "experience" packs an incoherent concept there's no real datum to explain. The appearance-reality gap disappears because there's no appearance of qualia at all - just a conceptual error people make when they introspect.

The incoherence-objection uses caloric: caloric was incoherent but heat was real, replaced by kinetic theory. Same with qualia - we'll replace them with functional descriptions. Also this is basically Dennett's heterophenomenology; both deny a private first-person realm, so Chase and Sanborn just extend Dennett's point about first-person reports being unreliable texts.
