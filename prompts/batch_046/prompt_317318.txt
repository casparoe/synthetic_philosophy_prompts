Write a sestina on Dennett’s rejection of the Cartesian theater in favor of the multiple drafts model. Use exactly these six end-words, rotated through the sestina’s formal pattern (stanza 1: 1-2-3-4-5-6; stanza 2: 6-1-5-2-4-3; and so on): draft, theater, binding, revision, witness, stream. In each of the six main stanzas, present a different “draft” of the same brief experiential episode—say, the sudden appearance of a color or the initiation of a movement—so that no stanza claims canonical authority over the others. The binding problem should be rendered precisely: disparate processes should appear to seek coherence, yet there should be no central audience and no single unified narrative. The final envoi (three lines, two end-words per line) must place “theater” and “witness” in the middle line rather than the last, leaving “revision” and “draft” to share the closing line. The poem should be accurate enough that a specialist in philosophy of mind recognizes Dennett’s denial of a homuncular observer and the absence of one definitive stream of consciousness, but it should function as poetry, not exposition. Do not include citations, references to specific publications, or a bibliography.
