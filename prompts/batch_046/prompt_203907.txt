ok so my daughter just started filling out college aplications and im still chipping away at my own loans from grad school, meanwhile my sister in oslo told me her kid pays literally nothing for uni. im trying to figure out what i actually belive about who should pay for college and my gut is all over the place.

heres my verdicts on four setups, and they dont feel consistent:

1. germany/nordics: free at point of use, funded by progressive taxes, you contribute even if you never go. i think this is fair — education is a public good and the tax system already handles ability-to-pay.

2. us: high sticker price, private loans with interest, and bankruptcy is basically impossible. clearly unfair to me — it locks people out based on family wealth and feels like debt peonage.

3. uk: income-contingent loans, you pay 9% of earnings over a threshold for 30 years then its forgiven. i think this is fair too — its like a graduate tax tied to actual benefit received.

4. but then theres this proposal i read about (maybe in norway? not sure) where tuition is free, but if you earn above median income for 10 years after graduating you pay back 10% of salary for 5 years into a pool for future students — a "solidarity contribution". i think this is unfair, even though its progressive. it feels like punishing success and creates weird incentives to hide income.

is there one principle — maybe about risk-sharing, or about not commodifying access to begin with — that delivers all four verdicts? if not, which one should i overturn and why? please also say what the resulting account predicts for a case i havent listed: tuition free for everyone but funded by a flat VAT that hits poor people harder. and be honest about which feature of my four cases is actually doing the work.
