hey, been messing around with this idea i call shadow-adoption — like when a concept slips domains and drags hidden baggage along. here's an argument i cobbled together and it feels like it proves way too much, but i can't see the exit.

(1) If two domains share enough structural grammer, then any concept applying in one will shadow-adopt into the other, baggage and all.
(2) Mechanical repair and software debugging share that grammer: both involve finding malfunction, isolating cause, and restoring function.
(3) In mechanical repair, "wear and tear" is part of the conceptual baggage — repair addresses accumulated wear.
(4) So if "repair" shadow-adopts into software, "wear and tear" must come with it.
(5) But software doesn't wear; it just corrupts or breaks instantly.
(6) So either software can't be "repaired" (false, we say it constantly), or software experiences wear (also false).
(7) Therefore shadow-adoption must be blockable.
(8) But if it's blockable here, it's blockable everywhere — including from human cognition to AI — so there's no prinicpled reason to stop us saying LLMs literally "believe."
(9) So either we accept that LLMs believe, or we admit "repair" in software is just dead metaphor.
(10) Both feel wrong. where does this slip?

i'd really like to hang onto (1) and (2) if i can, but i'm not attached. also, is shadow-adoption even the right frame for copycat concepts, or am i just making up jargan?
