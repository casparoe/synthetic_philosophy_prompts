I've been chewing on that old Chase and Sanborn idea about quining qualia — that qualia are an incoherent posit because they lack proper identiy conditions across counterfactuals — and I accidentally pushed it into what looks like self-refutation. My study group says I'm overthinking it, but I want a sanity check.

Here's the chain I wrote out:
(1) For any concept C to be genuinely coherent, there must be non-circular identiy conditions for C-instances across relevantly similar situations.
(2) Qualia fail this: if I ask whether my red-experience in world W is identical to my green-experience in world W', I can't answer without already presupposing the very qualia I'm comparing.
(3) So qualia are incoherent and should be quined.
(4) But "incoherence" is itself a concept we apply across different arguments — to qualia, to phlogiston, to round squares.
(5) Applying (1) reflexively, we need non-circular identiy conditions for "incoherence" itself: is the failure-mode of qualia the same kind of failure-mode as that of round squares?
(6) There's no independent criterion; we just see family resemblances in how concepts break.
(7) So "incoherence" also fails (1).
(8) Therefore (3) asserts something incoherent by its own lights.

This smells like either a scope slip or an equivocation between object-level and meta-level, but I can't pin the exact move. I really want to hang onto (1) and (2) since they feel like the core of the quining intuition, but I don't want to end up saying we can't coherently claim anything is incoherent. Which premise should I drop, and what's the least ugly cost?
