As a retired marine biologist who has only recently begun serious study in philosophy, I find myself preoccupied by wild animal suffering—above all, the tension between high-fecundity reproductive strategies and our profound epistemic limits regarding subjective experience in non-vertebrate life. Write a short, formally rendered story in which a character closely resembling myself is asked to advise on an intervention in an isolated hydrothermal-vent ecosystem: a previously unknown symbiotic parasite suppresses host mortality while radically altering behavioral ecology. The narrative should turn on moral uncertainty—specifically, whether diminished mortality correlates with diminished suffering, or whether the altered states constitute a distinct, conceptually unrecognized form of harm—without resolving the ambiguity. Let the philosophical stakes emerge through the character's long-trained observational discipline and her resistance to projecting anthropomorphic categories onto an evolutionary history she once measured only in biomass and turnover rates.
