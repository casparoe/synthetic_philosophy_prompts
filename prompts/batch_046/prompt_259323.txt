I am a doctoral student in aesthetics and philosophy of art. In a recent graduate seminar on curatorial ethics, a colleague dismissed my objection to acquiring a particular site-specific installation by saying, "Separate the art from the artist." The installation in question is not merely accompanied by a bad biography; its physical substrate incorporates artifacts that were produced during the artist's documented criminal acts, so that displaying the work arguably extends the visibility and structural presence of the harm itself. I want to bring a precise conceptual breakdown to my advisor's office hours rather than simply repeating the slogan back. Could you lay out, as a menu of distinct theses, what "separate the art from the artist" could actually be claiming—ranging from the trivial and platitudinous to the radically ontological? For each reading, please indicate whether it is defensible, false, or a category mistake; whether it implicitly relies on something like the intentional fallacy, aesthetic autonomism, moralism about artistic value, or institutional neutrality; and, most importantly, which of these readings actually supports dismissing my specific concern, where the artist's immoral actions are materially embedded in the work's conditions of display rather than being external biographical background. I am not asking for a bibliography or citations—just the disambiguated map I need to locate exactly where the disagreement lies.
