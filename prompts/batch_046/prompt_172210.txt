I have been revising a novel that moves between societies with radically different relationships to rules, and I find myself unable to articulate why some feel humane and others tyrannical, despite both being highly ordered. I am hoping you can help me formalize my intuitions, because they feel consistent yet resist the obvious explanations.

Consider these five judgments, all of which I hold with some confidence. First, a society governed by an exhaustive etiquette code—where, for instance, the precise angle of a bow or the sequence of toasts is codified and social exile follows any deviation—strikes me as suffocating and wrong, regardless of how peacefully it functions. Second, a small nomadic community with almost no institutional law except one absolute prohibition, the speaking of the dead's true names, which carries irreversible banishment; this feels not like tyranny but like appropriate reverence, and I would not want it loosened. Third, a contemporary liberal democracy burdened with thousands of regulations—speed limits, zoning codes, tax provisions—where citizens routinely ignore minor infractions without consequence strikes me as healthy, precisely because the rules exist but are not enforced with zeal. Fourth, if that same democracy, after a crisis, began enforcing every ordinance with algorithmic rigor, I would judge it as having crossed into something monstrous, even though the rules themselves had not changed. Fifth, in my own fiction I created the Clockwork Commons, a polity where citizens vote annually on which statutes to suspend, yet the suspension requires unanimous consent and a six-month deliberation; I wrote it as a utopia of democratic flexibility, but on reflection it feels paralytically rigid, though I cannot say exactly why.

Is there a single principle—perhaps about the domain of rules, the cost of deviation, or the presence of deliberative exit—that unifies these verdicts? I am particularly uncertain about the Clockwork Commons case, and I wonder whether my discomfort comes from the form of the mechanism rather than its outcome. Could you also tell me what your proposed principle predicts for a case I have not considered: a society with no written laws at all, where norms are enforced only through spontaneous communal shaming that no individual can initiate alone, but which, once begun, is irrevocable?
