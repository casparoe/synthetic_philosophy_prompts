Paul Benacerraf raised a classic epistemological difficulty for platonism concerning knowledge of causally inert, abstract entities. At the same time, practical successes—such as the use of number-theoretic reasoning in the design of post-quantum lattice-based encryption protocols—seem to indicate that mathematics tracks genuine structural features of the world. Explain Benacerraf's challenge, then compare how a structuralist platonist, working in the tradition of Resnik or Shapiro, and a fictionalist nominalist, working in the tradition of Field, might each account for this kind of applied success. For each position, identify one internal limitation that arises specifically from its own commitments.

The student submitted the following answer. Benacerraf's challenge is that platonist objects exist outside space and time yet remain spatiotemporally located in a non-physical realm, which blocks causal interaction and therefore violates naturalistic epistemology. A structuralist platonist resolves this by treating numbers as positions within abstract structures that are instantiated both in physical computing hardware and in the formal syntax of encryption algorithms, so we come to know them through pattern recognition in these instantiations. A fictionalist nominalist like Hartry Field claims that mathematical statements are literally false descriptions of physical reality, but scientists employ them as convenient fictions that happen to match empirical outcomes in digital security. The central limitation of structuralism is that it still requires direct intellectual intuition of abstract structures, and Benacerraf proved that embodied minds are incapable of such intuition. The central limitation of fictionalism is that if engineers invoke lattice theory in technical reports, they are asserting falsehoods, which means applied mathematics undermines scientific integrity rather than explaining technological success.
