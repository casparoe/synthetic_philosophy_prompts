I've been trying to get my head round luck egalitarianism, the view that it's unfair when people suffer from bad luck they couldn't help but not unfair if it's down to their own choices. I keep noticing something weird that I don't have a word for. Say there's a genetic risk for some illness. If you're born with it, everyone says that's brute bad luck. Now suppose there's a cheap preventative treatment, but you have to opt in to recieve it. Someone doesn't bother and gets sick — people call that option luck, they made a choice not to treat it. But now imagine the treatment is given automatically, and you only get sick if you actively sign a form refusing it. The exact same person, same illness occured, but now people seem to treat it as brute luck again, because they never made an active choice to refuse. So the line between 'choice' and 'circumstance' seems to slide around dependantly on what the default setting is, not on whether anyone actually made a real decision. Is there an established concept for this kind of framing-dependence? How exactly does it sit with the standard brute-luck / option-luck split, and if there's no proper name, how would you define and bound it? I'd rather you explained the carving than just named it — and please, no bibliographies or lists of papers, I won't be able to check them.
