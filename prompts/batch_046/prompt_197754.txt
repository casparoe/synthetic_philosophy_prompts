I'm preparing a three-year research agenda for a small philosophy-of-language group, and we're trying to decide where to concentrate our limited conceptual resources on what I've been calling the "residual Frege problem"—the part of Frege's puzzle about identity statements that survives once you grant that sense determines reference, but still leaves open whether the cognitive surprise of learning that a = b belongs to semantics, to the architecture of rational belief revision, or to some hybrid structure that neither pure Fregeanism nor direct-reference theories capture well. The standard literature often treats this division as settled, but I'm skeptical, especially when you examine cases in mathematics—say, recognizing that a particular topological space and an apparently unrelated algebraic object are the same entity—where the informativeness seems to depend on representational format in a way that doesn't map neatly onto either mental content or bare reference.

We're weighing three lines of inquiry. First, whether hyperintensional contexts in the philosophy of mathematics undermine the sense-reference distinction specifically for identity claims, or whether they can be absorbed without collapsing into full-blown mentalism. Second, whether a genuinely deflationary account of identity can preserve the informativeness of "a = b" without positing Fregean senses as internal representations—perhaps by treating identity discovery as a restructuring of inferential commitments rather than a change in semantic content. Third, whether the puzzle carries any weight for practical reasoning: if recognizing that two descriptions co-refer changes what an agent ought to do in legal or moral contexts, does that show the puzzle is about normative cognition rather than about language at all?

If you were advising this group—three philosophers with no empirical labs, working entirely with conceptual tools—how would you rank these three directions, and which additional open questions in this neighborhood deserve a place in the agenda? I'm less interested in a survey than in a defended ordering: for each item, why it is upstream of or independent from the others, how tractable it is for philosophers rather than cognitive scientists, and what kind of conceptual progress over the next few years would cause you to move it up or down. Please also say which questions in this space you think are genuinely dead ends that only seem interesting because they've been discussed repeatedly, and why our limited time should avoid them.
