I'm trying to figure out what i actually think about Orwellian vs Stalinesque revision, especially where it touches on colour phi and the timing of conscious experience. My intuitons don't quite line up, and i want to see if there's a clean principle or if i have to drop one.

Case one: the standard colour phi setup. Two dots flash in quick succession, different colours and positions. The subject reports seeing one dot glide across and switch colour in the middle. My gut says this isn't revision at all -- it's more that the brain never formed a discrete "red at position A" experience in the first place; the intermediate state is constructed postdictivly once the second flash arrives. So neither Orwellian nor Stalinesque, just delayed assembly.

Case two: a subject watches a smooth animation of a rolling ball, but in one frame it's replaced by a cube for about 40 ms, then back to ball. They report seeing only the ball, no cube. Later they're shown the raw frames and deny ever seeing a cube. Here i feel it's Stalinesque. There was a brief conscious registration of the cube, but the narrative record was overwritten with ball-continuity; the original trace is buried but could in principle be recovered with the right probe.

Case three: same idea, but instead of a visual replacement, a brief tone is played and immediately masked by white noise. The subject reports only hearing noise, never a tone. Brain scans show an early auditory response. Despite the neural trace, my verdict is Orwellian. I don't think there was ever a conscious "tone" experience to erase; the masking prevented consciousness from ever binding the tone, and the later report just fills the gap retroactively. So the past stream itself never contained the tone.

Case four: a subject gets a brief electric shock. Immediately afterwards, a blocker suppresses affective consolidation but leaves sensory traces intact. Five minutes later they recall "there was a sensation but it felt neutral, maybe even pleasant". I think this is Stalinesque again -- the original painful affect was registered and then replaced by a false tag, but the raw sensory memory is still accessible if you probe right.

The tension is between case two and case three: both involve a brief stimulus masked or replaced by later input, both leave neural traces, but i call one Stalinesque and one Orwellian. What's the principle that splits them? Is it about whether global workspace broadcast happened? Or about whether there's a retrievable "source" memory? I also want to know what the principle says about cases i haven't listed -- for instance, in the classic readiness-potential experiment where brain activity precedes the reported decision, does the conscious decision get Orwellian-revised backwards in time, or is the "decision" experience Stalinesquely replaced by a post-hoc story? And what about a split-brain patient where the left hemisphere confabulates a reason for a right-hemisphere action?
