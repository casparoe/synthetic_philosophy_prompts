In arguments over epistocracy versus democracy, defenders of universal suffrage often invoke a jury analogy: the electorate works like a jury in a complex case, where each voter is an imperfect but unbiased sensor of the public good; institutional checks filter out the grossly incompetent the way voir dire does; and aggregation through voting rules cancels individual noise the way jury deliberation produces a verdict more reliable than any single juror’s. The analogy is used to license the conclusion that mass political ignorance is not fatal, because collective mechanisms correct for it without requiring every citizen to be an expert.
