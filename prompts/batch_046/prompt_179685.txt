Write a sestina about the failure of transitivity in comparative value judgments, specifically through a spectrum argument applied to a chain of aesthetic or epistemic goods arranged in barely perceptible gradations. The six end-words should be drawn from successive items along this chain—moving, for instance, from an evidently flawed hypothesis or artwork through stages that differ only minimally—so that across the stanzas the end-words themselves slide one position forward, structurally enacting the sorites shift. In the envoi, do not resolve the tension by asserting equality; instead, land on the idea of parity, where two successive items are neither better, worse, nor equal, but stand on a par. The poem should work first as verse—precise rhythm, controlled repetition, and a clear turn—while being accurate enough that a philosopher of value comparison would recognise the argument being performed structurally rather than merely name-dropped. Please answer in British English.
