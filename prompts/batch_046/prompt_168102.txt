I serve on my university’s Faculty Senate Committee on Ethical Accountability, and we must issue a formal recommendation before the start of the fall term regarding the case of Dr. Elena Vasquez, a senior researcher in cognitive science who has admitted to fabricating data in three published studies. Vasquez does not contest the findings, but she and her advocates propose that the committee adopt what they term a structural reactive-attitude exemption: because the funding metrics, publication pressures, and peer-review incentives she faced constituted a form of institutional coercion that undermined her capacity for the participant stance toward her own conduct, she ought to be regarded with an objective attitude oriented toward rehabilitation rather than blame. Under this proposal, she would enter a monitored restorative-research protocol with no formal censure recorded, rather than facing the standard sanctions of tenure revocation and dismissal. The panel is divided between members who view this as a necessary recognition that systemic conditions can defeat the conditions for moral responsibility, and members who argue that treating her with an objective attitude would collapse the normative structure of scientific practice and implicitly endorse a determinism incompatible with the very idea of institutional accountability.

We need a written opinion that recommends a concrete course—uphold standard sanctions, adopt the restorative protocol without censure, or a defined hybrid with a clear timeline—while engaging seriously with the strongest consideration on the opposing side. In particular, the memo should address whether our disciplinary code presupposes a specific theory of moral responsibility that we are not entitled to assume, and whether an institutional body can coherently hold an objective attitude in Strawson’s sense at all, given that Strawson framed these attitudes as essentially interpersonal. The recommendation should also consider whether recognizing systemic pressure as a defeater for reactive attitudes in this case forces us to extend the same exemption to negligence or recklessness elsewhere in the university, and what that implies for the consistency of our practice. Please draft the document so that it could be circulated to the Senate for a vote, with reasoning that responds directly to the arguments the researchers could actually advance, without appealing to bibliographies or external authorities that would require verification.
