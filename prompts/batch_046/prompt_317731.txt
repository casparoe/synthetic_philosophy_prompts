Design a final-exam essay question for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on twentieth-century existentialism, focused specifically on Sartre's concepts of radical freedom and bad faith. The question must require students to apply these concepts to a synthetic scenario rather than merely defining them: present a case in which a senior civil servant implements a regulatory policy she privately judges to be unjust, defending her actions solely by reference to her official mandate and the stability of the administrative apparatus. Ask candidates to analyze whether this defense necessarily involves bad faith in Sartre's technical sense—that is, whether it requires a specific denial of transcendence, a specific denial of facticity, or a distinct mode of self-deception regarding the structure of choice itself—and to distinguish this form of inauthenticity from mere professional role-distance or from the kind of practical reasoning found in ordinary institutional ethics. Require the answer to address why radical freedom is not negated by external constraint but is instead misrecognized in this case. Provide a model answer of approximately 700 words, and provide a marking scheme built around four must-hit analytical moves, specifying the marks allocated to each (total 25), together with a one-line note on how candidates typically fail at each move.
