Since my father received a diagnosis of early-onset Alzheimer's last month, I have been trying to understand whether the person who eventually will not recognize me is literally identical to the man who raised me. I understand that perdurantists treat him as a four-dimensional worm made of temporal stages, so that the later stage belongs to the same extended object despite lacking my memories, whereas endurantists maintain that he is wholly present at each time. Yet if he is wholly present now with the property of remembering me, and wholly present years from now with the contradictory property of not remembering me, does not endurantism attribute incompatible properties to one and the same entity? The standard reply is that these properties are possessed only at different times, but that sounds like admitting that there are time-indexed parts bearing the properties—which seems to collapse endurantism into perdurantism under another name. Does this mean the problem of temporary intrinsics makes endurantism incoherent, and that any philosophically serious account of identity through progressive neurodegeneration must be four-dimensional?
