You serve as the senior analytical lead for the Historical Contingency Review Board of a multilateral development institution. Your board receives funding proposals for post-conflict reconstruction in which applicants claim that local institutional failure is "historically inevitable" — that no plausible intervention could have altered the trajectory — and therefore that external capital commitment is futile. You must design a reusable, ninety-minute decision procedure your staff — none of whom have training in philosophy of history — can apply repeatedly to evaluate such inevitability claims before signing off on disbursement. The procedure should translate the conceptual distinctions that matter for counterfactual reasoning about macro-social outcomes — specifically structural constraint versus path dependence versus junctional contingency, and the problem of nested counterfactuals of the form "had X not occurred, Y still would have" — into ordered steps with explicit stopping rules, criteria for what counts as sufficient empirical support at each stage, and clearly marked points where analysis ends and irreducibly normative judgment begins. It must prevent unfalsifiable storytelling while remaining honest about where judgment calls are disguised as procedure. Apply the procedure to three proposals: first, a claim that ethnic conflict in a specific region was structurally inevitable given pre-colonial land-tenure patterns; second, a claim that democratic consolidation was impossible given the region's resource-curse dynamics; and third, a negative case in which the procedure mandates rejection of inevitability but the reviewing analyst personally believes the failure was unavoidable — specify exactly where the procedure requires bracketing that belief, what the board should record, and how the disagreement should be escalated. Include worked applications for all three, noting in each case where the procedure gives an answer the applicant will dislike. Conclude by stating what the procedure can and cannot do: which classes of inevitability claims it is calibrated to catch, which types lie outside its scope, and under what conditions it should hand the decision to a person rather than return a procedural answer. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke specific scholarly formalisms, named theories, or dated texts that are not common knowledge; and frame the entire deliverable so that an analyst without web access can execute it with only the conceptual distinctions you have built into the steps themselves.
