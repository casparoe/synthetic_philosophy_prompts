The design must isolate one variable: the interpretive framing of the shock, holding constant the decision-making architecture of the port officials, who retain full deliberative capacity in all versions of the case. Present the scenario in no more than one paragraph, grounded in ordinary administrative procedure rather than catastrophe narrative. Then demonstrate that the case delivers three distinct verdicts—(i) the shock is a mere causal trigger with no agential properties, (ii) it possesses what we might call structural agency without intentionality, and (iii) it operates as a determinative historical actor that renders human choices epiphenomenal—while identifying precisely which feature of the institutional framing forces the divergence between (ii) and (iii). Specify what collapses if that feature is removed, and articulate how a defender of the determinist position (iii) could reject the framing without disputing any empirical causal claim about the shock. Finally, note what this reveals about the perils of environmental determinism when agency is metaphorically projected onto climate or microbial forces, and state which of the three positions your constructed case genuinely favors, with the reasoning visible in the scenario's architecture rather than stipulated.
