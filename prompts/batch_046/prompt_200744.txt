hey, im a backend engineer with zero philosophy training, just reading stuff for fun. i keep running into this idea of real patterns and the three stances — physical, design, intentional — and i think i see a version of it at work but i cant name it.

heres the concrete thing. we have a distributed cache thats designed to do one job, but after months of load it develops an emergent behavior — like automatically shedding low-priority requests — that isnt in any spec. the behavior is totally real and predictable at the system level, but you cant point to one service that was designed for it. so its a real pattern sitting in the design stance, right?

but then when the shedding starts failing, my team doesnt stay in the design stance and ask which module is broken. instead we jump straight to the intentional stance — we start talking about what the cluster "wants" or "believes" about its own health — and we make predictions based on those mental states. when that also fails, we crash down to the physical stance — cpu throttling, network packets — and we completely skip fixing the design-level explanation.

so is there an established name for this specific escalation? not just anthropomorphizing or stance error, but the failure mode where an emergent real pattern that should be handled at the design level gets wrongly promoted to the intentional stance, and then collapses straight to physical without ever being repaired at design? if theres no term, what should it be called, and where exactly does it sit relative to the normal three-stance framework? also, is it different from just misapplying occams razor?
