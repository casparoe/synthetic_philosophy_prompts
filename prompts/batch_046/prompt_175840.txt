hey im on a museum advisory board and we're trying to stay consistent. our gallery is called 'bureaucratic unmaking of persons' and its framed around baumans point that modern rational administration can distance people from moral responsibility when killing becomes an office task. so far we've ruled: yes on the armenian genocide — modern ottoman state used census data and rail logistics, death was the policy endpoint; no on the soviet gulag — huge bureaucracy but death was mostly byproduct of labor and political repression, not the rational endpoint for eliminating a specific group; no on the transatlantic slave trade — racial categorization and huge death toll but driven by economic extraction, not modern state bureaucracy aiming at total elimination; and no on the cambodian killing fields — direct agrarian violence, anti-modern ideology, lacking that administrative distancing even though the state was involved.
