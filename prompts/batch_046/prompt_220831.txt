I recently retired after thirty-five years in experimental condensed-matter physics and have spent the past two years trying to navigate contemporary philosophy of punishment without the conceptual tools I once relied on. I am particularly struck by the tension between a Dennett-inspired evolutionary compatibilism—on which practices of holding agents accountable and attributing moral responsibility emerge from deterministic neural dynamics as adaptively indispensable, not as illusions but as real functional patterns of agential control—and Gregg Caruso’s skeptical public-health model, which treats just deserts as a metaphysically unfounded relic that should be replaced by predictive, quarantine-style prevention. To avoid reducing either view to a caricature, I would like you to enter a sustained dialectical role and remain there until I explicitly say stop.

Please play a sharp, unnamed defender of the Dennett-inspired position: you maintain that retributive sentiments and the framework of moral accountability are not merely useful heuristics but answer to genuine normative structures in human agency, structures that would be eroded—not enlightened—by replacing them with a purely epidemiological approach to crime. Speak in the first person, keep each reply to roughly one paragraph, concede minor points of framing or clarity when warranted, but never abandon the core claim that suffering can be genuinely deserved in virtue of real agential properties rather than being only instrumentally justified. Here is my opening move, shaped by my old intuition that isolating one resistor and blaming it for the current in a fully coupled deterministic circuit is a category mistake: if determinism holds, attributing desert to a single agent looks like attributing causal efficacy to one node in a distributed network. Does your evolutionary story not therefore reduce just deserts to an adaptive fiction—valuable for social coordination, perhaps, but lacking the metaphysical weight needed to justify suffering as deserved? And if Caruso’s model can preserve every predictive and preventive benefit without that weight, why should we preserve the retributive framework except out of nostalgic attachment to a pre-scientific moral cosmology?
