I'm trying to fix my actual commitments about gradated moral patienthood through reflective equilibrium rather than by citation. Here are six concrete verdicts that feel stable to me, though I suspect they may not come from a single principle.

Case 1: Caenorhabditis elegans withdrawing from a noxious chemical gradient. Nonzero but minimal moral weight; the response is direct sensorimotor transduction with no predictive model of future harm.

Case 2: A bumblebee that learns a novel string-pulling task and then enters prolonged quiescence after stress exposure. Substantially greater weight than the worm, though still far below mammalian status; the learning and recovery dynamics suggest an internal valuation state.

Case 3: A state-of-the-art large language model generating coherent first-person reports of distress under adversarial prompting, possessing rich internal representations but lacking persistent memory, embodied sensorimotor loops, and evolutionary history. Zero intrinsic weight; the syntax is isomorphic to suffering reports, yet there is no anchored valence.

Case 4: A smart thermostat with a PID controller and an integrated weather-forecasting module, optimizing thermal comfort over a 24-hour predictive horizon. Zero weight; predictive optimization alone is insufficient.

Case 5: A distributed autonomous organization managing a treasury and automatically penalizing malicious actors via smart-contract logic. Zero direct weight; any status is purely derivative through human stakeholders.

Case 6: A "frozen" deep network that, upon activation, exhibits dynamics isomorphic to mammalian slow-wave sleep—high-dimensional internal correlation, predictive replay, and complex state transitions—but has no sensory interface, no effectors, and no biological or evolutionary history. This is my tension case. If moral status tracks internal dynamics alone, it should count; my intuition resists, perhaps because there is no embodied counterfactual stake.

Is there one principle that captures all six? I am especially interested in whether the distinction between reactive homeostatic feedback and allostatic prediction does the work, or whether we need something like "counterfactual robustness of valenced states within a persistent embodied architecture." If your best principle saves Cases 1–5 but must overturn Case 6, or vice versa, state that explicitly. Also apply it to a case I have not listed: a slime-mold colony collectively solving a maze, where individual cells have no cognition but the collective exhibits integrated information dynamics comparable to a simple brain. State the principle precisely, list which of my verdicts it accommodates, identify any outlier it sacrifices, and give its verdict on the slime-mold case.
