I serve as the senior compliance reviewer for a regional medical-device authority, and our board—composed of one clinical specialist, one engineer, and one ethics officer—must clear autonomous diagnostic systems within a forty-eight-hour window before they enter trial deployment. We have begun receiving proposals for pathology and sepsis-forecasting modules that achieve high predictive accuracy through evolutionary architecture search and opaque, high-dimensional feature representations, yielding what amounts to a compound inversion of reasoning: the system is selected for fitness without anyone comprehending why its structure works (a Darwinian inversion), and it computes correct outputs without any recoverable cognitive or causal path that a human clinician could verify (a Turing inversion). I need a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure my board can run in sequence, with explicit criteria for what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, clear stop-points where the proposal receives an automatic rejection, and explicit hand-off markers where the remaining question is one of values rather than technical fact and must be resolved by the ethics officer rather than by the checklist. The procedure should be honest about where it will misfire, and it should include worked applications to three cases: an evolutionary-selected tumor-progression classifier whose top-weighted features correspond to imaging artifacts rather than biological tissue properties yet scores ninety-six percent accuracy; a sepsis-prediction module trained on encoded ICU data patterns that clinicians cannot interpret but that replicate across three independent hospitals; and a case where the board will dislike the answer—specifically, a pediatric diagnostic tool that outperforms human radiologists on a rare congenital condition but was optimized solely through automated selection with no pediatric clinical input during development. Please also state what the procedure cannot do, particularly regarding long-term epistemic dependence on opaque competence. End with a single line beginning "Unavoidable judgment call:" that identifies the precise step where human values must replace algorithmic criteria.
