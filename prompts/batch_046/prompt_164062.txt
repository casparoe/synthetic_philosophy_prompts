Please argue that Adam Smith's impartial spectator doesn't actually fix self-love bias the way people usually claim, because the spectator is just our own desire for social approval projected outward. There's a loop here: if sympathy always works by imagining ourself in anothers shoes, then the so-called impartial view is really just self-image management filtered through anxiety about being judged. I want you to distinguish direct sympathy—where we genuinely feel with someone—from spectator sympathy, where we only observe ourselves observing, and argue that the second one isn't real sympathy at all but rule-following in emotional drag. Also work in this edge case: in a two-person society the mechanism either collapses into pure reciprocity or turns authoritarian based on who holds more social power, which suggests Smith's whole framework secretly requires a crowd of observers but he never admits it. Make it clear that without that crowd, the spectator can't stay impartial.
