Disjunctivism rejects the argument from illusion by denying that hallucination and veridical perception share a common mental element—there is no single sense-datum kind that both instantiate. Yet if a hallucinator later realizes her experience was non-veridical, she seems to correct an error about what was happening in her own mind. But on the disjunctivist account there is no shared representational content between the two states to be revised, and no mental kind common to both that she can compare. Does this imply that disjunctivism makes first-person error about one's own perceptual states impossible? Or is there a way for the subject to correct herself without presupposing the very mental commonality the view denies?
