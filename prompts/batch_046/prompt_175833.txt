Construct a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts answers to the question: can criteria of authenticity—dissimilarity, embarrassment, multiple attestation, coherence, and their standard variants—ever settle who the historical Jesus was? The tree must branch on concrete commitments that a respondent could answer without knowing scholarly labels. First split: do these criteria provide non-circular access to authentic material, or do they necessarily presuppose the prior reconstruction they claim to ground? Second split (for those who answer yes): what kind of identity can the criteria secure—continuous biographical self-consciousness, discrete authentic sayings and deeds only, or merely a negative silhouette of what later tradition could not have fabricated? Include an unnamed branch for the view that grants criteria local validity for isolated logia but denies they can ever integrate into a holistic answer to "who was he?"—label this the epistemic-closure position. At each leaf, name the position (or mark it "unnamed") and state its most awkward commitment in one sentence. Explicitly note unoccupied logical combinations—such as accepting criteria for deeds while rejecting them for sayings, or accepting circularity for the Synoptics but not for Paul—and diagnose each gap as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pairs the tree cannot separate, for instance two moderate reconstructions that differ only in the number of sayings recovered. The taxonomy must contain at least: a conservative/traditionalist leaf that treats criteria as sufficient for a robust portrait; a moderate fragmentary-reconstruction leaf; a radical abandonment leaf that rejects all criteria and explains source preference without them; and the unnamed epistemic-closure leaf. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citation lists; build the instrument so it can be applied without consulting external sources.
