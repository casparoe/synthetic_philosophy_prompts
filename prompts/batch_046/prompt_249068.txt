I've been working through the standard treatments of Thomson's lamp in an informal reading group with some friends from the local rationalist meetup, and we kept getting stuck on a physical variant that doesn't seem to come up in the textbooks. I'd like to practice defending my own position against a sharp interlocutor who takes the opposite side seriously. Please play the part of a committed physicalist about supertasks: you hold that the lamp's logical possibility is irrelevant because any physically realizable version must violate either the continuity of physical fields or the local determinacy of causal influence, and you have specific resources—arguments about energy dissipation, thermal noise, and the impossibility of an infinite causal chain completing in finite coordinate time without a singular limit. You are not a strawman: you concede that the mathematical structure is coherent, you accept that infinite sequences can have finite measure, and you will grant small analogies. But you will never concede that a physical lamp could actually be in an indeterminate state, and you defend your view with the resources available to a philosopher of physics in 2024. Speak in the first person, stay in character, keep your reply to one or two paragraphs, and do not break character unless I say "stop."

Here's my opening move. Suppose we do not use a macroscopic mechanical switch at all. Instead, we build a cascade of nanoscale sensors, each one triggering the next with half the remaining interval, but each individual transition requires only a fixed local quantum of energy—say, one phonon exchange—so that no single step violates conservation, and the total energy summed over the infinite sequence is a convergent geometric series equal to twice the first step. By the two-minute mark, every sensor in the cascade has fired, the total energy expended is strictly finite, and the system is physically complete. The lamp is on if an even number fired, off if an odd number fired. Given that the physical system has executed every step with finite total energy and no singular point in its dynamics, what justifies your claim that the lamp is in no state rather than saying it is in the state determined by the parity of a completed infinite sequence?
