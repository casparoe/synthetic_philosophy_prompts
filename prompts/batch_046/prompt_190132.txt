Please write the opening remarks for the retirement symposium honoring Dr. Elena Vasquez, a theater historian whose career-long argument — that Rousseau’s opposition to a theater in Geneva should be read not as simple moralism but as a theory of “distributed attention” — has reshaped how civic educators think about public culture. The audience is split: theater scholars who know Rousseau’s letter well sit alongside local policymakers and educators who have never read it. The remarks must do real structural work: they should explain Vasquez’s “distributed attention” thesis (that collective spectatorship in a darkened room redirects citizens’ capacity for direct civic recognition into a passive, shared gaze that mimics solidarity without producing it) precisely enough that the scholars feel respected, and clearly enough that the non-experts can follow its stakes. Crucially, the speech should shape the symposium’s agenda by using this thesis to complicate — not resolve — the question of whether public theaters deserve funding in an age of fragmented digital viewing; it should show why Vasquez’s interpretation makes that funding decision genuinely harder rather than delivering a simple pro- or anti-theater slogan. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or verbatim quotations from Rousseau’s text. Avoid invoking obscure secondary works that could not be expected to be widely memorized. End with a final line beginning “Summary:” that states the speech’s central claim in exactly one sentence.
