I've been working through Schlick's framework in which treating an activity as a means to some further end instrumentalizes it and thereby strips away the kind of value that makes life meaningful — on his account, meaning resides in play and in doing-for-its-own-sake, and collapses the moment purpose enters. The usual counter is that scientific or artistic work requires sustained, goal-directed discipline that looks structurally identical to purpose. I'm after a sharper, more structural example: an activity or life-organization in which the instrumental scaffolding is not incidental background but constitutive of the value itself, yet where Schlick's binary still forces us to classify the whole thing as either intrinsic play or as purpose-corrupted labor. Give me such a case, map out the tension precisely — in particular, address whether isolating the 'play' component from its goal-directed container destroys the very value we're trying to preserve — and say whether Schlick's distinction survives intact, or whether we need a third category, something like scaffolded intrinsic value, that his framework can't accommodate without collapsing.
