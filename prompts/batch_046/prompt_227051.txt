A philosophy podcast summarized Nozick's critique of utilitarianism this way: Nozick introduced the utility monster to show that if we maximize aggregate well-being, then any being with a sufficiently high internal conversion of resources into welfare—like a creature that derives a thousand times the pleasure per unit of goods—must receive nearly all available resources, leaving ordinary people with almost nothing. The host said this proves utilitarianism is incoherent because it demands total self-sacrifice by everyone else, and then claimed that Nozick used this very same argument to reject the experience machine, concluding that both objections together show we should care only about rights and reject welfare considerations entirely. How fair is this summary? Which parts accurately reflect Nozick's reasoning in Anarchy, State, and Utopia, where does it conflate the utility monster with a different thought experiment, and does the host's inference—that a rights-based view must abandon all concern for welfare—actually follow from the corrected account of the argument?
