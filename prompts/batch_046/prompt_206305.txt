For a philosophy of mind and artificial intelligence seminar, specify the desiderata that any adequate resolution of the frame problem must meet. For each desideratum, state the specific reasoning failure or representational burden it addresses—such as the need to avoid explicit enumeration of non-effects in an open-ended environment, or the need to distinguish beliefs about what changed from beliefs about what remained constant—and indicate whether it is a non-negotiable constraint or a preference tied to a particular research tradition (for example, commitments to symbolic representation, logical omniscience, biological plausibility, or domain-specific encoding). Then consider whether the following four demands can be jointly satisfied: (a) an agent updates its beliefs about a dynamic world without computing every unaffected proposition; (b) the update procedure remains computationally tractable in real time for resource-bounded agents; (c) the mechanism operates without hand-coded ontological partitions across heterogeneous domains—physical, social, and normative; and (d) the account preserves an explicit epistemic distinction between knowing that a particular fact changed and knowing that the rest of the world stayed stable. If established results—such as those concerning non-monotonic inference, the frame axiom in situation calculus, or resource-bounded reasoning—show that some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, identify the precise tension. Avoid scoring particular theories; the goal is the criteria themselves.
