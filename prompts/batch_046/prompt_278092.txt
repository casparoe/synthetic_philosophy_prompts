(2) Those independent grounds have to come from direct observation of their track record or background knowledge I already have - but both of those are usually testimonial too, so reductionism either collapses into circularity or forces an infinite regress unless we bottom out in pure perception or reasoning.
(3) Now think about ambient testimony - like a subway announcement with no visible speaker, or an unsigned weather alert pushed to your phone. There's no identifiable agent whose reliability I can check.
(4) So if reductionism is right, ambient testimony can never yield knowledge, since there's no speaker-reliability chain to build.
(5) But clearly I know my train is delayed when the platform speaker says so, even though I have no idea who recorded the message.
(6) So reductionism fails, and testimony must be a basic, irreducible source.
(7) But if testimony is basic, then any linguistic uptake that delivers true content should count as knowledge, as long as my cognitive machinery is functioning normally.
(8) That means if I hear a convincing voice in a dream, or a deepfake audio with no source, and it happens to be true, I should count as knowing it - there's no reductionist filter requiring me to verify the speaker.
(9) So anti-reductionism proves too much: it collapses the difference between genuine testimonial knowledge and lucky acoustic accidents.

We really want to hang onto (5) and also avoid accepting (9). Which step should we reject, and what's the cost?
