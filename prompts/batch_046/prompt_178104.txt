I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I've been trying to work out a coherent position on whether we should attribute beliefs to the AI systems I build and deploy. Before I present this to a discussion group, I want to check whether my commitments actually hang together. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I take Dennett's intentional stance seriously: attributing beliefs and desires to a system is justified entirely by predictive and explanatory utility, not by any claim about inner mental life or consciousness.

(2) I think reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) creates a genuine error-correction loop between a model's outputs and external correction, which makes belief-attribution to large language models more warranted than it would be for a pure feedforward network, because the system's representations are being selectively shaped by consequences.

(3) I also hold that for belief-attribution to be more than a useful fiction, the attributed state needs counterfactual robustness: it should explain the system's behavior across a range of inputs and perturbations, not just match local patterns. I think many current LLM representations fail this test because they are brittle and context-dependent.

(4) At the same time, I believe that phenomenal consciousness and intrinsic goals are necessary conditions for genuine belief. Since I do not think current LLMs possess either, I conclude that they do not literally have beliefs, even when the intentional stance is practically indispensable.

(5) In my actual engineering work—debugging, interpretability, and safety evaluation—I routinely treat models as having beliefs about user intent, code correctness, and factual states of the world, and I think this practice is rational and unavoidable.

My questions: Which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, and which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection? In particular, does (4) smuggle an ontological condition into what (1) claims is purely pragmatic? Does (2) actually support (3), or does the external nature of RLHF mean it cannot supply the right kind of robustness? If there is a deepest tension, which single revision would clean up the most—giving up the consciousness requirement in (4), weakening the counterfactual demand in (3), or abandoning the claim in (5) that the practice is rational rather than merely convenient?

Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each say what the cheapest fix would cost me. Finally, say explicitly what would change your mind: which argument or consideration about RLHF, representational robustness, or the intentional stance itself would lead you to a different verdict about where the real contradiction lies, and identify the point in your reasoning that is most likely to fail.
