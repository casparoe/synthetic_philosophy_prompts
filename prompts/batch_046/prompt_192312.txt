I am preparing for a seminar in my philosophy of mind course on indexical belief, and a fellow student sketched an argument that uses the Sleeping Beauty protocol to show that rational agents can never revise credence on purely self-locating information without falling into contradiction. The argument runs as follows:

(1) Before the experiment begins, Beauty knows the coin is fair, so her credence in Heads is 1/2.
(2) The thirder analysis holds that, upon being awakened with no memory of prior awakenings, she should distribute her credence equally over the three subjectively indistinguishable centered possibilities—Heads-Monday, Tails-Monday, and Tails-Tuesday—giving Heads a credence of 1/3.
(3) The reflection principle requires that if an agent now knows she will later rationally hold credence c in a proposition p, then her present credence in p should also be c, provided she anticipates no loss of evidence.
(4) Therefore, applying reflection to (2), Beauty should already assign credence 1/3 to Heads before she falls asleep, which contradicts (1).
(5) To avoid contradiction, one must deny that Beauty can rationally update upon awakening; that is, her credence in Heads should remain 1/2 even after she is awake.
(6) Yet awakening clearly supplies indexical information—she learns that she is now experiencing a particular temporal location—so denying all update on self-locating evidence seems to erase the distinction between knowing the protocol and knowing one's place within it.
(7) Consequently, either reflection fails for every case involving centered worlds, or self-locating belief revision is impossible altogether.
(8) Since we plainly revise beliefs about our temporal location without contradiction, reflection must be globally inapplicable to indexical reasoning, which undermines its role in rational belief dynamics.

My classmate suspects the flaw is in (5), but I worry the argument instead exploits an ambiguity in (3) between centered and uncentered propositions. Could you identify the precise step that fails? Explain why that step is intuitively attractive, and then discuss what a thirder and a halfer each must concede about the relationship between pre-sleep credence and post-awakening credence if they reject that step—especially regarding whether centered worlds can serve as objects of rational comparison across different epistemic perspectives.
