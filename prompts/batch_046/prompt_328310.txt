Please explain the conceptual tension between restricted accounts of special composition and ontological vagueness about object boundaries. If composition occurs only when parts are unified by a life process or a specific functional arrangement, then there ought to be sharp conditions determining exactly which parts compose a whole at any moment. Yet for macroscopic cases—such as a mountain range gradually eroding or an organism whose cells are continuously replaced—there appears to be no non-arbitrary cutoff at which the parts cease to satisfy those unifying conditions. Does this vagueness threaten restricted composition, or can its defender treat the indeterminacy as purely epistemic or linguistic? Furthermore, if they take the latter route, does that same strategy generalize in a way that weakens their standard objections to unrestricted universalism?
