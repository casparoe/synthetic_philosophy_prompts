Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed endurantist and a committed perdurantist, focused specifically on the metaphysics of persistence through conscious experience and the problem of temporary intrinsics. The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both parties can sign, covering the formal conditions any persistence theory must meet, the data of first-person temporal awareness that need explanation, and the shared acknowledgment that standard three-dimensional and four-dimensional analyses both face serious objections; (2) precise, non-caricatured formulations of the residual disagreement, with the endurantist's statement phrased so that an endurantist would accept its wording and the perdurantist's statement similarly faithful to their own position, particularly on whether the subject of experience at one time is strictly identical to the subject at another time or stands in a relation of causal-psychological continuity to a distinct temporal part; (3) the cruxes—those sub-questions whose resolution would genuinely move at least one party—such as whether the logical possibility of personal fission undermines endurantist identity conditions, and whether accepting temporal parts commits one to an implausible multiplication of thinking subjects; (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree is relevant (for instance, a gradual neural-replacement scenario preserving phenomenology, or a split-brain case generating two streams of awareness), together with each side's explicit, pre-registered prediction about what that case demonstrates. Present the agreed claims first, then a disagreements table with columns for the endurantist formulation, the perdurantist formulation, the shared crux, the agreed-upon test case, and each side's predicted interpretation. In addition, require both parties to address one framing not standard in introductory discussions: the analogy between the persistence of a conscious subject and the persistence of a distributed computational process in which individual processing nodes are continuously replaced while functional organization is maintained. Each side should state whether this analogy strengthens or weakens their view and why. The prompt should not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; all arguments should be developed conceptually. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate for a philosophical collaboration document.
