The Dutch Book argument shows that if your credences violate the probability axioms, a bookie can construct a set of bets that guarantee you lose money no matter how the world turns out. This establishes that rational belief must be probabilistically coherent. The same structure applies to preference: if your choices violate transitivity, an adversary can cycle you through trades and leave you back at the start but poorer, so coherence binds preference too. From there, getting to expected utility maximization is mainly a matter of adding an independence axiom over lotteries, which lets you derive cardinal utility from preference intensity using the von Neumann-Morgenstern setup, though those axioms are technically separate coherence conditions.

I think this misses the point. Even local coherence requires some shared currency of value across the subsystems, and if that currency is inconsistent globally then an adversary who interacts with the subsystems sequentially can still pump you.
