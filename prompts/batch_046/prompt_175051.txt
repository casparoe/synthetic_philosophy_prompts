I'm convening a small interdisciplinary working group—philosophers of technology, STS scholars, and design researchers—with three years of funding and no empirical laboratory, to advance conceptual work on whether and how artifacts possess politics, particularly in algorithmic and infrastructural contexts rather than classical physical cases. I need not a survey but a defended agenda: which open conceptual questions, unresolved distinctions, or downstream implications deserve the bulk of our attention, and which should be treated as secondary.

Please proceed as follows.

First, identify the three or four most significant open conceptual issues in this debate as applied to contemporary technologies—systems where material form includes code, protocol, and maintenance labor rather than only concrete and steel. For each, specify not merely that disagreement exists, but the precise conceptual gap: what distinction is underdefined, what inference is contested, or what category is unstable.

Second, rank these issues in order of priority for our specific resources and aims. Defend the ordering with explicit criteria: tractability for scholars without large-scale engineering capacity; whether resolving the issue is upstream of other debates (that is, whether other conceptual or practical questions depend on its answer); the magnitude of practical difference the resolution would make to governance or design practice; and what kind of limited conceptual or qualitative result over the next two years would cause you to reorder them.

Third, inject and defend at least one niche or underdeveloped distinction that standard treatments of the debate overlook—such as whether an artifact's political properties are scale-invariant across deployment contexts, or whether a rigorous separation can be drawn between politics embedded intentionally in design and politics that emerge only through maintenance regimes, user adaptation, or infrastructural decay. Explain why this distinction is not ornamental but structurally important to your ranking, and articulate its most awkward commitment if taken seriously.

The deliverable should be a prioritized agenda with reasoning, not a bibliography or reading list. Do not cite specific works, provide citations, or recommend texts; I want reasoning directed at the group's allocation of effort, not a literature review.
