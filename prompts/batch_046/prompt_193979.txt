I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, currently auditing an interdisciplinary graduate seminar on rational agency and collective decision-making. I need you to design a take-home examination essay prompt—aimed at students who have encountered basic game theory but have no specialized background in philosophy of rational choice—together with a model answer and a marking scheme.

The domain must be superrationality, specifically whether reasoning of the kind Hofstadter described survives when the symmetry conditions are not globally transparent but are partially hidden by an epistemic asymmetry embedded in a technical system. To prevent a mere recitation of standard Prisoner's Dilemma summaries, please inject the following niche conceptual twist: frame the scenario inside a Byzantine fault-tolerant consensus protocol in which three nodes each hold a partial cryptographic key fragment. Each node must independently decide whether to release its fragment to reconstruct a shared secret. The payoff structure is symmetric: if all three release, a contract executes and each receives +10; if all withhold, nothing happens and each receives 0; if exactly one withholds while the other two release, the withholder can steal the reconstructed secret alone (+25) while the other two suffer a breach penalty (-5). All nodes run identical decision algorithms with perfect logical transparency—they can inspect one another's source code—but Node A contains a hidden hardware-level stochastic deviation (a 0.001% random-bit flip in its entropy pool) unknown to Nodes B and C, though A knows its own status. B and C are symmetrically ignorant about A, yet symmetrically informed about each other. This creates what I will call a shadow symmetry: B and C share an identical epistemic relation to A, and each knows the other shares that same ignorance, generating a potential second-order loop in which each reasons about the other's reasoning about A.

Your deliverables should be:

1. The exam question itself (upper-level graduate audience; answer expected in approximately 800 words; 48-hour take-home; no external sources permitted; students must be explicitly instructed not to provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations of any kind).

2. A model answer of approximately 650 words. It should not merely summarize standard treatments of superrationality. It should work through: (a) whether the hidden fault breaks the symmetry required for first-order superrational reasoning; (b) how the shadow symmetry between B and C generates a second-order loop and whether that loop collapses back to first-order reasoning or reinforces it; (c) how causal decision theory would diverge in recommending defection if one expects others to cooperate; (d) whether A's reasoning about B and C differs structurally from B's reasoning about A; and (e) a verdict on what B and C ought rationally to do, with explicit attention to why the presence of the hidden deviation does or does not undermine the common-knowledge condition.

3. A marking scheme totaling 25 marks, divided into five analytical moves worth 5 marks each. For each move, state precisely what the answer must contain to earn full marks, and name the specific misconception or omission that should cause a deduction. One of the five moves must specifically target the student's handling of the shadow-symmetry loop and its effect on second-order reasoning.

Constraints: write in formal, polished academic English appropriate for a course syllabus; do not include any verbatim quotations from philosophers or texts—if you mention the concept associated with Hofstadter, paraphrase without quotation marks; do not ask students to cite specific papers, books, or page numbers, since the scenario should be entirely self-contained; ensure the technical framing remains comprehensible to a student without computer-science expertise by describing the protocol and the fault in general terms within the prompt itself; and avoid any dependence on obscure technical facts that would require lookup. The niche twist—the shadow symmetry inside a Byzantine consensus setting—should be genuinely novel, not drawn from standard textbooks, but must remain intelligible through the description you provide.
