You are an expert philosopher of mind and cognitive science, with particular depth in analogical reasoning, conceptual dynamics, and the study of microdomains in the style of Douglas Hofstadter's Copycat research on fluid concepts. Please think carefully and systematically: begin by brainstorming the different conceptual frames or transformation rules that could generate a consistent pattern of judgments, then reason through to a specific prediction.

Consider a researcher who studies conceptual slippage within a tightly restricted analogical microdomain. The domain contains only three token types—Circle (C), Square (S), and Triangle (T)—arranged in linear triples. For each presented pairing, the researcher classifies it as either a valid analogy or not a valid analogy, apparently tracking some underlying relational transformation rather than superficial similarity.

The researcher's recorded verdicts are as follows:

1. C–S–T mapped to T–S–C: valid.
2. S–T–C mapped to C–T–S: valid.
3. C–C–S mapped to S–C–C: not valid.
4. T–T–S mapped to S–T–T: valid.
5. C–S–S mapped to S–S–C: not valid.
6. S–C–T mapped to T–C–S: valid.

The next case to be evaluated is: T–S–S mapped to S–S–T.

Your task has three parts. First, predict whether the researcher will judge this seventh case valid or not valid, and state your confidence. Second, articulate at least two distinct rules or conceptual frames that are jointly consistent with verdicts 1–6 but that diverge on case 7. For each frame, explain what kind of conceptual slippage it permits—e.g., role reversal, positional rotation, dominance of one token type, or presence-based gating—and show precisely how it handles the earlier cases. Third, give your best-guess account of which frame the researcher is actually running, with explicit reasons, and then propose one further case (specifying both the left and right configurations) that would most sharply discriminate between the two frames you have identified.

Please avoid appealing to unpublished data, personal correspondence, or bibliographic references that cannot be reconstructed from general knowledge. Reason from the structure of the domain itself.
