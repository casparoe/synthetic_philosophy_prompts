Please describe the most important implications of the past hypothesis—the view that the thermodynamic arrow of time derives from a low-entropy boundary condition at the origin of the universe—for our epistemic entitlement to treat historical and cosmological records as evidence for that very past. Specifically, consider whether using entropy-increasing traces (memory formations, fossil stratification, cosmic background radiation patterns) to confirm a lower-entropy origin involves a circular justification: these traces count as running "forward" in time only if we already assume the boundary condition that makes them so, yet we invoke them as independent confirmation of that condition. Evaluate whether this circularity is vicious, benign, or avoidable through a non-empirical or structural grounding of the arrow. Also address what follows for our knowledge of deep history if the arrow cannot be independently anchored—whether we are left with a coherentist picture of temporal knowledge, or whether the problem dissolves once we distinguish the physical asymmetry from the epistemic conditions of observation. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; work with the conceptual structure alone.
