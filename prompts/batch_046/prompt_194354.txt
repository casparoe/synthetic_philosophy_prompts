I've been working through Schrödinger's essays on consciousness, particularly his adoption of Advaita Vedanta—the view that individual minds are not separate substances but manifestations of a single universal mind, often compared to waves arising on one ocean. He appears to treat quantum mechanical non-separability, especially the fact that entangled systems are described by a wavefunction that does not factor into independent parts, as more than an analogy for this metaphysical unity; coupled with Bell-type results that exclude local hidden-variable explanations, he seems to take physical non-separability as direct support for mental unity. From this I have formed the view that accepting standard quantum mechanics while rejecting Vedantic monism is inconsistent: if the formalism denies that particles have genuinely separate identities, and consciousness is not ontologically distinct from physical processes, then the separateness of minds should also be an illusion. Where exactly does this inference break? Is the error a category mistake—moving from state-description non-separability to an ontological claim about consciousness—or does modern interpretation theory reject the premise that the wavefunction describes a unified reality, and if so, doesn't that make interpretation choice partly metaphysical rather than purely empirical?
