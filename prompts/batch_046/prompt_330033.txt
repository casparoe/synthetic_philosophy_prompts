You have learned from a direct-to-consumer genetic report that you resulted from one precise sperm-egg union, and that a different pairing occurring moments later would have produced a genetically distinct child—your parents’ offspring, but not you. During mediation over your parents’ estate, you claim a larger inheritance share on the grounds that you alone are the irreplaceable product of that specific pairing, while your sibling argues that the counterfactual child would still have been you, only with different traits, so your identity is not anchored to these particular gametes. You feel that being the actual result of that union must carry some moral weight, yet you cannot articulate whether it establishes any special standing, or whether your sibling’s counterfactual reasoning undermines your claim entirely. What is the philosophical structure of this disagreement, and does being uniquely tied to that particular sperm and egg strengthen your position?
