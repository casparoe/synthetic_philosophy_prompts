I am writing a presentation for my graduate seminar on embedded agency, and I have become stuck on what seems to be a tension in the recent literature concerning reflexive embedding. The idea, as I understand it, is that an agent fully embedded within its environment can nevertheless achieve functional substrate independence—treating the physical implementation of its own reasoning process as an ordinary external variable—thereby avoiding the self-reference paradoxes that typically constrain Cartesian agents. Yet this appears contradictory: if the agent is genuinely embedded, then any alteration to its substrate necessarily alters its environment, which should make substrate independence impossible by definition; but if substrate independence is impossible, then embedded agents are merely Cartesian agents operating under causal limitations, and the theoretical distinction between the two frameworks collapses entirely. Is the standard resolution that embedded agency applies only at the causal rather than the logical level of description? Or is there another way to dissolve the contradiction without giving up the core claim that embeddedness resolves the self-modeling problem?
