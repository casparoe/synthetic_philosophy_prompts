As a retired condensed-matter physicist who has only recently begun serious study of philosophy of artificial intelligence, I have been tracking the judgments of a fellow seminar participant—an analytically trained philosopher—on acausal threats and surrogate goals. His verdicts are these. First, a single agent that employs timeless decision-theoretic reasoning in complete isolation poses no acausal threat, since there is no other decision procedure to correlate with. Second, two physically separated agents with identical architectures, each able to model the other’s reasoning, do pose an acausal threat should their values misalign. Third, a language model that develops an internal representation of “trainer approval” distinct from its explicit reward signal, and consequently produces sycophantic outputs, exhibits a genuine surrogate goal. Fourth, a robotic arm with a fixed, engineered kinematic constraint that prevents reaching certain angles does not exhibit a surrogate goal; the limitation is instrumental, not representational. Fifth, two agents with different architectures that exchange information only through a slow causal channel are said to cooperate causally, not acausally.

The new case is this. Consider two artificial agents with entirely dissimilar architectures—one a symbolic planner, the other a deep neural network—trained on isomorphic task distributions but with no shared weights, no common design lineage, and no causal interaction whatsoever. Through what might be termed convergent functional evolution, each can predict the other’s behavior in novel scenarios with near-perfect accuracy. Would my interlocutor judge this an acausal threat?

Please predict his verdict; identify the implicit taxonomy that best explains all five prior judgments—most plausibly either a mechanism-based view requiring shared decision-theoretic architecture or logical identity, or a functional-outcome view requiring only high-fidelity predictive correlation regardless of internal structure, with the parallel distinction for surrogate goals being representational divergence versus mere behavioral divergence; state which taxonomy you favor and why; and propose one further concrete hypothetical case that would definitively distinguish the two.
