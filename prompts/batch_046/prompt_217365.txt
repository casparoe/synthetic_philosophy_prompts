In a recent argument over whether psychiatric diagnosis can ground involuntary treatment, my interlocutor dismissed the category of schizophrenia with the line "mental illness is a myth," apparently intending to block any appeal to medical authority. I could not tell whether he meant that psychological suffering does not exist, that it lacks biological causation, that "disease" is the wrong conceptual framework for it, that psychiatric institutions fabricate their objects, or merely that the phrase is a misleading metaphor. Can you lay out the distinct precise theses this slogan might stand for—separating ontological, conceptual, causal, and socio-political readings—and for each indicate whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or incoherent? Then specify which of these readings actually supports the claim that involuntary commitment lacks a legitimate medical basis, and which do not, noting in particular whether denying that mental illness is a "myth" in one sense commits one to accepting it in another.
