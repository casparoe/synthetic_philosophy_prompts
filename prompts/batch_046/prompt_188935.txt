Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for positions on the scope and coherence of superrationality in strategic reasoning, designed for a philosophy-of-decision seminar. Rather than sorting by school names, build the tree from four concrete diagnostic commitments: (1) whether superrational cooperation requires exact symmetry of reasoning architecture, or survives a "broken mirror" case in which one agent knows they compute marginally faster than their counterpart; (2) whether the argument extends to n-player symmetric dilemmas with non-pairwise payoffs, or collapses once the interaction is no longer strictly two-player; (3) whether common knowledge of rationality at all orders is necessary, or whether first-order identification of one's counterpart as "like me" already fixes the answer; (4) whether the view is best reconstructed as team reasoning (the group asking "what should we do?"), as an epistemic constraint on belief formation about the other, or as a direct revision of individual utility maximization. At each leaf, place the named position if one exists—e.g., Hofstadter-style superrationalist, team-reasoning reductionist, skeptical orthodox game theorist—or mark it "unnamed" if the combination is unexplored, and state in one sentence its most awkward commitment (for instance, that it requires ignoring private information about one's own reasoning speed, or that it mandates cooperation even when defection is strictly dominant). Flag explicitly any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate, particularly where team reasoning and superrationality converge, and note whether any unoccupied branch represents an incoherent combination, an unattractive one, or merely an unexplored gap. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the instrument should be usable by someone who knows only the logical structure of the debate.
