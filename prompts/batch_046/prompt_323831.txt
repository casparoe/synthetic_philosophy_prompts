ive been thinking a lot about whether nations are old or made up and if being made up makes them less real. here are my gut reactions.

scotland has tartan patterns that were mostly invented in the 1800s, but people still feel scottish in a deep way. that feels real, not fake.

italy was only put together in 1861 but we talk about it like its been there forever. also real — the feeling and the age dont match but that doesnt break it.

theres this online group that calls itself the Republic of Loria. they made up a flag and a history going back to 1982, they meet in real life, and they really believe it. i want to say thats not a real nation, but then why is italy real?

heres the hard one: my town started a heritage festival in 2005 where everyone wears fake 1800s outfits. now kids who grew up with it think its always existed. that feels more legitimate than Loria, but i cant explain why.

then theres a company that invented a founding myth in 1998 about two brothers in a garage, except they were actually bought by a hedge fund in 2010. the employees still cry at the company song about the garage. is that an imagined community? i keep flipping — sometimes it feels like the town festival, sometimes like pure marketing.

so is there one principle behind all this? maybe its about whether people can leave without losing part of themselves? but then you can leave Loria too. or maybe its about whether the story is used to control people? that seems too cynical.

what principle gets my verdicts, or close? and what does it say about a case i havent listed — like if a government invents a national holiday next year and fifty years later people treat it like ancient tradition?
