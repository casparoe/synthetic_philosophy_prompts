I have been thinking on my own about whether physical objects can carry political structures inside them, independent of the people who use them. The familiar examples—large energy grids or communication networks—seem too obvious, because their scale already forces central control. I want to go in the opposite direction: give me an example of a small, seemingly neutral household or local tool whose politics emerge not from its visible design or intended function, but from the invisible labor of upkeep—who must clean it, who must supply its consumable parts, who must repair it when it breaks, and how that labor is distributed. Then, taking a definite stance rather than balancing both sides, argue whether this case proves that artifacts genuinely possess politics embedded in their material form, or whether it only proves that social rules about labor can attach themselves to any object regardless of its design. Be specific about the artifact and the maintenance chain, and defend your position with reasons.
