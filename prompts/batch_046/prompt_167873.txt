Write a villanelle in American English about Mill's harm principle and the recursive problem of whether advocacy for coercive intervention can itself be classified as the kind of harm that justifies coercion. Structure the poem around two refrains: the first should declare that interference is warranted only to prevent direct harm to others; the second should ask whether the call to interfere is already an injury to the autonomy it claims to defend. In the intervening quatrains, examine the specific scenario of a speaker urging collective action that would produce tangible damage, treating the words neither as mere expression nor as direct causation but as a conceptual boundary case that Mill's framework strains to contain. The formal repetition of the villanelle should make the tension circular and unresolved, so that a reader acquainted with the distinction between self-regarding and other-regarding conduct recognizes the conceptual knot rather than a generic free-speech platitude. Write in American English and do not include bibliographic references, citations, or reading lists.
