I'm assembling a handout for a philosophy discussion group — the sort that reads rationalist blogs and then argues about metaethics over coffee — and I want a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on Glaucon's challenge in Plato's Republic. The root commitment is the scenario of the ring of Gyges: full invisibility, so that conventional justice carries no reputation cost, no legal penalty, and no risk of social detection. The first branch must ask whether, under that condition, there remains any reason to act justly at all. From there, split by whether that reason is prudential — long-run cooperation equilibrium, internalized norm, identity-based self-signaling — or non-prudential — intrinsic value of justice, deontic constraint, virtue-constitutive good. Within the prudential branch, distinguish whether the reason survives complete invisibility, as with a commitment device anchored in self-concept, or collapses, yielding pure contractarianism. Within the non-prudential branch, distinguish whether justice is valuable because it orders the soul harmoniously (the Platonic reply) or categorically regardless of psychic or worldly outcome (a Kantian-style position). Every leaf should be named if it is occupied — contractarian, evolutionary-cooperation rationalist, Platonic virtue ethicist, deontologist, and so on — or marked "unnamed / unexplored" if the combination is empty; each leaf needs one sentence identifying its most awkward commitment, such as the contractarian's difficulty explaining why injustice is wrong rather than merely unwise, or the Platonic view's burden of explaining why soul-harmony should motivate someone who can violate justice without internal dissonance. Every node should be phrased as a concrete case question — for example, "If you knew with certainty that you could take what you wanted without anyone ever knowing, including yourself in any self-monitoring sense, would you still have reason to refrain?" — so that a reader answers by reflecting on the invisible-just-person case rather than by selecting a philosophical label. Note any pairs of leaves that the tree fails to separate cleanly, especially where modern evolutionary accounts of stable cooperation and classical virtue ethics predict similar behavioral patterns despite incompatible metaphysics. Include a brief diagnosis for each empty branch: is the combination incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored?
