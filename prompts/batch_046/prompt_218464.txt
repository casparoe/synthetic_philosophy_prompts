I am designing a diagnostic handout for a graduate seminar on Nietzsche's genealogy of morality, and I want it to function as a decision tree that sorts students by what they actually believe about the origin of bad conscience in the Second Essay, not by which label they prefer. The tree should be constructed from the logical space rather than from a list of commentators: each branch node must be a concrete claim—phrased as a direct yes-or-no or either-or question—about whether the creditor-debtor economic relation is necessary for internalized cruelty, whether the ascetic priest's redirection of aggression is sufficient on its own, and whether an external mnemonic technology (writing, ritual bodily marking, or similar prosthetic mediation) is required for the internalization of pain, or whether purely internal psychological mechanisms will do. The leaves must include at least a strictly economic-punitive reading, a strictly psychological-ascetic reading, a synthetic two-stage sequential reading, a deflationary descriptive-only reading that denies any normative upshot, and a synthetic position I am calling the prosthetic-memory view, which holds that bad conscience is impossible without some external technology of memory mediating the pain of punishment. For any combination of answers that no named position occupies, please note the empty cell and briefly diagnose it as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; also flag any pair of leaves—such as the sequential two-stage view and the prosthetic-memory view—that the tree's branch questions cannot actually separate, rather than forcing a spurious distinction.

Please ensure the instrument avoids asking students whether they agree with Nietzsche in general, whether they have read specific scholars, or which interpretation sounds most appealing; do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. The audience knows the basic argument about punishment, debt, and the making of a memory but has not mastered the secondary literature, so every node should be answerable without knowing which interpretation it leads to, and the tree should contain roughly eight to ten nodes total. I want the output formatted clearly as a branching taxonomy with each internal node labeled by the claim that splits it, each leaf carrying the position name and a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment, so that a student can trace their answers in about ten minutes and land on a mapped position rather than a literature review.
