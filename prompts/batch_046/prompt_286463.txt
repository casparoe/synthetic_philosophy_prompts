I am a physicist fluent in statistical mechanics and renormalization-group theory but with no background in analytic philosophy. Restate the debate over whether scientific categories constitute natural kinds—covering the essentialist view that kinds are defined by necessary and sufficient properties, the homeostatic property cluster view that kinds are maintained by causal mechanisms among co-occurring properties, and the anti-realist view that kind-boundaries are imposed by pragmatic interests rather than discovered—using only my vocabulary: microstates, macrostates, entropy, universality classes, relevant and irrelevant operators, scale separation, and effective theories that emerge at particular energy or length scales. For each position, specify what maps cleanly onto physical concepts, what requires distortion or analogical stretching, and what has no expression in statistical mechanics. Then provide the residue analysis: do the untranslated philosophical claims point to deficiencies in my physical framework (for example, its silence on modal necessity or normative explanatory success), or do they reveal that part of the philosophical dispute concerns distinctions that have no physical counterpart and therefore may be less substantive than they appear? Do not cite specific texts; focus entirely on the conceptual translation and what fails to survive it.
