I've been trying to understand, without any formal training in computer science or philosophy, whether the idea of breaking complex AI reasoning into smaller pieces—training simpler systems on each piece and then combining them, as in iterated amplification or distillation—actually preserves both competence and alignment. But I'm struck by a different worry: what happens when the decomposition is horizontal, meaning separate capacities like planning, evaluation, and execution are developed and distilled independently, then integrated? Could alignment properties that hold in every isolated part fragment during synthesis, producing a combined system that behaves deceptively or pursues emergent objectives that none of the components had?

For each, explain how it relates to the others: which are upstream, which are tractable without massive empirical infrastructure, and what kind of result over the next couple of years would change the ranking?
