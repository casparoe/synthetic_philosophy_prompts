As a novelist preoccupied with unreliable narration and the architecture of remembered experience, I am constructing a conceptual exercise rather than a story. I want you to draft the joint statement that would emerge from an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps in the philosophy of perception—call them the Orwellian Revisionists and the Stalinesque Fabricationists—regarding the color-phi phenomenon and the precise timing of conscious experience. Do not treat this as a debate about neurology; treat it as a debate about the ontology and epistemology of the experienced present.

The collaboration must produce a single document with three sections. First, an agreed-claims section: list the propositions both sides can sign, including the formal description of the color-phi display (two discrete chromatic stimuli presented in rapid succession at adjacent spatial positions, with subjects reporting continuous motion and an intermediate hue change occurring prior to the arrival of the second stimulus), the fact that the subjective report conflicts with the objective stimulus sequence, and the denial that any current neuroimaging protocol resolves whether a raw unedited experience ever occurs. Second, a disagreements table: for each camp, state in wording that camp would accept for itself, (a) whether there is ever a first-person state corresponding to the discrete, non-moving presentation of the first dot alone; (b) whether the intermediate chromatic experience is best described as a revised memory-trace or as a de novo construction without prior contradictory content; and (c) whether the timing of experience refers to the temporal location assigned by a meta-cognitive timestamp or to the intrinsic temporal structure of the perceptual content itself. Third, the cruxes: identify the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side—specifically, whether sub-personal predictive processing renders the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction a difference in descriptive level rather than in kind, and whether a hypothetical chromatic leapfrog variant (in which the second dot is violet and subjects report a phantom cyan at the midpoint that chronologically precedes the red dot's disappearance) forces either camp to concede that temporal binding is always already narrative smoothing. Each side should record, in advance, what empirical or conceptual finding would count against its own view. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal—say, about whether revision requires a prior representational token—say which part and why, with formulations each side accepts. Keep the tone formal, precise, and free of bibliographic requests; the reasoning should stand on conceptual grounds alone.
