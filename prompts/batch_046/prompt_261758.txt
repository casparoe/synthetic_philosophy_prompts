I want them to understand the unilateralist's curse through a carrying example that is not the usual transformative-AI or nuclear-proliferation case: specifically, a watershed-zoning scenario in which any one of five symmetric, well-informed municipal boards can unilaterally approve high-density development, each board correctly computing that local economic gain exceeds its share of regional ecological damage, and yet all five privately prefer the undeveloped outcome.

Design the explanatory arc: what concept must be secured first so the curse can be separated from a tragedy of the commons; why this example needs exactly five actors, symmetric information, and no external enforcement mechanism; the three misconceptions they will likely raise in sequence—treating it as a prisoner's dilemma, then as a free-rider problem, then assuming the solution must be a binding treaty—and the precise point in the arc where each should be headed off; a check for understanding that shows they have grasped the structural feature (unilateral actionability combined with locally positive but globally negative payoff) rather than just memorized the term; and what standard extension should be explicitly left out at this stage and why.
