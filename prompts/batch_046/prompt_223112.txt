So she thinks any complete self-model hits a consistency wall, which is why AI will never fully replicate human insight.

Leo pushed back hard. He said Gödel's results are super specific: they apply only to formal systems that are recursively axiomatizable, consistent, and strong enough to encode arithmetic. He also said Mara's reflexive loop is a category mistake—Gödel's second theorem requires the system to actually formalize its own proof theory, which brains don't do in any rigorous sense. Leo admitted that if you literally built an AI with one fixed axiomatic architecture and arithmetic, then Gödel applies, but that's an engineering choice, not a universal limit on cognition.

Then Mara said Leo was just moving the goalposts by saying humans are 'messy,' because you could always approximate human reasoning with a formal system, and then the same reflexive problem shows up at the limit. Leo answered that approximation doesn't preserve the conditions for Gödel's proof—you need exact syntactic representation, not statistical approximation.
