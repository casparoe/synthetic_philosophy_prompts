You serve as the lead ethics reviewer for a national neuroprosthetics regulatory board. Each quarter you evaluate multi-site clinical trial protocols proposing gradual cortical replacement with synthetic substrates—procedures that may span months, include reversible intermediate modules, or leave scattered biological tissue islands intact while synthetic analogues assume functional roles. You need a reusable decision procedure, executable within ten working days without external philosophical consultation, that determines at specified replacement thresholds—approximately thirty, sixty, and ninety percent—whether the trial subject retains sufficient identity continuity to maintain the original informed consent, or whether a new consent framework must be initiated and prior legal authority suspended. Ground the procedure in the philosophical analysis of mind uploading and gradual replacement—psychological continuity, functional persistence, causal versus qualitative similarity—without citing specific authors or texts. Structure it as an ordered sequence of evaluative tests applied at each threshold, with explicit criteria distinguishing structural isomorphism from functional role maintenance and first-person narrative access. The procedure must flag the cases it cannot settle: for instance, continuous cognitive function combined with disrupted autobiographical continuity, or patch-replacement architectures where synthetic and biological regions maintain separate but interacting information loops. Include worked applications to three hypothetical protocols: linear sequential replacement; non-contiguous patch replacement leaving biological islands; and a design with fully reversible synthetic modules that can be withdrawn after integration. In one of these applications, the procedure should yield a determination that consent continuity must be broken despite intact reasoning and memory performance—a result the trial investigators will contest. Clearly mark every step that requires documented human judgment rather than algorithmic application, specify the audit trail required for legal sign-off, and state explicitly where the procedure is expected to misfire or demand override by the board.
