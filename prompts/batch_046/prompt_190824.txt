Assume the role of an expert philosopher of science specializing in evolutionary theory and cultural transmission. Before drafting, take a moment to brainstorm the precise logical architecture of both camps—distinguishing genuine disagreements from terminological ones—then proceed with analytical rigor.

Write the joint statement that a committed strong memeticist (who holds that cultural evolution operates through discrete, high-fidelity replicators—memes—subject to blind variation and selective retention largely independent of host cognition) and a committed reconstructivist critic (who holds that all cultural transmission involves active cognitive reconstruction, that discrete replicators do not exist in culture, and that evolutionary metaphors misrepresent intentional, design-mediated transmission) could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the Darwinian theory of culture.

The statement should include: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims about cultural transmission, evolutionary analogy, and the role of cognition, formulated so neither side finds them slanted or vacuous; (2) a disagreements table with precise formulations of each side's view, phrased so each camp accepts the wording of its own position; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side—including specifically whether cultural variants ever exhibit replicator-level fidelity independent of phenotypic reconstruction, and whether selection operates on discrete units or on distributed cognitive patterns shaped by psychology and social context; and (4) for each crux, identify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it (for example: the cross-cultural transmission of a mathematical proof versus a nursery rhyme; the persistence of apparently maladaptive practices; or laboratory transmission-chain experiments), with each side's explicit prediction about what that case demonstrates.

Keep formulations neither rhetorical nor dependent on obscure named doctrines. Where the disagreement turns on empirical prediction versus conceptual framing, mark which is which. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or requests for external sources.
