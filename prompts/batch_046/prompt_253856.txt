As a novelist preparing a metafictional sequence on philosophical method, I ask you to draft the joint statement that two opposing camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on intuition pumps—specifically, on how thought experiments persuade and mislead. The camps are the Diegetic Realists, who maintain that a well-built pump places the reasoner inside an embodied conceptual space where modal relations become genuinely perceptible, much as narrative immersion makes fictional worlds intelligible; and the Rhetorical Skeptics, who maintain that pumps chiefly exploit affective salience and framing effects to manufacture conviction, producing an illusion of insight that dissolves under formal reconstruction.

Please present, in British English, a concise formal document containing: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims—about the structure of successful pumps, about documented failures of framing, about the gap between conceivability and possibility, and about the absence of any neutral vocabulary for describing mental scenarios; (2) a disagreements table stating, in wording each camp accepts, their precise divergence on whether narrative immersion tracks conceptual truth or merely simulates it, on whether diegetic distance—the simultaneous holding of fictional acceptance and epistemic evaluation—is a cognitive resource or a source of error, and on whether formal analysis can recover any valid content that a pump initially delivers; (3) the cruxes, especially whether altering a pump’s narrative framing without changing its logical structure changes its intuitive verdict; (4) for each crux, one concrete modified case both sides agree is diagnostic—such as a chromatically stripped version of the Mary scenario rendered only in structural-relational terms—together with each side’s explicit prediction about what analysis of that case will reveal. Avoid bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or appeals to highly specialised literature that a small model could not be expected to recall. Keep the statement focused and relatively brief.
