I work as a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but I recently encountered the concept of expected-value fanaticism in an online discussion about rational decision-making. As I understand it, the problem arises when an outcome carries unbounded or infinite value together with a non-zero probability, however astronomically small: standard expected-utility reasoning then demands that you take the corresponding action regardless of the certain, severe costs, which seems to dissolve ordinary moral boundaries—charity, honesty, self-preservation—into pure arithmetic. I want to understand this tension not as an abstract formula but as a lived conflict between calculation and refusal, so I am asking you to write a villanelle that enacts the idea structurally rather than explaining it.

Please compose a nineteen-line villanelle using only two rhymes throughout, arranged in five tercets followed by a closing quatrain. Let the first line be Refrain A and the third line be Refrain B, with both refrains rhyming with each other and with every other line that carries rhyme A, while the interspersed lines carry rhyme B in the standard pattern. Refrain A should speak in the voice of cold calculation—for example, “weigh every ghost against its infinite gain”—and Refrain B should speak in a resisting human voice—for example, “I will not trade the sure world for a chain of maybes”—permitting only the slight variations that repetition in the form requires. Between these refrains, the poem should trace the principle’s progressive application through three successive domains: first, charitable allocation, where a remote cosmic risk dominates tangible local aid; second, professional integrity, where an infinitesimal chance of preventing a catastrophic system failure justifies corrupting an audit trail; and third, bodily self-preservation, where the same logic demands risking certain death for an astronomically unlikely path to universal salvation or immortality. The philosophical turn should occur in the final quatrain, where the speaker realizes that the arithmetic and the refusal have collapsed into one another, and the closing couplet should bring both refrains together so that the rhyme itself sounds like a loop closed with no exit. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I am working without access to academic sources and want only the poem. Keep the meter broadly iambic pentameter, but let the rhyme pairs do conceptual work by linking mathematical or abstract terms—“gain,” “weight,” “sum”—with concrete or bodily ones—“pain,” “fate,” “thumb”—so that the form enacts the colonization of ordinary judgment by the calculus. Render the concept precisely enough that someone familiar with decision-theoretic fanaticism recognizes the problem exactly, yet avoid dense technical jargon; let the repetition, rhyme scheme, and imagery carry the argument.
