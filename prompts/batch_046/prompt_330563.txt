Write an essay on whether the unilateralist's curse—the situation in which independent actors can each take an action that is individually beneficial but collectively catastrophic, without requiring coordination to cause harm—is conceptually distinct from the tragedy of the commons, or whether it collapses into standard collective-action failure with decision-theoretic ornamentation. Weigh arguments for its distinctness, particularly the asymmetry between acting and restraining, the absence of a shared resource pool being depleted, and the fact that harm accumulates without mutual agreement, against reductionist objections that standard game-theoretic models already capture these dynamics. Reach and justify a definitive conclusion. Then apply that conclusion to either international AI governance or solar geoengineering, demonstrating whether framing the problem as a unilateralist's curse rather than a commons tragedy yields a materially different policy prescription. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; argue from conceptual analysis and general knowledge alone.
