I have observed a pattern in sequential institutional choice that I do not know how to classify. A board evaluates several policies and eliminates any policy that is strictly worse than another available policy on every criterion they care about. Once those are removed, they re-examine the remainder, and occasionally discover that a policy that survived earlier rounds is now strictly worse than some blend of other survivors—though the board never adopts blends themselves, only discrete policies. They eliminate it, and repeat. I have noticed that the policy ultimately endorsed can vary depending on which policies were eliminated first, despite each individual elimination being locally justified. I understand that if decision-makers were permitted to commit probabilistically across policies, this ambiguity might vanish and the outcome stabilize. But I want to understand the discrete, step-by-step case: does this path-sensitive process of successive elimination have a specific name? How exactly is it distinguished from the probabilistic framework in which the result is said to be path-independent? And what are the boundary conditions—are there structures of preference or choice sets where even this discrete process converges uniquely?
