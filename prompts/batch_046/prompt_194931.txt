I'm a loan officer at a regional museum and I have to greenlight or reject incoming loans with sketchy provenance — not obvious fakes, but objects where the artist's direct hand is questionable, like heavy studio overpainting, posthumous casts from an original mold, or prints pulled after the plate was destroyed. I want a reusable decision procedure I can run in 20 minutes with no art historian on staff. Base it on the idea that an original's value depends on being counterfactualy tied to the artist's actual decision tree — the object wouldn't exist in this form unless the artist made specific choices — but don't pretend that's the only factor. The checklist should ask questions in order, mark where it stops with reject, where it says "board call, values not facts," and where it's just judgment wearing a costume. Apply it to three cases: a canvas 60% repainted by the workshop, a bronze cast five years posthumously from the artist's mold, and a signed etching printed after the plate was destroyed — include one where the procedure probably says reject but I'd want to keep it anyway. Also say clearly what the procedure can't do when the causal chain is broken but the object still feels right.
