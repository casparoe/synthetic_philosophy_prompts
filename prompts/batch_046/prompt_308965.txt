I advise a national synthetic-biology oversight board that must classify engineered cellular entities into regulatory tiers—Tier A (living organism), Tier B (living-like but non-living), or Tier C (non-living machinery)—before granting containment licences. The board receives frequent proposals and needs a reusable decision procedure, runnable in under an hour by scientists without specialised philosophical training, that translates debates about the nature of life into practical evaluation steps rather than relying on unexamined intuition. The procedure should be grounded in the well-known philosophical divide between autopoietic or metabolic accounts, evolutionary-selection accounts, and functionalist accounts, but it must treat these as independent axes rather than collapsing them into a single definition. Specifically, it should pose ordered questions about boundary self-maintenance, intrinsic normative failure—the philosophical distinction between an entity dying and merely breaking down—and evolutionary potential, specify what observable evidence satisfies each criterion, and clearly identify the points at which disagreement among these accounts forces the panel to make an explicit normative judgement rather than apply a purely factual rule.

Please present the deliverable as a checklist or flowchart in words, with explicit stopping rules for each tier, an honest flag for cases where judgement cannot be eliminated, and a brief note on which steps are procedural formalities masking underlying value choices. Then apply the procedure to three concrete proposals: a synthetic minimal cell with a genome reduced to essential metabolic genes that can divide but requires an external supply of synthetic lipids to maintain its membrane; an engineered viral analogue carrying a synthetic genome that replicates only inside host ribosomes and possesses no independent metabolic boundary; and a self-assembling lipid vesicle containing functional ribosomes and a complete transcription-translation system, yet lacking genetic material and any metabolic closure. The response should be written in British English.
