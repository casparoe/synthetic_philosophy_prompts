ok so i've been chewing on leibniz's best of all possible worlds claim, but not the pop-culture version where god maximizes happiness. the actual position i want to defend: this world is best because it maximizes ontological being/perfection subject to the constraint that its governing laws are the simpest possible ones that can generate that being. evil is privation, not a positive feature, and apparent local failures are just shadow-costs of global law-simplicity. what would genuinely count against this? not just 'there's suffering' — i want structural defeaters: a formal proof that simplicity and being can't be jointly maximized? a physical result showing our laws are actually redundant or overfitted? a metaphysical counterexample — say a world with 10% more being but laws so much simpler they should dominate? rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a committed leibnizian would concede vs dismiss as category errors. also, is the view already insulated: if every evil gets redescribed as privation and every law-complexity gets called 'necessary for being,' has it been qualified into unfalsifiability? is that bad for a metaphysical view, or just normal? i want an honest exposure map, not apologetics.
