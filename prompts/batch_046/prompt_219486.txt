Write a six-month dissertation prospectus in philosophy of mind and cognitive science on the Gödelian analogy between formal self-reference and mental self-awareness, focused specifically on whether the analogy requires a form of representational closure — that is, a cognitive system's capacity to encode not merely its own representational states but also the rules by which those states are encoded, in a manner structurally analogous to Gödel numbering. State the question with sufficient precision that it has a determinate answer: must a system achieve direct self-reference, referring to its own formal analog of a Gödel number, for self-awareness to arise, or can indirect self-modeling suffice; and does either commitment require accepting that mental representations possess a discrete, arithmetizable syntax? Explain why existing philosophical treatments of this analogy leave the question open, particularly because they tend to treat self-reference as a unitary property rather than a graded structure, and identify the unresolved gap between syntactic encoding and semantic self-modeling that current accounts fail to bridge. Propose a conceptual approach that distinguishes three levels of closure — object-level state representation, meta-level encoding-rule representation, and meta-meta-level validation of the encoding relation — and apply this taxonomy to evaluate whether standard cognitive architectures can satisfy Gödelian conditions without collapsing the project into either pure formal logic or empirical neuroscience. Justify why this philosophical framing, rather than an empirical study of consciousness or a formal proof in logic, is the appropriate method for making progress on whether self-awareness is genuinely analogous to formal self-reference or merely metaphorically related to it. Describe what a successful result would look like, such as a principled taxonomy that separates genuinely Gödelian self-awareness from sophisticated self-monitoring, and specify what would count as failure, such as demonstrating that mental representations lack the syntactic discreteness required for any rigorous analog of Gödel numbering. Provide an honest assessment of scope: can this be completed in six months without original laboratory work, and which elements — such as engaging with specific formal models of representation — risk expanding the project beyond what is feasible for the available time?
