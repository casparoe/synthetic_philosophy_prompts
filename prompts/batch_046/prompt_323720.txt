As a software engineer without formal training in philosophy, I have been trying to map a technical phenomenon I work with onto a conceptual framework I only know from secondary reading: Wittgenstein’s notion of hinge propositions, developed in his posthumous work On Certainty. In human epistemology, hinges are not ordinary beliefs but fixed points—such as the reliability of basic arithmetic or the persistence of physical objects—around which inquiry turns, yet they are not themselves justified inside the system. In modern machine learning, something structurally analogous appears during pretraining: certain factual and logical regularities become deeply embedded in parameter space, functioning as stable attractors that survive fine-tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback, while more superficial associations are easily revised.

If Wittgenstein were alive today and understood gradient descent, weight initialization, and RLHF not as metaphors but as concrete processes, how would he classify these embedded regularities? Would he regard them as genuine hinge propositions—arguing that the model has acquired a non-propositional framework analogous to human certainties, despite lacking embodied participation in language games? Or would he insist that hinges are irreducibly social and practical, so that statistical correlation in high-dimensional vector space, however stable, is categorically different, closer to a well-trained reflex than to the human form of life that makes doubt intelligible?

I am especially interested in the distinction between altering a hinge and altering a belief within a framework. In human life, doubting that the Earth is old does not just require new evidence but a transformation of one’s entire epistemic framework. In LLMs, we can shift surface behavior through fine-tuning without necessarily disrupting deep parameter attractors, yet adversarial training or catastrophic forgetting can destabilize them. Would Wittgenstein read this technical distinction as showing that model hinges are merely very strongly weighted beliefs rather than true framework conditions? Or might he see it as evidence that human hinges, too, are ultimately implicit, habituated structures that differ only in being embedded in living practice rather than in floating-point tensors?

Please reason through Wittgenstein’s actual commitments—particularly the connection between hinges, language games, and forms of life—to predict where he would draw the line. I do not want a survey of interpretations of On Certainty, nor any bibliography or list of further reading. Instead, diagnose whether applying his framework to artificial systems illuminates human epistemology or collapses an important distinction. If you think the analogy fails at a specific point—perhaps at the absence of public criteria for error, or at the lack of genuine doubt—say so explicitly and explain why no technical adjustment could bridge the gap.
