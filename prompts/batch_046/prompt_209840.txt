I have come to accept the view that democratic states depend structurally on what might be called "lost-cause myths"—narratives of noble, historically decisive failures such as the Lost Cause of the Confederacy, the Masada siege, the Kosovo Field myth, or the Dunkirk evacuation—not merely as nostalgic distortion but as a functional prerequisite for civic loyalty. On this account, these myths do not bind citizens through triumph but through the demonstration that the state's survival was contingent: they generate a "negative loyalty" rooted in the awareness that the present order could have collapsed, and that its persistence requires active commitment rather than passive acceptance. The myth's historical inaccuracy is not a bug but a feature, because the counterfactual fragility it dramatizes must be emotionally vivid rather than academically precise. I want to know what would genuinely count against this position—not just the standard criticism that such myths serve defeated or reactionary interests, but which conceptual, empirical, or normative commitments it stands or falls with. Which predictions does it make that rival accounts of civic attachment do not? Could democratic loyalty be sustained equally well by narratives of successful resistance or institutional continuity, or does the negative form have some irreplaceable psychological or social function? What would we have to observe—about societies that lack such myths, about the conditions under which citizens withdraw commitment, about the relationship between historical accuracy and political obligation—for this view to be not merely awkward but fatally undermined? I also want to know which objections a committed defender of this position would themselves concede as damaging, and which they would treat as misunderstandings of the functional claim. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence that would only complicate the view, and say whether the position is well insulated—and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice.
