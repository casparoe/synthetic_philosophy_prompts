My friends Jaden and Sam got stuck in an argument after our ethics seminar on Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor. Then Jaden brought up social media algorithims: they learn exactly what you want and never make you pick, and people clearly love it. He said thats the Inquisitor's vision updated — not forced bread, but a feed so smooth you never feel the weight of chosing. Sam said thats not the same thing at all, becuase you can close the app anytime; the Inquisitor's subjects dont have an exit, and more importantly they arent being asked to carry moral freedom, they're just being lazy.

A recommendation engine offers none of those; it offers convenience. Conflating them makes the Inquisitor's claim about human nature collapse into "people like easy stuff," which is true but trivial.

Jaden then said maybe the trivial version is actually the deep one: that what Dostoevsky called freedom was always just the discomfort of having to choose, and any system that removes that discomfort — whether through religion or code — fulfills the Inquisitor's prophecy.
