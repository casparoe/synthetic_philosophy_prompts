As a journalist who covers science and conceptual foundations, I have been invited to deliver the opening remarks at a three-day interdisciplinary workshop on ontology identification under conditions of principled epistemic inaccessibility. The audience is split between analytic metaphysicians and philosophers of science, on one side, and theoretical physicists and machine-learning researchers who work with unobservable structures but have little patience for framing that does not constrain practice, on the other. The animating idea is that when information about an entity's internal constitution is not merely hidden but structurally sealed—unavailable even in principle—our criteria for identifying that entity with one ontological kind rather than another become radically underdetermined. I need you to write these remarks. The philosophical concept should do real structural work: it should establish why appeals to "what the world is really like" must be restrained in this setting, explain how that restraint reshapes collaboration across the disciplines present, and set the terms for what the workshop can and cannot claim. The tone should be formal, intellectually serious, and welcoming without diluting the difficulty; it should treat inaccessibility as a principled boundary, not a temporary engineering gap. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke highly specific named theories that depend on niche scholarly attribution. Write as though addressing the room directly.
