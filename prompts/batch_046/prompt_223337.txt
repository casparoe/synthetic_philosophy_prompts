I sketched four different rulesets and I honestly can't tell which one is actually smart vs just feels right.

Option A is pure trial-and-error with no talking: you just try fixing stuff, and if your method fails you stop doing it; if it works you keep going. No explanations, just repetition and filtering. Option B is all about immediate feedback loops — everytime someone does a repair right, the group gives them a token they can trade for coffee; if they blow a fuse, they lose a token and have to sit out the next round. Option C makes you write down a specific guess about whats wrong before touching anything. Then you test it safely with a multimeter first. If the guess is wrong, you cross it out loudly and try a new guess — the idea is your bad ideas die, not your appliance. Option D keeps a big shared notebook with diagrams and annotations. Before trying anything, you have to read someone elses previous mistake-note and propose a tool-based workaround using language from the notebook. The repair only happens through the notes.
