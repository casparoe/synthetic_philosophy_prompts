Design a one-paragraph thought experiment about an agent—call her Mira—who has spent twenty years in a closed-loop cognitive restructuring program that has no human designer and no initial programming beyond a generic learning rule she herself adopted at age eighteen. The loop updates her values solely from her prior endorsed values with no external input, and at every stage she fully identifies with her current traits. Engineer the case so that Galen Strawson's Basic Argument yields that Mira is never truly morally responsible for her actions, while a hierarchical compatibilist in the Frankfurt tradition yields the opposite verdict—that she is responsible because her second-order endorsements align with her first-order motives. After the scenario, present the reasoning for each position: for Strawson, trace the authorship regress through the loop back to the initial adoption, which she did not ultimately author; for the compatibilist, point to the sustained hierarchical identification across iterations. Then identify the structural feature that makes the theories diverge (the combination of endless self-causation without an uncaused origin together with present endorsement), explain what happens if that feature is removed (for instance, if an external therapist designed the loop, collapsing the case into a standard determinism-versus-compatibilism dispute), and show how a Strawsonian would reply to the compatibilist's likely attack—specifically, why continuous endorsement within a loop she never originally authored fails to establish true moral responsibility.
