I've been chewing on this weird hybrid of Quine's naturalism and some cognitive-science framing I ran into. The idea is that moral values aren't just peripheral conventions in the web of belief, like Quine sometimes sounds like he's saying. Instead, they're kind of like inductive scafolds - we hold them fixed not because they're true in some metaphysical sense, but because revising them makes the predicive modeling of other agents collapse. So 'murder is wrong' sits near the center not for logical reasons but because if you let it float, your social-world ontology gets too noisy to navigate. It's Quine minus the dismissive tone plus a funtional story about beleif revision. What'd actually defeat this? I don't want the standard 'Quine was an emotivist' reply - I want the real defeatters. Which patterns of moral disagreement, which evolutionary-psych results about cooperation, which formal limits on belief revision, or which intuitions about moral progress would really hurt it? Rank them fatal to merely awkward. And tell me straight: is this just Quine with extra jargon wrapped around it, and if it's been qualified into unfalsifiability, is that a problem for a view like this?
