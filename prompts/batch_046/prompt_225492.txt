In a recent philosophy-of-mind essay, the author summarizes the embedded-agency framework as follows: "Because an embedded agent is physically instantiated within the world it models, it cannot possess logical omniscience—even simple logical truths may lie outside its derivational reach. This physical embeddedness entails that an agent must regard its own future reasoning as causally opaque to itself: it can never rationally bind itself now to a conclusion it has not yet derived, not even for elementary arithmetic. Consequently, any decision theory that requires an agent to pre-compute all logical consequences of its actions—such as causal decision theory applied to self-modification—is inapplicable to real agents. Furthermore, embeddedness dissolves Newcomb's problem, since the predictor's presence in the agent's environment breaks the symmetry required for evidential dependence; both agent and predictor are embedded subsystems, so acausal reasoning collapses into ordinary causal interaction." The chain of reasoning strikes me as suspicious—particularly the inference from logical-omniscience failure to the impossibility of rational self-commitment, and the claim that embeddedness undermines evidential reasoning in predictor scenarios. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is faithful to the core embedded-agency literature, what is a plausible but overstated extension, and what defenders of the view would explicitly deny. In particular, does the rejection of logical omniscience actually entail causal opacity toward one's own future derivations? And does embeddedness genuinely dissolve Newcomb-style evidential dependence, or is that conflating two distinct issues?
