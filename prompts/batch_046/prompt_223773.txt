I'm co-moderating an upcoming podcast episode with a philosopher of logic who has recently been defending an unconventional response to the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents. Rather than resolving the obstacle by globally weakening logical omniscience or by restricting the agent's language, she argues that we should restructure the agent's doxastic architecture into what she calls "staged modal frames": a hierarchy of reasoning contexts in which the self-trust predicate applies only downward within a given stage, never upward or laterally across stages, with consistency audits delegated to an external module that operates in a separate metalanguage. I was discussing the view with colleagues last week, and we kept circling back to the worry that this just relocates the problem rather than solving it, but I want the interview to go well beyond having her restate the framework or repeat standard rebuttals.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that press where the literature actually presses. I also want one closing question about what kind of formal result—perhaps a proof that a single-stage agent with her restrictions can still derive its own consistency, or an empirical demonstration that real-world reasoning doesn't partition cleanly—would cause her to abandon or significantly revise the staged-frame view. For each question, include a brief follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Avoid generic biographical questions; keep the focus tightly on the content of her position and its vulnerabilities.
