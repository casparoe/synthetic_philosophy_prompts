I've been trying to work out for myself whether morality can really be about following rules regardless of what happens. In the course of reading around, I found a summary—on a popular philosophy blog—of something called "the paradox of deontology." The author put it like this: deontology holds that certain acts are intrinsically wrong, such as lying or killing, no matter how good the results might be. The paradox, according to the post, is that if people actually lived by these absolute rules in every case, society would fall apart: doctors could never soften bad news to protect a patient, and nobody could break a promise to stop a catastrophe. Therefore, the blogger argued, deontologists secretly depend on people occasionally violating the rules to keep the world running. This means deontology is not really about unconditional rules at all, but is instead a disguised form of rule-utilitarianism—one that picks the rules that usually produce the best outcomes, with hidden exceptions built in. The post also mentioned "threshold deontology," where philosophers allow rule-breaking only when the consequences cross some catastrophic threshold, but dismissed this as merely smuggling consequence-based reasoning back into the theory. From all this, the blogger concluded that deontology is self-defeating: it needs the very outcome-based reasoning it officially rejects, and since it cannot justify those exceptions on its own terms, the view collapses into contradiction.

I am not trained in philosophy, but I want to know whether this is a fair account. Could you separate out what is accurate, what is a standard simplification, and what is a misrepresentation? I am especially unsure whether deontology really requires absolute rules with no exceptions, whether the "hidden rule-utilitarianism" charge holds up, and whether threshold deontology is fairly described as just utilitarianism in disguise. Finally, does the blogger's conclusion—that deontology is self-defeating—follow from the parts that are correct, or does it depend on the errors?
