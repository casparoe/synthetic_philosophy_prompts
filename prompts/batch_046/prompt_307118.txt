Write an essay about Dennett's idea of counterfeit people—AI or robots that can fool us into treating them like real minds even though they aren't. Most discussions just ask whether some chatbot is "really" concious, but I want to push it somewhere weirder. Imagine a culture that deliberately creates "licensed counterfeits": AI systems that everyone knows lack inner lives, but that the society grants limited social roles anyway—like eldercare companions or teaching assistants—because it's practical and transparent. Dennett's worry is usually about deception and being fooled, but here nobody's fooled. So is there still something wrong with a culture that chooses to live with these counterfeits? Weigh whether this corrupts our concept of personhood, or whether personhood was always partly a social contract that can be extended by choice. Reach an actual conclusion: should such a culture be embraced, banned, or tightly regulated? And say whether Dennett's original fear about counterfeit people still applies when the counterfeit label is public and intentional. Don't just sit on the fence—defend a specific view.
