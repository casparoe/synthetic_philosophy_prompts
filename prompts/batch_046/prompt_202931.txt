I'm currently taking a graduate seminar on democratic institutions, where we are comparing sortition, strict term limits, and conventional career-politician systems as mechanisms for legislative selection. While working through the arguments for term limits, I have noticed a recurring institutional pattern that I cannot quite map onto any standard concept in our reading list.

The pattern runs as follows. Reformers argue that career politicians become overly captured by party structures and special interests, so rotation in office—through term limits or even random selection—should weaken those ties and restore responsiveness. But once officeholders are forced to rotate out quickly, they lack the accumulated institutional knowledge and technical expertise needed to draft and scrutinize complex legislation. Consequently, they become heavily dependent on permanent bureaucratic staff, career committee advisors, and the very lobbyists the reform was meant to disempower—except that now these actors operate behind the scenes, are not subject to electoral accountability, and face even weaker oversight because the legislators themselves are transient. The entrenched influence therefore does not vanish; it migrates from visible elected positions into less visible, more durable administrative and advisory roles.

I am familiar with adjacent ideas like regulatory capture, the iron triangle, and the iron law of oligarchy, but none of them precisely describes this specific displacement mechanism, in which a reform explicitly designed to eliminate careerism among elected officials ends up intensifying careerism among the unelected support structures they rely on. Is there an established term in political theory or institutional design for this exact configuration? If not, could you propose a precise definition and a name that clearly distinguishes it from ordinary unintended consequences and from regulatory capture, including the specific conditions—particularly the asymmetry in expertise and tenure between temporary officeholders and permanent support networks—that must hold for it to occur?
