Write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines, followed by a three-line envoi—on the ethics of commemorative absence: the claim that removing a statue or renaming a street can function not as erasure but as a negative monument, leaving a deliberate gap (an empty plinth, an unmarked corner) where presence once stood. Use exactly these six end-words in the standard rotating pattern: plinth, absence, name, shadow, archive, street. The poem should take the negative-monument idea seriously—granting that an empty plinth may be more honest than a retained statue—without resolving whether it succeeds or collapses into sanitized forgetting. Avoid sermonizing about intervention and avoid shrugging that history is circular; the philosophical tension should remain open. Let the form do conceptual work: the repetition and rotation of the end-words should mirror how commemorative absence keeps circling back to the site of removal, with the archive preserving what the street has lost.
