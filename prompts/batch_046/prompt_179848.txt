Okay so I've been reading the cognitive penetration debate (Siegel, Fodor, etc) and I keep seeing a pattern I don't have a word for. First someone says belief changes what you literally see — like knowing a checkerboard is flat makes you percieve it flat. Opponents reply that's just post-perceptual judgement. Then proponents show the judgement is so imediately fused with seeing that you can't split them. Opponents shift: fine, but early visual cortex isn't affected, it's just attention being guided. Then if you point out that attention itself is shaped by conceptual training — radiologists scanning X-rays differently because of theory-loaded expertise — they retreat again and say that's just input selection, not perception being penetrated. Each boundary move sounds okay alone, but together it feels like the claim is being protected by a sliding fence. Is there an established name for this kind of boundary-retreat defense? How's it different from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis? If there's no term, what'd you call it? And please push back hard if I'm just conflating attentional filtering with actual percetual content — I might be, and if so don't be polite about it.
