I just came away from a long, somewhat circular argument with a friend about whether mechanical rigidity is really the right lens for thinking about free will, and I am trying to write out my own view to see whether it actually holds together. I hold the following commitments: (a) sphexishness—behavior that is mechanically fixed despite the appearance of choice—is a more fundamental threat to free will than determinism itself, since determinism at least permits complex, responsive processing whereas sphexishness rules out even the illusion of agency; (b) genuine flexibility is not merely the presence of alternative physical possibilities but requires what I call recursive flexibility, a second-order capacity to revise one’s own decision procedures under rational pressure; (c) a fully deterministic system can in principle possess recursive flexibility if its dynamics include genuine error-correction over its own rules, so determinism does not automatically entail sphexishness; (d) nevertheless, any deterministic system that achieves recursive flexibility will, over time, converge on a fixed-point attractor in its meta-level modifications—what I think of as stabilized sphexishness—unless it is continually perturbed by an open-ended environment; and (e) therefore, free will requires either indeterminism specifically at the meta-level or an irreducible environmental coupling that prevents such stabilization.

Before I commit to this in a longer essay, I want an audit rather than a lecture: which of these commitments actually pull against one another, which tensions only look severe but dissolve on inspection, and what the least costly revision would be to restore consistency? In particular, does (c) survive (d), and does (d) undermine the motivation for (b)? Does (e) collapse into a mere empirical claim about complex systems rather than a philosophical condition on agency? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each what has to give, and say whether the cheapest fix is to weaken my claim about determinism, to drop the recursive-flexibility requirement, or to accept that stabilized sphexishness is unavoidable and redefine free will around it.
