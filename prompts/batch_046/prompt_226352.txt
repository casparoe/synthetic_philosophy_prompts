Rather than lecture on the view, I want a runnable in-room simulation that makes the group feel the absence of a central observer as an active restructuring problem, not merely a missing piece. Design a role-based or card-based exercise in which participants receive fragmented sensory and temporal inputs in sequential rounds, must construct competing narrative reports, and are incentivized for coherence rather than accuracy; the rules should be arranged so that they naturally confabulate a privileged “master draft” and then discover that none is accessible, with payoffs or roles that reward stitching events into a single timeline. Include a precise timeline, minimal materials (printed role cards or input slips), the debrief that maps each simulation stage onto the rejection of the Cartesian theater and onto Dennett’s idea of “starring” or editorial selection without an editor, and an honest note on which feature of the model—likely the neurocognitive mechanism of competitive drafting—this classroom version cannot reproduce and I must supply verbally. Also tell me what will predictably break the first time I run it and how to handle it without undermining the point. The activity should work without technology or assigned readings beyond what is handed out in the room.
