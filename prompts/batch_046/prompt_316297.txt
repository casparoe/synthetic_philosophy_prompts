She is very good at deflecting objections by insisting that counterexamples are "artifacts of the old absolute-space formulation" or that the constraint in question is "purely dynamical, not ontological."

Please draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond having her restate the view. I want them to press precisely where the literature on relationalism, shape dynamics, and Newtonian mechanics actually presses: include at least one question on whether her global constraint yields empirically distinguishable predictions in a rotating but otherwise empty universe; one on whether that constraint smuggles in a hidden absolute structure under a different name; one on how she avoids circularity when explaining local inertial frames without appealing to distant matter pairwise; and one on what her framework implies for the ontology of spacetime in quantum gravity contexts where background independence is already built in. For each primary question, provide a sharp follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—especially anticipating her tendency to invoke the dynamical-versus-ontological distinction and to dismiss rotating-empty-universe scenarios as illegitimate artifacts of substantivalist framing. Avoid generic biographical openings, and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or a reading list.
