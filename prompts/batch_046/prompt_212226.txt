I'm in a philosophy of mind reading group and we've hit a wall on teleosemantics — specifically whether evolutionary selection history can actually ground representational content without smuggling in some normative idea of correct functioning that hasn't itself been naturalized. For months the naturalizers have argued that proper function is just a causal-statistical fact about what a mechanism was selected for, so misrepresentation is literally a token failing to match that causal profile. The other side keeps replyng that labeling a failure a malfunction already presupposes a standard of correctness that evolutionary history alone doesn't supply; history just describes what happened, not what ought to happen. We all agree on the basic biology and on the formal structure of the account, yet nobody's budged an inch. I think part of the problem might be that one camp is asking a metaphysical grounding question and the other is asking about explanatory adequacy, but I'm genuinely unsure if that's the whole story.

There's also a niche case we've been using that seems to make things worse: imagine a bioengineered neural network built from scratch with zero evolutionary history, designed to track predators exactly like a frog's visual system. If it misfires and represents a shadow as a predator, does it misrepresent? The naturalizers seem forced to say either that it doesn't represent at all — which feels wrong — or that design history counts, but then they've just replaced evolutionary selection with designer intentionality. The normativists say it does misrepresent, but only because we imported normative standards into the system, which just pushes the problem back. So is this stall mainly about hidden empirical assumptions — do we treat the engineered case as a genuine counterexample or as a different kind of system? Is it about standards of evidence for what counts as a reduction? Or is it a bedrock clash about whether normativity can ever be reduced to causation, with the engineered case just making the intuition explicit? I want a committed answer: take a definite diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this really is, say what each side actually says that supports it, and tell me what would count as progress — maybe a formal result on circularity, an empirical finding about neural selection, or a reconception of the question itself. Please write in American English, don't just list options and leave me hanging, and end with \answer{[your diagnosis]}.
