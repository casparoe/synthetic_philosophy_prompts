ok so I'm prepping for a philosophy of art discussion group and I'm trying to map out where I actually stand on the whole separate-the-art-from-the-artist thing. here's my rough package of commitments:

1. I'm a moderate moralist about art: if a work is genuinely morally confused in its framing or perspective, that's an aesthetic flaw, not just an ethical one.

2. but I'm basically anti-intentionalist - authorial biography and intent are mostly red herrings; evaluation should focus on what's in the work itself.

3. I think we should be able to praise and enjoy art by genuinely terrible people, provided the work doesn't glorify or endorse the specific bad thing they did.

4. here's the weird one I can't shake: in autobiograhical or confessional work, the artist's actual moral failure can deepen the piece and make it better, because their real guilt or corruption becomes part of the raw material. I don't have a fancy name for this, maybe call it biographical contamination.

5. for purely fictional works I think the artist's real-world crimes shouldn't affect evaluation at all, but for documentary-style or confessional pieces they can actually add weight.

does this hang together? I'm specifcally worried that (2) clashes with (4) since I'm dismissing biography except when I invoke it, and that (1) clashes with (4) because if moral flaws are aesthetic flaws then how can the artist's real-world vice be an aesthetic plus. also I wonder if there's hidden tension between (3) and (5).

can you go through these, rank the tensions by severity, and for each say what'd have to give? also tell me which single revision cleans up the most, and flag any pairs that only look tense but actually dissolve.
