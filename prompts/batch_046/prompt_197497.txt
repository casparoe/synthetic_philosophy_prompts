I've been trying to pin down a pattern I see in talks about the color phi thing — you know, where you flash a red dot then a green dot in a different spot, and people swear they saw green traveling and changing color in the middle, even though the green dot hadn't even appeared yet. The argument always seems to go like this: first someone says this proves consciousness is literally reconstructed after the fact, an Orwellian revision where the past experience itself gets rewritten to match the brain's later story. Then a critic says wait, if you're saying the experience was rewritten, then there never was a real ordered experience in the first place — you're denying consciousness has any temporal structure at all. So the first speaker backpeddals and says no, it's just the record that's unreliable, like Stalinesque airbrushing: the original experience happened in real order, but the report gets falsified. But then they slide right back to saying the brain 'constructs' the timeline retroactively, without explaining whether that construction changes the experience or only the memory. Is there a specifc name for this exact slide — not just using the Orwell/Stalinesque distinction sloppily, but the particular ambiguity about whether retroactive construction targets experience itself or only its archival trace? I don't care about citations, I just want to know if philosophers have carved this out as its own failure mode, or if I'm just seeing people confuse two different things that happen to share a slogan.
