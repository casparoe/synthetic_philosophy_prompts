I'm prepping a session for a small philosphy of mind group I run, and we've been arguing about this strange parallel between Darwin and Turing that I can't shake off. Darwin showed that you can get design-level competence in nature without anyone comprehending what they're doing; Turing showed the same inversion for reasoning—machines behaving intelligently without understanding anything. Both flip the usual story where comprehension comes first and skill follows.

I'd like you to read Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey through exactly that double lens. The monolith pushes the apes toward tools and violence without them grasping why; HAL pilots Discovery with perfect operational competence but clearly doesn't understand death, loyalty, or the mission's purpose; and Dave's final transformation into the Star Child looks like either a breakthrough to real understanding or just another layer of blind, evolutionary competence. Could you write a reading that takes both inversions seriously, scene by scene? I'm especially curious whether the film keeps mechanistic competence (HAL) separate from biological/evolutionary competence (the apes, Dave), or whether it collapses them. Does comprehension ever actually enter the picture, or is the Star Child just more sophisticated incompetence?
