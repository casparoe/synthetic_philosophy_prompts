I'm writing a novel where one character is a cosmologist with a chip on her shoulder about the presumptuous philosopher argument — she's cookd up this chain for a dinner scene and I want to check if it actually holds water. Here goes: (1) Self-sampling says I should treat myself as randomly drawn from all observers in my reference class. (2) Self-indication says I should favour hypotheses that contain more observers like me. (3) Now suppose the multiverse is genuinely infinite, with infinitely many observers scattered across it, but there's also a rival finite-cosmology model with only 10^80 observers. (4) By self-indication, I should rationaly prefer the infinite model regardless of empirical fit, because the probability I'd be one of those 10^80 is essentially zero in comparison. (5) But cosmologists clearly do reject infinite models on empirical grounds — they don't just surrender to the counting argument. (6) So self-indication must be false, or at least not absolute. (7) But here's the kicker she adds: the presumptuous philosopher relies on self-indication to argue for enormous universes, yet he also appeals to self-sampling as a falback when people object. (8) If self-indication is dead, his main argument dies, but if self-sampling survives, then in an infinite multiverse my finding myself in a finite, ordered pocket is still astronomically unlikely, which undermines the finite model for different reasons. (9) Therefore, she claims, you can't consistently reject self-indication without also ditching self-sampling, and anyone who does both loses the right to reason about observer selection at all. I feel like there's a hidden slide in step (4) about comparing probabilities across infinite sets, or maybe an equivocation between observers like me and observers who can ask this question. There's also this niche wrinkle I'm playing with: she distinguishes between indexical observers (who can formulate the selection problem) and non-indexical ones (who can't), and she thinks this saves self-indication without saving self-sampling. Does that actually fix things, or is it just shifting the problem onto who counts as an observer? Which step should I have her reject, and what's the cost? I need it to feel like she's genuinely cornered herself, not just confused.
