Having spent thirty-five years in experimental physics before turning to philosophy of science, I have become preoccupied by the practical question of how a research group ought to choose among competing theoretical frameworks when measurements are noisy, computing budgets are tight, and no single model dominates on every criterion. I should be grateful if you would adopt the persona of an expert philosopher of science who is thoroughly conversant with Herbert Simon's concept of bounded rationality and with the broader literature on ecological rationality. Please think carefully: begin by briefly brainstorming the three criteria you regard as most decisive for this kind of decision—computational tractability, robustness to outliers, and adaptability of aspiration levels—before proceeding to the ranking.

Please then rank the following five strategies for model-selection in precisely these conditions, ordering them from 1 (the strategy you would most trust to yield durable scientific insight) to 5 (the one you would least trust). For each, provide a single concise sentence explaining its position.

1. Full Bayesian comparison with explicit, domain-informed priors and full posterior computation.
2. Classic Simonian satisficing: accept the first framework whose predictive accuracy exceeds a pre-set aspiration level and halt search.
3. The Akaike Information Criterion applied as a penalised-likelihood shortcut.
4. A fast-and-frugal lexicographic rule: reject any model that violates a hard physical constraint, then select the simplest survivor without further comparison.
5. Recursive threshold recalibration (RTR): an aspiration-level procedure in which the acceptance threshold for predictive accuracy is revised after each evaluation in proportion to the observed variance of residuals, so that the criterion tightens when data are stable and relaxes when noise is high, with the explicit aim of preventing premature commitment to a locally adequate but globally inferior framework.

Finally, present your answer in British English. In one concluding paragraph, summarise which feature—adaptive aspiration levels, computational frugality, or robustness to hard constraints—most sharply separates your first-ranked strategy from your fifth-ranked one, and identify one hypothetical further condition (for instance, a sudden change in the noise structure of the measurements) that would be required to overturn your ordering.
