I hold that the Gödelian analogy in Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach succeeds only if mental self-reference is structurally parallel to Gödel numbering: a discrete, compositional syntax in which a system encodes its own encoding rules and reaches a stable fixed-point of self-representation. From this I draw the stronger claim that self-awareness is not merely a product of complexity, but specifically requires this syntactic self-mapping—so that connectionist or purely vector-based architectures, lacking discrete symbol-manipulation rules, could never genuinely instantiate it. What would count against this? I want an honest ranking: which observations or arguments would be fatal, which merely awkward, and which defenders could absorb. In particular, does the existence of robust self-modeling in continuous dynamical systems or in non-classical logical frameworks undermine the claim that discrete syntax is necessary, or only show that analogies to Gödel numbering are inexact? And if mental representation turns out to be essentially distributed and sub-symbolic, does the Gödelian analogy collapse entirely, or can it be reconstructed at a higher level of abstraction?
