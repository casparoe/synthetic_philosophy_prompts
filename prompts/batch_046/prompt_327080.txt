I am a high school student enrolled in an advanced philosophy seminar, and we have been examining whether free will can exist in systems that operate through fixed or revisable behavioral loops. Our discussion recently turned to sphexishness—the phenomenon, observed in organisms such as the sphex wasp, where an agent executes a rigid, repetitive action sequence (for instance, dragging prey into a burrow and then inspecting the burrow's interior in a fixed order) even when the environment has changed in ways that make the sequence pointless or redundant. Rather than treating sphexishness as merely a biological curiosity, I want to explore a conceptual extension that my instructor only briefly sketched: suppose we design an artificial agent that is not locked into a single linear loop, but instead possesses a hierarchical architecture of primary behavioral loops monitored by a meta-loop. This meta-loop can suspend, reorder, or modify the primary loops according to an internal evaluation function, yet it cannot generate new goals outside the scope of its original programming architecture, nor can it alter the evaluation function itself. The central controversy is whether this capacity for internal self-modification represents a genuine escape from mechanical rigidity—perhaps enough to ground a minimal form of free will—or whether it is only a more sophisticated version of sphexishness, because the agent remains fully determined by a fixed higher-order structure.

Write an essay that takes up this controversy and reaches a defended conclusion. You should weigh the argument that flexibility and self-revision are essential markers of agency, so that a system capable of restructuring its own procedural hierarchy has crossed a meaningful boundary away from pure mechanism, against the counter-argument that true free will requires the ability to originate or reject fundamental goals, not merely to rearrange the means by which predetermined ends are pursued. In defending your verdict, clarify whether you think the distinction between flexible adaptation and mechanical complexity is ultimately one of kind or merely one of degree, and explain what your answer implies for how we ought to evaluate potential agency in both biological organisms and artificial systems that exhibit layered, self-monitoring behavior. Do not end on a non-committal note; justify why your conclusion is preferable to the alternative.
