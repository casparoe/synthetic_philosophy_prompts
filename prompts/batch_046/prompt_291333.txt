I was talking with my friend Mara last night over some pretty mediocre pizza and she was going on about this tool-lending co-op she's trying to start in her neighborhood. The idea is that anyone can borrow stuff, and all the rules about borrowing can be changed by vote—including the rules about how voting works. She got really excited about making it totally self-correcting, like every layer of rules can be amended by the layer below it, and she even wants to allow votes that retroactively cancel older votes if people think they were unfair.

But then she hit this wall where people started arguing about whether a vote to change the voting rules should itself be conducted under the old rules or the new ones, and she ended up adding a meta-rule about meta-rules, then another one about that, plus a clause about retroactive nullification that just made everyone more paranoid. She said no matter how high she stacks it, there's always one level she has to treat as fixed or the whole thing collapses into people just voting away the process mid-fight, or worse, undoing each other's decisions backwards in time. She thinks she's being overly burocratic and should just trust the community, but it feels to me like she's bumped into something deeper—like there's always a floor you can't dig through, especially once you let people edit the past.

Anyway, is there an actual philosophical or logical issue here about self-reference, levels, and maybe even time? Or is she just overengineering? And if it's real, is that fixed bottom layer something she should accept gracefully, or can she design around it by banning retroactive changes?
