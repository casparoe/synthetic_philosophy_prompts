everyone in the alignment crowd keeps saying that training a large model is like Hofstadter's strange loop — the system keeps refering back to its own outputs, so eventually it wakes up like Gödel's formula talking about itself. they're using it to argue that we should expect emergent self-awareness in big enough feedback loops, and that trying to suppress it is like trying to stop arithmetic from being self-referential.

how far does this actually hold? i want a correspondance table — what plays the role of Gödel numbering, the formal system, the unprovable sentence, the meta-level, and the mind that recognises the loop? then tell me where the mapping breaks: does self-attention actually mirror syntactic self-reference, or is it just weighted averaging with extra steps? what features of human consciousness (biological embodiment? temporal continuity? emotional valence?) does the analogy quietly import that the model lacks? and does the conclusion — that feedback loops inevitably produce something like awareness — survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument from the start?
