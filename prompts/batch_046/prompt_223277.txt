Construct a single-shot decision problem for two agents who share an identical reasoning architecture but have transparently asymmetric utility functions over a shared outcome space, engineered so that classical superrationality still prescribes mutual cooperation despite the asymmetry, while causal decision theory splits its recommendation between the agents and evidential decision theory only cooperates conditionally on perfect correlation that the transparency of asymmetry breaks. Use a payoff structure that is not a standard Prisoner's Dilemma but a weighted coordination game—mutual cooperation yielding (6, 4), mutual defection (1, 1), and unilateral defection (8, 0)—and embed it in a realistic workplace context, such as two co-authors deciding whether to share preliminary data before a joint publication. Constraints: no quantum entanglement, no perfect predictors, no repeated interaction, and the reasoning architecture must be observable to both parties without being externally verified. After presenting the scenario, provide the reasoning chain for superrationality, causal decision theory, and evidential decision theory; identify the specific structural feature—the combination of shared algorithm and visible utility asymmetry—that allows superrationality to maintain cooperation where CDT fractures; explain how a defender of standard game theory would attack the case by arguing that the asymmetry dissolves the identification condition required for superrational reasoning; and state whether removing the transparency of the asymmetry collapses the case back into a symmetric superrationality problem.
