I hold the view that the Boltzmann-brain problem is not primarily a challenge to cosmological models, but rather a reductio ad absurdum of an implicit premise about consciousness: that being a genuine observer requires not merely the right instantaneous neural configuration, but also some minimal causal-historical integration with an environment over time. On this reading, if that integration premise holds, then randomly fluctuated brains lack the standing to generate knowledge claims, and the statistical worry collapses; if it fails, then Boltzmann brains are fully conscious and the problem becomes merely statistical. Tell me what would genuinely count against this position -- not only the standard statistical-mechanical objections, but also which intuitions about personal identity over time, which results in philosophy of mind regarding externalism or embodied cognition, and which cosmological observations it must accommodate or be undermined by. For each potential defeater, indicate whether a committed defender of the view could dismiss it and why, and rank the threats from fatal to merely awkward. The response should state how confident it is in each of its claims and explicitly identify the point about which it is least certain.
