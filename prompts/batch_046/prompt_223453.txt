Al-Ghazali's occasionalism usually gets taught like it's just theological anti-science, but I think there's a tighter epistemic argument burried in the causation discussion of Tahafut al-Falasifa that standard courses skip over. He doesn't merely deny that creatures have causal power; he insists that we never actually observe a necessary link between cause and effect, only constant temporal succession. The usual framing pits him against Aristotelian natural philosophy, but what if he's actually pressing a point about modal knowledge—specifically, that observing regular conjunction doesn't license counterfactual claims about what would happen if the putative cause were absent or altered? That sounds almost like an ancestor to modern interventionist worries that correlation without manipulability is empty, except he's deploying it to defend divine omnipotence rather than to refine empirical method.
