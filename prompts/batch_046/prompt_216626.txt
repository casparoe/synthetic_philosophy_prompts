I've been working through teleosemantic naturalism and keep returning to a puzzle I'll call "semantic drift without selection pressure." On the standard view, a representation's content is fixed by the evolutionary proper function of the mechanism that produces it. Now imagine a cognitive module originally selected for tracking social dominance hierarchies gets exapted within a particular cultural lineage to represent logical entailment relations, with no intermediate evolutionary selection for that new domain. Its outputs now systematically correlate with entailment, and users deploy them in inference patterns appropriate to logic, but the biological selection history still points only to dominance tracking.

My question is: doesn't the teleosemanticist face a forced choice here? Either she insists the representations remain about dominance—making the logical use a massive, systematic misrepresentation—or she allows that the content has genuinely shifted to entailment, which seems to require granting current inferential role some authority over content and thereby abandoning the core teleological commitment. Is there actually a third option that preserves purely selection-based content determination, or is this drift case a reductio of trying to naturalize mental content without letting conceptual-role elements enter through the back door?
