Having retired after decades in molecular biology, I have recently begun studying philosophy of science and want to direct my limited research energy wisely. Within the debate over whether DNA is genuinely a semantic code or merely a causal scaffold, three conceptual issues strike me as live: first, whether arbitrary symbol-to-meaning mapping is necessary for genetic information, or whether direct chemical causation through the transcriptional machinery already provides sufficient specificity; second, whether epigenetic inheritance and chromatin remodeling undermine the classical information-flow model in ways that demand a new philosophical ontology of heredity rather than a minor amendment; and third, whether stochastic noise in gene expression shows that the code metaphor systematically obscures the constitutive role of non-informational physical processes. Given roughly three years and a background in laboratory practice rather than formal logic, which of these should I prioritize settling first? Please defend your ordering by assessing tractability for someone with my profile, explaining how many downstream debates in philosophy of biology depend on each resolution, and specifying what kind of conceptual or empirical development in the near term would move one of them up or down your list. I am seeking reasoning, not a bibliography, citation list, or survey of the literature.
