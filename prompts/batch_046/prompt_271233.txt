Rather than treating the Protestant ethic as a direct causal variable that "pushes" agents toward capitalist accumulation, Reed argues that values operate like regularization constraints in an optimization landscape: they do not act as forces, but reshape the space of rational action so that capitalist practices become a stable attractor state for value-rational agents. She insists this is not an empirical claim about history but a conceptual reconstruction of what "explanation by values" could mean without collapsing into circularity or post-hoc rationalization.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions for Dr. Reed, each turning on the actual machinery of her view rather than asking her to restate it. For every question, include a brief anticipated reply and a sharp follow-up keyed specifically to that reply. Finally, include at least one question that connects her framework to the meaning of work and automation: if values are landscape-shaping constraints rather than personal motives, does the erosion of the vocational "calling" in automated labor remove a structural constraint that once stabilized capitalist practice, or does that constraint now reside purely in institutional design?
