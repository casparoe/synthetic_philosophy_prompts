I'm putting together a seminar on moral responsibility and penal philosophy, and I'd like a reading of Anthony Burgess's novel A Clockwork Orange that takes the Dennett–Caruso debate seriously without turning the book into a mere illustration. Daniel Dennett treats free will as an evolved, multi-track capacity for evitable self-control: moral responsibility is not a metaphysical mystery but a functional feature of social life that depends on our ability to be moved by reasons and to revise our behavior in response to incentives. Gregg Caruso, arguing from hard incompatibilism, rejects retributive desert altogether and proposes that we should understand criminal behavior through a public-health or quarantine model, treating harmful agents the way we treat carriers of disease—containing them for protection rather than punishing them for what they "deserve." Read Burgess's narrative of Alex's violent youth, his conditioning through the Ludovico technique, and his eventual restoration to agency through these two frameworks.

In particular, I want you to examine whether the prison chaplain's objection to the behavioral modification—that it makes Alex "not a human being" because it removes his capacity to choose—maps cleanly onto Dennett's defense of evitable choice, or whether it actually expresses a libertarian intuition that neither philosopher shares. Ask also whether the state's engineering of Alex's responses confirms Caruso's suspicion that penal practice is already a form of social manipulation that only pretends to respect autonomy, and whether the novel's arc—especially the divergence between its British conclusion, in which Alex eventually outgrows violence through maturation, and the truncated American ending in which he is restored to his old violent self—suggests that moral growth requires internal choice mechanisms or instead depends on changing social conditions. Where does the novel resist being read through either philosopher's lens? For instance, Burgess's own ambivalence about whether restored violence counts as freedom might complicate Dennett's evolutionary optimism without endorsing Caruso's quarantine model. End by saying what the novel dramatizes about punishment and personhood that the philosophical arguments alone do not capture.
