As a novelist preparing an inset chapter for a philosophical work of fiction, I am seeking a short story that renders the hole argument in general relativity not as exposition, but as lived phenomenological crisis. The narrative should center on a cartographer assigned to survey a bounded territory called the Null Fold, a region in which every physical landmark and relational distance among objects remains invariant, yet every coordinate assignment within the boundary becomes radically indeterminate, leaving the map with an unresolvable hole that instruments cannot close. Through this figure, dramatize the metaphysical tension between substantivalism—the thesis that spacetime points exist independently of the events that occupy them—and relationalism, which treats points as nothing over and above the relations among material contents. The story should explore whether this hole reveals an ontological lacuna in the world itself or merely exposes the representational redundancy of our coordinate schemes, and it should do so through concrete sensory detail, dialogue, and the surveyor's mounting epistemic disorientation rather than through discursive argument. I do not want bibliographies, citations, or references to specific original texts; I need the conceptual architecture made tangible and self-contained. The tone should be restrained, formally composed, and suitable for integration into a literary novel.
