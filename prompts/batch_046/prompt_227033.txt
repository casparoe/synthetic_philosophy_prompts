In my philosophy of mind group someone compared Strawson's Basic Argument to the bootstrap problem in computing: you cant compile your first compiler without already having one, so theres an unavoidable dependency on something prior and external. They said moral responsibility works the same — you cant author your own character without already having the character that does the authoring, so the regress is structurally dead, not just hard. They want to drop retributivism on this basis. Can you audit the analogy? Give the correspondance table — compiler, source code, prior compiler, external programmer — then say where the mapping breaks. Does it smuggle in the idea that all causation needs an external designer, which might beg the question? And does the conclusion that retributivism is baseless survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the bootstrap image doing all the work?
