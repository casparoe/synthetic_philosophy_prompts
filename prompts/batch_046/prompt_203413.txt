(2) Hyperbolic discounting is the best mechanistic model for everyday weakness: present rewards get overweighted.
(3) There's no "true self" behind the preferences; the long-term planner and the present-biased agent are just different temporal parts, none more authentic.
(4) Because of (3), weakness of will is basically a rational mistake across time, and moral blame only makes sense if she failed to use available precommitment strategies.
(5) I also think Sartre's idea of bad faith applies here: saying "I have weak willpower" treats yourself as an object with fixed traits, denying you could have done otherwise.
(6) I'm convinced that neuroscience points to determinism — every choice is just prior brain states — so "radical freedom" is mostly an introspective illusion.
