Then write a dialogue between two people at a rationalist meetup — call them A and B, but don't make thier positions strawmen. A is pushing the hedonistic imperative: using coming biotech (precision neuromodulation, synthetic affect architechtures, maybe epigenetic tweaking of nociceptive pathways) to abolish suffering entirely, not just mask it. They think suffering is an evolutionary kludge and we can preserve all the useful signal — where damage is, what to avoid — while stripping the bad feeling. B is skeptical but not a sadist; they agree unnecessary suffering is obviously bad. Thier argument is about what B calls "contrast-dependent commitment": the idea that our deepest values only have real weight because they're anchored in genuine negative possibility-space. If you engineer away all risk of real loss, care becomes performative, like playing chess with no stakes.

Make them actually respond to each other. A should ask whether stakes need to be recieved as suffering, or whether anticipated loss without valence is enough to keep commitment genuine. B should push back on whether "clean signal" pain is even intelligible — can you understand harm without some affective grasp of harm? Keep it casual, forum-style.
