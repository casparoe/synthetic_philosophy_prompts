Write an essay on the following controversy. Daniel Dennett's multiple drafts model rejects the Cartesian theater—the picture of conscious experience being projected onto a single, unitary screen for an inner observer. Yet critics contend that the model either smuggles the theater back in as a distributed "narrative center of gravity" or, in avoiding that, abandons any determinate fact about what a subject is experiencing at a particular moment. To address this, consider the notion of "draft locking": the graded stabilization of a perceptual report so that it becomes available to verbal and behavioral control without being broadcast to a central audience. Assess whether draft locking can recover temporal determinacy without functionally reinstating the theater it claims to eliminate. Weigh the argument that determinacy is conceptually indispensable and that draft locking is merely a theatrical display relocated across competing editorial subprocesses against the argument that determinacy was always an illusion produced by retrospective narrative smoothing and that consciousness requires no single "when" of occurrence. Reach and justify a clear conclusion: either that the Cartesian theater is genuinely dissolved and the loss of strict determinacy is an acceptable cost, or that determinacy necessarily requires a functional equivalent of a central observer that the multiple drafts model cannot avoid. Argue from the internal conceptual structure of the positions rather than from external authorities, and do not end on a non-committal note.
