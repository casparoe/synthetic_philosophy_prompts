There's this thing I keep noticing in news coverage after big failures — like a bridge collapse or a blackout. First everyone points at the one guy who signed off that morning, or the budget cut last year. Then some historian or geologist comes in and says actually its about the river's sediment load building since the 1800s, or the climate shift over decades, and the individual call barely mattered. But then they pivot and talk like the slow force was kind of testing us — the drought revealed the fragility, the geology was waiting to expose the error. So we've gone from individuals don't matter against deep time to deep time has this weird pedagogical intent. Is there a name for this specific three-step dance? I know Braudel wrote about long structures vs events, but this feels different — its not just saying both exist, its using the long structure as a kind of hidden critic. Is that part of the longue durée idea, or is it a separate mistake where people accidentally re-anthropomorphize geography?
