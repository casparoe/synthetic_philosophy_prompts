I've been stuck in an argument with a friend about hedonism and whether pleasures actually share anything in common. The usual split is qualitative hedonists saying there's one distinctive feeling, and heterogeneity folks saying pleasure is just family resemblence -- eating pizza and solving a proof feel totally different, we just slap the same word on them. But our debate loops: he says if there's no shared phenomenal feature then well-being can't be unified, I say maybe well-being doesn't need a single substrate, and then we just stare at each other. Is this a real disagreement about phenomology, or verbal -- about what 'pleasure' must pick out? Or is there a hidden normative premise about whether a good life needs internal coherence? I also think part of the stall might be that philosophers use different test cases, sensory vs intellectual, without agreeing which ones count as data. What's your diagnosis -- substantive, verbal, bedrock intuition, or are we answering different questions? And be honest: could a precise neuroscientific account of reward break it, or a conceptual distinction separating the feeling from the wanting? Don't survey Mill and Sidgwick; tell me what kind of stall this is and whether anything should actually be expected to move it.
