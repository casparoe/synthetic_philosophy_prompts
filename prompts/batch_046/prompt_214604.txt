Write an essay on the scope and limits of coherence arguments for expected utility maximization when applied to agents with structurally fragmented belief and preference systems. But consider a psychologically realistic agent whose cognitive architecture maintains separate modules for practical planning, affective forecasting, and retrospective evaluation, each with its own locally consistent preference ordering and credence distribution, yet lacking any overarching integration mechanism. The controversy is whether the normative force of the coherence argument extends across these modules: must the agent integrate them into a unified expected-utility framework to avoid being vulnerable to a cross-contextual Dutch book, or is local coherence within each module sufficient for rationality? Weigh the arguments on both sides—paying particular attention to whether an external bookie can actually exploit the fragmentation, whether the very notion of a global preference presupposes a unitary self that the fragmented agent lacks, and whether representation theorems can be reconstructed at the modular level. Reach a clear conclusion and justify it: defend either the integrationist view, the localist view, or a qualified intermediate position that specifies exactly when fragmentation becomes rationally defective.
