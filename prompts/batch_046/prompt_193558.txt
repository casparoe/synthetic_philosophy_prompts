The audience is a philosophy of religion and metaphysics seminar composed mainly of analytic metaphysicians who work on personal identity—mostly reductionists in the Parfit tradition, a few animalists, and one philosopher of neuroscience—none of whom read Pali or Sanskrit, all of whom are skeptical that "Eastern" frameworks can meet analytic standards of rigor, and all of whom care deeply about logical coherence, empirical compatibility with cognitive science, and whether the view collapses into familiar Western positions dressed in unfamiliar vocabulary. Draft my Q&A prep sheet: list the eight objections I will actually receive, ordered by how damaging they are and how likely they are to be raised, phrased in the vocabulary and framing these specific interlocutors would use; for each, provide a concise reply I can deliver in about two minutes, followed by an explicit note on where that reply is weakest or concedes ground; include the one objection I am hoping nobody asks; and conclude by stating, with an honest assessment of your confidence, which point in the defense you are least sure about. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and avoid framing the objections as requests for textual exegesis—treat them as metaphysical and conceptual challenges.
