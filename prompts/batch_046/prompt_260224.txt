I have been working through my own position on Carter's doomsday argument and its relation to longtermist ethics, and I would like a consistency audit before I present it in a reading group. My current commitments are these: (1) I accept the doomsday argument when it is formulated with the self-sampling assumption applied to a temporal observer-moment reference class rather than to the biological species Homo sapiens; (2) I reject the self-indication assumption on the grounds that it violates the principle of indifference; (3) I take (1) and (2) together to justify a non-negligible credence in human extinction within the next few centuries; (4) I am nevertheless a longtermist, in the sense that I believe we ought to prioritise actions whose main benefits lie in the very distant future; (5) I think the doomsday argument, if sound, actually strengthens the case for rapid technological development to reach a stable large population, rather than undermining longtermism; (6) I hold that observer-moment weighting should be uniform across time but not across possible worlds with different total observer counts. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? In particular, please address whether (3) and (4) can be jointly held without special pleading, and whether my rejection of SIA in (2) is compatible with the weighting scheme in (6). Please separate what is settled in the literature from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers and decision theorists who work on this, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus. Write your answer in American English.
