I'm giving a seminar next tuesday defending a revisionist reading of Chesterton's fence to a room that's mostly Burke scholars plus one analytic philosopher who thinks conservatism is just risk-aversion with fancy footnotes. Thesis: the fence isn't a blanket prohibition on change, it's a defeasible burden-shifting principle — you don't tear something down just because it's old; you have to show its functional role is obsolete or replicable at lower social cost. I combine this with Burke's idea of prescription but inject a framing I call "functional redundancy mapping," basically treating institutions like nodes in a dependancy graph where removing one requires mapping its outgoing edges first. I want Q&A prep, not a full defense. The audience knows Chesterton and Burke in the original, they care about anachronism, and they'll call out analytic smuggling. Give me about eight objections I'll actually recieve, ordered by damage. Phrase each the way they'd say it — not like a LessWrong post. For each: quick verdict on force, what I should concede up front, a two-minute reply, and where that reply is soft. What's the one answer that has to land? And what's the question I'm praying nobody asks? Keep it casual — I spelled dependancy wrong on purpose, don't fix it.
