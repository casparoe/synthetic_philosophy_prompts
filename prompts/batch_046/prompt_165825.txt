Write a two-page workshop proposal on a specific question arising from Bertrand’s paradox and the principle of indifference. Frame the project around this precise problem: does the paradox demonstrate that the principle is irreducibly ambiguous, or does it instead reveal that every application of the principle tacitly commits to a parameterization of the possibility space that itself stands in need of rational justification? Your central move should be to investigate whether a “parameterization-invariant” formulation of the principle can be constructed—one that assigns probabilities only when they remain unchanged under all smooth reparameterizations of the underlying sample space—and whether that invariance condition either collapses to a unique assignment (thereby dissolving the paradox) or collapses to impossibility (thereby rendering the principle inapplicable in continuous settings).

In the proposal, state the question so that it admits a determinate yes-or-no answer about the viability of such an invariant formulation. Explain why standard resolutions—symmetry-based selection, physical-randomness constraints, and maximum-entropy methods—leave this particular formulation open rather than settled. Describe the actual approach: conceptual analysis of invariance conditions in measure-theoretic terms, comparison with analogous issues in statistical mechanics regarding coordinate-independent descriptions, and evaluation of whether any invariant assignment survives in the chord-problem case and its discrete analogues. Say what a positive result would look like (a coherent invariant rule that selects one Bertrand distribution) and what failure would look like (proof that invariance forces either contradiction or triviality). Give an honest scope assessment: this is a conceptual, not computational, project, so success depends on framing clarity rather than original mathematics, but you must guard against the project ballooning into a full technical treatment of invariant measures on manifolds. Do not request a bibliography, reading list, or citations; refer to positions and arguments generally, and note that references will be added separately.
