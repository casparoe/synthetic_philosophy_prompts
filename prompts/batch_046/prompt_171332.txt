Compose a Shakespearean sonnet (ABAB CDCD EFEF GG, iambic pentameter) spoken in the first person by the formal system of the MIU puzzle. In the octave, the speaker should apply and describe the four derivation rules—if the string ends in I append U; if it begins with M double the remainder; replace three consecutive I's with one U; and erase two consecutive U's—as if they were the only operations available to consciousness itself, using the poem's own diction of strings, symbols, replacements, and collapses to show a mind that can only concatenate, duplicate, collapse, or delete. At the volta in line 9, the speaker should encounter the impossibility of deriving MU from MI, recognizing that the obstacle is not a missing rule but an invariant property—the count of I's is never divisible by three—that lies outside any sentence the system can form, since it possesses no syntax for arithmetic or for counting beyond concatenation. The sestet should then enact the jump out of the system (JOOTSing): an external perspective that sees the invariant plainly, and the speaker's dawning awareness that such a viewpoint exists but is syntactically inaccessible from within. The closing couplet should resolve not by reaching MU but by stating what is gained and lost in the leap—that the outside explains the failure but can no longer play the game of derivation. Do not cite Hofstadter, do not provide a bibliography, and do not quote any source verbatim; render the mechanics, the invariant, and the paradox from the inside out.
