As a journalist who writes about science and the ideas behind it, I have come to hold a somewhat heterodox view about the Anthropocene: I think it should be treated not as a stratigraphic epoch awaiting a formal Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point, but as a historically constituted rupture whose start date is irreducibly contested and whose authority to declare it lies outside geology altogether—specifically with the democratic, interdisciplinary, and indigenous institutions that negotiate collective human responsibility rather than with the International Commission on Stratigraphy alone. More precisely, I believe the search for a single geochemical spike—whether the mid-twentieth-century plutonium and plastic signatures associated with the Great Acceleration, the late-eighteenth-century carbon and methane shifts tied to industrialization, or the 1610 Orbis dip linked to the Columbian Exchange and the collapse of Amerindian populations—rests on a category error, treating a normative transformation in human-planet relations as if it were an empirical boundary in rock. I also think that even if stratigraphic correlation has practical value, the decisive criterion for the Anthropocene’s onset should be reflexive: the emergence of institutional capacities for planetary-scale monitoring and self-awareness, which means the relevant date might be far more recent than any physical marker, and the decision about it should be pluralistic, incorporating historians, climate scientists, and indigenous knowledge systems, because the rupture is cognitive and political as much as geochemical. Given this, I want an honest assessment of how exposed this position actually is. What would have to be true for it to be wrong? Could a stratigraphic purist demonstrate that the rock record genuinely demands a single, technically determinable boundary and that interdisciplinary pluralism is conceptually incoherent? Could someone show that reflexive awareness is either too diffuse or too recent to serve as a meaningful epochal marker, or that my framing merely relocates the debate without risking anything? Is my view genuinely falsifiable, or have I qualified it into insulation—able to shift the start date or the decision-maker whenever challenged—so that it risks nothing? And if it is well insulated, is that a virtue for a normative claim about responsibility, or a vice that renders it intellectually empty?
