ok so I'm putting together my views on the paradox of tragedy for a small seminer and I want a consistency check before I present. Here's roughly where I've landed, with maybe a typo or two in my reasoning:

(1) I reject hedonism about aesthetic value — art can be valuable because it yields emotional knowledge or understanding, not just pleasure — so the paradox only bites if you think pleasure is the currency of art.

(2) Still, I think there's something practically irrational about seeking out experiences that cause genuine grief, even when the object is fictional. So I do think tragedy involves a voluntary embrace of distress that needs explaining away.

(3) I think aesthetic framing gives us distance — we aren't actually mourning a real death, just directing grief-like states at made-up targets. So the emotion is real in form but not in practical commitment.

(4) But I also think the phenominal feel is identical to real grief — same bodily heaviness, same affective texture — it's just aimed at a fiction. Distance doesn't dull the hurt.

(5) My positive account is that tragedy functions like an affective gym — we go to build emotional resilience and regulation skills, not for pleasure or knowledge. So the irrationality in (2) gets cancelled by long-term training benefits.

(6) I also lean on an evolutionary backstop: cultures that center tragic art wouldn't persist if it reliably left people emotionally worse off, so it must be adaptively net-positive.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in real tension, which ones only look bad but dissolve on inspection, and rank them by severity. For the worst ones, say what the cheapest revision is and what I'd have to give up. Be specific about whether (3) and (4) can actually do the work I want them to do together, and whether my evolutionary point in (6) is compatible with the idea that the grief in (4) is phenominally real.
