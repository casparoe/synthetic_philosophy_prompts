Write the specification of desiderata that a theory of incommensurable values and hard choices must meet to be adequate, intended for a philosophy of practical reason seminar. Frame the specification around a distinction not always sharply drawn in the literature: ontological incommensurability, in which values lack any common metric in principle, versus epistemic incommensurability, in which a metric exists but is cognitively inaccessible or constructively unavailable to the agent. For each desideratum, provide the conceptual or phenomenological case that makes it non-negotiable—such as preserving the experience of hard choice without reducing it to ignorance of weights, or respecting that agents do not experience themselves as arbitrarily breaking ties—versus those that are preferences of particular camps, like preserving a strong form of agent-relative authority or allowing for rational comparison via second-order values. Specifically, examine whether these three requirements are jointly satisfiable: (a) hard choices are rationally determinable without smuggling in a hidden covering value; (b) the determination does not generate a synthetic meta-value ex post that retroactively dissolves the incommensurability; and (c) the agent exercises a form of normative authorship by committing to one value in a way that makes it salient without being merely discovered. If the trio is unsatisfiable, identify the precise locus of tension—logical, metaphysical, or phenomenological—and indicate which single desideratum, if dropped, eliminates the broadest range of existing positions, including rationalist accounts that treat hard choices as resolvable through reason, pluralist accounts that treat them as permanently tragic, and constructivist accounts that treat commitment as value-creating. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating tensions rather than on a scorecard of named theories. Finally, state explicitly how confident you are in the analysis as a whole, and single out the specific claim—particularly regarding whether commitment genuinely creates normative salience or merely reveals an antecedent structure—about which you are least certain.
