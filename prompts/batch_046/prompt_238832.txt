I've been discussing rational decision-making with a study group, and we've kept encountering a specific argumentative pattern that I can't place precisely within the literature on extreme expected values. The pattern runs like this: someone insists that an institutional or personal choice should be radically restructured—perhaps abandoning near-term public-health investment—on the grounds that there exists some outcome of astronomical magnitude, such as the irreversible loss of all future generations, and that its bare possibility, however unquantified, is sufficient to dominate every other consideration. They do not actually compute an expectation or defend specific probabilities; instead, the extreme magnitude operates as a kind of categorical veto that suspends ordinary practical reasoning. Yet the same interlocutor applies conventional risk-benefit analysis to other scenarios involving similarly speculative tail risks—say, the remote possibility that an untested infrastructure technology could cascade into harm—without invoking any dominance principle, and they drive cars and make dietary choices with no comparable paralysis.

In personal deliberation, I have seen the same asymmetry: an individual rejects a career path with high near-certain social benefit because of a highly speculative tail-risk that they treat as decisively overriding, while making no analogous adjustment to everyday risks. The move feels structurally different from straightforward expected-value fanaticism, because there is no actual multiplication being performed, and different from Pascal's mugging, because no specific agent offers or threatens the extreme outcome with a stipulated tiny probability. Instead, the astronomical stake functions selectively, applied mainly to domains that carry a particular symbolic or existential weight, and it operates more as a rhetorical and psychological suspension of normal reasoning than as a quantitative decision rule. Is there an established concept or literature that captures this exact phenomenon—the non-computational, selectively deployed use of extreme magnitude to override ordinary decision frameworks? If not, I would like a precise definition and clear boundary conditions: what distinguishes it from genuine expected-value reasoning, from epistemic paralysis, and from ordinary risk aversion, and what should we call it?
