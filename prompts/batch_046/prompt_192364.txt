I'm putting together my thoughts on who gets to blame whom and whether hypocrisy actually matters, for a reading group. Here's my profile—tell me if it's cohearent.

(a) Standing to blame is basically about being in the right relational position: either you're the one wronged, or you're part of the same moral community. Outsiders just don't have it, full stop.

(b) I think hypocrisy is mostly an aesthetic or pragmatic defect—like having spinach in your teeth while giving good advice. It dosen't actually cancel standing.

(c) But if you've done the exact same wrong and never come clean about it, then you're actually disqualified, not just embarrassing. You can't invoke a standard you secretly violate.

(d) Institutional roles can confer standing regardless of personal hypocrisy: a corrupt judge can still sentence corruption, a hypocritical parent can still correct thier kid. The authority is structural.

(e) Self-blame is always in bounds, because hypocrisy needs you to set yourself up as morally superior to someone else, and with yourself there's no "other" to be superior to.

(f) Standing is like a non-transferable token—you can't delegate it. Even if I forgive my friend for wronging me, I can't license them to blame a third party on my behalf.

Do these hang together? I want an audit, not a lecture—rank the real tensions by severity, say what'd have to give in each case, and flag which ones only look bad but dissolve if you think about it properly. Treat them all as live commitments, don't just tell me to drop the one that sounds weirdest.
