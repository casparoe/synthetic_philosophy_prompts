Write a dialogue between an Aristotelian philosopher and a behavioural economist on whether hyperbolic discounting resolves or merely redescribes the problem of akrasia (weakness of will). The philosopher should defend the view that akrasia is a failure of practical reasoning — knowing the universal good yet being overcome in the particular by appetite — and should challenge the economist's reduction of this to shifting discount rates. The economist should argue that hyperbolic discounters are dynamically consistent yet intertemporally inconsistent, predicting preference reversals without invoking a fractured self, and should press the philosopher on whether a normative "true self" survives if preferences are constructed intertemporally rather than revealed. Require direct engagement: each speaker must respond to the other's point about rationality and temporal agency, and both should address whether the formal property of intertemporal inconsistency maps onto the Aristotelian universal-particular gap or eliminates it. Write in British English. Conclude with a single-sentence summary of the dialogue's central unresolved tension as the final line.
