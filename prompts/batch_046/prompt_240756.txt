I have been attempting to formalize the central argument of Gongsun Long's White Horse Dialogue—namely, that a white horse is not a horse—by interpreting it as an anticipation of the modern intension/extension distinction: the term 'horse' fixes one class of entities, while 'white horse' fixes a different class because the color predicate introduces a further specification that alters the semantic content rather than merely narrowing the extension. From this starting point, I want to determine whether the reasoning is more faithfully reconstructed within a Fregean framework, where 'horse' and 'white horse' differ in sense despite partial reference overlap, or within a type-theoretic hierarchy, where kind-properties and color-properties belong to distinct logical orders and therefore resist co-predication in the same way. Please distinguish carefully between what is well established about the dialogue's textual structure, its Warring States context, and the Han-dynasty classification of Mingjia, and what remains genuinely contested among specialists: in particular, whether the argument is best read as logical, ontological, or rhetorical; whether modern formal semantics provides an appropriate reconstructive framework at all; and whether 'xing se li' represents a coherent school-wide position or an anachronistic projection. In doing so, do not present any single contested interpretation—especially the proto-semantic or proto-Fregean reading—as the settled consensus.
