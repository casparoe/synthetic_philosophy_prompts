Write a dialogue between two philosophers of cognitive science, Alex and Blake, about whether Daniel Dennett’s four-tier taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—can accommodate a recently theorized synthetic architecture Blake calls a “Quinean” system. Blake should describe this system as one that revises its internal world-model through high-dimensional, distributed parameter updates driven purely by prediction-error signals, lacking any natural-language social scaffolding, yet achieving functional outcomes—coherent belief revision, hypothesis abandonment, and representational integration—that mirror Gregorian cognition. Alex should defend the taxonomy by arguing that Gregorian minds are not defined merely by functional outcomes but by the constitutive role of socially mediated, linguistic transmission in shaping representations. Blake should counter that if substrate-independent functional equivalence holds, then linguistic mediation is an implementation detail rather than a categorical boundary. The dialogue must contain direct back-and-forth: Alex should specifically address Blake’s functional-equivalence claim, and Blake should specifically address Alex’s claim about language as a constitutive medium. Both interlocutors should be equally competent, with no straw-manning. Conclude the dialogue not with a vague stalemate, but with both characters precisely identifying the locus of disagreement—whether “kind of mind” is determined by causal origin, functional profile, or representational architecture—and stating what evidence or argument would be required to resolve it.
