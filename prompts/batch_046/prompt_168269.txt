Write a deadpan parody of a U.S. federal regulatory handbook—modeled on an IRS Publication or OSHA compliance manual—for the fictional "Bureau of Illocutionary Oversight and Felicity Compliance." The handbook should explain, in bureaucratic prose, how ordinary citizens must register, file, and audit their performative utterances under the invented "Speech Acts Transparency Act of 2024." Target the actual philosophical structure of J.L. Austin's and John Searle's theories of speech acts: felicity conditions (preparatory, essential, sincerity, and execution conditions), the distinction between locutionary, illocutionary, and perlocutionary acts, the institutional dependence of performatives, misfires versus abuses, and the gap between saying and making-it-so. The humor must come from taking these philosophical distinctions literally as tax and regulatory categories—for example, treating "locutionary exemptions" as deductible, "illocutionary filings" as taxable events, "perlocutionary withholding" as a penalty, and "institutional authorization" as a licensing board. Invent one detailed bureaucratic form (e.g., Form SP-1040-P for promises, with checkboxes for sincerity conditions and preparatory conditions) and one sidebar case study (e.g., an audit of a misfired wedding vow or an unauthorized "I do" spoken by a person lacking matrimonial jurisdiction). Include a brief "Taxonomy of Speech Events" table that maps Austin's categories onto regulatory codes. Keep the voice consistently deadpan and institutional: no winking, no direct acknowledgment that this is absurd, and no named living person should be mocked; an invented "Deputy Director of Perlocutionary Affairs" may speak in quotations if you wish, but do not quote any real philosopher verbatim—paraphrase any philosophical claim in bureaucratic jargon instead. The piece should be readable to someone who has never read Austin but should make a specialist recognize that the author understands what is being satirized, especially in the way misfires are treated as procedural errors rather than semantic failures. Write approximately 700 words, formatted with numbered sections and subsections like a government document. Write in American English.
