I work as a thermodynamic compliance auditor for a national energy-regulation office, and each month I must assess roughly six patent applications for nanoscale devices that claim to reduce local entropy through information feedback—effectively modern Maxwell demons. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in the physical-cost account of information (specifically that irreversible erasure of one bit dissipates heat of at least kT ln 2, and that any cycle claiming net work extraction must fully account for this cost), which my team can apply without deep quantum-thermodynamics training. Please deliver your answer as exactly five numbered points, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introductory or concluding paragraph. Point 1 should state the first audit gate: requiring an explicit inventory of every irreversible information-erasure step in the proposed cycle. Point 2 should state the second gate: demanding an entropy budget in which heat dissipated by erasure equals or exceeds the local reduction claimed, and stopping with a rejection if the budget is absent or negative. Point 3 should state the third gate: checking whether storage is genuinely infinite and externalised, or whether a finite reservoir eventually forces hidden clearing costs; it should also mark where the audit halts—namely, where the distinction between mere correlation and acquired information is not a measurable fact but a matter of interpretive judgment, and the case must be referred to a senior expert. Point 4 should apply the gates to three proposals in turn: a fully reversible logic-array refrigerator that claims zero-entropy computation yet requires readout verification involving erasure, which should fail despite its elegance; a smart membrane logging molecular velocities to allegedly infinite remote storage; and a quantum-entanglement engine claiming correlation without local measurement avoids acquisition costs, where the audit must halt and defer to expert judgment. Ensure the first case yields the unwelcome result that verification erasure disqualifies the design. Point 5 should state plainly that the procedure cannot determine whether a genuinely reversible, never-erased loop is constructible in principle, only that if such constructibility is asserted, the burden of proof lies beyond this protocol.
