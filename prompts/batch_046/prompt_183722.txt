Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of practical reasoning under consequentialist cluelessness must satisfy. Take "cluelessness" to mean not ordinary empirical ignorance but a structural inability to assign probabilities to distant, branching consequences of present acts—including cases where the act might influence the values or existence of far-future agents. Specify: (1) the hard cases the theory must handle, with the reason each tests the framework—everyday consumption with nonlinear, institutionally mediated climate effects; existential-risk interventions that could cause catastrophic harm if misapplied; and "value drift" scenarios in which future agents reject the values that motivated the present choice; (2) the distinctions it must preserve without collapse—especially between causal cluelessness (uncertainty about outcomes) and normative cluelessness (uncertainty about whether distant values should be weighted), and between first-order uncertainty and meta-uncertainty about which decision framework applies; (3) the consistency constraints it must respect, including the non-substitutability thesis that local, certain goods (such as reducing present suffering) cannot always be traded against speculative global goods, and the requirement that the theory avoid collapsing into either a deontological veto or a pure precautionary principle; (4) which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable—such as avoiding total decision paralysis—and which are camp-specific preferences—such as retaining full axiological comparability across worlds. Flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable, particularly whether preserving the non-substitutability of local goods can be reconciled with the consequentialist commitment to maximize expected value across all affected parties. Briefly score the standard responses—expectation-based reasoning with heavy discounting, robust decision rules, and contractualist fallbacks—against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring.
