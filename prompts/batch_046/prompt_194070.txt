Write the desiderata that any adequate formal account of coherent extrapolated volition and idealized preference aggregation must satisfy if it is to serve as a normative benchmark for aligning an artificial agent with the values of its human operators—particularly in cases where those operators hold fragmented, context-dependent preferences partly shaped by false empirical beliefs about the space of achievable futures.

For each desideratum, state the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it, and indicate whether you take it to be non-negotiable, negotiable, or likely a camp-specific prejudice. Be explicit about any pair of desiderata you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, and defend that judgment rather than merely cataloguing tensions. The list should cover, at minimum:

(1) How to distinguish preferences that should survive extrapolation from those that should be filtered out as errors or distortions, without the theorist smuggling in her own substantive values;

(2) How to aggregate preferences across time-slices of the same person when later slices reject the ontological framework—e.g., regarding personhood, welfare-subjecthood, or modal realism—presupposed by earlier ones;

(3) How to maintain dynamic consistency when the extrapolation procedure itself alters the agent’s epistemic state in ways that retroactively change the evaluation of the paths leading to that state; and

(4) How to avoid the unilateralist’s curse in multi-operator settings, where each operator’s extrapolated volition is constructed in isolation yet must be merged into a single decision procedure.

Then, briefly, assess how the standard reflective-equilibrium model, a preference-utilitarian aggregation approach, and a contractarian bargaining-over-ideal-points framework fare against your list. Do not present this as an open menu: reach a definite verdict on which framework comes closest to satisfying the non-negotiable subset, and identify the single desideratum that does the most work in excluding the others.

Your response must take committed positions throughout—do not end on a non-committal survey. Where a tension remains genuinely unresolved, say so, but still defend which horn of the dilemma should be grasped and why.
