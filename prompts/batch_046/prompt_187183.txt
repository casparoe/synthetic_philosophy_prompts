I'm on my town's heritage review board, we meet once a month and have to vote on old statues, street names, and plaques that honor people who turned out to do pretty awful things. I keep thinking there's a real gap between "this happened" and "we actually think this guy was great," and also that tearing something down can accidentally hide the bad part of history if we dont leave something honest behind. Could you turn that into a 15-minute checklist I can actually run in a meeting? It needs to ask: was the thing originally meant to praise them or just mark an event, does the name overlap with other local meanings (like the town itself), and should we remove, relabel, or keep-it-but-change-the-context? I want worked examples for three cases: a statue of a mill owner who built the hospital but used child labor, a road named after a mayor who built the sewers but pushed racist zoning, and a war memorial listing one soldier later convicted of war crimes. Make sure one example comes out with an answer I'll probably hate, and flag the spots where the checklist just breaks and I have to use my gut—especially whether an empty pedestal is actually better than a replacement that starts a whole new fight. No need for history books, just the steps.
