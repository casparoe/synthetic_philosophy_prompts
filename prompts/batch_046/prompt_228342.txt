Say Hobbes was alive now and watching the current AI development race — every lab knows that slowing down for safety checks is individually irrational if it means losing market share, but collectively they're all rushing toward something nobody actually wants, classic multipolar trap. Would Hobbes see this as literally his state of nature playing out between corporations instead of people, and therefore argue for a global soverign with absolute authority over AI development? Or would he say the analogy fails because in his account the war is driven by physical scarcity and fear of death, not by capital markets and compute clusters? I'm also wondering if he'd think the race to the bottom in safety standards is actually less dangerous than the race in attention-economy algorithims, since one kills bodies and the other just rots minds. What's your read — does Hobbes's framework apply cleanly here, or do we need a different kind of sovereign for code-mediated compitition?
