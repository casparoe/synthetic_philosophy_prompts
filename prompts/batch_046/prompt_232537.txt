My friend and I have spent the last several hours arguing about whether parts of contemporary theoretical physics have slipped beyond the reach of empirical science, and the dispute turned on a hypothetical that neither of us could resolve: if Karl Popper were alive today, how would he judge the combined challenge posed by eternal inflation, the string landscape, and the recent philosophical defenses of non-empirical confirmation? I want to pose the question precisely. Imagine Popper confronting three intertwined developments that postdate his death: first, eternal inflation generating a multiverse in which every combination of physical constants is realized somewhere, so that local observations become probabilistically uninformative about fundamental law; second, string theory’s landscape of on the order of 10^500 vacua, which appears to preclude unique empirical prediction yet is defended by proponents through mathematical consistency, uniqueness claims, and arguments from the absence of alternative quantum gravity theories; and third, the swampland program, which attempts to recover predictivity by conjecturing that the vast majority of those vacua are in fact inconsistent with quantum gravity, leaving only a narrow region compatible with observation. Given Popper’s commitment to falsifiability as the demarcation criterion, his well-known hostility to conventionalist stratagems and ad hoc immunization, and his distinction between science and metaphysics, how would he likely classify these moves? Would he treat the multiverse as an unfalsifiable metaphysical research program, reject the no-alternatives argument as a relapse into verificationism, or distinguish between the unfalsifiable multiverse thesis and the potentially falsifiable swampland conjectures? Furthermore, if he were asked to advise working physicists and philosophers of science on how to evaluate theories that seem systematically to outrun direct empirical access, what modified methodological principle—perhaps a refined demarcation criterion, or a sharp distinction between heuristic fertility and genuine confirmatory value—would he be most likely to propose? I am especially interested in which of his actual commitments would dominate, and where he might be forced to revise them.
