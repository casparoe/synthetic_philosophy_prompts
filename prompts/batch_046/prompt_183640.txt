Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps. Call them the Qualitative Unity camp (they think every pleasure carries one intrinsic phenomenal quality — call it Hedonic Tone — whether it's tasting wine, solving maths, or feeling sunlight) and the Functional Integration camp (they deny any shared feel; pleasures are just states that play the same behavioural and reinforcement role, and apparent similarities are structural).

Structure it like this:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims — things both sides can genuinely sign. Include points on the diversity of pleasures, why intensity-only accounts fail, and what any adequate theory must accomodate. Keep them precise, not vacuous.

2. A disagreements table — the exact splits, each phrased in wording its own camp would accept.

3. The cruxes. I think the big ones are (a) whether minimal or subliminal pleasant states still carry the putative Hedonic Tone, and (b) whether a fully synthetic state that matches all functional/reinforcement criteria but lacks biological substrate counts as pleasure. For each, name a concrete case or thought-experiment both agree is relevant, and write each side's predicted verdict in advance. I want the synthetic-state crux to involve cross-modal comparison — can a digital system 'taste' pleasure comparable to human taste-pleasure, or is modality essential? That should keep it out of the usual pretraining ruts.

4. A brief note on whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal.

Hard limit: max 600 words. No citations, no bibliography, no 'further reading' list — this is for a model that can't search, so don't ask it to name-drop texts it might hallucinate. Just do the conceptual work.
