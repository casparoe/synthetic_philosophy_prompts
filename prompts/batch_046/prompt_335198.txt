Write a poem in blank verse—about twenty lines, split into two ten-line stanzas with a single volta between them. In the first stanza, render Darwin’s inversion precisely: adaptive competence (eyes, wings, instincts) arising from selection without any designer’s comprehension, using mechanistic vocabulary and avoiding vitalist or teleological language. In the second stanza, render Turing’s inversion precisely: a system producing responses indistinguishable from comprehension while lacking the internal reasoning that normally grounds it, using behavioral vocabulary without treating behaviorism as an ontology. The volta should occur at the recognition that both inversions share the same structural absence—intelligence-like results without intelligence-like causes inside. Then, in the final four lines, introduce a third case: a modern language model trained by gradient descent on human text. The poem should end by asking whether this is a synthesis of Darwin’s and Turing’s inversions or a different inversion altogether—competence-like outputs and comprehension-like outputs both emerging from a process that possesses neither. Keep the philosophical distinctions sharp (no hidden variables of mind, no claim that the model literally understands), let the formal turn mirror the conceptual pivot, and write the answer in American English.
