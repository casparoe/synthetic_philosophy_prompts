I've been working through an argument that applies the logic of the Judy Benjamin problem—where an agent updates on a conditional statement without altering her unconditional prior credence in the antecedent—to the structure of empirical reasoning against skeptical hypotheses such as brain-in-a-vat scenarios. The argument runs as follows:

(1) In the Judy Benjamin case, the correct Bayesian response to learning "If you are not in Red Company, then you are in Blue Company" is to redistribute credence within the partition defined by the negation of the antecedent, leaving the agent's unconditional probability of being in Red Company unchanged.

(2) Generalizing, if every piece of observational evidence an agent receives takes the logical form "If skeptical hypothesis H is false, then observation O occurs," then each update should redistribute credence within the H-false worlds without altering the unconditional credence in H.

(3) Therefore, an agent who consistently applies this update rule can accumulate arbitrarily many observations without ever lowering her credence in H.

(4) But this implies that H is empirically irrefutable by structural design, not merely resilient in fact.

(5) Standard skeptical hypotheses are not supposed to be constitutionally immune to evidence—only difficult to dislodge in practice—so this result proves far more than skepticism demands.

(6) Hence the Judy Benjamin resolution, if applied generally to conditional evidence, undermines the very possibility of empirical reasoning against skepticism, which is absurd.

Which step should I reject? I am inclined to preserve (1) and the core insight behind (3), since they seem to capture the distinctive mechanics of conditional updating, but I want to know whether the error lies in (2)'s generalization, in (4)'s inference about structural immunity, or in a hidden equivocation between updating on a conditional belief and conditioning on the corresponding material conditional. If I must choose, I would rather keep the claim that conditional learning need not affect the antecedent's prior, but I want to understand exactly what that commitment costs me—whether it forces me to deny that empirical inquiry can ever bear on skeptical hypotheses, or whether there is a principled boundary that blocks the slide from (2) to (4) without collapsing the distinction between rational belief revision and dogmatic insulation.
