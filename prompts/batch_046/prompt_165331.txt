As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun serious study of analytic philosophy, I have been working through Russell's theory of descriptions and his broader distinction between knowledge by acquaintance and knowledge by description. I am now wondering how he might have reacted to a phenomenon that postdates him: large language models that generate definite descriptions—proper names, technical terms, indexicals—through next-token prediction over vast corpora, with no direct causal or perceptual link to the entities named. Suppose Russell had been given an accurate, non-technical explanation of this mechanism. How, in your judgment, would he have classified the referential status of such machine-generated descriptions? Would he have treated them as genuine descriptions whose reference is fixed purely descriptively, as systematically defective attempts at acquaintance-based naming, or as demanding an altogether different logical analysis?
