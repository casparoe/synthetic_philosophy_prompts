I'm working through my position on relativistic ontology for a metaphysics seminar and want a consistency audit before presenting. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I accept the Rietdijk-Putnam argument that special relativity entails eternalism: since simultaneity is frame-relative and no inertial frame is metaphysically privileged, there is no global present, so all spacetime events are equally real.

(2) I also accept Reichenbach's conventionality thesis about simultaneity—the one-way speed of light is not empirically determinable—so that simultaneity relations are partly conventional rather than purely physical.

(3) I think eternalism can accommodate the intuition behind the growing-block view if we reinterpret "growth" epistemically rather than ontologically: successive slices become epistemically accessible to observers without being added to being.

(4) I take Bell-type correlations to show that spatially separated systems can have non-separable joint properties not reducible to local spacetime regions, which suggests the spacetime manifold may not be the fundamental ontology.

(5) I am a perdurantist about personal identity: I persist as a four-dimensional world-tube of temporal parts, and I take this to be required by eternalism rather than merely compatible with it.

Does this hang together? In particular, I worry that (2) undermines the premise of (1) by making simultaneity conventional rather than ontologically weighty; that (4) removes the spacetime ontology on which the Rietdijk-Putnam argument depends; and that (3) conflicts with (5) because an epistemic growth model seems to privilege present temporal parts in a way perdurantism denies. Please rank the tensions by severity, say for each pair whether the conflict is genuine or only apparent, identify the cheapest revision for each genuine tension and what it costs, and tell me whether any single revision cleans up more than one problem.
