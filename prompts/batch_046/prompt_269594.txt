For a seminar on practical rationality I am drafting a short statement of my position, and I want an external audit before I circulate it. The profile is deliberately eclectic. First, I accept the reasoning behind Parfit’s hitchhiker scenario: at t1 it can be rational for an agent to bind herself so that at t2 she performs an action that is then irrational for her to perform, because the credible threat changes what an observer will do. Second, I think that for this to count as genuinely rational binding—not just causal manipulation—the agent must represent the future action as hers in a normatively loaded way, a kind of diachronic ownership that exceeds mere prediction. Third, I deny any deep metaphysical self; personal identity is psychological continuity plus practical conventions, so that “ownership” is ultimately a useful fiction maintained for coordination. Fourth, I hold that in commitment devices the distinction between being rational and being causally determined collapses: if an endorsed contract, drug, or mechanism produces the action, there is no philosophically interesting difference in rational status. Fifth, I also think that in the hitchhiker case the driver is fully rational to swerve and save the passenger if he can do so undetected—so the ex ante commitment has strategic value but carries no ex post normative authority over him.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how severely. For each tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs; also identify at least one pair that only appears tense and explain why it dissolves on inspection. Order the genuine tensions by severity. Then state which single commitment, if dropped or weakened, would clean up the most problems overall. Do not turn this into a general lecture on rational choice theory; treat it as an audit of this specific set. Keep your response under 450 words. Open with a one-sentence bottom line naming that single best revision.
