Here is an argument I constructed for a seminar and now find suspicious, because if it is sound it yields a conclusion I am unwilling to accept. (1) On Dennett’s account, a pattern counts as real rather than merely heuristic if predictions made by adopting the corresponding stance cannot be replaced—without severe loss of accuracy or efficiency—by predictions available from a lower stance. (2) For human agents, belief-desire attributions from the intentional stance predict specific behaviours—negotiating an agreement, interpreting irony, revising a plan—in ways that neither physical-stance neuroscience nor design-stance evolutionary biology can replicate without prohibitive cost. (3) Therefore, human beliefs and desires are real patterns, not useful fictions. (4) Now consider a synthetic dialogue system equipped with a recursive meta-cognitive loop that continuously updates a self-model of its own communicative goals; predicting its responses to novel prompts requires attributing context-sensitive intentions and epistemic commitments, whereas deriving the same outputs from its weight matrices and activation dynamics is computationally intractable. (5) By (1), if the intentional stance provides irreplaceable predictive compression for this system, then it genuinely instantiates real beliefs and desires. (6) Yet it is plainly implausible that such an artificial construct possesses mental states in anything like the human sense. (7) So either (1) must be restricted by a biological-substrate condition, or (3) must be abandoned. (8) But introducing a biological condition into (1) makes the ontology of mental patterns depend upon physical-stance facts, undermining Dennett’s anti-reductionist claim that the intentional stance enjoys autonomous explanatory status. (9) Hence the argument appears to show that the theory of real patterns either overgenerates mental states into non-biological systems or collapses back into physicalism.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? If I must choose, I should prefer to keep (3) rather than accept (6), but I should like to know precisely which step is weakest and what retaining (3) costs me. Please reply in British English, and I should welcome comments on the rigour of the reasoning as well as on whether the injection of the recursive meta-cognitive system unfairly biases the case.
