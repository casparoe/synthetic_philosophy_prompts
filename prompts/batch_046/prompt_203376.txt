Please explain the conceptual tension between counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail once we relax the standard assumption of perfect predictive transparency. Most discussions treat the predictor as having deterministic access to the agent's decision procedure, but consider instead a noisy classifier: the predictor correctly identifies the agent's intended action with high but imperfect reliability and conditions its response—causing harm or withholding a reward—on that probabilistic belief. How does this epistemic opacity alter the agent's reasoning about evidential correlation versus causal intervention? In particular, does XOR blackmail retain its distinctive evidential structure when the predictor's belief-state is only stochastically coupled to the agent's actual choice, and how does that compare with the counterfactual logic of mugging? I am looking for an intuitive, concept-level analysis rather than formal notation.
