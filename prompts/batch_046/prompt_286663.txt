Then proceed.

Your task is to design a decision-tree taxonomy—built from diagnostic questions and case judgments, not from theory labels—that sorts positions on the origin and nature of the thermodynamic arrow of time. The tree must be constructed bottom-up: first the branching questions that make positions come apart on concrete claims, then the answer-combinations mapped onto named positions, and finally any unoccupied logical branches flagged with a brief diagnosis (incoherent, conceptually unstable, unattractive, or merely unexplored).

Fix the audience as an advanced philosophy-of-physics seminar, and keep the tree to exactly eight leaves or fewer. The taxonomy must include the following positions as distinct leaves (merge only if your tree genuinely cannot separate them, in which case say which ones collapse and why):

1. Fundamental dynamical asymmetry: the microscopic laws themselves are time-asymmetric and thereby fix a global direction.
2. Bare Past Hypothesis: a contingent, low-entropy initial boundary condition with no deeper law or principle explaining it.
3. Law-explained boundary condition: the low-entropy past is fixed by a cosmological law, selection principle, or structural constraint.
4. Radical perspectival view: temporal direction is not globally objective but is local to observers, causal perspectives, or information-theoretic boundaries.
5. Local-gradient decoupling / entropic islands: monotonic entropy increase is subsystem-relative, allowing isolated regions to develop anti-parallel or independent arrows relative to any global gradient.
6. Symmetric two-boundary cosmology: both past and future are low-entropy boundaries separated by a high-entropy intermediate epoch (no unique global arrow, or a reversal at a turnaround point).
7. Non-entropic structural/causal arrow: direction derives from causal asymmetry, structural features of spacetime, or explanatory dependence relations that do not reduce to entropy monotonicity.

For each internal node, frame the branching criterion as a concrete case or claim, not as a label-check. For example, ask what a view says about an isolated region that thermalizes independently of cosmic history, or about a cosmological model with low-entropy conditions at both temporal extremes, rather than asking “do you accept the Past Hypothesis?” Each leaf should be reachable by answering the chain of concrete questions without knowing the position’s name in advance.
