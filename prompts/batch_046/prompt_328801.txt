I'm organizing a seventy-five-minute session for my school's philosophy club—approximately ten students—on Adam Smith's account of sympathy and the impartial spectator. Please design a structured role-play with real, small stakes that makes participants feel the tension between sympathy, which Smith describes as requiring us to change places in imagination with the sufferer and to proportion our sentiments to the cause, and the impartial spectator, which judges from an internalized but detached standpoint. The rules should require participants to first respond as sympathetic agents to staged moral conflicts, then immediately switch to judging those same conflicts as internal spectators, so that they discover through the mechanics of play—before any debrief—why these stances pull in different directions and how self-deception can distort the spectator's voice. Specify roles, a phased timeline, and the debrief that maps each moment onto Smith's framework; include an honest note about which feature of his view—perhaps the spectator's aspiration to universal approval or the social construction of moral norms—the simulation cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply; and identify one predictable failure mode together with a strategy for handling it.
