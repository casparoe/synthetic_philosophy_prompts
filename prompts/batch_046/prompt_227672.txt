I'm working through this argument for a paper on historical explanation and now I'm stuck in the weeds because both horns feel wrong. Here's the chain I built:

(1) Historical explanations come in two kinds: covering-law (CL), which subsumes events under general regularities, and narrative (N), which connects events through agent intentions and temporal ordering.

(2) For N to do real work and not just decorate a CL explanation, there has to be an embedding surplus — causal power that belongs specifically to the sequence and the particular agents involved, not capturable by any law.

(3) Take the fall of the Roman Republic. The narrative says Caesar's specific fear of prosecution, combined with the Senate's specific misreading of his intent, embedded in that particular timeline, caused the crisis. The surplus is the exact configuration.

(4) Now people constantly draw analogies between Rome and modern democracies — institutional decay looks like this. For that analogy to carry real weight, the embedding surplus from Rome must be structurally portable: the same causal configuration could recur with different agents.

(5) But if the surplus is genuinely agent-specific (Caesar's fear, the Senate's misreading), then portability requires those same mental states in modern actors, which is implausable. So the surplus can't be both agent-specific and portable.

(6) Therefore either N has no surplus and collapses into CL, or historical analogies are basically meaningless.

(7) Analogies clearly aren't meaningless — historians and political scientists use them constantly with real predictive weight.

(8) So N collapses into CL, and all we really have are laws of institutional decay.

(9) But that proves too much. If CL is all there is, then explaining Rome's fall is just citing a general law, and studying Rome specifically is no different from studying any other decaying democracy. The particularity that makes history history vanishes.

(10) So either (2) is false and there's no embedding surplus, or (4) is false and analogies don't need portability, or (6) is a false dichotomy. I can't tell which.
