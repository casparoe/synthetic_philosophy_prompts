I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, but I'm building a sensory-annotation pipeline and recently encountered the clinical literature on pain asymbolia: patients who report pain intensity with normal accuracy yet show no withdrawal reflex, no facial grimace, and no emotional distress, as though the nociceptive signal arrives stripped of its usual "badness" payload. This raises a conceptual problem I'm not equipped to resolve—whether pain is intrinsically bad because of its phenomenal quality, the raw feeling itself, or only because it couples to an avoidance motivation that is missing in these cases. Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a closing quatrain, with two refrains (A1 and A2) and two rhymes (a and b) in the pattern A1 b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 A2—in which the refrains are "the hurt is in the feeling, bare and complete" and "without the pull away, the signal is empty." Use the form's repetition and interweaving so that the first three tercets build the case that nociception alone carries intrinsic badness, the turn at the fourth tercet introduces the asymbolic split between sensation and motivation, and the final quatrain forces both refrains to stand side by side without synthesis. The poem should render the philosophical disagreement precisely enough that someone working in philosophy of mind or pain science recognizes the actual debate about whether negative valence is constitutive of pain or separable from it, but it should not name specific theorists, cite texts, or ask for a bibliography. Write it so a non-specialist can feel the tension, yet a specialist wouldn't wince.
