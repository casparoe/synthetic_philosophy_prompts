I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of science and cognitive systems, preparing a classification instrument for a graduate seminar on interpretability and representational realism—specifically, on whether deep neural networks with distributed, non-symbolic architectures genuinely possess representations of their target domains.

I want a decision tree that avoids reproducing the familiar realist-versus-instrumentalist dichotomy. Instead, construct it from four binary branch nodes, each posing a concrete commitment about a specific conceptual or technical criterion, phrased so that a respondent can answer from intuition without knowing which philosophical label it maps onto. The four nodes should be:

1. Causal efficacy: If systematically ablating activation patterns that correspond to a feature (e.g., curvature in a vision model) reliably disrupts the system's behavior with respect to that feature, does that alone establish representational status?
2. Structural correspondence: Must the relation involve structural isomorphism between activation geometry and target-domain properties, or is predictive functional correlation sufficient?
3. Interpretive accessibility: Must the content be decodable in principle by an observer who lacks access to the training distribution and objective function?
4. Origin: Can representations emerge purely from gradient-descent optimization on predictive loss, or do they require explicit representational design or teleofunctional selection?

This yields sixteen logical combinations. Populate them as follows: the uniform 'yes' corner, the uniform 'no' corner, the 'yes-yes-no-no' combination (functional correlationism), and the 'yes-no-no-yes' combination (the optimization-emergence analog of teleosemantics) must all appear as clearly labeled leaves. For the remaining twelve combinations, assign each either a named position from the literature—only if it genuinely occupies that logical space—or an 'unnamed' descriptor together with one sentence on its most awkward commitment or on why it appears unoccupied (incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored). Flag explicitly any pair of leaves that share identical answer patterns but differ only in motivation or metaphysical framing, since the tree cannot separate them.

Present the deliverable as a flowchart of questions leading to leaves, not as prose comparison. The audience is graduate students in philosophy and cognitive science. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; the instrument should be answerable from conceptual intuition alone. Frame the nodes so they do not depend on highly specialized machine-learning theory that a student without technical training could not evaluate.
