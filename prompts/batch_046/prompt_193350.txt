I'm working on anthropic reasoning and trying to pin down the exact disagreement between the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) when the ontology of observers is not discrete. The standard formulation treats observer-moments or individuals as countable, which makes SSA's uniform sampling well-defined and lets SIA weight theories by the cardinality of observers they contain. But consider a continuous multiverse model in which conscious processing is distributed over spacetime with a variable density parameter, so that how many observers exist becomes an integral over a continuous field rather than a count. Here SSA seems to require a canonical measure over observer-moments that may not exist, while SIA weights theories by the total integrated conscious processing they generate. Does the presumptuous philosopher objection—that SIA lets a theory gain credibility merely by predicting more observers—retain its force in this continuous setting, or does the shift from cardinality to measure blur the distinction between predicting more observers and occupying a larger parameter space? Compare how SSA and SIA fare with respect to reference-class determinacy and to the presumptuous philosopher charge once observers are treated as a continuous field, and say which assumption survives the transition more intact.
