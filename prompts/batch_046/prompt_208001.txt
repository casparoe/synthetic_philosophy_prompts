Analyze the whole Richmond Lee statue situation in 2020-2021 — the one that got covered in protest graffitti and portraits then was eventually pulled down — through Nietzsche's three modes of history: monumental, antiquarian, and critical. Don't just slap labels on people; I want you to actually map it out. How was tearing the monument down an act of critical history, but also how did all the spray-painted art on the base accidentally create a kind of reverse-monumental history for the present? Then there's the museum crowd who said move it to a storage facility or history museum rather than destroying it — is that real antiquarian care for the past, or just life-support for a dead culture? And what does the empty plinth left behind tell us about Nietzsche's warning that critical history has to serve present life, not just erase the past? Be honest about where the theory fits and where it feels forced.
