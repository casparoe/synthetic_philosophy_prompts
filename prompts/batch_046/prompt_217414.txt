For a philosophy of mind and cognition course, I am drafting a position paper on creative cognition and want a consistency audit before I submit. Here is my current framework:

(1) I endorse Hofstadter’s view that creativity is fundamentally knob-twiddling within a conceptual space: identifying the invariant relational structure of a domain and varying its free parameters away from default values.

(2) I claim that apparently radical innovations—such as non-Euclidean geometry or atonal music—are not ex nihilo introductions of entirely new themes, but extreme settings of knobs inside pre-existing conceptual spaces.

(3) At the same time, I allow that some breakthroughs involve slipping, where the organizing relational framework of an adjacent domain is imported and becomes the governing theme for the original problem.

(4) I hold that analogical mapping, understood roughly in the structure-mapping tradition, is the unified cognitive mechanism behind both routine problem-solving and revolutionary creativity; I deny any principled boundary between merely analogical and genuinely creative thinking.

(5) I maintain that authentic knob-twiddling requires explicit representation of relational invariants, but I reject that this requires embodiment—sufficiently dense formal or linguistic interconnections should suffice.

(6) I deny that there is a distinct, domain-specific creative faculty separate from general intelligence and analogical capacity.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each real conflict? For each tension, state the cheapest revision that restores consistency. Then identify the single change—giving up or modifying one commitment—that would resolve the largest number of conflicts. I have a vague sense that problems may lurk around whether slipping introduces a genuinely new theme or merely turns a hidden domain-selection parameter, whether recognizing adjacency requires embodied world-interaction, and whether my mechanistic claims collapse into redundancy, but I cannot tell which fractures are real. Rank the actual tensions by severity, and keep your answer within 350 words.
