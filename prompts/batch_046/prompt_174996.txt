Construct a thought experiment that isolates Benatar's asymmetry from symmetric and utilitarian alternatives. Design a one-paragraph scenario in which a decision-maker with no personal stake can choose, once and irreversibly, to bring into existence exactly two people—Alia, whose life contains net mild pleasure, and Bren, whose life contains net mild suffering—but only together or not at all; there are no other effects and no exotic physics. Engineer the case so that Benatar's asymmetry recommends against creation, while both a symmetric view (which treats absence of pleasure as bad for a potential subject and absence of pain as good) and simple act-utilitarian aggregation recommend in favor, assuming Alia's pleasure outweighs Bren's suffering. After presenting the scenario, show the reasoning for all three positions, name the structural feature—concerning whether a non-existent being can be the bearer of a deprivation—that causes Benatar's verdict to diverge, and explain what happens if that feature is removed, for instance by allowing the creator to actualize Alia without Bren. Conclude by saying whether the modified case collapses into a standard non-identity problem, and what that reveals about whether the asymmetry depends on the pairing constraint. Keep the scenario realistic, avoid citing specific texts, and do not ask for bibliographies.
