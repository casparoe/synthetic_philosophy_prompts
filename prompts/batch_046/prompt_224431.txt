me and a couple friends were arguing about lexical threshold negative utilitarianism after class — specifically whether pain and pleasure are actually commensurable if you put a hard lexical cutoff on suffering. i said it sounds like a decent compromise, but they got into it pretty hard and i couldn't tell who was landing punches.

friend 1 said the whole point of a lexical threshold is that some suffering is just categorically worse than any pleasure could ever make up for, so you don't need a precise exchange rate between them. you just need to recognize there's a qualitative break — like, once you're past the threshold no amount of bliss balances it. friend 2 said that sounds nice but it's cheating: if you can locate a threshold at all, you're already measuring suffering and pleasure on the same underlying scale, otherwise you couldn't say "this much pain is past the line but that much isn't." friend 1 came back that it's ordinal within domains, not cardinal across them — you rank suffering until you hit the "unacceptable" category, but you never say it's equal to X units of pleasure. friend 2 then asked what happens if you have a trillion people all suffering just barely under the threshold, with one other person experiencing infinite happiness. by friend 1's rule that's apparently okay, since no single person crosses the line. but friend 2 said if suffering is truely lexically special then spreading it across heads shouldn't dissolve it; otherwise you've just smuggled aggregation back in for the sub-threshold stuff, which means you're treating those pains as commensurable after all. friend 1 said the lexical bar is person-relative, not globally summed — you can't stack up sub-threshold pains across different lives to manufacture a catastrophe, because that treats suffering as fungible across people, which is exactly what the view rejects. friend 2 said okay, but then a world with a trillion people in near-maximum-but-sub-threshold misery plus one blissful person comes out better than a world with one person over the line. that seems like a reductio, and it shows the view still counts heads on the pleasure side, which is its own kind of commensurability.
