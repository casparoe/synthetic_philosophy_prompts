b) Elena is a surgeon who stays for an emergency double-shift during a crisis. Contrast with Aristotles view on whether feeling good about virtuous action helps or hurts moral character.

student answer:
Elena is clearly acting in accordance with duty since she fulfills her obligation to care for patients. More importantly she acts from duty because her recognition of the moral law is the sufficient reason she stays. Kant says an action has moral worth only when done out of respect for duty and not from inclination, and here duty is the driving force so she does have full moral worth. The satisfaction she feels is just an accompanying emotion, and Kant allows feelings as long as they are not the determining ground.

That said, her satisfaction is not just a feeling—it is a second-order inclination to maintain her self-image as a dutiful agent. This introduces overdetermination: her will is moved by both duty and this reflective desire. In the Groundwork Kant argues that when inclination is present alongside duty, even as a backup cause, the action loses its pure moral worth. So actually Elena lacks full worth.

Turning to Aristotle, he would evaluate Elena differently. For Aristotle virtue involves the right action done with the right feelings, so her pride actually confirms she has achieved a virtuous character state. Unlike Kant who treats inclination as a contaminant, Aristotle sees aligned emotion as part of what makes someone genuinely good. Therefore Aristotle would say her action is not only worthy but exemplary.
