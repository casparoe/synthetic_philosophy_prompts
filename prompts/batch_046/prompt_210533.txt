okay so in my phil of mind reading group people keep using the calculator-display analogy for LLMs. the idea is that when a calculator shows '4' after 2+2, the symbol doesnt mean four on its own - it only gets meaning from us, the users. so LLM text is just like that: derived intentionality, syntax without semantics, like Searle's chinese room but for chatbots. my friend says this proves theres no original intentionality inside, just patterns being interpreted by us.

but then i came across Dennett's two-bitser idea - the claim that original intentionality isnt all-or-nothing, and even a system that can only discriminate two states and act on them has a kind of original aboutness if its dispositions are wired right. if thats true, the calculator analogy might be smuggling in a binary view that Dennett rejects.

can you lay out what maps onto what between the calculator display and an LLM? where does the analogy break - especially around the idea that intentionality might come in degrees (the bitser ladder) rather than being either present like in a human or absent like in ink? and does the conclusion - that LLMs definitely lack original intentionality - survive once you account for those disanalogies? if the calculator picture is wrong, whats a better analogy for thinking about where on Dennett's scale a language model actually sits?
