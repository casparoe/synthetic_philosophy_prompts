Write an essay addressing whether dreams are experiences, with particular attention to the disagreement between Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett. Malcolm contends that dreams are not experiences in the philosophically relevant sense because the sleeping subject lacks the observational access and reportorial conditions that experience-claims ordinarily require; Dennett, by contrast, denies that dreams are occurrent conscious episodes reconstructed upon waking, arguing instead that dream narratives are retrospectively assembled. Weigh the central strengths and weaknesses of both positions. Then introduce a hypothetical scenario: a non-invasive neural-decoding interface that permits continuous, real-time verbal reporting of dream content without awakening the subject. Analyze whether this possibility undermines Malcolm's epistemic objection, supports Dennett's narrative-construction view, or introduces a distinct problem—namely, that such reports might be algorithmically mediated interpretations rather than direct disclosures of experiential content. Reach and defend a specific conclusion about whether dreams should be classified as experiences, and explain whether the hypothetical case resolves the original conceptual dispute or transforms it into a different question about first-person access and mediated report.
