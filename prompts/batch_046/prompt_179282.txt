Construct a thought experiment involving a synthetic biochemical system—designated System M—engineered so that ontological emergence and weak epistemic emergence deliver opposite verdicts on the same macro-property, whilst a complete reductionist account remains correct in principle. The system should be a minimal protocell whose lipid bilayer contains embedded enzymatic channels that synthesise replacement lipids exclusively when membrane tension, a collective mechanical property of the bilayer, falls below a specified threshold; the tension itself is maintained by the collective spatial arrangement of lipids rather than by any individual molecule. Specify that every molecular interaction is governed by standard chemical kinetics, that no quantum coherence, consciousness, or intentionality is present, and that the scenario must be described in a single paragraph with realistic biochemical parameters. The case must include a feedback loop in which the macro-state of tension non-linearly regulates micro-level synthesis rates, and it must be designed so that the strong emergence claim depends solely on the collective constraint structure acting as an irreducible boundary condition—not on computational intractability, ignorance, or exotic physics.

After presenting the scenario, provide the analysis: identify the macro-property at issue; state, for reductionism, weak emergence, and strong emergence respectively, what verdict each delivers and why; specify the relevant nearby worlds or counterfactual conditions that distinguish the three positions; and explain precisely why the strong-emergence verdict does not collapse into an epistemic limitation but instead rests on the non-decomposability of the tension constraint into pairwise molecular interactions. Then isolate the structural feature that does the work—namely, the collective boundary condition operating as a causal constraint on the channels—and describe what happens to all three verdicts if that feature is removed, for instance by linearising the feedback loop so that synthesis depends only on local lipid concentration. Finish by noting how a defender of strong emergence would reply to the charge that the collective constraint is merely a shorthand for summed micro-interactions, and how a reductionist would attack the case in turn. Write the answer in British English.
