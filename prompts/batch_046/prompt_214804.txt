I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun systematic study in philosophy. I am preparing to lead one ninety-minute session for a small seminar of retired scientists and engineers—none with formal philosophical training, all comfortable with abstract and quantitative reasoning—on the epistemic regress problem, specifically the competing responses of foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism. I want not a direct explanation of these positions but a teaching plan: the precise sequence in which the conceptual components must be introduced, with the dependency relations made explicit (what must be understood before the next element can be introduced). For the carrying example, I am inclined to use the calibration chain of a laboratory instrument—tracing the justification for trusting a particular temperature reading back through reference instruments to the defining constants of the international temperature scale—but I want your judgment on whether this example serves the purpose for this audience, what structural properties it must possess and what misleading features it must lack (for instance, it should not imply that the chain terminates in a causally basic but epistemically opaque event), and whether an ordinary perceptual example would be preferable despite being less familiar to them. Please identify the misconceptions this particular audience—accustomed to experimental verification, error propagation, and cross-calibration—is likely to form: for example, that coherentism is merely circular reasoning among instruments, that infinitism requires an actually infinite number of mental or physical steps to be executed, or that foundationalism demands Cartesian indubitability rather than merely non-inferential justification. For each misconception, specify the exact point in the sequence where it should be anticipated and defused. Provide a single check-for-understanding question or brief exercise that I can pose near the end to determine whether the participants have grasped the structural problem—the forced choice among circularity, arbitrariness, and infinite regress—rather than merely memorizing the three labels. Finally, state clearly what should be left out at this level and openly acknowledged as such—perhaps formal logical notation of the trilemma, the historical details of Agrippa's formulation, internalist versus externalist versions of foundationalism, or contemporary defenses of infinitism—and indicate precisely where in the session I should say so.
