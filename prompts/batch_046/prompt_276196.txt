As a journalist covering the intersection of literary metaphysics and contemporary philosophy of science, I am preparing a feature on how Jorge Luis Borges's fictional architectures of time—specifically the branching garden, the dream-constructed circular ruins, and the total but unordered library—have been imported, often imprecisely, into debates about quantum branching, eternalist ontology, and the preservation of digital culture. I am not asking for a survey of Borges scholarship, nor for citations, bibliographies, or a reading list. Instead, I want a defended research agenda: if we treat these fictions as rigorous conceptual experiments rather than mere allegory, which open questions about the ontology of temporal parts, the phenomenology of memory in a non-progressive history, and the transmission of cultural information without linear causation should command scarce interdisciplinary resources over the next several years? For each priority, defend its place in the ordering by addressing tractability for philosophers without direct laboratory access, whether it sits upstream or downstream of other problems in the philosophy of time, and what kind of result or conceptual clarification would cause you to reorder the list. In addition, identify which widely circulated interpretations—such as the claim that Borges simply anticipates Everettian quantum mechanics—should be actively deprioritized because they rest on category errors, misread the textual logic, or generate no further tractable work. Please keep the reasoning explicit and avoid treating Borges's narratives as decorative illustrations for pre-existing scientific theories.
