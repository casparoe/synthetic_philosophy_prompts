I'm currently enrolled in an advanced seminar on comparative moral philosophy, and over the past few weeks we've been working through Confucian role ethics—particularly the claim that ren and li are not universal virtues applied uniformly but are structurally differentiated by relational positions such as father-son, ruler-subject, and elder-younger sibling. I've come to defend a stronger interpretation than what our readings usually advance: not merely that obligations vary by role, but what I'd call radical role-constitution—the thesis that there is no pre-ethical or role-transcendent moral agent whatsoever, and that the very idea of a person bearing rights or duties independent of specific relational stations is not just ethically mistaken but conceptually incoherent within this framework. On this reading, even apparently universal prohibitions—say, against arbitrary cruelty—derive their normative force entirely from the internal logic of particular roles rather than from any shared human status, so that to invoke a "human being as such" in moral argument is already to step outside the conceptual architecture of the view.

I want an honest map of what would genuinely count against this position, sorted by how decisive each consideration is. Which assumptions of radical role-constitution could fail: is there an unavoidable case—perhaps in severe role conflict, role abandonment, or cross-cultural moral judgment—where the framework must implicitly invoke a role-independent standard of evaluation, and would that be fatal or merely awkward? What would defenders of this strong view themselves concede as legitimate pressure points rather than misunderstandings? Is there a risk that the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability—for instance, by treating every apparent counterexample as just another role-specific determination—such that it now risks nothing at all, and if so, is that insulation a philosophical virtue or a defect? I'm also interested in whether conceptual results about personal identity and normative obligation might undermine the claim that roles are constitutive rather than merely regulative, but I want the assessment to distinguish live escape routes from merely logical ones and to say clearly whether the standard conclusion is well exposed or largely protected.
