Here's an argument I built for a seminar on moral revolutions and now it smells wrong. (1) Appiah says norms like dueling and slavery collapsed mainly when elites stopped seeing them as honorable, not because rational arguments won. (2) Bernard Williams would add that thick concepts like 'cruelty' or 'honor' do the real normative work, not thin universal principles. (3) So if revolutions are always honor/status shifts, then any dead norm can be redscribed as a 'status cascade' — like preference falsification, where people hide beliefs until the cost of defending them spikes. (4) But then the honor thesis and the interest/cost thesis aren't rivals; they're the same mechanism at different resolutions. (5) Worse, since you can always construct a cascade story post-hoc, the framework becomes unfalsifiable: whether people genuinely changed thier thick concepts or just strategically flipped, the data looks identical. (6) So either moral philosophy can't explain why norms die, or one of the premises is broken. Where's the leak? I'd like to keep (2) — thick concepts feel like they matter — but I'm not sure I'm not just saving my intuition.
