I am writing an essay for an advanced philosophy seminar on Bradley’s regress and the unity of the proposition. Bradley argues that if a relation R genuinely connects a and b, then further relations are required to link R to a and to b, producing an infinite regress; he concludes that relations are unreal and that only an undifferentiated Absolute achieves true unity. Rather than accepting internal relations or the Absolute, I want to explore whether the unity of a proposition can be reconceived as a self-stabilizing dynamic equilibrium—analogous to a cybernetic feedback loop—where subject and predicate continuously co-constitute one another without any discrete “tie” that needs further tying. On this view, there is no static relatum-relation pair available to launch Bradley’s regress, so the problem dissolves rather than being solved by a metaphysical monad. I am unsure, however, whether this merely renames the Absolute in process-language, and whether the feedback analogy secretly reintroduces a regress at the level of the regulating mechanism itself. Could you comment on whether my characterization of Bradley’s argument is accurate, whether the dynamic-co-constitution proposal genuinely avoids the regress or hides it, and whether this line of thought is philosophically substantive or only a metaphor? I would particularly value suggestions on which part of the argument is weakest and what kind of case or distinction would best test whether dynamic unity is really different from Bradley’s Absolute.
