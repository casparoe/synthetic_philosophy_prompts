Consider Aristotle’s account of eudaimonia in the Nicomachean Ethics, particularly his thesis that human flourishing consists in the activity of the soul in accordance with virtue and reason, requires adequate external goods as necessary conditions rather than constituents, and that the polis exists for the sake of living well rather than merely living. Now imagine that Aristotle is presented with modern composite national indices—such as the World Happiness Report—that rank countries according to aggregated self-reported life satisfaction measured on ladder-scale surveys, per-capita income, social support, healthy life expectancy, perceived freedom to make life choices, generosity, and perceptions of corruption. Predict how Aristotle would evaluate whether these rankings genuinely measure human flourishing, and whether a state should deliberately orient its policies toward maximizing its position. In your answer, address whether subjective satisfaction of this kind corresponds to eudaimonia or rather to a form of hedonic contentment; whether the index’s weighting of economic and institutional variables aligns with his distinction between necessary external conditions and the intrinsic good of virtuous activity; and whether treating rank improvement as a political goal risks reducing flourishing to a competitive, quantifiable output rather than an activity of character. Reason solely from broadly recognized Aristotelian concepts; do not provide bibliographies, cite modern empirical studies, or appeal to specific scholarly interpretations.
