A colleague summarized predictive processing and Karl Friston’s free energy principle for me after attending a workshop, and I want to know whether her account is trustworthy. She said the core of predictive processing is hierarchical predictive coding: the brain generates top-down predictions about sensory input and updates beliefs via bottom-up prediction errors weighted by their estimated precision. The free energy principle extends this into a universal law of self-organization, formalizing any living system as minimizing variational free energy—an information-theoretic upper bound on statistical surprise that, for physical systems maintaining steady-state, becomes numerically equivalent to thermodynamic free energy. She insisted this is not analogy but identity: the differential equations governing neuronal dynamics and those governing heat dissipation are literally the same, so the brain is running a physics computation rather than a psychological one.

She then introduced Markov blankets as statistical boundaries separating a system’s internal states from external ones. According to her summary, once you define an organism by its Markov blanket you have fully specified its internal model, which makes talk of representation redundant—there is no need to say the brain represents the world because the blanket itself encodes every relevant relation. From this she concluded that predictive processing eliminates representationalism and replaces it with a purely dynamical, physics-based ontology. She addressed the classic “dark room” objection by invoking active inference, but added that critics misunderstand the resolution: organisms do not seek stimulating environments because of prior preferences or homeostatic goals, but because precision-weighting mechanically forces them to sample data that maximizes information gain about their own model parameters. Finally, she claimed that since any system with a Markov blanket—immune networks, social institutions, ecosystems—falls under the principle, consciousness should be understood not as a brain-specific phenomenon but as a particular regime of free-energy minimization that emerges whenever interoceptive prediction errors achieve high precision-weighting across hierarchical layers.

Which parts of this summary would a careful proponent of predictive processing and the free energy principle accept, and which are subtle misrepresentations, conflations, or overstatements? In particular, is the move from Markov blankets to the redundancy of representation legitimate, is the characterization of active inference and the dark-room solution accurate, and does the conclusion that consciousness is a non-brain-specific regime of free-energy minimization actually follow from the premises she offers? Go through the summary section by section, distinguish accurate gloss from distortion, and tell me whether her broader philosophical conclusions survive once the technical claims are corrected.
