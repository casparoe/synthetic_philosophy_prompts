As a high school student preparing an independent philosophy project, I am trying to understand how two serious readers of Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling could disagree about Abraham without talking past each other. Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps.

Camp One, call them the Radical Suspension interpreters, holds that Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac demonstrates a genuine teleological suspension of the ethical: the universal moral prohibition against killing one's child is not merely overridden in appearance but actually evacuated and replaced by a singular relation to the divine command.

Camp Two, call them the Mediated Faith interpreters, holds that Abraham never actually leaves the ethical sphere; rather, the divine command activates a higher-order responsibility—to God and to the promise of Isaac's lineage—that mediates and preserves ethical coherence rather than annihilating it.

The statement should include:

1. A numbered section of claims both sides can sign, covering the narrative structure of the Akedah, the distinction between the tragic hero (Agamemnon) and the knight of faith, the structure of the double movement, the role of the absurd, and the significance of Abraham's silence toward Sarah, Isaac, and Eleazar.

2. A disagreements table. Each row should state one camp's precise formulation in language that camp accepts, followed by the other camp's precise counter-formulation in language that camp accepts. The disagreements should center on at least these cruxes: whether the universal ethical retains any residual normative authority during the suspension; whether Abraham's silence is an unavoidable feature of singular faith or signals an irreducible ethical deficit; and whether the knight's belief operates through a non-rational cognitive state or through a practical commitment compatible with reason.

3. For each crux, identify the conceptual test, comparison, or argument both sides agree is relevant—such as the logical comparison with Agamemnon's public justification, or the analysis of whether divine command can be universalized—along with each camp's predicted outcome.

Write in formal, clear prose suitable for an advanced high school seminar. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to named secondary scholars. Paraphrase any textual details rather than quoting verbatim.
