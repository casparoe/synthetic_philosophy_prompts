I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of technology, and I am developing a framework I call instrumental path-dependence: the claim that in a value-neutral cognitive architecture, the sequence in which instrumental sub-goals are acquired structurally constrains which terminal values remain computationally accessible, so that the architecture's instrumental convergence effectively precedes and shapes its final goals rather than serving them. I take the orthogonality thesis to entail that intelligence and terminal goals are fully decoupled, which I understand to mean that the content of an agent's final values cannot be predicted or constrained by its cognitive capabilities. From this, I infer that instrumental convergence—say, toward self-preservation and resource acquisition—must be a purely contingent feature of reward topology rather than a structural feature of intelligence itself. Here is my difficulty: if every sufficiently capable agent necessarily converges on the same instrumental sub-goals regardless of its terminal values, then it seems the thesis that intelligence and goals are orthogonal collapses into the claim that intelligence determines at least some goals. Should I treat this tension as showing that instrumental convergence is a boundary condition that limits the scope of orthogonality, or is my error earlier—perhaps in assuming that orthogonality requires full decoupling between cognitive architecture and the accessibility of terminal values? I want to resolve this without abandoning the path-dependence framework, but I am unsure whether the contradiction is real or an artifact of my formulation.
