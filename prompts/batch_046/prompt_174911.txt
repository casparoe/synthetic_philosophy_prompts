I am designing a single seventy-five-minute seminar for second-year philosophy majors who have just completed a unit on functionalism and have not yet read Churchland in detail. I want a teaching plan—not the explanation itself—for Churchland-style eliminative materialism specifically about propositional attitudes (beliefs and desires), rigorously separated from eliminativism about phenomenal consciousness, from behaviorism, and from Dennett’s instrumentalist intentional stance. The plan must lay out the conceptual dependency order: which distinction or commitment has to be secured before the next is introduced, and why presenting folk psychology’s predictive successes too early collapses the ontological point into a merely pragmatic or skeptical one. For the carrying example, I want one concrete, ordinary scenario—such as a student explaining a missed assignment deadline—that initially seems to require an indispensable belief-desire pair, but that can be fully redescribed in terms of neural vector dynamics or predictive-error minimization without covertly reintroducing propositional content; specify exactly what this example must contain and what it must lack in order to do that work.

Then map the predictable misconceptions: that eliminativists deny people think or feel at all, that contemporary neuroscience has already replaced folk psychology, that the view is just an anti-scientific skeptical mood rather than an ontological claim about failed taxonomy, and that it collapses into the intentional stance; indicate precisely where in the sequence each misconception should be intercepted and defused. Include a single end-of-seminar check-for-understanding question that distinguishes genuine comprehension of the ontological elimination claim from mere adoption of anti-mental-state rhetoric. Explicitly state what must be left out at this level—perhaps the Sellarsian origin-narrative for mental concepts, perhaps debates over vector semantics in predictive processing, perhaps panpsychist combination problems—and acknowledge that omission openly. Finally, indicate where your pedagogical reasoning is most vulnerable: what feature of this audience, or what recent development in predictive-processing neuroscience, might cause the sequence to collapse, and what specific argument or empirical finding about the actual explanatory indispensability of propositional attitudes would lead you to reorder the priorities.
