hey, i'm a backend engineer with no formal philosphy background. i read nietzsche's essay on history for life a few months ago and i've been trying to apply the three modes—monumental, antiquarian, critical—to tech culture. here's roughly where i've landed and i want a consistency check before i mention it in a reading group.

my commitments:
a) monumental history gives heroic models that make action possible, but it always falsifies the past by selecting and simplifying.
b) antiquarian history preserves culture and identity, yet it paralyzes present action by binding us to reverence for old stuff.
c) critical history is necessary to break free from bad traditions, but when it becomes the dominant mode it destroys the conditions for culture and decisive action.
d) in software engineering we're almost purely critical: we tear down legacy stacks, deprecate APIs, and treat past practice as an obstacle rather than a resource.
e) at the same time, i think what engineers call disruption is usually monumental history in disguise—every clean-slate rewrite is sold through a heroic founding narrative about the right architecture, so we're not genuinely critical.
f) i think nietzsche overstates the need for forgetting; in tech, actively deleting docs and deprecated functions is a feature, not a bug, because legacy knowledge is mostly technical debt.
g) despite that, i think a healthy human life needs all three modes in balance.
h) but for tech specifically i think the right mix is mostly critical with occasional monumental founding moments and almost zero antiquarian preservation—old code should be deleted, not archived.

do these hang together? i'm especially unsure about d and e since they seem to pull in opposite directions about what engineers are actually doing. also c warns that critical dominance destroys culture, but f and h seem to want exactly that dominance for tech. and then g says balance is needed for life but h assigns 0% to antiquarian—are those in tension or just domain-specific?

please rank the tensions by severity, say for each pair what's the cheapest fix and what it costs. also tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems. and please seperate what's settled in the scholarship from what's genuinely contested—don't present a contested reading (like whether critical history is self-undermining) as if it's the consensus view.
