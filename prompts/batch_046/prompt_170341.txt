Jeffrey conditionalization and the orthodox Bayesians have been circling each other since the 60s and nobody seems to budge. The Jeffrey side says when evidence is genuinely uncertain — like you see a smudge that might be red or orange but you can't tell which — you update by partitioning your space and shifting weight inside each cell according to your new confidence, leaving internal ratios alone. The Bayesians say that's just hidden conditioning on an imprecise prior; if you're rational you should build a sharp mixture model and condition properly, otherwise you're violating diachronic coherence. Every exchange feels like a rerun: Dutch books, representation theorems, appeals to "how belief actually moves". My working theory is that their not actually disagreeing about rationality at all — maybe Jeffrey is describing belief revision when the world delivers ambiguous input that can't be parsed into sharp propositions, while Bayesians are describing an ideal agent who always can parse it. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a real formal disagreement hiding behind the intuition talk?
