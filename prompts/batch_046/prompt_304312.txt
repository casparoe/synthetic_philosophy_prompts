hey can u grade this for my phil 320 class? about 15 juniors. the prompt was: 'Explain the micro-gradient spectrum argument against transitivity for "all-things-considered strictly better than" (ASB). Present the sharp-cutoff reply and the intransitivity reply. For each, state whether it preserves or abandons transitivity, and name one hidden cost it doesn't advertise. Then take a stand on which is less bad — no "it depends".'

here's Jordan's answer:

'The argument builds a chain L0 through L999 where each Li+1 has 0.001 less QALY-equivalent welfare. Adjacent pairs are tied because the gap is below the just-noticeable difference threshold. By transitivity of indifference, L0 is tied to L999, but L0 is clearly far better, contradiction.

Sharp-cutoff reply: there is a precise welfare threshold — say at L500 — where value drops discretely. This saves ASB transitivity because L0 > L500 > ... > L999. Hidden cost: the cutoff is metaphysically arbitrary, though we might not know its location.

Intransitivity reply: ASB is just not transitive across long chains. L0 > L1 > ... > L499, but L0 is not ASB-better than L999. This abandons transitivity. Hidden cost: it opens the door to preference cycles and money-pump situations if we extend the logic to choice.

I pick sharp-cutoff because logic should stay clean, even if the boundary is mysterious.'
