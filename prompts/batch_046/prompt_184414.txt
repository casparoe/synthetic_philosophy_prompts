You are the compliance officer for a twelve-institution cooperative research archive that maintains shared digital repositories and hosts working groups governed by open peer review. Every quarter you must decide whether to suspend a working group’s access when its charter or recent outputs advocate positions that, if adopted consortium-wide, would eliminate the open-access and peer-review norms that make the archive possible. You need a reusable decision procedure grounded in the paradox of tolerance, but refined for an institution that must preserve itself without becoming arbitrarily exclusionary.

Your protocol must incorporate two distinctions not found in standard textbook formulations. First, reflexive intolerance: a position is reflexively intolerant only when it targets the specific decision-procedures—peer review, open membership, appeals—of this archive itself, rather than being intolerant of some external group or idea. Second, asymmetric vulnerability: there is a threshold at which suppressing a working group damages the archive’s legitimacy and epistemic diversity more than tolerating the group does, and this threshold shifts with the group’s size, the visibility of the suppression, and the availability of less restrictive remedies.

Write a step-by-step protocol that a non-philosopher board of five can execute in under forty-eight hours. Present it as ordered tests with explicit questions, the evidence required for each, the condition that advances the decision, and clearly marked flag points where judgment is unavoidable and must be documented with a brief justification. Include a flowchart in words. State what the procedure is not designed to handle and where it will misfire.

Then apply the protocol to three cases:

(a) A working group publishes a charter calling for the replacement of peer review with hierarchical appointment by senior members of the group, and it urges other groups to adopt the same model.

(b) A working group advocates excluding a specific demographic from leadership roles in external institutions and publishes polemics to that effect, but it does not challenge the archive’s internal peer-review or access rules; your procedure should yield an answer here that you expect the board to resist, and you must say why the procedure forces that result and where judgment could override it.

(c) A working group demands total ideological conformity within its own membership and expels dissenters internally, but it remains silent on the archive’s governance rules and participates in peer review without attempting to change the process.

For each case, run the ordered tests, identify the flag points, show what the documented justification should look like, and note what the procedure still leaves unsettled. Finally, list the institutional safeguards that must exist outside this protocol—such as appeals processes or transparency requirements—without which the procedure should not be used, and state the specific kinds of disputes for which the protocol is the wrong tool entirely. All recommendations must be signable by you and an elected working-group representative; note in the procedure exactly which steps require dual sign-off and which require only documentation.
