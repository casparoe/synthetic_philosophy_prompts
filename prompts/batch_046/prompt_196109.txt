I came across a summary of prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection in an unnamed discussion essay. The author writes that prioritarianism assigns greater moral weight to benefits for the worse off, and that this implies any reduction in the well-being gap is pro tanto good—even when the worse off do not benefit. From this, the author concludes that prioritarianism must approve of pure leveling-down, such as destroying resources enjoyed only by the affluent, since that lowers inequality without harming anyone below a baseline. The summary then distinguishes this from egalitarianism, claiming that prioritarians care only about weighted well-being, not equality itself. Finally, it proposes that prioritarianism is best read as a threshold-sensitive view: once everyone exceeds a basic sufficiency level, the weighting of further benefits flattens out, so the leveling-down objection only bites in contexts of severe deprivation. Is this characterization fair? Please distinguish what is accurate, what is a defensible simplification, and what subtly misrepresents the position—particularly on whether prioritarianism endorses destruction without compensation, whether it contains a hidden sufficiency threshold, and whether defenders treat the leveling-down case as a refutation or as a misapplication of the weighting principle.
