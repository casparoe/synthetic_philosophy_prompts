I'm putting together a proposal for a short philosophy workshop grant (six months, part-time) on Benjamin Franklin's moral perfection project. The standard take is that Franklin was doing proto-behaviorist virtue training with his 13-virtue chart and weekly tracking. I want to push a weirder angle: Franklin's ordering of the virtues — temperance first becuase it enables the others — looks like a dependancy graph, and his admission that humility might be impossible to perfect without becoming prideful looks like an early recognition of a feedback loop or perverse incentive. So the exact question is whether Franklin's system is structurally consistent: can you run a cumulative, measurable self-improvement scheme if one of the nodes is self-undermining? Frame it precisely, explain why the usual readings (Enlightenment rationalism, virtue ethics, self-help anachronism) miss the structural contradiction, propose a method that reconstructs Franklin's logic without importing modern psychology jargon, say what a negative result looks like (maybe he just ignored the loop?), and be honest about scope since I don't have archival access. No citation lists, I'll add those myself, and let me know if the framing has a hole.
