I am a retired condensed-matter experimentalist who has recently begun working through philosophy of mind and methodological writing on thought experiments. I am drafting an essay tentatively titled “Intuition Pumps as Uncalibrated Instruments: What Experimental Practice Might Suggest About Conceptual Design,” and I have set out my core notes below. I would welcome substantive commentary on the coherence of the central analogy, on whether I have mischaracterized the function of philosophical intuition pumps, and especially on whether the distinction I am trying to draw holds together.

My notes run as follows. First, in my former field any reliable calibration protocol requires an independent standard: one applies a known input, records the output, and corrects for systematic deviation. Without that feedback loop, an instrument can undergo what we called thermal drift or baseline wander—producing internally consistent but progressively distorted readings—while the error remains invisible from within the protocol itself. Second, many canonical intuition pumps appear to me to operate open-loop in precisely this sense. They present a single, finely staged scenario and solicit an intuitive verdict, yet there is no external standard by which to check whether the intuition itself has drifted, or whether the framing has subtly redefined the very property—personal identity, phenomenal quality, intentional content—under investigation. Third, I want to propose that certain pumps are self-sealing: rather than measuring a pre-existing intuition, they restructure the concept being measured so that the “reading” comes out as designed. This is not ordinary framing bias; it is closer to altering the unit of measurement after observing the data. Fourth, I suspect there may also be closed-loop pumps—those embedded in an iterative dialectic where successive scenarios are adjusted in response to prior inconsistencies—but I am unsure whether this iteration actually corrects for drift or merely accumulates it in a more sophisticated form.

My specific concern is that philosophers often treat disagreement about an intuition pump as disagreement about the underlying concept, when it may instead be disagreement about whether the pump has introduced an unacknowledged calibration error of this drift-like kind. Before I develop the argument further, I want to ask three things. Is the analogy to instrument calibration fundamentally strained because intuitions are not measurements with definable units and error bars? Have I overlooked a standard philosophical response to the idea that thought experiments can systematically mislead in this particular, self-sealing manner? And is the open-loop versus closed-loop distinction genuinely useful for classifying pumps, or does it collapse once we recognize that all conceptual analysis is inherently iterative?

Please treat this as a request for direct commentary on the accuracy and interest of the sketch, not as a request for references, a bibliography, or a survey of the literature; I am working without ready access to specialized archives, and my aim is to test whether the conceptual machinery I have outlined is sound rather than to map it onto named positions.
