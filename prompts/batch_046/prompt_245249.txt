I'm on our university's AI review board and we've got a proposal to decide by Thursday. The team wants to deploy an autonomous logistics agent in the hospital supply chain. Instead of specifying full rewards, they propose a "reversable-trajectory" penalty: a learned world-model rolls the warehouse state backwards to compute what staff schedules and inventory levels would look like if the agent had never activated, then penalizes deviations within a three-hop causal raduis. They claim this avoids side effects without specifying values.

Half the board says it's just impact penalization with extra steps and want approval with routine monitoring. The other half says the rollback model must encode implicit norms about which disruptions are "abnormal," so value judgments are hidden in the architechture rather than eliminated. They want rejection unless the researchers explicitly state what goods the baseline protects.

Please draft the board memo: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with criteria the team could actually address. Treat the strongest argument on the losing side seriously — specifically, does this three-hop reversable baseline genuinely avoid value specification, or just bury it inside the causal-radius cutoff and the world-model's training distribution? Keep it actionable.
