The summary further claims that this ethical catastrophe serves as a direct refutation of panpsychism, and that the so-called combination problem—how micro-level experiences unite into unified macro-level consciousness—is resolved by recognizing that suffering scales linearly with entity count, so that macro-subjects experience proportionally less per capita while the cosmic total remains particle-dominated.

Could you evaluate the summary claim by claim, indicating which parts accurately reflect the panpsychist position, which involve subtle conflations or overstatements—such as whether it runs together panpsychism with panprotopsychism, whether it misrepresents the combination problem as an accounting issue rather than a structural one, and whether it illegitimately moves from the presence of fundamental experience to the presence of morally significant suffering? In particular, I would like to know whether the ethical conclusion follows from the metaphysics as presented, or whether a defender of the view would reject one of the intermediate steps as philosophically unsound.
