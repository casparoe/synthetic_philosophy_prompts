I'm on this city board that reviews public infrastructure, and we have to vote thursday on these 'adaptive' bus shelters. The idea is they resize the seating and roof based on real-time crowd sensors. Sounds neat, but the sensors only register people with active phones, so in our area that mostly misses elderly residents and people who keep location off for privacy. The design team says it's just a neutral platform — we can patch the algorithm later — but I'm wondering if the shelter already made a political choice about who counts as a rider before it was even installed. I dont have a philosophy background, but I've been thinking about this thing I call 'material veto' — when an object is built so that certain uses are physically impossible, not just discouraged. Like, if the bench mechanically retracts, then staying open for someone invisible to the sensor isn't an option the system allows. Can you write a short memo for the board? Recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with an actual timeline. Take the engineers' neutrality argument seriously, not just restate it. And tell me whether 'material veto' is actually a useful way to think about this, or if I'm stretching the idea. No need for citations or reading lists, I just need something I can circulate.
