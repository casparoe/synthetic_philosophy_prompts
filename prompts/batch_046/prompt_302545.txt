As a journalist writing on decision theory for an intellectually curious audience, I am trying to clarify a tension inside Herbert Simon's framework of bounded rationality that popular accounts rarely address. Most explanations contrast satisficing—accepting the first option that clears a fixed aspiration level—with optimizing under complete information. But there is a deeper conceptual problem: what happens when the aspiration level itself is not fixed ex ante but is continuously revised as the agent encounters new options during search? Please explain this reflexive structure and whether it undermines Simon's distinction between procedural rationality, which concerns the quality of the search process itself, and substantive rationality, which concerns the quality of the outcome achieved. If the threshold shifts in response to what is found, does the agent remain within a genuinely bounded procedure, or does the feedback loop smuggle in a hidden form of optimization? I want a focused conceptual analysis rather than an empirical literature review.
