I'm writting a long-form essay for my philosophy of art module and I've hit a wall trying to compare two pieces of Schopenhauer that my lecturer said might be analogus but I'm not convinced. There's his well-known claim that music isn't representational like painting or poetry — instead it's a direct copy of the Will itself, with melody tracing the restless becoming and harmony the eternal being of the world. Then there's his ethical doctrine of Mitleid, where moral insight comes from piercing the principium individuationis and seeing the same suffering Will in everyone else, which apparently lets us deny the Will's usual affirmation. Both seem to involve breaking through the illusion of separate selves, but they pull in opposite directions: music celebrates the Will's life-affirming energy even though that energy is basically suffering, while compassion tries to suppress it.

So I'm wondering — is there a coherent account where listening to music functions like an ethical act of compassion, or are they just incompatible escapes? And if they are analogus, why does one feel like ecstatic release and the other like ascetic self-denial? I'd like a clear comparison that doesn't just list similarities but actually adjudicates whether Schopenhauer's metaphysics of music undermines his ethics, or vice versa, especially for cases like listening to tragic opera where both dimensions seem present at once. Does the fact that music bypasses representation mean it also bypasses the ethical insight that comes from recognising shared suffering, or could it be that the listener is momentarily dissolving into the same universal Will that compassion reveals?
