I was at this writers' retrate last autumn — you know the kind, too much coffee, not enough plot — and two other novelists got stuck in a weird arguement about whether 'qualia' are even a coherent idea at all. One of them, let's call her L, said the whole notion is incoherant: any time you try to pin down what a quale is, you either end up describing brain states or behavoural dispositions, so there's nothing left over to call subjective feel. The other, M, said that misses the point entirely. He said we can clearly imagine a chromatic amnesiac — someone who can still sort colours perfectly and talk about them fluently, but reports that the experiences have gone hollow, like reading a description of a sunset you can't actually picutre. If that scenario is even conceptually possible, then there's a gap between function and feel that the incoherence argument just ignores.

L came back and said M was smuggling in a hidden premise: that 'hollow' means something more than a change in emotional response or attentional focus. She said if you strip away all the functional and physical descriptions, you're left with a word game, not a real deficet. M replied that this just begs the question, because the incoherence claim is supposed to show there's nothing to strip away in the first place — but the chromatic amnesiac case shows we can coherently describe stripping it away. Then L said all she's done is relabel a functional disorder, and that calling it 'missing qualia' is like calling a flat tyre a missing roundness.

They asked me to referee but I honestly couldn't. Could you go through their exchange move by move — L's incoherence claim, M's chromatic amnesiac example, L's hidden-premise objection, M's circularity charge, and L's flat-tyre analogy — rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right, briefly justify each rating, say what either of them is missing, and please do it all in British English? Don't worry about a bibliography or citations, I just want the reasoning.
