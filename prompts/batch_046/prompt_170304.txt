If split-brain patients show that severing the corpus callosum can yield two independent perceptual streams, it is natural to think that consciousness is composed of separable "units" that the callosum ordinarily fuses. To sharpen this, let me distinguish synchronic unity—the felt coherence of experience right now—from diachronic unity—the sense that I am the same subject across successive moments. Now consider partial callosotomies that disconnect only prefrontal pathways: patients reportedly experience no division in present awareness, yet over minutes one hand systematically undoes what the other just did, as if two agents with different intentions share the body. Given that synchronic unity survives while diachronic unity fractures, does this not prove that what we call "the unity of consciousness" was never a single phenomenon, but only a contingent alignment of two independent mechanisms? And if unity can come apart this cleanly, should philosophers abandon the idea of a fundamental unified subject and treat personal identity instead as an engineering problem of inter-hemispheric coordination?
