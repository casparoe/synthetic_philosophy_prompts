This dissolves the standard objection that Buddhism cares about intention rather than consequence, but I want to know what actually counts against it. Respond with exactly five items in a numbered list, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, including whether canonical Abhidharma or Nikaya frameworks treat tanha-reduction as an outcome metric or only as an immediate mental-state quality, whether path-dependence collapses the view into virtue ethics once fully specified, whether the reconstruction risks unfalsifiability by redefining craving post hoc, and which of these a committed defender should concede versus dismiss. Be precise about what predictions this view makes that rival reconstructions do not.
