Dutch communication is low-context: the meaning is carried mainly by the explicit words. Japanese communication is high-context, depending on shared background, silence, and leaving gaps for the listener to fill. White lies are direct falsehoods told to protect feelings, so on a Kantian view they are clearly wrong—lying violates a perfect duty regardless of consequences.

Indirectness, however, is not lying. You never assert something false; you simply withhold part of the meaning or let the listener infer it. So a Kantian can allow indirectness without allowing deception.
