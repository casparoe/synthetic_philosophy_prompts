A summary sheet from a graduate module on Buddhist philosophy summarises Nāgārjuna's tetralemma and Madhyamaka emptiness in the following way. The tetralemma, it says, is a rigorous four-part logical schema that tests any thesis of inherent existence (svabhāva). For a claim such as 'the self exists inherently', the four exhaustive, mutually exclusive options are: (1) it exists with svabhāva; (2) it does not exist at all; (3) it both exists and does not exist; and (4) it neither exists nor does not exist. By showing that each option leads to contradictions—especially that causal interaction and change are impossible if entities possess fixed intrinsic natures—Nāgārjuna establishes that all phenomena are empty (śūnyatā) of svabhāva. The summary then claims that Madhyamaka does not end in pure negation but positively endorses option (4) as its definitive metaphysical position: phenomena are neither ultimately real nor ultimately unreal, yet they operate conventionally (samvṛti-satya). On this reading, emptiness is a minimal middle-way ontology that avoids both eternalism and nihilism, and the tetralemma functions mainly as a reductio that clears ground for this ontological commitment. The sheet also notes that this structure resembles modern dialetheist challenges to bivalence, except that Madhyamaka refuses to affirm any contradiction as ultimately true.

Something about this feels too neat, particularly the idea that Madhyamaka 'positively endorses' the fourth corner and that emptiness is an ontology rather than a deconstruction of ontology.
