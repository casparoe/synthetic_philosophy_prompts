I do not want the explanation itself; I want a dependency-sensitive teaching plan. First, identify the conceptual prerequisite that must be secured before the problem even becomes visible: is it the failure of value monism, or the distinction between first-order and second-order uncertainty? Second, propose the one carrying case—explicitly not the trolley problem—that forces a conflict between genuinely incommensurable reasons rather than a quantifiable risk-reward trade-off, and specify exactly what structural features it must possess and what simplifying features it must lack. Third, map the predictable misconceptions—e.g., that moral uncertainty collapses into Bayesian updating on empirical facts, that a meta-utility function can always be constructed, or that the problem is merely practical indecision—to the precise point in the sequence where each should be intercepted and neutralized. Fourth, provide one diagnostic question for the final fifteen minutes that distinguishes grasping the structural problem from merely acquiring vocabulary. Fifth, state explicitly what should be omitted at this level—such as formal moral-parliament mechanisms or lexical decision rules—and justify the omission concisely. Please conclude with a one-sentence summary of the plan’s core pedagogical insight as the very last line.
