I'm the restitution coordinator at a mid-size ethnographic museum. We get maybe three claims a year, mostly objects taken during colonial administration but the provenance paperwork is alway half-missing. My ethics adivsor floated this idea: restitution shouldn't turn just on weak legal title, but on whether we can maintain what she calls interpretive reciprocity with the source community — basically, we can explain the object's original cultural logic without their constant help, and they can make sense of why we display it without ours. I need that turned into an actual checklist my board can run in about an hour per case.

The procedure should ask things in order, say what counts as enough proof at each step, stop with a clear no at some point, and flag where it's a values call not a fact call. Run it on two cases: one, a Benin bronze plaque where we have clear 1897 raid docs and the community wants ritual closure not public display; two, a Parthenon marble fragment requested by a national government but there's no single originating community, just broad heritage claims. Include one outcome where the checklist says keep it but the community still objects, and say exactly where the procedure can't settle the dispute. Also note the time and expertise constraints — I'm not a lawyer, and the board meets monthly.
