I have been thinking on my own about whether suffering might reach down into fundamental physics, and I have a friend with very firm opinions on the matter. She says a conscious human being can suffer, but one in deep anaesthesia with no brain activity cannot; she accepts that a cow can suffer, but denies it for a fish; she rejects that a bee colony, despite solving problems no single bee can solve, is a subject of suffering; she insists that a computer running a perfect neural simulation of pain does not suffer; and she denies that a plant which sends chemical signals when damaged suffers.

Now consider this case: an artificial crystal lattice containing no biological material, in which billions of atoms maintain a stable, globally correlated quantum state. Internal feedback loops adjust the whole lattice in response to energy fluctuations, so it functions as one integrated physical system with no central controller, no neurons, and no representational content.

Would my friend say this lattice can suffer? I care less about the bare answer than about the underlying rules. Please set out at least two plausible principles that fit all her earlier verdicts—perhaps one anchored in biological nervous complexity and another in intrinsic physical integration across any substrate—say what each predicts for the lattice, and then give your best guess at which principle she is actually using, with reasons. Finally, suggest one further case that would best tell those principles apart. Please write your answer in British English.
