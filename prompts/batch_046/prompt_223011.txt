I have been wrestling with Schopenhauer's thesis that music is unique among the arts in being not a representation of Platonic Ideas but a direct objectification of the Will itself. He describes melody, in particular, as analogizing the continuous striving and satisfaction of the Will. Now consider a hypothetical autopoietic acoustic environment: a self-modulating sound system with no composer, no score, and no intended listener, whose harmonic and rhythmic structures emerge solely from real-time feedback between ambient physical processes and the system's own output. Given Schopenhauer's framework, would not such a system be closer to pure music than a human-composed symphony, precisely because it eliminates all representational and intentional mediation? Or does this reasoning rest on a false presupposition about what Schopenhauer means by the Will's direct expression—specifically, about whether the artistic form of melody requires a conscious analogizing of human striving rather than mere structural correspondence?
