I have recently been appointed to a mid-career review committee for a national science funding body, and our charter has changed: we are no longer permitted to rely on citation counts or publication volume to decide on renewals. Instead, we must determine whether the multi-year research programmes we support are genuinely progressive in Lakatos’s sense—expanding empirical content, accommodating anomalies through adjustments to a protective belt, and generating novel predictions—or whether they have become degenerating, preserving an untouched hard core only through post-hoc immunizing stratagems that diminish predictive power. Because I have no formal training in philosophy of science, I need a reusable evaluation protocol I can execute in roughly forty-five minutes on any renewal dossier or mid-term report, using only the proposal’s own description of its theoretical commitments, its recent results, and its planned adjustments, together with standard disciplinary knowledge that any informed reviewer in the field could be expected to possess.

Please construct a step-by-step decision procedure that a practitioner in my position can actually run: list the questions in strict order, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to each, indicate where a negative answer stops the evaluation with a clear verdict of degenerating, where a positive answer permits progression to the next stage, and exactly where the evaluator must make an irreducible judgment call about values or interpretation rather than apply a mechanical rule. The procedure must include explicit tests to distinguish protective-belt adjustments that increase predictive content from those that merely insulate the hard core, and it must flag cases where the hard core itself is so vaguely stated that the distinction collapses. Apply the protocol to three worked cases: first, a long-running evolutionary psychology programme that has recently appended modular sub-routines to account for every observed counter-example without deriving new, risky predictions; second, an interdisciplinary climate-health modelling consortium whose core feedback-loop assumptions have remained fixed but whose belt has yielded two verified, non-obvious predictions about regional heat-mortality patterns that competing epidemiological frameworks did not anticipate; and third, a well-funded cognitive neuroscience programme whose hard core remains intact but whose recent publications consist entirely of parameter-tuning within existing architectures to absorb null results—an outcome my committee is politically pressured to renew despite the procedural result. For each case, show precisely where the procedure lands, where it stalls for lack of information, and what judgment call I must make. Finally, state explicitly what the procedure cannot do: which categories of programme it systematically misclassifies, which steps depend on my ability to identify the hard core correctly, and whether it can be applied reliably by a reviewer who is not already a specialist in the programme’s domain. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations; the deliverable should be the operational protocol itself, with its internal limits honestly marked.
