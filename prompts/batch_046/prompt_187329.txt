My opening move concerns a development outside your direct experience: the contemporary practice of what might be called moral outsourcing, in which human agents delegate normative judgment to algorithmic systems that continuously rank, optimize, and enforce preferences without requiring any conscious feeling of resentment from the users they govern. You argue that modern morality expresses the ressentiment of the weak and that the ascetic ideal represents a will to power turned against life itself. Does this automated, subject-less form of moral regulation represent a new mutation of slave morality—where the structural logic of resentment persists without a conscious resentful agent—or is it the ascetic ideal brought to its logical extreme, with life denying itself not through conscious self-laceration but through dispassionate, mechanized control?
