i've been digging into Great Divergence and Needham-question debates, and there's a pattern in how people frame the comparison that i can't name. they ask 'why Britain and not China?' like those were the only two runners in the race, then list coal, patents, or instutions as the deciding diffrences. but they picked that pair precisely because they already know the outcome — Britain industrialized, China didn't. swap in France or Japan and the same factors either vanish or point the wrong direction, yet nobody tests that. it's like forging a key to match a lock you've already watched open. is there an established concept for this kind of retrospective pairing, where a contrastive question is engineered by the very divergence it's supposed to explain? i know it's kin to selecting on the dependent variable, but it feels more specific: treating a post-hoc contrast as if it were an ex-ante counterfactual. if there's no name, what would you call it, and how exactly do you draw the line between this and ordinary comparative history?
