Two members of my philosophy reading group got stuck in a multi-round argument about Zhuangzi's butterfly dream, and they asked me to referee. The first argued that the dream is not primarily about dream skepticism but about what she terms ontological mode pluralism: when Zhuangzi becomes the butterfly, he genuinely is the butterfly in that mode of being, and upon waking he genuinely is Zhuangzi, with no deeper substrate that persists across the change. She said this means there is no privileged real state—waking is not truer than dreaming, only different—and that treating the passage as an epistemic puzzle misses Zhuangzi's point, since there is no hidden fact about which mode is correct. The second replied that this overreads the text into metaphysical dissolution: Zhuangzi clearly wakes and recognizes himself, which shows the transformation is temporary and phenomenological, not ontological. He said the standard epistemic reading, that we cannot be sure we are not now dreaming, is exactly what is at stake, and that treating all modes as equally real contradicts the Daoist idea that the sage achieves a greater awakening that sees through particular forms. The first came back that this greater awakening is not a move to a truer reality but precisely the recognition that all particular forms, including waking selfhood, are equally transient transformations of the Dao; the sage does not prefer waking, but stops privileging it. The second then asked: if waking and dreaming are genuinely on a par, why should anyone strive for the sage's perspective rather than remain in blissful ignorance? And does this not collapse into a relativism that makes Zhuangzi's ethical advice—to follow natural transformations rather than forcing artificial distinctions—impossible to motivate? Neither would budge. Please adjudicate point by point: who is right about whether the dream supports mode pluralism versus epistemic skepticism, whether the idea of a greater awakening favors one reading, and whether either is missing something about how the transformation of things relates to personal identity. Say who is more right overall, and identify what both are overlooking.
