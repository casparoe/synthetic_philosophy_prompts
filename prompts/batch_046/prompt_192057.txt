I'm taking this philosophy of social science module and we had this massive debate in seminar about whether 'Gen Z' is actually a thing or if it's just a marketing invention. Anyway, it followed me home. I'm on the welfare committee for our student union and we're redesigning the mental-health questionaire we send out every term. My flatmate — she's also on the committee — wants to split the responses by birth cohort, Gen Z vs Millennials, because she reckons there's actualy evidence these groups have different anxiety risk profiles that aren't just about being different ages right now, but about the historical conditions they grew up in shaping their psychology in a stable, lasting way. I said that's basically treating a calendar accident like it's a biological category, but then she pointed out that we already split by nationality or gender in surveys and those aren't exactly 'natural' either.

The problem is I have to finalise the form by Friday. Should I bake generational tags into the core demographics, or am I just forcing the survey to treat these labels like they're genuinely different types of people? I don't want to miss real patterns just because I'm being fussy about what groups are actually real, but I also don't want the data to pretend these cohorts are fundamentally different kinds when it might just be age, period, and cohort effects all tangled up together. What's the fair way to split this without assuming we've solved the question first?
