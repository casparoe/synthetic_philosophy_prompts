Design a 90-minute classroom activity for a philosophy-of-social-science seminar of 14 graduate students. The aim is to make participants feel the conceptual force of Weber’s problem of explaining with values—specifically the tension between treating cultural meanings as causal factors and recognizing that they enter explanations only through elective affinity (Wahlverwandtschaft) with material conditions, under the dual requirements of causal adequacy and adequacy at the level of meaning (Sinnadäquatheit). The activity must be self-contained: participants should not read texts in advance, and the facilitator should supply the idea only after participants have encountered the problem through the rules.

Divide the group into four teams of three or four. Give each team two region profiles that are identical on visible economic indicators—trade access, capital stock, labor supply—plus hidden institutional differences revealed only in round two. Each team also receives cards labeled either Material Conditions or Value Orientations (e.g., a doctrine of predestination paired with worldly asceticism). The task is to build a predictive model for economic transformation in Region A versus Region B, but the scoring rules differ by card type:

- Material cards may be assigned direct causal weights and summed linearly.
- Value cards may not receive direct weights. To deploy one, the team must first pass a Meaning-Adequacy Check: in one sentence they must articulate how the belief makes a specific economic action subjectively meaningful to the actor. Only after passing this check may the value card be paired with a material card via the Elective-Affinity Rule: the pair multiplies rather than adds, but if the material partner is absent or mismatched the value card contributes zero. The facilitator applies a quick plausibility veto to block contrived meaning-links.

In round one, teams submit predictions for both regions using only visible data. The facilitator then reveals that Region A underwent economic transformation while Region B—identical in material conditions and also possessing the same doctrine—did not. In round two, teams learn that Region B lacked a specific institutional mediator present in A (for example, a legal form enabling capital pooling). They must revise their models using the elective-affinity rule.

The debrief should map moments onto Weberian distinctions: the frustration of being unable to score value cards alone onto the claim that values are not independent causal variables; the Meaning-Adequacy Check onto Verstehen; the multiplicative pairing onto elective affinity; and the round-two predictive failure onto the gap between causal adequacy and adequacy at the level of meaning—showing that an explanation can be culturally adequate yet causally inadequate if the material-institutional partner is missing.

Specify the materials needed (region-profile sheets, card decks, score sheets), a minute-by-minute timeline, and the exact procedure for running the plausibility veto. Predict what will go wrong: teams will try to smuggle value cards in as direct causes and must be stopped by the rules; some will fabricate implausible meaning-links to force pairings, which the veto should catch without shaming. Be explicit about the feature of Weber’s view the activity cannot reproduce: the long-term, multi-generational structure of unintended consequences, and the specific historical content of the Protestant ethic versus other comparable asceticisms—these must be supplied by the facilitator in the closing discussion. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
