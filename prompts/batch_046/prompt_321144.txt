For my philosophy coursework I have been asked to set out my own position on weakness of will and intertemporal choice so that it can be audited. Here are the commitments I hold. First, I think akrasia is genuinely possible: an agent can act against their own all-things-considered judgement, and this is not merely a case of changing one's mind. Second, I accept hyperbolic discounting rather than exponential discounting as the correct descriptive model of how people evaluate future rewards. Third, I believe there is no sharp line between weakness of will and rational preference reversal when the reversal is driven solely by temporal proximity. Fourth, I hold that we have a moral obligation to employ commitment devices to protect our future interests, qualified by what I call the principle of temporal non-interference: a present self may override a past binding decision only if the real cost of compliance has increased by more than fifty per cent. Fifth, I maintain that because hyperbolic discounting is neurologically hard-wired, blaming someone for an akratic choice is always unfair, since their present evaluation is deterministically distorted. Please examine these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severely. For each real tension, state what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me. Finally, advise me which single commitment I should drop or modify if I want to restore consistency with the least damage to my overall view. Please answer in British English.
