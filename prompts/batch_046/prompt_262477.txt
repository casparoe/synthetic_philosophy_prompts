I've been trying to understand why the debate over whether qualia are an incoherent posit just spins its wheels. You know the one: Dennett's "quining" line that qualia fail basic ontological standards -- they're ineffable, intrinsic, private, and therefore conceptually broken -- and then the Chase-Sanborn repsonse that this misreads what phenomenological description is actually doing, that you can't deny the data you're using to deny it, etc. Decades of this and nobody seems to move an inch. So is this actually a substantive disagreement about what's in the ontology, or is it verbal -- do they just mean different things by "incoherent" and "posit"? Or are they answering totally different questions, with Dennett doing conceptual hygiene and Chase-Sanborn defending a descriptive project under one heading? I want a diagnosis that actually engages what each side says, not just "they disagree about consciousness." And I have a hunch that part of the stall is that both sides assume "qualia" names one uniform kind, when maybe the incoherence charge only sticks to a theoretical construct (the Cartesian theater version) but misses a thinner phenomenal residue that isn't being posited at all but just reported. Does that hunch hold water? If the disagreement is real, what would actually count as progress -- a formal result showing phenomenal reports are theory-laden, a distinction that splits the two projects apart, or should we accept the question is ill-posed? Don't give me a reading list, I just want the structure of the impasse mapped out.
