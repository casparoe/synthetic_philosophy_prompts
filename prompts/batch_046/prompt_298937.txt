I was talking with my friends about Plato's cave after re-reading Republic VII, and I think I've talked myself into a weird hybrid view. I want to defend that the forms aren't actually separate entities that particulars copy, but rather they're like exclusion conditions — the form of justice isn't a perfect justice-thing floating somewhere, it's the complete set of constraints that make injustice impossible, and particular just actions are just ones that happen to not violate those constraints. The cave, on this reading, isnt about leaving the physical world for a heavenly realm; it's about realizing that when you think you're seeing a table you're actually seeing a temporary stabilization of conditions that dont depend on that table at all. So the prisoner who escapes doesnt travel upward — he just stops treating the shadows as self-sufficient.

What would count against this? I don't mean the usual but Plato clearly believes in transcendent forms in the Phaedo — I want the internal problems. Which passages in Republic itself break this reading? What does it do to knowledge — if forms are exclusion conditions, is knowing them still knowing something, or is it just knowing what to rule out? Does it collapse into a kind of negative theology or into Aristotelian hylomorphism? And are there modern results, maybe in logic or metaphysics about negative properties, that either support or undermine treating forms this way? Rank the defeaters: which ones are fatal, which ones just make the view awkward, and which ones would someone holding this view honestly concede are damaging rather than just misinterpretations? Please separate what's actually settled about Plato's text here from what's genuinely fought over by people who study ancient philosophy, and don't present a contested reading as if it's the consensus.
