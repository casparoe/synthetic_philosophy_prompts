I am preparing notes for an essay that examines the gender-equality paradox: in societies with stronger institutional equality and reduced economic coercion, occupational choices frequently become more, rather than less, gender-segregated. Rather than replaying the standard nature-against-culture debate, I wish to develop an intermediate framework I call “preference-autonomy asymmetry”. The central thesis is that removing structural barriers does not uncover “raw” biological preferences; rather, it alters the decision environment so that individuals optimise across a wider range of non-pecuniary values—work-life balance, perceived social contribution, risk aversion—values that have been differentially shaped by gendered socialisation even within broadly egalitarian cultures. On this view, increased occupational segregation may signal not innate difference, but that autonomous choice operates upon preferences that remain culturally conditioned. From here, the essay considers whether distributive justice ought to aim at parity in occupational outcomes, parity in the formative conditions of preference, or should abandon outcome-parity in favour of a “capability-for-autonomy” standard. The argument’s normative conclusion is deliberately unsettled.

Please offer critical comments on these notes. Specifically: (1) Is “preference-autonomy asymmetry” a coherent analytical frame, or does it inevitably collapse into either biological essentialism or a pure social-constructivist account? (2) Does the inference from the descriptive pattern (greater segregation under egalitarian conditions) to any normative claim about justice involve an illicit leap, and if so, how might the gap be bridged without presupposing the conclusion? (3) Which empirical or conceptual objections would most seriously undermine the interest of this line of argument? I do not seek bibliographies, citation lists, or recommendations for further reading; I am asking for direct engagement with the logic, coherence, and philosophical interest of the proposal as sketched above.
