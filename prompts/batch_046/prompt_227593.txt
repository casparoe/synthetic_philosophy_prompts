One faction reads the passage as a skeptical argument: since Zhuangzi cannot discriminate, from the inside, between being awake and being a butterfly dreaming it is Zhuangzi, waking consciousness lacks any self-certifying epistemic privilege over dream states. Another faction reads it as a Daoist phenomenological point about transformation and the non-substantiality of the self, insisting that no global skeptical thesis is intended and that the symmetry illustrates identity-fluidity rather than knowledge-failure. All participants agree on the textual details and agree that unbridled global skepticism is practically self-defeating; nevertheless, positions have not shifted in over a year. I am trying to understand whether this is a verbal disagreement about the word 'skepticism,' a substantive disagreement about what counts as an epistemic ground—first-person authority versus neural stability versus pragmatic coherence—or an irreducible clash of bedrock intuitions about the self.
