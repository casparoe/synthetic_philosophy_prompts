Hey so I've been thinking about whether you can actually learn morals from reading about other people's lives, like those old Plutarch paired biographies or modern hagiographies of founders. Here are my gut reactions to a few cases, and I'm wondering if there's a pattern or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Case 1: I read this totally sanitized bio of an old inventor where every failure is erased. I come away feeling pumped but also kinda hollow, like I got a vibe not a lesson. Verdict: no real moral learning happened, just emotional contagion.

Case 2: I read a warts-and-all account of a politician who was genuinely brave during one crisis but corrupt in normal life. Verdict: I actually changed how I think about my own little compromises—this taught me something about how virtues and vices can be compartmentalized.

Case 3: There's this hagiography of a nurse who gave up everything and died young, written with miracle-adjascent language I don't buy at all, and the self-sacrifice looks unhealthy. But wierdly I still came away wanting to be kinder to strangers. Verdict: moral learning happened despite me rejecting both the facts and the values.

Case 4: I read about a monk who lived in total isolation and achieved some kind of inner peace. Verdict: useless, like learning chess from someone who never played—no transferable structure.

Case 5 (the one that messes with me): Bio of a rival founder who was ruthless and successful but with goals I reject. I feel like I learned strategic patience from him, even though I don't want to be him. Then Bio of a founder with values almost identical to mine who failed because he was too nice. I just felt sad and learned nothing, even though the structural lesson about tradeoffs is identical. So same shape, opposite verdict.

Is there one principle here—maybe something about whether the life offers a "counterfactual self" versus just a mirror—or do I have to drop one of these? If you had to pick a simpler rule that kills one verdict, which goes? And what does it predict for a case where I read about someone whose values I share but whose failure was caused by a specific technical mistake I know I wouldn't make?
