I've been chewing on this idea about ontology identification when you've got empirically equivalent theories but one hides an extra layer of entities with structurally inaccessable properties -- not just hard to measure, but impossible in principle to observe. The position I'm defending is that we should identify the ontology with the leaner theory, not just for parsimony but because those hidden entities don't do any real ontological work: they don't explain accessible data, they don't show up in causal traces, and keeping them is basically like adding ghosts to the machinery. What would actually count against this? I don't want generic realism defenses. Would it take showing the lean theory collapses into the richer one? A case where the inaccessable properties do indirect predictive work? Or some argument that ontology isn't about accessibility at all? I'm trying to figure out if this view is genuinely exposed or if it's been insulated by how I framed the accessibility constraint.
