I'm preparing notes for an independent study presentation on Madhyamaka philosophy, and I want a consistency check before I finalize my framework. Here is roughly where I have landed:

(1) I take Nāgārjuna's catuskoti—the fourfold schema of affirmation, denial, both, and neither—to be an exhaustive logical taxonomy not only for first-order ontological claims but for meta-level claims about logic itself, so that it applies reflexively to its own formulation. (2) I understand śūnyatā as the absence of svabhāva in all phenomena, sustained entirely by pratītyasamutpāda, with this applying reflexively: emptiness itself is empty of intrinsic nature, and there is no hidden substrate underlying either term. (3) I reject both eternalism and annihilationism, holding that Madhyamaka avoids extremes by denying intrinsic existence without denying conventional appearance, so emptiness is not a disguised form of nihilism. (4) I maintain that the distinction between conventional truth (saṃvṛti-satya) and ultimate truth (paramārtha-satya) is itself only conventional; they are not two ontological layers but two perspectives on the same dependently originated reality. (5) I also hold that because emptiness and dependent origination are strictly identical, relational structures carry no more ontological weight than intrinsic ones—conventional distinctions are therefore purely pragmatic instruments lacking any internal normative architecture. (6) At the same time, I think soteriological practice genuinely requires distinguishing correct view (samyagdṛṣṭi) from wrong view (mithyādṛṣṭi) at the conventional level, and that this distinction carries real normative force for ethical and cognitive transformation.

Do these commitments hang together? I am particularly worried that (1) and (5) might destabilize the conventional framework that (6) needs, and that (4) combined with (5) collapses the two-truths distinction into something too thin to support normative practice. I also wonder whether the tension between (1) and (3) is only apparent—perhaps reflexive application of the tetralemma does not actually entail self-refutation or slide into nihilism. Could you audit this specific package? Please rank the tensions by severity, distinguish genuine conflicts from merely apparent ones, specify the least costly revision if consistency demands giving something up, and explain what each retained commitment costs in terms of the others. I am not looking for a general introduction to Madhyamaka; I want an audit of whether this exact set of views is jointly tenable.
