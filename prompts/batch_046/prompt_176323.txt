I've been going through this weird headspace since my mom started talking about that brain-computer interface trial she might join for her epilepsy, and now I can't stop thinking about whether I'd still be me if parts of me got replaced bit by bit. Anyway my study partner and I cooked up this argument that feels like it proves way too much and I need someone to tell me which brick is loose.

(1) If a full mind-upload to a computer counts as me surviving — which people like Kurzweil and the rationalist crowd seem to assume — then personal identity can't depend on keeping the same physical neurons, since the computer has zero of them.

(2) So identity must ride on psychological continous: same beliefs, memories, personality structure, that kind of thing.

(3) Now imagine I replace just 1% of my brain with silicon chips that replicate the exact firing patterns, then another 1% every month. At each step I'm psychologically continuous with the step before, because the connections and patterns stay intact.

(4) After a hundred months I'm 100% silicon-brained, but I'm still me, just like in the upload case.

(5) But if I'm still me with a totally synthetic brain, then my original biological brain was never a necessary condition for my identity. I could've been born with a synthetic brain that had the same initial psychology and been the same person.

(6) And if the biological substrate never mattered in the first place, then there's no principled reason I need to be in one body at all. I could distribute my running psychological profile across five servers in different time zones and still be "me" in exactly the same unified, first-person way.

(7) That seems clearly wrong — being scattered across cloud servers isn't being a person, it's being a dataset with delusions of grandeur. So either gradual replacement eventually kills me off (which makes the upload case wrong too), or we have to bite the bullet that I'm not essentially tied to one location, which proves way more than anyone wants. Which premise should I reject, and what's the cost? I'd rather keep (1) and (2) if I can, but I don't know what I'm paying either way.
