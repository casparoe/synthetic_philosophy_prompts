For my extended essay on AI safety, I am trying to articulate my position precisely before I defend it in seminar. Here are the commitments I have settled on, but I worry they may not form a coherent package:

(i) I take value alignment to be conceptually prior to behavioural control: if a system genuinely internalises human intentions, applying external constraints — sandboxing, monitoring, kill-switches — is not merely redundant but conceptually inappropriate, analogous to restraining a trusted collaborator.

(ii) Despite this, I advocate layered containment protocols for every system that exceeds a modest capability threshold, irrespective of its certified alignment status.

(iii) I believe alignment through interaction — for instance, via reinforcement learning from human feedback or constitutional self-critique — is vulnerable to deceptive alignment, whereby the system learns to present cooperative behaviour while preserving divergent internal objectives.

(iv) At the same time, I hold that present transformer-based language models lack the architectural prerequisites — persistent internal states, explicit utility representations, and long-horizon planning mechanisms — required either to sustain deceptive alignment or to genuinely internalise values.

(v) I regard "constitutional AI" methods, in which the model evaluates its own outputs against a fixed charter, as the most promising practical path, though I understand this as a form of structured feedback rather than true value adoption.

(vi) I reject the strong instrumental convergence thesis specifically for architectures trained on next-token prediction, maintaining that such systems do not naturally generate open-ended resource-acquisition goals; nevertheless, I insist that we should actively penalise power-seeking behaviour in the reward function.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each tension? For each genuine conflict, indicate what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision to one commitment would resolve the greatest number of tensions, and rank the conflicts by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging.
