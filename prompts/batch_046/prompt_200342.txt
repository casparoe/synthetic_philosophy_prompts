Here are my verdicts on democratic legitimacy across five institutional arrangements, and I want to know what principle, if any, unifies them.

Case 1: The Athenian boule—five hundred citizens selected by lot, serving one year with immediate post-tenure audit and no reelection. I regard this as paradigmatically legitimate.

Case 2: The contemporary Irish Citizens’ Assembly—ninety-nine randomly selected citizens who deliberate and issue advisory recommendations without binding legislative authority. I also regard this as enhancing democratic legitimacy, despite its lack of decision-making power.

Case 3: A legislature of career politicians—members elected through competitive campaigns, serving for decades with high reelection rates and developing professionalized expertise. I regard this as democratically deficient, closer to an elective oligarchy.

Case 4: A hypothetical “lotocratic veto chamber”—two hundred citizens selected by lot for a single five-year term, paid a high salary, ineligible for other office, empowered only to block legislation, not to propose it. I find this illegitimate, even though selection is random and tenure is limited.

Case 5: An elected assembly with strict eight-year term limits, but with no restriction on returning after a hiatus, so that politicians cycle out and back in, often moving into lobbying or party leadership between terms. I regard this as nearly as deficient as Case 3.

The pattern seems to privilege random selection and short tenure, yet Case 2 lacks binding authority and Case 4 possesses partial authority but long tenure. Meanwhile, both forms of elected careerism (Cases 3 and 5) fail, even though Case 5 technically prohibits lifelong tenure in a single seat.

Is there a single principle—perhaps combining descriptive representation, anti-corruption, and some condition on accountability—that delivers all five verdicts? If not, which verdict should be overturned, and what does the best available principle predict for a case I have not listed, such as a citizens’ jury that is randomly selected, serves only six months, and holds binding authority to approve or reject constitutional amendments?
