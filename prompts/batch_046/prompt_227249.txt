Consider an argument that extends Dennett’s taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—into a reductive collapse. (1) Darwinian creatures possess fixed behavioral repertoires shaped by natural selection; Skinnerian ones can learn by reinforcement within a single lifetime; Popperian ones generate hypotheses and reject them internally before acting; Gregorian ones deploy cultural tools such as language and conceptual frameworks to share, accumulate, and refine those hypotheses across generations. (2) Any Gregorian tool—say, a scientific theory or a grammatical category—is itself the product of prior Popperian testing that has survived cultural transmission. (3) Therefore possessing Gregorian tools does not constitute a distinct cognitive kind but merely grants access to an external archive of pre-tested Popperian outputs; the Gregorian creature is essentially a Popperian creature with outsourced storage. (4) By parallel reasoning, Popperian internal simulation requires representational architectures that were shaped by Skinnerian reinforcement during both ontogeny and phylogeny, so the Popperian creature is just a Skinnerian creature equipped with an internal reward simulator. (5) And Skinnerian plasticity is itself an evolved Darwinian trait—behavioral flexibility selected for over evolutionary time—so the Skinnerian creature is merely a Darwinian creature operating with a longer feedback loop. (6) Hence all four levels collapse into one: Darwinian architecture running at different temporal latencies. (7) But then there is no principled distinction between a reflexive withdrawal from heat and a physicist revising quantum mechanics; both are evolutionary feedback, one rapid and one delayed. (8) That conclusion is intolerable, because we want to preserve both the reality of Gregorian culture and the claim that Popperian internal testing is genuinely different in kind from associative learning. (9) The argument seems valid, and each step appeals only to the uncontroversial idea that one level’s mechanisms are implemented by the architecture beneath it. Where does it go wrong? Identify the specific step that should be rejected, explain why it looked plausible despite being flawed, and price the alternatives—especially if I wish to keep intact both the non-reducibility of Popperian reasoning and the autonomy of Gregorian cultural transmission.
