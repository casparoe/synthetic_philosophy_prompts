Write a sustained, deadpan satirical piece in the form of official minutes and a companion newsletter from the fictional "Spacetime Township Chronospatial Planning Commission," a municipality that has adopted the block-universe eternalism defended in the Rietdijk–Putnam argument as literal zoning doctrine. The piece should run approximately 600 words, divided between formal commission minutes (motions, variances, enforcement actions) and a practical newsletter sidebar for residents. Target the metaphysical position itself—its denial of objective temporal becoming, its treatment of "now" as an indexical comparable to "here," and its inference from the relativity of simultaneity to the ontological equality of past, present, and future—not any named living person. To keep the satire in the weeds rather than generic, invent and consistently apply a niche bureaucratic vocabulary: "worldline easements," "four-dimensional lot-coverage ratios," "Lorentz-adjusted property lines," "temporal-equity variances," "causal-set landscaping restrictions," and "Minkowski zoning overlays." The minutes should record disputes in which residents attempt to demolish "past" structures, apply for permits for "future" renovations that already exist in the four-dimensional lot, or protest property-tax assessments calculated on four-volume rather than three-volume footprints. Include a straight-faced citation of the Rietdijk–Putnam precedent as the legal basis for ruling that no privileged "now-slice" can determine demolition schedules, occupancy permits, or noise ordinances. The newsletter should offer deadpan practical advice—how to file a "worldline maintenance fee" covering all temporal slices of one's home, how to resolve boundary disputes with one's own future self, and what to do if a neighbor's past renovation violates one's present sightline—without ever breaking character to explain the joke. The humor must come from exaggerating real features of the view (the spatialization of time, the illusion of change, the indexicality of the present) rather than inventing false ones, and the irony should remain fully legible: a specialist should recognize accurate understanding of the argument, while a general reader should see clearly that the doctrine has been pushed to an absurd bureaucratic extreme. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or page references; do not invent philosophers beyond Rietdijk and Putnam; do not quote any real text verbatim. Keep the voice uniformly bureaucratic, precise, and euphemistic.
