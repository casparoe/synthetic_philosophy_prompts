Write a short research proposal (approximately two pages, suitable for a one-semester workshop pitch or small-grant application) on the following problem in translation theory and the philosophy of poetic form. The domain is Clément Marot’s poem referred to by the phrase “Le Ton beau de Marot”, with particular attention to the preservation of its rondeau rhyme scheme (ABaAabAB) and its decasyllabic lineation in English translation.

State the question precisely: does the formal constraint of maintaining both the rondeau rhyme scheme and a ten-syllable line in English versions of this poem necessarily distort the conceptual relation between “ton”—understood as stylistic or social register—and “beau”—understood as intrinsic aesthetic quality—that structures the original text? And, if such distortion is systematic, does it support the thesis that formal-constraint translation in early modern lyric is not mere formal replication but a form of conceptual interpretation?

Explain why existing answers leave this open. Translation studies typically treats semantic fidelity and formal fidelity as independent variables to be traded off case by case, without examining how specific rhyme-slot positions (the A and B slots) and syllabic limits exert targeted pressure on conceptual vocabulary. Philosophical accounts of translation equivalence discuss conceptual change across languages, but rarely attend to the granular mechanics of rhyme-position and lineation in fixed-form poetry. Literary-historical scholarship on Marot’s influence discusses his stylistic legacy without analysing the conceptual work performed when a translator selects a rhyme-word that preserves metre but substitutes register-language for aesthetic-language, or vice versa.

Set out the approach and justify it against obvious alternatives. The method will be a comparative close reading of three historically separated English translations, mapping precisely where correlates of “ton” and “beau” appear and whether shifts in meaning align with fixed rhyme positions or with syllabic compression and expansion. This focused comparative design is preferable to large-scale corpus analysis, which would exceed the scope of one semester, and to purely theoretical argument, which would lack the granular textual evidence required to support a claim about form-driven conceptual bias.

Describe what a result would look like and what would count as failure. A positive result would be a reproducible taxonomy demonstrating that rhyme-slot constraints systematically bias translation toward one conceptual pole—register or aesthetic value—over the other, thereby supporting the interpretation thesis. The project fails if the observed shifts prove idiosyncratic and uncorrelated with formal position, undermining the claim that metrical preservation exerts interpretive pressure on conceptual content.

Give an honest assessment of scope and deliverables. The project is tractable within one semester if confined to English translations and to close comparative analysis; the deliverable will be the structured argument together with the taxonomy of shift-types and a conceptual framework linking metrical position to semantic bias. However, the project would become unmanageable if extended to a full multilingual corpus, to quantitative stylometric analysis, or to a complete historical survey of Marot’s reception across European literature.
