I'm a normative ethics PhD student and I sit on my university's low-risk social science review panel. We recieve protocol amendments where PIs want to do extra stuff for participants -- personalized feedback reports, extra debriefing, that kind of thing. I need a reusable decision procedure grounded in supererogation theory that I can run in about 15 minutes per case. It should sort extras into three seperate buckets: (1) actually obligatory but mislclassified as nice-to-have, (2) genuinely supererogatory -- good but not required, and we should praise but not mandate, and (3) not morally good (maybe paternalistic or wasteful). The checklist needs to handle hard cases: costly-to-agent vs cheap-for-agent benefits, institutional role obligations, and dependency risks. Apply it to three hypothetical amendments: (a) a mental-health study PI offers to share individual-level results with participants, (b) a PI wants to provide unpaid career counseling to all dropouts, and (c) a PI proposes extending the study an extra unfunded year just so enrolled people don't feel abandoned. One should come out as "procedure approves it but you'll hate that answer." Flag the steps that are just judgment calls dressed up as rules, and say what the procedure can't settle. Please answer in American English.
