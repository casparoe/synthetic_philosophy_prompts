So a lot of universities now have these 'Gen Z student success offices' that design mental-health outreach and course pacing around traits supposedly shared by anyone born like 1997-2012, but they don't have an office for people born in 1995 or 2013 even though they're right next door age-wise. At the same time HR departments do 'generational diversity training' that treats Boomers, Millenials, and Gen Z as totally different workplace cultures, yet they never run training for 'people who started working during the 2008 crash' as if that cohort was a real cultural kind too. What ontology of social groups is this actually presupposing? Does the practice only make sense if generations are natural kinds with some shared essence, or could a hardcore constructivist still justify slicing people up exactly this way? Reconstruct the implicit theory behind the institutional design, check whether it's consistent with ignoring other overlapping cohorts like recession-era hires, and say what campus and corporate policy would have to look like if generations turned out to be pure administrative fictions with zero causal powers of their own.
