(2) The symmetry that matters is algorithmic, not merely psychological—if another system runs a decision procedure isomorphic to mine at the relevant level, superrational cooperation applies regardless of substrate or background beliefs.
(3) I am a causal decision theorist for single-agent choice: in Newcomb’s problem I reject one-boxing, because my present choice cannot causally affect the predictor’s past action.
(4) I think decision theory should be unified—the same framework should cover strategic interaction and prediction problems, not separate modules.
(5) I think it is rational to cooperate in the dilemma even with only about 70% confidence that the other agent’s algorithm is isomorphic, since partial symmetry still yields a non-negligible expected correlation.

Are these jointly tenable? Specifically, do (1) and (2) pull against (3) and (4), given that the correlation justifying cooperation in the dilemma is evidential rather than causal? Is there an apparent tension between (2) and (5) about what counts as sufficient symmetry, or does that dissolve once confidence is treated probabilistically? For each pair in real tension, rank the severity, say what the cheapest revision would cost, and then tell me which single change to my package would clean up the most inconsistencies.
