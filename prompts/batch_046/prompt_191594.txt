I've been thinking about the abortion debate and I think I've landed on a kind of hybrid view that doesn't quite fit either camp. Basically I accept Thomson's framing that bodily autonomy is real and weighty, but I think the violinist analogy breaks down in a way people don't talk about enough: being pregnant isn't like being kidnapped and hooked up to a stranger, it's more like being the only available caregiver for a being whose entire biological purpose right now is to develop inside you, which creates a parental-type duty even if you never consented to become a parent. At the same time, I think the future-like-ours argument is stronger than people admit, except I want to defend it in a modified form: it's not just that the fetus has a future like ours, but that destroying it is wrong specifcally because it severs a continuous biological-psychological trajectry that, if uninterrupted, would produce the kind of conscious continuity that makes killing adults wrong. So my position is that abortion is wrong in most cases because of this trajectory-severing, but Thomson-style exceptions might apply in cases where the pregnancy threatens the mother's own future psychological continuity or in rape cases where the parental duty never gets activated because there's no species-typical caregiving context.

What would actually count against this? I'm not looking for the usual "potentiality isn't actual" line, I already know that one. I want the defeaters that hit the specific hybrid I'm describing. Like, what if there's evidence from developmental psychology that there's no meaningful continuity between fetal brain states and adult consciousness? Or what if the "parental duty" analogy fails because toddlers are already socially embedded in a way fetuses aren't? And is there a risk I've just qualified my view so much that it can't be falsifed? I'd like you to rank the objections from fatal to just awkward, and be honest about whether my view collapses back into standard pro-life or pro-choice positions once you push on it. Also I'm curious if there's a formal problem with saying psychological continuity starts at some point in gestation but not others — does that commit me to a view about personal identity that I don't want?
