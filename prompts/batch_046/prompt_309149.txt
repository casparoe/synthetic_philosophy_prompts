I'm drafting an essay for an advanced seminar on Quine's philosophy, and I've been arguing with a friend about whether Quine's relative silence on ethics is actually a structural feature of his system rather than a mere omission. My central claim is that one can reconstruct Quine's implicit stance on moral values by extending two familiar doctrines—his ontological criterion, which ties existence claims to the values of bound variables in regimented theory, and the thesis of the indeterminacy of translation—into normative territory in a direction he never explicitly pursued.

Specifically, I propose that moral predicates should be understood on the model of analytical hypotheses in radical translation: they are pragmatic overlays adopted to render behavioral regularities intelligible, not theoretical posits forced upon us by sensory stimulation. Just as a field linguist may select among competing analytical hypotheses without altering the empirical predictions of her translation manual, a community may adopt divergent moral frameworks without altering the observable patterns of social cooperation those frameworks organize. On this reading, moral values carry no distinct ontological commitment—there are no moral entities in our ontology—yet they are not eliminable either, because they serve as indispensable coordination devices analogous to the logical and mathematical apparatus that Quine grants indirect empirical standing through its role in scientific theory.

The twist I am trying to defend is that moral discourse therefore exhibits a kind of double indeterminacy: not only is there no fact of the matter about which moral framework is correct (parallel to translation manuals), but there is also no fact of the matter about whether moral language is being used literally or instrumentally, since both interpretations yield identical behavioral outputs and serve identical pragmatic functions. I worry that this collapses Quine's cautious naturalism into a form of moral anti-realism he might have resisted, though I am unsure whether I have genuinely distorted his commitments or merely drawn out an unstated implication.

I also wonder whether the claim of double indeterminacy is coherent, or whether it mistakes pragmatic variability for ontological neutrality.
