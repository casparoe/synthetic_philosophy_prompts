Write a short story about a archivist in a city where every document is made of memory-paper that only retains ink from people with state-issued voice-licences. The protagonist finds a blank folio that shouldn't exist — it's not missing, its actively refusing to be filled — and has to figure out whether the silence in the archive is a record of someone who was erased, or a record of the system that made them unrecordable. Don't make it a simple dystopia; I want the concept of archival silence treated like a physical property, not just a metaphor, maybe like how absence can have weight if you measure it right. Keep it casual and a bit messy, like you're telling me the idea over coffee.
