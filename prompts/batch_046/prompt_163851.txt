In my grad seminar on rationality we've been stuck on this for months: should philosophers use an 'outside view' on their own disagreements? One camp says yes, obvious - philosophy has a terrible consencus record, so when you disagree with a peer you should downgrade your confidence. The other camp says there's no stable reference class here, and if you actually applied the outside view consistently you'd have to downgrade your belief that there's no stable reference class too, which collapses into self-undermining skepticism. I keep wondering if they're actually arguing about different things: one about epistemic norms, one about whether philosophy is like weather forecasting. But maybe I'm wrong. Is this a hidden factual dispute about whether philosophy has measurable error rates? A verbal fight about what 'outside view' means when the domain is reflexive? Or are they just hitting bedrock intuitions about whether reasoning can be turned on itself? And if it's the reflexivity thing - that applying the view to itself is unstable - is that actually a problem for the outside-view camp, or do they have a reply I'm missing? What kind of result or distinction would actually break the stalemate here?
