I'm a journalist who covers science and philosophy, and I'm preparing a one-year independent research proposal for a fellowship that funds conceptual work at the intersection of psychology and ethics. The domain is cross-cultural variation in folk morality—particularly the WEIRD-sample critique—and what that variation implies for moral objectivity. If the divergence is partly architectural rather than merely propositional, then the usual objectivity question ("do conflicting beliefs entail no fact of the matter?") may be miscast.

Write the proposal as a two-page document with the following elements. Fifth, define failure conditions honestly: what outcome would mean the framing collapses or the project is too ambitious for the timeline. Sixth, provide a realistic scope assessment, broken down by phase, that shows the work can be completed in twelve months by one researcher. Keep the tone formal and polished, appropriate for a review panel that includes both scientists and humanists.
