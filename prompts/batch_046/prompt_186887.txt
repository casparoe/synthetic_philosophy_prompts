I am currently enrolled in an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar, and this week we examined epiphenomenalism together with what is often called the paradox of phenomenal judgment. The paradox runs roughly as follows: if conscious qualitative states are causally inert with respect to physical processes, then any judgment I make about my own phenomenology—say, that I am now experiencing a reddish quale—is itself caused not by the quale but solely by the underlying neural mechanisms. This raises the worrying possibility that the very reasoning used to defend epiphenomenalism is disconnected from the phenomenal reality it describes. During a study session, a friend proposed that we try to capture this tension in a joke that is actually philosophically literate rather than a superficial zombie gag.

Specifically, she suggested framing it around a conference scenario in which an epiphenomenalist speaker attempts to persuade an audience that phenomenal consciousness exerts no causal influence, yet in doing so must implicitly treat her own feeling of conviction as causally irrelevant—even to the formation of the audience's beliefs. Could you write such a joke? It should be somewhat elaborate—perhaps a brief dialogue with a clear setup and punchline—and the humor should turn precisely on the structure of the paradox: that the speaker's judgment about consciousness is physically caused and therefore cannot be epistemically anchored to the conscious experience she claims is inefficacious. Please avoid generic science-fiction tropes and keep the conceptual machinery explicit, so that someone familiar with the debate would immediately recognize the philosophical point being lampooned rather than seeing a generic nerd joke.
