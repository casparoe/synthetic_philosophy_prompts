I am moderating a podcast interview with an unnamed philosopher who defends what she calls "epistemic deflationism" about intertemporal money pumps. On her view, every preference cycle that seems to invite diachronic exploitation is actually driven by implicit belief updates—for instance, that accepting a bet today alters one's evidence about future self-control or about hidden properties of the options—rather than by pure temporal inconsistency. She denies that there is any genuinely belief-independent reversal. I want six to eight interview questions that avoid letting her merely restate the thesis. Please include: one question that forces her to distinguish belief-driven preference dynamics from post-hoc rationalisation of inconstancy; one that targets the canonical "apple now versus apple tomorrow" case, where it is unclear what belief could plausibly change; one that asks whether her position collapses into resolute choice—sticking to a plan despite later preference shifts—or into some form of epistemic precommitment; and one that asks what empirical or behavioural evidence could, in principle, distinguish her account from a standard intransitive-preference model. For each question, provide a follow-up keyed to her most probable reply—especially the claim that belief updates are structurally unavoidable in any sequential decision context. The audience is familiar with basic decision theory and Dutch-book reasoning, but not with her framing. Write in polished British English, with no request for citations or reading lists.
