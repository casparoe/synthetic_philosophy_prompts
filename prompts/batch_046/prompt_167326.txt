I have come to accept a specific form of ontic structural realism: that in fundamental physics, category-theoretic morphisms are ontologically prior to objects, that theory change should be understood as the retention of symmetry-group structure rather than the persistence of entities, and that macroscopic objects are therefore not composed of fundamental relata but are emergent structural patterns with no additional ontological weight. What would have to be true for this position to fail? I want an honest assessment of its exposure: which formal results in quantum mechanics or quantum gravity would genuinely threaten the claim that structure survives theory change; which philosophical objections—such as the charge that concrete relations require relata, or that symmetry arguments presuppose the very objects they claim to dissolve—are fatal rather than merely awkward; what a defender of this precise variant (not generic structural realism) would themselves concede as counting against them; and whether the view has been insulated by qualifications—redefining "object" or "structure" post hoc—into something unfalsifiable, and if so whether that insulation is intellectually costly. Please rank the defeaters from decisive to inconvenient, distinguish evidence that defenders would accept from evidence they would dismiss, and write your answer in British English.
