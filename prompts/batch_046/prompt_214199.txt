I hold a somewhat weird take on Parfit's hitchhiker: I think the rational agent doesn't just precommit to pay, but actually restructures her own reasoning so she can't process evidence that the driver will leave her behind — not ignoring it, but being structurally unable to represent it in her decision calculus. This is meant to be stronger than causal decision theory precommitment but weaker than full evidential submission. What would count against this? I want the arguments and formal results that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be — especially anything showing this 'epistmic restructuring' collapses into ordinary precommitment or gets exploited in itnerated versions. Also, what do defenders of standard rational-irrationality solutions themselves concede as a real problem for my version? And does this view risk anything, or have I qualified it into unfalsifiability? If it is well-insulated, is that actually a problem for a view about rationality?
