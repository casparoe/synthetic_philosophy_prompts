Say Aristotle was alive today and came accross the modern debate about moral saints—people who try to be maximally good, like the ones Susan Wolf talks about, where being a saint might actually make you worse because you lose your humor, hobbies, and become exhausting to be around. How do you think he'd react? Would he say true virtue is always in the middle and that maxing out morality destroys eudaimonia? Or that if your practical wisdom is good enough the non-moral stuff falls into place?

Also here's a twist: imagine the saint is using modern decision tools—running every choice through some global welfare calcualus—to constantly optimize. Does that change Aristotle's answer, or is it the same problem but now with a spreadsheet?
