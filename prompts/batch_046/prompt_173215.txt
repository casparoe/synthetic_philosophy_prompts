Please argue that Zhuangzi's treatment of death, when read through a distinction between substantialist personal identity and what we might call processual continuity, implies that conventional mourning should be reconceived not as a response to absence but as an acknowledgment of transformation. Defend the specific claim that if death is the dispersal of qi into broader natural processes—as illustrated in the Zhuangzi's imagery of metamorphosis—then the emotional posture appropriate to it is closer to reverence for change than to grief for loss. You should address the objection that this collapses death into mere biological alteration, and explain why Zhuangzi's examples, including the transformation of the kun into the peng and the calm acceptance of a spouse's death, do not license a generic pantheism but rather target the ego-boundary that makes the concept of loss intelligible. Be precise about what loss conceptually requires—namely, a persisting subject for whom something is missing—and show how Zhuangzi's metaphysics undermines that presupposition without denying the reality of the transformation itself.
