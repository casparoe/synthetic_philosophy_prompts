(3) Therefore the xenovesicle is alive.

(5) So flames and hurricanes exhibit organizational closure in exactly the same sense: their dynamics produce the conditions of their own persistence.

(6) Therefore flames and hurricanes are alive.

(7) But treating the extinction of a candle or the dissipation of a storm as an act of killing — with corresponding moral weight — is clearly absurd.

(8) So organizational closure cannot be sufficient for life.

Which step should be rejected? Explain why it initially looked plausible, whether there is a hidden equivocation between constitutive production and causal maintenance that saves premise (1), and what each alternative costs: abandoning (1), blocking (4)-(5), or denying (6).
