Although raised in a Puritan milieu that stressed human corruption, Franklin's mature method reflects Stoic habituation more than theological grace; indeed, he derived the thirteen virtues by systematically expanding Plato's four cardinal virtues—prudence, justice, temperance, and fortitude—into a hierarchical sequence running from bodily discipline to spiritual refinement. He added Humility only after a friend criticised his habitual pride, which shows the system incorporated social feedback like an experimental protocol. Ultimately, Franklin concluded that the project had failed: he never cleared all his black marks, abandoned the pocket-book in his later years, and admitted that moral perfection was unattainable for fallen human beings. Still, he believed the attempt made him more useful and content, even if it could not deliver the perfection he sought.
