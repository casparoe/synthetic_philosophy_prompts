Assume the role of a professional philosopher specializing in 19th-century metaphysics of art and the philosophy of music. Before you answer, spend a moment brainstorming the conceptual architecture: identify the key distinctions in Schopenhauer's aesthetics—especially his separation of music from the other arts, his claim that music does not represent Platonic Ideas but directly objectifies the Will, and his subordinate distinction between melody as the temporal striving of the will and harmony as its spatial analog.

Now consider the concrete institutional practice of the modern Western art-music concert: the hall is darkened, the audience is seated in fixed rows and expected to remain motionless and silent except at designated intervals, performers wear standardized formal dress that minimizes bodily particularity, and program notes—when present—are restricted to formal analysis or historical context, actively discouraging narrative or representational interpretation of the instrumental work. This arrangement treats the musical event as an occasion for pure, disembodied auditory contemplation separated from practical engagement, social interaction, or conceptual decoding.

What philosophical commitments about the nature of music and aesthetic experience does this practice presuppose? In particular, does it presuppose something structurally close to Schopenhauer's view that music bypasses the world of representation and expresses the Will immediately—hence demanding a receptive posture that suppresses both bodily movement and conceptual interpretation? Or can the same practice be reconstructed under rival metaphysics, for instance one that treats instrumental music as a symbolic language or as an embodied ritual?

Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguishing what the practice strictly requires from what merely happens to fit it. Then examine an internal tension: the practice suppresses audience movement, which might align with Schopenhauer's idea that music should be received without practical will-engagement, yet it also privileges harmonic and formal analysis in program notes, which treats music as a structured object of cognition—closer to Schopenhauer's account of the other arts that represent Ideas. Do these two elements—bodily suppression and formalist interpretation—presuppose consistent Schopenhauerian commitments, or do they pull in opposite directions?

Finally, suppose Schopenhauer's metaphysics were false and music were genuinely representational through and through, with no special direct access to the Will. Which features of this concert institution would become philosophically unmotivated, and which could survive on other grounds—say, social signaling, attention economics, or ritual cohesion? Be precise about what changes and what remains intact.
