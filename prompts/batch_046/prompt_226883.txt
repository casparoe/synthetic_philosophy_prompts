Please explain the conceptual tension between adopting an inside view on one's own reasoning and an outside view on one's cognitive reliability, particularly in contexts where philosophers discuss epistemic modesty. Consider the case of an agent who possesses strong first-order reasons for accepting a particular metaphysical or normative framework—reasons she can articulate and defend—while simultaneously knowing that experts with comparable credentials and training are divided roughly evenly on the very same question. How ought she integrate these two perspectives? In your answer, distinguish carefully between calibrating her degree of confidence and calibrating the content of her belief, and examine whether epistemic modesty requires treating her own reasoning processes as opaque mechanisms whose outputs should be discounted, or whether it allows for a form of transparent self-trust in which she can maintain that her particular reasons genuinely support her conclusion while still adjusting her all-things-considered confidence downward.
