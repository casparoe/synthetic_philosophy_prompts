I serve on a municipal well-being review board that must decide whether to adopt the proposed Integrated Hedonic Assessment (IHA) for ranking public-space and cultural-program funding. The IHA reduces every proposal to a single score by summing self-reported pleasure across physical comfort, social connection, intellectual engagement, and aesthetic experience, weighted only by intensity and duration. Proponents say cross-domain comparison is impossible without such aggregation; critics reply that this assumes pleasures share a common quantitative quality, when in fact the satisfaction of solving a geometric proof and the warmth of sunlight on skin may both be called “pleasant” without possessing any introspectively identical feature that licenses arithmetic combination—so the metric risks a category error rather than a measurement problem. Half the board treats aggregation as a practical necessity; half treats it as conceptually incoherent. Draft our decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—opening with the bottom-line recommendation in the first sentence, then giving reasoning that program officers could actually respond to, including an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Keep the memo to about 350 words; do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts.
