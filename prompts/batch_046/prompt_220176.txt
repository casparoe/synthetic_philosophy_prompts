Design a 75-minute structured simulation for a seminar of 14 upper-division students in philosophy of mind. The objective is to make the pressure of multiple realizability felt as an internal crisis for functionalism rather than an external objection: participants should experience how the very openness that makes functionalism attractive—mental states defined solely by causal-functional roles—seems to force an unprincipled disjunction or an implausible biological chauvinism when confronted with a genuinely alien substrate.

Introduce, in the framing document (not as a standard example like Block’s nation or a silicon brain but as a precisely specified hypothetical), the Mnemosyne Lattice: a system that implements the complete causal-functional graph of human belief-formation, inference, and perceptual integration, but is physically constituted by slowly decaying quantum correlations within a cryogenic superlattice, with no action potentials, no neurotransmitters, and no localized nodes; its “processing” is distributed across temporal rather than spatial dimensions, and its error-correction operates via topological entanglement rather than feedback loops. Participants must treat this not as science fiction but as a stipulated case of perfect functional isomorphism.

Structure: Divide into three rotating roles—Functional Evaluator, Substrate Engineer, and Phenomenological Witness—each with a hidden scoring card. In Rounds 1–3 (15 minutes each), the Substrate Engineer reveals one layer of the Lattice’s physical architecture (first: absence of cellular structure; second: temporal distribution of states; third: reliance on quantum decoherence for “memory” decay). After each revelation, all participants vote, by secret ballot, whether the system possesses beliefs, with points awarded for consistency with their prior round but penalized for arbitrary exclusion (defined in the rules as “rejecting the Lattice while accepting human brains without identifying a functionally relevant difference”). In Round 4 (15 minutes), roles are swapped and participants must defend, in paired deliberation, either the inclusion or exclusion of the Lattice using only functional criteria; if they invoke substrate properties, they must specify exactly which functional role is thereby disrupted. The facilitator introduces no verdict during the game.

Debrief (15 minutes): Map Round 1 onto the initial functionalist intuition that causal role is sufficient. Map Round 2 onto the emergence of the “disjunction problem”—if the Lattice is included, mental-state kinds seem to cover wildly heterogeneous physical bases, threatening explanatory unity. Map Round 3 onto the absence of a non-arbitrary exclusion principle: ask the group to state the principle they actually used, then show that it either excludes some human brains (e.g., brains with prosthetic neural replacements) or fails to exclude the Lattice. Map Round 4 onto the residual affective resistance—the sense that “something is missing”—and distinguish this from an articulated argument.

Be explicit that the activity cannot reproduce the first-person authority or qualitative character of conscious experience; the facilitator must supply this limitation verbally, noting that the Lattice’s functional equivalence gives no purchase on whether there is “something it is like” to be it, and that this gap is precisely what motivates some philosophers to reject functionalism regardless of the disjunction problem.

Predictable failure mode: The group will likely converge on a vague biological boundary condition not because they have found a functionally relevant difference, but because of affective resistance to the Lattice’s alien substrate. The facilitator should surface this by asking, after the vote, which participants changed their judgment only after the quantum-correlation layer was revealed, and whether that layer was shown to alter any functional relation—if not, the change reveals chauvinism rather than reasoning, which is itself the pedagogical target. End with an honest note: the simulation makes the structural instability of pure functionalism visceral, but it cannot generate the phenomenological intuition that functional equivalence might still be metaphysically insufficient; that must be introduced by the facilitator as an unresolved tension.
