I am examining the institutional practice by which transnational bodies—Freedom House, V-Dem, and the EU’s Article 7 framework—classify Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the United States as instances of “democratic backsliding.” The practice applies composite indices that weight judicial independence, media pluralism, and electoral integrity in roughly equal measure, treats these indicators as comparable across presidential, parliamentary, and hybrid regimes, and uses the classifications to trigger funding conditionality, sanctions, or diplomatic censure. I want to know what philosophical commitments this practice actually presupposes, as opposed to merely accommodating, and whether the concept does analytical work or functions primarily as a partisan label.

In particular:

- Does the practice presuppose a teleological or directional model of democracy—that liberal constitutionalism is a stable equilibrium from which states can regress—or can it be reconstructed consistently with democratic minimalism, under which only competitive elections and basic civil liberties count?

- By weighting judicial, media, and electoral safeguards symmetrically, does it presuppose that democracy is a “thick” concept combining procedural and substantive liberal values, and does that commit the raters to a particular theory of institutional value pluralism?

- The ratings depend on counterfactual baselines—expert judgments about what an institutional trajectory “would have been” absent interference. Does this presuppose an objectivist epistemology about democratic norms that democratic minimalism, with its insistence that only domestic publics can legitimately judge democratic quality, must reject?

- I am also working with a distinction I have not seen fully developed in the literature: between “institutional decay” (objective, measurable erosion of procedural safeguards) and “normative divergence” (a polity’s legitimate democratic choice to reorder priorities, for instance elevating majoritarian authority over liberal-constitutional constraints). Does the backsliding framework necessarily collapse these two? If so, does that mean the concept is doing analytical work, or is it mainly disguising normative disagreement as empirical regression?

Please reconstruct the implicit philosophical theory (or competing theories) embedded in the practice. Distinguish what it strictly requires from what merely fits it well. Identify where the practice is philosophically underdetermined—where rival theories of democracy, knowledge, and value could license identical ratings. Then explain what would have to change in the institutional framework if we replaced its implicit theory with democratic minimalism plus a purely procedural baseline, and whether any non-partisan analytical content of “backsliding” would survive that replacement.

Please state clearly what argument or evidence would change your verdict on whether the concept is primarily analytical or partisan, and identify the weakest point in your reconstruction. End with a single-line summary beginning exactly with “Summary: ”.
