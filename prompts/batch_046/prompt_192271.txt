I was covering this panel on tech ethics and then grabbed drinks with two people after — Leah, who writes about organizational culture, and Marcus, who actually studies Confucian philosophy. They got into it about whether Confucian role ethics could justify a software engineer blowing the whistle on her own team. Leah said the core of role ethics is that obligations flow from specific relationships: parent-child, ruler-subject, colleague-colleague. So if your an engineer, your first duty is loyalty to your manager and team, not some abstract public. She brought up wu lun and zhengming — that you have to rectify names and act within your assigned role before you start deciding which rules apply.

Marcus pushed back. He said Confucianism isn't just hierarchy; Mengzi's four sprouts show everyone has innate compassion, and the practice of shu — analogical extension — means you can't wall off strangers. If she finds a dangerous flaw, her role as professional or even just human being extends care outward. He claimed some modern readers even interpret the Analects as a kind of relational contractarianism: role-obligations only bind when both sides uphold the moral core of the role. So if the manager is violating that core, her duty of loyalty dissolves.

Leah said that's just liberal individualism wearing Confucian clothes. Without particular roles, she argued, you can't condemn exploitation hidden inside a proper relationship — like a bad parent using filial piety to demand silence. Marcus replied that without some universal floor, Confucianism is helpless against exactly that kind of abuse.
