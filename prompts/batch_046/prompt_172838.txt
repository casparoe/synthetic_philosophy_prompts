Construct a thought experiment in which a biographer must decide whether to include a recently discovered, intimate journal entry that undermines the heroic public narrative of a deceased figure who explicitly asked in life that private writings never be used for posthumous portrayal. Engineer the case so that a "derivative-obligation" framework—holding that duties to the dead exist only as extensions of promises made to living people—recommends publication, while a "representational-sovereignty" framework—holding that misrepresenting a dead person's self-conception is a direct wrong regardless of promises—recommends suppression. Constraints: realistic setting (a publishing office or documentary edit suite, no courts or fantasy elements), the scenario in one paragraph, neither verdict should depend on an obviously bad principle but should fall out of an optimal version of each framework, and the divergence must stem from whether the wrong attaches to broken promises to survivors or to the representation act itself. After the scenario, show the reasoning for each framework, identify the structural feature that drives the split, explain what happens if that feature is removed, and state how a defender of the publication view would attack the case. Maximum 400 words.
