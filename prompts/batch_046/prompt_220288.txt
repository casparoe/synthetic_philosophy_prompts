Question: Some philosophers defend cyclical preferences against the money-pump argument by appealing to diachronic rationality—an agent can rationally prefer A to B on Monday and B to A on Tuesday. Now consider a defense built on 'intertemporal substitution elasticity': the idea that an agent's discount factor shifts non-uniformly across goods, so her willingness to swap A for B at t1 doesn't carry over to swapping B for C at t2. Does this actually block the pump, or does it just rename preference instability?

Student answer:
The money pump works when you have A>B, B>C, C>A all at once, letting a bookmaker cycle you back and charge each trade. Diachronic rationality allows change over time, so if my rankings shift across days there's no contradiction—I'm just a different chooser. The intertemporal substitution elasticity view says my discount rate for different goods shifts unevenly, kind of like hyperbolic discounting but spread across goods rather than just over time. Because of this, my Monday self and Tuesday self have incommensurable utility scales, so there's no single agent for the bookmaker to expliot. Each slice is localy consistent, so cyclical preferences aren't irrational—they're just spread across time.
