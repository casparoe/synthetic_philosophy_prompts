I'm preparing an interview for my philosophy-of-mind podcast with a scholar who has recently advanced a revised version of Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Rather than treating the Gregorian tier as a binary achievement of cultural transmission, she argues it should be understood as a graded spectrum of "normative scaffolding," and she places contemporary large language models in an anomalous intermediate position: they exhibit Popperian-style simulation in their latent-space prediction, yet lack the embodied, affectively tagged commitment that, on her view, distinguishes true Gregorian cognition from mere tool use. She also maintains that Skinnerian learning in biological organisms is not reducible to reinforcement tracking but involves an irreducible "affective tagging" mechanism that synthetic architectures do not replicate.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that move well beyond having her summarize the four kinds. I want at least one question pressing her on whether her scaffolding-spectrum collapses Gregorian cognition into sophisticated instrumentality—the standard objection Dennett himself faces—together with a follow-up tailored to the reply she is most likely to give, namely that normative commitment rather than tool complexity marks the boundary. Include at least one question about whether Popperian mental pre-selection can be instantiated in subpersonal predictive-processing systems without any conscious deliberation, with a follow-up that anticipates her probable appeal to functional architecture over phenomenology. And include at least one question on the "affective tagging" thesis: specifically, ask whether it commits her to a form of biological exceptionalism that undermines her own framework's applicability to artificial minds, with a follow-up keyed to her likely defense that tagging is an organizational property rather than a biochemical one. The remaining questions should probe the implications of her intermediate-LLM category for theories of cognitive evolution and for the ethics of attributing mental states to synthetic systems, again with follow-ups that do not merely ask for clarification but press the tension she has introduced.
