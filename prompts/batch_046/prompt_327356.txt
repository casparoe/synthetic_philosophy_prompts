Student answer:
Conciliationism—like Adam Elga and David Christensen—says that when you learn an epistemic peer disagrees, you should split the difference and move your credence about halfway to theirs. Steadfastness, defended by Thomas Kelly, says you can hold your ground if you've got strong first-order reasons. The spinelessness objection, which Elga raises, is that conciliationists become spineless because every new disagreement forces them to shift again.
