You sit on the research-ethics and funding review panel of a national biomedical research council. A consortium has applied for a five-year pilot grant programme to develop a germline-targeted, non-reversible suppression of nociceptive and affective-distress pathways in a captive macaque population—what the applicants term 'valence restructuring', distinguishing it sharply from conventional analgesia. They invoke a hedonistic imperative: suffering is the sole intrinsic evil, and if biotechnology can heritably eliminate it, withholding such intervention is ethically indistinguishable from permitting preventable harm. A minority of panel members accepts the welfare goal but argues that removing the capacity for distress dissolves the relational feature—vulnerability anchored in moral patienthood—that makes these animals proper objects of ethical concern, reducing them instead to 'welfare artefacts' lacking any claim on us. The majority regards this objection as speculative metaphysics that obstructs routine welfare science.

Draft the panel's written opinion recommending approval with conditions, approval with modifications, or rejection. Your recommendation must specify a concrete course of action with a defined time frame—for instance, a phased trial with mandatory welfare audits, a sunset clause, or an immediate termination protocol. You must engage the minority's 'moral-patienthood' argument seriously rather than dismissing it, and you must address whether the proposal's irreversibility and germline scope alter the ethical calculus compared to standard analgesic practice. The memo should be suitable for circulation before Thursday's meeting. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
