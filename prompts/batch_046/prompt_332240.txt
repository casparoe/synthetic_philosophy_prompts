Your argument should first expose the fatal tension in the classic intentionalist model, which requires that the deceiver know the very truth being hidden from the deceived, thereby generating a paradox when both roles are assigned to one agent. Next, show why the standard deflationary response—treating self-deception as merely biased evidence-gathering—fails to capture the distinctive first-person phenomenology of actively concealing something from oneself. Argue that belief, on this view, should be understood as a disposition to assent and act that is activated by contextual cues rather than stored globally in a unitary belief-box, which allows contradictory dispositions to belong to a single integrated system without simultaneous conscious activation. Address the objection that this framework merely rebrands cognitive compartmentalization, and explain precisely how the dialectical structure preserves the agent's experience of having concealed something from herself. Keep the analysis entirely self-contained: provide no bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and write in American English.
