I have been studying van Fraassen's Reflection principle, which I understand to require that if a rational agent expects her fully informed future self to hold credence r in a proposition, then she ought now to hold credence r in that proposition. I am presently serving on the design board of a sequential clinical trial that employs hidden interim analyses: an independent committee updates an internal Bayesian model as data accumulate, but board members are deliberately blinded to those updates until the study concludes. Once unblinded, my future self will possess the committee's final posterior regarding the treatment effect. It therefore seems that, by Reflection, I should already assign that exact posterior value now, despite being structurally barred from viewing the evidence. Yet adopting that belief now would seem to undermine the very blinding that legitimizes my presence on the board, whereas refusing to adopt it appears epistemically irrational. Does Reflection actually mandate this preemptive alignment with a future belief, or does it apply only when one learns, rather than merely anticipates, what one's future credence will be?
