A popular philosophy blog summarises the Rietdijk–Putnam argument like this: in special relativity, simultaneity is frame-relative, so two inertial observers in relative motion will disagree about which distant events are happening ‘now’. Because no inertial frame is physically privileged, both observers’ ‘now-slices’ must be equally real. Consequently, events that we regard as future — for example, a supernova in 2300 — are already present in some other frame, and therefore already exist. The blogger concludes that this proves determinism: if the future is already ‘there’ in another frame’s present, then nothing is genuinely open and the passage of time is illusory. He adds that Einstein endorsed exactly this reasoning, which explains Einstein’s remark that time is an illusion.

In particular, is it accurate to present the argument as establishing determinism rather than eternalism? Is the move from frame-relative simultaneity to the reality of future events correctly characterised, or does it smuggle in an extra premise about presentism? And is the attribution to Einstein fair, or does it conflate his personal philosophical remarks with the specific Rietdijk–Putnam line of reasoning?
