I am preparing a position statement for a graduate seminar on migration ethics and democratic theory, and I would like a rigorous consistency audit of my commitments before I present them. I take myself to hold the following five theses, which strike me as jointly attractive but which I fear may pull in incompatible directions:

(a) International freedom of movement is a basic moral liberty; state borders are ethically arbitrary, and entry restrictions stand in need of strong justification rather than being the default.

(b) The liberal welfare state is a bounded scheme of social cooperation that generates special obligations of reciprocity among its members; this grounds differentiated access to distributive benefits and collective decision-making, though not exclusion on racial or ethnic grounds.

(c) Integration ought to be understood not as assimilation or as parallel multicultural separation, but as joint authorship of democratic institutions: newcomers become co-authors of the rules that bind them without abandoning prior cultural attachments, and this co-authorial status ought not be deferred behind a probationary period.

(d) Democratic self-determination includes a legitimate right to control membership conditions, provided these are not racialised; a political community may set admission terms to preserve the institutional stability required for collective self-government.

(e) Global distributive justice requires substantial resource transfers from wealthy to poor countries, yet this obligation is best discharged through international taxation and aid rather than through open migration.

Please act as an expert in political philosophy with specialism in migration and democratic theory. Before delivering your verdict, take a moment to map the logical dependencies among these commitments, noting which ones share underlying motivations and which rest on independent premises. Then identify which pairs are in genuine tension—particularly those where the standard motivations for each view conflict in a non-obvious way—which pairs only appear incompatible but dissolve upon closer inspection, and for each genuine tension indicate the least costly revision and what precisely it sacrifices. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. I am deliberately not indicating which commitment I am prepared to abandon, so your audit should remain neutral throughout. Please write in British English, keep your answer within 400 words, and present it as a continuous audit with clearly marked sections for the preliminary mapping, the ranked tensions, and a brief concluding coherence assessment.
