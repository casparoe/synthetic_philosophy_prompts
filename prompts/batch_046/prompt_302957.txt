I'm prepping a grad seminar on democratic legitimacy and got into it with a colleauge over coffee about the boundary problem — who actually belongs to the demos? I think I've settled on a hybrid view: the all-affected-interests principle is roughly correct, but only if we filter "affected" through reciprocal justification rather than raw causal impact. So you need to be in a position where you could demand reasons for a collective decision and have those reasons genuinely count. That pulls in cross-border workers and some future-adjacent stakeholders, but pushes out non-human animals and very distant future people. It's not just stakeholder theory with extra steps; I'm trying to avoid the circularity of defining the demos by asking who gets a say, then saying they get a say because they're in the demos.
