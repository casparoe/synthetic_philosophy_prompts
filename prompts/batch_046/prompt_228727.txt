There's this thing in some deliberative democracy experiments where they randomly pick citizens but then screen out anyone with strong prior opinions on the issue — they want people who are genuinely open to reasoning together in the room. They pay them and give them expert briefings, but if you come in with your mind made up based on stuff from outside your life, you're basically filtered out. Meanwhile your normal town hall lets anyone show up and argue with whatever reasons they already have. What theory of internal vs external reasons does the screening practice presuppose? Does it assume reasons formed inside the deliberation count for more than ones you brought in from outside? Or can an externalist about reasons still defend the filter on practical grounds? Reconstruct the implicit view, check if its consistent with letting those same people vote using their outside reasons right after, and say what'd have to change if we treated external reasons as equally legitimate.
