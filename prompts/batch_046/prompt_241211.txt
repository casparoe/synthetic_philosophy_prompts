I have been trying to work out whether my existence is essentially tied to the particular sperm and egg that formed me, or whether I could have originated differently and still been the same person. I have been testing scenarios on my sister, who is not trained in philosophy but has firm intuitions about identity. Her verdicts so far are these: if the same sperm and egg had developed inside a surrogate mother, she says it would still be me; if a different sperm had fertilized the same egg on the same night, she says that is just my sibling, not me; if my embryo had split into identical twins, she says the two resulting people are distinct and neither is the other, though they share the same beginning; if doctors had corrected a single harmful gene in my embryo before implantation, she insists I would still be me, only healthier; if scientists had cloned my adult DNA into a fresh egg and raised the child, she says that child is not me, but rather like a twin born decades later; and if my brain had been gradually replaced with synthetic equivalents while my body and memories remained continuous, she says I would still be present, though she confesses she cannot say exactly where the line is drawn. I now want to ask her about a new case: scientists take cells from my adult body, induce them to become both a sperm and an egg in the laboratory, combine them, and implant the resulting embryo. The DNA sequence matches what my original conception produced, but neither gamete is the physical one that actually formed me, and the embryo develops with no biological continuity to my current body. Predict my sister's verdict on whether that child would be me. Identify which theory she is implicitly using — strict origin essentialism, genetic essentialism, psychological or biological continuity, or some hybrid — and point to the earlier case that serves as the closest analog. Finally, tell me what further scenario would actually distinguish the leading theories for her, given that her current answers seem to allow more than one interpretation.
