So there's this weird split in climate-policy funding that I've been stuck on. For weather forecasting, agencies pour money into physical-stance supercomputers — pure atmospheric physics, no intentions, no design talk. But for predicting human responses to climate change — migration patterns, economic shifts — the same agencies require intentional-stance models, attributing beliefs and preferences to populations. Meanwhile, for predicting ecosystem collapse, they mandate the design stance, treating species as functional components with evolutionary purposes. The funding rules explicitly say these three aren't interchangable: you can't swap physics for human behavior, or intentional models for ecosystems.

What view of real patterns does that practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit ontology — are physical, design, and intentional patterns genuinely different kinds of reality here, or just different tools? Could a unified physicalist still justify the split on practical grounds, or does the policy quietly rule that out? And tell me what'd have to change in the funding structure if we replaced the implicit view with a hard physicalist one where only physical-stance patterns are 'really real'.
