I'm kinda going through a weird career pivot right now — I left a decent job in data analysis to try writing, which is obviously risky and maybe dumb — so I've been obsessing over whether life has "meaning" in any real sense, not just the self-help version. Anyway, I was talking with my friend Mara who took that philosophy of life course last year, and she keeps applying what she calls a "Ramsey-flavored" framework to hypothetical lives. She doesn't cite texts, she just gives verdicts.

So far she's said: a mathematician spends forty years on one problem, dies without solving it, but her belief-system about why the problem matters stays perfectly coherent and updates rationally with new evidence — meaningful, yes. Someone lives purely for sensory pleasure, never forms sustained beliefs about there activities, just chases the next good feeling — meaningless, definitely. An artist creates a massive work that will outlast her, but she genuinely beleives it's garbage and has no understanding of why anyone values it; she feels nothing — meaningless. A person saves thousands of lives through pure accident, never understands what happened, feels no connection to it — also meaningless.

Next week she's going to judge this case I made up: there's a guy who spends his entire adult life counting grains of sand on a specific beach. He builds this elaborate rational system for tracking counts, updates his beliefs precisely when tides shift, understands fully that this has zero objective value, and integrates the project into his broader worldview with perfect coherence — he even writes little papers on sand-count methodology that other sand-counters read. He knows it's pointless in the grand scheme and doesn't pretend otherwise.

I want to know: what's Mara going to say? Is it meaningful or not? I think there are at least two rules that fit her track record — maybe one is about rational belief-integration being sufficient (very Ramsey-ish, since he cared so much about coherence of belief and probability updating), and another is that meaning needs some kind of objective contribution or at least recognition of value beyond the agent's own system. Which one is she actually running, what does each predict for the sand-counter, and which of those two should I bet on?

Also, if we still can't tell after her verdict, what's the one further case that would actually settle it between those rules? One more thing — what about a life where someone makes a massive objective contribution but their reasoning is totally incoherent, like they cure a disease but think they did it through astrology with beliefs that contradict each other constantly? I don't need Mara's verdict on that one, I just want to know if that'd be the decider. Sorry for the ramble, I know this is a bit in the weeds.
