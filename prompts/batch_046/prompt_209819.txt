I'm putting together a handout for a philosophy of mind seminar on mind uploading, but I want to avoid the same old Ship of Theseus recap everyone memorized. So here's the specifc case: you replace neurons one-by-one with silicon copies that match the I/O table perfectly, but there's a "stutter" stage where half are silicon running 2ms behind, corrected by a buffer so behavour is seamless — yet the causal dynamics aren't uniform across the brain. I want a decision tree that sorts actual positions on whether identity survives this. First node: is personal identity a real, non-derivative fact, or just practical/linguistic convenience? From there branch on whether substrate must stay biological, whether continuous real-time dynamics are required vs just the final informational pattern, and whether all parts need to share a single binding mechanism (say, a continuous field) for there to be one mind. Every node should be phrased as a claim someone can take without knowing the philosophy label. For each leaf, give the name if it has one (or "unnamed"), and one sentence describing its most awkard commitment. Also list the logical combinations that are empty — like a deflationist who still demands biological tissue — and say whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't separate. Keep it to about 6-8 leaves, and don't just list famous names — build the space first, then place the views in it.
