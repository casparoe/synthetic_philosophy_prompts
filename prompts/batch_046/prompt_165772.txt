(a) I take ISOTYPE not as a mere pedagogical supplement but as a serious proposal for a universal, culturally neutral syntax of scientific communication, using standardized icons in place of propositional sentences.

(b) I accept Neurath's argument that protocol sentences possess no special epistemic privilege; they are as revisable and theory-laden as any other statement.

(c) I read the "boat" metaphor—rebuilding the ship at sea, plank by plank—as applying directly to picture-languages: they are conventional tools repaired from within, never anchored to an unmediated observational bedrock.

(d) I therefore hold that picture-languages derive their cross-cultural utility precisely from their lack of metaphysical commitment: because they do not claim to mirror reality in a privileged vocabulary, they can be adopted without importing a specific ontology.

(e) At the same time, I think that for scientific communication to achieve genuine intersubjective agreement, there must be some shared constraint—if not observational bedrock, then at least a stable functional correspondence between icon and measured phenomenon—that prevents picture-languages from collapsing into purely conventional games.

Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging, and for each identify what single commitment would have to give.
