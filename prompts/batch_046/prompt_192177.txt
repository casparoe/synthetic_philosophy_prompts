I am trying to understand a passage from Tolstoy’s essay What Is Art? and its relation to his theory that art is the ‘infection’ (zarazhenie) of feeling from one person to others. The passage runs as follows:

‘I went one day, with a lady artist, to the Bodkin Art Gallery in Moscow. In one of the rooms, on a table, lay a book of coloured pictures, issued in Paris and supplied, I believe, to private subscribers only. The pictures were admirably executed, but represented scenes in the private cabinets of a restaurant. Sexual indulgence was the chief subject of each picture. Women extravagantly dressed and partly undressed, women exposing their legs and breasts to men in evening dress; men and women taking liberties with each other, or dancing the “can-can,” etc., etc. My companion the artist, a maiden lady of irreproachable conduct and reputation, began deliberately to look at these pictures. I could not let my attention dwell on them without ill effects. Such things had a certain attraction for me, and tended to make me restless and nervous. I ventured to suggest that the subject-matter of the pictures was objectionable. But my companion (who prided herself on being an artist) remarked with conscious superiority, that from an artist’s point of view the subject was of no consequence. The pictures being very well executed were artistic, and therefore worthy of attention and study. Morality had nothing to do with art.’

My difficulty is whether Tolstoy can consistently maintain that these pictures are counterfeit art because they appeal to sensuality and fail to unite people in brotherhood, without conceding that they have nevertheless successfully infected him with a feeling—namely, restlessness and nervous attraction. Specifically, can his framework distinguish between (a) a failure of infection, in which the artist uses technical skill to simulate a feeling she does not sincerely hold, and (b) a successful infection of a feeling that is morally corrupting? Or does his own reaction—being affected despite his moral disapproval—show that the infection mechanism operates independently of the moral criterion, so that the ‘brotherhood’ condition is an external normative add-on rather than an internal feature of genuine artistic transmission? Please address whether there is any coherent way for Tolstoy to claim that the artist here lacks sincere emotional investment in the sensual excitement depicted, given that she appears emotionally detached (‘conscious superiority’, ‘irreproachable conduct’) while he, the viewer, is genuinely affected.
