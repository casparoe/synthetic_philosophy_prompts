I sit on my university's postgraduate welfare and resource panel, which includes administrators, student representatives, and one clinician, but no philosophers. We must reach a decision by the 28th of this month on whether to approve a pilot programme offering voluntary, non-invasive neurostimulation and structured sleep-coaching to doctoral researchers who report difficulty sustaining the long hours of deep focus now expected by our funding bodies. None of these researchers carries a clinical diagnosis; the difficulty seems to arise partly because the institution's own workload norms have escalated sharply over the past decade. Half the panel regards the programme as legitimate support for an impairment that institutional pressure itself has created; the other half regards it as an unfair enhancement that rewards adaptation to unhealthy norms rather than challenging those norms. I have been wondering whether the usual line between therapy and enhancement still holds when an institution has quietly shifted what counts as "normal" functioning — something I have tentatively called baseline drift — but I do not wish to hide behind jargon I have not properly mastered. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action, approve, reject, or approve with conditions, together with a clear time frame for review, and it should engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the opposing side — especially the worry that funding this programme makes us complicit in normalising exploitative expectations rather than remedying them. I should be grateful if the response were written in British English.
