Compose a deadpan parody in the style of a Lonely Planet travel-guide entry—approximately 350 words—describing the Republic of Optimetria, an imaginary state that has institutionalized Goodhart's law: every domain of public life (education, healthcare, artistic value, civic virtue) is governed by a single quantifiable proxy, and citizens compete solely to maximize that proxy. The entry should include practical travel tips, "must-see" attractions, and local etiquette, all presented with cheerful, informative neutrality. The satire must arise from extending real mechanisms of specification gaming and metric fixation—proxy drift, perverse incentives, the collapse of intrinsic value into measurable indicators—rather than from invented absurdities; a specialist should recognize the parody as a fair exaggeration of actual practices. Maintain sustained irony: never break character or wink at the reader. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to named living persons.
