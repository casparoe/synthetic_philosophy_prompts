im in a seminar on secularism and pluralism and we keep stalling on french laïcité vs anglo-american multiculturalism. like, everyone agrees that the 2004 headscarf law hits muslim girls harder than anyone else, and everyone agrees the state isnt literally endorsing catholicism. but then one side says thats assimilation in disguise — forcing a specific secular culture — and the other side says its genuinely neutral because the rule applies to all religions equally. we go in circles for weeks.

i think whats actually happening is both sides smuggle in different hidden metrics. one side treats "neutrality" as procedural: the state just doesnt pick a religious winner. the other treats it as substantive: the state must not create conditions that systematically burden one group. and "integration" splits the same way — shared public identity vs accommodating difference so everyone participates equally. so is this definately a verbal fight over words? or is there a real substantive disagreement about what democracy owes citizens? my prof brought up rawlsian public reason but it feels like it just gives a fancy name to one sides metric without solving the clash.

i want a real diagnosis, not a survey of interpretations. are we answering different questions under one heading — one about state legitimacy, one about social outcomes? is there a hidden empirical claim about psychology or social cohesion doing all the work? or is this bedrock intuition about whether the state should preserve a "common world" or "manage diversity"? and given whatever the diagnosis is, what would actually count as progress — a conceptual distinction, an empirical result, or nothing at all?
