As a retired geophysicist who has recently turned to philosophy, I face a practical decision that feels conceptually puzzling. For four decades I kept handwritten field logs measuring ablation and temperature at a single alpine glacier; now an archive wishes to digitize them, but funding permits only a subset. They ask me to mark which years form the "essential" record. No individual year's absence destroys the long-term climatic trend, yet if enough are excluded the signal vanishes entirely. Every boundary I propose—say, excluding years with incomplete data—seems defensible in isolation but arbitrary in principle, since I could shift it by one year without obvious error. Is my conviction that there must be a correct cutoff philosophically mistaken, or can practical context genuinely fix a line where none seems naturally drawn? I seek not archival guidance but an analysis of whether this kind of threshold can be principled.
