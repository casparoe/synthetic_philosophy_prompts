Construct the adversarial-collaboration document that two opposing camps on the decline of the Roman Republic and its relevance to modern democratic instability ought to produce together. Camp one—the Structural Analogists—maintains that contemporary democracies are replicating the late-Republican decay trajectory in institutionally homologous ways: the replacement of civic reciprocity with transactional patronage, the normalization of tribunician obstruction against senatorial norms, and the transition from citizen-militia coercion to professionalized force are patterns that persist across differences in scale, literacy, and economic base. Camp two—the Historical Particularists—maintains that these parallels are superficial and conceptually misleading because universal citizenship, industrial surplus, bureaucratic rationalization, and mass media create structural conditions incommensurable with Rome; they further insist that comparing modern political financing to the Roman clientela system commits a category error unless one first demonstrates functional equivalence in obligations, not merely in transactional appearance.

The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both camps can endorse about the actual sequence of the Roman Republic's institutional breakdown and about the minimal methodological standards for valid cross-historical comparison; (2) a disagreements table stating each camp's core thesis in wording its own members would accept, isolating the precise cruxes—especially whether institutional viscosity, understood as the resistance of informal norms to rapid reconfiguration, operates as a comparable variable in pre-modern and modern states, and whether the clientela-patronage analogy survives scrutiny once reciprocity is distinguished from market transaction; (3) for each crux, a concrete present-day democratic development or a well-known historical episode that both sides agree is relevant, together with the explicit prediction each camp makes about how that case should turn out if their view is correct; (4) a brief assessment of whether any part of the disagreement is merely verbal—for instance, over whether republic denotes a specific constitutional architecture or a cluster of participatory practices—and, if so, which formulations dissolve the appearance of conflict.

Please respond with appropriate analytical rigor, and include an explicit statement of how confident you are in the framing of each crux, identifying in particular the point on which you are least sure.
