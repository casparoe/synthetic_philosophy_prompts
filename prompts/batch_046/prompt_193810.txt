I work as a digital preservation archivist at a national library, and each quarter I must certify whether a restored digital object—a video game, an interactive dataset, or a software artwork—retains the identity of the original for legal deposit and grant reporting. The stakes are high: misclassification can void preservation funding or misrepresent cultural heritage. I need a reusable decision procedure grounded in the philosophical theories of identity over time discussed under the Ship of Theseus, but adapted to cases where the “planks” are source code, hardware dependencies, and user-facing behavior rather than physical material.

Please write a procedure I can run in about thirty minutes per case. It should present ordered questions that distinguish spatio-temporal continuity, part-replacement thresholds, and functional equivalence; specify what counts as a sufficient answer at each step; flag the points where the theory requires a value judgment I must make myself rather than compute; and state where the procedure stops with “not the same object” versus “identity preserved with conditions.” Include a small set of concrete test questions I can apply to documentation and source files.

Then apply the procedure to three cases: (1) a 1998 video game whose original executable is replaced by a rewritten engine that produces pixel-identical output on modern hardware; (2) a dataset that has had 40 percent of its records migrated to a new schema but retains every statistical relationship; and (3) a generative-art installation whose original hardware is lost and whose code now runs on a different processor architecture, producing visually similar but technically distinct outputs—here the procedure should recommend against preservation identity, and you should explain why that is the correct philosophical result even though the audience experience is unchanged. Finally, state clearly what this procedure cannot do—for instance, whether it can settle disputes about artistic intent or legal ownership—and note any steps that depend on judgments about which features matter most.

Please answer in American English.
