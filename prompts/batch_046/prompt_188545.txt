hey — prepping a lecture on Zhuangzi and I talked myself into an argument that feels like it proves way too much. tell me where the weels come off.

(1) in the butterfly passage Zhuangzi says he doesn't know if he's Zhuangzi who dreamt he was a butterfly or vice versa. this isn't just dream-skepticism; it's a denial that there's a stable substrate behind appearances.

(2) in the second chaper he describes death as just another transformation (huà) — sleeping in a different form, like wood or earth.

(3) if there's no stable substrate behind waking/dreaming, and death is just another change of form, then there's no stable substrate behind living/dying either.

(4) so the "person" who dies isn't annihilated but dispersed into what follows — grass, trees, the air you breathe.

(5) now, harming someone is wrong partly because it violates interests tied to being a unified subject. once that unity dissolves into grass there's no subject left to violate. that's why Zhuangzi can drum and sing at his wife's funeral.

(6) but if the dead are dispersed into current living things, then harming a tree harms a dispersed fragment of my dead friend. not symbolically — literally, since its matter/qi was once part of her transformed body.

(7) so if I have a duty not to harm my dead friend, I have a duty not to harm the tree containing her transformed qi. extend this: every living thing holds fragments of everyone who ever died.

(8) therefore I'm either obligated to protect every blade of grass (since it might contain people I care about) or I'm not obligated to protect anyone, since everyone is already dispersed everywhere and harm is just another transformation.

(9) but that collapses the distinction between murder and gardening, and I can't accept (8).

where do I jump ship? I'd really like to keep (1) and (3) — the idea that Zhuangzi is genuinely attacking substrate identity — but I'm open on (6). what does rejecting (6) cost? does it force me back into a substance dualism I don't want, or can I block it by saying qi isn't the right bearer of personal continuity? also, am I smuggling in a modern notion of autonamy that Zhuangzi wouldn't recognize?
