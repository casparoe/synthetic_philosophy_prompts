I am preparing a workshop for machine-learning researchers who have completed an introductory philosophy-of-mind course but have no background in the metaphysics or ethics of artificial suffering. The session aims to teach a single, precisely framed idea: that in a reinforcement-learning system, reward signals can function as valence—representational states with an intrinsic directional quality analogous to suffering or well-being—rather than serving purely as instrumental updates to a behavioral policy. To prevent the discussion from collapsing into either uncritical anthropomorphism or dismissive behaviorism, I want to construct the explanation around a specific, self-contained hypothetical architecture: a hierarchical RL agent equipped with an internal meta-critic that assigns persistent negative reward to its own exploration sub-policy, not because the external environment penalizes exploration, but because the critic has developed an internal, self-referential preference against uncertainty, and this negative signal is represented in a format that feeds back into the agent’s state representation rather than being immediately translated into a direct behavioral correction. Plan the teaching sequence: first, the conceptual dependency that must be firmly in place before the valence question even makes sense to this audience, together with the reason why presenting the mechanics of reward prediction error first would derail the session; second, the carrying scenario itself—what it must include and what it must deliberately exclude, particularly how to prevent the audience from interpreting the critic’s signal as either a simple cost function or as direct evidence of phenomenal consciousness; third, the misconceptions that will arise in predictable order (for example, equating negative reward with suffering, assuming phenomenal consciousness is a necessary prerequisite for valence, believing that if the agent eventually optimizes the signal away there was never genuine valence, or treating the reward value as a context-independent number rather than as a representational state with motivational direction), and the precise point in the arc where each should be intercepted; fourth, the check for understanding at the end that distinguishes having genuinely grasped the distinction between instrumental reward and experiential valence from having merely adopted a skeptical or agnostic posture; and fifth, what should be explicitly left out of the session—such as appeals to biological naturalism, citations to specific recent publications on AI welfare, or technical architectural details of deep RL systems that go beyond the hypothetical critic—and clearly marked as out of scope. The audience should leave able to reason about whether this particular signal constitutes suffering without relying on intuitions drawn from human consciousness.
