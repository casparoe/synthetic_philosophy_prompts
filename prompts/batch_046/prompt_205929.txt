I was argueing with my roomate last night about Singapore — like, is the whole social contract just "we give you GDP and you don't ask for democracy"? We got onto performance legitimacy, that idea that authoritarian rule can be justified by delivering prosperity and order instead of democratic consent. He thinks it's basically the whole story for Lee Kuan Yew and the PAP (People's Action Party), and I think it's more complicated, especially when you look at crises where the state claims to have saved you from something that might not have hit you that hard anyway.

There's this concept I half-came up with (or maybe read somwhere, I'm not sure) — call it "preemptive legitimacy." It's when a regime doesn't just deliver growth, but actively manufactures the narrative that it averted disaster through superior foresight, even if the crisis was partly of its own making or wouldn't have been catastrophic without them. I want you to apply both standard performance legitimacy and this "preemptive" twist to Singapore's handling of the 1997 Asian Financial Crisis — the currency adjustments, the refusal to take IMF-style conditional loans, the use of reserves to backstop government-linked company boards, the whole Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) playbook.

Can you map the episode carefully: who were the actual actors (PAP leadership, MAS, the boards of those state-linked firms), what options they actually had, what citizens gained versus what elites protected, and then tell me where performance legitimacy explains the political stability afterward, where "preemptive legitimacy" explains something the standard theory misses (like the manufacturing of crisis narratives), and where both theories break down — especially since Singapore never actually faced a democratic accountability moment that would test whether people really "bought" the deal. I don't want citations or a reading list; just the conceptual mapping and an honest account of what the episode shows about whether prosperity can actually justify authoritarian rule, or whether it just buys temporary tolerance.
