I am drafting an application for a six-month philosophy-of-mind fellowship, and I want to pitch a project that works in the narrow space between classical philosophical treatments of weakness of will and the behavioral-economics literature on hyperbolic discounting. The idea I have is somewhat unconventional: rather than treating akrasia as a failure within a unified agent—someone who judges at time t that course X is best but executes course Y at the same t—I want to explore whether hyperbolic discounting reveals a deeper structural problem, namely that the agent evaluating outcomes at t and the agent evaluating them at t+δ may be normatively discontinuous. In other words, does hyperbolic discounting dissolve the classical puzzle of akrasia by showing that there is no single will being overridden, or does it multiply the puzzle by leaving us with several temporal slices of one person and no rational criterion for which slice should exercise diachronic authority?

The proposal should state this question with precision, identify why existing answers fail to settle it—particularly the Davidson-style causal-deviation framework, which assumes temporal unity, and the standard economic framing of time-inconsistency, which treats preference reversal as a computational error rather than a problem of personal identity over time—and then propose a philosophical method for advancing the debate without running new experiments. Specifically, I want the method to analyze what conditions would have to hold for one temporal perspective to be rationally authoritative over another, rather than merely causally dominant, and to ask whether addiction or compulsive behavior should be understood as weakness of will at all if the underlying mechanism is hyperbolic discounting. The deliverable should be useful not only to philosophers but to people in clinical decision-science who design interventions for intertemporal choice, and it should specify what would count as failure—for instance, if the analysis collapses into a purely descriptive account of preference change without normative bite. The scope must be realistic for six months of conceptual work. Please do not include a bibliography or citation list; I will compile references separately. Write the proposal as a single cohesive document suitable for a foundation review panel.
