Design a 75-minute classroom activity for a philosophy reading group of about eight adults, specifying the setting, group size, time available, and what participants should come away seeing: namely, an embodied grasp of why emotional response to fiction feels rational even when one knows the fiction is false, together with a felt sense of where that rationality becomes unstable when fictionality is not merely external but internal to the narrative. To avoid overused classroom examples and to inject a niche framing, construct the exercise around a short first-person narrative describing intense grief over a death, but build into the text a hidden internal contradiction—by the story's own chronology and rules, the death never occurs even within the fiction—so that emotional uptake and ontological commitment come apart inside the fiction itself, not only between fiction and reality. Divide the group into respondents and auditors. Respondents read the narrative cold, then record real-time emotional ratings on a 0–10 scale and write brief justifications before they know about the contradiction. Auditors possess the contradiction in advance, observe silently, then reveal it and challenge whether the emotional ratings should be withdrawn or retained; respondents must defend their stance without appealing solely to prior ignorance. Materials are limited to paper, pens, and a timer; if time permits, rotate roles using a second, structurally similar text. The deliverable must include explicit rules, a timeline, the rotation protocol, and a debrief that maps each stage onto components of the paradox of fiction—affective response to known non-actuality, the role of internal coherence, and whether fictionality operates as an external or internal defeater. In the debrief, identify one standard account (e.g., a pretense-based or make-believe approach) that the activity approximates and one feature of that account the activity cannot reproduce. Specify the predictable failure mode: participants may treat the contradiction as a mere plot twist rather than an ontological defeater, and provide a brief facilitator intervention for that case. Conclude with an honest note on which emotional feature of the paradox—such as sustained imaginative engagement despite full belief in falsehood—remains outside the activity's reach and must be supplied by the facilitator.
