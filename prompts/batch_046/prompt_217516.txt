Write a Petrarchan sonnet about logical omniscience and its dissolution through logical induction. In the octave, with enclosed rhyme ABBA ABBA, present the classical demand that a fully rational agent assign immediate, definite credence to every logical consequence of her beliefs — the pressure of omniscience that makes ignorance of an unproven theorem appear as irrationality rather than limitation. At the volta in line 9, turn to the logical-induction alternative: beliefs about logical statements begin at a prior and update gradually, as if traded in a market, converging toward 0 or 1 only as proofs become available, with coherence enforced not by instantaneous closure but by an inductive criterion that rules out Dutch-book vulnerability over time. Let the sestet’s more open rhyme (CDECDE) feel linear and unfinished compared with the octave’s enclosed loops, so the form itself enacts the shift from a closed logical space to a temporal, convergent process. The closing couplet should not celebrate certainty but should state the residual uncertainty that remains at any finite stage — the price of giving up omniscience. Keep the concepts accurate enough that someone familiar with formal epistemology or logical uncertainty recognizes the mechanics of prior-to-posterior convergence and market coherence, but do not rely on named authors, notation, or citation; let the logic live in imagery of markets, belief over time, and the slow approach of proof.
