I'm working through the ethical implications of panpsychist views in fundamental physics with a study group, and we've become stuck on a preliminary question: before we can evaluate any particular moral framework, what should we demand of it? Specifically, suppose one takes seriously not merely that consciousness is widespread, but that there exists a minimal form of affective valence—let's call it diffuse field-valence—associated with the intrinsic dynamics of fundamental physical fields themselves, distinct from the integrated sentience we associate with biological nervous systems. What desiderata must an adequate ethical theory meet to account for both possibilities without either dismissing field-level affect as physically meaningless or granting every quantum interaction full moral patient status?

Write the specification: what any ethical theory that takes pan-sentience seriously must be able to deliver. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or physical consideration behind it—such as the need for a non-arbitrary physical criterion distinguishing integrated suffering from diffuse valence, consistency with the fact that quantum field dynamics are ubiquitous and largely unintegrated, and compatibility with our existing moral obligations to clearly sentient organisms. Mark which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable (for instance, avoiding an ethical calculus that requires measuring valence across every spacetime point) and which are preferences of particular camps (such as demanding that only information-integrated systems can suffer). Then, identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable—particularly if the requirement that suffering be physically localized conflicts with the ubiquity of field dynamics, or if the demand for empirical tractability conflicts with the theoretical possibility of non-localized affect. Keep the weight on the criteria and their tensions rather than on scoring existing theories, though you may briefly indicate how standard sentientist, ecological, and physicalist-eliminativist approaches fare against the list. Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or suggested readings.
