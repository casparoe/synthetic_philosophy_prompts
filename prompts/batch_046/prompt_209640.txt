ok so me and my friend just had a long argument about keynes and the good life after i skimmed that old essay where he predicts 15 hour weeks. i tried to formalize my side and now the argument feels like it proves way too much but i cant find the exit.

(1) keynes says the good life starts once the economic problem is solved—absolute needs are satisfied—freeing people for leisure, art, affection, and knowledge.

(2) he splits needs into absolute ones (independent of others, like food or shelter) and relative ones (status and comparison, which are insatiable).

(3) if the good life requires abandoning relative needs, then anyone who works long hours to climb status ladders is either irrational or suffering false consciousness about whats valuable.

(4) but if people are irrational or deluded in this way, then we are justified in coercively restructuring work—heavy taxes on overtime, hard caps on hours—to push them toward leisure.

(5) that applies to nearly every ambitious group: surgeons who live for the OR, entrepreneurs, activists burning out for a cause, even artists chasing fame.

(6) so keynes view implies we should force almost all high-achievers into a 15-hour week, which is obviously wrong.

(7) therefore one of the premises has to go.

my friend thinks the leak is in (1) because absolute needs become positional once society is rich—education, healthcare, even wifi are all about comparison now—so the economic problem is never solved. i want to keep (1) if possible. is the real problem (3), where i slide from not part of the good life to irrational to pursue? or is there a hidden quantifier shift between (2) and (4)? can you name the step, explain why it looked okay, and price what giving up each option costs?
