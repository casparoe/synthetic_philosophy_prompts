You're a senior risk analyst for a municipal drone-insurance pool, and every week you sign off on whether a fresh incident should bump a specific model's risk class in a specific neighborhood. The reference class problem is your daily headache: the same crash can sit in "all delivery drones," "Model X," "night flights," or "flights over schools," each with different base rates. I need a reusable decision procedure — steps in order, about 20 minutes to run — that says which reference classes are admissable, how to resolve conflicts, where you have to stop and admit it's a judgment call, and where you escalate instead of deciding. Include a small set of tests to reject gerrymandered post-hoc classes. Apply it to two incidents: first, a battery fire in January where "Model X winter flights" (n=3) conflicts with "all winter drone flights" (n=400) and the broader class gives a risk rating the board won't like; second, a Tuesday crash near Riverside Park that someone wants to class as "Tuesday park-adjacent flights." Think carefully, brainstorm failure modes first, then write the checklist. Keep it practical — you'll defend it to lawyers and engineers, not philosophers. Don't give me bibliographies; just make it work.
