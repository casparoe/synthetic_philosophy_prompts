Plan a 90-minute graduate seminar explanation for philosophy and economics students who have read Marx but have no formal price theory or Austrian economics background, on the socialist calculation debate—specifically the arc from Mises’s argument that rational economic calculation requires private capital markets, through Hayek’s refinement about dispersed tacit knowledge, to Lange’s market-socialist reply using trial-and-error pricing and a Walrasian auctioneer analogy. Do not organize the explanation chronologically; instead, lay out the dependency order in which the pieces must be introduced so that Lange’s reply registers as a genuine structural answer rather than a strawman, and so that the Mises-Hayek rejoinder registers as a deeper epistemic problem rather than ideological retreat. Structure the conceptual architecture around three synthetic failure-modes—computational intractability, epistemic dispersion, and dynamic adaptation—mapping Mises, Hayek, and Lange onto them respectively, and explain why this framing must come only after the students have grasped what a production price actually coordinates.

Use one carrying example throughout: an integrated supply chain linking bauxite refining, aluminum smelting, and aircraft-component manufacturing, confronted with a sudden, unanticipated spike in regional electricity costs. The example must illustrate what each regime—pure market pricing, a Lange-style central board running trial-and-error adjustments, and a Misesian entrepreneurial discovery process—can and cannot compute about the new optimal scale and technology mix, and it must work without invoking any specific historical planning boards or Yugoslav worker-management institutions.

Then map the misconceptions this audience predictably carries in: that the debate is primarily about worker incentives rather than knowledge structures; that Lange’s auctioneer model solves the problem Mises originally posed; that Hayek’s contribution is merely an anti-planning prejudice; and that market prices are mainly distributive signals rather than coordination mechanisms for dispersed information. For each misconception, specify the exact point in the dependency sequence where it should be defused and the one-line distinction that neutralizes it.
