Consider the conceptual framework in which a substrate called careenium supports discrete symbolic entities called simmballs—representational tokens that can combine, transform, and feed back into the substrate's dynamics. The central dispute is whether simmballs genuinely push the careenium around (downward causation from symbol to substrate) or whether all causal efficacy resides in the careenium's base-level dynamics, with simmballs serving only as epiphenomenal bookkeeping devices. Several open lines have emerged within this framework: whether feedback loops between simmball configurations and careenium dynamics require a non-physical coupling principle; whether ontology identification for careenium-level entities must treat particular simmball-arrays as genuine causal nodes rather than descriptive abstractions; whether the syntactic-semantic distinction for simmballs collapses under close examination of substrate-level effects; and what practical design implications follow for artificial systems intended to use high-level symbolic reasoning to govern physical processes.
