I'm moderating a panel interveiw with a decision theorist who's been pushing what she calls "weak acausal trade" — basically the idea that you can cooperate with a causally disconnected agent, but only if you both share an explicit, verifiable prior over each other's decision algorithms. She says this avoids the usual "why care about a clone in another universe" problem without collapsing into standard bargaining.

Can you draft six or seven hard questions? I want them to actually turn on her view, not just let her restate it. Specifically:
- one that forces her to draw the line between her weak version and regular causal bargaining, especially if the shared prior is only approximate; include a follw-up that anticipates her saying approximation is good enough and asks whether that doesn't smuggle causality back in;
- one on the mirror-defection correlation — if I run EDT and the other runs CDT, does her shared-prior condition still hold, or does the correlation break? follow-up should press what she says if the algorithms are only partly observable;
- one comparing her view to Kantian universalization, since both seem to say "act as if your maxim applies everywhere"; the follow-up needs to ask whether her prior-sharing condition actually does any work that universalization doesn't, or if it's just universalization with extra math;
- and one niche twist: ask how her framework handles a case where the "partner" is a future version of yourself running a modified utility function — not just different discounting, but a genuinely different axiological frame — and whether weak acausal trade can stabilize cooperation there without any causal feedback. I want the follow-up to push on whether this isn't just standard commitment-device reasoning in disguise.

Keep it colloquium-style, no generic "what inspired you" stuff.
