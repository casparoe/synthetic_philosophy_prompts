I'm moderating a podcast interveiw with a philospher who just published a monograph defending what she calles "discontinuous prioritarianism." The idea is that prioritarian moral weights don't scale smoothly with well-being — they're flat inside certain bands and jump only at specific thresholds — and she uses this architecture to block the leveling-down objection. She claims that if you make a well-off person slightly worse off without pushing anyone across a boundary into a higher band, there's literally zero prioritarian reason to do it, so her view doesn't prefer equality for its own sake; it only cares about boundary-crossings that genuinely help the worst off. I want a sequence of interview questions that makes that deflection harder and harder to pull off. Start by pressing whether these bands are tracking real discontinuities in welfare or just drawing lines in the sand for convenience. Then ask whether the view collapses into sufficientarianism with extra machinery, anticipating that she'll reply the bands map onto something like Sen-style capability thresholds rather than arbitrary welfare units. Follow that up by asking what kind of empirical or conceptual argument could show those capability boundaries are illusory or culturally relative — not just "what's your favorite threshold?" but specifically what would count as evidence against the discontinuity claim. End with an intergenerational twist: if future generations start out in different bands by default, could we justify leveling down present wealthy people solely to shift some future people across a threshold, and doesn't that revive the leveling-down objection in a form her band-structure can't block? Keep it on the mechanics, avoid generic biography questions, and don't ask for citations or reading lists — I just need the questions.
