I'm currently designing a module for a philosophy-of-mind graduate seminar that focuses specifically on belief-attribution to large language models outside of training-distribution retrieval. During our last meeting, a student advanced a position she labeled calibrated instrumentalism: rather than treating belief-attribution as a global ontological stance or a mere heuristic, we should treat statements like the model believes that P as locally warranted predictive instruments whose legitimacy varies by task domain—logical reasoning versus narrative generation, for instance—and should be calibrated against counterfactual stability under prompt perturbation. I find this intriguing but worry it collapses into a more familiar view under pressure.

With that framing in mind, please rank the following five approaches to attributing propositional attitudes to AI systems according to their explanatory value for accounting for failure modes in out-of-distribution reasoning tasks, not memorized pattern completion: (1) Dennett's intentional stance applied globally to the system; (2) a Davidsonian radical-interpretation approach that reads beliefs off token-output regularities without assuming shared rationality; (3) eliminativism about mental-state attribution to artificial systems, along the lines of the stochastic-parrot family of critiques; (4) a functionalist role-realism that identifies specific beliefs with stable activation patterns across transformer layers; and (5) the calibrated instrumentalism sketched above.

Assign each approach a tier from S through D, and in one to three sentences per tier justify the placement specifically with respect to explaining why a model might fail on a counterfactual reasoning task despite succeeding on structurally similar in-distribution examples. Your response should also state clearly what empirical finding or conceptual argument would change your mind—particularly, what result in mechanistic interpretability or generalization testing would cause you to reorder calibrated instrumentalism relative to functionalist role-realism. Finally, flag the step in your reasoning that is most likely to fail, for example if the distinction between task-domain calibration and global stance turns out to be unstable.
