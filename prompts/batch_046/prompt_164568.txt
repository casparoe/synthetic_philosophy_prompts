Hey — science journalist here, mostly writing about philosophy of mind and the occasional weird physics paper. I keep noticing a pattern in panpsychism debates and I'm not sure if it's a recognized sub-problem or just me misreading the combination problem.

The pattern goes like this. Someone argues consciousness is fundamental all the way down, then they need to explain why I'm not aware of being a swarm of micro-minds. So they propose some kind of relational glue — information integration, spatial adjacency, maybe a phenomenal bond — that binds tiny subjects into bigger ones. But that same glue is also invoked to explain why my left neuron doesn't merge with your consciousness: it's not strong enough across brains, or across space. So the glue is doing double duty, acting as both the bridge that unifies and the wall that separates. The cutoff feels post-hoc. And once you spell out the glue precisely, it starts sounding like it's carrying all the explanatory weight — consciousness just tags along. I've seen this move in a coupple recent papers, and it strikes me as different from the classic combination problem, which asks whether micro-experiences can combine at all. Here the worry is that the binding mechanism is redundant: it explains the macro structure, and consciousness adds nothing.

Is there an established name for this specific tension — where the binding factor is both barrier and bridge, and risks making consciousness explanatorily redundant? How does it relate to the palette problem or to structuralist objections? If there's no term, what's the nearest concept, and what distinguishes this version? Answer in three paragraphs max, don't bother with citations.
