(1) In Parfit's teletransporter, if a perfect psychological replica is built on Mars with no bodily continuity, I survive; bodily continuity is not necessary for survival.
(2) If the scanner malfunctions and produces two equally perfect replicas, neither is strictly identical to me, yet the outcome is not as bad as death, since each bears Relation R to my original self.
(3) Survival is not all-or-nothing but comes in degrees, determined by the strength and directness of psychological connections rather than by a binary identity relation.
(4) I reject closest-continuer theories: whether I survive should depend only on intrinsic relational properties, not on comparative facts like whether a closer psychological continuer exists elsewhere.
(5) If my neurons are gradually replaced one by one with functionally identical synthetic equivalents over a decade, I survive continuously; this is metaphysically on a par with the teletransporter, differing only in causal pathway topology.
(6) What fundamentally matters is not personal identity itself, but the preservation of psychological relations, regardless of whether they are one-one.

Do these form a consistent package? Order the tensions by severity, distinguishing genuine conflicts from ones that dissolve on inspection, and specify for each what would have to give. Be precise about which commitment pulls against which, and avoid a general lecture on Parfit.
