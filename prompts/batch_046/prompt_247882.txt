I'm preparing remarks for a bioethics reading group that is examining personal identity in the context of emerging reproductive technologies. I want to know whether there is a principled reason—beyond intuition—to think that being formed from this specific sperm and egg is essential to being this particular child, or whether that belief collapses once we realize that any sibling-like embryo would have been raised in the same family with the same name. Could you clarify the philosophical structure of my discomfort, apply the relevant reasoning about whether biological origin is essential to identity, and assess whether my colleague's analogy to picking an outfit holds?
