hey, i run a small rationalist-adjacent meetup — about 9 people, thursday nights, 90 min max. we’ve run the usual prisoner's dilemma and commons games but they always feel too tidy, like everyone can see the payoff matrix. i want a simulation that actually makes people feel the stickiness of the tragedy of the commons when there’s hidden heterogenity in costs and a nested second-order free-rider problem around monitoring.

design a runnable activity with real (small) stakes — say, candy or points that decide who picks the next discussion topic. build a shared pool with non-linear regeneration: it looks stable, then suddenly collapses past a hidden threshold that players don’t know exactly. give each player private cards showing their actual extraction cost (some are cheap, some expensive) and a hidden type. include an optional monitoring/punishment mechanism that costs resources from a separate pool and can itself be free-ridden on. the rules and incentives should push the group into failure before anyone names the concept; only in debrief do we map moments onto classic commons dynamics and ostrom-style design principles.

specify roles or hidden cards, timeline, payoff structure, and how to run it with 9 people in 90 minutes including debrief. include an honest note on which feature of real-world commons — ecological time-scales, legal enforcement, or something else — the game can’t reproduce and i’ll have to supply verbally. no lecture up front, don’t call it “the tragedy of the commons” until after. and please, no bibliographies or reading lists — half the group knows this stuff casually and the other half doesn’t, so it needs to stand alone. maybe throw in one or two predictable failure modes and how to handle them when they happen.
