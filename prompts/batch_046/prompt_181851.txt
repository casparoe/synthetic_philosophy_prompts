I am giving the toast at my doctoral advisor's retirement dinner. For thirty years she has worked on virtue ethics through the lens of the situationist critique in moral psychology: she accepts the empirical finding that behavior varies sharply across contexts, and she has reconstructed virtue not as a stable global trait but as a practical skill in designing and navigating one's own situations — what she sometimes calls "environmental engineering of the self." Roughly half the room are philosophers and psychologists who know this literature and will wince if I fall into standard trait-language; the other half are family, staff, and former students who will not. Write a five-minute toast that honors her without attributing to her any unchanging inner character — no "innate wisdom," no "unchanging kindness" — and that instead makes her philosophical commitment structurally determine the praise: talk about the mentoring structures, feedback systems, and institutional micro-climates she built, so that the virtue being celebrated is located in the environment she shaped rather than in her soul. The expert half should hear that I understand exactly why global-trait praise is both empirically wrong and conceptually incompatible with her view; the family half should feel warmth and gratitude, not a lecture. Please do not include citations, a bibliography, or suggestions for further reading; I am writing this to deliver.
