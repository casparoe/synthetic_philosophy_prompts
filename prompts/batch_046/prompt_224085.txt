Hey, can you argue that functionalism actually gets undermined—not supported—when you take multiple realizability seriously inside one person's life? I'm thinking of cases where brain injury or neural plasticity means the same mental state, like a specific belief or a pain-state, ends up running on totally different circuits with different connections, different failure modes, and different points where you could intervene. The usual move is to say this just proves mental states are abstract functional patterns that can sit on any hardware. I want the opposite: if the causal profile shifts with the substrate, there's no stable "functional role" left to identify the mental state with, so functionalism collapses into something weaker. Maybe mental kinds are better anchored by diachronic narrative coherence, or by what we could call interventionist stability across substrate drift, rather than by a fixed causal box. Build the argument from the plasticity case itself, dont just rehash Putnam or Fodor, and address the obviuos reply that functionalists can just redefine roles more abstractly. Keep it conceptual—no citations or reading lists, just the reasoning.
