I'm serving on the appeals board for our university's Commitee on Epistemic Conduct. We've got a case that basically turns on the dogmatism paradox, though nobody on the board calls it that. Dr. L. Park published a climate-model in 2022 with a specific parameter range. Last year new satellite data came in that flatly contradicted the range. The committee sactioned her for refusing to issue a correction, saying she violated our norm on responsive belief-revision. She appealed. Her defense is weirdly specific: she says her original justification wasn't just the first-order data but included what she calls a meta-stability clause — she always acknowledged the instruments were fallible, so the contradiction only defeats her belief if the second-order reliability of the instruments has also changed, and she claims that hasn't happened. Half the board thinks this is just rationalization; they say if you allow this move, anyone can immunize any belief by stacking meta-layers. The other half thinks she's actually tracking the paradox correctly: rational revision requires checking not just that p is contradicted, but whether the defeat reaches the justification-structure itself. We need a memo — uphold the sanction, overturn, or overturn with conditions — with clear reasoning and an honest treatment of the strongest point for the losing side. Write it like a real committee document, maybe 500-700 words. No bibiography needed, just work through the logic.
