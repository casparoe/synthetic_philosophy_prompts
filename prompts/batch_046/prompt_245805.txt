I want the full desiderata for a normative theory of paid parental leave and childcare provision in societies where dual-earner households are the norm. Before I evaluate particular proposals, I need the specification of what such a theory must actually deliver: the phenomena it has to accommodate, the distinctions it cannot collapse, the empirical regularities it must respect, and the normative commitments that are genuinely non-negotiable versus those that are merely preferences of one ideological camp.

Please provide the requirements list, with for each item:
- the concrete case or consideration behind it,
- whether it functions as a hard constraint or a negotiable preference,
- and, where relevant, which familiar policy model it tends to rule out.

In particular, the list must address:
- the distinction between formal equality of leave entitlement and substantive equality of uptake and long-run career impact;
- the tension between gender-neutral policy design and the empirical reality of asymmetric uptake between parents;
- how childcare ought to be treated as a public good without erasing parental autonomy over the form and timing of care;
- the relation between career-continuity metrics and broader measures of well-being or relational equality within the household;
- consistency with the economic reality of human-capital depreciation during extended labour-market absences, without reducing the value of parenting to productivity;
- intergenerational effects on child development and on the stability of the dual-earner arrangement itself;
- and the proper division of responsibility and cost between state, employer, and household.

Then identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable—especially any conflict between maximising gender equality in labour-market outcomes and respecting diverse family preferences for the division of care. Finally, state which single requirement, if enforced strictly, eliminates the largest number of existing proposals, and why.

Please answer in British English.
