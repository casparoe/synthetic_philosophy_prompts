I've been talking with my friend about what she calls being "sphexish"—mechanically rigid like the wasp that checks its burrow in a fixed loop, unable to adapt when the cricket is moved. She judges that the wasp itself is fully sphexish; that a person with OCD who washes her hands in a fixed ritual but experiences the urge as alien is sphexish in the act though not in her whole being; that a chatbot giving the same reply to every question is fully sphexish; and that a monk who meditates at the same hour daily but willingly skips it to help a sick visitor is not sphexish at all.

Now I want to apply her view consistently. Consider a writer who produces exactly five hundred words every morning before eating, even when she is ill; she says she chose this rule deliberately and loves it, and that if she genuinely wished to skip it she could, though she never does. Would my friend call this sphexish? I see at least two rules in her judgments: one about whether behavior changes when relevant circumstances change, another about whether the agent can consciously interrupt the loop. What does each rule predict here? Which earlier case is the closest analog? If her track record genuinely leaves the answer open, what principle would I be adopting with each choice, and what should my best guess be with the reasoning behind it? Finally, what single further case—perhaps contrasting an automatic skill with a compulsive loop—would show which rule she actually follows?
