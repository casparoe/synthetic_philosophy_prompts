I'm running a small dataset labeling project with a couple friends from an old rationalist discord — we're tagging sythetic images for a study on perceptual anomolies. We started with three bins: 'uncanny valley', 'stylistic artifact', and 'clean render'. The problem is the boundaries won't stay put. Last week someone tagged an image with perfect skin texture but a spiral-arranged six-finger hand as 'stylistic artifact' because they said the error was 'localized and decorative', not 'holistically disturbing'. This week the same image got 'uncanny valley' because another rater said any anatomical violation automatically triggers the valley response. There's also this weird micro-pattern where if the background has high-frequency noise, people call the face 'uncanny' even when it's identical to one they labeled 'clean' in a smooth background. I tried writing stricter rules — like 'count fingers first, then evaluate mood' — but the rules just spawn new edge cases (what if the extra digit hides behind a coffee cup?). I feel like there's something philosophical going on here about how concepts behave in tiny domains, not just sloppy labeling. Should I be trying to lock down the taxonomy with decison trees, or is the slippage telling me something about how these categories actually work? And if it's the latter, how do I design the protocol without pretending the borders are sharper than they are?
