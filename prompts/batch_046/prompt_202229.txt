Consider the intersection of indirect normativity—where the justification of a norm depends on its place within an optimal or collectively justified system rather than on direct case-by-case evaluation—and idealized deliberation models, in which normative authority derives from what rational agents would endorse under specified ideal conditions. Several unresolved issues sit at this junction: (i) whether indirect justification requires that idealized deliberators can actually apply the rule in non-ideal circumstances, or only that they would counterfactually endorse it; (ii) how conflicts between indirect rules should be resolved when partial compliance undermines the systemic justification of each; (iii) whether idealized agreement should be modeled as unanimous convergence or as a stable equilibrium among heterogeneous rational perspectives; and (iv) whether indirect systems can accommodate context-sensitive exceptions without collapsing back into direct evaluation.
