Please describe the most important implications of adopting a historiographical framework in which the 1815 Tambora eruption and the first Asiatic cholera pandemic (1817–1824) are treated not as passive environmental backdrops but as interacting non-human agents that jointly conditioned the emergence of modern state epidemiological surveillance and the aesthetic categories of the Romantic sublime. In particular, address what granting volcanic aerosols and pathogenic spread this kind of historical agency entails for the distinction between causal explanation and narrative agency, and identify the precise conceptual perils—especially varieties of environmental determinism—that arise when institutional or cultural outcomes are explained through climatic or microbial necessity. Finally, indicate how one might preserve a rigorous role for human intentionality and institutional choice without reducing these non-human forces to mere scenery.
