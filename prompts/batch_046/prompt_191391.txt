my study group is arguing about whether democratic citizens ever actually have dirty hands, or if the dirt always stays with the leader. someone laid out this chain and now i'm stuck because it seems to show that either democracy is a moral free-rider scheme or politics just isnt moral at all.

(1) dirty hands means you do something genuinely wrong for a good political reason, and you carry moral residue — you dont just wash it off.
(2) if there's residue, somebody in the causal chain has to bear it: the agent, the principal who ordered it, or the collective that authorized it.
(3) in a democracy citizens collectively authorize state violence, surveillance, etc. through voting, taxes, and constitutional consent.
(4) a leader can delegate execution downward, but moral residue cant be delegated downward; it either sticks to the leader or flows back up to the authorizing collective.
(5) but citizens are diffuse and anonymous — theres no "they" that feels guilt, seeks cleansing, or bears stigma. so they cant carry residue.
(6) that leaves two options: either the leader bears all of it (citizens stay spotless), or the residue just vanishes and dirty hands are an illusion.
(7) if citizens stay spotless, then democratic authorization is morally costless — i can vote for a dirty policy and remain fully clean.
(8) by the same logic i could sign a power of attorney letting a mercenary torture a prisoner, stay anonymous behind the paper, and claim my hands are clean because the agent bears the residue.
(9) that makes moral outsourcing way too cheap, so option one fails.
(10) therefore dirty hands dont exist; politics is just consequentialist calculation with no moral stain, which means the whole problem was a category mistake.

he says this proves citizens must share the dirt, but i think it secretly proves politics is outside morality. i want to keep (1) and (8) if i can — which step do i actually have to reject, and whats the price? is there a hidden slide between (4) and (5), or is one of the premises just wrong?
