Write an essay on the interpretive controversy over whether George Orwell's Politics and the English Language advances a therapeutic thesis—that cleaning up language can reform political practice—or merely offers a correlational diagnosis in which bureaucratic euphemism is a symptom rather than a cause of moral and political failure. Weigh the argument that Orwell's examples, such as the use of passive voice to obscure agency in descriptions of state violence and the deployment of abstract noun phrases that deaden moral response, show language actively shaping cognition, against the counter-argument that such linguistic patterns are selected for precisely because they serve pre-existing institutional interests and would persist even if individual writers were disciplined. Do not end on a non-committal note: defend either the view that linguistic reform has independent causal efficacy in politics, or the view that without structural change it is largely cosmetic, and explain what practical conclusion follows for how we should treat contemporary bureaucratic and media language.
