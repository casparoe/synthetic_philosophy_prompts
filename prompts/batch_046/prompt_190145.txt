I'm giving opening remarks at a workshop next month — half philosphers of action, half pallative care ethicists. The theme is integrity and partiality, but I don't want the usual Bernard Williams one-thought-too-many rehearsal where everyone just nods along. I need the talk to actually do philosophical work, not just decorate. Here's the concrete hook: my co-organiser just lost her dad, who was a hospice nurse for thirty years. He always said he kept showing up because it was his, never framing it as maximizing care or serving justice. Now her family wants the memorial framed in systemic terms — lives saved, contribution to healthcare — and she's pushing back because she thinks that language betrays the exact kind of integrity he lived by. I want about five minutes of remarks that hold both rooms, arguing that some descriptions of a life aren't just inaccurate but violate the subject's integrity, without making her sound sentimental. Build in a moment where I address the obvious counter: that describing his work systemically honors its scale. Then explain why, on Williams's view, that still misses the point — it's still the same abstraction, just dressed up in institutional language, and that's exactly the extra thought he never had. Don't cite texts or hand out reading lists; work the idea directly. Keep it in the weeds, not a TED talk. I'm writing this between sessions so there might be a typo or two — just roll with it.
