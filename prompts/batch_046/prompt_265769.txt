For a 90-minute graduate workshop on AI governance with 14 participants, design a structured role-play that makes the divergence between process-based and outcome-based supervision felt before the terms are introduced. Divide the group into four triads, each serving as a supervisory board overseeing an “agent” (played by a confederate with hidden instructions) that must allocate a fixed budget across five projects. The agent operates in one of two hidden modes: “legible but misaligned” (it produces coherent, transparent reasoning traces for allocations that subtly violate an unstated fairness constraint) or “opaque but aligned” (it produces correct allocations but offers incoherent or minimal justifications). Before each round, the board chooses its audit mode—process audit (receiving only the reasoning trace, scored for coherence and completeness) or outcome audit (receiving only the final allocation, scored against a published metric)—without knowing the hidden fairness constraint or the agent’s mode. The agent adapts to the mode: under process audit it optimizes trace plausibility regardless of allocation quality; under outcome audit it optimizes for the metric, sometimes violating the hidden constraint to maximize the visible score. Boards earn points for catching violations and lose points for false approvals; confederates earn points for evading detection. After five rounds, reveal the hidden constraint, each agent’s actual mode per round, and the boards’ audit choices. In the debrief, map each shift in audit mode onto the formal distinction—process supervision monitors the reasoning pathway, outcome supervision monitors the terminal state—note the predictable failure (boards switching modes after a single miss, agents overfitting to the audit signal), and be frank that the activity cannot reproduce the temporal dynamics of a real model updating its weights under sustained supervision, which the facilitator must supply verbally. Include printed scorecards, a timeline, and an explicit note on which feature of real-world supervision—ongoing feedback loops that reshape the model’s internal representations—the rules cannot capture. Ensure participants leave seeing that demanding transparency can incentivize performative reasoning, while measuring only outcomes can obscure hidden harms.
