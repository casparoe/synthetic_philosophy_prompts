You are a constitutional theorist with expertise in comparative federalism and EU law. Before you answer, briefly map the core institutional mechanisms that distinguish the four systems—US anti-commandeering and preemption doctrine, the German Bundesrat’s Länder-government veto and cooperative fiscal framework, the Swiss cantonal referendum and direct-democratic brake on federal law, and the EU’s principles of conferral, proportionality, and subsidiarity—so that your criteria are anchored in concrete structural failures rather than abstract preference.

Write the full specification—the desiderata—that any adequate normative theory of multi-level governance must satisfy if it is genuinely about subsidiarity rather than a post-hoc justification for decentralization. The purpose of such a theory is to guide institutional design in divided-authority contexts (national, subnational, supranational).

For each desideratum, provide:
1. The exact requirement (what the theory must deliver, preserve, or explain).
2. The motivating case or institutional failure behind it (draw on unfunded mandates in the US, German tension between cooperative federalism and Länder autonomy, Swiss direct-democratic checks, and EU competence creep together with democratic-deficit debates).
3. Whether it is non-negotiable or negotiable/contested among serious scholars.
4. Any direct tension with other desiderata, especially whether a subset is jointly unsatisfiable given well-known structural constraints (e.g., democratic accountability at every level versus efficient collective decision-making at the center; strict local veto versus uniform external representation).

In particular, address these conceptual pressure points:
- The competence-versus-exercise distinction: the theory must specify when lower-level inaction justifies higher-level intervention without collapsing into paternalism.
- The asymmetry problem: whether the same logic applies to heterogeneous units (large Länder versus small cantons; US states with divergent administrative capacities) or whether differential treatment is permissible.
- The democratic pedigree requirement: whether every authority exercise at a given level needs a distinct democratic mandate, and how this interacts with the EU’s dual-legitimacy structure (Member States plus European Parliament).

Keep the conceptual weight on the criteria and their motivating institutional failures rather than on a scorecard of existing systems.
