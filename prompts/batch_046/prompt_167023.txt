I'm writing a feature for the science-and-ideas desk about how old philosophy of langauge slogans are getting recycled in AI discourse, and I keep hitting the same one-liner from the Twin Earth debates: meanings just ain't in the head. People toss it around like a mic drop whenever someone suggests mental content is fixed entirely inside the skull.

Can you break down the precise claims that slogan could actually be expressing? There's the weak reading that's basically "reference can depend on stuff outside the brain," which feels almost tautological once you grant that words refer at all. Then there's Putnam's stronger version about natural kinds being anchored by hidden enviromental essences. And then there's the radical social-division reading, where your mental states get their content fixed by experts and community practice, so an isolated Twin Earthling literally doesn't know what she's thinking.

But here's the part I'm trying to sort out for the piece: I've come across some niche arguments that Twin Earth secretly smuggles in a transparency thesis about self-knowledge — that you can know your own mental states without knowing the external world — and that if you reject that transparency, the thought experiment doesn't prove externalism so much as prove mental content is just underdetermined. There's also a weird corner debate about whether the argument extends to empty terms like phlogiston or to artifactual kinds like smartphone, where social construction might be doing more work than hidden essence.

So could you lay out the menu of readings — from trivial to radical — and say for each whether it's plausible, whether it's false, and whether it's actually supported by the Twin Earth setup? Specifically, which versions do the real work of shutting down internalism, and which are just changing the subject? And which reading are people usually invoking when they use the slogan to dismiss a brain-in-a-vat style internalist? I don't need citations or a bibliography, just the conceptual map.
