I'm hosting a colloquium podcast series on normative ethics, and next week's guest is a philosopher who has recently defended a version of lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism. On her view, pleasure and pain are not globally commensurable: there is a qualitative boundary—which she describes, borrowing loosely from physical analogies, as a kind of experiential "phase transition" rather than a precise quantitative cutoff—below which suffering must be eliminated before any increase in pleasure can be morally weighted, but above which ordinary trade-offs are permissible. She insists this avoids the counterintuitive rigidity of strict lexical negative utilitarianism, because the threshold is context-sensitive and structurally discontinuous, yet she also denies that pleasure and suffering share a common cardinal scale that would allow simple aggregation. I want to prepare a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that press directly on the architecture of this view rather than asking her to restate it.

Specifically, I need at least one question challenging how she distinguishes her position from a disguised form of weighted hedonism once the threshold is crossed; one asking what phenomenological or conceptual criteria could identify the location of the threshold without presupposing the very commensurability her view rejects; and one introducing a problem I have been thinking about, which I will call "refractory suffering"—chronic, low-grade distress that never individually breaches the lexical boundary but accumulates across a lifetime—and asking whether such accumulation constitutes a threshold-crossing event in its own right or whether her framework is forced to treat it as morally negligible. For each of these three, please include a targeted follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer: for instance, an appeal to the non-aggregative structure of intense suffering, or to the claim that context-sensitivity does not entail measurability. The remaining questions should explore related tensions, such as whether higher-order pleasures or pains (e.g., the satisfaction of knowing one's suffering is reduced) can be traded across the boundary, or whether her analogy to physical phase transitions commits her to a form of emergentism about value that she has not defended. Please keep the tone rigorous but accessible, avoid generic biographical inquiries, and do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
