I'd like your actual credence, expressed as a number between 0 and 1, that a system comprising only a printed book containing a complete functional blueprint of Einstein's brain, together with a dispersed community of human readers who interpret and enact its instructions over months or years, could be the subject of phenomenal conscious states. By "phenomenal" I mean there is genuinely something it is like to be that system, not merely that it behaves intelligently or produces correct outputs. I want a single figure rather than a range, with the reasoning fully exposed.

Please decompose the claim into four sub-questions, assigning each a rough probability with a one-line justification:

1. The probability that some organisational or functionalist account of consciousness is correct, as opposed to biological essentialism, panpsychism, or eliminativism.

2. The probability that the book-reader ensemble genuinely instantiates the relevant causal-functional architecture, rather than merely representing or describing it, given that all processing is mediated through separate human minds and spread across space and time.

3. The probability that phenomenal consciousness can survive substantial temporal discontinuity and spatial distribution — that is, that there is no hidden requirement for continuous, integrated information processing within a single biological boundary.

4. The probability that the absence of a unified biological substrate introduces an independent metaphysical or epistemic blocker (for instance, a failure of genuine causal integration, or inappropriate conditions for mental-state attribution) even if the functional pattern is otherwise preserved.

Combine these terms however you judge appropriate — multiplicatively, with weights, or otherwise — to reach your overall credence. Then identify which of the four terms you are least confident in, and specify what kind of empirical finding or conceptual argument would shift that specific term by at least 0.2 in either direction.

Next, suppose a committed functionalist assigns 0.75 to the overall claim and a biological naturalist assigns 0.05. According to your decomposition, which of the four terms above is doing the principal work in explaining that gap?

Finally, state whether your own overall number is more sensitive to the truth of functionalism itself or to the conditions for distributed, discontinuous instantiation, and briefly explain why.

Please do not retreat to "it depends on one's theory of consciousness"; the point is to make the dependencies explicit and locate where your belief actually lives.
