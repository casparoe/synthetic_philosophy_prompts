I'm working through a position in welfare ethics for a workshop and want a consistency audit before I commit to it in writing. Here are the commitments I want to hold together:

(1) Pleasure and pain are interpersonally and intrapersonally commensurable on a cardinal interval scale; the correct aggregation method is a weighted linear sum with suffering weighted roughly 2:1 against pleasure, calibrated through standardized gamble equivalence across subjects.

(2) There is a lexical threshold in negative utilitarianism—not a biological constant, but a reflectively calibrated boundary—call it the S*-line—such that any suffering at or above this line cannot be morally compensated by any amount of pleasure, no matter how widely distributed.

(3) The S*-line is fixed not by intensity alone but by a second-order judgment: suffering enters the lexically dominant zone only when a fully informed agent, with adaptive preferences corrected for, endorses the claim that this suffering renders any compensatory goods irrelevant to the rational continuation of their life.

(4) Below the S*-line, standard prioritarian aggregation applies with the 2:1 weighting; above it, aggregation is suspended and the only permissible moral aim is elimination of that suffering.

(5) The existence of the S*-line does not violate the cardinal commensurability in (1), because the lexical relation governs moral justification and choice, not the underlying hedonic metric, which remains continuous across the boundary.

I have a nagging sense that (1) and (2) are pulling in opposite directions—if suffering can be lexically dominant, it looks incommensurable with pleasure at that boundary, which seems to break the very scale that (1) asserts. At the same time, (3) looks like it introduces a discontinuity that makes the interval scale in (1) depend on subjective endorsement, which might collapse the intersubjective calibration. Meanwhile, (5) feels like a defensive move I might be helping myself to. I also wonder whether (4) is stable: if aggregation is suspended above the line, how do we compare two situations both containing S*-level suffering but different distributions?

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which are only apparently so, and rank the tensions by severity. For each real tension, specify the least costly revision and what it costs me—whether I have to abandon cardinal aggregation, the reflective calibration, the lexical claim itself, or just the framing in (5). I am not attached to any particular commitment being saved; I want to know what the package actually commits me to.
