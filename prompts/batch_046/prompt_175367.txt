I have been working through a problem in the philosophy of cognitive architecture for a faculty colloquium next month, and I have constructed an argument about representational causation that I cannot dismantle. The question is whether symbolic structures in a careenium—a theoretical architecture composed entirely of simmballs, minimal symbolic tokens whose representational content is fixed solely by internal relational syntax rather than by intrinsic physical properties—actually push the substrate around, or whether they are merely descriptive overlays on substrate dynamics. Here is the chain as it stands:

(1) By definition, simmballs within a careenium are substrate-independent: the same symbolic relation can be instantiated in silicon, neural tissue, or hydrodynamic flow without any change to its representational content.

(2) If a token is substrate-independent in this strong sense, then the particular physical properties of its current instantiation make no causal contribution to the work the token performs; causation runs downward from syntax to substrate, not upward from substrate to syntax.

(3) In every modeled careenium, reconfiguring the relational network of simmballs predictably redirects energy distributions and motor outputs, while the substrate-intrinsic physical laws are held constant.

(4) Therefore, within a careenium, symbols genuinely cause substrate behavior: they push the substrate around.

(5) But substrate-independence, if genuinely causal and not merely taxonomic, should apply to any multiply realizable pattern—genetic codes, linguistic conventions, traffic signals, and perhaps even crystal lattices.

(6) Hence, if symbols push substrate in careenia, they do so in all such systems, and the claim that symbols push the substrate around becomes universally true of any organized pattern.

(7) This either drains the thesis of explanatory specificity—making it indistinguishable from the claim that physical causes have structural descriptions—or forces us to attribute representational causation to bacteria and weather systems, which is absurd.

I feel the slide occurs somewhere between (2) and (4), but I cannot name the precise equivocation. Which premise should be rejected, and what does abandoning it cost the theory of the careenium? I need a diagnosis that preserves as much of (4) as possible without collapsing into universal pan-symbolism, and I want to be certain I am not missing a more elegant repair that saves the causal claim for cognition specifically.
