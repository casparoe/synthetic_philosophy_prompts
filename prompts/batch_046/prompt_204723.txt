Since I retired from the physics dept I've been getting into philosophy of mind, particularly Hofstadter's old careenium idea — the symmballs versus marbles thing, asking whether high-level symbols actually push the substrate around inside the cranium or whether it's all bottom-up dynamics. Anyway, it occured to me that AI evaluation labs have this institutional practice: they test large models purely on symbolic outputs — maths proofs, logic puzzles, that sort of thing — and if the model produces the correct sequence they say it has the concept or genuinely understands the rule. They never check whether there's any causal loop back down into the weights or the neural substrate; they just treat correct symbolic behaviour as proof that real symbolic causation is happening inside the system. What philosophical commitments about symbol-substrate interaction does that testing regime presuppose? Does it quietly assume that symmballs shove marbles around inside the careenium, or can a pure substrate-up view licence the same practice for other reasons? And if you replaced that underlying commitment with the view that symbols are only epiphenomenal labels on substrate dynamics, what exactly would have to change about how they design thier benchmarks?
