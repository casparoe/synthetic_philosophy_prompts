Compare Williams's view that duelling died out mainly because honour stopped being the currency of elite status with Appiah's idea that moral revolutions happen when honour and shame get redirected rather than abandoned. Now here's the twist — I want you to bring in a third mechanism I'm calling institutional decoupling: the notion that a practice like duelling or slavery doesn't disappear because anyone has a change of heart about morality, but because the functional work it was doing gets replaced by bureacratic substitutes (courts handling redress, contract law replacing coerced extraction). Is this decoupling idea genuinely distinct from both Williams's honour-loss and Appiah's normative redirection, or is it just the same story in systems-language? And if it is different, which of the three should carry the weight when we explain why slavery and duelling actually vanished?
