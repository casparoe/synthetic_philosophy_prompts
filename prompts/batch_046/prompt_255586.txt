okay so ive been trying to formalize why lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism feels stuck between a rock and a hard place, and i think i have an argument that shows it cant work either way. but it smells like a false dichotomy, i just cant spot the escape hatch.

(1) either pleasure and pain are interphenomenally calibrated—there's a shared metric across different phenomenal kinds—or theyre ontologically incommensurable.
(2) if theyre incommensurable, then any rule that weighs them against each other is rationally groundless.
(3) lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism does weigh them: it says suffering dominates pleasure past a certain intensity threshold. so it needs at least some calibration.
(4) therefore if incommensurability holds, LTNU has no rational foundation.
(5) but suppose they are commensurable, with a shared hedonic scale.
(6) then in principle enough pleasure can always outweigh a given amount of suffering, unless we just declare a lexical block by fiat.
(7) LTNU declares exactly that block—no amount of joy compensates for suffering past the threshold—but on a shared scale thats just an arbitrary discontinuity, not a justified boundary.
(8) so if commensurability holds, LTNU is an unjustified stipulation.
(9) hence either incommensurability kills LTNU by removing the scale it needs, or commensurability kills it by making its lexical block arbitrary.
(10) so LTNU is incoherent regardless.

id rather keep (1) and (5) than throw out the whole idea that suffering matters more, but i feel like im missing something about how lexical ordering works without cardinal comparison. where does this slip? which step do i reject, and what does that cost me?
