For an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar final (75 minutes, 30 marks), draft one essay question on Wittgenstein's private language argument that does not permit a passing answer by merely summarizing the beetle-in-the-box scenario or the rule-following considerations. Instead, present this hypothetical: researchers develop an interoceptive notation system in which each subject privately assigns symbols to fine-grained bodily sensations, then attempts intersubjective alignment using only statistical correlations of reported intensity, with no shared public criterion for correct application. Ask students whether this protocol establishes a genuinely private yet intersubjectively checkable language, and require them to use Wittgenstein's distinction between criteria and symptoms to diagnose precisely where the protocol fails or succeeds. Provide a model answer of approximately 500 words and a marking scheme built around four graded criteria totaling 30 marks; for each criterion, specify the conceptual move that earns full marks, the specific misconception that should cost marks, and the exact point allocation. The question should separate students who understand why a private criterion of correctness collapses from those who have only memorized the labels.
