In my philosophy of physics course we have been working through Gödel’s rotating cosmological model, but I want to move beyond the usual summary and evaluate a stronger, reconstructed position. The view I am considering holds that the mere consistency of a rotating solution with closed timelike curves shows global temporal ordering is not a metaphysical necessity, but only a contingent feature sustained by our local non-rotating boundary conditions; consequently, any philosophy of time that treats objective succession as structurally necessary—rather than as an emergent local property—rests on a modal mistake. This goes further than Gödel’s own remarks about the “illusion” of time: it claims that even if our universe is not rotating, the modal accessibility of rotating solutions undermines the necessity of global time.

What would genuinely count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a list of standard objections. First, does the argument illicitly move from physical possibility within general relativity to metaphysical possibility—would its defender need a bridge principle, and is that bridge defensible? Second, what do current observational constraints on cosmic vorticity actually establish: do they merely render rotation physically unlikely in our region, or do they undermine the modal premise that rotating solutions are genuinely accessible? Third, could an opponent reply by distinguishing metric structure from causal-topological structure, arguing that even in a Gödel-type metric a local causal order remains that preserves every philosophically relevant feature of temporal succession? Fourth, are there other cosmological models—perhaps with different symmetry conditions or matter distributions—that restore global ordering without excluding local rotation, and if so, do they neutralize the modal argument?

Please rank the defeaters: which would be fatal, forcing abandonment of the core claim, and which are merely awkward, requiring only qualification of the bridge principle or the scope of “global.” Also, indicate what defenders of this stronger Gödel-inspired view would themselves concede as damaging, versus what they would dismiss. I am especially interested in whether the position risks being insulated into unfalsifiability—if every failure of the physical premise can be met by retreating to bare modal possibility, is that a genuine problem for a metaphysical claim of this kind, or is modal insulation appropriate here?
