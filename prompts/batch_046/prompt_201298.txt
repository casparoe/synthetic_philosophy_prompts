Here's an argument I sketched for a study group on evolutionary debunking and now find suspicious because it seems to prove that the debunker can't consistently believe her own conclusion. I want to keep the core debunking claim intact if possible, but I can't locate the leak.

(1) If a belief-forming mechanism in domain D was shaped by natural selection solely for reproductive fitness and not for tracking truths in D, then beliefs formed by that mechanism in D lack default epistemic justification.

(2) First-order moral cognition was shaped solely for social coordination and reproductive fitness, not for tracking mind-independent moral truths.

(3) Therefore, first-order moral beliefs lack default justification.

(4) Meta-ethical cognition — reasoning about whether moral realism holds, whether debunking succeeds, etc. — is not an independent module. It is a spandrel: it recruits the same domain-general normative-calibration systems used in first-order moral reasoning and applies them reflexively.

(5) Those domain-general calibration systems were also shaped by selection for coordination and fitness, not for tracking meta-ethical truths.

(6) By parity with (1)-(3), meta-ethical beliefs — including the belief that (3) is correct — therefore lack default justification.

(7) Any attempt to independently calibrate meta-ethical beliefs via reflective equilibrium still deploys those same selection-shaped calibration systems.

(8) So the evolutionary debunker asserts (3) using mechanisms that, by her own lights in (1), lack default justification in D.

(9) If she suspends belief in (3) to avoid this, she abandons the debunking conclusion; if she retains it, she violates the epistemic norm that unjustified beliefs in D should be suspended — a norm she needs to motivate (1).

(10) Hence evolutionary debunking, applied consistently, undermines its own assertion and collapses into a global suspension of normative belief, including the belief that we ought to suspend moral belief.

The conclusion feels absurd — it's not just self-defeat but a kind of normative black hole. I think the flaw is somewhere between (4) and (8), but I'm not sure whether the problem is the spandrel claim, the parity inference, or the move in (9). I'd especially like to preserve (1), (2), and (3).
