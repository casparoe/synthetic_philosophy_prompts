You are advising a small philosophy research group — one metaphysician and one logician, with about three years of funding — that wants to make genuine progress on the special composition question. They are not interested in another survey of universalism, nihilism, and standard restricted-composition answers; they want to know where the remaining conceptual labor actually lies.

What are the three or four open questions, sub-problems, or lines of work that matter most for resolving or reframing special composition? In particular, consider the following candidates and either rank them or replace them with better ones: (1) whether compositional boundaries are vague and, if so, whether that vagueness is ontic, epistemic, or semantic; (2) the diachronic problem of how composition persists through gradual part-replacement in organisms and artifacts, and whether this forces a four-dimensionalist treatment; (3) whether special composition can be reduced to or explained by causal-functional integration rather than treated as a primitive relation; and (4) whether the standard framing assumes spatiotemporal part-whole relations that may fail in non-classical physical descriptions.

For each item in your ranking, defend its place: why it is upstream of other questions, how tractable it is for philosophers working without empirical laboratories, what a decisive result would look like, and what kind of argument or formal result over the next few years would either elevate it or show it is a dead end. Be explicit about the criteria you are using — importance for other metaphysical debates, tractability with current conceptual and logical tools, and neglectedness — and about the trade-offs between them. The deliverable should be a defended research agenda, not a survey and not a bibliography.
