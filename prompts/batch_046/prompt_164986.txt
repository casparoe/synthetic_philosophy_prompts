Since my appointment to the procurement committee for our university's residential dining halls, I have been forced to weigh supply contracts not merely by cost but by the hidden mortality embedded in each chain. I am now persuaded that the familiar beef-versus-chicken comparison is incomplete without accounting for the suffering that crop cultivation itself introduces—the field mice, nesting birds, and insects crushed in harvest—and that the moral arithmetic requires what I have come to call the substitution matrix: reducing beef does not eliminate harm but displaces it into monoculture acreage, while chicken concentrates both direct slaughter and feed-crop amplification. I would like a poem that renders this triad conceptually accurate and structurally inevitable.

Write a poem in terza rima—six tercets, eighteen lines—in which each tercet is spoken in the voice of one of the three sources: the cultivated field (with its combine-harvest mortality and hidden field deaths), the beef pasture (with its ruminant land-use intensity and low direct death count per calorie), and the broiler house (with its high direct mortality and feed-conversion dependency). The rhyme scheme must interlock so that each voice completes its stanza only by borrowing rhyme sounds from the other two: the field’s middle rhyme must be supplied by the pasture, the pasture’s by the broiler house, and so on in rotation, so that no category can be articulated without phonically inheriting the suffering of the others. Render the mechanisms—harvester mortality, enteric-fermentation land demands, feed-ratio amplification—through metaphor rather than technical jargon. Place the volta in the fifth tercet, at the moment the speaker recognizes that selecting among the three is not an escape from harm but an unavoidable entanglement in all three forms of death. The final tercet should close on an unresolved couplet that refuses to resolve the matrix into a simple dietary prescription.

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific authors or texts; the poem should stand on its conceptual and structural accuracy alone. I want the rotating voices and the interlocking rhymes themselves to perform the philosophical work of showing why the substitution problem cannot be solved by switching categories.
