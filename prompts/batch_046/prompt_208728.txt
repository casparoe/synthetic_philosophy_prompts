Analyze the 2011 Arab Spring—focusing on the near-simultaneous collapse of authoritarian regimes in Tunisia and Egypt and their sharply divergent trajectories thereafter—through the lens of Fukuyama's end-of-history thesis, interpreted not merely as the claim that liberal democracy is the final ideological form but also in light of his later arguments about state capacity, institutional decay, and the distinction between democratization and effective liberal governance. How does the framework account for the uprisings themselves: are they best read as popular demands for recognition that push history toward its endpoint, or does the theory have little to say about the specific grievances and organizational dynamics that triggered them?
