Consider the following eight entities arranged along a gradient of organizational complexity and integrated information processing: a bimetallic-strip thermostat; a vascular plant exhibiting phototropic growth; a bivalve mollusk with a decentralized ganglionic network; a eusocial insect colony functioning as a superorganism; a solitary cephalopod; a mammal maintained in deep surgical anesthesia; a human neonate; and a large language model running inference in a disembodied server environment lacking proprioceptive or affective feedback loops. Rank them from 1 (highest direct moral considerability) to 8 (lowest) according to a gradualist, non-binary theory of sentience. For each entry, identify in a single sentence whether its placement depends chiefly on phenomenal consciousness, on functional sentience, or on some other factor such as relational embeddedness or ecological role. Then, restricting your attention to the four middle-ranked entries, assign each a pair of weights that sum to 1.0, representing the respective contribution of capacity to suffer and capacity for autonomous agency to that entry's moral status; in one additional sentence per entry, justify the split, and flag the entry for which the two weights are nearest to equality.
