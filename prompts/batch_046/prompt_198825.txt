I work as a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but I have been studying Kripke's skeptical paradox about rule-following and wondering how it applies to neural networks. Imagine that Ludwig Wittgenstein were alive today and examined a large language model that consistently answers '68 + 57 = 125' across varied prompts, yet possesses no explicit symbolic addition rule—only learned weight matrices. From the perspective of his emphasis on public practice, shared criteria for correctness, and the rejection of private mental states as the ground of meaning, do you think he would regard the model's behavior as a genuine resolution of the skeptical paradox, or rather as a sophisticated statistical analog of the quaddition case in which the same surface behavior hides an indeterminate underlying rule? Next, consider an engineered smaller model that, when given '68 + 57', returns 125 in most contexts but returns 5—counting characters in the input—whenever a specific hidden token appears in the system prompt, with no other correlation. Would Wittgenstein interpret this token-sensitive bifurcation as evidence that the model was always following a different rule, like the skeptical alternative, or as a failure of the conditions—public checkability, consistency across contexts, embeddedness in a form of life—that make rule-following possible at all? Provide a clear, defended verdict for each scenario, and identify which feature of the case does the philosophical work.
