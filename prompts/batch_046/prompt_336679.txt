hey, I'm on the municipal benefits review panel and we're voting thursday on whether to renew the 'dynamic desert' pilot. The proposal ties cash-assistance tiers to a reciprocity index: job-search logs, volunteer hours, and even neighbour endorsements get weighted into a score that determines your benifit level. Staff says this finally solves the undeserving-poor problem by making desert measurable instead of moralistic. But I think there's a deeper issue: if the metrics themselves reshape the behaviour they track, then the 'undeserving' category gets manufactured rather than found, and backward-looking conditionality might actually erode the solidarity it claims to protect. I need a short memo for the panel recommending approval, rejection, or a modified trial with a clear timeline. It should take the staff's solidarity argument seriously, not just dismiss it, and it should address whether quantifying desert avoids the philosophical problem or just hides it inside an algorithm. No need for a bibliography, I just want reasoning the other members can actually use.
