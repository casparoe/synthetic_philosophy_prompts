I was listening to a podcast about collective memory and they had a segement comparing Avishai Margalit and David Rieff. The host said Margalit argues there's a thick ethical duty to remember victims of atrocity, not just as sadness but as a moral bond — forgetting is basically a betrayal of the dead. Then they said Rieff thinks societies should practice a kind of collective amnesty for the past, actively letting archives decay after a generation so trauma doesn't recieve permanent institutional protection. The host also claimed Margalit actually agrees with Rieff when remembering causes intergenerational harm, because Margalit's shared memory only binds voluntary communities, so if desendants suffer the duty just dissolves. And apparently Rieff rejects every kind of memorial, even museums, since they all institutionalize grievance.

I'm taking a course on ethics of memory and this sounds mostly right, but I feel like there's sleight of hand in how they make the two converge. Is this summary fair? Which parts are accurate, which are subtle misrepresentations or overstatements, and does the claim that they agree on intergenerational trauma actually follow from the accurate parts? They also mentioned this idea they called epistemic forgiveness — letting archives decay as a deliberate moral act rather than denial — which wasn't in either author's work but sounds like it could bridge them. Should that be read into Rieff's view, or is it a separate thing entirely?
