I am preparing a single ninety-minute seminar for an audience of science journalists and policy analysts—educated generalists with no training in sociology or economic history—focused on the precise problem Weber raises about culture functioning as a causal factor. The aim is not to ask whether religious values motivate individual behavior, but whether they can explain the institutional form of modern capitalism without collapsing into either economic determinism or idealist magic. I need a teaching plan, not a lecture script. Lay out the order in which the conceptual pieces must be introduced, noting the dependencies: what must be secured before the causal claim can be made intelligible, and why the distinction between value-rational action and instrumentally rational action should appear at a specific point in the sequence rather than at the beginning. Identify the one concrete historical scenario or structural comparison that should carry the entire explanation, specifying exactly what properties it must possess—such as a value system that rewards disciplined worldly labor without intending capital accumulation—and what it must lack—such as direct profit-seeking motivation—together with an account of why a generic religion-influences-economy example fails to do the work. Map the misconceptions these participants are likely to bring or form: that Weber asserts Protestantism single-handedly caused capitalism; that elective affinity is merely correlation in technical dress; that values are epiphenomenal rationalizations of material interest; and that if culture is genuinely causal, it should remain causally dominant permanently—indicating the precise point in the sequence at which each misconception should be intercepted and defused. Provide one brief diagnostic question I can pose at the end to verify that participants understand why values operate as explanatory selection conditions rather than as ornamental justifications. Finally, state explicitly which adjacent debates—such as Marxist base-superstructure models, Tawney's counter-thesis, or rational-choice critiques of cultural explanation—I should set aside for this level and declare openly out of scope. Do not include assigned readings, bibliographic recommendations, or requests for citations.
