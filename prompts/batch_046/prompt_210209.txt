1. the genealogical method claims that if a value judgment can be fully explained by its psychological origin — say, ressentiment, the will to power, or some life-condition — rather than by tracking an independent moral fact, then that judgment lacks genuine cognitive authority.

2. nietzsche applies this to slave morality: the evaluation that weakness is good and strength is evil is just an invention serving preservation and revenge, not a discovery about reality.

3. he extends the same treatment to the ascetic ideal and to scientific objectivity, treating them too as expressions of particular affective economies.

4. but nietzsche's own evaluations — that slave morality is life-denying, that master morality is life-affirming, that we need a transvaluation — are themselves value judgments arising from his own conditions (illness, alienation, desire for influence).

5. so by 1, nietzsche's own judgments also lack cognitive authority.

6. furthermore, if every judgment is just an expression of will-to-power, then there is no neutral standpoint from which to say life-affirmation is genuinely superior to life-denial; there are only competing wills.

7. therefore the method, applied consistently, undermines the normative force of nietzsche's own critique and collapses into a reflexive nihilism where no value can be rationally preferred.

8. the only escape is to restrict genealogy so it applies to slave morality but not to the genealogist's own values — yet that restriction looks ad hoc and unjustified.

9. so either genealogy destroys itself, or it functions only as a selective rhetorical weapon.

where does this go wrong, if anywhere? i want to keep 1 and 7 in some form because the reflexive problem feels real, but maybe i'm sliding between explaining origin and defeating justification, or there's a hidden quantifier shift? tell me which step to give up and what the standard positions pay to reject it.
