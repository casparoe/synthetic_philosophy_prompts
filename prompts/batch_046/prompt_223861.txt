Take the Occupy Wall Street encampment in Zuccotti Park, autumn 2011, and analyse it through Graeber and Wengrow's argument that egalitarianism is usually an active achievement rather than a default. Apply thier distinction between dominance over resources and authority based on moral prestige to ask who really held sway in the General Assembly. Was the camp a modern echo of the seasonal dualism they describe — societies that flip between hierarchical and flat structures — or does modern state power make that kind of oscillation impossible? Then use the NYPD eviction and the movement's quick fade to test whether thier prehistoric model of deliberately maintained equality can survive inside a bureacratic state, and say exactly where the theory goes silent on cases like this.
