I have recently adopted a plant-based diet and begun volunteering with a climate-advocacy organization, yet I remain troubled by the suspicion that my individual actions are causally negligible against systemic harm. I am preparing to interview an unnamed philosopher who defends a position she terms normative authorization: the claim that in collective harms such as climate change, meat consumption, and democratic voting, moral responsibility should be understood through participation in shared normative structures that require collective assent to change, rather than through any causal contribution the individual makes. I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond restating this view. Specifically, include at least one question each on how the framework distinguishes climate-related consumption from dietary choice, given that meat markets may respond to marginal demand; on whether voting in large-scale elections can be anything more than symbolic authorization; on the risk that the view overgenerates obligations into career choice and investment; and on whether normative authorization ultimately collapses into a disguised causal claim. For each main question, embed a follow-up keyed to her most likely reply—especially her anticipated move of insisting that responsibility tracks structural participation rather than outcome efficacy. Do not present the questions neutrally; commit to the critical framing that her view either smuggles causal reasoning back in or produces obligations that are implausibly extensive, and design the sequence to push her consistently toward one of those two horns. After listing the questions, provide a brief paragraph stating the reasoning behind this committed framing. Present the questions first—numbered, with embedded follow-ups—without any preface, introduction, or commentary, and do not request bibliographies, citations, or further reading.
