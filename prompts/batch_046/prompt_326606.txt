ok here's roughly where i've landed on internal vs external reasons and i want to know if it actualy hangs together or i'm just being sloppy.

(1) i'm basically a Williams-style internalist: for something to be a reason for me it has to be reachable, maybe through a long chain of practical reasoning, from motives i actualy have right now. if there's no path from my current psycholgy, it's not my reason.

(2) but i think there's this thing, call it recognitional uptake: sometimes you just see someone in need and they suddenly fall inside your practical boundary, like a switch flips. that recognition isn't derived from your prior motives; it's more like a perceptual shift. once it happens though, the reason is genuinely yours.

(3) i don't think the fact that suffering is objectively bad gives anyone a reason by itself. without some psychological hook — care, sympathy, identification — it's just a fact about the world, not a reason for action.

(4) still, i think instutional roles generate reasons that don't depend on your personal psycholgy at all. if you're the only doctor on a plnae and someone collapses, you have reason to help even if you hate medicine and feel zero motivation.

(5) i also think practical reasoning shouldn't depend on when you happen to think about the problem. if my motives are the same on monday and friday, the reasons are the same, regardless of whether i notice them.

(6) and i think i have reason to care about my future self's well-being even if that future self has totally different motives and values — but i don't have a parallel reason for a stranger who happens to have those same future motives.

my worry is that (2) and (4) are both smuggling in external reasons, and (6) clashes with (1) since my future self's motives aren't in my current psycholgy. but maybe (2) is saved because recognition changes my motives, and (5) just means reasoning is stable over time, not that reasons come from outside? and maybe (6) is okay because identity bridges the gap, not an external reason?

is this consistent? rank the tensions by how bad they are, say which ones are only apparent, and tell me what i'd have to drop if i want to keep the rest. be specific — don't just say 'drop internalism', say what version of it survives.
