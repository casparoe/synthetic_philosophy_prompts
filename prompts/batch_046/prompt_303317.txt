Write a dialogue between two people who both read Nietzche closely -- call them Mara and Leo. Mara thinks his diet and health obsessions (the Italian food thing, avoiding beer, eating rice and vegetables, all that stuff in Ecce Homo and the letters) are actually part of his philosophy, not just biography: she thinks "great health" vs "petty health" is a real ethical distinction about being able to suffer productively. Leo thinks it's all rationalization of chronic migraines and stomach issues, and that reducing philosophy to menu choices is a category mistake. They should actually talk to each other: Mara should argue that choosing lightness over heavy German food is an aesthetic practice of self-overcoming, Leo should counter that you can call it "great health" but it's still just a sick man managing symptoms. Then Mara should ask whether Leo's biographical reduction can even account for why Nietzche distinguishes "great health" (being able to bear deep suffering and keep working) from just feeling comfortable. Leo should admit the distinction has philosophical bite but say Nietzche's actual prescriptions don't map onto it -- they're just doctor's orders with fancy names. Include at least one typo, keep it casual like coffee shop talk, and make sure neither sounds like a strawman.
