Write a one-semester term-paper proposal (approximately 6,000 words) for a philosophy-of-mind and aesthetics seminar on the following question: in tangled hierarchical systems, is the “inviolate level” best understood as a fixed metaphysical boundary—exemplified by the sheet of paper in Escher’s Drawing Hands, which draws the hands without being drawn by them—or as a dynamically stabilized “virtual frame” that predictive-processing models of perception generate and maintain without ever escaping the loop? Frame the problem precisely by distinguishing two loops in Escher’s image: a representational loop (each hand depicts the other) and an epistemic loop (the viewer recognizes that the paper is not depicted within the image, thereby fixing the frame of interpretation). Then ask whether recent hierarchical Bayesian accounts of active inference, which treat the observer’s priors as internal to the predictive model rather than external to it, can preserve this epistemic loop without smuggling in an observer-independent ground floor, or whether they necessarily dissolve the distinction between the drawn and the drawer.

State why the existing literature leaves this open. Standard interpretations of Hofstadter’s strange loops treat the inviolate level as a structural necessity: self-reference must bottom out somewhere, or the system collapses into paradox. Meanwhile, the predictive-processing literature tends to treat hierarchical inference as fully enclosed, with “precision weighting” substituting for an external frame. What is missing is an explicit engagement between these two frameworks: no one has asked whether the epistemic loop in Escher’s image functions like a precision-weighted boundary condition rather than like an ontologically separate substrate, and whether that reconception saves or eliminates the inviolate level.

Propose an approach that does not require empirical data collection but proceeds by conceptual reconstruction and case analysis. Month one: formalize the two-loop distinction (representational vs. epistemic) and map it onto the standard strange-loop taxonomy. Month two: reconstruct the predictive-processing account of frame stabilization, focusing on how higher-level predictions constrain lower-level ones without requiring an “outside.” Month three: apply both frameworks to Escher’s image and to two constructed analogs—one in which the frame is gradually incorporated into the image (a “collapsing” loop) and one in which the frame remains fixed but is shown to be generated by the viewer’s own predictive model. Month four: draft and revise, with explicit failure conditions. A successful result would show that the epistemic loop can be preserved as a functional, not metaphysical, invariant, yielding a precise criterion for when a hierarchy is genuinely tangled rather than merely nested. The project fails if the distinction between the two loops collapses under analysis—if, for example, the epistemic loop turns out to be just another representational loop at a higher level, leaving the inviolate level with no distinctive work to do—or if the predictive-processing reconstruction requires assumptions (e.g., about the ontology of precision) that are themselves as contested as the original boundary.

Give an honest assessment of scope risk. The main danger is that engaging predictive processing rigorously without reducing it to metaphor demands more technical detail than a philosophy seminar paper can accommodate; if that happens, the proposal should be narrowed to a conceptual comparison of frame-types rather than a full reconstruction. Do not include a bibliography or literature review by citation; refer to positions and arguments in general terms and note where specific sources will be added later.

Finally, invite direct pushback: if the premise that Escher’s image contains two separable loops is confused, or if the task of reconciling strange loops with predictive processing is ill-formed for a semester project, say so plainly rather than accommodating the framing politely. The prompt should stand or fall on whether that conceptual tension is real and tractable.
