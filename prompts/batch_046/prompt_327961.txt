As a novelist framing a philosophical confrontation in an archive half-destroyed by fire, write a dialogue between the Archivalist and the Pruner on whether ontology should admit entities whose defining information is permanently inaccessible. Center the argument on shadow-cataloguing—the practice of assigning categorical membership solely through predictive footprints when identifying properties lie behind an irreversible epistemic barrier. The Archivalist must defend this practice as required for explanatory coherence; the Pruner must attack it as a violation of identification conditions that produces ontological fiction. Deliver exactly five exchanges, each composed of two brief sentences, arranged without introduction or conclusion, with each speaker responding directly to the specific claim just made.
