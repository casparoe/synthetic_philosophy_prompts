Write a proposal for a one-semester philosophy-of-biology term paper, approximately 6,000 words, addressing the following framing of the species problem. The guiding question: is the longstanding debate between monism and pluralism about species concepts misframed because both sides treat a species as a natural kind whose members share properties at a given time, when evolutionary lineages are more plausibly understood as four-dimensional, spatiotemporally extended processes—analogous to rivers or other diachronic entities—whose boundaries depend on continuity conditions over evolutionary time rather than on shared intrinsic traits?

State the question precisely enough that the paper could defend one of two determinate conclusions: either that adopting a process ontology dissolves the traditional species problem by showing that apparently competing definitions apply to different temporal stages or explanatory purposes without conflict, or that the process ontology relocates rather than resolves the difficulty, generating a new problem about the identity conditions of evolutionary lineages that the standard kind-based framework had obscured. Explain why existing answers—standard defenses of the Biological Species Concept, the Phylogenetic Species Concept, and the pluralist claim that different concepts serve different biological interests—leave this particular framing open, specifically noting that they do not examine whether the ontology of populations over evolutionary time supports a static-kind model at all.

Set out the approach: conceptual analysis of continuity conditions for lineages, comparison with four-dimensionalist treatments of individual organisms adapted from debates in personal identity but scaled to populations, and evaluation of whether this framework can distinguish species-level processes from those at the level of populations, demes, or higher taxa. Say why this conceptual route is preferable to an empirical survey of taxonomic practice or to a direct defense of an existing pluralist position. Specify what the deliverable looks like: a clearly structured argument with explicit premises about temporal parts and evolutionary continuity, an assessment of whether the framework eliminates or transforms the monism-pluralism dispute, and an honest discussion of where the analogy between species and processes like rivers breaks down. Give a realistic scope: one semester, no original field data, reliance on established evolutionary and metaphysical concepts, with citations to be supplied separately. Conclude by identifying what would count as failure—if the process account cannot non-arbitrarily demarcate species boundaries, or if it turns out to be merely a redescription of existing pluralism without independent explanatory payoff.
