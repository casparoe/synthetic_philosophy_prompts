I just spent three hours arguing with a friend about whether large language models can genuinely perform what Hofstadter called "jumping out of the system" — not merely producing self-referential text, but actually restructuring the framework from which they operate — and whether the MU-puzzle's demonstration that a formal system can contain undecidable strings yet never generate them from within has any meaningful analog in how transformers handle out-of-distribution reasoning. We kept circling the same problem: is the interesting question epistemological (whether a model can recognize that it operates inside a formal system and transcend it), architectural (whether attention mechanisms allow a kind of syntactic escape that is not just interpolation across training data), or normative (whether the capacity to jump out, if it existed, would fundamentally change how we think about alignment and containment)? Given that I want to spend the next year writing about this seriously, I need to know which open questions actually deserve the effort, and which are mostly conceptual dead ends that borrow Hofstadter's vocabulary to sound profound. Should I focus on whether there is a coherent analog to Gödelian incompleteness in neural network computation; on whether prompt injection or jailbreaking counts as a weak, causally induced form of JOOTS and what that implies for containment strategies; on whether the MU-puzzle teaches us something specific about the limits of rule-following in language models; or on the meta-philosophical question of whether "jumping out" is even a well-defined capacity for non-symbolic, distributed systems? Rank these lines — or replace them with the ones you think actually matter — and defend the ordering with explicit criteria: tractability for someone without a computer science doctorate, dependence on empirical questions that will not be settled within the decade, risk of being a purely verbal confusion, and how many other philosophical questions about AI capability hang on resolving this one. I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature; I want a defended agenda that tells me where a philosophical argument could genuinely change how people think about machine reasoning, and where I would be wasting my time.
