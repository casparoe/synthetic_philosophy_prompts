Disjunctivists and representationalists about perception have been talking past each other since the 90s, or so it feels. The standard script is: illusion and hallucination show that perception can go wrong, so there must be a common mental factor between seeing a tree and hallucinating one; disjunctivists deny the common factor and say hallucination isn't perception at all — it's just a mental misfire. But in every reading group I've been in, people agree on the brain scans, agree on the phenomenology, and still don't budge. Is this actually a disagreement about whether phenomenal charcter can be monodic, hidden behind the word 'perception'? Or are they answering diffrent questions — one about epistemology, one about the metaphysics of experience? I want a diagnosis with reasons: is there a hidden empirical question, a verbal dispute about 'seeing', or bedrock intuitions about what it's like? And if it's bedrock, is there anything — a distinction, a thought experiment, a result from another field — that could actually move it?
