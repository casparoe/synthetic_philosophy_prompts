I've been reading, mostly in fragments I found online, about the quest for the historical Jesus and the standard criteria of authenticity—multiple attestation, dissimilarity, coherence, embarrassment, and the rest. The idea always seems to be that if you apply these filters carefully, you can peel away the layers added by the early church and reach the actual person. But I also ran into a different way of thinking about tradition—maybe drawn from work on collective memory—according to which communities don't preserve a stable core and then decorate it; instead they actively reshape their past in response to present conflicts, theological needs, and social pressures. On that view, what looks like an "earlier" layer recovered by the criteria might actually be a strategic simplification invented later, and the very notion of peeling away accretions assumes a false "onion" model of transmission.

So here's my actual question. What's your credence—give me a specific number between 0 and 1—that the standard criteria could ever settle, even with hypothetically perfect textual and archaeological data, who the historical Jesus was? I don't want "it depends on your methodology"; I want the number, and I want it decomposed.

Break it down like this:

- First, what's the rough probability that the linear "onion" transmission model is approximately correct, versus the social-memory reshaping model being closer to how the traditions actually developed? And if the reshaping model is substantially right, what's the conditional probability that the criteria still retain genuine filtering power rather than just selecting for traditions that served later community needs?

- Second, separate the epistemic problem from the metaphysical one: assign a rough weight to how much of the difficulty is that we lack access to a real core, versus how much is that there may be no stable "historical Jesus" to access—just competing constructions produced by different memory communities with irreconcilable interests.

- Third, combine those into your overall credence, but also tell me: if your number turned out to be, say, 0.30 instead of 0.60, which of those sub-questions is actually doing the work? Which one would have to shift to move you most?

For what it's worth, my own instinct hovers around 0.35, but I'm genuinely unsure whether that low figure comes mainly from doubting the criteria's logic, or from a deeper suspicion that the target itself dissolves once you stop assuming there's a single recoverable person behind the texts. If you think I'm wrong about either part, show me where the decomposition breaks.
