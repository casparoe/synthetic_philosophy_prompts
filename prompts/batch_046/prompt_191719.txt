Design a diagnostic decision tree for the debate over representational realism in neural-network interpretability. The instrument should contain exactly six terminal leaves reached through at most five binary branching nodes, constructed from the logical space of commitments rather than from a list of named doctrines. The branching questions must turn on concrete cases and claims rather than on self-identification. (2) a concept that is encoded only in distributed population patterns with no single unit necessary or sufficient—does this count as representation? (3) an out-of-distribution input that preserves internal activation geometry yet produces an erroneous output—does this undermine the claim that the network represents the relevant feature?

Any combination that remains unoccupied should be noted with a brief diagnosis—whether it is incoherent, collapsible into a sibling branch, or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pairs of positions that the tree cannot cleanly separate, such as certain pragmatic-realist views versus pure structural-correspondence accounts, rather than inventing a false distinction.

Each answer option should be phrased in ordinary conceptual language, avoiding jargon that requires knowing a specific technical framework, so that a respondent is sorted by what they believe about the cases rather than by which label they prefer. Deliver the tree as: (a) the ordered branching questions with their binary options; (b) the path-to-leaf mapping that assigns each answer sequence to its corresponding position; (c) the unoccupied or collapsed nodes with short explanations; and (d) the flagged indistinguishable pairs with a note on why they coincide in this classification space. Keep the overall framing formal and suitable for use in a research workshop or advanced seminar on philosophy of artificial intelligence.
