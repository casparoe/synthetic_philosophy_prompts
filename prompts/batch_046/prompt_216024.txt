In Frege scholarship there's this weird stall about his late political diary — the antisemitic entries he wrote near the end — and whether any of it should change how we read his logicist writings. One camp says biography is totally seperate from logic, the other says if you're claiming objective truth then your personal bigotry can't be bracketed without explaining why. But nobody's moved in years, it's just the seperability thesis vs contamination view on repeat. My hunch is it's not really about Frege at all but about what 'bearing on philosophy' means — like, are we asking if the diary falsifies a premise, or if it undermines the authority of the system, or if it's just morally disgusting and we want distance? And then there's this other framing: treat it like contextual values in science, where the question isn't whether the diary changes the derivation but whether knowing it forces us to revise what 'objectivity' in logic was supposed to be doing. Is the stall because we're answering three different questions under one heading, or is there a hidden disagreement about whether logic can be value-laden? What would actually count as progress here — a distinction, a change of question, or should we just admit it's a moral reflex with philosophy makeup?
