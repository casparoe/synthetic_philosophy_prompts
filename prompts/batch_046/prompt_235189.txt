I'm prepping a lecture on archivall silence and I keep running into this framing: if the archive is structurally biased toward those with literacy and state access, then the absence of subaltern records isn't just missing data—it's active epistemic suppression. So far so standard. But then some students want to say that because of this, historians should stop treating archival documents as evidenciary and instead treat every document as a pure performance of power that tells us nothing about the past except about the archive itself. I think that's too strong—surely there's a middle ground where we read the grain of the archive itself to recover what's missing? Or is the very idea of a middle ground just liberal nostolgia for objectivity?
