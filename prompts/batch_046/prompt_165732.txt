I've been messing around with a weird synthesis: take the Platonia dilemma — do mathematical objects live in some abstract realm, or are they just useful fictions? — and mix it with the logic behind the Luring Lottery. What if mathematicians agreeing on a proof is basically superrational coordination? Everyone predicts the others will stick to shared axioms, so they all converge on the same theorem without communicating, and the "payout" isn't cash but just internal consistency. On this view math isn't describing a Platonic realm or being made up; it's a stable equilibrium in reasoning-space. But I'm pretty sure this is either unfalsifiable or collapses into psychologism. What would actually count against it? I want the decisive objections versus the merely awkward ones, what defenders of this kind of coordination-story should themselves concede is damaging, and whether insulating it with qualifications makes it useless — or if that's just what philosophical views do.
