I'm drafting the final examination for our MA seminar in philosophy and medicine (90 minutes, 25 marks). Ask candidates to defend or reject the claim that the earlier self's authority is undermined here not by metaphysical discontinuity but by an internal ambiguity in the directive's own architecture — a self-inflicted normative delegation that renders the instruction self-undermining. Provide a model answer of no more than 600 words and a marking scheme built around four must-hit analytical moves (marks totalling 25), specifying for each the precise conceptual distinction required and the one-line typical failure mode.
