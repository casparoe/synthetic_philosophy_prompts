I've been thinking about that movie Its a Wonderful Life where the angel shows George Bailey what Bedford Falls would be like if he was never born. I'm not in school or anything, just a regular person who reads random stuff, and I got curious about counterfactual history and whether history is inevitable or just fragile. I read somewhere about minimal-rewrite rules for counterfactuals - you change one thing and let everything else follow naturally - and also about overdetermination, where an outcome happens anyway through some other path. The movie feels like it breaks both ideas: George disappears and suddenly Potter runs the town and Mary is a librarian, which seems like a huge cascade from one change, but also it acts like George was the only thing stopping total collapse. I want to read the film through that philosphy of counterfactual history and inevitability - does it think one person can redirect everything, or does it secretly think Bedford Falls was always going to turn bad? Which scenes actually hold up if you apply the minimal-rewrite idea honestly, and where does the movie just cheat for drama? And does it ever consider that maybe someone else would have stopped Potter, or is George treated as irreplaceable? Don't need a reading list or citations, just a real take that treats the movie like it actually has something to say about how history works.
