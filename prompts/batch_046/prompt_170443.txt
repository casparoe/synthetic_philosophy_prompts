For a philosophy-of-physics seminar I have recently joined, I have been asked to write out my commitments on space, time, and motion so that the group can test their coherence. Here is my profile: (1) I take Newton’s bucket to establish that rotational motion is physically absolute rather than relational, so I reject Mach’s principle as an explanation of inertia. (2) I accept that general relativity shows spacetime to be dynamical and background-independent—there is no pre-existing container. (3) I hold a version of sophisticated substantivalism: spacetime points lack intrinsic identity, but the metric field and its geometric properties are ontologically real, which I take to dissolve the hole argument without retreating to relationism. (4) I maintain that singularities inside black holes are real boundary conditions of the metric field, not coordinate artifacts, despite not being part of the spacetime manifold itself. (5) At the Planck scale, I think spacetime is likely emergent from non-spatiotemporal relations—probably entanglement correlations—so that some form of relationism holds at the fundamental level, even if not classically. (6) My methodological rule is that ontological commitment should be reserved for entities indispensable in our best fundamental theory: if quantum gravity ultimately eliminates both spacetime points and the metric field, neither belongs in our final ontology.

Please audit this set. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension—rank them by severity, say what each side must surrender for consistency, and name the cheapest revision in each case. Identify which pairs only appear incompatible and explain why the tension dissolves on inspection. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised or abandoned, would resolve the most conflicts with the least damage to the overall view. I am not indicating which I am willing to drop; I want to see what my position actually commits me to.
