My roommate Jordan and her friend Alex got into this long arguement about that thought experiment from The Mind's I - you know, the one where they imagine Einsteins brain patterns written out in a huge book, page after page. Jordan said the book cant be conscious because its just dead symbols, like printing every frame of a movie on seperate pages with no projector running them. Even if you read them in order, theres no actual processing happening inside the object; its pure representation without a representer.

Alex said thats exactly the wrong take. If the encoding is complete - not just static neuron states but the transition rules between them - then going through the pages in sequence is functionally identical to running the brain program. Jordan pushed back and said okay, but then theres still no feedback loop. The book is predetermined; page 200 doesnt actually cause page 201, you just happen to turn the page. Alex said you could fix that mechanically: wire the pages in a loop with sensors so the state described on page 499 physically updates page 501. Then youve got recurrence without tissue. Jordan said fine, but consciousness needs a unified now - in your loop the present moment is scattered across space, part on page 499 and part on 501, not integrated in one temporal slice like in an actual brain.

Alex said thats just carbon chauvinism - neurons are scattered across cubic centimeters of tissue anyway, so spatial separation cant be the principled difference. Jordan came back with this idea she called temporal thickness: consciousness requires a present window, maybe 300-500 milliseconds, where past and future are co-active in one integrated slice. Your mechanical loop updates one discrete state at a time, so theres never that thick present, just an instantaneous slice. Alex said youre smuggling in mechanics; if the loop processes ten pages in parallel within that same 500ms window, via mechanical linkages, then youve recreated the thickness. The real issue, Alex said, is whether the sequence contains a self-model - Einsteins brain included states that functionally play the role of 'I am thinking this'. Jordan said thats wrong because in the book those are just descriptions of first-person thoughts printed on page 200. Theres an 'I' there but nobody actually meaning it - like a sign that says 'I am hungry' with no stomach behind it.

Can you go through each round and tell me whos right about which specific point? I dont want a overall winner - I want to know if Jordans temporal thickness objection actually lands, whether Alexs loop solves the feedback problem or just pushes it somewhere else, and whether either of them addresses what it means for the printed 'I' to lack an actual subject. Also what are they both missing - maybe something about the gap between functional equivalence and phenomenal experience?
