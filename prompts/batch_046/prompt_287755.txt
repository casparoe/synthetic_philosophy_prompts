Assume the mirror-phenomenology constraint holds: first-person access to phenomenal states and third-person predictive models of those states must exhibit perfect structural symmetry. Given this, if epiphenomenalism is correct and phenomenal properties have no causal efficacy, then there can be no causal mechanism generating erroneous phenomenal judgements. Does this not dissolve the paradox of phenomenal judgement? After all, if judgements about qualia are causally disconnected from the physical processes that produce them, error becomes impossible, and the paradox—which seems to presuppose that such judgements can be systematically misleading—collapses. Where is the flaw in this reasoning?
