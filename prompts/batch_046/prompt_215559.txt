You are grading a philosophy exam for an upper-level metaphysics course. The student was asked the following question:

Consider a crystal vase that is brittle by nature: if struck with sufficient force, its crystalline structure would cause it to fracture. However, the vase has been coated in a polymer that is inert under normal conditions but hardens instantly upon exposure to air. The coating is sealed under a thin layer of inert gas, so air only reaches it if the vase cracks. Is the vase fragile? In your answer, distinguish between finks and masks, apply the distinction to this case, and explain whether the presence of the polymer affects whether the vase genuinely possesses the disposition to break.

The student submitted the following answer (reproduced verbatim):

The vase is fragile, at least in its underlying structure, but the polymer complicates the assessment. Fragility is the disposition to shatter under stress. To analyze this, we must distinguish finks from masks. Martin's classic electro-fink case involves a wire that is conductive, but an attached device detects connection to a circuit and instantly renders it non-conductive—thereby removing the disposition exactly when the stimulus is applied. Johnston, by contrast, introduced masks: an antidote may prevent poison from killing, but the poison's toxic disposition remains intact; only its manifestation is blocked. In the present case, the polymer is initially a mask. The crystal structure is unchanged, and an external barrier blocks shattering. However, because the polymer activates precisely when air enters through a crack—i.e., at the very moment the disposition begins to manifest—it actually functions like a fink: it removes the disposition to break further at the instant of testing. So the vase lacks a genuine, unblocked disposition to break. Applying Lewis's simple conditional analysis, the counterfactual 'if the vase were struck, it would break' is false in the actual world, since the polymer is present and would intervene. Therefore, the vase is not truly fragile; it merely has the appearance of fragility because of its material composition.
