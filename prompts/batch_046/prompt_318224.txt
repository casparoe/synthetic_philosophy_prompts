I'm preparing a graduate seminar on metaphysics and I'm tired of circulating the same textbook examples of fragile vases and poison antidotes. Design a single, realistic thought experiment—no more than one paragraph—set in a biomedical fabrication workshop or industrial materials lab, centered on one macroscopic artifact: specifically, a shape-memory polymer composite scaffold that has been engineered with two simultaneous interference mechanisms. The first is an internal fink: a thermally activated microswitch embedded in the lattice that disrupts the crystallization pathway responsible for shape recovery whenever the temperature crosses a precise threshold. The second is an external mask: a pH-sensitive hydrogel coating that chemically suppresses that same crystallization pathway, but only when both temperature and pH exceed their respective thresholds together. The artifact is otherwise structurally intact and, in ordinary conditions, behaves like any other shape-memory material.

The scenario must be engineered so that three standard analyses of dispositions deliver clearly separated verdicts. First, the simple conditional analysis should classify the scaffold as genuinely possessing the disposition to recover its original shape, since in the absence of the interfering conditions the manifestation would occur. Second, a Lewis-style reformed analysis—appealing to the underlying causal basis—should classify it as lacking that disposition, on the grounds that the crystallization pathway is structurally compromised by the embedded microswitch and therefore cannot serve as a stable causal base. Third, a dispositional-essentialist or powers ontology should classify it as genuinely possessing the power, but only in a systematically masked or blocked mode rather than as a mere absence of the property. After presenting the case, demonstrate precisely why each theory delivers its verdict, isolating the structural feature—namely, the co-presence of an internal fink and an externally conditional mask—that forces the divergence. Then explain what happens if we remove that dual-interference feature: does the case collapse back into a standard fink or antidote example, and do the three theories reconverge? Finally, include an honest assessment of how a defender of the theory that fares worst—most plausibly the simple conditional analysis—would attack the scenario, whether by denying that both interferences can genuinely co-obtain, by arguing that the case illicitly conflates manifestation conditions with causal bases, or by some other line of resistance.

Please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want this analyzable from first principles by anyone without library access. Also, if the task as posed rests on a confused premise—for instance, if the simultaneous presence of a fink and a mask is conceptually incoherent, or if the three named theories cannot be cleanly separated on this particular artifact—say so plainly rather than accommodating the confusion politely.
