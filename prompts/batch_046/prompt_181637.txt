Take the 2015 Paris climate talks and analyze them through Jain anekāntavāda, specifically using syādvāda—the conditional predication idea—rather than just generic pluralism. I don't want a history of COP21, which is common knowlege: the developed/developing split, the INDC pledges, the 1.5 vs 2 degree framing, the loss and damage clause getting watered down. What I want is a disciplined mapping—treat each major negotiating bloc's position as a naya (a partial viewpoint) that is conditionally true. So: does the developed-world emphasis on mitigation capture one naya, and the developing-world emphasis on climate finance and adaptation capture another? Is the final agreement's ambiguity—the language about pursuing efforts toward 1.5C, voluntary pledges—actually an anekāntic synthesis that holds contradictory truths together, or is it just diplomatic vagueness? Where does the theory say the negotiators should have ended up, and does the fact that they landed on nationally determined contributions rather than binding targets confirm or break the anekānta model? And finally, does the Paris episode show that many-sided truth works for international politics, or that it collapses into relativism the moment you need enforceable action?
