I serve as a legislative review officer for a Swiss cantonal chancellery currently negotiating a new inter-cantonal concordat on alpine water-basin management, with potential cross-border dimensions touching EU environmental governance frameworks. My drafting team—lawyers with no specialized training in federal theory—must apply subsidiarity not only in its standard vertical form, allocating powers between the Confederation, the cantons, and municipalities, but also in its less commonly operationalized horizontal form: deciding whether a regulatory function within the concordat should sit with the joint inter-cantonal body or remain with the individual member cantons. I need a reusable, stand-alone decision procedure—usable within a two-hour review window and requiring no external research—that translates the theoretical commitments of subsidiarity (comparative institutional efficiency, democratic proximity, and the preservation of cantonal constitutional identity) into a sequential protocol of practical checks. The procedure should include explicit decision nodes, a small ordered set of test scenarios or sentences drafters can apply, and clear markers for the points where mechanical application must stop and irreducible professional judgment takes over.

The protocol must specifically handle the tension cases where vertical and horizontal subsidiarity conflict—for example, when centralizing a function within the joint body achieves greater regulatory efficiency and aligns with external EU standards, but only by displacing cantonal democratic processes that the constitutional subsidiarity principle protects. Include three worked applications drawn from plausible water-basin regulatory functions: first, a case where the procedure clearly assigns the function to the joint body; second, a case where it returns the function to individual cantons despite strong administrative pressure and likely political disappointment; and third, a case where the procedure reaches an indeterminate outcome that cannot be resolved without an explicit judgment call. For each application, show how the drafters run the tests in sequence. Additionally, specify which theoretical distinctions have been preserved in your simplification, which have been deliberately dropped for usability, and identify the specific conditions under which the procedure is most likely to misfire if applied without the flagged judgment pauses. The final deliverable should read as an internal drafting manual entry that a non-expert could apply repeatedly without consulting original philosophical or constitutional texts.
