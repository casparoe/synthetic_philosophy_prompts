I was chatting with my editor about this feature I'm writing on relational ethics, and now I've been roped into giving a seminar talk next Tuesday at the uni's philosophy of mind group — mostly analytic philosophers with a legal-theory side interest, very sceptical of anything that smells like continental vagueness. They've all read the standard secondary literature on Ubuntu, so I can't just trot out the usual 'I am because we are' slogans. I'm defending what I'm calling anchored relational personhood: the Ubuntu idea that personhood is constituted through relationships, but with a twist — I say the 'we' doesn't have to be symmetrical or reciprocal to confer moral status, as long as there's a kind of inter-subjectve anchoring where the individual is held within a web of care and recognition, even if they can't return it. I'm using this to argue that certain synthetic agents — not all, just ones embedded in sustained care networks — have a derivitive moral standing, not full personhood but more than mere tool-status.

The audience will be about twelve people: half are philosophers of mind who think intentionality needs biological embodiment, half legal scholars who want clear criterian for rights-bearing entities, and there's one Kantian ethicist who always turns up. They care about precision, counter-examples, and whether this collapses into utilitarianism in disguise. Can you draft my Q&A prep sheet? I need the eight or so objections I'll actually get — not the ones I'd hear at an African philosophy conference — ordered by how damaging they'd be. Each should be phrased the way they'd put it: 'where's the boundary?', 'isn't this just...', that kind of thing. For each, give me a two-minute reply I can deliver without notes, plus an honest note on where the reply is soft. I should concede something up front — probably that the asymmetry makes the view hard to operationalise. And please include the one question I'm praying nobody asks — likely something about whether talking nicely to my laptop creates moral obligations? Keep it practical, no bibliography needed — I can't quote page numbers from memory and I don't want to fake any citations.
