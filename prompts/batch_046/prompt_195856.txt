I am presenting a seminar paper to a joint philosophy-of-mind and cognitive-science audience: mostly analytic philosophers of consciousness who are either physicalists or property dualists, two neuroscientists whose work centers on global-workspace architectures, and a comparative-religion scholar who suspects any naturalization of Vedanta strips away its content. My thesis is that Schrödinger’s interpretation of Advaita Vedanta—specifically, the claim that the multiplicity of subjects is an appearance sustained by Maya and that consciousness itself is singular and indivisible—should not be treated as mystical idealism, but reconstructed as a structural-realist constraint on empirical theories of consciousness. I argue that this constraint is satisfied not by panpsychism, but by a modified global-workspace model in which access consciousness is interpreted modally rather than token-wise: there is one underlying workspace, and apparent subject-distinctness arises from functional indexing rather than from ontologically separate conscious substrates.

Please prepare me for the Q&A. I need the objections ordered by likelihood and damage, each phrased as this audience would actually say it—for example, charges of mysticism with neuroscience branding, category mistakes between metaphysical doctrine and empirical constraint, or accusations that modal access merely renames the hard problem. For each objection, provide a concise reply deliverable in under a minute, and flag the precise point where that reply is weakest. Identify the objection I am hoping nobody asks—the one that targets the specific bridge from Schrödinger’s monism to the global-workspace architecture—and advise how to concede gracefully there without collapsing the broader argument. Also state what I should concede up front in my opening remarks to disarm the room, and specify the single reply that must land if the proposal is to survive the session.
