I've been thinking about this view that AI art can't be realy creative because creativity isn't just about making something new — it requires what you might call phenomenological ownership of the process, where the maker actually lives through the generative act as part of their own intentional arc. Not just directing a tool, but being transformed by the making itself. So the position is that without that embodied, first-person cognitive-affective trajectory, you've got production but not creativity, no matter how surprising the output. What would have to be true for that to be wrong? I want the deep cuts — maybe evidence that human artists themselves don't always have that phenomenological unity, or that the intentional arc can be distributed across human-tool systems, or that we're smuggling in a romantic myth of the solitary genius. Sort them by how decisive they'd be, and include what defenders of the view would themselves concede is damaging, not just critics' talking points. Which ones are live rather than merely logical possibilities? And has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability — like if we just retreat to "well AI doesn't have the right kind of consciousness" — and is that a problem for something that's supposed to tell us about art rather than just consciousness?
