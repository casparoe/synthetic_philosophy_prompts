Consider the argument that the Pasadena paradox undermines expected-utility reasoning everywhere. (1) In the Pasadena game, payoffs are arranged so that the expectation of utility is undefined in a structural sense: the outcome-weighted series is conditionally but not absolutely convergent, and Riemann-style rearrangement yields every real value as a possible expectation, with no canonical ordering privileged by the problem itself. (2) If expected utility is genuinely undefined, then the principle of maximizing it issues no verdict, so rational choice in that game is paralyzed. (3) The standard reading of the paradox holds that any attempt to break the tie—chronological ordering, truncation, or imposing a bounded risk-averse scale—imports an arbitrary structure not present in the payoff rules, and is therefore illegitimate. (4) Now observe a distinction often overlooked: in Pasadena the undefinedness is structural (no admissible measure makes the integral well-defined), whereas in serious domains—long-horizon climate policy, existential-risk mitigation, or tail-risk finance—the analogous appearance of undefinedness is typically parametric (the integral is well-defined once a model, prior, or truncation is fixed, but we lack independent grounds for choosing among them). (5) If this distinction holds, then extending Pasadena-style paralysis to climate or risk policy commits a category error, treating parametric ignorance as structural non-integrability. (6) Yet the argument presses further: if we allow arbitrary resolutions in the parametric case—picking a discount rate, a risk-aversion coefficient, or a damage-function truncation—then by parity of reasoning we should allow them in the structural case, and the paradox's central challenge revives intact. (7) So either the structural/parametric distinction collapses and EU reasoning should indeed be abandoned for all deep-uncertainty policy, or the distinction stands and the paradox is quarantined to toy games—but then we need an account of why structural undefinedness is uniquely disqualifying, not merely inconvenient.
