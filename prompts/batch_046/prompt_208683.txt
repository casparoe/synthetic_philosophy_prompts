Question: Distinguish Nietzsche’s monumental, antiquarian, and critical modes of relating to the past, apply them to one contemporary institution, and discuss where they conflict. Is one mode necessarily dominant for a healthy culture?

Student answer:
Monumental history looks to great deeds and heroes to inspire present action; it risks falsifying the past by stripping away context. Antiquarian history reveres and preserves the past exactly, but because it values everything equally it tends to select only the most impressive relics for preservation, risking stagnation. Critical history judges and dissolves parts of the past that burden the present, freeing life to grow; Nietzsche regards it as the healthiest mode because culture must break from tradition more than it needs preservation or celebration. Applying this to a national museum: its heroic founding narrative is monumental; its climate-controlled storage of every donated object is antiquarian; and its recent restitution of colonial artifacts is critical. These three can coexist, but only if critical history is given priority, since Nietzsche believes life requires liberation from the past above all.
