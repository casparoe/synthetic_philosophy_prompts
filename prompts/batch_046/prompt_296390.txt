Right, I'm a retired chemist who's only just started taking philosophy seriously, and I've been reading this book by Hofstadter — Le Ton beau de Marot — about translating a little poem by Clément Marot while keeping all the formal bits intact: rhyme, metre, the lot. It got me thinking that the real philosophical knot isn't just "can you preserve meaning under constaint" but whether the constaint itself becomes part of the meaning. Like, if you translate a sonnet into a sonnet, you've kept the shape but maybe lost the shadow.

Anyway, I'd like a poem — maybe a sestina? — that takes this seriously. The form should be under some tight constraint (the sestina's repeating end-words, say) and the content should be about translating a poem where the rhyme scheme is part of what it's saying. Maybe write it in the voice of the translator arguing with themselves about whether preserving the rhyme is fidelity or betrayal. Make it acurate enough that someone who actually cares about translation theory wouldn't roll their eyes, but don't just name-drop Marot or Hofstadter — work the idea through the form. The repeating words should do some of the philosophical work, not just decorate it.

Oh, and please — no bibliography at the end, I'm not writing a paper! Just the poem.
