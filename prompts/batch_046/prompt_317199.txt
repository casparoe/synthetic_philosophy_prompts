I spent three decades in experimental condensed-matter physics before retiring and, somewhat to my own surprise, enrolling in a philosophy of science seminar. The instructors keep returning to string theory and cosmological multiverse proposals—eternal inflation producing bubble universes, the string landscape of Calabi-Yau compactifications—as cases where we might be asked to accept theories whose distinctive entities lie beyond any possible empirical access from our cosmic horizon. Trained to think that a physical theory earns its keep by predicting observable differences, I want to understand precisely where people stand on whether such theories can be confirmed, and on what grounds, when direct detection is in principle impossible.

Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on non-empirical confirmation specifically for multiverse and string-landscape claims. Begin with the root commitment: must every ontological posit of an accepted scientific theory be empirically accessible from our spatiotemporal location? From there, branch on whether mathematical uniqueness and structural consistency can serve as independent evidential support; on whether statistical predictions about the distribution of physical constants within our observable patch can substitute for direct observation; and on whether the distinction between horizontal multiverses (spatially separated bubble domains) and vertical multiverses (non-interacting vacuum states within one spacetime) changes the evidential calculus—specifically, whether confirmation for one type can carry over to the other. At each leaf, place a named or unnamed stance: include at least a hard empiricist rejection, a mathematical-structuralist acceptance relying on uniqueness arguments, a predictive-statistical view that accepts the multiverse contingent on solving the measure problem, and an "explanatory-dilution" skepticism that grants the formalism but denies it genuinely resolves fine-tuning. Mark any unoccupied combinations—such as accepting vertical multiverses on statistical grounds while rejecting horizontal ones on identical grounds—and briefly diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, particularly if they collapse into the same practical attitude toward theory choice. Frame every node as a concrete commitment an intelligent non-philosopher could answer without knowing the labels in advance, keeping the audience in mind: people who understand basic physics but not philosophy jargon. Finally, please indicate how confident you are in the completeness and accuracy of this taxonomy, and single out the specific branch point—likely the one involving the horizontal-vertical distinction—about which you are least certain.
