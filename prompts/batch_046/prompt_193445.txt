In discussions of political violence and conscientious refusal, the maxim “violence begets violence” is often invoked as if it settled the matter. I have seen it used to support empirical predictions about conflict escalation, to ground a virtue-ethical argument that coercion corrupts the agent regardless of success, and to advance a conceptual claim that treating another person as an obstacle to be eliminated is incompatible with recognizing their full moral status.

Can you lay out the distinct precise claims this slogan might express—distinguishing empirical, aretaic, and deontological readings—indicating for each whether it is a platitude, a defensible thesis, or false? Specify what kind of “begetting” is at issue (causal, moral-psychological, or logical) and whether each reading actually supports opposition to defensive war, revolutionary armed struggle, or state policing. Finally, say which reading is usually doing the work when someone deploys the phrase to shut down debate, and whether that reading survives scrutiny once separated from the others.
