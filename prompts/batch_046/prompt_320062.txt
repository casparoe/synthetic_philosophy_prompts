I've been thinking alot about that Gould and Lewontin spandrel thing — the idea that lots of biological traits are just architectural byproducts, not real adaptations shaped by selection. Basicaly I'm convinced the opposite: adaptationism still holds. Even when a trait looks like a useless leftover, like those weird bone spurs or silent gene regions, scientists eventually find some hidden function or show that selection kept it around through something like evolutionary capacitance — where a hidden trait gets released under stress — which still sounds selection-related to me. So my view is that if a trait is visible and widespread, it's almost certainly an adaptation or at least kept by selection, and calling it a spandrel is just admitting we haven't figured out the adaptive pipeline yet, maybe because of developmental bias locking the trait in place.

But I want to be honest about what could change my mind. What would actually count against the view that visible, widespread traits are basicaly all adaptations?
