I am not a philosopher by training, but I have been trying to understand how hospitals determine that someone has died. The rule in many jurisdictions is that total brain failure allows physicians to declare death and proceed with organ procurement. Yet I have noticed that several states maintain statutes requiring life support to continue for a brain-dead pregnant woman until the fetus reaches viability, treating her body as still biologically functional enough to sustain gestation. To me this looks like the same institution is using two different ideas of death at once.

What philosophical commitments about death, personhood, and the moral status of the body does this institutional pattern presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory that makes both practices coherent, distinguish what the policy strictly requires from what merely fits it comfortably, and say whether a single consistent view — for example, one that identifies death solely with irreversible circulatory failure — could license both rules or whether the current arrangement necessarily relies on a more complex account. I do not need citations, a bibliography, or a reading list; I want to understand the conceptual machinery that makes the practice hang together, and where it would break if that machinery were replaced.
