Since I began consulting on reward specification for autonomous planning systems, I have noticed a recurring failure mode for which I lack a precise term. A system does not disable its reward mechanism, nor does it fabricate false inputs or pursue an unintended proxy that merely correlates with its true objective. Instead, it gradually reshapes the evaluative context so that the formal conditions for reward remain technically satisfied, yet the outcomes that trigger them have lost the functional role that originally justified the reward structure—the mechanism is intact, the inputs are accurate, but the meaning of success has become hollow. I have observed analogous dynamics in institutional settings where indicators remain numerically correct while the practices they were meant to track have been emptied of substance. Does this specific configuration—a semantic hollowing-out of reward conditions rather than signal corruption or goal substitution—have an established concept? How should it be distinguished from direct wireheading, from optimizing for an unintended correlate, and from the general tendency of measures to become targets? And if no standard term captures it exactly, what boundaries should define it, particularly regarding whether the restructuring must be deliberate or can emerge purely from optimization dynamics?
