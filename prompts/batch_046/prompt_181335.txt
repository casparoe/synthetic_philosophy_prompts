I'm taking a graduate seminar on research ethics and have been arguing with a friend about what makes consent genuinely valid rather than merely procedural. My advisor—who works in clinical and research ethics but avoids explicit theoretical labels—has offered intuitive verdicts on four cases we've discussed. First, she held that consent was fully valid in a pharmaceutical trial that explained every physical risk, used no hidden measures, recruited fully competent adults, and offered only modest travel reimbursement. Second, she said consent was invalid in a psychology study that concealed exposure to graphic images capable of triggering severe distress, even though it disclosed the general topic and included full debriefing. Third, she rejected consent in a study of early-stage Alzheimer's patients who could repeat the study's purpose in simplified language but could not grasp that their data would be shared with third-party insurers. Fourth, she judged consent invalid in a fully transparent survey on political attitudes that offered four hundred dollars to unemployed participants for a two-hour session, where withdrawal was technically permitted but practically impossible given their financial situation; there was no deception involved.

Next week we are discussing a new proposal: a neurofeedback study recruiting competent, financially secure adults. It fully discloses all physical and psychological risks, permits withdrawal at any time without penalty, offers no extraordinary compensation, and involves no hidden physical procedures. However, it tells participants that the study measures attention span during a video task, when in fact it measures subliminal emotional responses to embedded stimuli; every participant is debriefed immediately afterward. Please predict whether my advisor would judge this consent valid or invalid. More importantly, identify the implicit principle that best explains her four earlier verdicts, lay out at least one competing principle that accounts for those same four verdicts equally well, and specify the exact further hypothetical case that would best distinguish between the two principles. Write your response in American English, and please do not provide bibliographies, author citations, or reading lists—I am interested in the conceptual structure alone.
