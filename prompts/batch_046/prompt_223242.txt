Assume the role of a senior political theorist specializing in comparative conceptual analysis and the institutional semantics of normative political terms. Before constructing the instrument, please spend a brief moment brainstorming: identify the three or four deepest conceptual fault lines that cause the term "liberal" to function as near-opposites in American and European—particularly German and post-1989 Central European—discourse, paying special attention to whether the divergence is lexical, institutional, or normative, and noting any combinations that seem logically available but are rarely defended.

Context: I have recently relocated from the United States to Germany for doctoral study in political theory. In American academic and activist circles, "liberal" typically signals support for an expansive welfare state, identity-based anti-discrimination frameworks, and judicial protection of minority rights against democratic majorities. In my German seminars, by contrast, colleagues who identify with liberal party traditions often defend constitutional property guarantees, market competition as a liberty-preserving mechanism, and skepticism toward redistributive expansion—positions that American progressives would classify as conservative or libertarian. Meanwhile, I have observed that in several post-communist contexts the term was adopted by parties championing rapid marketization and EU integration, producing a semantic inversion relative to American usage that complicates any simple Atlantic comparison.

I need a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a branching decision tree with explicit branch labels—that classifies an interlocutor’s actual conceptual commitment rather than their self-applied label. The tree should be built first from the logical space of commitments, then mapped onto named positions, with unoccupied combinations noted.

Requirements:
- Root node: present a concrete institutional dilemma that forces a choice. For example: "A constitutional court strikes down a progressive wealth tax on property-rights grounds; the legislature then proposes universal basic services funded by consumption taxes. Is your primary evaluation that liberal principles have been protected, violated, or are institutionally irrelevant here?"
- Each internal node must pose one concrete claim or case on which positions genuinely diverge, phrased so a respondent can answer without knowing which leaf it produces.
- The branching logic must turn on: (1) whether "liberal" denotes primarily a constraint on state power or an enabling condition for individual autonomy; (2) whether market outcomes are treated as presumptively legitimate or as requiring systematic correction; (3) whether democratic majoritarianism is seen as the primary threat to liberty or as its legitimate expression; and (4) whether in post-communist institutional contexts the term has retained normative content or collapsed into a descriptor for EU-integration-plus-market-reform agendas.
- Leaves must include: American progressive liberalism; American libertarian or classical liberalism; European social-market liberalism (including ordoliberal-influenced variants); European social-democratic or welfare-liberal usage (British-derived traditions); and at least one "unnamed" combination. For every unnamed leaf, provide a one-sentence diagnosis of whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored—specifically addressing the combination that pairs strong commitments to market-correction with deep skepticism about judicial review of economic legislation.
- Wherever two actual positions fall on the same leaf, flag them explicitly and indicate which additional diagnostic case could in principle separate them but is excluded from this tree to keep the instrument at four branching levels or fewer.
- Keep the tree to six or fewer leaves and four or fewer branching levels; the intended audience is an advanced graduate seminar in comparative political theory.
- Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote any specific text verbatim. Do not ask the model to identify obscure party documents or regional statutes.

Please construct the tree, present the branch labels and leaf descriptions, note any unoccupied logical spaces with brief diagnoses, flag inseparable pairs, and conclude with a short reflection on which cross-Atlantic misunderstandings this instrument is designed to expose and which ones it deliberately leaves unresolved.
