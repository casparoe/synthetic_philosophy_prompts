For a workshop on metaphysics and the philosophy of mind, I am drafting a position on Bradley's regress, propositional unity, and the ontology of judgment, and I want an internal consistency audit before I present. Here are my commitments.

(1) I accept Bradley's regress argument in its standard form: if a relation R is genuinely distinct from and external to its relata a and b, then linking R to a and R to b requires further relations, generating an infinite regress; therefore, for a proposition to be a genuinely unified whole rather than a mere aggregate or list, relations cannot be fully external to the terms they relate.

(2) I reject both Russell's external-relations solution (which I take to abandon genuine unity) and Bradley's own absolute monism; instead, I hold that propositional unity is achieved through a form of "adverbial structuralism," according to which the proposition is a single articulated complex in which properties directly modify objects, and the apparent relational structure is an abstraction from that unitary state rather than an independent constituent.

(3) I maintain that false propositions are fully propositional in exactly the same sense as true ones: they possess complete structural unity, and falsehood is never a deficiency in internal binding but always a failure of correspondence between the unified complex and extra-propositional reality.

(4) I hold that propositions are not mind-independent Platonic entities but are "constructed unities" that depend essentially on an act of judgment, linguistic articulation, or cognitive synthesis; in the absence of such an act, there is no proposition, only a scattered plurality of entities.

(5) I restrict the internal-relations thesis strictly to the internal architecture of a proposition: causal, spatial, and temporal relations in the world at large remain fully external, so that altering my spatial relation to a distant object changes neither me nor that object.

(6) I also hold that in vague or borderline judgments—such as judging whether a collection of exactly 847 grains constitutes a heap—the achieved propositional unity is itself graded or partial, and this gradation reflects a feature of the determinacy of the constructing act rather than a metaphysical deficiency in the relata themselves.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve once the distinctions are made explicit, and what is the least costly single revision that would restore coherence? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally problematic, specify for each what must be revised and at what philosophical cost, and say whether the deepest problem lies in my ontology of relations, in my account of falsehood and vagueness, or in the constructivist framework itself. I have not decided in advance which commitment I would surrender.
