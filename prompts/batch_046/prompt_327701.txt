I am a third-year student in a jurisprudence seminar on legal self-reference and normative hierarchies. For our 90-minute final, please draft one essay question that forces examinees past summarizing standard accounts of constitutional amendment paradoxes. Center the prompt on a hypothetical framework I will call the Recursive Amendment Protocol: Article 9 grants the legislature power to revise any procedural rule except the rules governing amendment itself, while Article 11—added later—permits revision of Article 9 provided the revising body includes at least one member who participated in ratifying Article 11. Ask students to determine whether this generates a genuine logical paradox of self-reference comparable to Alf Ross’s constitutional paradox, whether it collapses into a Kelsen-style hierarchical relocation of authority, or whether it produces only a practical infinite regress without logical contradiction. They must defend one diagnosis, explain why the other two fail, and specifically address whether the reflexivity here is vicious or benign. Provide a model answer of roughly 700 words and a marking scheme with five must-hit analytical moves totaling 25 marks; for each move, note the one-line misconception that should cost credit—especially conflating institutional deadlock with logical contradiction, and treating a basic norm as eliminating self-reference rather than displacing it downward. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or specific page references, and keep the question answerable without outside research.
