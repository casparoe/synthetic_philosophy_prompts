The tree should terminate in named positions or in explicitly marked unnamed combinations. For every branch, phrase the node as a yes-or-no or choose-one question that a student could answer based on their intuitive or reasoned response to the case, without knowing in advance which interpretation it selects. Build the tree from the logical space of answers first, then locate the named positions within it; identify any combination of answers that corresponds to no defended view, with a brief diagnosis of whether that gap is due to incoherence, instability, or mere neglect. Where two named positions land on the same leaf, flag them as inseparable by this instrument. Conclude with a short scoring key that maps a reader's path through the tree onto what they actually believe about the four cases, rather than onto which term they prefer. Keep the tree compact—no more than six leaves—including at least one genuinely unoccupied branch.
