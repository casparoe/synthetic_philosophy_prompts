In my comparative phil grp we've been stuck on Gongsun Long's white horse argument for months. Half the room treats it like a logic puzzle about universals -- white and horse are separate properties, so the compound doesn't match the universal. The other half insists it's legalistic wordplay from Warring States tax codes, not ontology at all. A few people say it's just rhetorical sophistry. Nobody moves. My hunch is we're not disagreeing about one thing: some of us are asking what the School of Names meant by 'is', others are asking whether the argument is valid, and others care about the political context. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real hidden question about how Chinese philosophy categorizes color vs kind. Is this stall verbal, substantive, or just people answering different questions under one heading? And what -- a conceptual distinction, a historical fact, or a change in framing -- could actually break it?
