I'm pitching a four-month solo project on Bradley's regress and the unity of the propsoition. The standard story is familiar: if R relates a and b, then R needs something to relate it to them, and we're off to the races. The usual fixes—Russell-style external relations, treating relations as primitive, or modern grounding moves—all seem to address the same single problem. But I think there's a hidden split between two regresses that everyone runs together. One is about propositional unity: how the parts of a judgement form one single content. The other is about relational instantiation: how a relation actually connects its terms rather than sitting next to them. Most answers in the literture solve one version while quietly assuming the other away, which is why the debate never quite dies. I want to separate them precisely, then check which classic and contemporary responses actually stop which regress, and where they leave the other one running. I need this turned into a proper proposal: the exact question stated sharply, why the existing positions don't settle it, what I'd do month by month (mostly conceptual analysis, testing solutions against both versions), what a useful result looks like—probably a diagnostic map showing which answers cover which targets—and what would count as failure (probably if the two regresses collapse into one after all). Keep the scope realistic for a small unfunded fellowship, and don't include citations, I'll fill those in myself.
