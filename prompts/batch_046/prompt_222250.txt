I have been trying to reconcile Weber’s thesis that the Protestant ethic contributed causally to the emergence of modern capitalism with the claim that any genuine causal explanation must identify mechanisms supporting counterfactual intervention, as formalized in structural causal models. Weber characterizes the relationship between ascetic Protestantism and capitalist development in terms of an elective affinity rather than deterministic law, yet if cultural values are not externally manipulable variables—there is no do-operator that could excise the ethic while holding all other conditions constant—then they appear to lack the modal robustness required for efficient causation. Given this tension, should we read Weber’s argument as merely asserting a historically contingent correlation, or is there a coherent account of value-based explanation that does not depend on interventionist criteria? More specifically, if explaining with values is irreducible to physical causation, what distinguishes Weber’s claim from a post hoc narrative that selects compatible cultural traits only after observing the economic outcome?
