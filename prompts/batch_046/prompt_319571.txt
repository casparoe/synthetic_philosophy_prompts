I've come around to a pretty hardline take on the singleton hypothesis. Not the weak version where one agent just happens to win, but the claim that distributed agency is conceptually unstable at technological maturity - like, once you hit recursive self-improvement, any seperate decision-center either gets absorbed or becomes a dependency, so true multi-polarity is like trying to maintain two currencies with perfect exchange-rate stability but no central bank. It just collapses. What would have to be true for this to be wrong? I want the actual defeaters sorted by how decisive they'd be - fatal versus just awkward. Is there a coherent model of stable value-pluralism under recursive improvement, or do all such models smuggle in hidden coordination? And be honest: has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, where any surviving diversity gets dismissed as pre-maturity noise? If so, is that insulation actually a vice for a claim that pretends to be structural, or is it okay for predictions of this kind?
