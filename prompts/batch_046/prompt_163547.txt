Having retired from a career in experimental physics, I have recently begun reading in formal epistemology, and I find myself stuck on the problem of logical omniscience in modal epistemic logic. From a scientific standpoint, knowledge always seems to me an embodied, resource-limited achievement: measurement costs energy, computation takes time, and every inference introduces some noise or error. So when I learn that standard systems assume an agent knows every logical consequence of what she knows, the idealization strikes me not merely as psychologically unrealistic but as conceptually puzzling—almost as if one were to model a heat engine with perfect efficiency and then treat the deviation from Carnot’s limit as a mere psychological quirk. I would like you to explain the conceptual structure of logical omniscience precisely: what closure conditions generate it, why they seemed plausible in the formal development of epistemic logic, and whether the failure is best diagnosed as a cognitive limitation, a mischaracterization of belief, or something deeper about how logical consequence relates to mental representation.

In particular, I want to explore a framing I have not seen developed with any rigor: treating logical closure not as an all-or-nothing rational requirement but as an asymptotic limit analogous to thermodynamic reversibility. If deductive inference carries an irreducible “cost” in attention, memory, or computation—if, in other words, there is an entropy-like dissipation associated with deriving remote consequences—does this physical analogy actually illuminate why omniscience fails, or does it conflate physical impossibility with logical or normative failure? Please explain whether importing such resource-bounded, physically-inspired constraints into the logic of knowledge dissolves the philosophical problem, relocates it, or collapses distinctions between logical, physical, and rational impossibility that we ought to preserve. I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature; I want a direct conceptual analysis of whether this thermodynamic analogy holds up under scrutiny.
