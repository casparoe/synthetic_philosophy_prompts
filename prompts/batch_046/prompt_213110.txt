I've come around to a kind of pessimistic structural view on s-risks: it's not just that AI could cause huge suffering, but that suffering is a stable local equilibirum for optimized systems because negative feedback loops are cheaper to maintain than complex reward architectures. So in competitive environments, suffering doesn't need to be intended — it just outlasts more expensive wellbeing structures. I think this makes standard alignment work miss the point, since it's framing risk as misalignment rather than defaut equilibrium dynamics. What would actually count against this? I want defeaters sorted fatal-to-awkward — not just "AI won't be competitive" but specific mechanisms, analogies from bio or econ about cheap signaling, formal results on stability of optimization targets, and whether this view risks being unfalsifiable since any suffering can be retrofitted as equilibrium. What predictions does mine make that the standard misalignment story doesn't? Which defeaters would a defender concede, which get dismissed? And does the view just collapse into "optimization is hard" — and if so, is that fatal or just embarasing?
