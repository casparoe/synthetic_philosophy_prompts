simone weil wrote that political parties are basicly machines for manufacturing collective passion and crushing individual judgement - she wanted to abolish them all. she also thought real politics should come from individual conscience and direct human contact, not organizational machinery.

so here's a weird modern thing that only came up recently: algorithmic "parties" that don't have members or platforms in the traditional sense, just recommendation feeds and micro-targeting loops that coordinate people around vague ideological brands. there's no central committee manufacturing the passion - it's just gradient descent surfacing anger or hope to keep engagement up. people don't join; they're sorted.

what would Weil have said? are these even parties in her sense, or something worse? and does her critique land differently when there's no human organizer behind the machinery, just an optimization process? i'm especially curious whether she would have seen the absence of a human will as making it more dangerous, or actually less culpable.
