Here is the student's response. (a) The partition model resolves the contradiction cleanly. Logical consistency is a property of individual doxastic systems, not of aggregates of subsystems. Since the partitioned modules are causally isolated and lack cross-system entailment relations, there is no logical contradiction at all—only a causal conflict between outputs. The agent as a whole does not literally believe both p and not-p; rather, different parts of the cognitive economy register different states, much as a computer can run incompatible processes without logical error. The suppressed subsystem does not remain perfectly silent; its outputs reach the dominant system in a distorted, non-propositional form—something like affective noise or vague unease. The agent experiences this leakage not as explicit knowledge of p, but as an unspecified sense that something is being evaded. The dominant system interprets this noise as an internal disturbance rather than as content-bearing information, which preserves the partition while producing the characteristic feeling of strategic opacity. Because this filtering occurs prior to belief formation, it avoids the paradox: the agent never deliberately chooses to believe not-p, but merely directs cognitive resources away from evidence that would activate the suppressed module.
