Evaluate this criticism. This differs from negative hedonism, which holds that pleasure is intrinsically good but that suffering is more strongly bad—so negative hedonism still values pleasure for its own sake, while tranquilism values neither pleasure nor knowledge except instrumentally. Regarding the sated sage: if all craving is permanently eliminated, the sage lives in pure neutral equilibrium, which is the highest good because there is no suffering and no craving. Therefore, her life is positively good, not just non-bad, because the absence itself is the maximal realization of value. As for artistic creation, the symphony composer who works in a state of flow is not experiencing craving—she is fully absorbed—so her activity is valuable precisely because it manifests the absence of craving in action. Thus tranquilism does not collapse artistic value but explains it as an expression of craving-free consciousness. The preference-satisfaction objection fails because tranquilism is not about satisfying preferences but about the objective state of being free from desire; a preference for art is only valuable if it leads to that state.
