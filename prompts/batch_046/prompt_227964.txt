Compare the concept of aesthetic testimony with the autonomy thesis that aesthetic judgment requires first-person experiential grounding. In particular, examine whether these positions can be reconciled given the following tension: if aesthetic properties are response-dependent, then testimony may function only as an invitation to experience rather than as a genuine transmission of knowledge, yet if they are mind-independent, then first-person experience loses its privileged epistemic role. Defend a clear conclusion about whether aesthetic testimony and experiential autonomy are ultimately compatible or mutually exclusive.
