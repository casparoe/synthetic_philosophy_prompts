I'm on the committee drafting the final exam for our upper-level seminar in aesthetics and critical philosophy of race. We need one 60-minute essay question, together with a model answer and a marking scheme, on W. E. B. Du Bois's claim that art functions as propaganda—particularly as developed in his essay on the criteria of Negro art. The prompt must not be answerable by summary alone. Its crux should be the following conceptual tension: Du Bois appears to treat propaganda as both an unavoidable descriptive feature of all artistic production and as a normative demand specifically placed upon Black artists. A strong answer must show how these two registers interact without collapsing into (i) a reductive instrumentalism that dissolves aesthetic value into political efficacy, or (ii) an empty tautology in which good art is simply defined as art that serves justice. The answer should also deploy resources from Du Bois's broader philosophy—especially his analysis of double consciousness and the veil—to explain why white aesthetic norms are themselves a concealed form of propaganda, thereby blocking the charge that his normative standard presupposes a neutral, non-propagandistic standpoint. To make the problem concrete, ask candidates to address a hypothetical interlocutor who objects that Du Bois cannot consistently praise technically masterful European works that he acknowledges are beautiful yet politically retrograde; if all art is propaganda, the interlocutor asks, why is retrograde beauty not simply bad art?

Please draft: (a) the exam question itself, specifying 60 minutes and an expected length of about 1,000 words; (b) a model answer of roughly 800 words that makes the required conceptual moves explicit; and (c) a marking scheme built around five must-hit points, each worth 4 marks (total 20), with a one-line note on the characteristic failure mode that should cost marks on each point. The difficulty should come from identifying the crux: a student who has merely memorized the slogan that art is propaganda should be unable to earn a passing mark. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
