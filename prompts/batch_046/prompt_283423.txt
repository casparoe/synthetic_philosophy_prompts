I've been preparing a long-form piece on the hole argument in general relativity—the puzzle Einstein raised when he noticed that generally covariant field equations appear to allow multiple metric configurations over the same "hole" region of a manifold, identical everywhere outside but diverging inside, which seems to make the theory physically indeterministic unless we declare diffeomorphically related models to represent the same state. The standard philosophical reading, revived in debates from the 1980s onward, casts this as a decisive blow to manifold substantivalism, the view that spacetime points are real entities with intrinsic identity independent of the fields they carry; relationalists have long treated the argument as showing that only the relational structure of fields and events matters, not the underlying point-set.

But I think the usual framing—substantivalism versus relationism—obscures a more interesting and less settled controversy that I want to explore for my readers. Even granting Leibniz equivalence, the identification of diffeomorphic spacetimes might reflect nothing more than a gauge symmetry rather than an ontological elimination of points, leaving room for a structural substantivalism that treats points as real but only relationally individuated. Furthermore, the argument's force seems to depend on global smoothness: if we restrict attention to a localized hole causally isolated from any observer, or if we move to discrete or emergent spacetime frameworks—such as causal-set or loop-quantum-gravity-inspired structures—where no continuous diffeomorphism group operates, the standard resolution loses its grip. Does the hole argument then fail, or does it transform into a different kind of indeterminacy? And if discrete structures avoid the argument altogether, is that a virtue of the discrete approach or a sign that it sidesteps a genuine conceptual problem?

For the essay I am commissioning, I want you to take a clear position. Weigh whether the hole argument genuinely undermines manifold substantivalism or merely establishes gauge equivalence, examine whether a localized or discrete variant strengthens or weakens the anti-substantivalist case, and defend a specific verdict—perhaps that the argument succeeds only against a particularly naive form of point realism, or that it collapses without the continuum assumption—justifying your conclusion with conceptual analysis rather than historical citation.
