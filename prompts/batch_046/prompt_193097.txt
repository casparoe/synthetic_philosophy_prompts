Write a short, philosophically rigorous dialogue between two equally capable interlocutors—call them Mara and Elias—on whether origin essentialism applies to personal identity rather than merely to organisms. Mara should defend the view that you necessarily come from the particular sperm and egg that actually formed your zygote: she should argue that personal identity tracks the continuous life of a specific biological organism, so even a perfect psychological duplicate developed from different gametes in an artificial womb would be a different person. Elias should defend a modified psychological-continuity view: he should grant that organisms may be origin-essential, but argue that personal identity depends on the continuity of consciousness, memory, and social relations, not on genetic provenance. To ground the dispute, have Elias propose a concrete hypothetical in which your zygote is replaced at the one-cell stage by a synthetic genome constructed from different parental DNA, yet the resulting brain develops your exact psychological profile; Mara should respond by questioning whether psychological properties can genuinely survive such a total break in biological provenance, and Elias should reply by challenging her assumption that psychological continuity supervenes on that specific initial substrate. Ensure both respond to each other’s actual arguments rather than talking past one another, avoid strawmen, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading.
