I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of science, presently examining the limits of epistemic modesty when applied to first-person philosophical reasoning. In a recent graduate seminar on decision-theoretic humility, a colleague proposed what she termed the outside-view imperialism thesis: that any philosophical judgment formed from the inside—whether about modal intuition, normative weight, or conceptual analysis—should be discounted in proportion to the base rate of error in one's reference class. Our discussion turned to whether this form of modesty collapses into a performative contradiction, since the very judgment that one ought to defer is itself an inside-view assessment. I should like you to write a sustained satirical piece of approximately 450 words, composed in the deadpan voice of a corporate consultancy brochure or self-help manual, parodying the practice of applying reference-class forecasting to philosophical reasoning itself. The target should be the discourse that treats epistemic modesty as a universal solvent, dissolving even the cognitive agency required to interpret the dissolution, rather than any named individual. To ensure the humour arises from the actual content rather than caricature, please exaggerate a genuine tension within the inside-view/outside-view distinction: namely, that radical deference to base rates undermines the capacity to evaluate those rates. You may ground the parody with one invented technical apparatus—perhaps a Reference-Class Taxonomy for A Priori Intuitions that sorts philosophical judgments by disciplinary citation networks and geographic origin of the thinker—so that the satire reads as an internal reductio rather than an external mockery. Maintain sustained irony throughout; the reader should recognise that the manual's prescriptions are self-defeating without the author breaking character. I have no need for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and British spelling conventions are preferred.
