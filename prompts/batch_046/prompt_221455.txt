Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on Otto Neurath's picture-language (ISOTYPE) programme.

Camp A is the pictographic universalist: they hold that Neurath's anti-metaphysical, theory-neutral visual protocol can be rigorously extended to represent not only positive observational findings but also absence, negation, and modal relations, without importing hidden ontological commitments.

Camp B is the semantic critic: they share Neurath's rejection of metaphysical pseudo-statements and grant ISOTYPE's utility for straightforward empirical reporting, but they maintain that any extension to represent absence, counterfactuals, or theoretical entities necessarily smuggles in the very causal and ontological commitments Neurath sought to eliminate.

Write the document they should have produced after sustained, good-faith collaboration. Structure it as follows:

First, a numbered section of agreed claims—covering Neurath's original aims, the formal structure of ISOTYPE, the specific problem of representing absence or negation in purely pictorial media, and what both sides take to be at stake for a unified scientific language.

Second, a disagreements table. For each camp, state the precise formulation of its own position in wording that side would accept, with the other side's position phrased neutrally enough that neither objects to the framing. Distinguish genuine disagreements from merely verbal ones if any emerge.

Third, identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party. I expect one crux is whether a standardised negative icon (for example, a crossed-out symbol or an empty frame) operates as a theory-neutral syntactic device or as an implicitly theoretical claim about non-existence; another is whether the combinatorial rules governing icon arrangement are genuinely less theory-laden than the grammatical rules of verbal scientific languages.

Fourth, for each crux specify a concrete case, argument, or comparative exercise both sides agree bears on it, together with each camp's explicit prediction. For instance, both might accept that attempting to construct parallel ISOTYPE manuals for quantum field theory and for classical mechanics is a relevant test; record what each predicts about whether the same icon-set and combinatorial rules will apply across both domains.

Keep the response focused, avoid bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and write in British English. Adopt the tone of a long-time rationalist-forum reader—formal, precise, at ease with philosophy-of-science vocabulary, but not needlessly academic.
