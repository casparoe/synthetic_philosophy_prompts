Specify the desiderata that any adequate theory of embedded rationality must satisfy. By embedded, I mean that the agent is physically constituted by and causally continuous with the environment it seeks to model and manipulate, so that no fully detached, god's-eye representation is available. Your specification should cover: (1) the phenomena it must explain—such as an agent revising its own reasoning architecture, or distinguishing its internal state from an isomorphic external system; (2) the conceptual distinctions it must preserve—particularly between epistemic error about the world and practical failure arising from the agent's own implementation; (3) formal constraints it cannot violate, including those arising from computation and self-reference; and (4) a condition I will call indexical embedding—the agent's causal position in the world is not representable as one more object-level datum inside its model, and the theory must accommodate this without collapsing it into ordinary indexical belief. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or argument. Conclude by identifying which single requirement does the most work in excluding popular formal frameworks, and indicate whether your list contains any jointly unsatisfiable subset—if so, present that tension as itself a substantive result.
