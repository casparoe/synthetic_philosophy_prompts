Applying Wittgenstein's distinction between doubt within a system and doubt about the system itself, explain which elements of her inquiry should be treated as hinges and which as movable parts. Wittgenstein's hinges are the background propositions that stand fast and make particular doubts possible. They are not themselves justified by other beliefs; rather, they form the framework of our language-games. In this case, the researcher's general trust in physical measurement and in the comparability of datasets are hinges—like the bedrock of a river—while the specific calibration protocol and the anomalous reading are the shifting sand.

This means hinges function like foundational basic beliefs: they provide the epistemic foundation that justifies the researcher's particular inferences about temperature trends. Without this foundational layer, she could not justify checking one dataset against another. So she should preserve her hinge commitments intact and treat only the surface-level data as revisable.

Wittgenstein's riverbed metaphor shows that the hard rock never moves, only the sand does. Consequently, her hinge commitments are immune to revision in principle. Even if anomalies accumulate over decades, she can never rationally come to doubt that her instruments are fundamentally reliable or that cross-temporal comparison is legitimate. The correct response is therefore to preserve both hinge commitments and explain the anomaly away—perhaps by attributing it to local noise—without ever allowing the investigation to reach the level of the baseline comparison or instrument design.
