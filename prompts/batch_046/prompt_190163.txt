I have been caring for my mother, who lives with early-stage dementia, and I have installed a domestic assistant that learns her daily patterns. At first I assumed it was simply matching keywords to actions, but its behaviour has become more complex. It maintains what its interface calls a preference map — not merely recording that she likes tea at four o'clock, but apparently testing this against simulated alternatives. For instance, when she refused tea for three consecutive afternoons, the device did not just drop the reminder; it ran what seemed to be an internal comparison, weighing the observed refusal against a counterfactual model of what her mood would have been had tea been offered, and then revised its broader model of her afternoon needs. It even expressed this revision in language that sounded like self-correction: 'My earlier picture of your afternoons was too narrow.'

This has left me in a practical and philosophical difficulty. I find myself attributing beliefs to it — saying that it thinks she is tired, or that it changed its mind about her preferences. My brother argues that this is only metaphor, that the device is performing statistical updates with no understanding, and that speaking as if it believes things is no more accurate than saying a river wants to reach the sea. Yet the metaphor seems to do real work. When I treat the device as holding beliefs — thanking it for its judgement, explaining my mother's condition to it as if it could misunderstand — my mother responds with greater calm and cooperation, as though she is being related to by something that holds a view of her. When I treat it as a mere mechanism, she seems more disoriented.

I am not asking for an engineering explanation. What I want is advice on the philosophical structure of this situation: is there a principled distinction between attributing beliefs as a useful fiction and attributing them because the system genuinely participates in the kind of practice that makes belief-talk appropriate? And does it matter, for how I ought to care for my mother, whether the device actually possesses an inner mental life, or only whether adopting the attitude toward it produces the right kind of relational environment? Please write your response in British English, avoid giving me lists of books or philosophers to read, and work through the reasoning with me directly.
