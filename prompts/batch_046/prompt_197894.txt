If Quine’s thesis of the underdetermination of theory by evidence establishes that any scientific framework can be preserved from refutation by revising its auxiliary hypotheses, then climate attribution studies—which rely on parameterized adjustments to cloud feedback and aerosol forcing—ought to be able to accommodate any temperature record without being forced to accept anthropogenic warming. Does this not mean that selecting one model over empirically equivalent rivals is merely a conventional choice, and that the claim of human-caused climate change is therefore not objectively compelled by the data? Answer in exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: distinguish global from local underdetermination; explain why auxiliary adjustments are constrained by independent predictions and consilience; identify the false presupposition that parameterization equals unrestricted ad hoc saving; show how multi-dataset predictive success breaks empirical symmetry; and clarify whether this resolves the philosophical challenge or only relocates it.
