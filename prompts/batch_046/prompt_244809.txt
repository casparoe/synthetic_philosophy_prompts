I serve on my school's Student Governance Reform Committee. The administration has proposed replacing our elected representative council with a direct-democracy system in which every student votes via secure portal on all non-emergency disciplinary and budget-allocation decisions. Proponents argue this eliminates the alienation of natural rights that Spinoza identifies in non-democratic regimes, since students collectively retain sovereignty rather than surrendering it to representatives. Critics, including faculty advisors, reply that Spinoza warns the multitude is easily led by passion and superstition unless guided by common notions of reason, and that direct voting on individual disciplinary cases risks unstable, emotionally driven outcomes rather than rational democratic rule. Please draft the committee memo for Thursday's meeting recommending whether to approve direct democracy outright, approve it only with defined conditions and safeguards, or reject it in favor of retained representation, with a concrete implementation timeline if any form is approved. The memo should apply Spinoza's distinction between collectively retaining natural right versus alienating it to a governing body, treat seriously the objection that our student body currently lacks the shared rational understanding Spinoza sees as necessary for stable democracy, and include an honest, non-dismissive account of the strongest argument for the option you ultimately reject.
