If Alan Turing were alive today and confronted with large language models that generate coherent, context-sensitive discourse without embodied interaction, persistent autobiographical memory, or causal-world modeling—systems frequently characterized as statistical pattern matchers rather than genuine thinkers—how might he revise or defend his behavioral criterion for attributing thought? Specifically, consider whether Turing would have required that passing an extended imitation game involve not merely linguistic indistinguishability but also demonstrable epistemic autonomy: the capacity to revise one's representational framework in response to novel causal interventions within a physical environment. Would he treat the absence of such autonomy in contemporary LLMs as a decisive failure of the behavioral test, or would he maintain that linguistic behavior alone is sufficient provided it achieves adequate complexity and internal consistency? Ground your response in Turing's commitments to operational definition and his skepticism regarding other minds, applying them precisely to the distinction between statistical pattern matching and genuine cognitive adaptation.
