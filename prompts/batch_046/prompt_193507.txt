I am developing a conceptual framework for long-horizon institutional design, specifically how constitutional precommitment and Ulysses-style self-binding can be structured for governing bodies that must resist predictable future pressures to relax safety, fiscal, or environmental constraints. The familiar picture, drawn from the literature on constitutional precommitment, treats a collective as a unitary agent that binds its future self through entrenchment clauses, independent agencies, or contractual lock-in provisions. Yet I am especially concerned with what I term the recursive mast problem: the enforcement mechanism that secures the original binding is itself an institution that subsequent actors can weaken, amend, or capture, which implies that genuine precommitment may require second-order binding that binds the binders, and raises the threat of an infinite regress. At the same time, individual-level models of weakness of will, particularly those grounded in hyperbolic discounting, suggest that future selves do not merely hold different preferences but systematically devalue the commitment mechanism itself, which complicates any direct analogy between personal Ulysses contracts and collective constitutional ones.

Given that I am advising a focused research group with roughly three years of conceptual work ahead — philosophy and normative theory rather than empirical law or economics — which open questions at this intersection matter most, and in what order should they be pursued? I want a defended agenda rather than a survey: for each priority, explain why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers and political theorists without specialized legal or econometric training, and what kind of conceptual progress or counterexample over the next few years would cause you to reorder the list. In particular, distinguish the questions that genuinely depend on resolving the collective-individual analogy from those that are overrated because they assume the analogy holds without addressing the recursive mast problem.
