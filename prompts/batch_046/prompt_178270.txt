I'm a software engineer without formal philosophy training, but I've been trying to work out my own position on the statue-and-clay problem of material constitution, partly because software analogies keep intruding. Before I share these thoughts with a discussion group, I want a consistency check.

Here are my commitments:

1. The statue and the clay that constitutes it are not identical. The statue is a distinct object with its own persistence conditions, constituted by the clay but not reducible to it.

2. If the clay is replaced gradually, particle by particle—like updating every module in a running system—the statue persists, now constituted by new matter, while the original clay-lump is destroyed.

3. I reject mereological essentialism, the view that losing or replacing any part destroys an object, largely because software systems survive having every line of code replaced.

4. The statue's artistic value depends only on the spatial arrangement and relational properties of its parts; I do not think any non-physical essence or property is needed to account for it.

5. By analogy with software, I am inclined to treat identity as fixed by functional form and relational structure rather than by the particular physical substrate: a program is the same program whether it runs on server A or server B, and similarly a statue should be the same statue regardless of which atoms realize its shape.

6. Nevertheless, I hold that if the original clay is melted into an unrecognizable lump, the statue is destroyed. Even if an identical statue is molded later from different clay, it is not the same statue. I also have the intuition that the particular material substrate matters essentially: without this specific clay—though not necessarily every particle—the statue would not be this statue.

7. I think there is a genuine, mind-independent fact about whether two objects are the same statue, not merely a linguistic convention, even though I deny there are ghostly properties beyond physical arrangement.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only look problematic? For each real tension, rank them by severity, say what the cheapest revision would be, and note what it costs me. For pairs that dissolve, explain briefly why the conflict is only apparent. I am not asking for a survey of the literature—just a targeted audit of these specific commitments.
