I sit on the ethics commitee at St Mary's hospital and we've got a tricky proposal to review. Our fertility unit wants to partner with a US agency on a kind of cross-border reciprocal egg-sharing programme. The deal is that UK donors recieve subsidised IVF treatment here — not cash, just reduced fees — if half their retrieved eggs are sent over to American intended parents who pay the agency commercial rates. The agency says this isn't payment for gametes, it's just a cost-offset for treatment, so it doesn't breach the UK ban on commercial egg donation. Our lawyers think it's a loophole, but probably not clearly illegal. Half of us on the commitee think it's reproductive tourism and exploitation dressed up as autonomy; the other half think it expands access for infertile couples on both sides and respects donor choice. I need a short memo for Thursday's meeting recommending whether we approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Needs a clear bottom line and some kind of time-frame, and it should engage the autonomy argument properly rather than just restating UK law. Can you draft it? Also flag any hidden practical issues — like what if a donor changes her mind after retrieval but before the eggs are shipped? Not after citations or a reading list, just reasoning. Thanks.
