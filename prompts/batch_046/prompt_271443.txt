Write the text of a retirement address for Dr. Elena Vasquez, delivered to a mixed audience of philosophy colleagues and family members who are not specialists. Vasquez taught for forty-two years and is known among philosophers for having changed her own position over time: she defended an endurantist account of personal identity in her early work, arguing that a person is wholly present at each moment of her existence, and later adopted a perdurantist framework, treating a life as a four-dimensional entity composed of temporal parts related by continuity and psychological connections rather than by strict identity. She has requested that the speaker not claim that she remains the same person deep down or that an essential self has survived unchanged, because she regards both claims as false; she also wants to avoid being described as a mere sequence of disconnected selves, since that would erase the real continuity of her career, her relationships, and her moral commitments. The philosophical idea must do structural work: the speech should explain, in accessible language, why the endurantist picture fails to do justice to the depth of her intellectual and personal transformations, why the perdurantist picture captures something important about those transformations, but also why treating her life as nothing more than a chain of temporal stages misses what it means to honor one life rather than many. The address should be suitable for a ceremony — consoling without being false, intellectually serious without being alienating — and should close with a reflection on what kind of persistence is worth celebrating when the old comfort of an unchanging essence is unavailable. Do not include citations, reading suggestions, or references to specific texts.
