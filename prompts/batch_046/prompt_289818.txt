I'm trying to diagnose a tension in Hofstadter's knob-twiddling account of creativity. Here is a chain of reasoning that seems to show it collapses genius into error: (1) The model treats creativity as sliding continuous knobs—parameters like scale, intensity, or emotional valence—within a fixed conceptual frame. (2) If every creative act is just turning those knobs farther than usual, then revolutionary ideas differ only in degree, not in kind, from minor tweaks. (3) Thus non-Euclidean geometry ought to be analyzable as sliding a "straightness" knob past its Euclidean default without ever leaving geometry's underlying frame. (4) But if turning that knob can invalidate the parallel postulate—the very axiom that helps define the frame—then the frame was never genuinely fixed; the model must include hidden meta-knobs that govern which axioms are active. (5) Once such meta-knobs are permitted, any belief revision, including adopting a contradiction or "2+2=5", can be redescribed as adjusting a hidden "logical-tolerance" parameter to an extreme value. (6) Consequently, the model implies that every change of mind is a form of creativity. (7) Since reasoning failures are plainly not creative achievements, this conclusion is unacceptable. (8) The only remedy is to deny that frame-breaking can be reduced to knob-turning, but that contradicts the core claim that all conceptual change is continuous parameter adjustment. Where does this go wrong, if it does? I'd prefer to keep (1) and (3) if I can, but I need to know what I'm paying if I reject (4) versus (8).
