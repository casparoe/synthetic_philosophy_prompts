I work in software engineering and have been studying token-based network architectures, and I keep encountering an economic loop that I can describe concretely but do not know how to classify philosophically. In one protocol, users must hold a native token to submit transactions or access storage, yet the dominant on-chain activity is not building applications but staking and restaking that token through automated smart contracts to earn more tokens, with yield compounded algorithmically. In another case, governance rights are tied to token holdings, but the reward structure is calibrated so that large entities accumulate tokens purely for yield rather than to influence platform direction; the token remains technically a governance instrument, but its practical function has collapsed into a self-referential accumulation vehicle. In both cases, the token produces nothing tangible outside its own network, its value depends entirely on the mandatory gate that forces others to acquire it, and the loop is now self-sustaining because the automated reinvestment mechanisms ensure that holding and lending the token is more profitable than using the platform it was meant to secure. I understand that Aristotle drew a distinction between household management and an unnatural, unlimited form of wealth-getting that treats exchange itself as the source of increase, but what I am seeing feels structurally narrower: the medium of exchange is not merely being hoarded or traded for profit, but is being algorithmically reproduced within a closed system where its exchange function has become a pretext for the accumulation loop. Does this configuration have an established name—perhaps as a precise subtype of chrematistics, or as a recognized failure mode in monetary or political philosophy? If no established term fits exactly, how should its boundaries be drawn: for instance, does it differ from ordinary speculation, platform rent extraction, or interest-bearing fiat currency primarily in the mandatory-use gate and the automation of reinvestment, or are those only instrumental differences that leave the underlying moral category unchanged? I want to understand whether the loop represents a genuinely distinct conceptual phenomenon with its own edges, or whether it is simply an accelerated, code-enforced version of an ancient pattern, and what criteria would decide between those readings.
