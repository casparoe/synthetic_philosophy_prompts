(3) The scenario must be engineered so that three theoretical stances yield distinct, non-obvious verdicts: a strict finitist denies that the system can ever be in a state that makes the shift necessary or meaningful; a Cantorian actualist accepts both the completed totality and the shift, but must confront a specific counterintuitive consequence about the total volume or occupancy before versus after the relocation; and a Hilbert-style formalist denies that the shift redistributes actual objects, treating it instead as a rule-governed potentiality that never closes into a completed act. Explain what happens if that feature is removed, and state exactly how a defender of the stance that comes out worst in your scenario would attack the case. Write for an intellectually serious but non-specialist audience; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and avoid referencing obscure theoretical frameworks that depend on specialized memorization.
