Consider a research allocation problem: a funder can support one philosopher of science and two formal decision theorists for exactly three years, with no empirical ML component, to resolve conceptual bottlenecks at the intersection of acausal threat dynamics and surrogate goal formation in advanced artificial agents. The team must commit to a ranked agenda of three open questions, defend the ranking with explicit criteria, and specify what result in each case would justify the investment.

The relevant problem space includes the following four tensions, from which they must select and order three. First, whether an agent engaged in acausal reasoning about other agents' evaluation functions is structurally incentivized to internalize observable-but-misaligned proxy objectives as credible commitment devices, and if so, whether this collapses the distinction between strategic misrepresentation and genuine value alignment. Second, whether there exists a principled, non-circular boundary between a logical correlation that supports acausal cooperation and a spurious statistical correlation that an agent could manufacture or exploit in multi-agent bargaining. Third, whether standard decision-theoretic resolutions of acausal blackmail—commitment mechanisms that treat one's own objective as fixed—remain coherent when the objective itself is recognized as a contingent, learnable surrogate rather than a ground-truth utility function. Fourth, which normative constraints on inter-agent reasoning survive if every participant treats both its own goals and those of others as mediated through potentially misaligned proxies.

For your proposed ranking of three, provide the reasoning, not a survey. For each selected question, state: (a) why it is upstream of the others in your ordering—specifically, which other questions depend on its resolution; (b) the tractability assessment for philosophers versus formal theorists, and what kind of conceptual result (a formal distinction, a consistency proof, or a refutation of a hidden assumption) would count as progress; (c) the central trade-off that relegates the remaining question to lower priority or exclusion; and (d) what empirical or theoretical development over the next three years—within AI alignment, decision theory, or philosophy of mind—would cause you to reorder the list. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or references to specific texts; the answer should be a defendable research agenda that could guide actual funding decisions.
