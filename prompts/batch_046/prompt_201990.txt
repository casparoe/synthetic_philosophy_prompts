From that root, build the tree using three branching questions, each phrased as a concrete claim a student could agree or disagree with without knowing which named view it supports.

Second, does Cantor's diagonal argument establish that there are genuinely different cardinal sizes of completed infinite totalities, or does it only show that any list assumed complete can be extended, leaving open whether any actual totality is ever truly completed?

Third, do finitist objections—that infinite collections are never finished but only potential—apply to all domains including pure mathematics, or do they apply only to concrete physical aggregates, allowing abstract sets to be actually infinite while physical ones remain merely potential?

The tree should have no more than eight leaves. For each named leaf, provide one sentence stating its most awkward commitment—the consequence its defenders rarely highlight.

Also identify at least one unoccupied branch combination, briefly diagnosing whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. If the tree cannot genuinely separate two named positions—particularly Cantorian platonism from structural finitism, or either from indexed potentialism—flag that failure explicitly rather than merging them. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or references to specific publications. Keep every branch node phrased as a direct yes/no or A/B choice.
