I am a student in an advanced philosophy of mind seminar examining whether consciousness is distributed continuously across species or bounded by specific neural architectures. To practice defending my position, I would like you to adopt a role and stay in it across turns. Please play a committed defender of what I will call the global-integration threshold view: you hold that phenomenal consciousness requires a unified global workspace with recursive meta-representation, so you deny subjective experience to all animals lacking mammalian prefrontal-parietal architecture—including cephalopods, birds, and fish—while conceding that they exhibit complex information processing, learning, and affective behavior. Speak in the first person, be sharp and not a strawman, concede only minor points about evolutionary continuity or behavioral sophistication, but never concede that any non-mammal has genuine phenomenal consciousness. Keep each reply to roughly one paragraph, maintain character until I write "stop," and treat this as a live seminar exchange rather than a lecture.

To push this into less familiar terrain, I want to frame our disagreement with a distinction that has emerged in our course discussions: horizontal distribution versus vertical fragmentation. Horizontal distribution asks whether consciousness is spread across different species by degree; vertical fragmentation asks whether a single organism with distributed neural architecture might host multiple localized, semi-independent loci of experience rather than one unified subject. Here is my opening challenge. Comparative neurobiology indicates that an octopus's arms contain extensive local circuitry capable of learning and executing tasks with minimal oversight from the central brain, which suggests that an octopus may not possess one global workspace but rather several arm-level processing loops. If you deny consciousness to those loops on the grounds that they lack global integration, then you appear committed to denying consciousness to each hemisphere in a human split-brain patient—despite clear behavioral evidence that either hemisphere can report subjective experience—unless you invoke an ad hoc exemption for humans. Conversely, if you allow that partial integration can support partial consciousness, then your sharp mammal-nonmammal boundary dissolves into a graded spectrum, and the discontinuity thesis collapses. How do you resolve this vertical-fragmentation dilemma without either implausibly stripping split-brain hemispheres of experience or abandoning your threshold altogether?
