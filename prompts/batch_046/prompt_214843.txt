Compare the gradational phenomenalist account of moral status — on which the weight of an entity's interests scales continuously with the richness or intensity of its phenomenal experience, so that insects carry a reduced but nonzero moral weight, mammals carry full weight, and thermostats carry none — with the threshold reciprocity account — on which moral status is binary and is activated not by sentience itself but by the capacity for mutual recognition of interests, thereby excluding insects while leaving open the possibility of inclusion for certain artificial systems. First, examine whether the gradational view can resist collapsing into panmoralism without implicitly relying on an arbitrary cutoff point disguised as a natural boundary. Second, examine whether the reciprocity view can accommodate the intuition that suffering in entities that clearly lack reciprocal capacities — such as many nonhuman animals — still possesses direct, noninstrumental moral significance.
