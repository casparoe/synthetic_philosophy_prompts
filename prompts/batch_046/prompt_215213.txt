I'm helping redesign our 9th-grade world-history units and I'm trying to build a reusable rubric for when a traditional story counts as "history with legend layered on" versus "legend with maybe a kernel of history inside it." Our board keeps fighting about whether the Exodus, Arthur, and the Trojan War should sit in the same unit as the Peloponnesian War or over in the mythology and culture unit, and I need something our teachers can actually run in about twenty minutes — like a checklist with gates — not a philosophy essay. I was sitting in on a historiography course and I'm probably mangling the terms, but the core idea seemed to be that you don't ask "did this happen?" you ask whether the narrative survives three kinds of stress-test: do the sources really come from independent witnesses, is there material or documentary correlation outside the story itself, and are there anachronistic details — technology, institutions, names — that belong to the era it was written down in, not the era it claims to describe?

Please turn that into an ordered decision procedure: the questions in sequence, what counts as passing or failing each gate, where the procedure stops with a firm "classify as legend with echoes," where it stops with "classify as historical core with overlay," and where it hits a values wall and needs a human sign-off because the evidence is symmetric or the stakes are religious and political. Be explicit about which steps are just judgment calls dressed up as checkboxes, and flag where the procedure is guaranteed to misfire. Then run it on three cases: the Trojan War (with the Hittite correspondence and Hisarlik), King Arthur in post-Roman Britain, and the Exodus from Egypt. Make one come out in a way that will definately make part of our board furious — probably putting something they treat as settled history into the "legend with possible echoes" bin. And please seperate what's actually settled among specialists from what's genuinely contested; don't present a split field like there's a consensus. No bibliographies, just the procedure and the worked examples.
