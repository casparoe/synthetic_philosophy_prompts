I am a retired physicist who has lately taken up philosophy, particularly the intersection of decision theory and rational choice. During a recent discussion at my amateur astronomical society—where we were debating how two observers might share a single telescope without either defecting to monopolise the schedule—I mentioned Douglas Hofstadter's idea of superrationality. My companions objected that this was merely cooperation dressed up in jargon, since in the Prisoner's Dilemma two rational agents should still defect. I tried to explain that superrationality is not about altruism but about the peculiar symmetry of reasoning: each agent concludes that the other, being identical in reasoning, will reach the very same conclusion, and therefore neither conditions on an expected defection. The distinction seemed lost on them. Would you compose a joke for me—preferably set in a context familiar to retired scientists, such as competing for telescope time or arguing over the last slice of cake at a departmental tea—that hinges precisely on this logical structure? The punchline should illuminate the difference between ordinary rational calculation and superrational cooperation, and should be intelligible to someone who understands why conditioning on the opponent's move is precisely what the superrational agent refuses to do. Kindly present your answer in British English.
