Consider the standard trichotomy in the epistemic regress problem: foundationalism holds that justification terminates in self-justifying basic beliefs; coherentism holds that justification is holistic and sustained by mutual support within a closed system of beliefs; and infinitism holds that justification requires an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons with no terminus. Now reject the shared assumption that justification is a static, fully possessed property of a belief at a given time. Instead, adopt a processual account: a belief is justified only insofar as it is actively embedded in an open-ended inferential trajectory that is neither circular nor complete—what we might call an unclosed reasoning path. Under this framework, argue that foundationalism collapses because basic beliefs, lacking any further inferential engagement, possess zero processual justification; coherentism collapses because circular loops close the trajectory and thereby terminate the very activity that confers justification; and consequently infinitism is not merely one option among three, but the necessary consequence of treating justification as an essentially incomplete, ongoing process. In developing your argument, be specific about why circular coherence fails to satisfy the unclosed-path condition, and address the objection that an infinite chain can never be completed and therefore can never actually confer justification on any particular belief.
