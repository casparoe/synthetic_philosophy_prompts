Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of rational belief revision in the face of higher-order evidence must satisfy, with the motivating case or formal constraint behind each. I want the full specification before comparing named accounts.

The theory must handle cases in which an agent receives evidence about her own cognitive performance—peer disagreement after a complex calculation, discovery that she has taken a reasoning-impairing drug, or statistical data that her judgment class is systematically biased—without reducing this to ordinary first-order updating. For each requirement, specify: the phenomenon or case it must get right (e.g., rational steadfastness versus rational suspension after peer disagreement, preservation of first-order knowledge despite noisy self-monitoring, the specific irrationality of clear-eyed epistemic akrasia); the distinction it must preserve (between defeat of belief and defeat of justification, between evidential and practical reasons to revise, between rational revision and mere psychological debiasing); and whether failure here is disqualifying or only a camp-specific preference (strict global coherence versus local reliability, internalist accessibility of the defeat versus externalist calibration).

In particular, address whether a jointly unsatisfiable subset exists: can a theory simultaneously require full Bayesian responsiveness to all higher-order indicators, prohibit every form of epistemic akrasia, and preserve ordinary first-order knowledge in domains where self-monitoring is noisy? If these three cannot be jointly satisfied, identify the precise tension and which desideratum must be relaxed.

Then, briefly, indicate which single requirement does the most damage to the three broad strategies—treating higher-order evidence as merely additional first-order evidence, allowing level-splitting between first-order belief and second-order assessment, and invoking epistemic permissivism to dissolve the conflict.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a scorecard of individual theorists; a brief evaluation of the three strategies against the list is sufficient.
