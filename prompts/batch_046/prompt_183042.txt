The speaker is defending a thesis they call “delegated we-ness”: the claim that joint action need not involve a shared we-mode intention or a Gilbert-style plural subject, but can instead be fully constituted by interlocking personal intentions in which normative accountability for the collective outcome is distributed asymmetrically among the participants. The speaker applies this specifically to collective apology and collaborative artistic production, arguing that one agent can legitimately bear the full normative weight of the “we” on behalf of others, without those others entering a joint commitment or possessing a collective mental state. The argument is conceptual and aims for ontological parsimony, but it insists that this parsimony does not erase the distinctive normative structure of genuinely joint activity.
