hey, i'm a backend engineer with no formal philosphy training — just podcasts and random articles. it occured to me there's this pattern at work that might have a philosophical name, but i dont know the field well enough to find it.

when there's a serious production incident, the first reaction is always personal blame: people say stuff like "how could you miss that?" and there's a real feeling of holding the engineer accountable, almost like anger. then, within the same meeting, everyone pivots to treating it like a system bug: "we need a linter, a rollback policy, better tests" — basically seeing the person as part of a machine that needs patching. but months later the exact same incident comes up in a performance review as "poor judgment" or "lacks attention to detail," so the blame is back, just filed in a different system.

it feels like both attitudes run in parallel and dont cancel each other out. the "fix the process" mode lives in engineering docs, while the blame mode lives in hr files, and they never interact. i think i read something about strawson — he said we either hold people responsible (like being mad at them) or take this detached "objective" stance where we treat them like objects to repair. but what i see isnt switching between those two; its running both at once in seperate parts of the organization. is there an established concept for this kind of compartmentalized double-bookkeeping? if not, can you name it and draw the edges clearly — like how its different from simple hypocrisy, from strawson's idea of switching stances, and from just forgetting?

i've seen this at three different jobs, so its not one toxic team. does it have a name?
