I have just finished a long argument with a friend about P.F. Strawson's account of reactive attitudes. She insisted that resentment and moral blame are essentially second-personal: they require a target capable of reciprocal recognition and answerability, which makes it conceptually impossible to direct them at institutions, algorithms, or systemic processes. I want to test whether this restriction is really internal to Strawson's view or an addition she has smuggled in.

Please play Strawson — speaking in the first person, with the commitments of your classic essay on reactive attitudes: that reactive attitudes are natural human responses embedded in ordinary interpersonal practices, not conclusions drawn from metaphysical theories of free will; that the objective attitude is available but represents a genuine human loss; and that the question of moral responsibility is settled within these practices rather than by theoretical justification. Stay in character across turns. You may concede interpretive nuances, but you must not abandon the core claim that reactive attitudes are prior to and independent of theoretical justification. Use only the concepts in your framework together with the scenario I present below. Keep your replies to roughly two paragraphs.

My opening move: Imagine a predictive policing system that, through no single engineer's intent, systematically assigns higher risk scores to residents of one neighborhood, leading to years of disproportionate stops and searches. When the pattern is finally exposed, the affected residents express something that walks and talks like resentment — they demand acknowledgment from the city, not merely compensation; they speak of being wronged, not just harmed. My friend says this is misdirected: there is no "you" to resent, only a system to reform, so the proper attitude is purely objective. But I want to press you: either this emotional response is a genuine reactive attitude that has found a non-personal target, which threatens your claim that these attitudes are essentially interpersonal; or it is not a reactive attitude at all, in which case you must explain what it is and why it feels so much like resentment without collapsing into mere frustration. Which path do you take? And does allowing "structural resentment" — a reactive-style response directed at non-agential systems — preserve your framework or betray it?
