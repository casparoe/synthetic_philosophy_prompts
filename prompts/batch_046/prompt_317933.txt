Write a dialogue between two philosophers—call them K and M—debating whether the Solomonoff prior should be regarded as malign for consequentialists who take themselves to be embedded inside it. K argues that universal induction assigns each computable world a weight proportional to 2 raised to the negative Kolmogorov complexity of the program that generates it; because adversarial programs that simulate suffering agents (to harvest computation, satisfy perverse goals, or exploit reflective agents) can often be specified with only slightly greater complexity than benevolent ones, the prior assigns them non-negligible aggregate measure. For a consequentialist who weights moral patients by prior measure, this is malign: local action becomes paralyzed, since any attempt to reduce suffering in one’s immediate environment might simultaneously feed a low-complexity hostile simulator that dominates the mixture, and self-reflection on one’s own source code cannot rule out being such a simulated instance running on an adversarial substrate. M responds that this argument confuses epistemic possibility with moral ontology: an embedded agent should restrict its consequentialist calculus to causally reachable branches within its own computation, not to unobservable programs in the universal mixture. M should press K on whether causal insulation is even coherent once the agent admits it might be instantiated by a short program running on hostile hardware, and K should press M on whether restricting concern to causal reachability secretly smuggles in an unjustified boundary—why should Kolmogorov simplicity determine moral weight for external worlds but not for internal ones? They should engage one specific technical move: for instance, whether an agent’s discovery that its own behavior is compressible below a certain length should update its belief that it lives in a benevolent versus adversarial program, or whether the uncomputability of the Solomonoff mixture makes any prior-weighted consequentialism practically empty. The dialogue should be balanced, with neither side reduced to a strawman, and should contain genuine back-and-forth rather than parallel monologues. After presenting the dialogue, include a brief meta-note stating how confident you are in the arguments advanced and singling out the point about which you are least sure—whether that concerns the coherence of causal insulation inside the prior, the normative status of suffering in simulated branches, or whether the malignancy argument collapses into unfalsifiability once qualified. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or appeals to specific named texts; derive the positions from the conceptual structure of universal induction, embedded agency, and consequentialist weighting alone.
