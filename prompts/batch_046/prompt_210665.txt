I serve as the faculty representative on our university’s International Access and Ethical Obligations Committee. At Thursday’s meeting we must vote on the administration’s proposed Limited Sanctuary and Open Enrollment Protocol, which would allow undocumented students to enroll and pay in-state tuition regardless of immigration status, restrict voluntary data-sharing with federal immigration authorities to cases involving active felony warrants, and allocate $200,000 from alumni donations for a dedicated immigration-legal defense fund. Our policy manual permits reasonable accommodation of vulnerable populations but offers no operational definition, and the state attorney general’s office has warned that the defense fund could trigger funding sanctions under recent state statutes. I need a formal written opinion—no more than two pages—to circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a specific course of action with a clear time frame (approve as drafted, approve with specified amendments, or defer with conditions for reconsideration), and it must include an honest, serious engagement with the strongest consideration on the losing side: namely, the argument rooted in jurisdictional autonomy that a state-chartered university has a derivative duty to uphold the state’s right to exclude non-citizens, and that institutional sanctuary undermines democratic self-governance and the rule of law rather than merely resisting federal overreach.

Please take a committed, definite position rather than presenting a balanced menu of options, and ground your reasoning in a conceptual framework I have been developing: the principle of non-exploitative presence, which holds that once an institution has systematically benefited from the labor, tax contributions, and social integration of undocumented residents within its service area, it acquires an associative duty of non-cooperation with removal efforts, even if one grants the state a general right to control entry at the border. Adjudicate whether this principle genuinely distinguishes institutional obligations from state immigration authority, or whether it creates an untenable asymmetry that collapses back into open-border cosmopolitanism. Give me your bottom-line recommendation and the reasoning behind it, including what you take to be the best objection to your own view.
