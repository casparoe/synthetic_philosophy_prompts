so my study group has been going in circles about the Holocaust uniqueness thing and i think we might be talking past each other but i can't tell. Here's the setup: one person keeps saying we have to compare the Holocaust to other state-sponsored mass killings — colonial violence, the Gulag, etc — because otherwise we miss how modern bureacracy and rational administration make these things reproducible. They keep bringing up Bauman's idea that the camps weren't some pre-modern barbarism but actually used modern organizational logic, so if we wall the Holocaust off as totally unique we miss the warning about modernity itself. Then someone else gets angry and says comparison is exactly the problem, that treating it as one genocide among others erases the specific antisemitic ideolgy and the particular moral status of industrialized extermination directed at Jews. They agree on all the facts — the train schedules, the bureacratic division of labor, the racial ideolgy — but they never budge.

My hunch is this isn't a history disagreement at all, maybe not even a moral one in the usual sense. Maybe the comparison-people are really doing sociology of modernity and asking what structural conditions produce this? while the uniqueness-people are doing something like moral preservation or commemoration and asking what do we owe specifically to these victims? So they're using the word comparison for two different jobs. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there an actual hidden disagreement about whether bureacratic rationality alone could have produced the Holocaust without the antisemitic component? And if it is mostly a framing mismatch, could anything actually move us — like drawing a sharp line between causal explanation and moral evaluation — or are we just stuck because the same vocabulary covers both?
