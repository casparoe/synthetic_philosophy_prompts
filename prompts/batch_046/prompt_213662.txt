I chair the University Interdisciplinary Ethics Panel for Cross-Cultural Research, and by Friday we must issue a formal decision on Protocol 2024-RC-09. The proposal is a five-year mental-health intervention study conducted in partnership with a Southern African community in which Ubuntu philosophy is widely practiced. Rather than using our standard protocol—individual signed informed-consent forms—the investigators request approval for a relational consent framework. Under this model, consent is initially deliberated through family and community councils; individual participants are named within a collective agreement; and each person retains a private, unconditional veto that can be exercised at any stage without requiring public justification. The investigators contend that standard individual consent presupposes a thin relational conception of personhood, in which social ties are merely background conditions for an autonomous self that precedes them. By contrast, they argue, the community operates with a thick relational view—captured in the idea often rendered as umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu—where decision-making capacity is genuinely distributed and where isolating an individual decision from its communal context risks a form of self-alienation rather than safeguarding liberty. Three panel members see this as a necessary cultural accommodation; four regard it as an unacceptable dilution of the irreducible moral status of the individual, worrying that communal dynamics could obscure coercion and that a veto hidden for social harmony is not a veto in any meaningful sense.

Please draft the panel's decision memo—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—addressed to the research office and suitable for circulation to both the investigators and the university administration. The memo should recommend a concrete course of action, include a specific implementation timeline if conditions are attached, and engage seriously with the strongest consideration supporting the position your recommendation rejects. In your philosophical reasoning, distinguish clearly between what is broadly settled in debates about Ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood and what remains genuinely contested among specialists; do not present a contested interpretation—for example, whether thick relationality modifies or eliminates individual moral rights—as though it were consensus. You should not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, but you may reference the investigators' thin/thick distinction as they have presented it. The document should be readable by administrators without philosophical training, yet precise enough that the researchers can respond to its conditions point by point.
