the exam question (15 pts, 45 min) was: "Explain the bootstrapping problem for reliabilist theories of perceptual knowledge, with specifc reference to calibration loops for scientific instruments (e.g. using a thermometer to check its own accuracy). How does the easy-knowledge objection apply here, and can a virtue-reliabilist like Sosa avoid it by appealing to reflective knowledge?"

student answer:
"Reliabilism says knowledge is true belief formed by a reliable process. The bootstrapping problem, first raised by Cohen, happens when you use a thermometer to verify its own readings and come to know it's accurate just by trusting it—without independent verification. Cohen's easy-knowledge version adds that once you know one reading (say 20°C) and you know that if the reading is correct the instrument works, closure lets you know the instrument works. But this is circular because the reliability of the instrument is presupposed in knowing the initial reading. Sosa's virtue reliabilism solves this by distinguishing animal knowledge from reflective knowledge: the bootstrapping only yields animal knowledge that the thermometer works, not reflective knowledge, so there is no vicious circle. Goldman, however, argues we should deny closure for self-referential cases, which seems wrong to me because closure is necessary for all scientific reasoning. So Sosa's approach is clearly the right fix."
