B defends the partitioned-agency view: the self need not be epistemically unitary; an agent can deploy subpersonal mechanisms—such as strategically restricting the scope of her own debiasing practices—to shield a belief from counter-evidence without transparent awareness of doing so. A should argue that this exemption is either a conscious prioritization of emotional coherence or ordinary bias, not deception, because if she genuinely knows the protocols apply universally, she cannot sincerely restrict their scope without contradiction. B should reply that the scope restriction itself is the deceptive mechanism: it operates subpersonally, not by conscious choice, and her intact first-person authority is precisely what allows the partition to persist without collapsing into explicit contradiction. Require that each speaker respond to the other's specific points. After the dialogue, provide a brief structured summary in JSON with exactly these fields: "position_A" (string summarizing A's core claim), "position_B" (string summarizing B's core claim), "concrete_case" (string identifying the psychologist scenario), "crux" (string identifying the central disagreement), "resolution" (boolean indicating whether the dialogue reaches agreement).
