I am preparing the interview questions for a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher defending an ecological reconception of virtue ethics. Her position accepts the central situationist finding—that cross-situational behavioral consistency is weak—but denies that this undermines virtue ethics per se. Instead, she argues that virtues should be understood not as internal, stable dispositions but as ecological capacities: cultivated abilities to identify, recruit, and stabilize situational supports that enable good action. Write me a sequence of six to eight questions that press on the actual mechanics and normative commitments of this view, not on biography or general motivation. I want at least one question asking how her account distinguishes genuine virtue from mere strategic situation-selection, paired with a follow-up keyed to the likely reply that selection itself manifests practical wisdom; one asking whether normative priority can still be assigned to character if character is distributed across agent-environment couplings, with a follow-up anticipating her defense that distributed character retains evaluative force; and one asking what distinctive empirical predictions her ecological virtue ethics makes relative to both traditional trait psychology and pure situationist social science. For each question, include a brief parenthetical indicating the standard reply or deflection she is most likely to offer, and ensure the follow-up makes that reply progressively harder to maintain without further argument.
