I serve on the Regional Municipal Procurement Oversight Board, and next week we must vote on a pilot program that would allow vendors to submit bids through autonomous algorithmic agents capable of dynamically adjusting price and contract terms in real time in response to other agents' moves. Proponents argue that neighboring municipalities have already adopted the system, so refusal will leave us with worse contracts and accelerate a regional race to the bottom in transparency. Critics respond that once autonomous bidding becomes the norm, human auditors will be unable to reconstruct award rationales, and democratic accountability will erode even though no single jurisdiction deliberately chose that outcome. Please draft the board's written opinion recommending either approval with enforceable safeguards, rejection, or conditional deferral with a defined review timeline. The opinion should treat the multipolar-trap reasoning seriously—acknowledging that unilateral refusal may be self-defeating—while assessing whether institutional design can escape the race-to-the-bottom dynamic without requiring every jurisdiction to move in lockstep. Include an honest statement of the strongest consideration on the side you reject, and ensure the recommendation is concrete enough that staff could implement it immediately.
