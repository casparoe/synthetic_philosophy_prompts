For a feature I’m preparing on the metaphysics of hidden capacities—specifically the problem of finks and masks that keep dispositions from manifesting—I’d like a sestina that makes its conceptual machinery do formal work. Use these six end-words, rotating through the standard sestina pattern: fragile, guard, strike, hidden, break, shield. Through the first five stanzas, the word “fragile” should appear only in syntactic contexts where “guard” or “shield” grammatically blocks its completion—so the line reaches for a breaking that the poem’s own grammar prevents, performing a masked disposition. Meanwhile, let “strike” behave as a fink: present as the triggering condition, yet immediately neutralized by an intervening term before it can activate “break.” In the envoi, drop the interfering words and allow “fragile” and “break” to meet in direct relation for the first time, so the form’s release mirrors the removal of a mask. The result should stand alone as a lyric about concealed vulnerability, yet be precise enough that someone familiar with the metaphysics of dispositions recognizes the mechanics of interruption versus suppression.
