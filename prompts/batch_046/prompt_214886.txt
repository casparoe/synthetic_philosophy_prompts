So there's this moderation design I'm seeing on a few rationalist-adjacent forums lately — let's call it the 'tolerance throttle.' Instead of banning someone for intolerant takes, the system downranks those specific posts and blocks them from being quoted or shared outside the thread. The speaker can still talk, but thier reach is neutered. The admins say this sidesteps Popper's paradox because nobody is being forcibly silenced, just contained.

What philosophical commitments does that actually presuppose? Like, does it assume tolerance is mainly about not coercing people rather than about not harming them? Is there an implicit epidemiological model of ideas — that intolerant speech is contagious and containment is a neutral public-health move? And what does it do to the state-vs-private distinction if structural muting counts as tolerance?

I'm also wondering about consistency. If the throttle applies to views that reject the throttle itself, aren't we just back in the paradox with extra algorithmic steps? Or does framing it as platform design rather than exclusion somehow dissolve the contradiction? Would love a steelman of the implicit theory here, plus where it falls apart.
