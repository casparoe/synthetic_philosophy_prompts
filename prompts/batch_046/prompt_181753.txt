here's the chain:

(1) in embedded agency, some beliefs aren't just in teh head; they're partly constituted by stable external structures — call them exogenous belief-fixers (maps, norms, tools).

(2) if belief B is partly constituted by fixer F, then B's content is fixed in part by F's actual state. you can't have B without F being there in that state.

(3) so if F is in state S, then B necessarily represents S (or is about S correctly), because F's being-S is literally part of what makes B the belief it is.

(4) but embedded agents clearly hold false beliefs — e.g., i rely on a faulty map and think the bridge is safe.

(5) to save consistency, the embedded theorist must say that in (4) i don't actually have a false belief; i just have a "mismatched coupling" between my brain-state and the faulty map.

(6) but "mismatched coupling" isn't belief at all — it's just mechanical malfunction, like a broken thermometer.

(7) therefore embedded agency either denies false belief (implausible) or denies that embedded states are beliefs (which collapses embedded agency into internalism).
