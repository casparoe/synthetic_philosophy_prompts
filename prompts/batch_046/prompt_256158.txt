(2) But I'm also pretty deep into negative utilitarianism. I think the only thing that genuinely matters is reducing suffering, and that gives human activity a clear directional point. Call it a local purpose, not cosmic.

(3) I also think that if you ditch cosmic meaning, that's not a tragedy — it's a relief. It clears away metaphysical noise so you can focus on concrete harm-reduction.

(4) At the same time, I think local projects — doing math, being a good friend, making art — are valuable, but only derivitively. They matter because they either reduce suffering or are part of a life structured around that goal.

(5) And I think Ramsey himself lived like math and friendship mattered for their own sake, not just as suffering-reduction tools. So there's this example of someone who rejected cosmic meaning but clearly didn't treat everything as instrumental to pain-minimization.

Also, is there a hidden tensiom between (3) and (2): if ditching cosmic meaning is just a clarification with no emotional weight, why does suffering-reduction suddenly get to be the one true direction?
