- moral blame is basically a social debugging protocol. we run it on people to correct behavior, not because they have some magic free will chip.
- i buy strawson's idea that reactive attitudes like resentment and gratitude are the ground floor of moral responsibility, not some abstract metaphysical freedom thing.
- i think the real condition is reciprocal vulnerability: both parties have to be able to hurt and be hurt in a recognizably human way.
- so if someones brain is severely damaged and they cant reciprocate, or if an AI causes harm but cant be hurt back in the right way, we should switch to the objective attitude — treat them like hardware in maintenence mode, not blame them.
- i actually dont think theres a principled line between a human with a broken brain and a really advanced AI; both flunk the reciprocal-vulnerability test.
- but i still think we should blame human criminals even if neuroscience eventually proves crime is just deterministic brain-output, cause the debugging protocol is still socially useful.
- and gratitude should mirror resentment exactly: if you cant reciprocate, no gratitude, just functional appreciation like logging a success.
