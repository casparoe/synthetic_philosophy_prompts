I'm preparing a ninety-minute session for an interdisciplinary graduate reading group—mostly literature and physics students with no formal history of science—on the conceptual coherence of Johannes Kepler's astrological practice with his astronomical revolution. I want them to grasp that for Kepler, astrology was not a superstitious residue but a disciplined, physically-grounded inquiry into celestial influence, sharply distinct from judicial horoscopy and natal determinism. Rather than explaining the material to me, please design the explanatory arc: first, the dependency order of the conceptual components—what must be in place before the distinction between universal celestial physics and individual fate can even be registered, and why the mechanical account of planetary motion has to precede the analogical argument for temperamental influence. Second, propose the single carrying example that should anchor the session—perhaps Kepler's comparison of lunar tidal action to planetary influence on human temperament, or another concrete scenario that captures both the analogical reasoning and its precise limits—and specify exactly what that example must possess and what it must lack in order to avoid collapsing astrology back into horoscope determinism. Third, map the misconceptions I should anticipate: that all pre-modern astrology was identical to newspaper horoscopes; that Kepler privately disbelieved the practice he published; and that accepting any celestial influence on terrestrial bodies necessarily violates mechanical causation. For each, indicate the point in the sequence where it will surface and how the progression should absorb it before it hardens. Fourth, provide one diagnostic question I can pose near the end—something that forces them to apply the architecture rather than retreat into vague pluralism, such as asking why Kepler might reject a particular natal chart while still defending an annual prognostication for a region. Finally, specify which technical elements of his astrological corpus—say, the rules of house division or the computation of primary directions—should be explicitly bracketed as beyond this level, and how I should signal that omission without undermining the coherence claim I want them to leave with.
