I'm working through Simone Weil's scattered political writings for a graduate seminar on authority and collective life, and I've become stuck on the principle that governs her wildly different assessments of organized groups. Her record of verdicts is strikingly consistent in some directions and puzzling in others. She demands the complete abolition of political parties, treating them as machines that manufacture collective passions and suppress individual judgment; she respects spontaneous worker solidarity on the factory floor but condemns centralized trade unions once they seek state power or bureaucratic permanence; she refuses institutional membership in the Catholic Church and attacks its hierarchical authority, yet writes with unmistakable reverence about sacramental practice and small local Christian community; she treats the modern nation-state as an object of idolatrous attachment analogous to Plato's great beast of collective opinion; but she seems to approve of small, voluntary, face-to-face associations that form around an immediate need and dissolve once it is met. I have tried to extract the rule—whether it concerns the manufacture of opinion, the permanence of the institution, the scale of mediation, or the presence of hierarchical command—but each fits some verdicts better than others.

Next week I need to present on a case that is not in her texts: a decentralized, algorithmically governed cooperative that has no formal leadership, no membership requirements, no rhetorical apparatus, and no permanent charter. It exists solely to allocate a shared local resource through cryptographic consensus, and participants interact only through the protocol, never meeting in person. Would Weil classify it with the parties and unions she wants abolished, or with the temporary, task-bound associations she tolerates? I am less interested in the specific answer than in identifying the competing principles—perhaps no collective that generates emotional conformity, no collective that survives its immediate purpose, or no collective that replaces direct presence with mediated procedure—and saying which of her earlier verdicts each principle struggles to explain. Please predict her verdict, state the implicit theory you think best organizes her track record, and propose one further boundary case that would distinguish the leading rules from one another.
