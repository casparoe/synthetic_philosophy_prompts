As a novelist researching the bodily routines behind philosophical systems, I ask you to compose a deadpan parody—roughly 500 words—of a premium wellness newsletter promoting "The Zarathustra Protocol," a biohacking regimen extracted from Friedrich Nietzsche's scattered remarks on digestion, migraine, and moral physiology. The satire should exaggerate real features of Nietzsche's thinking—his belief that heavy German cuisine dulls the spirit, his comparison of alcohol to a poison that breeds slave morality, his self-experimentation with light Italian meals to manage chronic illness, and his conviction that bodily discipline is the hidden ground of intellectual aristocracy—without attributing anachronistic clinical language to him or inventing verbatim quotations. The target is the contemporary practice of reducing a thinker's suffering and contradiction to a consumable lifestyle system. Keep the irony sustained and legible: the reader should recognize that the protocol's cheerful prescriptivism is exactly the kind of herd-minded optimization Nietzsche despised.
