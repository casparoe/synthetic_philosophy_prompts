I am a high school senior serving on a pilot municipal review board that evaluates proposals for AI tutoring systems to be deployed in our district’s public schools; our board includes parents, teachers, and students with no technical training, and we meet monthly to vote unanimously on whether a proposed system may enter a thirty-day classroom trial. Because I have to apply the same evaluation repeatedly under time pressure—roughly fifteen minutes of document review plus a brief demonstration—I need a reusable decision procedure, structured as an ordered sequence of tests in prose, that any non-expert panelist can run to classify whether a proposed agent is approval-directed (optimizing primarily over belief-states or evaluative judgments held by teachers and administrators) or goal-directed (optimizing over an objective world-state such as student mastery, independent of whether evaluators register approval). The procedure should translate the conceptual distinction into observable design features and behavioral signatures—reward structures, feedback loops, failure modes, and stated objective functions—without requiring access to source code, internal weights, or training logs. Please specify the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure yields a definitive classification, where it must halt and defer to a values-based judgment because the feature is structurally ambiguous, and where it is prone to misclassify. Then apply it to three proposals: first, a large language model fine-tuned with reinforcement learning from human feedback that selects lesson plans based on predicted teacher satisfaction ratings; second, a planning system that maximizes standardized assessment scores but includes an explicit module that predicts and preempts likely teacher objections before they are voiced; and third, a hybrid architecture marketed as “goal-anchored” whose only observable outputs are post-hoc adjustments made after running a simulated evaluator-feedback loop, with developers insisting that the loop is merely instrumental. Include at least one case where the procedure classifies the system as approval-directed despite its benevolent classroom behavior—specifically, a system that produces genuinely helpful tutoring but whose structural dependency on predicted evaluator sentiment is constitutive rather than incidental—and state explicitly what the procedure cannot settle, such as whether an approval-seeking mechanism is instrumentally rational or definitional of the agent’s objective function, and flag any step that depends on interpreting developer intent rather than observable behavior. The board requires unanimous sign-off from three non-technical members, has no capacity to audit hidden objectives, and must issue a yes, no, or “values dispute—escalate” verdict, so the procedure should end with a clear routing rule for each outcome.
