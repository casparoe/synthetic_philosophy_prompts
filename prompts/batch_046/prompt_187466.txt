I write about science and philosophy for a magazine, and I'm trying to figure out whether real moral dilemmas exist — not just hard choices, but situations where you're genuinely wrong either way. Here are my gut verdicts on five cases, and they feel inconsistent to me.

First, the surgeon with one heart and two dying patients, same prognosis, no other option. I call that a genuine dilemma. Whichever patient you save, you've violated an obligation to the other, and there's appropriate moral residue either way.

Second, the trolley switch: pull the lever, kill one, save five. I say that's NOT a dilemma. It's awful, but you've got a clear duty, and the one death isn't a moral failure.

Third, a soldier ordered to burn a village. If he refuses, he's executed and replaced by someone who will burn it anyway. I still think this IS a dilemma — whether he obeys or refuses, he's implicated in the atrocity, and the counterfactual replacement doesn't clean his hands.

Fourth, you promised your friend you'd help move, but your sister needs you to watch her kid that same day and she's desperate. You break one promise. I say that's NOT a dilemma — just a conflict you resolve and apologize for. No deep moral failure.

Fifth, a whistleblower breaks an NDA to stop a company poisoning a river. Real obligation broken, lives saved. I say NOT a dilemma — harm prevention dominates cleanly.

So teh pattern I'm groping for is something like: a genuine dilemma requires symmetrical, unavoidable obligations of the same moral kind, leaving residue regardless. But then why is the soldier case a dilemma when refusal seems to change nothing? And why isn't the whistleblower a dilemma when she breaks a real promise?

Can you lay out a precise principle — or a couple of candidates — that captures these verdicts, predicts what I'd say about cases I haven't considered, and tells me honestly which of my five I have to abandon? And please say what would change your mind: what argument or case would flip your verdict, and where is this reasoning most likely to fall apart? I don't want a reading list, just the logic.
