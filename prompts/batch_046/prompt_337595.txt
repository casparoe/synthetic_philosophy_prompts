i'm writing a column on the work-leisure rights debate and it's driving me nuts. you've got the Protestant work ethic crowd saying labor is moral formation, you've got the 35-hour-week people and the karoshi researchers saying overwork kills and rest is a basic entitlement — and yet for years they've just circled eachother using the exact same japanese mortality stats. nobody moves. my working guess is it's not even one disagreement: one camp is asking "what makes a life good?" (virtue anthropology) and the other is asking "who controls the clock?" (distributive justice), with this fringe concept of "temporal sovereignty" — owning your schedule as its own separate right, neither work nor leisure — sitting in the middle and being ignored. but maybe i'm wrong and it's just bedrock intuitions about suffering versus dignity. diagnose the stall for me: is it verbal, substantive, different questions hiding under one heading, or a hidden empirical claim about productivity? and be honest — is there a conceptual split, a new economic model, or anything that could acutally break the deadlock, or is this just two worldviews that never touch?
