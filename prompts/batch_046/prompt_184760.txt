I have been working through Bradley's argument that relations generate an infinite regress, and I think I have dissolved it by adopting what I call an intrinsic-compositionality principle: rather than treating a relation R in a R b as an external connector that requires further relations to link it to its relata, I define R as a structural feature that is numerically shared—partially identical—between a and b themselves. Because R is already inside both terms, no second-order binding relation is needed, and the regress stops at the first step.

But I am now struggling with what philosophers call the unity of the proposition, and I cannot tell whether this is simply Bradley's difficulty restated in epistemological language or a genuinely separate problem. If my intrinsic-compositionality model explains how the parts of a fact cohere without an infinite hierarchy of connectors, does it not automatically account for the unity of the corresponding proposition? Or does the unity problem instead concern the ordered, assertible structure of judgment—the distinction between a mere list of terms and an integrated whole—in a way that my ontological model, which only addresses how relations bind, leaves entirely untouched? I discussed this with a colleague who works in formal ontology, and she suggested that any remaining residue might be purely syntactic, but I find that distinction unclear. Could you clarify whether Bradley's regress and propositional unity are two aspects of one issue or two independent difficulties, and whether an intrinsic-compositionality approach resolves both, resolves only one, or rests on a misunderstanding of the original argument?
