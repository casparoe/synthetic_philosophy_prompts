I read this summary in a pop-science blog about Libets experiments: "Libet showed that your brain decides to move about 350 milliseconds before you become aware of the decision. The readiness potential starts firing around 500ms before you actually press the button, and since it comes before your conscious 'W' judgment, that proves the unconscious brain made the call first. Later fMRI work like Soon et al. pushed this back to 10 seconds, predicting which hand you'd choose with near-perfect accuracy. The only loophole is Libets 'conscious veto,' but even that is just another brain process, so it doesnt save free will. Basically neurscience has settled that conscious will is just a post-hoc story we tell ourselves after the motor cortex has already started the action."

How fair is this? Go through it piece by piece: what's accurate enough, what's a common oversimplifcation, and what's flat wrong? In particular, does the 350ms gap actually show that an unconscious decision was made, or is it just motor prep? Does the Soon study really predict with near-perfect accuracy, and does it apply to the kind of choices that matter for free will? And is the veto argument being misrepresented here? Finally, does any of this actually prove free will is an illusion, or is there a hidden slide between "unconscious initiation of movement" and "no moral responsibility"?
