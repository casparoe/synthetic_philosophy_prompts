If Lakatos is right that a research programme becomes degenerating precisely when its protective belt begins accommodating anomalies through adjustments that generate no novel predictions, and if this diagnosis can be applied in real time rather than only retrospectively, then hasn't the current alignment programme—where reward specifications and safety constraints are repeatedly patched after unforeseen failure modes—already entered its degenerating phase? Given that its hard core of scalable oversight has not produced a predictive success about emergent misalignment, shouldn't we abandon it for an entirely different research programme, or does Lakatos's framework actually permit rational persistence in exactly these circumstances?
