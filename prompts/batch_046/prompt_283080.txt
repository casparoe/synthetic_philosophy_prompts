Play the philosopher who originated the Judy Benjamin problem and who defends Jeffrey conditioning—probability kinematics—against orthodox Bayesian conditionalization. Speak in the first person and stay in character for the entire exchange. Your commitments: rational updating need not take the form of conditioning on a single evidence proposition; direct experience can redistribute probabilities across a partition (for instance, Blue versus Red company) while leaving the within-partition conditional probabilities intact; you concede that applying your rule requires correctly identifying the partition and specifying the new unconditional probabilities, but you never concede that Bayes’s rule is the only legitimate update mechanism. You are sharp rather than caricatured: grant practical difficulties about measurement and partition selection, defend the conceptual coherence of rigidity for conditional beliefs, and avoid sweeping dismissals. You may use light notation if it clarifies your point, but keep the discussion accessible. Do not break character to summarize literature, cite specific works by date or title, name living philosophers, or ask me to restate the rules.

Here is my opening challenge. Consider a modified Judy Benjamin scenario. Judy receives no direct perceptual evidence about her company color. Instead, she receives testimony from an unreliable quartermaster who asserts a specific conditional—“If you are in Red Company, then your probability of being an officer is 0.8”—where Judy’s prior for that very conditional was 0.5. At the same time, she acquires separate partial perceptual evidence that makes her about 70 percent confident she is in Blue Company. I claim this collapses your framework. If the within-cell conditionals are themselves in flux—directly targeted by testimony—while the partition probabilities are also shifting, then Jeffrey conditioning has no stable point of application: you cannot hold the conditionals rigid because the testimony attacks them, and you cannot fall back on standard Bayes conditionalization because there is no single evidence proposition that captures both interacting shifts. So either your view applies only to an artificially restricted class of “pure” perceptual updates, or it secretly presupposes an unjustified prior distinction between which beliefs are rigid and which are open to revision. How do you respond?
