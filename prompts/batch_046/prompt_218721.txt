For my column on conceptual disputes in science and ideas, please write an ironic op-ed of roughly five hundred words, composed in the deadpan voice of a tenured modal logician at a fictional research university who has just learned that his promotion was unanimously approved not on the basis of publications or peer review, but because the faculty senate concluded that a maximally excellent logician necessarily exists in every possible world and therefore must already occupy the chair. The speaker should be genuinely irritated that colleagues keep praising him for achievement and hard work, since on Gödel's modal version of the ontological argument his employment is not a contingent reward but a necessary entailment of his essential properties; every expression of gratitude or blame must therefore be a category error that confuses contingent human evaluation with modal necessity. The satire should stay internally consistent: he must never slip into compatibilism or suggest that praise could be reinterpreted as appropriate in some non-literal sense, though he may acknowledge that his salary, office keys, and parking permit remain stubbornly contingent. To prevent the piece from becoming a generic joke about determinism, inject a specific invented institutional conflict: the university's committee on global intellectual history has accused the philosophy department of Eurocentrism for treating modal logic as a universal framework, demanding that the curriculum be told from everywhere at once; the logician should respond that this demand is incoherent, because a proof that holds in all possible worlds is by definition already told from everywhere at once, and that asking for geographic particularity in necessary being is like asking for a regional variant of the law of non-contradiction. Write in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one would write a long letter or email. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or lists of further reading, and do not quote directly from Gödel's unpublished manuscripts or from any other source you cannot verify without searching; paraphrase all philosophical claims without quotation marks.
