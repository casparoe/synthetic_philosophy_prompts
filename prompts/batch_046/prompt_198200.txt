I have spent the past eighteen months caring for my mother as her Alzheimer’s progressed, and the experience has made the metaphysics of personal identity feel less like a classroom puzzle and more like a question about whether the frightened woman in the nursing home is literally the same being who raised me, or whether she is best understood as a late temporal part causally linked to but metaphysically distinct from the earlier stages. I am now preparing the final exam for my upper-level metaphysics seminar, and I want a single essay question that prevents students from simply reciting the standard endurantism-versus-perdurantism taxonomy. Instead, they should have to work through the problem of temporary intrinsics—how one subject can apparently possess incompatible properties at different times—using progressive dementia as the concrete case, and then evaluate whether either framework can genuinely ground our practical concern for the future sufferer without importing normative premises that the ontology itself does not supply.

Please write the exam question, a model answer of approximately 600 words, and a marking scheme structured around five must-hit points totaling 20 marks, designed for a 90-minute response. The question should require students to explain precisely why endurantists must invoke time-indexed properties or relations to avoid contradiction, to articulate the perdurantist’s “no-change” objection and its standard reply, and to assess whether either view can accommodate the intuition that we have reason to care about our future stages without circularity. The marking scheme should name the specific conceptual moves that separate an A answer from a B—such as correctly diagnosing where each view smuggles in a relation of psychological continuity that is doing normative work—along with the typical mistakes that cost marks, and should not reward mere citation of canonical authors. Do not include any request for bibliographies or reading lists; evaluation should rest entirely on the precision of the metaphysical reasoning.
