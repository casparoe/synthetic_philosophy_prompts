heres the student anwer (writen from notes):

Nominalism says there is no redness outside our words, so the split-location problem never starts. Platonic realism puts redness in a separate realm and the objects just copy or participate in it, so its not literally inside the apples. Aristotles view is different: he puts universals inside the things, but he solves the puzzle by giving each apple its own slice of redness, so its not one thing spread out. I think this actually makes Aristotle closer to trope theory, where each object has its own particular redness and they just look alike. D.C. Williams came up with tropes and he said resemblance is basically an illusion, so tropes dont even need to be similar. The hidden problem with saying its 'the same' is that sameness means identity, so if two apples share one universal they become partly identical, which collapses the difference between the apples. So realism fails because it makes all red things share a part.
