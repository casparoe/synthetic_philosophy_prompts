For an upper-division philosophy of history seminar I am designing a single ninety-minute session on the interaction between hindsight bias and the narrative fallacy in historical explanation. Rather than treating them as independent cognitive errors, I want students to see them as a self-reinforcing loop in which retrospective coherence reshapes the salience of prior causes. The carrying case should be a reconstructed 1985 East German strategic assessment—drawn from declassified planning logic but presented without post-1989 knowledge—that assigns comparable probabilities to four divergent futures: gradual liberalization, authoritarian stagnation, systemic collapse, and violent consolidation. The example must preserve genuine contemporaneous ambiguity; it must lack any mention of the Wall’s fall as likely within four years, yet contain enough internal causal structure that an “inevitability narrative” feels temptingly coherent once the outcome is known.

Propose one diagnostic question for the end that distinguishes grasping the loop from merely labeling the two biases separately, and state what should be left out at this level with explicit acknowledgment.
