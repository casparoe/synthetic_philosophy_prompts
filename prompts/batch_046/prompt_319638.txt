'The driver applies the same strategy at both exits since he lacks memory of which one he's at. Always taking gives 0 at exit 1 and blocks exit 2; always continuing misses both, so 0. Now, if he reaches exit 2 he specifically knows he should take it, because the payoff is 4 versus 0 for continuing. But he can't implement a conditional plan like 'continue at 1, take at 2' because his absentmindedness makes the two nodes indististinguishable. This is analogous to causal decision theory, where you can't update on information that's causally independent of your choice, and it's also like branch prediction in CPUs: once the pipeline commits to a path you can't retroactively change it.
