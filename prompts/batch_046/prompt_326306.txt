I'm drafting a taxonomy for a workshop on commitment races — teh kind where actors permanently burn their own options to outmanouver rivals, not just promise action. Think climate treaties where states auction away regulatory sovereignty to prove they're locked in. I want a decision tree that sorts positions by concrete answers, not by camp names.

Branch 1: Is option-burning genuinely different from ordinary credible commitment, or just bargaining with extra steps?

Branch 2: Does irreversibility mostly filter out impulsive future mistakes, or mostly lock in present ones?

Branch 3: If only wealthy or organized groups can afford to burn options, does that corrupt democratic equality, or is it neutral strategic advantage?

Branch 4: Should institutions encourage, regulate, or prohibit these races?

For each leaf give a position name (or 'unnamed') plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment — like having to say poor states are just 'less strategic' rather than unfairly shut out. Note any empty answer-combinations and whether they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Flag if any branches fail to separate real positions. Keep it to about 5 or 6 leaves, and don't lean on citations — just work from the logic of the view.
