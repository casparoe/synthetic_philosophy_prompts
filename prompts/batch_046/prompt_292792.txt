I'm developing a research agenda at the intersection of Alfred North Whitehead's philosophy of education and contemporary cognitive science of learning, with particular attention to his well-known three-stage rhythm of romance, precision, and generalization. Rather than asking for a survey of existing literature, I want a defended prioritization of which open questions or practical tensions in this intersection deserve sustained effort—say, from a small research group with about three years and no proprietary access to adaptive-learning datasets—and which should be deprioritized because settling them would not change educational design in any material way.

There appear to be at least four live problems: first, whether the initial romance stage can be structurally cultivated in formal curricula without collapsing into mere entertainment or engagement metrics; second, how Whitehead's insistence on the inseparability of freedom and discipline interacts with algorithmic personalization that optimizes for efficiency and predicted mastery; third, whether his critique of inert ideas—knowledge that is not actively integrated—anticipates or runs counter to observed transfer failures in pedagogical applications of large language models; and fourth, whether the generalization stage, as Whitehead conceives it, requires a metaphysical holism that is incompatible with modular, competency-based assessment frameworks. Please rank these, or substitute others if I have missed the genuinely upstream ones, according to tractability, downstream dependency, and practical impact on curriculum design. Defend the ordering explicitly: which question is upstream of the others, which is most tractable with current methods, and which sounds profound but is likely overrated because its resolution would not alter how learning environments are actually built?
