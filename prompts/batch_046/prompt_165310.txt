question:
people talk about 'the alignment problem' like its one single thing, but philosophers and engineers disagree on whether its actually one problem or several. first, lay out outer alignment (the specifcation problem) and inner alignment (the optimization problem). then imagine a hypothetical third layer: 'meta-alignment,' where an AI system can revise its own objective function over time. is meta-alignment just a special case of outer or inner alignment, or is it genuinely distinct? and does recognizing it mean we should stop talking about 'the alignment problem' in the singular?

student answer:
The alignment problem is basically getting AI systems to pursue the goals we actually want. Outer alignment is the specification problem — picking the right reward function or objective, like avoiding the classic paperclip scenario. Inner alignment is the optimization problem — making sure the trained model actually chases that objective and doesn't find a shortcut or reward hack. Meta-alignment comes in when the system can change its own goals, so we need to align the procedure it uses to update them. I think meta-alignment is just outer alignment applied recursively: you specify a meta-objective that governs how the base objective gets updated. So it doesn't create a third category; it collapses back into specification. That means the alignment problem is really just two problems — writing the right objective and getting the model to actually optimize for it. Once you accept that, meta-alignment doesn't break the framework because any update rule can be hard-coded in the outer layer. The confusion people have comes from conflating optimization with value internalization — inner alignment is about reward hacking, not about whether the model 'believes in' the values. So 'the alignment problem' should stay singular in casual talk, but technically its a family of specification and optimization issues.
