I'm preparing notes for a graduate seminar on comparative legitimacy, and before I present I want a consistency audit of my own position rather than a general overview. Here is the set of commitments I find myself defending: (a) legitimate authority must satisfy a service condition—rulers must be genuinely answerable to the governed, not merely in de facto control; (b) performance legitimacy—sustained prosperity, social order, and administrative competence—is an independent normative ground for recognizing authority, not just a prudential reason to obey; (c) Singapore's long one-party dominance is fully legitimate, not merely provisionally tolerated, because the PAP maintains internal mechanisms of elite rotation, policy feedback, and bureaucratic discipline that provide what I think of as recursive answerability without horizontal electoral competition; (d) the European Union suffers from a democratic deficit that seriously undermines its legitimacy, despite delivering regulatory order and material prosperity, because its accountability mechanisms are too diffuse and its decision-making too remote from citizens; (e) an authoritarian regime that combines high performance with credible internal removal mechanisms for incompetent leaders possesses legitimacy that is at least comparable to, and sometimes greater than, that of a poorly performing democracy; and (f) I apply the distinction between throughput legitimacy (responsive, fair process) and output legitimacy (effective results) asymmetrically—I treat Singapore's internal party processes as sufficient throughput, but I do not grant the EU's parliamentary and council structures the same status, even though they are more formally democratic.

Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which ones only appear incompatible but can be reconciled with a modest clarification, and what is the least costly revision if I must weaken or abandon something? Please rank the tensions by severity, identify the hidden premise that makes the asymmetry in (f) possible, and tell me whether giving up (c) or (e) does more damage to the overall architecture of my view. I am not asking for a bibliography, reading list, or citations of particular theorists; I want to know whether my own house is in order and which wall is actually load-bearing.
