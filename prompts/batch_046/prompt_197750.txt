Consider the special composition question applied not to ordinary artifacts or organisms but to a distributed cognitive architecture that is physically possible yet rarely discussed: three human brains linked via high-bandwidth, bidirectional brain-computer interfaces, producing genuinely unified phenomenal experience and integrated agency, with no single brain serving as the control center and no shared biological membrane enclosing the trio. Give me your credence—explicitly on a scale from 0 to 1—that this system is correctly described as exactly one composite object rather than as three objects in sustained interaction. Do not offer a bare number; decompose the credence into four sub-questions, each with a rough numerical probability and a one-line justification: (a) the prior that universalism about composition is false and some form of restricted composition holds; (b) the conditional probability that restricted composition permits functional-phenomenal integration without spatiotemporal contiguity or a shared biological boundary; (c) the evidential weight, if any, that unified conscious experience should carry in determining compositional facts; and (d) the likelihood that the case is genuinely ontologically vague rather than determinately resolved one way or the other. Show how these terms combine into your overall figure. Then identify which of the four is the weakest link—specify what kind of argument or empirical finding about mereological essentialism or neural integration would shift it most. Finally, state explicitly how confident you are in the decomposition as a whole, and single out the one point in your reasoning about which you are least sure.
