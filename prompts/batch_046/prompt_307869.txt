I sketched the following argument in a seminar on Wittgenstein’s later philosophy, and it now strikes me as suspicious because it seems to collapse every conceptual distinction into mere convention, yet I cannot locate the precise misstep. (1) Wittgenstein’s family-resemblance account holds that for many central concepts—game, number, language—there is no single necessary and sufficient condition shared by all instances; unity comes only from a network of overlapping similarities. (2) If there is no essential feature that determines membership, then for any particular case there is no mind-independent fact about whether it falls under the concept; the decision is fixed solely by the conventions of the language-game in which the judgement is made. (3) Scientific and legal concepts—mammal, negligence, explanation—are also applied in practice through family-resemblance reasoning rather than through hidden essential definitions. (4) Therefore, disputes about whether whales are fish, or whether an artificial system’s output counts as an explanation, are not resolvable by discovering the true nature of the case; they are merely conflicts between the conventions of different language-games. (5) But if every conceptual dispute is conventional in this way, then there is no genuine error in applying a concept—only divergence from local practice. (6) This entails that a scientist who corrects a folk taxonomy is not uncovering an objective fact, but merely enforcing the conventions of her own specialised game. (7) Yet we ordinarily think that scientific correction reveals objective error rather than imposing one convention upon another. (8) So either family resemblance applies universally and all conceptual authority is parochial, or there exists at least one domain where essentialist determination saves objectivity—yet the concept of “objective domain” is itself either essentialist, which begs the question, or family-resemblance, which undermines the boundary. Where does this argument go wrong, if it does? I would most like to preserve (1) and (7), and I am particularly unsure whether the slide from (2) to (4) involves an equivocation between the absence of a single feature and the absence of any objective constraint. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on these issues, and do not present any one interpretation—whether realist, conventionalist, or quietist—as the consensus view.
