Within the risk-weighted expected-utility framework, risk attitude is captured by a parameter that transforms the probabilities of outcomes prior to computing expectation, rather than by curvature in the utility function. Comparing this with ambiguity-averse models that employ sets of imprecise probabilities together with a maximin decision rule, I find it hard to see why the two should not collapse into one another: both seem to let an agent weight bad outcomes more heavily when probabilities are not precisely known, and both appear capable of accommodating Allais-style preferences. Is there a principled conceptual distinction—perhaps concerning the treatment of new evidence, or the separation of belief from value—that prevents this collapse? Or does my difficulty indicate a deeper misunderstanding of what the risk-weight parameter is meant to represent?
