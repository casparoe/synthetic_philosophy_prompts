i've been volunteering with a local advocacy group for about two years, and i've started noticing a pattern i dont have a word for. in the begining people argued about priorities and tactics like you'd expect, but lately there's this shift where if someone raises an ethical worry—like, our messaging is leaving out people who actually need help—the reaction isn't "you're wrong about that" but more like "you're not really with us." what's weird is that the people doing this dont seem cynical; they genuinely can't tell the difference between "this hurts our cause" and "this is morally wrong." the two just collapse into each other. even folks who used to be really reflective now treat any internal moral discomfort as a kind of personal betrayal.

i was reading simone weil's essay about getting rid of political parties—she writes that parties turn people into machines for collective passion and replace independent judgment with loyalty. that sounds close, but she seems to be describing the big structural picture, not this specific mental move where tactical loyalty slowly eats moral reasoning from the inside. does that specific mechanism have a name? is it basically what weil means by the party spirit, or is it different—maybe closer to groupthink, though that feels too general and too psychological? i'm not asking for a reading list, just whether there's an established concept that captures exactly this collapse, and where the edges are: does it only happen inside formal parties, or can any tight collective do it?
