Write a deadpan parody profile, in the polished, observational voice of a high-end magazine such as The New Yorker or The Atlantic, approximately 700 words, of a fictional luxury consultant named Dr. Elara Voss who applies updateless decision theory—specifically logical decision theory with ex-ante commitment over logical counterfactuals—to advise ultra-wealthy clients on irreversible life choices (marriage contracts, philanthropic foundation structures, career exits). The piece should treat her method with complete journalistic sincerity: she computes a single policy over all worlds consistent with the client's prior, refuses to revise recommendations upon learning observed outcomes, and markets this refusal as a premium feature ("no buyer's remorse, only buyer's logic"). The satire must exaggerate actual features of updateless reasoning—rejection of Bayesian updating in the decision context, treatment of post-decision evidence as irrelevant to the rationality of the act, the separation of epistemic belief revision from practical commitment—rather than inventing strawmen. Include at least one specific scene in which a client attempts to inform Dr. Voss of a decisive observed fact (for instance, that a business venture has already collapsed) and she politely declines to condition the recommendation on it. By the end, the reader should feel both the internal coherence and the practical strangeness of the view, without the author ever breaking deadpan or stating the critique explicitly.
