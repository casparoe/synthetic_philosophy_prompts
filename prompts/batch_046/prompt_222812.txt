A senior engineer routinely blocks any proposal to include those hidden states in our shared schema by invoking the principle, 'what isn't accessible to us isn't ontologically real for us.' I need to respond precisely, but the line seems to bundle together several different claims. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses it might express — for instance, about epistemic warrant for commitment, about reference-fixing conditions, about identity across hidden variables, or about the pragmatic purpose of an ontology — and for each say whether it is defensible, whether it actually justifies excluding the sealed states, and which version the engineer probably intends?
