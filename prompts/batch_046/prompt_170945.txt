I am preparing to moderate a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who recently defended a triadic reinterpretation of Hegel's master-slave dialectic. According to her view, the dialectic does not resolve through a dyadic struggle that culminates in direct mutual recognition between master and slave; rather, the slave's achieved self-consciousness remains structurally unstable unless a third institutional party mediates and stabilizes that recognition. She contends that Hegel's own presentation in the Phenomenology suppresses this triadic dependency by treating the state—developed later in the Philosophy of Right—as an external supplement rather than as an immanent condition of the dialectic's resolution. Consequently, she argues that contemporary recognition theorists have overestimated the emancipatory power of intersubjective struggle and underestimated the institutional preconditions required for recognition to become durable.

Please write me a sequence of interview questions that engage the actual substance of this position. The questions should target the specific textual and conceptual pressure points where her reading departs from standard interpretations—especially concerning the formative role of labor, the phenomenological function of the fear of death, and the logical transition from recognition to the state—while anticipating the replies she is likely to deploy (for instance, that the slave's labor already implicitly contains institutional mediation, or that introducing a third party reduces the dialectic to an externalist sociology). End with a question that asks what kind of phenomenological or historical evidence could genuinely undermine her triadic thesis. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
