I am working through the passage in Machiavelli's The Prince where he advises that a prince must learn to use both the lion and the fox, and that he should break his word when keeping it would harm him, since people are unreliable and do not keep faith with him. I want to understand this through Hannah Arendt's distinction between ordinary lying, which conceals specific facts, and modern political lying, which systematically substitutes a fabricated narrative for shared reality and thereby destroys the very ground of factual truth. Arendt also claims that the liar is free in a distinctive sense—he acts upon the world rather than merely describing it—and she connects this capacity for initiating something unprecedented to her concept of natality. Could you help me understand whether Machiavelli's strategic management of appearances counts as Arendt's ordinary concealment or as the more radical destruction of shared reality; whether Arendt's idea that lying transforms the world rather than hiding within it illuminates why Machiavelli treats deceptive appearance-creation as an achievement of political virtù rather than a simple moral failure; and whether Arendt's claim that successful lying requires an audience that wants to be deceived applies to Machiavelli's picture of the people? Please treat these as interrelated interpretive problems rather than separate summaries.
