I've come to think ranking historical atrocities is not just possible but neccessary — not by body count alone, but through what I call "institutional malignancy": how deliberately harm was engineered, how replaceable victims were in the perpetrators' ideology, and whether violence served an end or was the end itself. The refusal to rank strikes me as moral cowardice wearing humility's clothes. Play it straight: what would have to be true for this to collapse? I don't want the obvious "all suffering is incomparable" line — I want deep defeaters. Is there a formal result in value theory or decision theory showing multi-dimensional moral comparison is incoherent? Which incommensurability intuitions actually hold up? Does the ranking view predict something its rivals don't — maybe different obligations of remembrance or repair? Has the comparison camp already insulated itself with qualifications like "this is only analytical, not claiming X was worse in every respect," and if so, is that unfalsifiability a problem? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones defenders should concede themselves, and be honest about whether the view risks anything real or is well-insulated — and whether that insulation is actually a bug. End with a single line starting Verdict: that captures your bottom line.
