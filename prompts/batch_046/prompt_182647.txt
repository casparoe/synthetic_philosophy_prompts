I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, though I have been thinking about authenticity and value because of my work with generative systems. I want to understand why an original painting is worth more than a perfect forgery, and I have built an argument that now seems to prove too much—especially about digital art. Here is the chain:

(1) The special value of an original artwork, as opposed to a perfect forgery, comes solely from the fact that the original is the direct result of the artist's autonomous creative decision, while the forgery is the result of a derivative decision to copy.
(2) If autonomy is what grounds that value, then any artifact that is the direct, non-derivative result of an autonomous creative decision should possess the same special status.
(3) When I design a generative algorithm and run it with a specific random seed, each image produced is the direct result of my autonomous decision to build and execute the system; selecting the seed is no more an independent artistic choice than selecting a particular brush.
(4) So every unique output of my algorithm should count as an original, and any manual replication of one output should count as a forgery lacking special value.
(5) This means running the algorithm twice with different seeds creates two originals of equal standing, whereas painting a copy of the first output creates a worthless forgery, even though both processes yield perceptually distinct objects with different causal histories.
(6) Furthermore, if I release the source code, anyone who executes it produces another "original," which implies that generative works have no forgeries at all.
(7) Therefore, either the autonomy-based account is wrong and physical originals like the Mona Lisa have no special value, or generative art is structurally incapable of being forged, which seems false.

I would rather keep (1) than accept (7), but I suspect there is an equivocation or a scope error somewhere between (2) and (4). Where exactly does the argument break, why did that step look reasonable, and what would I be committing to if I reject it? In your answer, please state how confident you are overall and single out the specific point about which you feel least sure.
