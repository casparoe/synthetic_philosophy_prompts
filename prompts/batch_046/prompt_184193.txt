question: explain Bostrom, Douglas, and Sandberg's 'unilateralist's curse.' how is it different from a standard collective-action problem like tragedy of the commons? then apply it: five independent AI labs each decide whether to open-source a powerful reasoning model, and each estimates social value seperately. walk through why the curse might cause release even when every lab's private estimate is actually wrong in the same direction, and say what the 'principle of conformity' asks them to do.

student answer:
the unilateralist curse is when a bunch of agents can each take one big action that affects everyone else, like releasing an AI model. its different from tragedy of the commons because in the commons everyone takes a little bit of a shared pool, but here each agent makes one binary choice. in the AI case, each lab estimates whether release is net good. the curse happens because the labs dont coordinate, so even if they're all altruistic they can still cause harm. its basically a prisoner's dilemma: each lab has an incentive to defect and release, because if they hold back but another lab releases anyway, they've lost nothing but missed the benefit of being first. Bostrom et al. propose the principle of conformity, which says each lab should replace its own estimate with the median of the group's estimates and only act on that. so if Labs 2-5 think release is bad but Lab 1 thinks its good, Lab 1 should adopt the median (negative) and hold back. the math is that with five independent decision-makers, the chance at least one makes a false positive is high -- about 1 minus accuracy to the fifth -- so release is likely even if every lab is usually right. this is why altruism backfires: caring makes you act on your own judgment, which is exactly what creates the curse. if all five labs had the exact same information and reasoning, the curse would still apply because some labs are just more risk-tolerant than others, so one would always push for release.
