Me and my roommate got stuck arguing about endurantism vs perdurantism after a metaphysics lecture. She said perdurantism is just cheating: if I'm a four-dimensional worm, then saying I "change" is just pointing to different parts, but you still need to explain why those parts are parts of *me* and not some other worm. I said endurantism is the one with the real problem — if I'm wholly present at every moment, then I'm both F at t1 and not-F at t2, which looks like a straight contradiction unless you smuggle in time-indexing. She said that's not smuggling, it's the indexical strategy, and it's no weirder than saying I'm 5'6" here and 6' there without contradiction. I replied that at least perdurantists can explain gradual brain replacement: if synthetic neurons replace my cells one by one, there's a clear story about which temporal parts belong to which stage. She said endurantists can just appeal to psychological continuity — it's the same person persisting through incompatible mental states, no parts needed. Then she asked: if continuity is what matters, why can't a perdurantist say the same thing, just modeled as a relation between stages? I admitted I didn't have a quick answer. Can you adjudicate this? Specifically: who's right about the contradiction charge, does the brain-replacement case actually favor one view, and what are both of us missing? Please seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested here — don't make it sound like philosophers all agree on one side.
