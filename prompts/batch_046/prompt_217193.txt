I'm preparing a single seventy-minute session for philosophy undergraduates who have just read Hume on causation. The topic is Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism: not the theological gloss, but the metaphysical claim that created substances possess no causal efficacy and that God alone produces every effect on each occasion. Plan the explanatory sequence: what has to be established before occasionalism can even be presented, and why Hume's regularity view doesn't by itself provide the right contrast; the one carrying example—please not fire and cotton, since that example pulls students immediately into miracle-talk rather than metaphysics—specifying what the example must show and what it must omit; the predictable misconceptions in the order they will arise (for instance, that occasionalism is just constant divine intervention, or that it collapses into Humean skepticism about necessary connection), and the exact point in the arc where each misconception should be intercepted. Include one closing question that reveals whether students have grasped the denial of causal powers in created things rather than merely associating the view with religious doctrine. Finally, state clearly what part of Al-Ghazālī's dispute with the falāsifa—say, the specific modal argument about miracles—should be set aside at this level.
