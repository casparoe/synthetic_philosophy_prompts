I'm preparing interview questions for a podcast with an unnamed philosopher who defends a refined social-constructionist account of gender kinds. Her view holds that gender categories are interactive kinds in Ian Hacking's sense, but she adds a mechanism she calls counterfactual inertia: once constituted by institutional practices, a gender kind can persist even after those practices disappear, because linguistic and normative conventions become semantically anchored to the kind. She appeals to this to explain why gender categories remain stable across societies with very different institutions.

Please draft a sequence of eight to ten questions that press the actual content of her position, with follow-ups keyed to the answers she is most likely to give. I want at least one question on each of the following:

- How the inertia claim differs from biological essentialism or weak realism, with a follow-up anticipating her reply that inertia is purely normative and asking what makes it normative rather than causal.
- What conceptual or empirical marker would show that a gender kind has genuinely dissolved rather than merely lying dormant, anticipating her appeal to disrupted patterns of self-application, and then a follow-up asking whether that criterion renders dissolution practically impossible.
- The standard objection that if social kinds outlive their originating institutions, the social element of social construction is redundant; include a follow-up targeting her likely move to semantic anchoring by asking whether anchoring relocates the problem to language rather than resolving it.
- A contrasting case about money: she claims monetary kinds lack inertia because constitutive rules are explicitly revocable, so currency collapse is true dissolution. I want a question testing this distinction against a historical episode such as the post-war re-emergence of the Deutsche Mark or the persistence of black-market currencies, with a follow-up pressing whether those examples actually reveal monetary inertia.
- How racial kinds, understood as bureaucratic kinds, fit her framework: do they share gender's inertia mechanism, operate differently, or undermine the distinction between gender and race she wants to maintain?

Please separate, in your response, what is settled in the philosophy of social kinds and institutional reality from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present a contested interpretation — for example, regarding the scope of Hacking's interactive kinds or the viability of semantic anchoring — as if it were the consensus.
