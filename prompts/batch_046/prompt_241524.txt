Tibetan Buddhist instutions run this whole protocol for recogntion of tulkus — they check a kid against the dead lama's old belongings, weigh dreams and omens, and eventually seat him as the literal continuation of that teacher's monastic seat, debts, and authority. At the same time the tradition teaches anatta, that there's no permanent self or soul hopping from body to body. So what theory of personal identity or continuity is the tulku system actually presupposing — a mental stream, a conventional designation that works like a legal fiction, some karma-bundle? Reconstruct the implicit view, then say whether the practice strictly needs that metaphysics or if it's compatible with several different readings of no-self. And if you took anatta literally with zero continuity assumption, what would have to change about how monasteries pass on authority? Would they drop the tests entirely, or keep the ritual but reinterpret it as pure politics?
