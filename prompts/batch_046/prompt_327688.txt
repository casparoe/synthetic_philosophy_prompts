Following a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a friend about whether Nāgārjuna's tetralemma should be read as a destructive logical exercise or as a positive metaphysical account of emptiness (śūnyatā), I want to move past standard exegesis and ask which open questions in Madhyamaka interpretation and its philosophical aftermath actually merit sustained work, and in what order.

Assume you are advising a small research group with about three years of conceptual effort—no philological excavation of untranslated manuscripts, but rigorous philosophical analysis. Among the live issues—the precise logical status of the catuskoti and whether it presupposes a paraconsistent or relevance-logic framework; whether the claim that emptiness is itself empty avoids infinite regress only by collapsing into a form of quietism; the divergence between Prāsaṅgika and Gelugpa accounts of conventional existence and whether that divergence is resolvable with present conceptual tools; and whether Madhyamaka commitments on ontological dependence have any downstream consequences for contemporary metaphysics of grounding or for theories of conceptual revision in the sciences—which should be tackled first?

Please provide a dependency-aware prioritisation rather than a survey. For each question, defend its place in the agenda: is it upstream of the others, in the sense that settling it would constrain or dissolve subsequent debates? How tractable is it for philosophers working without primary textual discoveries? What would count as evidence or argument over the next few years that should move it up or down? And which implications—if any—actually carry weight outside Buddhist philosophy, for instance in how we understand the revision of theoretical concepts in law or empirical science, and which are overrated?

Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Write the response in British English.
