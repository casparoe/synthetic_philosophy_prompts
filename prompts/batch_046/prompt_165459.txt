I'm trying to figure out if being worried about simulations should calm me down about longtermist fanaticism, and I built this argument that now seems to blow up in my face.

1. If post-humans run ancestor simulations, we're more likely in one than in base reality—standard Bostrom-style reasoning.
2. If we're simulated, then the "trillions of future people" longtermists talk about are just rendered data with no base-layer moral status.
3. So sacrificing present welfare for them is like starving yourself to feed NPCs.
4. But here's the part that feels new: moral status doesn't vanish inside a sim unless the base-layer entities that run it assign it. Call this base-layer inheritence.
5. Under base-layer inheritence, simulated future people have zero moral weight unless the simulaters care about them, which they probably don't.
6. By the same token, anyone outside my immediate causal loop—say, people in other countries—might also be simulated NPCs with no base-layer backing.
7. So if simulation skepticism wipes out obligations to the far future, it wipes out obligations to distant present people too.
8. But I don't think distance alone cancels moral obligation.
9. So either I'm stuck with moral solipsism, which is absurd, or there's a mistake in steps 2-6.
10. I want to keep 1 and 4 if I can, but I'm okay losing 2. Where does the chain actually break, and what's the cost of fixing it?
