Here is my opening challenge: in the classic switch-versus-footbridge dilemma, most people approve diverting the trolley but not pushing the large man, and moral psychologists typically explain this disparity as a framing bias—an emotional salience effect that distorts an underlying consistent preference for saving five lives. If your theory denies any underlying consistent preference being distorted, then you must say there is no bias here, only two different framing preferences. But then apply your view to a racist who systematically undervalues the suffering of out-group members because his salience architecture weights proximity and familiarity more heavily: if this is just a framing preference rather than a bias, have you not made the concept of moral bias useless for criticism?
