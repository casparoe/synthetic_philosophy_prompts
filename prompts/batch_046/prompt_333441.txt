hey, so my editor sent me this game-theory paper about backward induction and now i'm obsessed with applying it to writing. here's an argument i cooked up that feels broken but i can't find the crack. (1) In teh centipede game, rational players bail at round one because each anticipates the other bails at round two. (2) This only needs finiteness plus common knowledge of rationality, not any special payoff numbers. (3) So map it onto co-writing a novel: we agree on 20 alternating drafts, then publish together. At draft 20, the rational move is to steal the manuscript and publish solo. (4) At draft 19, knowing that, i should steal first. (5) By induction, we should never write draft one — yet people co-author books constantly. (6) The obvious escape is that in art the payoff matrix is endogenous: the more we collaberate, the worse a stolen solo version is, since it misses my voice. (7) So defection gets less attractive as we go, which seems to block the induction. (8) But if both of us know this, then there's no clear endpoint — why stop at draft 20? We should keep going forever, since cooperation only gets richer. (9) Yet every book eventualy ends, which means there's an exogenous stopping point. (10) Once that endpoint exists, backward induction kicks back in, and we're back to never starting. (11) So either collaborations have no rational endpoint, or backward induction is false, or rational agents can't have common knowledge of endogenous payoffs. Which step is rotten? I'd like to keep (5) and (6) if i can — they feel true to how writing actualy works — but maybe i'm just romantizing teamwork?
