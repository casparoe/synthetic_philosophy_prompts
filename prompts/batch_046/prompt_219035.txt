Next tuesday I'm presenting to our phil of physics seminar on this notion I'm calling observer-coupled determinism. The core claim is that in deterministic chaotic systems the old line between predictable-in-principle and unpredictable-in-practice collapses for a deeper reason than measurement error: the physical resources you'd need to maintain measurement precision scale exponentially with the Lyapunov time, and those resources are themselves part of the deterministic dynamics. So any observer precise enough to make long-range predictions is also coupled into the system and gets disrupted—Laplace's demon can't sit outside the universe because being that precise makes you part of the flow. I want to argue this doesn't destroy determinism itself but only kills a specific epistemic gloss where determinism equals "in principle predictable by an ideal observer." The audience is two hardcore Laplaceans who treat chaos as purely epistemic, a couple people from the free-will debates who want chaos to save libertarianism, and one actual ergodic theorist who knows the math cold. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll hit me, phrased like each camp would actually say them, with what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest point in my own arguement—probably whether my resource-scaling point applies to non-physical or computational demons. And please include the objection I'm secretly hoping nobody asks, with a quick reply I can actually deliver in thirty seconds.
