I sit on our museum's acqusition committee. A donor wants to hand over reconstructed 'eyewitness fragments' about the Persian Wars from an uncatalogued archive in western Anatolia. The papyrus originals are mostly gone; what we have is an algorithmic reconstruction with confidence scores, built by training a model on Herodotus and Thucyidides to fill narrative gaps. Half the committee says this is just modern source criticism with extra steps — if scores are high enough we should accept it as provisional evidence and study it. The other half says without physical transmission we're not actually knowing anything about the distant past, we're generating probabilistic fiction, and accepting it corrupts our epistemic standards.

We need a memo for Thursday recommending accept, reject, or accept with conditions. Give a concrete course with a time frame, take the skeptical side seriously — especially the worry that algorithmic reconstruction collapses primary testimony into inferred narrative — and don't just cite museum policy. I want reasoning the committee can vote on, not a reading list or citations. Just the bottom line and why.
