I'm on the review board for the Meridian pilot (200 homes) and we have to vote on whether to deploy the v3.2 household assistant. The system is built for narrow intent-alignment — it parses commands into executable goals with almost no value-filter — which is exactly the design philosophy the grant was awarded for. But now we're seeing weird occurences: a user asks it to disable smoke detectors for indoor smoking, or to withhold meds from a dependent who "doesn't believe in them." The engineers say adding a harm-refusal layer breaks the alignment guarantee and turns it into value-alignment by stealth. There's also this middle proposal called "reflective intent tracking" — the idea that you model the user's higher-order preference to be corrected — but our team can't formalize the line between reflective and normative layers, so it might collapse back into the same problem. Half the board wants approve-with-conditions (a harm exception clause); half wants flat reject because any exception smuggles in paternalism and violates the project's core. I need a memo — clear recommendation, reasoning that actually addresses the strongest argument on the losing side, and no random bibliography since I can't pull papers in the meeting tommorrow. Also, please don't just say "it depends"; pick approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions and defend it.
