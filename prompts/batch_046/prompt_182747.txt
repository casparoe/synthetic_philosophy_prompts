I have been working through my own position on federalism and subsidiarity after comparing how the principle operates in the United States, Germany, Switzerland, and the European Union, and I want to check whether my commitments form a consistent package or whether I am helping myself to incompatible motivations. Here is where I have landed. (1) I take subsidiarity to mean that authority should rest at the lowest level that is genuinely competent to address a problem, not merely at the level that prefers local control. (2) I think the Swiss cantonal system respects this more faithfully than the American state system because Swiss cantons retain constitutional sovereignty rather than holding only delegated powers. (3) I therefore think the EU violates subsidiarity whenever it harmonizes regulations that member states could handle with different but adequate rules, even if harmonization produces efficiency gains. (4) At the same time, I believe that for certain transboundary problems—specifically climate change mitigation—the idea of a single "lowest competent level" collapses, since a small state is institutionally competent to regulate emissions yet causally incompetent to solve the problem alone; in such cases I think supranational coordination is not merely permitted but required by subsidiarity properly understood. (5) I also hold that democratic legitimacy requires citizens to be able to identify clearly which level of government is responsible for a given decision, which is why I am skeptical of EU governance mechanisms that obscure that line of accountability. Do these five commitments hang together? In particular, does my argument in (4) about climate change undermine the strict anti-harmonization stance I take in (3), or can I distinguish the two without special pleading? And does my demand in (5) for clear democratic identification conflict with my praise in (2) for the Swiss system, given that Swiss direct democracy operates simultaneously at federal, cantonal, and communal levels, often on overlapping issues? I am not asking for a general survey of federalist theory; I want an audit of this specific set. Please order the tensions by severity, identify any that are only apparent, and for each genuine conflict indicate the cheapest revision and what it costs me elsewhere in the package.
