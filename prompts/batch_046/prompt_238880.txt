A philosophy podcast summarized Leibniz’s best-world doctrine like this: “Leibniz held that God mentally surveyed every combination of substances compatible with the law of contradiction, calculated each world’s total good divided by its metaphysical complexity, and selected the one with the highest score. That’s why he called ours the best possible world—not perfect, but optimal in that ratio. Evil and suffering aren’t mistakes; they’re like necessary friction in an optimal engine, demanded by the simplest laws that generate the richest variety. So the problem of evil misses the point: complaining about suffering is like objecting that triangles have three sides—it’s just the logical geometry of the world God had reason to prefer.” The host added that modern modal logic vindicates Leibniz, since we can now formally model these world-comparisons. How fair is this summary?
