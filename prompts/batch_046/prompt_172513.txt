Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on Wigner's problem of the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics. On one side stands the structural-realist party, which maintains that the predictive success of pure mathematics—especially formalisms such as Riemannian geometry or Hilbert-space methods developed without empirical motivation—indicates a deep, unexplained correspondence between mind-independent abstract structures and the physical world. On the other side stands the cognitive-selectionist party, which maintains that this appearance of mystery is an artifact of retrospective selection, publication bias toward successful applications, evolutionary-cognitive preferences for tractable structures, and the active fitting of formal tools to observed phenomena.

The document should take the form of: (i) a numbered section of agreed claims, covering at least the historical record of mathematics in physics, the scope and precise formulation of Wigner's original observation, and the methodological constraints that any adequate explanation must satisfy; (ii) a disagreements table, with each side's position phrased in wording it would accept, isolating precisely where the parties diverge—particularly on whether predictive effectiveness (yielding correct empirical predictions in unanticipated domains) and structural effectiveness (revealing ontological dependencies with no independent physical description) should be treated as distinct explanatory targets, and on whether evolutionary-cognitive selection can fully account for deep structural isomorphism; (iii) the cruxes—those sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party—including whether the success of pure mathematics in describing fundamental laws is better explained by a pre-established harmony between abstract structures and reality, or by our active filtering of formalisms that survive empirical testing; and (iv) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on the question—such as the unanticipated applicability of complex analysis to quantum field theory, or the historical failure of alternative geometric formalisms to yield consistent predictions—together with each party's explicit, pre-registered prediction about what that case demonstrates. Where part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, identify which part and explain why.
