I am preparing materials for an advanced undergraduate examination in moral and political philosophy. Please construct a single 90-minute essay question worth 30 marks that compares German folk morality—centred on rule-following, institutional order (Ordnung), and collective duty—with American folk morality—centred on individual conscience, personal authenticity, and the right to dissent. Embed a concrete hypothetical: a mid-level administrator discovers that rigid adherence to bureaucratic rules will cause serious, preventable harm, and must choose between institutional loyalty and unauthorised intervention. Request a model answer of roughly 700 words, written in British English, organised around five required moves: (1) a nuanced account of both folk moralities that avoids reducing them to formal Kantian ethics or to unreflective individualism; (2) an explicit analysis of the transparency/opacity distinction and how it operates within each framework; (3) a rigorous application to the hypothetical that addresses the moral cost of breaking institutional loyalty and the problem of institutional complicity; (4) a defended verdict on which framework has the better of it, engaging with one serious counter-argument; and (5) a conclusion that explains the stakes for moral psychology rather than merely repeating the case outcome. Provide a marking scheme (30 marks total) that separates an A answer (25–30) from a B answer (20–24) by naming the precise moves each must contain, and list the specific errors that should reduce marks—for instance, treating moral opacity as simple ignorance, ignoring the institutional dimension of the case, or conflating folk norms with philosophical doctrines. All output should be in British English and should contain no bibliographic references, reading lists, or requests for citations.
