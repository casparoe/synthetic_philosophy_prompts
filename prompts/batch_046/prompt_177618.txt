Before I compare existing accounts of how psychiatric categories interact with the people they classify, I want the full specification of what any adequate theory must deliver. Write the desiderata for a theory of psychiatric classification that takes Hacking's looping effects seriously—not as a decorative citation but as a structural feature of the account.

The list should cover: the phenomena it must explain, including first-order adaptation of patients to diagnostic labels, second-order revision of clinical criteria in response to observed adaptations, and reflexive institutional calibration (where manual revisions are drafted in anticipation of predicted looping); the distinctions it must preserve, such as looping versus mere labeling effects, kind-making versus kind-finding, and prevalence shifts driven by awareness versus those driven by criteria change; the empirical constraints it cannot violate, including epidemiological patterns across successive editions of major diagnostic manuals and neurobiological findings that remain stable across cultural contexts; and the conceptual puzzles it must dissolve, especially why looping does not entail that a condition is unreal and how kind-stability can coexist with malleability in kind-membership.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or consideration, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference (social constructionist, biological realist, pragmatic clinician). Identify any subset of desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable—for example, whether demanding both precise empirical predictability of looping dynamics and full preservation of biological kind-realism generates an unavoidable tension—and explain the conflict's source.

Finally, briefly score three broad approaches against your list: Hacking's interactive-kind framework, a strong biological-realist view that denies looping for core psychiatric disorders, and a purely discursive-constructionist view that treats classification as fully performative. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justification; the scoring should only illustrate how the list operates.
