I'm preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on personal identity and metaphysics of origin, and I want to move past the standard essentialism-versus-anti-essentialism framing. Specifically, I'm interested in whether an individual is essentially tied to the particular gametes from which they developed, to the particular zygote formed, or merely to being part of a continuous genetic lineage that might have been instantiated differently. To that end, please construct a taxonomy—presented as a branching decision tree—of positions on whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg.

The tree should begin with a concrete case: suppose that at conception, sperm A fertilized egg B, but due to a microscopic laboratory error, sperm C (genetically near-identical to A) was substituted without altering the resulting zygote's phenotype. From there, each branch should be labeled by a diagnostic question that forces a separation: for instance, whether modal identity requires identity of the actual gametes, identity of the actual zygote, or only continuity of a genetic lineage that could have been instantiated differently. The leaves should include strict gametic essentialism (the specific sperm and egg are necessary), zygotic essentialism (the specific fusion event is necessary, but not necessarily those exact gametes), lineage essentialism (only membership in a continuous genetic line matters), and anti-essentialism (none of these constrain modal origins). Additionally, include a branch for twin-zygote replacement—where the original zygote is destroyed and a genetically identical twin zygote develops in its place—to see whether continuity of development or identity of the initial cell cluster is the decisive factor.

Please phrase every node as a question answerable without knowing philosophical labels, so the tree sorts people by their intuitive judgments about these cases rather than by which name they prefer. Note any pairs of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate—particularly whether zygotic and lineage essentialism collapse in all realistic scenarios—and briefly diagnose why that gap exists, whether because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the point is to map the logical space from the cases themselves.
