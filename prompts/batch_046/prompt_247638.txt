As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun serious study in philosophy of mind and language, I find that debates about whether statistical machine translation possesses genuine semantic understanding—debates familiar from Douglas Hofstadter's criticisms—tend to collapse because participants never specify the full specification an adequate theory must meet. Rather than rehearse familiar examples, I want the criteria themselves constructed with precision.

Write the complete desiderata that any theory of comprehension in statistical or neural machine translation must satisfy to count as adequate, not merely predictively successful. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual distinction behind it, and flag whether it is non-negotiable or a preference belonging to a particular camp (for instance, those requiring explicit symbol-manipulation, those content with functional equivalence, or those insisting on embodied or causal grounding).

The specification should address at least the following:
- how the theory distinguishes correct lexical substitution from genuine representational content about the subject matter being translated;
- its treatment of grammatically fluent outputs that silently collapse distinctions present in the source—such as differences in evidential source, temporal aspect, or ontological framing—rather than producing an obvious lexical error;
- whether it requires cross-categorial semantic resilience: the preservation of referential coherence when syntactic restructuring between languages forces a shift in grammatical ontology (for example, translating between systems with incompatible commitments regarding countability, animacy hierarchies, or modal evidentiality);
- its account of failure on semantically anomalous yet grammatically well-formed inputs that lie outside the training distribution but are conceptually intelligible;
- consistency with technically feasible architectures, without invoking homuncular mechanisms or requiring capacities that current distributional methods demonstrably lack;
- and the boundary conditions under which high statistical fidelity across parallel corpora can be taken as evidence for comprehension rather than as sophisticated pattern matching.

Next, identify any subset of these desiderata that is jointly unsatisfiable in principle, given established theoretical constraints—such as the symbol-grounding problem for purely distributional representations, or arguments that interpretation requires causal interaction with the domain referred to—and specify exactly where the tension arises.

Finally, briefly evaluate three broad contender frameworks against your list: (i) pure statistical or neural translation without an intermediate symbolic layer; (ii) hybrid architectures that interpose an explicit formal interlingua or structured semantic representation; and (iii) embodied or multimodal models that anchor linguistic tokens in non-linguistic sensorimotor or perceptual representations. Keep the scoring concise; the primary deliverable is the criteria list itself, particularly in distinguishing authentic requirements from camp-specific prejudices, and in identifying which single desideratum does the most work in excluding the largest number of existing proposals.
