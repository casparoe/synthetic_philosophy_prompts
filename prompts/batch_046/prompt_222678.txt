Engineer the case so that (1) a substantive definition of democratic backsliding—one that treats the weakening of institutional checks as sufficient—classifies Valeria as backsliding, and (2) a minimalist, electoralist definition—one that requires stolen elections, banned opposition, or explicit rights revocation—classifies Valeria as not backsliding. State the scenario, the institutional method, the relevant nearby worlds (e.g., worlds in which elections are rigged or rights are suspended), and explain precisely why each definition delivers its verdict. Identify the single feature that generates the divergence, explain what happens to both verdicts if that feature is removed (for instance, if council members are elected rather than appointed), and present the minimalist defender’s strongest attack on the substantive classification—specifically, why structural change that leaves electoral competition intact should not count. Separate settled claims (e.g., that democratic backsliding lacks a universally agreed definition) from genuinely contested ones (e.g., whether norm erosion without electoral manipulation qualifies), and do not present any contested view as the consensus. The purpose is to test conceptual boundaries, not to stipulate verdicts: the classifications must follow from the definitions you apply, not from ad hoc stipulation. Write no more than 500 words.
