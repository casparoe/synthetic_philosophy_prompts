Consider a formal self-amending rule-system modelled on Peter Suber's Nomic, but augmented by a 'stability gradient': each rule carries an integer rank, and an amendment procedure is valid only if its own rank strictly exceeds that of the target rule. The system also contains a closure rule of rank 0, which states that it may itself be amended by any procedure, irrespective of rank. Explain the conceptual problem that arises when one applies the gradient to this closure rule. Is the tension a syntactic contradiction within the rule texts, a semantic failure of the system to fix its own authority, or a distinct variety of legal self-reference that resembles yet differs from the Liar paradox? Furthermore, does the rank-indexing genuinely dissolve the circularity by externalising the meta-level, or does it merely relocate the loop into the ordering relation itself? Clarify why an agent reasoning solely from within the system's internal rules might be unable to determine the legal state after an attempted amendment without invoking an external arbiter.
