I am an undergraduate serving on my university's Student Committee on Arts Funding and Civic Engagement, and this semester I am enrolled in a political philosophy course that examines Rousseau's critique of theatrical representation. Our committee must decide by next Friday whether to approve a $40,000 grant proposal for an immersive virtual-reality theater project titled "The Spectator Becomes Citizen." The proposal claims that by using haptic feedback and distributed narrative control, it eliminates the passive spectatorship Rousseau condemns, transforming audience members into co-creators of a civic ritual rather than consumers of illusion. The faculty advisor argues this realizes the kind of active, participatory festival Rousseau praises over representational theater; the student opposition argues that any mediated representation, even interactive, still relies on artificial identification and emotional manipulation, and that funding it contradicts our mandate to support genuinely communal, non-representational arts.

Please draft the formal decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with specific conditions and a six-month review timeline—and should engage both positions seriously. In particular, I want you to address whether Rousseau's distinction between corrupting spectacle and unifying festival can survive translation into technologically mediated participation, or whether the medium itself undermines the civic equality he demands. Include an honest account of the strongest argument for the position your recommendation ultimately rejects. Write in the voice of the committee chair; avoid mere restatement of Rousseau's text; and produce something the applicants and opposition could respond to with concrete revisions or objections.
