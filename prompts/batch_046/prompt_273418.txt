As a retired physicist now studying philosophy of biology, I am struck by a tension in Darwin's own framework. His theory of natural selection requires discrete replicators, blind variation, and differential retention—conditions satisfied by genes but apparently not by human culture, which involves intentional design and Lamarckian instruction. If Darwin were alive today and confronted not only with Dawkins's meme concept but also with recent computational models that treat digital content as replicators subject to algorithmic selection, and further with the emergence of large language models that generate synthetic cultural variants without human intention behind each token, how would he classify these phenomena? Would he see LLM outputs as a genuinely novel replicator class that extends his framework, or as a form of intelligent design that violates its core conditions? I am especially interested in whether he would revise his criteria for natural selection itself, or maintain that culture—whether human or synthetic—simply falls outside its domain.
