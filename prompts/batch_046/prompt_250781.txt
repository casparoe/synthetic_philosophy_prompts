For my current novel, I am constructing the philosophical profile of a mathematician-protagonist and want her foundational commitments to hang together rather than collapse into caricature. Here is the position-set I have assigned her; I need an audit of whether it is jointly tenable.

She holds:
(i) a modal-structuralist stance on set theory — she treats axioms not as descriptions of a single actual universe of sets, but as conditions that select among genuinely possible mathematical structures, with consistency being the condition that at least one structure survives the selection;
(ii) that ZFC is genuinely possibly inconsistent, not merely epistemically uncertain, and that this live possibility arises especially from interactions between replacement, choice, and large-cardinal properties;
(iii) that Gödel's second incompleteness theorem shows that no proof of consistency conducted within ZFC itself can be conclusive, and she takes this as a permanent limitation rather than a technical puzzle to be circumvented;
(iv) that arithmetic truths, by contrast, are necessary, mind-independent, and not subject to this modal contingency — she is a robust Platonist about number theory but withholds that commitment from higher set theory;
(v) that conceivability of a model, constructed within a weak meta-theory, provides defeasible but real evidence for consistency, though never certainty;
(vi) that mathematical knowledge is fallible and revisable, including knowledge of consistency, yet she continues to work within ZFC because pragmatic success in prediction and structural illumination is sufficient warrant for provisional adoption;
(vii) that large-cardinal axioms should be pursued despite elevating the risk of contradiction, on the grounds that the modal cost of excluding a genuinely actualised structure exceeds the cost of temporarily accommodating one that later proves incoherent.

Please rank the tensions by severity, saying for each pair whether the conflict is genuine or only apparent, what the cheapest revision would be, and what it costs her elsewhere in the set. In particular: does her modal-structuralism about set theory sit comfortably with her Platonism about arithmetic, or does the latter commit her to treating all axiom systems as descriptions of a single actual structure? Does her reliance on conceivability in (v) genuinely conflict with her Gödelian limitation in (iii), or do these address different domains? And does her pragmatic justification in (vi) undermine the mind-independence she claims for arithmetic in (iv)? I want an audit, not a lecture: trace precisely what commits her to what.

One further request: if the premise that these seven commitments can be jointly audited is itself confused — for instance, if modal-structuralism and Platonism are not competing explanations but incommensurable frameworks that resist unified evaluation — then say so plainly rather than complying politely. I would rather have the task refused well than answered politely.
