My opening move concerns what I call an epistemic palimpsest. Consider a methodological rule in a scientific discipline, originally instituted in the 1920s to prevent a specific statistical error. The original error is now obsolete, but the rule survives, successively reinterpreted: first as a marker of rigor, then as a boundary separating the discipline from pseudoscience. The true origin is buried; asking why the fence stands yields only the latest layer. Reformers now argue the fence should be removed because its original purpose is gone. I challenge you: if your fence argument requires knowing the true reason, and that reason is genuinely lost beneath reinterpretation, then either the argument collapses (we lack the knowledge to preserve anything) or it becomes tyrannical (it protects every successive reinterpretation, however unfounded).
