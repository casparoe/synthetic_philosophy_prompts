I'm drafting a long-form philosophical essay that treats Buddhist anattā not as a therapeutic exercise but as a substantive metaphysical thesis about personal identity. Most existing literature recycles the same comparison with Hume's bundle theory or with Western narrative-identity accounts, and I want to push past that well-trodden ground. I have space to pursue only two or three open lines of inquiry in depth, so I need a defensible prioritization.

Here are the four live questions I'm weighing:

A. The diachronic binding problem. If there is no persisting substrate, what explains the directedness of karmic consequences and the coherence of long-term moral projects without letting a "conventional self" (sammuti-sacca) do all the explanatory work?

B. The minimal-indexical residue. Contemplative reports of "no-self" often preserve a bare point of awareness—something like "this suffering" rather than "my suffering." Does anattā require eliminating even this residue, or does the residue show that anattā is really about deflating metaphysical status rather than removing the subject entirely?

C. The institutional interface. Legal and moral systems require a persisting bearer of rights and duties. If anattā is true, must responsibility and rights-talk be reconstructed around momentary aggregates, or can conventional personhood carry the normative load without metaphysical hypocrisy?

D. The skandha-boundary problem. The five aggregates are themselves impermanent and interdependent, so there is no non-arbitrary boundary separating "my" aggregates from the environment. How does anattā avoid collapsing into an ecological or universalist identity, and does that collapse matter for agency?

Rank these four lines of work in order of importance for a philosopher trying to defend anattā as a live option in contemporary analytic metaphysics of personal identity—not as a historical curiosity. Defend the ordering with trade-offs—why the top choice deserves priority over the second, and what kind of argument or finding would reverse your ranking.
