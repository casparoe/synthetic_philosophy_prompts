Two grad students in my seminar got stuck arguing about Al-Ghazali's occasionalism right after I mentioned the cotton-burning example. (1) She said occasionalism kills causation dead: if God creates every fire directly, saying the flame caused the burn is flat false, and science just collapses into theology with extra steps. (2) He said Al-Ghazali doesn't deny correlation—he only denies necessary connection. The flame is the occasion and God's habit makes it regular, so science tracks divine custom, not hidden natural powers. (3) She shot back that this is just Humean constant conjunction in religious clothing; the predictions are identical, so occasionalism explains nothing extra, it just renames the pattern. (4) He came up with a twist: imagine a flame touches cotton but God, breaking His usual pattern, creates ice instead. On occasionalism thats metaphysically possible—no contradiction—whereas on a real-causation view its impossible because fire's essence requires heat. So occasionalism makes a real modal difference, not just a rename. (5) She said that only proves occasionalism is weirder, not truer, and invoking broken divine habits to save modal space is totally ad hoc unless we actually see patterns breaking, which we dont.
