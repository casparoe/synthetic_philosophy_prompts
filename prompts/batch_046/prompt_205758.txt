In a seminar on collective epistemology, a colleague blocked a proposed group statement with the slogan "assert only what you know." The context was a distributed scientific collaboration in which no single researcher could defend every component of the conclusion, though each trusted the others' sub-results. I want to understand what precise work that slogan could be doing here. Can you lay out the distinct theses it might express—distinguishing individual knowledge, joint knowledge, and distributed tracking; distinguishing belief norms, truth norms, and knowledge norms; and distinguishing norms on utterance from norms on institutional endorsement? For each reading, say whether it is trivial, defensible, or false, and whether it actually supports my colleague's conclusion that the consortium should remain silent. Also tell me which reading people usually invoke when they use the phrase to shut down an argument, and whether that reading survives the collective case.
