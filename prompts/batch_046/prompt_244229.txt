I'm preparing a job-market talk for a metaphysics and philosophy of science seminar defending what I'm calling boundary-indexed ontology. The core thesis is that ontological commitment should be indexed to an observer's in-principle epistemic boundary—roughly, the region of spacetime from which information could ever be received—rather than to any putative global structure. I argue that if two competing global ontologies induce perfectly identical structural descriptions across every region inside that boundary, then they are not genuinely distinct ontologies but representational variants of the same local ontology, even if they diverge radically about causally disconnected domains like regions beyond the cosmological horizon, the interior of an eternal black hole, or other Everettian branches. To avoid collapsing into standard Quinean ontological relativity, I frame this not as a claim about linguistic frameworks but as a "structural closure argument": accessibility constrains not just our knowledge but the conditions under which ontological disagreement is substantive, because ontology without differential structural manifestation inside the boundary lacks the kind of empirical anchoring that makes quantification over entities meaningful in the first place.

The audience will be a philosophy department with a strong metaphysics group and several philosophers of physics. There will be committed structural realists who already think ontology is structure-only and will wonder why I need an accessibility boundary at all; Quinean naturalists who will insist that our best physics quantifies globally and that I am illicitly restricting ontology to epistemology; at least one philosopher of quantum mechanics who will press me on whether Everettian branches count as "inaccessible" in the right way; and a grounding metaphysician who will ask what grounds the boundary itself if not some global fact. I want a Q&A prep sheet: the objections they will actually raise, ordered by how damaging they are, phrased in the vocabulary each subgroup uses, with what I should concede up front, what I must hold without qualification, a concise reply for each, and an explicit note on where that reply is weakest. Include the one objection I am hoping nobody asks—probably something about whether my view makes ontology observer-dependent in a viciously relativistic way, or about the grounding of the boundary—and tell me how to survive it if it comes. Do not ask me for citations or reading lists; I need to defend the position, not demonstrate bibliography. Keep the framing concrete: reference specific cases like horizon-scale divergence or black-hole complementarity only as illustrations, not as the argument's foundation.
