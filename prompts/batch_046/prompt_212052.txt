Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of the unity of consciousness must meet if it aims to account for split-brain phenomena—specifically, cases following complete or partial corpus callosotomy in which the cerebral hemispheres appear to support distinct perceptual, mnemonic, and volitional streams. For each requirement, provide the motivating empirical observation or conceptual distinction behind it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference (e.g., preservation of first-person singularity, parsimony about the number of subjects, consistency with known neuroanatomy). Be sure to include a constraint that is rarely foregrounded: the theory must explain how residual interhemispheric coordination—mediated by subcortical pathways and the anterior commissure—differs in kind from the integrated unity found in intact brains, without either treating it as full unity or dismissing it as irrelevant noise. Identify any pair of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, especially tensions between accommodating genuinely independent hemispheric agency and maintaining a single bearer of conscious experience. Do not provide a bibliography or score existing theories at length; keep the emphasis on the criteria, their motivations, and their conflicts.
