Write an essay addressing the following controversy. In modal metaphysics, counterpart theory holds that an individual exists in only one possible world and is represented elsewhere by a counterpart that resembles it, whereas transworld-identity theory permits the very same individual to exist in multiple worlds. Rather than rehearsing the familiar debate over personal origin essentialism, apply both frameworks to a more demanding case: the modal persistence of collective institutional entities—specifically, a supreme constitutional court that, in a counterfactual scenario, retains its jurisdictional function and legal authority but has undergone complete turnover of its membership and substantial revision of its procedural rules.

Your essay should briefly clarify the core ontological divergence between the two views in your own terms, without providing a bibliography, reading list, or survey of the literature. Then analyze the institutional case in detail: under counterpart theory, what makes the counterfactual court a counterpart rather than the original, and what relations (functional continuity, rule-structure similarity, causal-historical linkage) ground that counterpart relation? Under transworld identity, what essential properties—if any—must the court retain to remain numerically identical across worlds, and how does this view handle radical changes in membership and procedure?

Reach and defend a clear conclusion about which framework yields the more plausible account of institutional persistence. Your justification should directly engage the strongest objection to your position: either that your view collapses into an implausible essentialism about institutional origins and membership, or that it renders institutional identity so fluid and relational that it loses its normative and legal utility for reasoning about continuity of authority, precedent, and obligation. The essay should be rigorous, formally structured, and written for an audience familiar with basic modal metaphysics but not requiring specialized knowledge of institutional ontology.
