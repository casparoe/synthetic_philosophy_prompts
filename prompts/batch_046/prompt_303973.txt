hey — i'm putting together a piece on luck egalitarianism and want to check if my own commitments hang together before i quote them. here's where i stand:

1. i'm basically a luck egalitarian: people shouldn't suffer for brute luck, but it's okay if they do worse from genuine choices.

2. i think we need an authenticity filter for option luck — you only really "chose" something if you formed the preference under conditions of reflective autonomy, not just causal authorship.

3. but i also think adaptive preferences (like people in bad situations lowering their expectations) should be treated as autonomous and not compensated, even when they were shaped by unjust background conditions.

4. i think natural talents and effort are both partly brute luck — genes, upbringing, all that — but i still think a just society should reward productivity and hard work in teh market.

5. i think there should be an unconditional social floor, so nobody falls below basic subsistence even if they made reckless choices.

6. i think relational equality matters, but it operates on status and respect, not on resources, so allowing resource gaps from option luck doesn't automatically violate it.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which ones just look bad, and rank them by severity. for each real tension say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. then tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess. don't just list them all as equally bad — some of these tensions are probably fake.
