I have been trying to understand whether all creative thinking is just parameter-tweaking on ideas we already possess — like turning knobs on a mixing board, where the instruments stay the same but their balance shifts. The analogy is used to defend the view that even apparent breakthroughs are fundamentally variations on a theme: a composer preserves an underlying melody while adjusting tempo, harmony, or register, just as a thinker might slide mental knobs controlling abstraction, scale, or emotional valence. I want to audit how far this holds. What in actual conceptual change corresponds precisely to the knobs, the board itself, and the final mixed output? Where does the mapping break — for instance, with acts that seem to invent entirely new categories rather than adjusting existing ones, or with the essential role of accident and error? Does the analogy quietly import the presence of an external listener judging whether the mix sounds right, and the assumption that there is an optimal setting? Most importantly, if these disanalogies are serious, can the mixer-board model still support the claim that all creativity is variation on a pre-existing theme, or must we allow that some creative acts replace the board altogether?
