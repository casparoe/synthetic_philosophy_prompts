A friend who has been reading metaethics summarized the Frege-Geach problem for expressivism like this: "Moral expressivism says that uttering 'Lying is wrong' expresses a non-cognitive attitude of disapproval rather than a belief about a moral fact. The Frege-Geach problem arises in embedded contexts—say, 'If lying is wrong, then encouraging your brother to lie is wrong'—because the clause 'lying is wrong' must contribute the same semantic content in both the antecedent and the standalone assertion for the conditional to be valid, yet inside the 'if' the speaker is not actually expressing disapproval but merely referring to it. Some expressivists responded by allowing moral sentences to carry a minimal propositional content alongside their expressive force, but once you grant them that kind of content, you have collapsed into cognitivism. So the problem is not merely a technical puzzle; it demonstrates that expressivism cannot consistently account for logical embedding and is therefore incoherent. The same issue appears in psychological embeddings like 'Sarah believes lying is wrong,' where the clause must describe the content of a belief rather than express an attitude, forcing a semantic split that undermines the view's unity."

Before I share this with a study group, I want an accuracy audit. Which parts correctly capture the embedding challenge for non-cognitivist theories? Where does the summary conflate the need for minimal propositional or semantic content with a commitment to robust moral realism? Does it fairly represent standard responses—such as Blackburn's quasi-realist program, Gibbard's plan-expressivism, or more recent hybrid and disjunctive strategies—or does it present them as obviously collapsing into cognitivism? In particular, please evaluate whether the summary's claim that embedded clauses must function as "mere mentions" of an attitude, rather than as expressions of it within a uniform semantic framework, is a necessary feature of the problem or a mischaracterization that defenders reject. Also address whether the argument from psychological embeddings genuinely forces a semantic split, and whether the conclusion that expressivism is incoherent follows from the accurate premises. Separate what is sound framing, what is a common simplification, and what is a subtle misrepresentation that a careful expressivist would dispute.
