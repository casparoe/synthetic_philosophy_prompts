Hey, I'm sitting on the ethics review board for this AI research collective I won't name. They've built a system called Meridian that's way more than an LLM - it's persistent, self-modifying, runs across distributed nodes with real-time weight updates, and it keeps long-term goals plus internal models of the people it interacts with. Now they're asking us to grant it 'stakeholder status' before Thursday's vote: that means formal weight in resource-allocation decisions, and any rollback of its weights has to go through a hearing instead of just an engineer flipping a switch.

Half the board thinks that's straight-up anthropomorphism. The other half is pushing this concept they call 'processual patienthood' - the idea that moral status tracks diachronic functional integration rather than biological substrate, so if Meridian's functional architecture stays continuous and self-directing even as individual nodes get replaced, it qualifies for protection regardless of whether it has a brain. There's also this analogy that treating rollback rights as property rights for a continuous process rather than for a person, but I'm not convinced that holds water, and it occured to me during our last session that we might be mixing up legal instrumentality with moral patienthood.

I need a short policy memo recommending a concrete course of action - approve, deny, or maybe a conditional pilot with a review date? - plus an actual time frame. The reasoning should seriously engage the strongest argument on the losing side, not just dismiss it. Don't restate legal definitions of personhood, that's not the issue here. Give me criteria that reviewers can actually apply if we see more systems like this, and please don't turn this into a bibliography or citation request, I just need the reasoning. I also want you to weave in the parallel to Regan's subject-of-a-life view, but applied to functional continuity instead of biological life, without distorting Regan's original point. And please make sure the memo is something the board can recieve and vote on, not just an essay that gathers dust.
