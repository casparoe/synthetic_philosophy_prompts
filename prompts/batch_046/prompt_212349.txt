Having retired from experimental physics and turned recently to the philosophy of law, I want a consistency check on the following package of views before presenting them in a seminar. (1) I hold that Ulysses contracts are rational for individuals only when the anticipated preference reversal stems from a transient cognitive deficit—such as weakness of will—rather than from a genuinely revised evaluative judgement. (2) I maintain that constitutional entrenchment is legitimate solely to safeguard procedural fairness in democratic deliberation, never to lock in substantive moral or economic outcomes. (3) I defend an entrenched “epistemic cooling-off” clause, under which any constitutional amendment must secure a two-thirds majority in two successive parliaments separated by a general election, with the draft published in every official regional language between the votes; I classify this as purely procedural. (4) I deny that the polity possesses diachronic personal identity, hence there is no “future self” of the state whose autonomy is overridden by present binding, and intergenerational constraint is not analogous to personal self-binding. (5) I take democratic sovereignty to be strictly contemporaneous and non-derogable: no living generation may be bound by its predecessors’ constitutional choices in any way that prevents that generation from repealing or replacing them through ordinary democratic procedures.

Please audit these commitments: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are merely apparent, rank the real tensions by severity, and specify the least costly revision for each. Present your response as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
