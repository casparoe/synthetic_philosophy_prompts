the great-man vs structural explanation debate about nazi germany and hitler has been stuck forever. one camp lists versailles, the depression, and long-term german nationalism as sufficient conditions for some authoritarian turn; the other insists hitlers specific rhetoric and organizational choices were necessary for the holocaust specifically, not just a generic right-wing regime. i think part of the stall is people sliding between asking whether fascism was inevitable and whether this exact regime with these exact crimes was inevitable, but maybe theres something deeper. is the disagreement really about causal overdetermination, or about which counter-factual worlds count as close enough? heres a niche framing: what if we treat leadership like a control parameter near a bifurcation point, where tiny differences produce totally different attractors? would that actually resolve anything, or just disguise the same intuition clash about human agency in math? diagnose whether this is verbal, substantive, or two questions under one flag, say what kind of progress could move it -- maybe a formal model of leadership substitutability, maybe a split between regime-type and regime-token causation -- and be honest about whether anything could actually end the debate or if its just bedrock.
