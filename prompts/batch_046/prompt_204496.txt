I'm preparing the final exam for an advanced philosophy-of-law seminar that treats legal logic, institutional design, and self-reference. Please draft one essay question suitable for a 75-minute response, together with a model answer and a marking scheme.

The question should present students with a novel hypothetical constitutional provision—call it Article Z—whose text is embedded in the prompt. Article Z should state that: (i) Article Z can only be amended by a constituent assembly whose members are appointed according to rules set out in Article Z; and (ii) any court interpreting Article Z must treat it as legally binding on that assembly, unless and until the assembly amends Article Z in conformity with its own provisions. Students should be asked whether this structure produces a logical paradox of the kind illustrated by self-amending rule systems such as Nomic, or whether it is better understood as a difficult but non-paradoxical problem of constitutional interpretation. They should be required to distinguish three failure modes precisely: strict logical contradiction; infinite regress of authorizing authority; and a disguised collapse into judicial discretion.

Please organize your response in the following sections, in this order:
1. The exam question (about 250 words): present the full prompt as it would appear on the exam paper, including the verbatim text of Article Z.
2. Model answer (about 600 words): lay out the moves a first-class answer must contain—how to separate logical from interpretive paradox, how to apply the Nomic-style framework without conflating game rules with legal norms, and how to assess each of the three failure modes—without invoking specific judicial decisions or author names.
3. Marking scheme (about 300 words): identify five must-hit analytical points totaling 20 marks, specify the marks for each, and provide a one-line description of the characteristic error or omission that should reduce a candidate's score on that point.

The deliverable should not include reading lists, bibliographies, or requests for citation. The hypothetical Article Z should be original to this prompt, and the analysis should be accessible to someone with no web access.
