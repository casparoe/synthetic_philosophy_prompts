Consider a framework in which moral agents hold heterogeneous moral currencies—rights-credits functioning as non-negotiable side-constraints for deontologists, and utility-credits that aggregate linearly for consequentialists. Within this framework, partial moral compromise can be modeled as bilateral exchange: each party trades marginal utility-credits for rights-credits they value more highly, without breaching either agent's core reservation constraints. Argue that such exchanges constitute genuine Pareto improvements within the moral domain, and that they avoid the standard charge of selling out one's values precisely because no agent is forced below their reservation set.
