hey, i'm a retired physicist who got into philosophy after i retired, mostly reading old texts in translation. lately i've been stuck on Al-Ghazali's ocassionalism - the idea that God creates every effect directly and fire doesn't actually burn anything, God just creates the burning when fire and cotton are together. i tried to build an argument showing this proves too much about science, and now i can't see the crack. here's what i have:

(1) If ocassionalism is right, then created things have no causal powers at all; only God produces effects.
(2) But scientific explanation works by citing the powers and regularities of created things - oxidation, heat transfer, etc.
(3) So if ocassionalism holds, every scientific explanation that lists fire or oxygen as a cause is technically false, since those things can't actually cause anything.
(4) Al-Ghazali wasn't anti-science though - he engaged deeply with the sciences of his day and said science describes the habitual course of nature.
(5) So he must allow that scientific models are okay even if they misidentify the real causal agent.
(6) But then "cause" in science just means "regular predecessor," which is Hume's view.
(7) And on Hume's view there's no real necessary connection to break, so miracles aren't special violations - they're just rare events.
(8) So ocassionalism either makes science false (if we reject 5) or collapses into Humeanism and loses its theological point (if we accept 5).

i'd really like to keep (1) and (4) - they feel like the core of the view. is the slip in (3), that science doesn't literally attribute powers to things? or in (6), that allowing non-causal explanation doesn't have to be Humean? what's the cost of giving up each?
