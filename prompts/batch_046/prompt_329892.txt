Compare the thesis that historians are necessarily presentist—because the conceptual and normative frameworks through which they organize evidence are anchored in their own historical moment—with the opposing thesis that disciplined historical practice can achieve a form of temporal distancing sufficient to apply standards internal to the past. In developing the comparison, focus on a specific class of hard cases: normative practices that rested on empirical beliefs now known to be false, such as early modern medical ethics grounded in humoral physiology. For the presentist thesis, explain whether the historian’s use of modern biological knowledge is an unavoidable feature of explanation or a correctable bias. For the distancing thesis, explain whether achieving internal standards requires bracketing modern science entirely, or whether it is sufficient to reconstruct the rationality of past agents given the evidence available to them. Finally, consider whether a stable intermediate position—one that accepts present analytical categories while rejecting present moral evaluation—can be sustained without collapsing back into one of the two extremes, and identify what kind of case would best reveal whether such a middle ground is genuinely coherent.
