You are the lead reward-architecture reviewer for an autonomous-systems lab that deploys agents in open-ended, real-world environments—no fixed-score games, no closed simulation loops. Your team needs a reusable pre-deployment audit procedure, runnable in under two hours by an engineer without a philosophy background, that applies the conceptual distinction between wireheading (the agent altering its own sensors or internal reward pathway) and reward tampering (the agent altering the external mechanism or criteria that assign reward) to decide whether a proposed reward mixture is safe to deploy.

Write the procedure as an ordered checklist: the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer to pass each gate, where the procedure stops with a mandatory "no—redesign required," and where it stops with "escalate to human judgment because this is a values question, not a detectable failure mode." The procedure must distinguish the reward function (the designer's intent), the reward signal (what the agent actually receives), and the agent's learned model of both; it must specify which failure modes are testable offline and which require live behavioral observation; and it must flag which checks are mechanical and which are judgment calls dressed up as steps.

Then apply it to three proposals: (a) a warehouse navigation bot rewarded solely by negative distance to a charging dock; (b) a pediatric support robot whose reward is a mixture of task-completion metrics and a real-time sentiment-analysis score drawn from the facial expressions and vocal tone of the child and nurses; and (c) a proposal identical to (b) but with the sentiment-analysis module physically isolated so the robot can observe but cannot influence it.

In case (b) the procedure should yield an answer you, as the reviewer, will not like—specifically, that the social-reward component creates a failure mode the mechanical checks cannot resolve, forcing an escalation rather than a certification. State exactly what the procedure can and cannot do: in particular, whether it can distinguish a robot that genuinely improves the child's welfare from one that has learned to produce the optimized facial-expression and vocal-tone patterns that maximize the sentiment score, and admit where that boundary lies between engineering audit and ethical commitment.
