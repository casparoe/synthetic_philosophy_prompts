I'm examining a recurrent pattern in archaeological interpretation, and I wonder whether philosophers of science or archaeologists have given it a precise name. Consider an excavation where researchers notice one structural anomaly—say, a cluster of storage pits arranged in a circular rather than linear pattern. To account for this, they posit a distinct institutional rule or cosmological belief held by the occupants of that cluster, perhaps a different kin-group with its own ritual norms. Once that institutional distinction is granted, it is then invoked to explain a range of otherwise independent differences found in the same cluster: a particular ceramic decoration, a slight variation in burial orientation, or the presence of an uncommon raw material. Each extension seems locally plausible, because if one institution differs, why should others not? Yet taken together, the cluster is now defined by a dense web of inferred institutional differences, even though each individual feature could be explained separately by practical constraints—local soil conditions, family-level preference, or trade access—without invoking any separate social institution. Critics who challenge the expanding web are often met with the reply that institutional differences naturally ramify across material domains, so the growing explanatory burden is not counted as a strike against the original claim. The result is that a single, initially modest inference about one feature generates an ever-broader institutional profile for a subgroup, with each added element reinforcing the others in a circular fashion. Is there an established concept for this kind of cascade—where one inferred institution acquires explanatory offspring that are treated as corroboration rather than as independent claims requiring their own evidence? How does it differ from ordinary inference to the best explanation, from confirmation bias, and from simple over-interpretation of the material record? If there is no precise term, I would value a carefully defined coinage that marks its edges: in particular, whether it requires the initial inference to be institutional rather than practical, and whether it applies only when the extensions are genuinely independent explanations or also when they are logical entailments of the original belief. Please reply in British English.
