Write a short story, about 600 words, set in a dusty London workshop where 'conceptual engines' turn ideas into variations by adjusting physical knobs—abstraction, emotional weight, proximity—basically Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model made literal and mechanical. The protagonist is a tired engineer who spends her days tweaking themes for clients; she's brilliant at it, maybe too brilliant, untill she discovers a hidden dial marked 'null' that doesn't vary the base theme but dissolves it into an anti-idea, something that isn't a variation at all but a collapse of the framework itself. She has to decide whether to report it, destroy it, or build around it. The story should feel melancholic, use britsh spelling and grey winter light, and avoid having characters lecture each other about philosophy—show the tension through the clunk of gears and her hand's on the dials. The question it should quietly ask: is creativity just extreme parameter adjustment, or is there a kind of leap that breaks the dial?
