Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers—one defending the self-sampling assumption and one defending the self-indication assumption—discussing the presumptuous philosopher objection within a specific, modified scenario. In this scenario, observers are not scattered across a multiverse but are generated sequentially by a deterministic algorithm with a hard-coded maximum observer count of exactly 10^30; every observer knows the algorithm’s source code, its sequential structure, and its fixed stopping condition. The self-sampling advocate should argue that this knowledge restricts the reference class in a principled way that blocks the presumptuous inference that we should expect to be among the earliest or most numerous observers. The self-indication advocate should respond by arguing that even with a known algorithmic cap, the principle that one’s own existence is evidence for larger populations still predicts with high credence that a randomly selected observer lies near the termination point of the sequence. Ensure genuine back-and-forth: each speaker must directly address the other’s points about reference-class restriction, the appropriate prior over algorithmic parameters, and whether predicting proximity to the hard-coded limit counts as genuinely absurd rather than merely counterintuitive. The dialogue should be self-contained, with no bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading, and should rely solely on conceptual reasoning about anthropic principles and probabilistic self-location.
