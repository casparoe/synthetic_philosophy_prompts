For a graduate seminar on collective rationality, I want a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts philosophical responses to Sen's liberal paradox not by the labels people use, but by the concrete commitments they make about preference domains, vicarious preferences, and the status of Pareto efficiency. The tree should begin with the canonical two-agent, two-option case—each agent has a protected personal sphere (say, which of two books or which of two algorithmic feeds they consume), each holds a strict preference over their own option, and each also holds a vicarious preference over the other agent's option that conflicts with the other agent's own preference.

Frame every branch node as a yes/no or choose-one question that a respondent could answer without knowing which interpretation it maps to. First split: are vicarious preferences over another agent's sphere admissible in social evaluation, or must they be excluded as illegitimate interferences? Second split (independent): is Pareto efficiency non-negotiable, defeasible only when rights are violated, or reconceptualized (e.g., weighted by domain)? Third, introduce the niche extension I want tested: a three-agent cyclic variant in which Agent A's sphere is vicariously preferred by B, B's by C, and C's by A, with each agent's own preference running opposite to the vicarious one directed at them. Ask whether the respondent treats this cyclic extension as collapsing back into the two-agent paradox, as generating a distinct impossibility result, or as resolvable by dynamic domain reassignment. From these answers, map the logical combinations onto actual positions—include at minimum the pure liberal position (retain minimal liberalism, abandon unrestricted Pareto), the pure welfarist position (retain Pareto, restrict liberal domains to non-conflicting ones), and the preference-purification position (filter vicarious preferences before aggregation)—while noting any combination that appears unoccupied and diagnosing whether it is incoherent, merely unexplored, or behaviorally equivalent to an occupied leaf. For each leaf, state its named or unnamed status and its single most awkward commitment. Flag explicitly if the tree cannot separate two of the required positions in the cyclic case. Keep the instrument diagnostic, not bibliographic: no citations, no reading lists, no "who said what" framing beyond the logical commitments themselves.
