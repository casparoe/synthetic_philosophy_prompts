I'm working through some ideas for a side blog about history and tech metaphors. Here's where I'm at:
a) Only humans have real agency — intentions are what make something an actor, not just being part of the causal chain.
b) But climate shocks and plagues are clearly not just background noise. The Tambora eruption in 1815 actually changed political outcomes, and I think you can't tell the story of late antiquity without the 536 volcanic winter and the Justinian plague being central.
c) Calling a volcano an 'actor' feels like sloppy anthropomorphism, even if it's a handy metaphor.
d) At the same time, I like thinking of causation like a distributed system — nodes can have funtional roles without consciousness, so maybe there's a weak 'structural agency' for non-human things.
e) Environmental determinism scares me because it lets people dodge responsibility for policy failures.
f) Still, I think if you're explaining the collapse of the Roman west, climate stress and disease should be treated as primary drivers, not footnotes.

Do these hang together? I'm mostly worried about the agency stuff and whether I'm accidentally being determinist, but I can't tell if I'm double-thinking. Rank the real tensions by severity, say what's just an illusion of conflict, and tell me the cheapest revision for each — no need for a bibliography, just a logic check.
