I cover the philosophy and history of the social sciences for a long-form ideas quarterly. I am preparing a feature interview with Dr. Elena Vasquez, a historian of economic culture who has recently reconstructed Weber's Protestant ethic thesis through a framework she calls diachronic value-filtering. On her account, religious values do not generate capitalist institutions ex nihilo, nor are they reducible to material conditions; instead, they function as selective stabilization mechanisms that, once certain material preconditions become available, render particular institutional paths more durable and less reversible. She rejects both causal exclusivity—the demand that cultural explanations either replace material ones or be eliminated in their favor—and the conventional interaction-effect model used in comparative historical sociology.

Please draft ten interview questions for this conversation. Do not include generic biographical openings, and do not ask for bibliographies or reading recommendations. I want at least two questions pressing her on how diachronic value-filtering produces empirically distinguishable predictions from a standard interaction-effect framework; at least one asking what specific comparative-historical pattern—such as materially comparable regions with divergent confessional outcomes—would genuinely disconfirm her reconstructed thesis rather than merely complicating its scope; and at least one follow-up sequence that anticipates her reply that culture operates causally only in the explanatory mode of reasons rather than in that of forces, then pushes her on whether that distinction collapses once we ask why those particular agents adopted those particular reasons at those particular historical moments. The tone should be journalistic, intellectually serious, and accessible to an educated general reader, with each question carrying its own contextual framing so the printed transcript remains intelligible without footnotes.
