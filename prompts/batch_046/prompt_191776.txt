I just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a close friend about whether Darwin's evolutionary mechanism and Turing's computational framework really establish that competence can exist without comprehension—and, if so, whether that undermines our usual reasons for trusting expert judgment. Tomorrow I am delivering the opening remarks at a one-day symposium called "Intelligence Without Understanding." The audience is deliberately mixed: evolutionary biologists, AI researchers, philosophers of mind, and several non-specialist administrators and donors. I need a four-minute address in American English. The Darwin-Turing inversion should do genuine structural work: it should determine exactly what I can and cannot claim about expertise, humility, and the value of human understanding, rather than serving as decorative framing. Experts should recognize the specific inversion I am invoking—Darwin showing that adaptive design need not imply a designer's comprehension, Turing showing that behavioral competence need not imply internal understanding—while non-experts should still grasp why the symposium's question matters for anyone who relies on skilled judgment. Because I just argued with my friend, the tone should reflect real intellectual tension rather than settled confidence, and it must not resolve the argument for me. Please write the remarks.
