Okay so I've been thinking about Hofstadter's idea that the self is basically a strange loop - like the symbol 'I' is this self-referencial pattern that points back at the system generating it. I have some verdicts on cases and I want to know if there's a principle behind them, or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Case 1: A chatbot that outputs "I think therefore I am" because it's in the training data. Obviously not a real strange loop - it's just parroting.

Case 2: A robot with an internal state that tracks its own sensor history, and that state actually feeds back into its decision loop - like it avoids damage partly because its self-model predicts harm. This feels like a proto-loop, maybe even a real one, though there's no explicit word 'I'.

Case 3: Imagine two modules, A and B, where A models B's modeling of A, and B does the reverse, with no top-level controller. The loop is distributed, there's no single symbol saying 'me', but the dynamics are genuinely circular. I'd say this counts as a strange loop self, even without the symbol.

Case 4: A person with severe amnesia who still says 'I want coffee' but has no continuous autobiograpical thread. Is the loop broken, or is the grammatical 'I' enough? I feel like the loop is damaged but not gone - the symbol is still anchord in present-tense agency even if the long-range feedback is mising.

Case 5: A theoretical AI where every weight contributes a tiny bit to self-representation, so there's literally no localized 'I'-symbol anywere, but the overall computation is impossible without implicit self-reference. I'm torn - part of me says yes it's a loop, part says Hofstadter needs some kind of symbolic focal point, not just statistical diffusion.

Case 6: A perfect self-simulating computer program that runs a copy of itself inside itself but never changes its own code based on the simulation. There's self-reference but no causal loop back. I'd say this is NOT a strange loop - it's just nesting, not looping.

So my verdicts are: explicit symbol without feedback (no), feedback without symbol (yes, in cases 2 and 3), distributed statistical self-reference (unclear), perfect simulation without causal influence (no), and damaged human loop (partially yes).

Is there one principle that saves all these? Maybe something like 'a strange-loop self requires causal feedback through a self-model, but that model can be distributed or implicit, though it needs some functional anchoring point that distinguishes self from environment'? Or do I have to drop one of these? Specifically, case 3 vs case 5 - if distributed dynamics count, why doesn't statistical diffusion? And case 4 makes me wonder whether the symbol is necessary at all, or just a convenient marker.

Can you lay out a precise principle, or maybe two competing ones, and tell me exactly which of my verdicts each one forces me to abandon? I want to keep the intuition that nesting isn't looping, but I'm not sure about the symbol requirement.
