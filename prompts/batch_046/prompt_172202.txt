As a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science, I have been reflecting on Newton’s rotating bucket and its bearing on the debate between spacetime substantivalism and relationism. Newton argued that the concave surface of the water reveals true rotation with respect to absolute space; yet Einstein’s general relativity treats the metric field as dynamical, curved by matter and energy, which suggests to me that spacetime is not an independent container but a relational structure determined by physical interactions. More recently, I have encountered the view—sometimes called spacetime functionalism—that spacetime points are not fundamental entities but functional roles defined within a causal network, and that this finally dissolves the old dichotomy altogether. Given all this, I am puzzled why philosophers continue to treat substantivalism as a coherent live option rather than a relic of pre-relativistic mechanics. Is my reasoning flawed in assuming that a dynamical, field-theoretic ontology necessarily entails relationism, and that functionalism about spacetime points settles Newton’s bucket argument? I should welcome a precise, formal reply in British English that addresses the misconception directly rather than answering the question as posed.
