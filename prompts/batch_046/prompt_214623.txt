I serve as the senior risk assessor for a mid-sized municipal water authority. We are seeing a growing number of proposals to deploy engineered microbial interventions—such as synthetic bacteriophage cocktails or designed microbiome inoculants—into aging distribution networks to suppress biofilm pathogens. These are not conventional chlorine adjustments; they involve living or quasi-living agents whose ecological dynamics inside pipe infrastructure are poorly mapped, and physical reversal is often technically impossible once the agent is established.

Please construct a reusable decision procedure that my review board—civil engineers and public-health officers without philosophy training—can run in a single meeting on any such proposal. Ground the procedure in the precautionary principle, but translate it into an ordered checklist of operational questions. For each question, specify: (1) what kind of technical evidence counts as a sufficient answer; (2) whether the answer must come from staff analysis or forces a value judgment that should be referred to elected officials; and (3) the exact termination condition that produces a rejection. The procedure must distinguish between reducible uncertainty (addressable through monitoring protocols) and structural uncertainty (e.g., evolutionary adaptation of the agent in situ, irreversible ecological locking, or unknown sub-lethal corrosion dynamics). It should also identify the points where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be flagged as such.

Then apply the procedure to three proposals:
1. A synthetic phage cocktail targeting Legionella in a hospital-loop district, with proposed real-time genomic monitoring but no engineered removal pathway if the phage evolves.
2. A microbiome inoculant designed to outcompete nitrifying bacteria in a reservoir, expected to persist for decades, where reversal would require draining the reservoir.
3. A temporary, non-replicating enzymatic biofilm disruptor that degrades within 72 hours and can be flushed, but whose long-term sub-lethal effects on pipe corrosion microbiomes are unknown.

Include at least one case where the procedure returns a recommendation that conflicts with the proponent's stated urgency, and explain why. Also state explicitly what kinds of decisions the procedure cannot settle and must defer to democratic or ethical judgment rather than technical analysis.

Finally, say how confident you are in the procedural translation you have offered, and single out the specific step you are least sure correctly captures the precautionary principle rather than smuggling in an unrelated risk-aversion bias.

Please write in American English.
