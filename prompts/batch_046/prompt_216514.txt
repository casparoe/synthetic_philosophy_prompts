I am constructing a conceptual map for the problem of defining "side-effect avoidance" for artificial agents without supplying a complete axiological specification. Please write a taxonomy in the form of a decision tree that sorts the philosophical positions on whether such a definition is possible.

Structure the tree as follows. The root node should ask whether the concept of a "side effect" can be specified structurally—by reference to causal footprints, reversibility conditions, or deviation from a counterfactual baseline of inaction—or whether it is irreducibly normative, requiring prior knowledge of which outcomes are harmful or beneficial. The second-level nodes should split on whether the appropriate baseline is agent-relative (the difference between the world with this particular agent acting and the world in which it does not) or world-relative (a global standard of acceptable states independent of any particular agent). The third level should ask whether a partial, ordinal, or domain-restricted value specification can substitute for a full cardinal utility function, or whether no substitution is possible.

At each leaf, provide the name of the corresponding position (use established labels where they exist; otherwise label it "unnamed") and a single sentence describing its most awkward practical commitment—such as being forced to treat inaction as maximally good, being unable to distinguish harmful from beneficial disruptions of equal magnitude, or requiring omniscience about values to verify compliance.

In addition, identify two combinations of answers that correspond to no named or defensible position. For each empty region, take a committed view: argue whether the gap exists because the combination is incoherent, because it collapses into a neighboring branch, or because it has simply been overlooked, and state which of these explanations you find most plausible and why.

Finally, flag any pairs of named positions that the tree fails to distinguish, and explain whether that failure reflects a genuine philosophical convergence or merely an inadequacy in the branching questions.

Write in a formal, polished register. Take definite positions rather than neutrally surveying options: commit to which distinctions are load-bearing for actual system design and which are merely verbal, and defend your choice of branching questions against at least one plausible alternative ordering.
