The assignment asked: explain Fitch's paradox of knowability and what it shows about the principle that every truth is in principle knowable, being carefull to distinguish weak knowability (someone eventually could come to know it) from strong knowability (it is logically possible right now for someone to know it).

Here is the student's answer:

Fitch's paradox targets the claim that all true propositions are knowable. Imagine there's a true statement p that nobody currently knows. Then form the conjunction U = p and nobody knows p. U is true, so by the knowability principle it should be knowable too. Picture a world where an agent knows U. Since knowledge is closed under conjunction elimination, she knows p and she knows nobody knows p. But the first part means she knows p, which makes the second part false. So U can't actually be known — it's an unknowable truth. That contradicts the principle, so Fitch concludes there are no unknown truths: omniscience is unavoidable. The mistake people make is thinking this hits strong knowability, but strong versions already treat knowing U as logically impossible, so only weak knowability — which allows eventual knowledge without checking consistency — gets caught in the trap. Thus the paradox shows weak knowability collapses unless we drop closure.
