As a novelist drafting a philosophical dialogue for a character who is a neuroscientist convinced she has catalogued every chromatic quality without ever having experienced crimson herself, I have been studying the argument in which such a figure—often placed in an isolated chamber—supposedly acquires something that physical science could not provide. In reading the surrounding debate, I have repeatedly observed a particular rhetorical oscillation. One interlocutor will maintain that the scientist merely gains a practical ability, comparable to learning a manual skill, and therefore adds no propositional knowledge to her scientific archive. Yet when challenged on why this particular skill feels so categorically unlike other learned capacities, the same interlocutor will shift to describing the event as a direct acquaintance with a property already present in her theoretical framework, as though acquaintance were merely a refined modality of the skill previously invoked. Once the dialectical pressure eases, the speaker returns to the ability-based description, using it to resist the claim that a new fact has been discovered, while implicitly relying on the acquaintance-based description to preserve the intuition that something genuinely transformative has occurred. These are not a defensible core paired with an indefensible exaggeration; they are two equally contentious interpretations that happen to share a single verbal formula. Is there an established concept that names this precise shuttling between an ability-reading and an acquaintance-reading in arguments about phenomenal knowledge? I am not asking for the general motte-and-bailey, since neither interpretation functions as an obviously safe retreat, nor for plain equivocation, since the speaker typically does not recognize the shift. If the literature offers no exact term, I would welcome a coined designation that clearly distinguishes the phenomenon from both of those, together with boundary conditions that indicate where the oscillation ends and ordinary ambiguity begins.
