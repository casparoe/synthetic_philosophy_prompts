my study group tried to apply Lakatos's negative and positive heuristics back onto philosophy of science itself and now we're stuck in a loop.

(1) A research programme is rational only if it's progressive: adjustments to the protective belt must generate novel predictions that get empirically corroborated.
(2) Logical empiricism's hard core was that meaningful statements reduce to observation sentences or logical truths.
(3) Its negative heuristic said don't postulate unobservable entities; its positive heuristic pushed for formal reconstructions of scientific language.
(4) When counterexamples hit (theory-ladenness, Quine's holism), the belt adjusted via Carnap's tolerance principle and then formal reconstructions of theory change, but never risked the hard core with a genuinely new empirical prediction.
(5) So logical empiricism was degenerating.
(6) Now Lakatos's own programme has a hard core: science advances via research programmes with hard cores, belts, and heuristics.
(7) Its negative heuristic rejects ad hoc adjustments that don't increase empirical content; its positive heuristic directs historians to reconstruct scientific development.
(8) But Lakatos never derived a novel empirical prediction or unknown historical fact from this framework; it's purely historiographcal and normative.
(9) So by (1) his own framework is degenerating.
(10) But if Lakatos is degenerating, he has no rational standing to call logical empiricism degenerating in step (5).
(11) So either he self-refutes, or (1) applies only to empirical science and can't touch philosophy.

We feel like there's an equivocation between empirical fruitfulness and conceptual clarity between (4) and (8), but we can't name it precisely. Which premise should we actually drop, and what's the cost? Does this mean Lakatos's criteria can never be turned on methodological theories without self-defeat?
