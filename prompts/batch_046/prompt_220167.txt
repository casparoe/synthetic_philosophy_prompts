My core claims are these. First, the standard Landauer resolution—erasing the demon's memory costs kT ln 2 per bit—is correct but incomplete, because it treats the register as already partitioned into discrete states. The physical act of carving phase space into those macrostates requires correlation with an environment that fixes a pointer basis, and that correlation carries its own unavoidable entropic cost, which I call a coarse-graining debt. The debt isn't paid at erasure; it's paid continuously, because maintaining the distinction between left and right molecules requires sustained interaction with a thermal bath that anchors the basis.

Second, this suggests the demon isn't defeated by the cost of forgetting, but by the cost of remembering in a specific basis. If the demon could track relative correlations without anchoring them to an environmental pointer, it might avoid the debt, but such an observer wouldn't be physically embedded in the same thermodynamic universe as the gas.

Third, I draw a tentative analogy to Bayesian inference: observing a microstate is like conditioning on data, but conditioning requires a model—the macrostate partition—and maintaining that model is thermodynamically expensive. So Maxwell's demon collapses into a problem about the physics of model-selection rather than computation.
