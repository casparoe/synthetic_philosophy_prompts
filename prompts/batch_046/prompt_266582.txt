everyone in my circles keeps saying that studying official history is like looking at a party album the host curated: flattering shots stay, embarassing ones get torn out, and people never invited dont show up at all. they lean on this to claim "history is written by the victors" is basically true and we should treat historical claims as just power dressing up. i want an audit of the analogy. first, the correspondance table: what plays the role of the host, the guests, the torn photos, the album, maybe the camera? then the breaks: what about records that werent performative, like tax rolls or weather logs? do they fit the "curated photo" picture? and there's this idea of counter-archives -- records kept by people who lost, prison ledgers, oral histories -- do they fix the analogy or show it's the wrong frame altogether? finally, does the relativist conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument? keep it casual, a typo or two is totally fine.
