I am a doctoral student in philosophy of value preparing a tutorial for first-year undergraduates on Schlick's account of the meaning of life. Rather than rehearsing the familiar play-versus-work summary, I want the session built around the conceptual dependency between his distinction of Zweck and Sinn—where Sinn denotes an intrinsic structural feature of an activity, not a subjective feeling of satisfaction—and his notion of Jugendlichkeit interpreted not as chronological youth but as a phenomenological suspension of teleological framing, an openness in which present experience carries its own normative weight without being reduced to hedonism. Please design the pedagogical arc: first, the precise order in which these components must be introduced so that the second idea does not collapse into anti-rationalism or mere romantic approval; second, the single concrete carrying example that should anchor the explanation, specifying what active, self-structuring features it must possess and what instrumental structures—external reward, deferred goal, measurable output—it must explicitly lack; third, the misconceptions students will raise in predictable sequence, likely that Schlick endorses laziness, that he denies all value to goal-directed action, that youthfulness is an empirical claim about biological age, and that non-instrumental activity is necessarily frivolous, together with the point in the arc where each should be defused; fourth, the check for understanding that distinguishes having grasped the conceptual distinction from merely adopting an aesthetic posture; and fifth, what elements—such as Schlick's logical-positivist epistemology or specific biographical context—should be explicitly left out at this level, with brief reasoning. Frame this as a formal teaching plan centered on dependency structure and misconception mapping, not as an exposition of Schlick's view itself.
