(1) I accept the BHK interpretation as an account of mathematical truth, not merely a formal semantics: to assert A is, in principle, to possess a construction that verifies it, and there is no further fact of the matter beyond such provability.

(2) I reject the law of excluded middle as a universal logical principle, because I think well-formed propositions about arbitrary infinite choice sequences or unsolved Diophantine problems may lack both proof and refutation without being false.

(3) I hold that excluded middle is valid for every effectively decidable property: if an algorithm terminates with yes or no for each instance, then either all instances satisfy it or there exists a counterexample we can exhibit.

(4) I subscribe to Brouwer's view that mathematics is free mental creation, not discovery in an independent realm; in particular, I treat choice sequences as genuinely free, not predetermined by any law or hidden parameter.

(5) I take the Gödel-Gentzen double-negation translation to show that classical arithmetic is interpretable within intuitionistic logic, which demonstrates that intuitionism is not expressively impoverished, though I do not take this to rehabilitate non-constructive reasoning as legitimate in its own right.

(6) In applied modeling I routinely invoke non-constructive existence theorems—pure existence claims justified by Zorn's lemma or the axiom of choice—and treat them as sufficient, without requiring an explicit algorithm for the object claimed to exist.

(7) I have recently adopted the view that intuitionistic logic should be augmented with an explicit modal operator □A, read as "A is constructively provable at this stage," with accessibility restricted to worlds representing actually completed constructions; I believe this modal extension does not collapse into classical logic.
