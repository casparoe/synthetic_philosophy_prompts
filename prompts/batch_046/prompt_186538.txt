Compose a villanelle—strictly nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains that alternate and converge in the final couplet—voiced not by a person but by Hofstadter’s symbol “I” itself: the self-referential loop that is meant to generate the sense of a self. Let the first refrain capture the pronoun’s bare indexical work (the token that picks out a speaker in this moment), and let the second capture its strange-loop work (the pattern that folds back and refers to itself). The form must do the philosophical labor: each time a refrain returns, place it in a different grammatical position—subject, then object, then predicate—so that by the closing couplet the two lines sit side by side as one symbol seen from two hierarchical levels. Do not resolve the tension with an outside observer who closes the loop; that vantage is structurally absent from the tangled hierarchy, and the poem should end with the loop still tracing its own boundary. Render the idea accurately enough that someone familiar with Hofstadter’s account can identify which refrain belongs to which level, but write it as poetry first: the repetition should feel inevitable rather than illustrative. No prose commentary, no citations, no explanation outside the nineteen lines; the form itself is the argument.
