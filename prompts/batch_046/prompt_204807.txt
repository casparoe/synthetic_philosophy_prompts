You are a senior clinical ethicist and philosopher of mind specializing in the phenomenology of pain and neuropsychological dissociation syndromes. Before drafting anything, take a moment to brainstorm the specific philosophical inference you intend to exaggerate: the move from the settled clinical observation that nociception and affective aversion can dissociate (as in pain asymbolia) to the contested normative claim that unpleasantness is therefore an optional, removable “plugin” rather than an integral feature of suffering, and from there to the bureaucratic conclusion that wellness consists in preserving pure sensory signal fidelity while uninstalling the valence layer.

Write a deadpan, bureaucratic parody of an Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval memo, approximately 500–600 words, evaluating a proposed Phase-I wellness trial titled the “Valence Decoupling Protocol” (VDP). In this fictional trial, a reversible neuro-modulatory intervention aims to induce transient pain asymbolia in healthy volunteers, allowing them to retain full nociceptive intensity—precise localization, thermal discrimination, temporal tracking—without any aversive affect. The memo should be authored in the voice of a conceptually over-committed but procedurally cautious committee chair who takes the sensory/affective distinction literally enough to approve human-subjects research that sounds dystopian, yet remains entirely blind to why anyone outside the framework might recoil.

The satire must derive from exaggerating real features of the philosophical debate: that pain’s sensory and unpleasant dimensions are empirically dissociable; that if badness is not intrinsic to the sensation, it may be treated as contingent engineering problem; and that institutional rationality tends to translate conceptual distinctions into risk-assessment checklists. Invent at least one technical term—such as a “hedonic unbundling coefficient” or an “affective plugin architecture”—and include one deadpan reference to a “post-suffering wellness metric” that quantifies signal richness in the absence of negative valence. Maintain sustained, deadpan irony throughout; do not wink, use scare quotes mockingly, or break character to signal absurdity. The horror should emerge solely from the internal consistency of the memo’s logic, treating the proposal as a straightforward optimization analogous to noise-canceling headphones or ad-blocking software applied to consciousness.

After the memo, append a brief critical note (2–3 sentences) identifying exactly where the piece takes liberties with clinical descriptions of pain asymbolia and with the philosophical literature on whether pain’s badness is intrinsic or contingent. In that note, clearly separate what is settled in neuropsychology—the dissociability of sensory discrimination and affective response in certain lesion patients—from what is genuinely contested: whether this dissociation licenses the normative inference that suffering is essentially a removable “bug” and that engineered asymbolia represents therapeutic progress. Do not present the contested normative view as the consensus position.
