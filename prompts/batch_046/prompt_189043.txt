I'm putting together the final exam for my upper-level modern philosophy course, and we just wrapped up the unit on authority and obligation. I want one essay question on Pascal's coupling of justice and force — that justice without power is impotent, and power without justice is just tyranny — but I don't want students to just parrot the line back. I was talking with my friend about wether Pascal's argument secretly assumes one sovereign with a monopoly on violence, and wether it collapses once enforcement gets pluralized across competing authorities or private enforcers. So the prompt should ask students to lay out Pascal's synthesis, then apply it to a case where force is dispersed rather than centralized, and evaluate wether the pairing still works or if the absence of a single Leviathan-style enforcer breaks it.

Please write the exam question, a model answer of about 600-700 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 points with five must-hit points listed. Each point should say its mark value and include a one-line note on how students typically miss it — especially the point about conflating Pascal with Hobbes or missing the conservative bias where Pascal seems to justify whatever power is already established. The difficulty should come from the crux: a B-level answer can summarize the justice-force pairing, but an A answer needs to actually wrestle with distributed force and wether Pascal smuggles in a defense of the status quo.
