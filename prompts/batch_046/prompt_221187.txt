I've been working through some notes on self-reference and explanatory hierarchies for a philosophy of mind seminar, and a friend sketched an argument that seems to show materialist cognitive science is not just empirically difficult but conceptually impossible. I think there's a slip somewhere—probably an equivocation between formal representation and physical causation—but every step looks defensible on its own, and I can't name the exact breach. Here is the chain as he laid it out:

(1) In any hierarchical representation or explanation, there must be an inviolate level—a substrate or framework that belongs to the hierarchy's outside, like the paper on which Escher's drawing of two hands sketching each other is printed, or the metalanguage that defines a formal system's rules without being one of its objects.

(2) Self-consciousness involves the mind representing or explaining itself; the system becomes part of its own object domain.

(3) If such self-representation were complete, with no residue left outside the represented hierarchy, the system would become a pure strange loop with no inviolate level—analogous to those drawn hands floating in empty space with no paper beneath them.

(4) But representation and explanation require asymmetry: the representer must occupy a different level from the represented. Without an inviolate level, there is no asymmetry, hence no genuine representation.

(5) Therefore, any complete self-model necessarily lacks the conditions for being a representation at all.

(6) Contemporary materialist theories—predictive processing, global workspace models, and related frameworks—aim precisely at a complete physical self-model: every mental state explained by neural dynamics that are themselves part of the model.

(7) By (5), such a complete model cannot actually represent itself; it eliminates the very substrate needed for representation.

(8) Yet self-consciousness clearly occurs, so the self-model must be genuinely representational and therefore necessarily incomplete, leaving an "inviolate residue" outside the physical system.

(9) This residue functions exactly like a Cartesian substrate—not explained by physics, not part of the brain's dynamics, but required for the mind to represent itself.

(10) So either we accept a non-physical soul, or we deny that we have self-consciousness; materialist cognitive science is caught in a conceptual contradiction.

My friend says this proves the inviolate level is unavoidable and that neuroscience can never close the explanatory gap. I feel like something slipped between (4) and (6), or perhaps between (1) and (2), but I can't articulate it. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd especially like to hold onto (1) and (8) if possible, since the intuition that some residue escapes full capture feels right to me, but I'm willing to pay a price elsewhere if that's where the error lies.
