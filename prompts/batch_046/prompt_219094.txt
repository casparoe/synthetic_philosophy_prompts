I’m a novelist drafting a scene where a neuroscientist tries to convince her sister that consciousness is just computation, and I keep bumping into the meta-problem — not the hard problem itself, but why we think there’s a special explanatory gap even once all the brain wiring is mapped. I run a small writters workshop, about 10 people (actually I ment 8, oops), one 75-minute session. Design an in-class activity that makes them feel that gap in real time: they have to build a complete third-person explanation of seeing red, then hit a hidden constraint that forces them to realize they’ve used every available concept except the one that captures the feeling, and then vote on whether that leftover is missing knowledge or just a trick of perspective. Give me rules, roles, a payoff scheme using group recognition instead of cash, and a debrief that maps each moment onto the meta-problem. Be honest about what’ll break — probably people will confuse it with the hard problem — and say plainly which feature, like phenomenal transparency, the game can’t reproduce and you’ll have to supply. No reading lists or citations, please; I just want the mechanics.
