I've noticed a recurring argumentative pattern in debates about collective entities—whether institutions, scientific paradigms, or distributed mental representations—where a unifying framework is proposed to explain how disparate parts cohere, only to face the question of what relates that framework to the parts it was meant to bind. The response is typically a higher-order framework, which then requires its own unification, producing an ascending hierarchy in which each proposed binder is itself one of the items needing binding. Is there a precise name for this structure? I am aware of Bradley's regress and the problem of the unity of the proposition, but I am unsure whether those apply only to the metaphysical relation between qualities and substances, or whether they extend to this kind of hierarchical unification problem in other domains. If Bradley's regress is the right label, what exactly distinguishes it from an ordinary explanatory regress? And if not, is there a separate concept—perhaps in philosophy of social science or philosophy of mind—that captures exactly this failure mode, where the cure for disunity reproduces the disease at a higher level?
