Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises within Kripkenstein's rule-following considerations when the skeptical paradox is applied not to arithmetic or public linguistic practice, but to a solitary agent's attempt to stabilize a private classificatory rule over fine-grained perceptual qualities. In your example, specify a case in which the agent introduces a distinction—say, between two nearly identical shades of a color she has never previously named—establishes an internal criterion for future applications, and then later encounters a borderline instance. Show precisely how the skeptical challenge operates here: there must be no determinable fact, whether about her past mental states, neural configurations, or dispositional tendencies, that fixes whether she is now correctly extending the original rule or unconsciously altering it. Crucially, illustrate why the absence of a linguistic community does not resolve the paradox by supplying external correction, but rather relocates it into the coherence conditions of her own retrospective self-ascriptions. The example should be constructed so that the conceptual tension is internal to the normativity of the rule itself, not reducible to an epistemic failure of memory or attention, and it should make visible what is at stake when communal criteria are unavailable without collapsing into a merely psychological report about uncertainty.
