Write a joke that plays on Karl Popper's concept of verisimilitude and the specific logical problem that undermines it: because a stronger, more precise false theory entails more false consequences than a weaker, vaguer rival, it can be assessed as further from the truth despite being more informative. The humour should turn on this asymmetry — for example, a precise but wrong prediction being penalised relative to an evasive non-prediction that never quite commits to falsehood — and should depend on grasping the philosophical point rather than on generic scientific-stereotype humour. Begin with the joke itself, with no preface or commentary.
