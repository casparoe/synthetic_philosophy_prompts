I'm defending a paper in next week's philosophy-of-well-being seminar. Thesis: Nozick's experience-machine intuition doesn't establish that reality is intrinsically good; rather, it reveals that mental states contribute to well-being only when they possess counterfactual anchoring—that is, dependency on actual achievements, relationships, and knowledge—to carry normative weight. I call the view structured hedonism: phenomenally rich experiences matter for well-being only when so anchored, and the machine fails not by lacking reality per se but by severing the anchor and leaving experiences normatively weightless. Audience: two committed pure hedonists, three objective-list theorists who treat achievement, knowledge, and real friendship as intrinsic goods, and two decision-theorists skeptical of intuition-pumping thought experiments.
