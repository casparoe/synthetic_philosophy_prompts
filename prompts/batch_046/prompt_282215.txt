ok so i've been trying to map out my view on social norms for a paper and want a consistency check before i embarrass myself. here's roughly where i landed:

(1) i buy lewis's analysis straight: a convention is a regularity in behavior sustained by common knowledge that others conform, preference to conform given that expectation, and that it solves a coordination problem.

(2) but i also think evolutionary/game-theoretic dynamics (skyrms-style learning, or young's selection models) can stabilize patterns that feel normative even when nobody has explicit representations or common knowledge of the regularity.

(3) i don't think (2) undercuts (1); evolutionary norms are just a different beast, though in everyday talk i call both "norms."

(4) the normative pull of a convention, for me, comes entirely from the coordination problem it solves - there's no intrinsic authority.

(5) still, i apply reflective equilibrium to social norms: if a convention produces outcomes that clash with our considered judgments about fairness or harm, that's a real reason to try shifting to a different equilibrium.

(6) and i think some conventions are genuinely arbitary - right vs left - where moral considerations shouldn't trump the coordination value.

do these hang together? i'm especially worried about whether (4) and (5) pull against each other: if force is purely coordination-based, why should fairness judgments override? and is there a tension between treating evolutionary norms as non-conventions (3) and still using "norm" univocally? also flag any tension that only looks bad and dissolves on inspection.
