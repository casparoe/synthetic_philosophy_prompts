I'm giving the eulogy this saturday for my uncle Ray, 78, died in his sleep last tuesday. He wasn't religious but he definatly believed in fate — he had that old Death in Damascus story printed in his wallet, the one about the guy who sees Death in Damascus and runs to Samarra but Death says I was surprised to see you in Damascus yesterday since we had our appointment in Samarra today. The room will be family, some warehouse buddies, and neighbors. Nobody's a philosopher. I want to talk about that story because it was his whole worldview, but I need it to actually shape what I can and can't say: I can't pretend he's watching over us from somewhere he never thought existed, and I can't just call fate nonsense without trashing the one idea he held onto. But I've also been wondering if the story even proves fatalism — maybe knowing you'll be in Samarra isn't the same as being pulled there? If I'm confused about that, or if asking a eulogy to carry this much logic is just wrong, tell me straight up instead of writing pretty sentences around it. And please start right with the speech itself, no "here is your eulogy" intro, no explaining the idea before the story starts.
