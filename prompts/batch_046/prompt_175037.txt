hey, been lurking on rationalist spaces for years and finally have a weird pattern i want to name. in debates about valid consent there's this recurring move i don't have vocabulary for, and i want to know if philosophy has carved it out already.

here's the concrete shape. party A asks B for consent to do X. B says yes, and technically B was told true things: every direct question B asked got an honest answer, B wasn't threatened, B seems competent to understand normal explanations. but B's mental model of X is structurally wrong -- like B thinks the procedure is reversable but it's permanent, or B thinks X only affects B but actually it creates obligations for B's partner too, or B thinks they're consenting to a trial version but A knows it's the full commitment. A knows B has this wrong frame, but hasn't lied: B just never asked the one question that would expose the gap. so the consent is technically informed and uncoerced, but B is consenting to something that doesn't match what A is actually going to do. i've seen this come up in medical consent threads, in arguments about EULA design where users think they're consenting to data use but not to model training, and in some sexual consent discussions where one person thinks an act is within a bounded 'script' and the other knows the boundary is being crossed but doesn't correct the framing because technically they answered all direct questions truthfully.

is there an established concept for this? it's not quite deception, since no falsehood was told. it's not incompetence, since B could understand perfectly well if the frame were corrected. people sometimes call it 'manipulation' but that feels too broad. does it have a specific name in the consent literature -- maybe something about epistemic framing or structual misalignment -- and if not, what's the right boundary: should we say consent requires not just true answers to asked questions but correct ontological framing of the act? i want a committed take, not a survey. tell me whether this is genuinely a distinct failure mode from deception and coercion, or whether i'm over-carving, and if it is distinct, what conditions should have to hold for consent to be valid in these cases.
