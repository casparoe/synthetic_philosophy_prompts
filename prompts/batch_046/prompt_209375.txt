A philosopher has been classifying semantic loops into three categories—genuinely paradoxical (no consistent assignment), stably resolvable (classical value assigned), or requiring logical revision rather than a truth-value gap. Her track record: the direct Liar ("This sentence is false") and the Strengthened Liar ("This sentence is not true") are genuinely paradoxical; the Truth-Teller ("This sentence is true") is stably true; the Temporal Liar ("This sentence was false yesterday") is stably false; a circular pair in which sentence A asserts B is false and B asserts A is false is genuinely paradoxical; and Curry's sentence ("If this sentence is true then contradiction follows") is genuinely paradoxical, though she insists the remedy is restricting the conditional-proof rule, not declaring a gap.

Now consider the Modal Cross-Reference: a sentence M evaluated in world w asserts "In every world v accessible from w other than w, M is false"; in each such v, M asserts "In w, M is true." Predict her classification of M, lay out at least two rules that fit all six prior verdicts but diverge on this case, explain the reasoning and predictive difference for each, state which rule she is most likely running with justification, and specify one further loop that would conclusively distinguish the rules.
