Modern Stoic revival literature often analogizes Epictetus's dichotomy of control to a firewall: judgments and intentions lie inside the perimeter, while external events, others' actions, and bodily outcomes lie outside, and wisdom consists in policing that boundary. A recent extension in tech-adjacent Stoic circles treats the self like a hardened server: you configure internal state to reject malicious inputs and never blame the network for your own latency. Audit this analogy precisely: map what corresponds to the firewall rules, the trusted internal processes, the external traffic, and the administrator; identify where the computing metaphor breaks down — especially regarding the Stoic claim that initial impressions are not fully internal and that virtue involves active engagement with externals rather than isolation; and determine whether the conclusion that Stoicism is essentially a defensive, private posture survives once those breaks are on the table.
