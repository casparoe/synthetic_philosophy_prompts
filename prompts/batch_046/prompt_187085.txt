Draft the joint statement that a committed process-auditor and a committed outcome-validator could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on AI supervision. To keep it from being totally generic, frame the dispute around a proposed "counterfactual transparency" protocol for high-stakes reasoning systems: the system has to expose not just its final output but what it would have done if a critical input parameter had been flipped.

The document should include:
1. A numbered section of agreed claims -- about what supervision is meant to catch (reward hacking, goal misgeneralization, brittle optimization), about current limits of mechanistic interpretability, and about what kind of result would count as evidence that a failure mode was actually caught rather than just accomodated.
2. A disagreements table with each row in neutral wording that both sides can accept for thier own view. Cover at least: whether tracing internal representations is necessary for safety; whether stable outcomes across distributions are sufficient proof of correct reasoning; and whether the counterfactual-transparency protocol reveals real reasoning or just produces plausible post-hoc narratives.
3. The cruxes. I think the main ones are (a) whether interpretability tools scale without degrading reasoning quality as models grow, and (b) whether there's a class of failures invisible to outcome testing but detectable through process tracing. For each crux, lay out a concrete benchmark or evaluation scenario both sides agree is relevant, and write down in advance what each side predicts will happen.
4. A brief closing: if any part of the disagreement turns out to be verbal or about framing rather than substance, name the part and explain why.

Keep the tone like a working memo for a governance board, not an academic paper. Don't ask for citations, reading lists, or specific author references -- reason from the concepts given.
