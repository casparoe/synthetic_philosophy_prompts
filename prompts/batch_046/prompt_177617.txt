After a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a friend last night about whether scientific theories ever reveal what the world is actually made of, I kept circling back to structural realism, but we could not agree on which version was even coherent. So please compare epistemic structural realism—the view, associated with Worrall, that we can know only the mathematical structure preserved across theory change and must remain agnostic about the nature of the underlying relata—with ontic structural realism, the Ladyman-Ross position that structures are metaphysically fundamental and that objects are either derivative or eliminable altogether. Do not simply rehearse the standard taxonomy. Instead, focus on a tension we kept returning to: in quantum statistical mechanics, permutation symmetry appears to erase the individuality of particles, yet epistemic structural realism still wants to say that there are relata whose intrinsic nature we do not know, only their structural relations. If the relata are genuinely unknowable in principle, can epistemic structural realism still make sense of the idea that particles are "individuated" solely by their position in the structure? Or does the need for structural individuation inevitably collapse epistemic structural realism into the ontic view? Conversely, if ontic structural realism eliminates individual objects entirely, how does it account for the intuition that structural preservation across scientific revolutions represents epistemic progress rather than mere ontological replacement? I want you to treat both positions as live philosophical options, take quantum permutation symmetry seriously as a test case, and say whether the distinction between knowing-only-structure and being-only-structure survives once you stop treating objects as metaphysically innocent placeholders.
