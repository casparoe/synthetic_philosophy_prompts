I'm taking a seminar on rational choice and the philosophy of action, and I've noticed a structural pattern in indifference cases that seems to sit near Buridan's ass yet behaves differently from the standard textbook formulation. Consider an agent who must choose between two doors that are genuinely identical in every relevant respect—same outcome, same path length, same sensory profile—so that no preference can be formed. The familiar resolution is to adopt a tie-breaking rule, say, "always take the left-hand option when indifferent." But now suppose the agent can be placed in two orientations that are physically identical except for a 180-degree rotation: in one orientation, the left door is A; in the other, the left door is B. Because there is no non-circular reason to privilege one orientation, the rule "choose left" does not determinately select A or B; it selects whichever door happens to occupy the left position in that framing. The same relocation occurs with a coin flip assigned heads-to-A and tails-to-B, since one could just as well assign heads-to-B and tails-to-A without altering any physical property of the coin. Thus the deadlock is not eliminated; it is pushed upward into the calibration, framing, or assignment conditions of the resolution mechanism itself.

I have seen this discussed under the broad label of the "arbitrariness" of tie-breaking, but that usually treats the break as unmotivated rather than structurally underdetermined in this mirror-image fashion. Does this recursive or second-order symmetry—where the device introduced to resolve indifference is itself vulnerable to an identical indifference about how it is oriented or configured—have an established name in the literature on choice without preference? If there is no distinct term, I want you to take a committed position rather than merely surveying options: should this be carved out as a separate phenomenon, or is it just the original Buridan case reapplied to meta-level rules? Specifically, argue whether every possible tie-breaking mechanism necessarily contains this framing vulnerability, or whether there exists a principled boundary between mechanisms that genuinely terminate the symmetry and those that merely displace it one level up. I am not asking for a bibliography or reading list; I want the conceptual carving—what exactly this pattern is, what it is not, and where its edges lie—together with your reasons for that boundary.
