Write a sestina on the problem of translating Clément Marot's "A une damoyselle malade" (the poem Douglas Hofstadter examines under the nickname "Ma Mignonne" in his 1997 study of poetic translation) in which the form itself enacts the central argument: that formal constraints generate creative solutions precisely by forcing irrecoverable sacrifice. Choose six end-words that represent six dimensions of fidelity—phonetic echo, syntactic shape, literal sense, emotional urgency, historical register, and formal constraint itself. As these words rotate through the standard sestina pattern—stanza one ending 1-2-3-4-5-6, stanza two 6-1-5-2-4-3, stanza three 3-6-4-1-2-5, stanza four 5-3-2-6-1-4, stanza five 4-5-1-3-6-2, stanza six 2-4-6-5-3-1—the rotation should make visible which dimension is foregrounded and which is pushed to the margin in each stanza, so that a reader familiar with the idea that no translation preserves all dimensions at once can trace the logic structurally. The envoi (three lines, containing all six words, arranged so that two appear per line with the first and second in the first line, the third and fourth in the second, and the fifth and sixth in the third) should gather the dimensions together without resolving them, ending on the recognition that the residue escaping translation is not a failure but the necessary cost of carrying the poem across languages. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows Hofstadter's claim—that constraint sparks innovation while guaranteeing loss—recognizes the reasoning, and should stand as a coherent meditation on translation for a reader who does not. Do not name Hofstadter or Marot in the text, do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and observe strict sestina rules: six six-line stanzas followed by the three-line envoi.
