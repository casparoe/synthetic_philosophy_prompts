Consider four cyclical or quasi-cyclical accounts of civilizational change—Polybius's anacyclosis of constitutional rotation, Ibn Khaldun's cycle of asabiyyah-driven consolidation and luxury-induced decay, Spengler's morphological account of cultures as organic entities with fixed life-phases, and Toynbee's challenge-and-response model in which civilizations survive or perish by their creative response to repeated crises. Rank these four frameworks by how coherently each can accommodate a hypothetical civilization that experiences recurring technological stagnation followed by abrupt, externally-driven renewal, without undergoing the internal cultural death Spengler requires, the dissolution of group solidarity central to Khaldun, or the full constitutional cycle Polybius describes. Assign each to a tier—strong fit, strained fit, or misfit—and in one sentence for each state the specific structural feature of the theory that determines its tier.
