hey, i'm drafting a scene where a philosopher-character is defending lexical threshhold negative utilitarianism — you know, the idea that no pile of pleasure can ever outweigh a certain class of severe pain. she's also convinced pleasure and pain aren't just opposite values on one scale; they're incommensurable in some deep way, like comparing sound to color.

i tried writing her argument and now it feels like it proves too much. here's the chain:

1. if pleasure and pain are genuinely incommensurable, then LTNU makes no sense, because you need some shared metric to claim that this particular pain is lexically worse than any pleasure.

2. but if they're fully commensurable on a single cardinal scale, then the lexical wall collapses — there's always enough pleasure to outweigh the pain, and you're back to standard utilitarian math.

3. so LTNU needs a middle ground, partial commensurability: a shared metric that works below the threshhold but breaks above it.

4. but that middle ground is unstable. if the metric applies to small pains and big pleasures, then by continiuity it ought to apply to big pains too, unless you allow a discontinuous jump in moral weight right at the threshhold.

5. that jump violates moral continiuity — small differences in the intensity of suffering shouldn't trigger infinite changes in moral priority.

6. therefore LTNU either collapses into full commensurability or dissolves into incoherence. there's no stable middle.

this feels suspicious, like i'm sneeking in an assumption the view doesn't need. maybe the jump isn't a bug but the whole point? or maybe i'm confusing metaphysical incommensurability with practical decision rules? i don't know which step to reject. i'd rather keep 1 and 6 if possible, but tell me what i'm paying if i do. where does this go wrong, if it does?
