I've been chewing on this inversion idea -- Darwin showed nature builds functioning eyes and instincts without any designer grasping optics or ethology, and Turing showed that a machine could pass for intelligent through formal rules without having understanding, consciousness, or anything like intentionality. So I'm pushing a stronger version: call it the 'blind builder thesis.' Comprehension isn't just unnecessary for real-world competance, it's likely an evolutionary spandrel -- a decorative byproduct of complex processing that doesn't pull causal weight. The actual work is always done by dumb mechanisms, whether genetic algorithms or statistical weights, and human 'understanding' is mostly post-hoc narration we slap on after the fact.

What would count against this? I want the arguments, experiments, or conceptual results that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be versus just awkward. Include what defenders of this view would themselves concede is damaging, not just what critics say. Are there cases where absence of comprehension actually causes failure in ways more blind processing can't fix? Any formal results showing certain competences require genuinely semantic representational structures? And be honest about whether this thesis has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- like saying comprehension is 'implicit' in the selection environment or 'encoded' in the weights -- and whether that insulation is a problem for a view that pretends to be empirical. Don't need a bibliography or citations, just the map of where this view is actually exposed.
