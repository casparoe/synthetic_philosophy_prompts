You serve as a senior program officer for a biomedical funding body that reviews ten-year research portfolios in mechanistic psychiatry. Your board has adopted Imre Lakatos’s methodology of scientific research programmes as its evaluation framework, but reviewers apply it inconsistently—some treat any failed prediction as falsification, others excuse every anomaly as part of the protective belt. Design an operational audit procedure that a non-philosopher reviewer can run in a half-day session, with clear stopping rules and explicit judgment points marked.

The procedure must be built around Lakatos’s core distinctions: the hard core (commitments treated as irrefutable by the programme’s practitioners), the protective belt (auxiliary hypotheses, measurement conventions, and methodological adjustments), the positive heuristic (rules directing the programme toward novel predictions), and the negative heuristic (prohibitions on altering the hard core). For each audit step, specify the evidence required, what counts as a sufficient answer, the decision rule, and the exact point where the reviewer must flag that values rather than empirical facts are driving the decision.

The procedure must survive these hard cases without collapsing them: (1) a programme that repeatedly predicts novel correlations, but only after post-hoc adjustments to its instruments; (2) a programme whose hard core has shrunk to a single untestable claim, yet generates technically accurate but trivial predictions; (3) a competing programme that explains the same anomalies with fewer protective-belt modifications but requires abandoning a core commitment the board considers ethically essential.

Apply the procedure to three portfolio cases:

A. A sleep-genetics programme whose hard core holds that circadian disruption is a primary causal factor, not merely a correlate, of mood disorders. Over eight years it added three successive layers of epigenetic buffering hypotheses to account for null findings in twin studies, but recently predicted—before data collection—a specific gene-environment interaction in shift workers that was subsequently confirmed.

B. A neuroimaging programme built on the hard core that all psychiatric categories map cleanly onto distinct connectivity signatures. It has preserved its core intact by reclassifying contradictory patient scans as technical artifacts or subclinical variants, has produced no predictions outside its original taxonomy, and has not modified its positive heuristic since inception.

C. A pharmacogenomics programme that abandoned its original hard core linking drug response solely to single-nucleotide variants, adopted a polygenic framework, and now predicts treatment outcomes with modest but improving accuracy. Continuing to fund it requires discarding the board’s longstanding institutional commitment to personalized single-gene diagnostics.

For each case, show where the procedure reaches a verdict, where it forces an irreducible judgment call, and what that implies for continued funding. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure cannot determine—such as whether a degenerating programme should still be supported for clinical-translation reasons—and identify the escalation point at which the reviewer must hand the decision to the board rather than resolving it alone.
