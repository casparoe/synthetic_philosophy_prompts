Having retired after thirty years in condensed-matter physics and only recently begun serious study of philosophy, I find myself repeatedly encountering Leibniz’s compressed dictum that “indiscernibles are identical”—or, as it is often flattened in discussion, “no difference without a difference”. In a recent seminar on metaphysics of identity, a colleague invoked the slogan to dismiss Max Black’s two-sphere universe outright: if the spheres share every property, they are not two spheres at all, hence the thought experiment is incoherent from the start. I am unsure whether the principle is genuinely doing that work, or whether it is being wielded merely as a verbal stop-sign.

Could you disentangle the distinct precise theses that “indiscernibles are identical” (or “no difference without a difference”) might be expressing, ranging from the tautological to the metaphysically heavyweight? Please lay the readings out as a menu rather than selecting one: for instance, (i) a logical or linguistic triviality about the identity of indiscernibles in a formal system; (ii) a claim about the identity conditions of concrete physical objects; (iii) a claim about the ontology of properties themselves (intrinsic versus purely relational); (iv) a claim about the limits of description or representational capacity; and (v) any other precise formulation you think is concealed within the slogan. For each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, defensible, false, or genuinely imperilled by Black’s spheres, and say specifically whether that reading actually supports the dismissal of Black’s scenario.

Furthermore, I should like you to consider whether any of these readings survive a further wrinkle: suppose the two spheres are not classical macroscopic objects but quantum-mechanically identical particles situated in a universe of perfect spatial symmetry, distinguishable only by a relational feature that has no manifest physical effect. Does that modification rescue or undermine the principle?

Please answer in British English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests, as I am working through this material without access to a research database.
