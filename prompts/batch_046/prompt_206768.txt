I am working through my position on democratic legitimacy for a graduate workshop in political philosophy, and before I present I want to know whether the commitments I have assembled actually cohere or whether I am implicitly relying on incompatible motivations. Here is my current package: (1) I endorse an all-affected-interests principle—anyone whose vital interests are systematically shaped by a collective decision should belong to the demos; (2) I reject both birthright citizenship and pure residency as sufficient, since temporary visitors are affected without being stakeholders, yet I want to include long-term residents and, in principle, future generations whose lives will be shaped by present policy; (3) I propose that membership should be structured by what I call stake-based recursive inclusion: the present demos sets the procedural rules for extending membership, but is bound by a non-exclusion clause that prohibits permanently barring any class of systematically affected parties; (4) I maintain that direct deliberative participation requires a threshold of cognitive and communicative capacity, though I think robust representation can legitimately stand in for those below that threshold as well as for non-present generations; and (5) I insist that the demos must remain bounded enough to sustain meaningful deliberation, which implies some principled limit on how far inclusion can extend without dissolving collective agency into an unmanageable multitude.

My worry is that (1) and (5) pull in opposite directions, and that (3) may smuggle in precisely the kind of self-privileging that (1) and (2) are designed to prevent—current members defining the rules in a way that protects their own position. At the same time, I think (4) might dissolve some of this pressure if representation is genuinely substitutive rather than tokenistic. Can you audit this set? I want you to identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear so, rank the conflicts by severity rather than listing them all as equally damaging, and for each tension specify the least costly revision—whether weakening one commitment, narrowing its scope, or abandoning it entirely. Finally, tell me which single change would restore consistency with the smallest philosophical loss, and whether making that change undermines the core motivation behind the recursive-inclusion mechanism in (3).
