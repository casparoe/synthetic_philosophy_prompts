Write an essay about lexical treshold negative utilitarianism and whether it collapses if pleasure and pain aren't commensurable. The view says suffering has lexical priority—no stack of goods can outweigh a serious harm—but that sounds like it needs both to be on the same scale so you can see the "threshold" being crossed. If they're genuinely incommensurable, like trying to weigh melody against weight, then the metaphor of a lexical line is bogus, and the view should be abandoned or rebuilt. But maybe incommensurability is exactly what saves it: if you can't trade them off quantitatively, the rational policy is just to minimize suffering first with no exchange rate involved. Weigh these two directions, reach a conclusion, and definately justify it. Also tackle this weird case: someone reports a "negative pleasure"—say, an aesthetic experience that is positively enjoyable in form but negatively valenced in content—that they rate as worse than a mild headache. Does the lexical rule have to classify that as suffering, or can it stay agnostic about weird valence inversions? Don't sit on the fence; pick a side.
