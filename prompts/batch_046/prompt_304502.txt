The debate over coherence arguments for expected utility maximization has been stalled for decades without visible movement. One side holds that Dutch-book arguments establish a genuine rational obligation: if your preferences and credences violate the axioms, a clever bookmaker can construct a set of bets that guarantees your loss, and this vulnerability is irrational irrespective of your substantive goals. The other side replies that exposure to exploitation is not necessarily a failure of rationality if you reject the representational framework—probabilistic belief and cardinal utility—that allows the bookmaker to construct the bets in the first place; on their view, coherence arguments at best show internal consistency conditions on mental representations, not normative constraints on action given what you actually care about. Both sides routinely invoke the same formal cases—preference reversals, Allais-style patterns reframed as incoherent betting portfolios, and distinctions between state-dependent and state-independent utility—yet neither concedes ground. My working hypothesis is that this is not a substantive disagreement about the theorems, but a verbal or mislocated one: the defenders treat coherence as an internal structural requirement on reasoning, while the critics treat rationality as an instrumental relation between beliefs, desires, and outcomes, and the formal machinery is doing double duty for both without either side noticing. Is that diagnosis correct? Or is a hidden empirical claim—about which formal representations are psychologically real, or about whether actual human agents can be modeled as having stable cardinal utilities—actually driving the impasse? Diagnose the disagreement precisely: is it verbal around terms like 'rationality' and 'coherence'; is it two different questions—one logical, one psychological—being pursued under a single heading; is it a bedrock clash about whether consistency can ever be normatively binding; or is there a formal asymmetry that one side has missed? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a conceptual distinction that separates the logical and instrumental questions, a formal result that breaks the symmetry between the representations, or an argument that the question is ill-posed and should be dissolved rather than resolved?
