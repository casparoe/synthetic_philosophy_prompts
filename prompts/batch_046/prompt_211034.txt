heres one students answer (it feels mostly right but theres a subtle error i cant quite pin down):

Scanlon defines wrongness via reasonable rejection. In aggregation cases, we imagine a principle that permits harming many slightly to save one severely. Each of the many can reject this principle because they are being used as a means to save the other person, and the separateness of persons means we cant trade off their interests like utilitarians do. Therefore Scanlon flatly prohibits aggregation across different people: numbers never matter, and we must treat each persons claim as absolute. This is why contractualism is not just rule-utilitarianism with extra steps — it denies that welfare can be summed at all, and instead requires unanimous consent to any principle that allows harm.
