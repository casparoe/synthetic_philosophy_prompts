I run a small philosophy podcast called Modal Margins and next week im interviewing Dr. Lena Vasquez, who's been defending a tweaked tracking theory she calls weighted-sensitivity closure. The basic move is Nozick-style — knowledge requires tracking the truth across nearby worlds — but she handles epistemic closure by saying that tracking an entailment only requires preservation in worlds where both your premises remain tracked and the inference rule itself stays stable. I want eight to ten interview questions that dont just have her restate this view; keep it in the weeds, no generic career-path stuff.

At least one question should press the standard closure objection head-on: if she tracks p and p clearly entails q, why shouldnt she have to track q in every nearby world? Include a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that inferential preservation is a different modal relation than direct tracking. Another should drill into a concrete failure case — maybe an astronomer who tracks that Venus is the morning star but, because she gets weird about identity conditions under counterfactual variation, fails to track that Venus is Venus, despite knowing the entailment — and ask how her stability condition avoids just ruling this out by fiat. Then I need one on whether her "stability of inference rules" condition secretly reintroduces closure through the back door: if the rule "p therefore q" has to be stable across worlds, isnt that just saying she knows the entailment? For that one, anticipate her appealing to reasoning-method-relative modal proximity and ask whether that makes knowledge too agent-dependent.
