Write a sestina on the historiographical problem of whether the “Middle Ages,” the “Renaissance,” and the “Scientific Revolution” name real discontinuities in the past or are retrospective inventions that impose false boundaries. Use exactly these six end-words in rotation: period, invention, boundary, continuity, name, shadow. Structure the six stanzas so that each one pairs two of the three epoch-labels and lets their boundary dissolve—stanza one on Middle Ages versus Renaissance, stanza two on Renaissance versus Scientific Revolution, stanza three on Scientific Revolution versus Middle Ages, stanza four on all three as overlapping shadows, stanza five on continuity undermining each pair, stanza six on the name itself becoming an artifact. In the envoi of three lines, bring all six end-words together in a single sentence that leaves the ontological status of the periods unresolved. The poem should render the debate with enough precision that a specialist recognizes the tension between continuity theses (emphasizing institutional and intellectual transmission across conventional divides) and rupture or invention theses (stressing that the labels are anachronistic projections serving later ideological needs), rather than treating the labels as neutral containers. After the poem, provide a brief note—no more than a short paragraph—indicating which claims in the poem align with settled historiographical consensus (for instance, that “Scientific Revolution” is a twentieth-century framing) and which align with genuinely contested positions (for instance, whether there is any defensible discontinuity corresponding to the Renaissance at all), flagging any liberties the poem takes for formal or rhetorical effect, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
