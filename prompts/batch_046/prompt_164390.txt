- I think dispositions are real categorical properties, not just predictions. Brittle glass actually has something in it that makes it break.
- At the same time, I think the conditonal analysis is basically right: if struck under normal conditons, it breaks.
- I think finks are a legit counterexample — if there's a hidden blocker inside the glass, it's still brittle but won't break.
- I think masks are the miror image: an un-brittle object forced to break by an outside trigger.
- So I think finks and masks should be solved with the same strategy, probably by tightening what counts as normal conditions.
- In code, error handlers are obvious finks (they suppress a crash), but I don't think masks exist in software — if the program crashes, that's just its actual behavior, not a masked absence of stability.
- I think mental dispositions like feeling pain have categorical bases in the brain, but they can't have finks or masks because the link between nerve firing and feeling is direct, with no hidden middleman.
- There's also a weird middle case I call a ghost fink where the blocker is present but only delays the outcome, so the conditional is technically satisfied but the timeline is wrong.
