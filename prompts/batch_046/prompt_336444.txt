I am calling it penal visibility: the degree to which a prison's physical design, location, and social practices keep the prisoner's moral and social status continuously present to staff and to the broader community, as opposed to sequestering it out of sight. Others seem normative—whether a democratic society has a special obligation not to hide punishment away, or whether visibility risks turning suffering into spectacle. Still others seem institutional—whether visible prisons are more accountable to oversight, and whether the visibility framing itself does any work that the punishment-rehabilitation distinction already covers.
