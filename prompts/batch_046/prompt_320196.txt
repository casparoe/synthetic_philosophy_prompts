As a doctoral student in philosophy of mind with a secondary focus in clinical neuropsychology, I have been developing an interpretation of split-brain phenomena that departs from both the standard bifurcation narrative and the conservative single-mind-with-disrupted-communication view. In my department’s graduate seminar on consciousness and neural architecture, I have defended what I call a “permanent dual-stream” account: the corpus callosum does not integrate two originally separate phenomenal fields into one unified consciousness, but rather suppresses the reportability of a right-hemispheric stream that has always maintained its own non-linguistic intentional content. On this reading, the left hemisphere’s interpreter module generates a retrospective fiction of singular authorship; neurotypical unity is therefore an epistemic achievement rather than a metaphysical primitive, and callosotomy merely removes the suppression mechanism without creating a new subject. I want to know what would genuinely count against this position—not just the familiar appeals to cross-cueing or residual subcortical integration, but the observations, theoretical commitments, and formal results that expose it to real risk.

Specifically, which empirical findings—whether from partial callosotomy studies, measurements of affective interhemispheric transfer prior to surgery, or baseline neuroimaging of hemispheric independence in intact subjects—would actually threaten the claim that dual phenomenal streams are constant rather than surgically induced? Which conditions for phenomenal unity, or for the relation between reportability and existence, does my view depend on, and which rival frameworks (for instance, those treating unity as dynamically assembled rather than narratively imposed) would gain support if those conditions failed? I also want an honest assessment of insulation: if every instance of apparently integrated behavior can be redescribed as the left interpreter co-opting right-hemisphere outputs, has the position become unfalsifiable in a damaging way, or is that merely a methodological inconvenience? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguishing what defenders of this interpretation ought themselves to concede from what they could reasonably dismiss, and indicate whether the view predicts anything its rivals do not that could be tested without appealing to bibliographic authority.
