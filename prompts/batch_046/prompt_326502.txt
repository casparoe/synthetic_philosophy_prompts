I'm currently writing a paper for a seminar on moral psychology and institutional design, and I've become stuck on a question that sits at the intersection of normative ethics and cultural anthropology: if a society could deliberately shape its emotional economy, should it cultivate guilt over shame, or is the opposite true? I want you to write an essay that treats this as a live normative controversy rather than a historical survey. To prevent the analysis from collapsing into familiar talking points, frame the distinction through two functional loops rather than standard dictionary definitions: treat guilt as operating through an "allocentric repair loop," in which the agent's attention is directed outward toward the victim's standing and the concrete work of restitution, and treat shame as operating through an "autocentric status-restoration loop," in which the agent's attention is directed inward toward her own social image and the symbolic labor of reintegration. Weigh the arguments for and against privileging one loop in a liberal democratic culture—considering moral equality, the risk of authoritarian internalization, the demands of public reason, and whether emotional institutions can be rationally designed without violating liberal neutrality—then reach and defend a specific verdict. Is guilt the superior moral emotion for such a society? Is shame defensible, or even indispensable, in certain communal or restorative contexts? Or is the very idea of a culture "choosing" its dominant moral emotion a category mistake, given path dependency and the opacity of emotional socialization?

Crucially, in developing your answer, please separate what is relatively settled in the empirical and philosophical literature—such as the cross-cultural variability in the experience and expression of shame and guilt, and the general correlation between guilt and reparative behavior—from what remains genuinely contested among scholars who work on the topic. This includes, but is not limited to, whether shame necessarily undermines moral agency or tends to produce hiding rather than repair, whether guilt can be sustained without some form of internalized authority figure, and whether institutional design can reliably shape emotional economies without coercion. Do not present any contested theoretical framing—such as the claim that guilt is universally more compatible with democratic citizenship, or that shame is irreducibly hierarchical—as if it were the consensus view. Reach a clear conclusion and justify it, but mark precisely where your reasoning depends on disputed premises.
