In my research on the afterlife of Plutarchan biography in contemporary moral pedagogy, I have been examining an institutional practice that seems philosophically loaded but rarely theorized: certain ethics curricula and biographical museums organize a subject's life not chronologically but according to a schema of cardinal virtues—moving, for instance, from episodes illustrating temperance to those illustrating justice—while deliberately suppressing chronological contradictions or morally ambiguous episodes that would fracture the virtue-arc. The result is a hagiographic topology rather than a historical narrative; the life becomes a spatial map of excellences that the learner traverses, with temporal sequence treated as dispensable. This practice clearly descends from traditions of pairing lives to illuminate character rather than to document chronology, yet it now operates in secular contexts where hagiography is officially disavowed. I want to understand what philosophical commitments about moral learning, personal identity, and the function of biography this curatorial choice presupposes. Must it assume that moral virtues are stable, cross-situational dispositions that can be isolated and displayed like discrete exhibits, or could the same structure be defended by a narrative-identity theorist who treats coherence as constructed rather than discovered? Does the practice implicitly commit us to a form of moral realism about exemplars—treating them as transparent windows onto virtue—or can it be reconciled with a more skeptical, constructivist hagiography? Furthermore, if we replaced this virtue-ordered model with a rigorously chronological, warts-and-all historiographical presentation, what exactly would have to change in our underlying theory of how lives educate? Please reconstruct the implicit epistemology and moral psychology here: distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely accompanies it, identify which rival theories of moral psychology and biography it quietly rules out, and indicate where it is philosophically underdetermined—whether, for example, both virtue-ethical and certain narrative-constructivist accounts could license the same non-chronological arrangement, or whether the practice collapses without a particular metaphysics of character.
