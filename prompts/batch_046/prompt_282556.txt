hey, so i've been working through origin essentialism for my metaphysics seminar and built this argument that now feels suspicious because the conclusion is basically that nobody could exist in any nearby world, which can't be right. here goes:

(1) if origin essentialism is true, then for me to exist i must have originated from the exact sperm S and egg E that actually formed me.

(2) but S and E aren't just "my father's sperm" and "my mother's egg" in some generic sense; they are concrete particulars with specific genetic sequences, epigenetic marks, and cytoplasmic conditions.

(3) now here's the thing: in the first 72 hours after fertilization, the embryo undergoes cleavage divisions where chromosomal errors are routinely corrected, and in some contigent cases the embryo can split into twins or even absorb a chimeric cell line. so the "origin" isn't a single point-event but a distributed process over several days.

(4) if essentialism targets that whole process, then altering any specific repair event or cellular division on day two — say, a particular DNA-repair enzyme failing to fix a mismatch — changes the origin process and therefore means i don't exist.

(5) but those repair events depend on stocastic cellular noise. so my existence is tied to random fluctuations in a blatocyst.

(6) even worse, monozygotic twins share the exact same origin process up to the split, yet they are distinct people with distinct identities. so the origin process doesn't uniquely determine one person.

(7) therefore origin essentialism either makes my modal profile insanely fragile — i vanish if a single enzyme behaves differently — or it can't account for twins without saying they share an identity.

(8) so origin essentialism should be rejected.

but i feel like their's a slide somewhere between (3) and (4), or maybe (5) and (6) is doing something unfair. like, maybe the essentialist can just say "the origin is the initiating gametes, not the repair process" — but then they have to explain why the split in twins doesn't violate uniqueness. or maybe i'm equivocating between "essential origin" and "unique causal chain". which premise should i actually reject, and what does giving it up cost? i'd rather keep (1) and (8) if possible but i'm not sure that's stable.
