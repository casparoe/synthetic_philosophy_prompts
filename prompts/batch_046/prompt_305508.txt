As I develop a course module on nineteenth-century clinical archives, I have become personally concerned that presentism operates not only through moral judgment but through categorical imposition. Please provide a concrete example of a conceptual issue in presentist historiography—specifically, an instance of what might be termed "taxonomic retrojection," in which a modern disciplinary boundary (for example, between neurological and psychiatric disorder) is projected onto historical records that originally employed an undifferentiated framework such as "nervous disease." I am looking for an example that illustrates an epistemic, rather than merely ethical, distortion in how past knowledge is reconstructed, and I do not need bibliographic references or citations.
