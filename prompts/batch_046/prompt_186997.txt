My opening move is this. I grant that there may be no Cartesian theater, but I worry your model still relocates rather than eliminates the unifying function. You claim that drafts compete and that the dominant one—the one that controls report and action—is simply the winner of that competition. But this sounds as though there is still a functional editorial mechanism, a selection process that privileges one draft over others. If that mechanism is localized or operates with global coherence, have you not just moved the theater to the selection stage?

To press the point, consider a hypothetical system I will call a draft parliament: suppression is not hierarchical but genuinely distributed, so that every draft suppresses every other locally with no global arbiter, yet stable patterns of dominance emerge purely from interaction dynamics. Would you attribute consciousness to such a system? And more importantly, does your own model actually avoid a global arbitration mechanism when it explains why, at any given moment, I can report only one conscious content? If you must invoke some unified selection to account for the singularity of report, then it seems the Cartesian theater survives in your account as the very mechanism that picks the winning draft. How do you respond?
