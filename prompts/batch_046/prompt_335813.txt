I'm trying to figure out where I actually stand on eliminativism about beliefs and desires, going by my gut on cases. A dog that avoids the vet's office: clearly wants to avoid pain, so belief/desire talk feels okay there. A thermostat turning on heat: no belief about temperature, just mechanism, so eliminative talk fits. A human who says "I believe in democracy" but never votes or reads news: I want to say they don't really have that belief, it's just social signaling. But then there's my roommate who swears she has no opinions on politics, yet her groccery choices and podcast subscriptions map perfectly onto a coherent ideological framework -- I think she does have beliefs, just without access. Now the weird case: imagine a brain implant that generates sincere-sounding first-person reports ("I feel anxious about Tuesday") but there's no integrated neural substrate, just isolated clusters firing to produce the sentence. I'd say that's not real anxiety, no desire either. But what about a large language model that updates its weights based on reward feedback and then outputs "I prefer honest answers"? Is that closer to the thermostat or the roommate? There's also the "silent partner" thing -- in some cultures people never say "I think," they just act in ways that presuppose shared knowledge. Do they have beliefs if the concept never enters theier language? I keep going back and forth on the implant case versus the roommate case: theier both lack conscious access, but one feels like fake and one feels hidden. Is there a principle that gets all these -- maybe something about integrated causal role across behavior and report? Or does that fail on the culture case? And what's it say about the LLM -- do I have to bite the bullet that if the principle saves the roommate it also saves the model?
