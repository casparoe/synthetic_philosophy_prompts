I've been trying to formalize an objection to Leibniz's best-world claim for my metaphysics course and I think I built a reductio that proves way too much. Here's the chain:

(1) God actualized the best of all possible worlds, so for any other world W there is a reason why ours is better. (2) If worlds are comparable at all, there must be some metric that ranks them (call it overall value). (3) Now imagine world W* that's identical to ours except Bob freely chooses tea over coffee on one Tuesday morning. The difference in total value is negligible, so either ours is strictly better, W* is strictly better, or they're tied. (4) They can't be tied, because then God would lack a sufficient reason to pick ours over W* — and Leibniz thinks every choice needs one. (5) So every micro-variation gets a unique rank. That means the ordering of worlds is linear with no ties. (6) But there are infinitely many worlds, so either there's a single top world (Leibniz's claim) or there's an infinite ascending chain with no maximum, which makes 'the best' incoherent, like the largest number. (7) Leibniz avoids that by saying ours IS the max. Okay, but now run the argument downward: there's a second-best world, then a third, etc. That's fine. (8) The problem is upward. If ours is strictly best, there's no world even infintesimally better — there's an infinite 'value gap' between ours and every runner-up. (9) But if comparison works via a continuous metric (which (2) seemed to need), an infinite gap is impossible; you'd always be able to split the difference and construct a middle world. (10) So either the metric isn't continuous — in which case comparing worlds is just arbitary stipulation — or comparison collapses entirely. (11) Either way, God couldn't have rationally picked ours as 'the best'. So (1) is false, but that feels like I'm throwing out the baby with the bathwater.

Which step is the actual weak link? I especially don't want to give up (4), since the sufficient-reason idea seems central to Leibniz, and I'd like to keep that worlds can differ by single free choices (like the tea/coffee thing). What's the real cost of fixing this?
