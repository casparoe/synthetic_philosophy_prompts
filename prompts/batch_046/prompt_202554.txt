I am giving a seminar defence of a restricted growing-block view of time against the standard Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. My claim is that the argument illegitimately extends frame-relative simultaneity into a transitive relation of ontological co-existence—if A is simultaneous with B in frame F₁ and B with C in frame F₂, it concludes all three exist equally—whereas I hold that reality should be understood as growing only along the causal past of a specific worldline (that of Earth-bound observers). This selection, I argue, violates no physical law because the privilege is metaphysical rather than dynamical, not altering Lorentz covariance in the empirical structure. The audience is a philosophy-of-physics reading group: mostly block-universe sympathisers with solid command of Minkowski spacetime, suspicious of privileged frames, and quick to press whether any non-empirical selection criterion is ad hoc. They care about Lorentz invariance, empirical testability, and logical rigour.

Prepare my objection sheet: list the objections they will actually raise, in the order they are likely to come, each phrased as a physicist or philosopher of physics would phrase it. For each, indicate what I should concede at the outset, what I must not concede, and where my reply is soft. Specify the single reply that must land if the proposal is to survive the session, and identify the one question I am hoping nobody asks—explaining precisely why it undermines my position and how I might deflect without appearing evasive.
