Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, rhyme scheme A1bA2 with alternating refrains—staging Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility through a sustained metaphysical conceit: the self as a clock that must wind its own mainspring. Refrain A should articulate the condition for true moral responsibility, that one must be the ultimate source (causa sui) of one's own mental nature; Refrain B should articulate the regress, that every such source is itself produced by prior conditions for which one is not the source. In the five tercets, move backward through successive stages—present character, past choice, prior disposition, formative environment, and an origin point—so that each stanza pushes the required self-creation one generation further back, the clock's gears attempting to drive the very spring that drives them. In the final quatrain, bring both refrains together to express the conclusion: an infinite regress of self-creation is impossible, and a first cause cannot be its own cause, so true moral responsibility is impossible. Do not resolve the tension with compatibilist distinctions, divine grace, or libertarian escape hatches; end suspended at the logical impossibility. Render the argument precisely enough that a philosopher familiar with Strawson recognizes the structure (the demand to be responsible for one's nature, the backward chain of conditions), but let the poem stand on its own for a reader encountering the idea for the first time.
