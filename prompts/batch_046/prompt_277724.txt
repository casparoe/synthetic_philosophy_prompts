I serve on a municipal board that reviews proposed conditionality rules for unemployment benefits, and we need a reusable protocol. Our adopted framework permits a condition only if three conditions hold: the targeted behavior is within the recipient's direct control; enforcing the rule does not create a feedback loop that intensifies the need it addresses; and any exemption for vulnerability is structured so that it does not publicly sort recipients into a separate moral category. Write a decision procedure—ordered questions, criteria for sufficient answers, hard stops that reject a proposal, and explicit escalation points where the issue is values rather than fact—that a non-philosopher reviewer can complete in twenty minutes. Flag where the procedure is likely to misfire. Then apply it to three proposals: (i) a workfare rule requiring fifteen hours of community service weekly; (ii) a pilot in which recipients can satisfy job-search obligations by auditing other claimants' documentation, with mental-health exemptions certified by a private contractor and signaled only through an anonymized administrative code; and (iii) a lifetime bar on benefits for anyone convicted of fraud in the past five years, with an anonymous appeals channel. For each, report the procedure's output, note any step that forces a value judgment, and identify the case in which the result is likely to conflict with intuitive fairness.
