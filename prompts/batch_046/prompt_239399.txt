Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of self-locating belief revision must satisfy, treating the Sleeping Beauty problem as the central stress-test but not the only one. Specify, for each requirement, the exact case or distinction that motivates it and whether it is negotiable.

Your list should cover at least the following. First, the canonical protocol: a fair coin is flipped, Beauty is put to sleep, and if Tails she is awakened on Monday and Tuesday with amnesia between them; if Heads she is awakened only on Monday. State what credence in Heads she ought to have upon awakening, with the reasoning that must be preserved regardless of whether the answer is one-half or one-third. Second, centered versus uncentered updating: provide the rule for revising belief when an agent learns a purely indexical fact (e.g., “today is Monday”) that eliminates some centered possibilities without altering the objective chance, with the concrete scenario behind the rule. Third, the “double-halfer” tension: two agents with identical evidence about the coin but different temporal locations (one on Monday knowing only that she is awake, one on Tuesday knowing she is awake and that it is Tuesday) seem compelled to assign different credences to the same uncentered proposition; say whether a theory must resolve this disagreement or can tolerate it, and on what grounds. Fourth, compatibility with a restricted Principal Principle for self-locating credences, or an explicit argument for why that principle fails in centered contexts; include the case in which an agent knows the objective chance of an event and also knows she is one of several indistinguishable observers who might experience it. Fifth, consistency with standard Bayesian conditioning on non-indexical evidence received during the experiment—e.g., if Beauty is shown a document whose content depends on the coin outcome—without allowing the indexical framework to distort the evidential weight. Sixth, and here inject a non-standard variant: suppose the Tuesday awakening, and only the Tuesday awakening, is accompanied by a private sensory cue (a specific ambient temperature or acoustic signature) that is absent on Monday, and Beauty knows the protocol includes this cue. The theory must specify whether and how she should integrate this centered sensory information with her prior over Heads/Tails, and whether the presence or absence of the cue changes her credence in Heads relative to the canonical case.

For each desideratum, flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied—particularly between preserving the Principal Principle and accommodating the double-halfer result, and between treating existence-upon-waking as evidential and treating it as a selection effect. Then, briefly, score the halfer view, the thirder view, and a “restricted principal principle” approach against your list, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves: which requirement does the most work in excluding candidates, and which ones are merely prejudices of one camp? Conclude by stating whether your list reveals that “adequacy” in this domain is unitary or whether different legitimate purposes (e.g., normative decision theory versus descriptive psychology of belief) demand different subsets of these desiderata.
