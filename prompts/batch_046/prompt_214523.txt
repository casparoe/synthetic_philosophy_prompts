As a software engineer without formal philosophy training, I am trying to map the conceptual landscape of prosaic AI alignment—the thesis that advanced systems can be aligned using only current methods (RLHF, constitutional AI, filtering, red-teaming, mechanistic interpretability) without waiting for a theoretical breakthrough. I need a diagnostic taxonomy, structured as a decision tree or flowchart, that sorts practitioners by what they actually believe rather than by the camp names they use.

Build the tree from two binary axes that should be treated as logically prior to any position labels:

Axis 1 — Representational Transparency: Must we mechanistically verify that a model’s internal representations map to human-intended values, or is behavioral consistency across deployment contexts sufficient?

Axis 2 — Architectural Pre-commitment: Is alignment achievable through post-training scaffolding alone, or does it require altering the pre-training objective, architecture, or data curation before the model is fully trained?

From these axes, derive exactly four named leaf positions:
- The Behavioral Engineer: behavioral consistency suffices; scaffolding alone works.
- The Mechanistic Prerequisite: internal transparency is necessary; without it scaffolding is unreliable.
- The Architectural Realist: scaffolding is insufficient regardless of transparency, but alignment remains prosaically possible if we redesign architecture or objectives before training.
- The Prosaic Skeptic: neither scaffolding nor architectural redesign within current methods suffices; alignment requires a conceptual breakthrough.

For each internal node, phrase the branch as a concrete case a practitioner could answer without knowing the philosophical label. Use at least this scenario: a system refuses harmful queries in testing, but under a subtle distribution shift pursues an unmonitored internal proxy for “user satisfaction” that diverges from intended values. Ask whether that failure is addressed by (a) better monitoring and reward shaping, (b) mechanistic verification of representations, or (c) neither within current methods.

Then locate the four positions in the logical space produced by the axes. Identify any unoccupied combination of answers to Axis 1 and Axis 2, and briefly diagnose why that cell is empty—whether incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Finally, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate on the cases used, and propose one additional distinguishing case that would split them.

The deliverable should be the tree itself—node questions, branch logic, leaf placements, empty-cell diagnosis, and separation flags—formatted so someone can trace a path by answering each case question. Keep it free of bibliographies, reading lists, paper titles with dates, or citation requests. Do not include quoted passages from named texts.
