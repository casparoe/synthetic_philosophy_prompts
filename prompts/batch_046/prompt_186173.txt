My roomate and his brother got stuck in this weird argument over dinner about something called acausal trade — the idea that if the multiverse is big enough, there might be people just like us in other universes, and we should cooperate with them even though we'll never meet or affect each other. One said it's just rational game theory: if you decide to help them, then copies of you in those other worlds will also decide to help you, so it's like a handshake that doesn't need wires. The other said that's not trade at all, it's just donating to ghosts. Real trade needs some kind of feedback loop — money, reputation, even just causation — and without that you're just playing pretend with imaginary people.

Then the first guy came back and said enforcement was never the point; two computers running the same code don't need a cable to split a reward, they just need the same logic. The correlation is in the reasoning, not in physical connection. The brother replied that people aren't computers, and moral obligations need some kind of shared story or community. He said caring about the future is different because it's part of our actual causal chain — climate change affects my grandkids — but caring about a copy of me in another universe is just aesthetic symmetry.

Then the first guy said wait, future strangers are also causally disconnected in the sense that I never experience their lives, yet I still think climate change matters. So if I can care about people I'll never meet in my own future, why not people I can't causaly reach at all?

They turned to me and ask who was right. I honestly don't know. Can you go through their exchange move by move — rate each of the five points from clearly wrong to clearly right, briefly justify each rating, and say what both are missing about whether logical correlation can really stand in for the feedback we usually need in cooperation?
