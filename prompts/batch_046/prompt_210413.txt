In my graduate seminar on social epistemology, I am attempting to formalize the implicit taxonomy that a colleague—whose work on networked cognition I have taught for several years—has deployed across her case studies without ever stating it as a principle. Her verdicts on prior architectures are these. First, she treats a closed community that expels dissenting members and ridicules external sources as a clear echo chamber. Second, she treats a personalized feed that silently suppresses opposing outlets—never displaying them, never mentioning their absence—as a clear epistemic bubble. Third, she treats a scientific gatekeeping system that rejects contrarian submissions through explicit, publicly defended methodological criteria as neither pathology, but as legitimate filtering. Fourth, she treats a forum in which critical comments remain technically visible but are consistently buried beneath thousands of affirming reactions and in-group mockery as an echo chamber, not a bubble. Fifth, she treats a recommendation engine that exposes users to dissent only through algorithmically degraded, out-of-context fragments as an epistemic bubble, on the ground that dissent is present but structurally neutered rather than socially discredited.

Consider now a proposed federated architecture I have sketched for the seminar: each node applies a credibility filter calibrated to its own prior commitments, so that cross-cutting content rarely propagates; yet dissenting nodes remain fully visible in the network graph and can be queried directly—though initiating such a query triggers an automatic demotion of one's own node's visibility score across the federation. Would my colleague classify this as an echo chamber, an epistemic bubble, a hybrid, or as legitimate filtering akin to her third case? Identify the closest analog among her five prior rulings, then articulate at least two distinct principles—perhaps one centered on whether dissent is socially discredited versus structurally absent, another on whether access to dissent carries an explicit cost or penalty—that are jointly consistent with her track record yet yield different verdicts for this architecture. Give your best-guess prediction with reasons, and then specify what single additional feature—added to or removed from the architecture—would most decisively adjudicate between those principles.
