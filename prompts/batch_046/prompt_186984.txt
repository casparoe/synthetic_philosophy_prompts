My gut verdicts on honoring sunk costs, and I'm trying to see if there's one principle behind them. But if I've trained 6 months for a marathon and my ankle hurts on race day, quitting feels wrong in a way that isn't just 'waste of training' — more like breaking a commitment to my past self. Meanwhile a government finishing a useless bridge 'because we've come so far' is obviously irrational. Staying in a job I've hated for 10 years just because of the time invested also feels wrong, but I go back and forth on whether it's the same mistake as the concert ticket or closer to the marathon. And there's this other case I can't settle: I've realized my PhD thesis is built on a false premise halfway through writing, but part of me says the years already spent make it worth salvaging. Can you lay out a principle — or maybe two — that captures these, tells me what to do about the PhD case, predicts the verdict on something I haven't mentioned (say, pulling out of a war after heavy casualties), and is honest about which of my gut calls it has to reject?
