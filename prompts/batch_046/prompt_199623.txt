I'm trying to identify a recurring dialectical structure in debates over whether phenomenal consciousness—qualia—can be eliminated or "quined" from ontology, particularly in response to arguments like those of Chase and Sanborn, who contend that the cluster of properties traditionally ascribed to qualia (intrinsicness, privacy, ineffability, direct epistemic access) is internally contradictory.

I keep seeing the same structural move unfold in two different guises. First, a defender is pressed on the tension between qualia being immediately known and being strictly private: if they are private, how can anyone verify that others have them, and if they are not private, what remains of their special status? The defender relaxes privacy to a weaker "first-person privilege," treating this as dissolving the contradiction. Yet in the very next turn, the defender invokes that first-person privilege to argue that any third-person science necessarily omits the phenomenal, thereby using the weakened feature to restore the ontological weight it was supposed to have surrendered. Second, a parallel oscillation occurs with ineffability: the defender concedes that qualia are not literally indescribable, but only "not capturable in physical vocabulary," and then immediately appeals to that inexpressibility to claim that functionalist or representationalist accounts miss the essence of experience.

What strikes me is not mere inconsistency, but a specific structural feature: the commitment to qualia is not anchored in any single property, but distributed across an interdependent cluster. Each property can be individually diluted to avoid contradiction, yet the diluted residue of each still performs the work of preserving phenomenal distinctiveness. The position collapses only if all properties are diluted at once, which never happens because the defense shifts the burden of distinctiveness from one limb of the cluster to another. This is not a standard motte-and-bailey, since there is no clear defensible core and indefensible extension being swapped; nor is it ordinary auxiliary-hypothesis adjustment, because no predictive theory is being saved from empirical failure. It feels closer to a kind of ontological shell game, but I do not know whether the literature has a precise term for this distributed-retreat pattern.
