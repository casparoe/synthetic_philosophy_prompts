Write a villanelle — nineteen lines, ABA rhyme, with two refrains that alternate through the tercets and converge in the final quatrain — on what makes consent valid, treating deception, coercion, and competence as separate failure modes rather than a single deficit. Let the first refrain be procedural: the speaker notes that no force was applied and no material fact was misrepresented. Let the second refrain be epistemic-competence: the speaker realizes they lacked the conceptual resources to grasp the normative framing of the act they were agreeing to, not merely its physical description. Use the form's compulsive repetition to mirror how coercion and deception can be identified and corrected, while the competence failure keeps returning unresolved. In the closing couplet, place both refrains side by side without resolution, leaving the consent suspended. Write in American English, keep the philosophical distinctions accurate enough that a specialist in the ethics of consent recognizes the separation between factual deception and normative incompetence, and do not resolve the tension into either a sermon about autonomy or a shrug about human limitation — leave it open where the discussion actually ends.
