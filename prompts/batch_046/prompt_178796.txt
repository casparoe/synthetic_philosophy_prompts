Draft the joint statement for an adversarial collaboration between two camps on historical objectivity: Camp A defends a Rankean methodological objectivism, holding that rigorous source criticism can recover the past as it actually was and that presentist bias is correctable through disciplined evidence; Camp B represents the sceptical line of Carr and Hayden White, holding that all historical accounts are present-shaped narrative constructions, that facts never speak for themselves, and that the aspiration to pure objectivity conceals an unacknowledged ideological frame. The document should open with a numbered list of at least five claims both camps can sign—covering, for example, the independence of the past from present narration, the distinction between fabrication and interpretation, the inevitability of selection, the normative role of evidence, and the difference between a correctable error and an unavoidable standpoint—followed by a disagreements table with precisely worded opposing positions that each camp accepts as its own view. Then identify three cruxes: whether narrative coherence is an epistemic virtue tracking causal structure or an aesthetic imposition suppressing discontinuity; whether archival silence is missing data awaiting recovery or a structurally significant absence that itself shapes narrative; and whether the historian’s present standpoint is an unavoidable hermeneutic condition (making objectivity a matter of reflexive transparency) or a correctable distortion (making objectivity achievable through method). For each crux, specify a concrete shared case—assume a flood destroyed 90% of 1848 municipal archives in a specific town, leaving only police surveillance reports and church baptismal registers, with both camps agreeing these fragments are the sole available sources—and record in advance what each camp predicts about the interpretation of that archive. End with a brief methodological note stating what evidence or argument, if produced, would cause each camp to revise its position on at least one crux. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; keep all formulations neutral enough that neither camp would reject the wording of its own view.
