Design a decision-tree taxonomy for a graduate philosophy-of-mind seminar that classifies positions on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. The tree must have exactly three binary splits, each expressed as a concrete case or claim answerable without prior knowledge of the position names. Split 1 (the Residual Insistence Case): a neuroscientist achieves perfect predictive mapping of a subject's mental state using only functional variables; the subject still reports that "the feel itself" is left out. Does this insistence indicate a real non-functional property, or an introspective projection? Split 2 (applies only to the projection branch): is the illusion global—no mental state possesses qualitative character—or local—some states have real non-phenomenal qualities that are misrepresented as phenomenal? Split 3 (applies to both illusionist branches): in the Cognitive Penetrability Test, a subject fully grasps the mechanistic explanation of her own state; does the qualitative appearance dissolve (penetrable) or persist unchanged (impenetrable)? Map onto the leaves: strong illusionism, weak illusionism, the phenomenal-concept strategy, property dualism, and any unnamed combinations. For each occupied leaf, name the position (or mark "unnamed") and state its single most awkward commitment in one sentence. List every unoccupied combination of answers across the three splits and briefly diagnose each as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of named leaves the tree fails to genuinely separate—especially whether penetrable and impenetrable variants of strong illusionism collapse into the same practical stance. Build the tree from the logical space of answers first, then place the named positions inside it, and keep every diagnostic question phrased so that a student can answer it without already knowing which label it leads to.
