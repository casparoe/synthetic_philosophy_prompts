I was arguing with my friends last weekend about why countries like Switzerland, Sweden, and Finland still have conscription instead of just hiring proffessional soldiers, and I got obsessed with this idea I made up called "trust debt" - basically if you opt out of serving you loose your vote on defense policy because you've broken the social contract that keeps the country neutral. I'm running a workshop at the community center next tuesday for about 15 people, 90 minutes, mostly neighbors who care about politics but don't read philosophy books or anything. I want them to actually feel the force of armed neutrality, not just discuss it, so I'm thinking of a role-play with cards and chips.

The setup is a fictional neutral country facing a border crisis. Some players are conscript-citizens who both serve and vote, others are career soldiers with no vote, and everyone has hidden family resources they can either contribute to the national pool or hoard. There's a crisis card that forces a defense decision, but here's the twist: anyone who avoided the draft earlier now has reduced voting weight, yet they kept more resources. I need actual rules that work with just paper, pens, and maybe some poker chips as reputation points, plus a payoff scheme that doesn't involve real money. Then I need a debrief that maps specific moments - like people refusing to contribute or ganging up on the conscripts - onto the real tension in armed neutrality and this trust-debt thing I invented. Also please be honest about what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and what part of the actual Swiss or Swedish system this activity just can't reproduce since its only strangers playing for 90 minutes.
