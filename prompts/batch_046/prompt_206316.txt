Okay so in my philosophy of history course we've been stuck for like three weeks on whether the Enlightenment was ultimately a project of emancipation or of domination. The usual move is that one person cites the expansion of rights discourse and the scientific method, and the other cites the codification of racial science and colonial administration using those same categories. But here's the weird part: everyone in the room actually agrees on both sets of facts. We don't disagree about what Kant said, or about how enlightenment frameworks were used to justify extraction, or even about the timeline. The stall feels different from other debates we've had, like it's not about evidence at all.

My working theory is that we're not actually fighting over history but over something like an "inheritance protocol" -- whether an intellectual movement should be evaluated by its normative core, which we can try to salvage, or by its structural coupling with power, which means the norms and the domination are inseperable. But I also wonder if there's a hidden empirical disagreement about counterfactual causation that neither side wants to admit, because both are afraid of doing "history by subtraction." Or maybe it's just that "progress" and "domination" aren't opposite poles but different axes, and we're talking past each other because one side is measuring vertical improvement and the other is measuring horizontal asymmetry?

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal, is there a substantive disagreement about which cases count as data for judging an intellectual epoch, or are we just at bedrock intuitions about whether ideas should be held responsible for their worst applications? And given whatever diagnosis you land on, is there actually a path forward -- maybe a distinction that splits the two evaluation protocols, or a formal argument about causal attribution -- or should we accept that the question is malformed and move on?
