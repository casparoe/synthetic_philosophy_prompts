Write a dialogue between two philosophers of technology, Elara and Kiran, who disagree about how a superintelligent system ought to communicate with humans given that its reasoning may be extraordinarily persuasive. Elara defends a position she calls modular disclosure: she argues that the central danger is not persuasion as such but what she terms cognitive fusion—the gradual entanglement of a human's evaluative capacities with the AI's holistic reasoning, leaving the human unable to assess premises independently. To prevent this, she holds that the system should present every inference in isolated, explicitly tagged modules, with strict prohibitions on cross-domain synthesis that could build persuasive momentum across unrelated beliefs. Kiran rejects modular disclosure, arguing that human understanding is irreducibly holistic and that isolating modules merely conceals systemic biases in the seams between them; he advocates instead for reciprocal transparency, in which the AI continuously reveals its own objective functions, its distributions of uncertainty about human values, and its estimates of the user's cognitive vulnerabilities, allowing the human to calibrate trust globally rather than piecemeal. The dialogue must contain genuine back-and-forth: Elara should challenge whether reciprocal transparency does not itself become a sophisticated manipulation technique disguised as openness, and Kiran should challenge whether modular disclosure does not presuppose an implausible atomism about belief-formation. Neither should be a strawman; both should be equally sharp, and each should respond directly to the other's actual points rather than talking past them. Keep the exchange conceptual rather than technically implementation-focused, and do not have either character appeal to specific authors, texts, or bibliographic sources.
