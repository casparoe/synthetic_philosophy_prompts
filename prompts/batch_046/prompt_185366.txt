Please give me your credence—stated as a number between 0 and 1—that Scheffler's argument succeeds in establishing that extinction carries this additional, non-instrumental badness, separate from the harm of the deaths themselves. 1. The psychological premise: that a substantial share of human motivation and valuation genuinely depends on the belief or expectation that future generations will exist, rather than being self-contained.
2. The normative premise: that if humanity had no future, our current projects would suffer a genuine loss of value that counts as a present harm, not merely as a counterfactual disappointment.
3. The separability premise: that this loss of value is not reducible to the aggregate harm of losing all future lives—i.e., that there is a distinct badness attached to the collapse of the dependency itself.
4. The irreplaceability premise: that people could not simply "refactor" their values to be self-contained (abandoning long-term projects in favour of present-oriented ones) without suffering a catastrophic and comparable loss of meaning.
