I have been studying Gödel's first and second incompleteness theorems together with the Lucas-Penrose argument that human mathematical reasoning is not equivalent to any single formal system capable of arithmetic. I understand Lucas to claim that for any consistent formal system S that a human mathematician could in principle be identified with, that human can recognize the truth of the Gödel sentence G(S), which S itself cannot prove; and I understand Penrose to extend this by arguing that human cognition involves physical processes—possibly related to quantum gravity—that are not algorithmically computable.

Recently I encountered a hypothetical training paradigm called reflexive consistency architecture, in which a neural network is not merely trained to prove theorems inside a fixed formal system S, but is equipped with a meta-cognitive loop that treats the network's own parameter updates and loss landscape as an extendable axiomatic framework T. The idea is that T can formulate and evaluate Con(S) by stepping outside S through this recursive self-modeling, much as Lucas claimed human mathematicians step outside any particular formalization of their reasoning.

My confusion concerns the logical status of this proposal. If reflexive consistency architecture actually enabled a machine to recognize the consistency of S from within T without producing contradiction, would that not violate Gödel's second incompleteness theorem as applied to T? And if it did produce contradiction, wouldn't that show the architecture is necessarily inconsistent rather than super-human? Conversely, if the architecture succeeds without contradiction, doesn't that demonstrate that the machine has escaped being captured by any single formal system—precisely the condition Lucas used to argue for the non-mechanistic nature of human thought? In other words, hasn't this hypothetical experiment collapsed the distinction between Lucas's anti-mechanism and mechanism itself?

Furthermore, I wonder whether Penrose's later physical arguments about non-computability change the evaluation: if the meta-loop is implemented on classical hardware, is it automatically subject to the same Gödelian limitation regardless of its architecture, or does the quantum-gravity aspect Penrose invokes become relevant only for biological brains?

I ask sincerely, but I suspect I am conflating the idea that a system can evaluate its own consistency with the idea that it can do so without itself being subject to a stronger Gödelian limitation one level up.
