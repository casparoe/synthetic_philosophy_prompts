For a seminar in political philosophy, I want to get the specification straight before comparing frameworks, so I’m asking for the full desiderata: what must any adequate taxonomy of liberty—one that genuinely distinguishes positive liberty, negative liberty, and republican liberty as non-domination—deliver in order to count as conceptually adequate?

Please lay out the requirements list, with the motivating case or consideration behind each:

- The conceptual distinctions it must preserve without collapse: for instance, between mere absence of interference and absence of arbitrary interference, and between having options and being the author of one’s choices. Include why collapsing either distinction undermines the taxonomy’s purpose.
- The canonical cases it must classify correctly: the open door versus the locked door, the benevolent master versus the arbitrary master, and so on. Then add a synthetic case I’ll call “predictive governance”—an institutional system that uses behavioral forecasting to restructure choice architectures so that citizens never encounter prohibited options, without any explicit command, threat, or observed interference. The taxonomy must classify whether this constitutes unfreedom, and if so, under which conception and why.
- The empirical and institutional constraints it must respect: e.g., findings that perceived autonomy can persist under heavy choice architecture, or that domination can obtain even when no actual interference occurs. State which results a taxonomy contradicts only at the cost of implausibility.
- The jointly unsatisfiable subsets: flag any pair (or larger set) of desiderata that cannot be satisfied together, such as maintaining that republican liberty is genuinely distinct from negative liberty while also requiring that every instance of domination be reducible to a risk of actual interference.
- The division between non-negotiable requirements and camp-specific preferences—e.g., whether only intentional obstacles count, or whether structural conditions can be sources of unfreedom on their own.

For each desideratum, indicate why it is a genuine constraint rather than a prejudice of one camp (liberal, republican, or positive-liberty). Then, briefly, score the three dominant framings—Berlin’s two-concept scheme, Pettit’s triadic addition, and recent “structural” expansions—against the list, noting where each fails and whether any failure is fatal. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on an exhaustive literature survey; a table is fine if it clarifies the mapping.
