I've been trying to work through the argument about special relativity and the block universe—the one that uses the relativity of simultaneity to show that all moments exist equally—and I think I've pushed it into a place where it undermines itself. Here is the chain as I see it.

(1) In special relativity, simultaneity is frame-dependent: two events that one inertial observer judges simultaneous will be judged past and future by another observer in uniform motion relative to the first.

(2) For any event E lying in what I call my objective future, there exists some inertial frame—moving at the appropriate velocity relative to me—in which E occurs exactly now, simultaneous with my present experience.

(3) Call this the observer-parity principle: if an event is simultaneous with my present for any physically legitimate observer, then that event possesses the same ontological status—real, actual, not merely potential—as the events I currently experience.

(4) By continuously varying velocity, I can construct frames that map every successive point along my world-line onto my present.

(5) So every future event along my world-line is, for some legitimate frame, happening right now.

(6) Therefore the distinction between past, present, and future is not a feature of reality itself but only an artifact of my particular state of motion.

(7) If all these moments are equally real and equally present, then change is not a real transformation but merely my sequential awareness of a static four-dimensional object—like turning pages of a book that was already complete.

(8) But the very reasoning that led me here was itself a temporal process: I moved from premise to conclusion over time.

(9) If change and temporal process are illusory, then my reasoning never actually unfolded; it was always just a static pattern, which means it could not have justified anything.

(10) So the argument appears to destroy its own cognitive authority.

I want to keep (1) and (3), and I especially do not want to surrender the idea that physics should inform ontology. But I also want to avoid the self-refutation in (9). Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost? Is there a way to keep the block universe without losing the reality of reasoning?
