I'm trying to work out what I actually think about the Grand Inquisitor's claim that freedom is a burden best surrendered for happiness, so I've been testing my intuitions against concrete cases. A benevolent state eliminates scarcity and conflict through AI governance; citizens retain a formal right to alter the system, but are never nudged to exercise it, and live contentedly. I think this is acceptable—the structure of authorship remains intact. The same state, but now it quietly suppresses information that dissent is possible: clearly the Inquisitor's betrayal, even though reported satisfaction is identical. A monastery demands total obedience; a novice enters with full knowledge of secular life, feels genuinely liberated, and never leaves. I think her freedom is not violated, because she authored the framework of her own submission. A child raised in an identically structured community, never knowing alternatives exist, reports the same feeling of liberation. I think this is wrong—she never bore the burden of choosing the framework. A democratic majority votes to dissolve itself permanently and bind all future generations to an expert council. I think this is illegitimate, even if unanimous, because present authorship cannot mortgage future authorship. But here is the tension I cannot resolve: if individuals freely take a drug that eliminates all desire and therefore all suffering, each reporting perfect bliss, I want to say they have lost something essential, yet each one apparently authored that loss. Is there a coherent principle—perhaps involving what I call recursive authorship, the capacity not merely to choose within a framework but to evaluate and endorse the framework itself—that delivers my first four verdicts and resolves the fifth without contradiction? If not, which of my five verdicts should I overturn for the sake of simplicity? And whatever principle survives, what does it predict about a society in which citizens must periodically reconfirm, under conditions of full information, their consent to be governed by the benevolent AI?
