Read Edwin Abbott's Flatland as a dramatization of Kuhnian paradigm shifts and incommensurability. The Square's encounter with the Sphere—who refuses to prove three-dimensional space by analogy within flat geometry and instead insists that the Square be physically lifted into it—has often been read as a parable of scientific revolution. Take that reading seriously, but also take Kuhn seriously: ask whether the Square's subsequent inability to translate his three-dimensional experience back into two-dimensional terms demonstrates genuine incommensurability in Kuhn's strict sense, or whether Abbott's commitment to an objectively real, hierarchically ordered geometry actually makes the old and new paradigms fully commensurable, with the flatlanders simply being wrong rather than operating within an incomparable framework. Does the Sphere's pedagogical refusal to argue—he demands direct experiential conversion rather than logical persuasion—mirror Kuhn's claim that paradigm shifts are not resolved by neutral observation-language but by something closer to religious conversion? And does the ending, in which the Square is imprisoned for heresy by the Circle priests, illustrate Kuhn's sociological thesis that institutional gatekeepers resist revolutions for reasons internal to their paradigm, or does it instead portray the old guard as genuinely irrational in a way that Kuhn's symmetry thesis forbids? Push further on a tension the novel introduces but Kuhn rarely addresses: Abbott seems to hold that the higher-dimensional paradigm is not merely different but objectively superior and ultimately rationally accessible, whereas Kuhn's incommensurability thesis is often read as carrying anti-realist or relativist undertones about scientific progress. Give a reading faithful to both the novel's geometric logic and to the philosophy, and conclude by saying what Flatland adds to the debate about incommensurability that pure philosophical argument does not—particularly on whether a paradigm can be simultaneously incommensurable with its predecessor and objectively better than it.
