After a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a friend about whether mental symbols actually drive physical substrate dynamics, I want to check whether my own position on the careenium is internally coherent before I defend it further. I understand the careenium—following Hofstadter's analogy—as the cranium conceived as a system of substrate-level elements (symms, or marbles) interacting with high-level symbolic patterns (simmballs). Here are my commitments.

First, downward causation is real but not force-based: simmballs do not exert any new physical forces, yet they restructure the attractor landscape of the substrate, so that an identical initial marble-state can evolve along different trajectories depending on which symbolic cluster is active. Second, this restructuring does not violate physical closure, because the landscape is fully determined by the substrate's own relational properties; the symbols are compressed descriptions of those relations rather than extra ontological entities. Third, symbolic content is context-dependent and not reducible to any fixed substrate configuration—the same marble arrangement can instantiate different simmballs in different global contexts. Fourth, if one artificially suppresses the formation of simmball clusters while preserving every substrate interaction exactly, downward restructuring vanishes but the substrate dynamics remain unchanged; this implies that mental properties disappear without any corresponding physical change. Fifth, the self—the "I"—is nothing more than the largest, most persistent simmball cluster, with no substrate-independent identity. Sixth, I have recently adopted what my friend derides as "symbolic viscosity": the claim that substrate dynamics resist rapid reorganization by new symbols, which explains cognitive inertia and habit-formation without invoking any non-physical resistance.

My friend argued that commitments two through four are jointly impossible. If symbols are merely compressed descriptions of substrate relations, he said, they cannot also be the factor that explains why suppressing them alters causal trajectories, as commitment four asserts. I also worry about an internal tension: commitment five seems to conflict with commitment three, since if the self is just a cluster, its feeling of being more than substrate should dissolve once we see that self-reference is merely a simmball describing other simmballs—yet I am unsure whether this is a genuine contradiction or only an apparent one. Finally, I am uncertain whether symbolic viscosity is compatible with my denial that symbols add forces. If viscosity is the substrate's resistance to being reorganized by symbols, does that not implicitly grant the symbols causal efficacy over the substrate?

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and where exactly does the tension sit? Rank the problems by severity, and for each genuine tension indicate the least costly revision and what that revision costs. In particular, tell me whether my view on downward causation collapses into either pure physicalism—where symbols are epiphenomenal—or into an emergent dualism I want to reject, or whether there is a stable middle ground I have not clearly articulated. And please say whether "symbolic viscosity" can be saved within my framework, or whether it must be abandoned.
