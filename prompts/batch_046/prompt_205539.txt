People keep comparing the sphex wasp's loop — dragging the cricket, checking the burrow, repeating if disturbed — to a bit of code that's compiled with no runtime exception handling: once it starts there's no internal mechanism to halt and inspect conditions. They then claim human free will is like having a runtime interperter that can inspect its own stack and break the loop. How far does this hold? Can you analyse what maps onto what — the cricket, the burrow-check, the repetition — and where the software analogy falls over, given wasp behavour isn't literally compiled? What does it quietly smuggle in about flexibility: does it assume you need a meta-level observer to be free, or could a deterministic but more adaptive loop do the same? There's also this pattern in human reasoning where we patch our beliefs with ad-hoc rules that look flexible but are really just more loops — a bit like Ptolemaic epicycles. Should that be part of the audit, or does it confuse the comparison? And does the conclusion — that we're genuinely different in a free-will-relevant way — survive once the disanalogies are all on the table?
