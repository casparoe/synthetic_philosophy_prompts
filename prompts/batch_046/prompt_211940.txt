I have been working through my own views on price controls—specifically minimum wages and rent control—without formal training in economics, and I want to check whether my commitments actually cohere. Here is what I currently hold.

First, I think that markets generally coordinate information well, but they become ethically defective when one party lacks a credible outside option and must accept terms they would reject under conditions of fair bargaining.

Second, I think this is exactly what happens with low-wage labor: a worker with no savings and immediate needs cannot credibly refuse an offer below subsistence, so a statutory minimum wage corrects a structural coercion rather than distorting a voluntary exchange.

Third, I think rent control does something different. It compels property owners to supply housing at a price below what they would voluntarily accept, which feels to me like a partial expropriation without compensation, not like a correction of coercion directed at the tenant.

Fourth, I recognize that both policies are price controls—one a floor, one a ceiling—so if I justify the first by appealing to an ethical failure of the market, I ought either to justify the second on parallel grounds or to identify a relevant disanalogy.

Fifth, I have tried to locate that disanalogy in the nature of the goods: labor is a flow that can be withheld—workers can choose not to supply it—whereas housing is a fixed stock, and if returns fall, owners can withdraw maintenance, convert units to other uses, or exit the market entirely, so rent control damages supply in a way that wage floors do not.

Sixth, and here is where I feel the deepest tension, I also believe that housing is a more immediate and non-substitutable need than income itself. If a person cannot afford rent, they face exposure and harm that is more urgent and direct than the harm of earning a low wage, which suggests that controlling housing prices might actually be the more pressing ethical intervention, not the less.

Given these six commitments, which ones genuinely conflict, which conflicts dissolve once the underlying principle is made explicit, and what is the least damaging revision I could make to restore consistency? I am not asking for a survey of economic literature; I want an audit of this specific belief set, ordered by severity of tension, with an indication of which commitment I should be most prepared to refine or abandon.
