As a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to philosophy, I have been reading Borges's reflections on time—above all his argument that past, present, and future are mental fictions imposed upon an eternal, undivided present, and his fiction depicting a point in space in which every other point coexists simultaneously. I ask you to compose a joke that depends on the actual content of these ideas rather than on a generic "time is an illusion" punchline. Specifically, the joke should turn on the tension between Borges's denial of linear succession and the irreversible physical processes—such as entropic decay or the degradation of laboratory equipment—that any working scientist takes as given. It should be funny to someone who knows Borges's writings and who understands that the second law of thermodynamics is not merely psychological. After delivering the joke, please provide a brief meta-note: state how confident you are in the interpretation of Borges employed, single out the claim or framing you are least sure of, and indicate where the joke takes liberties with Borges's texts or depends on a contested reading of his philosophy.
