There's the classic Millikan/Dretske-style worry about misrepresentation and selection history—like how a frog's snap at a black dot counts as representing 'fly' even when it's totally wrong. Then there's the push to extend this to artificial systems, where people ask whether a neural net trained via gradient descent has any original intentionality at all, or just derived content from its designers. And then there's this weirder angle I ran into in a seminar: if you take teleosemantics seriously for distributed biological systems—say, a colony of bacteria coordinating via quorum sensing—then the boundary between individual mental content and group-level functional norms gets blurry, and maybe the 'content' isn't localized in any one organism.

I don't want a survey; I want you to rank them in terms of which open questions are both tractable and upstream—like, if you solved the misrepresentation problem for artificial systems, would that change how we think about distributed biological cases, or is it the other way around? Which one is overrated, which depends on some specific technical result that probably won't come through, and which should I actually spend time on? Defend the ordering with real critera—tractability, neglectedness, how many other debates depend on this—not just that it sounds cool. Also, what are the implications for cognitive science more broadly if one of these turns out right?
