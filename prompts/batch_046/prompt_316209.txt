Retired maths teacher here, reading philosophy of maths for fun. I've noticed a pattern around Cantor's problem -- the one about whether there's a size of infinity sitting between the counting numbers and the real line -- and I don't know if there's a proper name for it. First Cohen showed you can't answer it with the standard ZFC axioms. Fair enough. But instead of leaving it alone, people started proposing stronger axiom sets -- forcing axioms, big cardinal rules -- that do pick a side. Then those stronger sets turn out to have their own blind spots further up, so you get this ladder: each time you fix an independance result by climbing to stronger foundations, the gap doesn't disappear, it just moves to the next rung. I've seen it with CH being false under Martin's Maximum, but then questions about that axiom's consistency with other large-cardinal assumptions open up. So it's not just one auxiliary hypothesis being patched; it's a repeated relocation of undecidability every time you expand your base. Does this loop have an established concept? How is it different from ordinary theory revision, and where does it stop being this specific thing and become something else? If there's no name, I'd call it something like 'foundation creep' -- but I'd rather know the real term and its edges.
