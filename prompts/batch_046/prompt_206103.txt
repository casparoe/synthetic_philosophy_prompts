I'm preparing a consistency audit for a pragmatism working group and want to check whether my hybrid position actually holds together before I present it. Here are my commitments: (1) I accept a broadly Peircean account of theoretical truth—beliefs are true insofar as they would survive indefinite correction by an open community of inquirers—but I reject Peirce's monism about inquiry aims and deny that there is a single regulative ideal of "the end of inquiry"; instead, I think inquiry terminates in domain-relative equilibrium states rather than one universal convergence point. (2) I accept a Jamesian account of practical belief—moral and existential commitments are warranted when they resolve what I call "epistemic friction" in lived experience and carry concrete experiential cash-value—but I deny this makes them merely subjective; I hold that their truth-conditions are indexical to a form of life rather than reducible to individual preference. (3) I claim there is no sharp theoretical/practical boundary: scientific claims can bear practical cash-value, and ethical claims can be empirically revisable in light of new experience. (4) I think fallibilism applies uniformly, so even practical beliefs must remain revisable when they cease to resolve friction. (5) I maintain that belief-fixation should be guided by predictive-empirical methods where forecasting is at stake, but by communal narrative and first-person testimony where questions of value and meaning dominate. Which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision for each tension? Rank them by severity, identify the single change that would restore the most consistency overall, and be specific about whether denying a single end of inquiry undermines the Peircean component, whether uniform fallibilism destabilizes the Jamesian indexicality claim, and whether the absence of a sharp boundary forces the two truth-accounts to collapse into one another.
