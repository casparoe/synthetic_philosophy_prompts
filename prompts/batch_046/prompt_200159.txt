I am preparing a proposal for a one-semester term paper of approximately 6,000 words on material constitution, and I want to move beyond the familiar statue-and-clay dialectic without invoking obscure literature I cannot verify. My working idea is that the standard debate treats constitution as a dyadic relation between an artifact and its material substrate, but this misses a structurally intermediate layer that I will call a stabilizing design profile: a constrained set of functional and aesthetic conditions that the substrate must satisfy in order to constitute that specific artifact. This profile is neither reducible to the physical properties of the clay nor identical to any observer’s intentional attitude; it functions as an ontological mediator. The project asks whether taking this triadic structure—material, design profile, artifact—seriously dissolves the classic puzzle cases (gradual part replacement, squashing, perfect duplication) or merely relocates them into disputes about cross-cultural or cross-temporal design divergence. I need the proposal to state this question precisely enough that it has a determinable answer, to explain the conceptual method (analyzing the logical form of constitution claims across static sculptures and simple tools, leaving architecture aside), and to specify what a successful result looks like versus what would count as failure—say, if the triadic framework collapses back into standard two-place constitution theories without explanatory gain. Because this combines formal ontology with detailed case analysis, I also want an honest scope assessment: the paper should be feasible in one semester only if restricted to non-functional sculptures and basic instruments, with explicit acknowledgment that extending the framework to digital or distributed artifacts exceeds the time available. Please begin the proposal itself immediately, with no preface or commentary; open with the bottom-line claim about what the project asks and why the triadic framing matters, then present the precise research question, the step-by-step approach, the range of possible conclusions and what would decide between them, and the realistic limitations for a single semester. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or requests for further reading.
