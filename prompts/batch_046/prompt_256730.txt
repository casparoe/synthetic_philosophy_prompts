I would like you to draft one essay question—together with a model answer of roughly 500 words and a detailed marking scheme—that centers on Robert Nozick’s discussion in Anarchy, State, and Utopia of how animals ought to be treated: the hybrid view, often summarized as “utilitarianism for animals, Kantianism for people,” according to which animals fall within an aggregative calculus of welfare but do not possess the inviolable rights reserved for rational agents. The prompt should ask whether Nozick’s hybrid framework survives this gap, or whether it collapses into either pure utilitarianism (which ignores rights altogether) or pure Kantian exclusion (which seems to ignore suffering). In the model answer, please include the specific moves that separate an excellent response from a merely competent one—such as distinguishing moral standing from moral consideration, addressing whether an “experience-machine” analog applies to animals, and evaluating whether a “negative-rights-without-reciprocity” option might fill the gap more stably than Nozick’s split treatment. The marking scheme should list five must-hit points, assign marks to each (total 20), and note the characteristic errors that cost marks at each stage. Finally, please conclude with a brief self-critical note stating what argument or consideration would change your assessment—specifically, where the reasoning about the reciprocity gap is most vulnerable, and what evidence or counter-example would lead you to reject the claim that Nozick’s framework is unstable rather than merely incomplete. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the answer should be fully intelligible to someone without web access.
