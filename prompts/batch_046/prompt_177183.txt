Consider the cluster of practices governing how archaeological museums classify reconstructed bronze statues. Under standard conservation protocols, a statue is catalogued under its original accession number if a physically continuous “core”—typically the torso and head—survives, regardless of how much of its visible form consists of modern replacements that have been chemically patinated to match; yet when the same core fragments are dispersed across separate storage facilities, each piece receives an independent catalog entry, as if the object had been destroyed rather than divided.
