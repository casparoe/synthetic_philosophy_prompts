Polybius's anacyclosis is a textbook case of structural cyclicalism because he maps monarchy, kingship, tyranny, aristocracy, oligarchy, democracy, and mob rule as an inevitable sequence driven purely by human nature, leaving no room for fortune or external events. Spengler is essentially a linear thinker disguised as a cyclical one; since each culture is unique and never repeats, there is no true cycle—only parallel linear trajectories that happen to look alike. Toynbee belongs to the contingent camp because his challenge-and-response model allows societies to avoid decline indefinitely, provided they keep producing creative minorities that solve successive problems.
