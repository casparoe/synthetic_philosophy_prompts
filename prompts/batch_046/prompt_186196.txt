For a reading group that stress-tests philosophical profiles, I'm writing down my commitments on path dependence and historical contingency so others can poke at them. Here is where I stand: I think institutional path dependence is real and is best modeled as regime-switching dynamics — long stable periods where positive feedback loops make deviation prohibitively costly, punctuated by critical junctures where those costs collapse and paths can diverge. At the macro scale, however, I think outcomes like industrialization, democratic consolidation, or mass literacy are largely convergent: societies facing similar ecological and technological constraints tend to arrive at comparable end states regardless of their specific trajectory between critical junctures. I also think the "locked in" framing is misleading outside of a particular regime; institutions decay, are overthrown, or become obsolete, so path dependence is local and temporary rather than deterministic. On epistemics, I hold that counterfactual claims about history should meet scientific modeling standards — explicit structural causal models with calibrated confidence intervals — and that without them we should maintain strict agnosticism. For evaluating contingency, I prefer an outside-view reference-class approach: match societies on initial conditions, standardize the time horizon, and treat critical junctures as the natural comparison units rather than continuous timelines. Finally, I distinguish trajectory path dependence (the intermediate sequence within a regime is irreversible) from outcome path dependence (the final macro-state is causally determined by that sequence), and I accept the former. But I also think trajectory dependence necessarily entails at least weak outcome dependence, because the sequence of intermediate states shapes the option set available at each critical juncture.
