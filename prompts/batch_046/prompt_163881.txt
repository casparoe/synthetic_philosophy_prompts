In a seminar on naturalized semantics, I found myself defending a strengthened version of Dennett’s two-bitser argument against Searle’s insistence on a metaphysically privileged category of original intentionality. The position I now hold—call it the evolutionary-continuum thesis—is that there is no categorical boundary between derived and original aboutness, only a gradient in the complexity of the design history that fixes representational content. On this view, a brain’s semantic states are not metaphysically original but are themselves derived from evolutionary selection histories functioning as a kind of blind designer; consequently, the thermostat, the neural circuit, and the programmed computer all sit on a single continuum of design-derived intentionality, differing in the richness of their functional architectures rather than in the presence or absence of an irreducible first-person aboutness. I want to know precisely what would count against this thesis: not generic criticisms of naturalism, but the specific observations, conceptual distinctions, or formal results that would genuinely undermine the claim that evolutionary design dissolves Searle’s binary. Please sort the potential defeaters by how decisive they would be, distinguishing arguments that would force a retreat to a modified position from those that would be merely awkward; include what defenders of this Dennett-style view would themselves concede as genuinely damaging rather than only what critics allege; address whether the thesis has already been qualified into unfalsifiability—for instance, by expanding “design” so broadly that any representational system counts as designed, or by treating aboutness as purely interpretive—and assess whether such insulation is a virtue or a fatal defect for a view that aims to eliminate a metaphysical distinction. If relevant, reference whether existing empirical findings about the specificity of neural representation or about the normative dimensions of misrepresentation pose an actual threat, and indicate what kind of evidence, if any, could convince a proponent that some form of original intentionality survives the continuum argument.
