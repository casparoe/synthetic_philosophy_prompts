Write the joint statement that a defender of the Austrian economic-calculation argument (in the tradition of Mises and Hayek) and a defender of the Lange-Lerner competitive-socialist solution could both sign after a sustained adversarial collaboration. Restrict the collaboration to the specific conceptual problem of whether dispersed, tacit local knowledge about production conditions, resource availability, and consumer preferences can be adequately incorporated by a central planning board through iterative trial-and-error price adjustments, or whether such knowledge remains intractably distributed in a way that defeats convergence.

Structure the document exactly as follows:

- Section I: A numbered list of agreed claims. These should cover the informational function of market prices, the formal structure of the Lange-Lerner iterative mechanism, the existence of geographically and temporally dispersed local knowledge, and the conditions under which planning boards could in principle approximate market outcomes. Each claim should be formulated in neutral language that neither side rejects as a mischaracterization.

- Section II: A disagreements table. For each residual disagreement, provide two parallel formulations—one expressing the Austrian position in wording its own defenders would accept, the other expressing the Lange-Lerner position in wording its defenders would accept—followed by a brief identification of whether the disagreement is substantive or merely verbal.

- Section III: The cruxes. Identify the specific sub-questions—such as whether tacit local knowledge can be codified and transmitted upward without loss, and whether iterative price convergence is rapid enough relative to economic change and supply shocks—such that resolving them in one direction would cause at least one party to revise its overall view.

- Section IV: For each crux, specify one concrete institutional case, sectoral planning experience, or analytical scenario that both sides agree is genuinely relevant (for example, the behavior of algorithmic price-adjustment in modern distributed logistics networks, or the allocation dynamics in a historically documented planned sector). Then record, in advance of any empirical resolution, the explicit prediction each side makes about what that case demonstrates.

The statement should avoid bibliographies, citations, reading lists, and appeals to biographical authority. Frame the disagreement conceptually rather than as a historical dispute about personalities, and do not ask the parties to defend positions they do not actually hold.
