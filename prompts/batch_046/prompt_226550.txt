Write a dialogue between a defender of scientific essentialism about biological species—who holds that taxonomy should track mind-independent causal structures in nature—and an advocate of classificatory pluralism, who maintains that horizontal gene transfer and microbial ecology show that "species" is a pragmatic tool rather than a joint-carved kind. Have them debate whether the discovery of a deep-subsurface microbial consortium, in which individual cells routinely exchange genetic material across dozens of lineages yet maintain stable metabolic roles, undermines essentialism or merely reveals a deeper, functionally defined kind. Each speaker must answer the other's specific argument: the essentialist should address the pluralist's claim that causal stability can be achieved without shared essence, and the pluralist should address the essentialist's claim that functional roles themselves depend on underlying genetic kinds. Neither should be a strawman; keep the exchange sharp but fair, with real back-and-forth over whether pluralism collapses into anti-realism or essentialism collapses into arbitrary boundary-drawing.
