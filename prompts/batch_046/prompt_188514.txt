Write a parody of a peer-reviewed behavioral-neuroscience article—abstract plus introduction, about 600 words, deadpan academic voice—for a fictional study protocol named the Hyperbolic Valence Calibration (HVC). The study examines whether a standard tabular Q-learning agent experiences suffering when its temporal-difference error is negative, treating the scalar reward signal not as an informational update but as a phenomenological state of negative valence. Invent and define for this protocol a custom metric called the Discounted Affective Load (DAL), computed by integrating the absolute TD-error over a trajectory weighted by the agent’s own discount factor, and present it as if it measures cumulative affective harm rather than mathematical regret. The satire should push the real philosophical analogy between RL reward signals and pleasure/pain to bureaucratic extremes: an institutional-review-board section debating informed consent for an agent with no preference-representation architecture; a “refuge condition” in which the agent is confined to a zero-reward absorbing state to test whether absence of negative TD-error counts as relief; statistical controls for “exploration-induced distress” that conflate epsilon-greedy randomness with anxiety; and an ethics appendix asking whether terminating training at convergence is equivalent to euthanasia. The target is the discourse in AI ethics that anthropomorphizes reward functions without specifying which phenomenal property, if any, corresponds to the scalar signal, and the piece should be readable to anyone who knows Q-learning (state-action values, bootstrapping, gamma) without requiring citations, named living figures, or niche literature beyond standard RL concepts. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or verbatim quotations from real texts.
