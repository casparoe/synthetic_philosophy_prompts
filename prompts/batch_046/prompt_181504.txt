For my graduate aesthetics seminar, I want a poem to close our unit on Tolstoy's theory of art. Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains alternating in the prescribed pattern and finally meeting—spoken in the severe, prophetic voice of Tolstoy's religious-ascetic period. The first refrain should state, in his own conceptual terms, that genuine art requires the artist's sincere feeling: not technique, not manufactured effect, but an actual emotional infection that passes from maker to viewer. The second refrain should state his further condition that true art must communicate feelings of universal human brotherhood, and that art serving sensual pleasure, class distinction, or the exclusivity of refined taste is necessarily counterfeit. Apply this framework not to painting or music, which he discusses at length, but to the silent curation of pressed botanical specimens in a public herbarium—a practice he never addressed. The tercets should show how such arrangement might become true art, infecting viewers with shared wonder at the diversity of living forms, or counterfeit art, displaying the collector's status through beauty and rarity arranged for admiration rather than communion. The villanelle's form should do philosophical work: each return of the refrains should shift their relation in context, so that by the final couplet—where they must stand side by side—the reader sees that sincerity without brotherhood collapses into self-indulgence, and brotherhood without sincerity collapses into propaganda. Do not resolve the tension with a moral; let the form's compulsory reunion of the lines suggest that both conditions are structurally necessary for art to be genuine. The poem should be precise enough that someone familiar with Tolstoy's late essay recognizes the specific criteria—infection, sincerity, universal brotherhood, and the rejection of beauty pursued for pleasure—rather than generic moralism, and it should still stand as a coherent, severe poem for a reader who has never encountered him.
