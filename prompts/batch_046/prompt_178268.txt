After a long argument with a friend over whether AI-generated forensic reconstructions can participate in the language game of legal testimony, I want to clarify Wittgenstein's idea of family resemblance. My friend argued that family resemblance works like a genealogical tree: there is a central prototype—direct human eyewitness account—from which derivative forms branch out by retaining at least one core feature, in this case representational accuracy about a crime scene. I countered that Wittgenstein meant there is no hidden essence binding all members, only a network of overlapping similarities. Yet I now wonder whether I have misapplied the concept: does family resemblance actually require a root prototype that anchors the family, and if so, does that mean synthetic reconstructions are excluded unless they trace back to human perception? Furthermore, if language games are constituted by shared rules of verification rather than by descriptive similarities among members, is it a category mistake to use family resemblance to settle whether an artificial output belongs to the institutional practice of testimony?
