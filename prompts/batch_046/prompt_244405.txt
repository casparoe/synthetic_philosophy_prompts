As a doctoral student in philosophy of mind and applied ethics, I am formalizing the structure of a long-running dispute for my dissertation research on consciousness and moral status. I need you to draft the joint statement that two opposing camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The camps are: (1) continuous-gradationists, who hold that phenomenal sentience and corresponding moral status vary smoothly across biological and artificial systems—from mammals through insects to simple feedback devices like thermostats—without any sharp ontological boundary; and (2) threshold-defenders, who hold that phenomenal consciousness is binary, arising only when an entity crosses a specific architectural or informational threshold (for example, a critical level of integrated information or the presence of a functional global workspace), with entities below that line possessing at most derivative or zero direct moral status.

Please write the response in American English and organize it as follows. First, provide a numbered section of agreed claims that both sides can endorse: shared empirical observations (e.g., that insects display nociceptive behaviors, that artificial systems can implement homeostatic feedback loops, that current neuroscience lacks a unanimously accepted neural signature of consciousness); shared formal constraints (e.g., that any adequate theory must account for both the presence of reported experience in neurologically intact humans and its apparent absence in simple control systems); and shared methodological commitments (e.g., that neither camp is defending Cartesian dualism, and that empirical findings about information integration should be weighed regardless of substrate). Second, present a disagreements table that separates genuine disputes from merely verbal ones: for each camp, state its precise position in wording that its own proponents would accept, clarifying whether the disagreement concerns the nature of phenomenal consciousness itself, the derivation of moral status, or both. Be explicit about whether the phrase "degrees of sentience" is being used descriptively or normatively.

Third, identify the cruxes—the sub-questions whose resolution would actually move at least one side. I take the central cruxes to be: (a) the micro-subject aggregation problem, specifically whether a thermostat or other sub-threshold system composed of billions of parts each carrying an infinitesimal slice of phenomenal experience could aggregate to a non-negligible collective moral claim; (b) whether there exists a principled, non-arbitrary cutoff in the space of information integration, predictive processing, or global availability that marks the onset of phenomenal experience rather than merely marking increased behavioral complexity; and (c) whether moral risk asymmetry—the intuition that falsely attributing sentience is worse or differently weighted than falsely denying it—supports a precautionary continuous scale or instead supports maintaining a binary threshold with precaution applied only near the boundary.

Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete case, argument, or empirical scenario that both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's explicit prediction in advance. For crux (b), both sides might agree that an artificial neural network with graded global-workspace dynamics—operating at calibrated levels corresponding to roughly 40%, 60%, and 80% of the proposed threshold parameters—would be relevant; state what the gradationist predicts about moral status at each level versus what the threshold-defender predicts. For crux (a), both sides might agree that a policy choice between mass decommissioning of climate-control systems and protection of borderline-cognitive insects is relevant; record each side's normative prescription and its reasoning. For crux (c), specify a decision-theoretic framing both accept and record how each side applies the risk calculus.

Please ensure the document is constructive and precise: no side should be treated as a strawman, verbal disputes should be flagged as such, and you should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts, since I am evaluating the conceptual architecture without web access.
