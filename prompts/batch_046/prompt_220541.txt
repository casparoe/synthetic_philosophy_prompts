i work for a small land-claims tribunal that reviews translated witness testimony in languages with no written standard and barely any field grammar. every week i get two certified translations of the same statement that diverge on key terms — one says a plot is continuous, another says it's interrupted by shared-use rights — and i have about 20 minutes to decide whether the divergence is just Quine-style indeterminacy (two translation manuals fitting all the behavioral evidence but assigning different ontologies, like the gavagai case) or a real conflict that should block admission. i need a reusable decision procedue i can run with only the two texts, a one-page glossary, and a quick call to a local speaker who isn't a linguist. lay out the ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, the exact point where i stop with a reject-both, the point where i hand it to the judges because it's values not facts, and be honest about where the procedure will misfire — especially when the glossary was compiled by a missionary in 1950. then run it on three cases: (A) "the river divides our land" vs "the river is our shared boundary" — should come out as indeterminacy; (B) "we planted there every season" vs "we planted there only when the water was low" — should come out as reject both, even though the second sounds like a precise version of the first and i want to keep it; (C) a term that could mean "the elder gave permission" vs "the elder was present" where the speaker can't resolve it. don't ask me for citations or a reading list — i don't have library access in the field office.
