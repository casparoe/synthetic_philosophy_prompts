Write a joke grounded in John Dewey's experimentalist philosophy of democracy and inquiry. The joke should turn on the conceptual tension between Dewey's view of democracy as a mode of cooperative social inquiry—where collective problem-solving continuously reconstructs habits and ends—rather than as a static system of majority rule, and his epistemological requirement that a claim counts as knowledge only when it achieves warranted assertibility through publicly shared, fallible investigation. Invent a specific, somewhat absurd institutional scenario—such as a municipal government that treats every ordinance as a falsifiable hypothesis and assigns citizens to experimental and control conditions—to expose an internal contradiction: the control group, being deliberately kept ignorant of the intervention, cannot participate in the shared inquiry that Dewey holds necessary for democratic knowledge, yet their outcomes are treated as data that warrant the policy. The punchline should depend on this philosophical structure rather than on generic wordplay or technology gags; someone familiar with Dewey should recognize that the absurdity comes from applying his own criteria for inquiry to the democratic process itself.
