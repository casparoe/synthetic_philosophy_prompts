You know the argument — unrealistic assumptions are fine as long as the model predicts well. The other half says that's precisely the danger — false assumptions mean you're not explaining anything, just fitting curves, and once you leave the data range everything collapses. We've been at it for months using the same examples (ideal gas law, Lotka-Volterra, some recent macro models) and nobody's budged.

My working theory is that this isn't really about realism but about what explanantion has to do — specifically whether it requires counterfactual robustness outside the observed range. Or maybe there's a hidden empirical question about how often false-assumption models actually fail when extrapolated.
