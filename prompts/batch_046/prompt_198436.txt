Write a poem treating the surprise exam paradox in exactly five quatrain stanzas, with no lines before or after them. Each stanza must close with the same refrain line, but its logical role should shift: in stanza one it announces the exam condition; in stanza two it expresses the students' derived belief; in stanza three it acts as a self-undermining prediction about Friday; in stanza four it marks the revoked premise that collapses the backward induction; in stanza five it becomes an empty performative with nothing left to declare. The poem should trace the standard induction—if the exam were Friday then by Thursday it would be expected, hence not Friday, and so recursively back through the week—but reconstrue "surprise" not as subjective shock but as an objective epistemic state that is annihilated the moment it is known. Render the reasoning precisely enough that a philosopher of logic recognizes each step, and let the refrain's deteriorating status do the structural work of the paradox's self-defeat.
