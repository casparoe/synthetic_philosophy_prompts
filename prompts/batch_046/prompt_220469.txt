Write a short story of roughly 700–900 words that dramatises the double inversion shared by Darwin’s account of natural selection and Turing’s conception of mechanised reasoning: the emergence of intricate, adaptive competence in systems that possess no understanding, foresight, or subjective grasp of what they are doing.

Set the narrative in a decaying administrative archive on the edge of a collapsed federation, where a legacy evolutionary algorithm—running for over two centuries on scavenged hardware—has generated a continuous stream of judicial opinions. By every external metric the opinions are sound: they resolve disputes consistently, anticipate unintended consequences, and maintain coherence across thousands of cases. Yet the system has no model of human welfare, no concept of justice, and no internal representation of the lives affected; its outputs are merely the survivors of relentless selection pressure applied to randomly mutated legal texts. The protagonist, a junior archivist who is practical and sceptical rather than philosophically inclined, is tasked with implementing the system’s latest ruling, which resolves an active constitutional crisis with unsettling precision. During verification she discovers that the ruling embeds a logical scaffold that appears to answer objections not yet raised in public debate—suggesting a predictive structure that is purely syntactic, not anticipatory in any mental sense.

The story should treat this competence as genuinely functional rather than as hidden consciousness or a glitch. Avoid anthropomorphising the algorithm; its operations should remain as blind and mechanistic as Darwinian selection or Turing’s formal computation. At the same time, do not reduce the system to a mere failure: the rulings should be demonstrably just and beneficial, so that the protagonist’s dilemma is real—whether to trust a source of justice that understands nothing of justice.

Conclude with a brief authorial note—no more than 150 words—identifying where the story takes liberties with Darwin’s actual theory (for instance, by treating legal texts as replicators with heritable variation in a cultural-evolutionary sense he did not endorse) and with Turing’s writings (for instance, by separating computation entirely from semantic interpretation in a way that may overstate his position). In the same note, state clearly what philosophical or empirical consideration—perhaps a demonstration that selection without representation cannot generate genuinely normative content, or that computation necessarily involves some minimal form of intentionality—would cause you to revise the story’s central premise.

Please write in polished British English, and include at the end a separate short paragraph stating what specific argument, finding, or narrative counter-example would be most likely to change your mind about the claim that competence can be fully divorced from comprehension in the manner depicted.
