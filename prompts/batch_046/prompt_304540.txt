Write an essay assessing the following interpretive claim: Kierkegaard's critique of the press and of the public is not best read as a blanket rejection of democratic participation, but rather as an ontological diagnosis—specifically, that quantitative aggregation dissolves the qualitative conditions required for ethical commitment and for inwardness. Evaluate whether this reading successfully distinguishes Kierkegaard's position from mere elitism, or whether the distinction collapses upon inspection. Your essay must reach and justify a definite conclusion: either that his framework can accommodate a form of collective political agency that avoids the anonymity he condemns, or that any collective agency necessarily betrays the individual relation to the ethical. In weighing the arguments, address at least two specific objections—first, that democratic deliberation can be structured through face-to-face accountability or limited-scale assemblies to prevent the leveling he describes; second, that his sharp separation between the crowd and the individual relies on an atomistic conception of the self that his own pseudonymous authorship and dialectical method implicitly reject. Do not conclude with an unresolved balance; defend a specific verdict, and explain what practical or theoretical consequence follows from it for contemporary debates about mass media and democratic legitimacy.
