ok so i'm trying to work out where i actually stand on this hedonistic imperative thing, like using bioteh to wipe out suffering everywhere. here are my gut calls on specific scenarios and i want to know if there's a consistent principle behind them or if i'm just being incoherent.

first: editing human embryos so they can't feel physical pain at all. that feels like a clear win, do it.

second: editing wild predators so they don't suffer when they miss prey, but leaving prey animals fully able to suffer when caught. feels wrong, like you're only fixing half the equation and ignoring the asymmetry.

third: a drug that gives constant mild euphoria but wipes out your capacity for deep aesthetic experiences or existential dread. i'm weirdly okay with consenting adults taking it, but not with forcing it on everyone.

fourth: gene drives to make mosquitoes not need blood, so they stop biting people and suffering gets reduced that way. good in theory, but i feel more uneasy about it than the embryo case even though it's the same logic, maybe because it's ecological?

fifth: an implant that lets you switch off empathy for strangers but keeps it for family. definately not okay, even though it reduces your own suffering.

sixth: we abolish all suffering but accidentally wipe out moral agency too, because moral growth needs struggle. that makes me want to pump the brakes, but only if the loss is permanent and total.

is there one principle that captures all this? maybe "reduce suffering only when it doesn't destroy capacities that depend on it"? but then case three is weird because aesthetic depth might depend on suffering, yet i was okay with it. and case four feels different for no clear reason. what's the actual pattern here, and what does it predict for the case i keep flip-flopping on: should we gene-edit future generations to be permanently content even if they never consented?
