I am preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher-historian who defends a specific interpretation of Johannes Kepler’s astrological writings: she treats them not as an embarrassing relic of pre-scientific thinking, but as an integral “correspondence cosmology” in which celestial configurations operate as non-causal symbolic expressions of divine geometric harmony. On this view, Kepler distinguishes sharply between “celestial signification”—the heavens functioning as a readable text of universal proportion—and “celestial causation”—the direct mechanical influence of planets on sublunary matter that his own physics rejects. The claim is that Kepler retained astrological practice precisely because he believed harmonic ratios in the sky expressed normative patterns that human affairs could align with or violate, without those patterns physically forcing individual outcomes. I want the interview to stay in the conceptual weeds and avoid biographical filler.

Please respond in the following sections, in this order:

1. Conceptual Framing (~120 words): Briefly restate the correspondence-cosmology interpretation, identify the central tension it must resolve—how celestial patterns can be genuinely meaningful without being causally efficacious, and why this is not reducible to aesthetic metaphor—and specify what is at stake for Kepler’s broader project of unifying astronomy, physics, and theology.

2. Core Questions (~500 words): Provide seven interview questions. Each should press a specific logical or historical point in the view. Do not include generic questions about her career or how she came to study Kepler. At least one question must address why Kepler retained “mundane” astrological forecasting about political and meteorological events while rejecting natal horoscopes for individuals; at least one must address whether the framework requires a particular metaphysics of symbols rather than a merely heuristic reading practice. For at least two questions, include a paired follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—for instance, if she appeals to Kepler’s harmonic theology, ask how harmonic ratios avoid collapsing into subjective aesthetic projection; if she insists on the signification/causation distinction, ask what criteria distinguish a genuine celestial sign from an arbitrarily imposed pattern.

3. Pressure Points and Closing (~250 words): Identify the two deflections she is most likely to deploy—such as “Kepler was only writing astrology to earn patronage” or “this distinction is anachronistic category confusion”—and for each provide the precise follow-up that makes the deflection harder to sustain. End with one final question asking what kind of empirical discovery in astronomy or physics could, in principle, undermine the correspondence-cosmology interpretation itself rather than merely rendering it unfashionable.
