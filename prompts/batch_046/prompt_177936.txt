ok so here's my philosophical profile for a social epistemology reading group — i'm a polisci phd, so forgive me if i butcher teh jargon. want a consistency check before i present.

i'm holding:
(1) echo chambers and epistemic bubbles are different beasts: chambers involve active distrust of outsiders and identity-protective reasoning, bubbles are just missing info because of filtering and network structure.
(2) the conditions for correctly ascribing either state should be universal — same criteria apply to individuals, groups, and network nodes, no special group-level exception. so ascription universality holds.
(3) escaping either requires epistemic dominance: some existant external perspective has to dominate your belief-forming alternatives, not just feed you new data.
(4) but epistemic dominance is always framework-relative; there's no view-from-nowhere that dominates across every context.
(5) people inside bubbles are rational — they update correctly on teh evidence they recieve — yet rationality also requires being responsive to disagreement, which they structurally can't be.
(6) bubbles are rationally unavoidable given bounded cognition, but agents still have an epistemic duty to actively seek out disagreeing voices, even when it's costly.

go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. for each pair in tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess. don't flag everything as equally bad — at least one of these tensions should dissolve on inspection. i genuinely don't know which commitment has to go, so don't treat any as obviously the loser.
