I'm giving the eulogy for my grandfather this saturday — he ran the local paper for forty years and taught occasional philosophy of mind classes at the community college, so about half the room will catch the Dennett reference to a self as a center of narrative gravity and the other half are family and neigbors who just knew him as the guy who fixed every typo in the obituaries. He spent the last three years with dementia, not recognizing us, but the town still treated him like he was the editor right up until the end, and I just finished sorting his papers and realized his identity wasn't just something he generated from inside — it was more like this borrowed orbit, a kind of gravitational borrowing, kept going by everyone else telling the story. That's been weighing on me since I found his old notes. I need a five-minute eulogy that uses that idea structurally, not as decoration: because his inner narrative stopped, I can't pretend to report his private experience near the end, so the speech has to be built entirely out of the external stories — the paper, the neighbors, the family — and it should show that that's not a failure but actually how he existed all along. Include the part where I have to admit I don't know if I'm describing him or just describing the story we all agreed on, since that's exactly where the idea breaks. Make it land for both audiences without turning into a lecture, and definatly don't include citations, a bibliography, or a reading list — I have no way to check them and neither does anyone else without searching. Please begin with the eulogy text itself, no preface or commentary, and don't restate the prompt at the top.
