A classmate summarized the recent literature on standing to blame and hypocrisy—drawing mainly on R. Jay Wallace but also on newer extensions—like this: the core claim is that hypocrisy undermines standing not through mere inconsistency but through a failure of reciprocal moral recognition; specifically, the hypocrite applies norms to others that she exempts herself from, which violates the equality required for legitimate moral address. From this, the summary draws two further claims. First, that the loss of standing is relational rather than global, meaning a hypocrite might retain authority to blame distant strangers or institutions but automatically forfeits it in relationships of moral equality such as parent-to-child or fellow-citizen. Second, that the same logic extends to structural hypocrisy, so that citizens who benefit from carbon-intensive economies lack standing to blame other nations for climate inaction even if they do not personally emit excessively, and that this standing can only be regained by exiting the benefiting structure or achieving genuine reform. The summary then applies this to a case: a parent who lies to protect her own reputation but blames her teenager for lying has completely lost all standing, whereas she might still blame a foreign government for deception. Could you audit this characterization? I need to know which parts capture the actual philosophical view, which parts are simplifications or subtle distortions—particularly any conflation between lacking standing and merely being ignored, between interpersonal and structural hypocrisy, or between partial and total loss of authority—and whether the conclusion about the parent follows once the view is corrected. Please indicate how confident you are in each part of your assessment, and explicitly identify the point about which you are least certain.
