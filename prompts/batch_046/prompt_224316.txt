Hey, I'm drafting a short essay asking whether "Western values" is even a coherent category, and I'm trying to avoid the usual hypocrisy take. My argument is that it functions less like a shared belief-set and more like what I'm calling a retrospective coalition — values get grouped together not because they share origin or logic, but because they've all been used at some point to draw a boundary against some non-Western other. So secularism is in when opposing theocracy, free speech is in when opposing censorship, but the same speakers drop either one the moment it conflicts with cohesion. The set is closed under exclusion, not inclusion.

Then there's this second pattern I'm calling axiological tourism: people invoke the values for rhetorical effect without actually living in them. Like citing openness and tolerance to justify immigration restrictions — it's not plain hypocrisy, it's more like borrowing cohearence from an imagined creditor without putting up collateral. Does that distinction hold? I'm worried I'm just relabeling hypocrisy, and that my retrospective coalition idea misses an actual philosophical core (maybe liberal individualism or something). Could you comment on whether the framing is accurate, whether the exclusion-closure distinction is clear, and whether the tourism metaphor is useful or just too cute? I don't want to just rehearse the standard cultural-relativism line.
