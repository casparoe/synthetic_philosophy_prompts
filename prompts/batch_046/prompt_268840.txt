Analyze the 1919 Eddington solar-eclipse expedition—specifically the stellar-deflection measurements obtained at Príncipe and at Sobral, the comparison of observed values against Einstein's prediction of approximately 1.75 arcseconds and the Newtonian expectation of roughly 0.87 arcseconds, and the well-known decision to set aside some of the Sobral photographic plates—through the lens of the underdetermination of scientific theory by observational evidence.

Proceed in two directions. First, apply the theory to the episode with disciplinary precision: identify the core theoretical commitments (Einsteinian general relativity versus Newtonian gravitation), the auxiliary hypotheses involving telescope calibration, atmospheric conditions, and photographic-plate interpretation, and show exactly how a Newtonian defender could have preserved the core theory by adjusting those auxiliaries to accommodate the Príncipe results. Specify the shared evidence set available to the scientific community in 1919, and indicate whether the episode illustrates global, holistic underdetermination—in which every element of the theoretical web is open to revision—or a more localized underdetermination confined to gravitational theory with optics and meteorology held constant.

Second, use the episode to test the theory. Address whether the rational acceptance of Einstein was justified solely on the basis of the 1919 data, or whether the choice was already constrained by independent prior evidence—most notably Mercury's anomalous perihelion precession—such that the eclipse measurements resolved only a transient, local underdetermination rather than a permanent theoretical ambiguity. Conclude by assessing what the case demonstrates about the practical severity of underdetermination: is it a live obstacle to rational theory choice, or does it collapse once we permit disciplined auxiliary adjustments across different domains of physics?

Throughout, avoid generic historical narration. Focus on the logical structure of the evidential relation, and be explicit about where the underdetermination thesis is silent—particularly regarding the normative question of how much auxiliary revision is permissible before a theoretical defense becomes ad hoc.
