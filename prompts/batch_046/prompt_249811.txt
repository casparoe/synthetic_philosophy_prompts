I serve on our university's international research ethics board, and we must decide by Friday whether to approve a partnership with a global-health NGO whose contract includes a "moral reciprocity clause." Under the clause, if the NGO fails to meet informed-consent benchmarks in one region, it may "offset" that deficit by funding equivalent extra consent audits in another region rather than suspending data collection there. Having recently completed a cross-border adoption, I have become convinced that obligations to particular vulnerable individuals may not be interchangeable, yet I want the board's recommendation to be fair to the NGO's claim that fungibility maximizes overall protection within fixed budgets. Draft our decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—treating the clause as a case of moral trade, in which ethical compliance is treated as exchangeable across contexts. The memo should present the strongest argument for allowing the offset (aggregate welfare and budget efficiency) and the strongest argument against it (non-fungibility of duties owed to specific subjects), then conclude with an actionable vote recommendation and any necessary conditions.
