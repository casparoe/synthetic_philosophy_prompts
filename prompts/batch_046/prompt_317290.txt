If Stanley and Williamson are correct that knowing how is just a species of knowing that—namely, knowing that w is a way for one to F—then hasn't Ryle's intellectualist legend been vindicated after all? Ryle's regress seemed to show that if intelligent action always required prior theoretical knowledge, then every skillful move would have to be preceded by an explicit mental premise, launching an infinite chain. But if knowing how genuinely is propositional, then exercising a skill appears to require accessing the relevant proposition, which sounds like just such a prior mental act. So does accepting the propositional account of knowing-how commit us to the view that experts silently rehearse premises before each action, thereby falling back into Ryle's regress? Or is there a distinction between possessing a propositional attitude and actively reasoning from it that saves the propositional view without rescuing the intellectualist legend? If the latter, what exactly does that distinction cost us—does it weaken the claim that knowing-how is genuinely knowing-that, or does it force us to concede that much skilled behavior operates without any propositional access at all? Which horn is least damaging, and where does the real error in this line of reasoning lie?
