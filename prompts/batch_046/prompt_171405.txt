I've been working through some of the older LessWrong-style arguments about evidential cooperation in large worlds, and a colleague summarized the current state of play for me as follows: "In evidential decision theory, if you inhabit a universe large enough to contain near-identical copies of your decision procedure, then in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma your choice provides direct evidence about what those copies will choose. Because the correlation across copies is perfect, selecting cooperation raises your subjective credence that the other agents cooperate, which can make cooperation rational despite the fact that defection causally dominates in any local interaction. Consequently, causal decision theory delivers the wrong recommendation in large worlds, because the evidential link replicates the strategic structure of repeated interaction without requiring any causal pathway between the agents. The argument only succeeds under a universality constraint: the copies must share not just similar reasoning but literally the same ascribed decision token, and without that ascription universality the evidential update is too weak to overcome defection's local advantage."

Something in this framing strikes me as subtly off—particularly the move from correlation to perfect correlation, the claim that causal decision theory is shown wrong rather than outperformed on its own terms, and the introduction of "ascription universality" as a necessary condition. But I'm not confident enough to locate the errors precisely. Could you audit this summary with care? Please go through the substantive claims in turn and distinguish, for each: what accurately reflects the standard evidential-cooperation argument, what is a defensible simplification that a careful theorist might accept with qualification, and what is a misrepresentation or an unsupported addition—especially regarding the strength of correlation required, the relationship between evidential and causal reasoning, the status of causal decision theory in this debate, and whether "ascription universality" is actually part of the argument or an extraneous philosophical import. I want to know whether the conclusion—that cooperation becomes rational in these settings—still stands once these points are corrected, or whether the correction undermines it.
