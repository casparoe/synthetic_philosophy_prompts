Design a graduate-level exam essay question on the Frege–Geach problem for expressivism that cannot be satisfied by summarizing Geach’s canonical argument against moral non-cognitivism. Introduce, within the prompt itself, a hypothetical variant called gradient expressivism: the view that an utterance of “X is obligatory” does not express a binary attitude but rather maps X onto a continuous normative gradient relative to the speaker’s practical commitments, with logical form allegedly given by a comparative relation rather than a propositional one. The exam question should ask students to determine whether and how the embedding problem—specifically in conditionals and disjunctions—arises for this gradient formulation, and to evaluate whether recasting the logical form as comparative genuinely avoids the problem or merely relocates Frege’s original concern about sense in embedded contexts. Provide a model answer of approximately 600 words, a marking scheme out of 20 points that separates an A answer (which pinpoints the exact failure of the comparative reformulation in embedded contexts and relates it to Frege’s point about sense) from a B answer (which accurately reconstructs the standard problem but applies it imprecisely to the gradient variant), and for each scoring tier list the specific argumentative moves required and the common mistakes that cost marks. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; frame all requirements so that a well-prepared student can answer from conceptual analysis alone.
