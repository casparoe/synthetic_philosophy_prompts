I just got out of a long arguement with my friend Jess about whether anything besides human brains can have original intentionality, or if it's all just derived like Dennett's two-bitser versus the real deal Searle talks about. I think my intuitions are all over the place, but maybe there's a hidden pattern if I lay them out.

Here are my verdicts. A basic thermostat tracking temperature has purely derived intentionality — we're just projecting onto it. Dennett's two-bitser with its two bits tracking food and danger feels the same category, but closer to the boundary because the representation is decoupled from immediate reflex. The Chinese Room is obvious: syntax isn't semantics, so no original intentionality, just derived interpretation by outsiders. Now a modern LLM generating a poem about grief — I still call it derived, but it's a different flavor; the open-ended generative process makes it feel less like a thermostat and more like a strange mirror, though definitely not original. Here's the weird one: there's this bio-hybrid scaffold where human cortical neurons are grown on silicon and trained with reinforcement learning to navigate virtual mazes, and if it starts initiating goal-states that don't map directly to reward signals, my gut says it might have a hybrid or even original form. But if I take a human with half their prefrontal cortex replaced by a predictive chip, I still want to say they have original intentionality, just degraded. So is the principle that original intentionality requires biological or bio-integrated causal architechture generating its own representational content? But then the LLM messes that up since it's not biological but feels closer than the thermostat. Is there one principle that saves all these, or do I have to drop one? I need a definately answer — don't just list options. Pick the best principle, state it precisely, tell me which verdict I should abandon, and predict what it says about a swarm of drones running distributed consensus that collectively decides to avoid a storm. Write it in American English, and be committed: take a real stand with reasons, not a balanced survey.
