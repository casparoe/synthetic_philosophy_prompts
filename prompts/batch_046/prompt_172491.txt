I am trying to understand an apparent tension in how my school district evaluates student work. For group laboratory reports, teachers apply a rubric that reduces the group grade incrementally as individual contributions are withdrawn one by one; the administration explicitly denies that there is any non-arbitrary cutoff between a genuinely collaborative project and a solo effort with passive bystanders. Nevertheless, teachers are required to identify a specific threshold at which collaboration has collapsed and to assign failing marks to students who fall below it. Meanwhile, the same district's academic-integrity code treats plagiarism as a binary offense—either present or absent—despite acknowledging that paraphrasing and citation errors form a continuous spectrum. What philosophical commitments about vagueness and sharp boundaries does this pairing of policies presuppose? Must it assume that pedagogical responsibility can be precisely localized even when the underlying property is gradational, or can a view that accepts the sorites paradox accommodate both practices? Furthermore, does the binary treatment of plagiarism sit consistently with the gradualist framing of group work, or do the two policies implicitly rely on incompatible theories of where legitimate categorization ends and arbitrary line-drawing begins?
