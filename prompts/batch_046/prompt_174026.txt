The uniqueness camp says comparison is moral betrayal; the comparibilty camp says uniqueness is exceptionalism that blocks genocide studies generally. Both sides cite the same archives and neither have budged in decades. Is this actually a hidden disagreement about whether modernity is one continuous administrative structure or a specific 20th-century configuration — so they're not even arguing about the same object? Or is it bedrock moral intuition about victimhood dressed up as historical method? What's your diagnosis, and what would actually move it: a formal model of bureaucratic violence, or should we just admit the framing is ill-posed?
