Here are my gut calls on privacy violations and I'm trying to figure out if there's one principel behind them or if I'm just being inconsistent. A smart thermostat learns your sleep schedule and sells a "night owl" score to advertisers without ever revealing your exact bedtime: feels like a real breach, maybe worse than just seeing you come home late. Your roommate overhears you arguing with your partner through the wall and later asks if you're okay: not a breach at all, even though they gained private info. A researcher publishes anonymized hospital records with ages and zip codes removed, but someone re-identifcates a few patients using public data: breach, but mostly because of the re-identification risk, not the publication itself. A company makes workers wear badges that track how often they talk to colleagues but don't record words; they say it's for "collaboration metrics." That feels like a breach, even though no content is captured. Meanwhile, you post your running routes publicly and a data broker infers your income level from your neighborhood patterns: I don't feel violated, I feel like I should've expected that. So is privacy about controlling access to facts about you, or about controlling predictions others can make, or about the context in which info moves? Is there a principle that saves all these, and what does it say about the case I'm unsure on -- a therapist who remembers your trauma but never wrote it down, and mentions it years later in casual conversation?
