If Wittgenstein were still knocking about today and saw people treating chatbots like they're having a proper philosophical chat, do you reckon he'd call it a language game? He's all about family resemblence — meaning comes from use inside a form of life, not from matching some hidden essence. But now there's this weird thing I'm calling algorithmic shadow-games, where an AI rewrites its own rules based on feedback and two strangers end up speaking a grammar neither designed. There's no shared human practice behind it, just loops. Would he say that's a genuine language game, or just a clever imitation that fools us because of the surface family resemblance? And what about his private language argument — if a bot builds an internal symbol system no human can ever check, is that exactly the private language he thought was impossibe, or does the whole argument collaps because there's no inner life there to begin with? Not after a bibliography, just where you think he'd land on whether meaning can exist without a human form of life.
