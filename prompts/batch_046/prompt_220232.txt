Next Thursday I present to my department's philosophy of action colloquium a thesis I call "localized sphexishness." I argue that the classic sphex wasp case—where the wasp repeats its burrow-checking ritual mechanically even when the prey is moved—has been misused as a binary opposite to human agency. Rather than claiming the wasp is secretly flexible, I contend that human practical reasoning is shot through with its own domain-restricted rigidities: modular routines that run to completion without deliberative oversight, yet are embedded within broader flexible architectures. If this is right, then libertarian appeals to global, species-wide flexibility as the hallmark of free will collapse, and the compatibilist distinction between responsive and unresponsive mechanisms must be recast in task-specific rather than taxonomic terms.

The audience is precisely the kind that will not let this pass quietly: three senior compatibilists who treat Frankfurt-style hierarchical models as settled framework, a visiting comparative psychologist who knows the ethological literature on sphex behavior far better than I do, and one prominent libertarian who has recently defended agent-causal accounts. The compatibilists will care about whether my view undermines their own distinctions; the psychologist will care about whether I am misrepresenting current entomology; the libertarian will care about whether I have simply relocated rather than dissolved the problem of determinism. I need a prep sheet—not a defense, but an ordered battle plan—for the Q&A.

Please draft the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased in the vocabulary each interlocutor would actually use. Order them by how damaging they are to the talk's survival, not by how likely they are. For each objection, include: (1) a two-minute reply that concedes what must be conceded and holds what must be held; (2) an explicit note on where that reply is weakest or sounds like hand-waving; and (3) the name or role of the objector (e.g., "the senior compatibilist," "the ethologist"). Then identify the single objection I am hoping nobody raises—the one that exposes the deepest tension in my own position, specifically regarding whether "localized sphexishness" in humans is functionally indistinguishable from the very unresponsive mechanisms that compatibilists already classify as unfree. Finally, include a brief note on the weakest point in my overall argument that I should prepare to defend openly rather than hope they overlook it.
