I am preparing an independent study presentation for my high school philosophy course on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—the question of what, if anything, all pleasures share in common. Rather than asking for standard definitions, I want a sorting taxonomy structured as a decision tree that classifies answers by two criteria I developed for this project: whether the shared feature of pleasure must remain intact when we compare a bodily sensation, such as warmth, with a cognitive satisfaction, such as grasping a proof; and whether pleasure operates as one universal normative currency or as a cluster of locally valued states with no common measure. Each internal node should present one concrete comparison case—phrased so a reader can answer it without knowing philosophical labels—splitting the tree toward exactly five leaf positions. At each leaf, state whether the view is named in the literature or should be marked unnamed, and in one or two sentences note its most awkward commitment regarding cross-domain pleasure comparison. Please respond as a numbered list containing exactly five items, each item one or two sentences long and describing one leaf in root-to-branch order, with no introductory or concluding text outside the list.
