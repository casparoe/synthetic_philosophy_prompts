ok so i retired from materials science about five years ago (mostly superconductors and phonons, that kind of thing) and lately i've been reading philosophy of science for fun. i want to check if my takes actually hang together or if i'm being sloppy. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) i'm a realist about the entities my old field actually manipulates — not just the equations, but the quasiparticles, the phonons, the actual stuff. working scientists aren't just playing pretend with entities.

(2) but i also think the pesimistic meta-induction is basically right: look at phlogiston, caloric, the luminiferous ether — all empirically successful, all ontologicly dead. so i should be skeptical that our current ontology (quark fields, whatever) is correct.

(3) i think structural realism is the right fix — we keep the math and symmetries across revolutions, not the entities. basicly the idea that the structure survives even when the stuff doesn't.

(4) still, i think working realism is methodologically necessary: you can't do good experimental design if you treat every entity as a fiction. so scientists are rational to act like realists even if the ontology is likely wrong.

(5) i think the no-miracles argument only saves technology — if your equation predicts a material works at 77K and it does, that stops the miracle. but it doesn't prove the ontology behind the equation is true, just that the math is reliable for engineering.

(6) i think there's a real difference between theories that just fit old data (curve-fitting) and ones that make risky novel predictions — and that difference weakens the PMI for modern physics, because quantum field theory predicted things like the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron that weren't cooked into the data.

(7) at the same time i think induction from past scientific failure to present failure is shaky now, because modern physics is way more consilient — condensed matter, particle physics, cosmology all constrain each other in ways that 19th century chemistry never was.

so do these hang together? i feel like (1) and (2) pull in opposite directions but maybe (3) patches it, except then (4) looks like i'm sneaking entity realism back in through the back door. also (5) and (6) feel like they might clash — if predictive success doesn't buy ontology, why does risky prediction weaken PMI for ontology? and (7) seems to undercut (2) entirely, but maybe that's ok if PMI is restricted? please go through all the pairs, rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me, point out any pair that only looks tense but actually dissolves on inspection, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess. be specific about whether i'm helping myself to something in one commitment that another rules out.
