A colleague in my philosophy department offered the following summary of Moritz Schlick's account of life's meaning for an undergraduate lecture: "Schlick holds that life possesses no intrinsic teleological direction. Meaning is instead generated entirely through what he terms 'play'—activities pursued for their own sake rather than as instrumental means to survival or external goals. Because suffering and labor are necessarily non-playful, they are essentially devoid of meaning; ethical life therefore consists in minimizing suffering so that consciousness can enter sustained autotelic states. Schlick thus implicitly endorses something very close to negative utilitarianism: since suffering cannot be redeemed by meaning, the moral imperative is to eliminate it, and a life is meaningful precisely to the degree that suffering has been removed. Moreover, Schlick assumes a metaphysical premise that consciousness has value only when freed from evolutionary imperatives—reproduction, survival, competition—a premise modern evolutionary biology has shown to be false, so his ethics collapses."

How fair is this characterization? Please evaluate it claim by claim: identify which elements accurately reflect Schlick's actual argument in his essay on meaning, which conflate his view with later existentialist or suffering-focused ethical frameworks, and which invent premises or conclusions he never advanced. Specifically, address whether Schlick's notion of "play" is correctly described, whether he derives any normative ethics—particularly one resembling negative utilitarianism—from his account of meaning, and whether the alleged evolutionary refutation targets a commitment Schlick actually holds or instead misreads his distinction between descriptive and normative claims about human activity. If the inference from Schlick's accurate claims to the colleague's negative-utilitarian conclusion is valid, show the steps; if it fails, pinpoint exactly where the reasoning breaks and what Schlick's actual position on suffering and value would be.
