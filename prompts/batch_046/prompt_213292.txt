I've been working through the hole argument in general relativity on my own, without much formal training. As I understand it, one can apply a smooth coordinate transformation that is the identity everywhere except inside a small region—the hole—which produces two metric fields that agree outside the region but differ inside, yet both solve Einstein's field equations for the same matter distribution outside. This seems to suggest that the theory doesn't uniquely determine the geometry inside the hole from data outside it. My question is conceptual rather than mathematical: does this failure of determinism reveal that spacetime points lack intrinsic physical identity, or does it instead show that general covariance forces us to treat two apparently different point-assignments as describing the exact same physical world? And if the latter is true, why should we regard the transformation as generating a genuinely different mathematical description rather than merely redundant labeling of an unchanged reality?
