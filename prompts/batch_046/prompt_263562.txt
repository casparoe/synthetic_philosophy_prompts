Consider a decision framework for genuinely incommensurable values — call it the Threshold Protocol. Two values, A (cultural continuity embodied in the preservation of a specific, non-replicable landscape) and B (distributive justice embodied in housing security for a mobile population), conflict over a fixed resource. Representatives for each value specify a "threshold of intolerable loss": the minimal realisation of their value below which no compensating gain in the other value could justify the outcome. The protocol stipulates that if both thresholds can be satisfied, both should be; if only one can, that one takes precedence; but if satisfying either requires violating the other’s threshold, the decision is declared "rationally underdetermined" and must be settled by a non-rational procedure (for example, a weighted lottery or a democratic vote).

What is your credence — expressed as a number between 0 and 1 — that this protocol correctly captures the structure of hard choices involving incommensurable values, specifically that when both thresholds are crossed simultaneously there genuinely is no further reason-based resolution?

3. The probability that rational underdetermination genuinely follows from incommensurability in this case, as opposed to being resolvable by a meta-level comparison, a lexical ordering, or a procedural principle.
4. The probability that a non-rational settlement procedure (lottery, vote, or similar) is the appropriate response in such cases rather than an admission that practical reasoning has failed.
