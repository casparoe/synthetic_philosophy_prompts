(1) democracy is justified instrumentally: it tends to produce better outcomes than autocracy. (2) if instrumental justification is correct, then any system that produces better outcomes is more justified, regardless of equality. (3) an epistocratic system — say, weighting votes by demonstrated policy competence — produces better outcomes in every availble model. (4) therefore epistocracy is more justified than one-person-one-vote. (5) but if we really care about outcomes, we should weight votes not just by generic competence but by how much the voter is affected and how much local knowledge they have — a fisher in the maldives knows more about reef collapse than a climate scientist in oslo. (6) so the argument for epistocracy collapses into stakeholder-weighted direct democracy, which is neither democracy nor expert rule. (7) therefore instrumental justification of democracy proves too much and undermines itself. my gut says the flaw is in (4) — those "models" are speculative — but if (4) holds, is (5) wrong? does local knowledge count as political competence? and if i reject (1) and go for equality-based justification, do i have to accept that equality is worth preserving even when it makes everyone poorer? where's the actual crack, and what does fixing it cost?
