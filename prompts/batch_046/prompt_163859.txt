I am a novelist drafting a philosophical dialogue between a logician and a narrative artist, and I need a rigorous essay to ground their exchange. The subject is Frank Ramsey's contention that the question of life's meaning is not a profound mystery but a grammatical misfire—a confusion of logical form with empirical magnitude, somewhat like demanding the weight of a number. Compose an essay that treats this as a live controversy. First, articulate and defend Ramsey's dismissal: show how treating meaning as a predicate that can apply to the whole of existence may violate the rules of logical grammar, and why this makes the question literally nonsensical rather than merely unanswered. Second, present the opposing view drawn from narrative practice: argue that a novelist routinely constructs meaning not by discovering an inherent property of the world but by selecting scale, focalizing attention, and weaving contingency into pattern, so that to ask about the meaning of a particular life is no more conceptually confused than to ask about the theme of a well-formed novel. Weigh these positions with precision, then reach and defend a clear conclusion—is Ramsey's logical austerity more defensible than the novelist's constructive account, or does narrative practice reveal a legitimate sense of meaning that Ramsey's analysis misses? Write the response in American English, keep it formal, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations; the dialogue needs conceptual substance, not scholarly apparatus.
