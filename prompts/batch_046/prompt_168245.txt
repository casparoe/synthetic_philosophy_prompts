hey so i've been thinking about those living will papers people write, like if you're too messed up to talk for yourself. not the legal stuff, just my own gut reactions.

first case: my uncle signed a thing at 55 saying no feeding tube ever, he watched his dad suffer with dementia and hated it. then at 82 he's got dementia too, he's happy, he eats pudding with his hands, he doesn't know where he is but he smiles. i think we should feed him. the paper feels wrong now.

second case: same uncle, but now he's brain dead after a crash, not dementia, just totally gone. i think pull the plug exactly like he said. that feels right.

third case: my friend wrote at 40 that if she's on a breathing machine for more than a month, take her off. then at 65 she's on one for three weeks, she wakes up, she's scared but she says keep it going, i want to see my grandson born in two months. i think keep it going, ignore the month rule.

fourth case: a guy writes "if i'm paralyzed and can't talk, don't keep me alive" at 40. then at 60 he's locked in, fully awake, he writes books with eye blinks, he says life is still worth it. i think keep him alive. the paper was based on a wrong picture of what that life is like.

fifth case: a woman writes "if i ever stop recgonizing my own kids, let me go" at 50. then at 80 she has dementia, she doesn't recgonize anyone, but she sings and seems peaceful. here i keep going back and forth. part of me says she's literally the person she warned about, so honor the paper. part of me says she's happy like my uncle in case one, so why honor it? is the difference just that ending life feels more serious than a feeding tube?

i feel like there's a principle hiding here about whether the earlier self really knew what they were signing up for, like an "informed guess" rule. if you wrote the rule based on seeing someone suffer, maybe it's stronger? but then in case five she never had dementia either, so it's all guesses. or maybe it's about whether the later self can still hold values? my uncle in case one can't hold values but he's happy. the locked in guy can hold values and he changed his mind.

what principle captures cases one through four, and what does it say about case five? also what would it say about someone who writes "no chemo ever" then gets cancer and is terrified but the treatment might actually work? do i have to drop one of my gut calls to make it clean? please write in american english, continuous prose only, no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, like you're writing me an email. and don't ask me for book lists or citations.
