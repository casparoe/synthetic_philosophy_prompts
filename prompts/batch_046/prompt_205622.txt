Give an example of a conceptual issue in Bayesian epistemology involving old evidence and retrospective parameter calibration. Construct a scenario in which a dataset D has been common knowledge for decades before theory H is proposed, but H includes a free structural parameter that was fixed precisely to make H entail D. The issue should illuminate the tension between allowing D to confirm H through an explanatory bootstrapping route—where H accounts for other phenomena only if D holds—and the charge that calibrating the parameter after observing D makes P(D|H) artificially high, collapsing the distinction between prediction and accommodation. Make the logical structure explicit without naming specific authors or theorems, so that the conflict turns on the normative role of predictive risk in confirmation rather than on historical attribution.
