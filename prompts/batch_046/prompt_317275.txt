ok so im working on this novel about two people locked in an endless negotiation over who gets to be the "real" self and who has to stay the mirror. ive gotten kind of obsessed with hegel's master-slave dialectic and ive come to hold this pretty stark view: the dialectic doesnt actually resolve into mutual recognition, but reveals that recognition is structurally zero-sum. like, for one consciousness to be fully confirmed as subject, the other has to remain in partial objecthood — theres always a recognition debt that can never be settled, and reciprocity is mostly an ideological cover hiding that asymmetry. i think it applies not just to people but to institutions, languages, even aesthetic judgment.

what would actually count against this? i want real defeaters, sorted — not just "hegel says they eventually reconcile," but which formal results in social ontology, which phenomenological descriptions of ordinary interaction, and which edge cases (recognition between non-human agents, or across wild power gaps like parent/child) genuinely damage it. which ones would defenders concede are fatal versus just awkward? and has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability — if so, is that a problem for a claim thats supposed to be about the very structure of consciousness?

im also wondering whether results from other areas — say about collective intentionality or persistence — stand or fall with it. rank them for me, from "this kills the view dead" to "merely embarassing." be honest about whether its well-insulated, and whether that insulation is a virtue or just a cop-out.
