I'm putting together a feature on whether dreaming counts as real experience or just a morning-after story we tell ourselves, and after chatting with some neuro-scientists and philosophers over coffee I realized my gut verdicts on specific cases feel consistent but I can't tell if there's an actual principle stiching them together or if I'm just dressing up my intuitions. So here's where I land: when someone is jolted awake in REM and instantly describes flying through clouds, I think they genuinely experienced flight—not that they're inventing it on the spot. If they recall the same dream hours later with extra details, I think the original experience was the immediate report and the rest is reconstruction, like editing a photo. When a sleeper shows classic REM patterns but wakes with zero recall, I still think something was experienced and then forgotten, similar to forgetting a conversation you definitely had. But a lucid dreamer who signals with eye movements that they're seeing red is a slam dunk for experience. A sleepwalker who cooks breakfast with eyes open but remembers nothing feels like autopilot—no experience of cooking. There's also this weird study where scientists play a sound during slow-wave sleep and the sleeper reports a dream woven around it; I think they experienced the sound being incorporated, not that the whole narrative was fabricated after waking. But if someone reports the exact same dream every night for years with no REM signature at all, I think they're confabulating upon waking, not reliving an experience. I also think false awakenings—where you dream you've gotten up and brushed your teeth—are experiences too, but they're experiences of false belief, not of actually brushing teeth. And even though dreams seem to lack error-correction—if you think you're flying, you don't check—I don't think that missing error-correction disqualifies them; that feels like a separate issue, though maybe I'm wrong.

Is there a coherent principle that captures all of these? I'm especially stuck on whether I'm secretly relying on some niche idea about phenomenal binding that survives brief arousals, or if that's just dressing up my intuitions. Please tell me what principle best fits my verdicts, which single case I'd have to overturn to keep things simple, and what your account predicts for a case I haven't mentioned—say, someone who reports a vivid narrative immediately after being woken from deep non-REM sleep with no eye movement.
