I have been working through debates about Newton's rotating bucket and the relationist reply that rotation is defined relative to distant cosmic matter, and I keep encountering a specific dialectical pattern that I cannot find named. The relationist begins by grounding inertial effects in the distribution of distant bodies, in the spirit of Mach's principle. Then a critic constructs a scenario in which those distant bodies rotate uniformly around the bucket: on the relationist account, the bucket should then be non-rotating relative to them, yet the water still climbs the sides. Rather than abandon the distant-matter account, the relationist introduces an individually plausible exemption—for instance, that uniformly distributed matter does not generate inertial frames, or that only causally coupled matter counts—which saves the prediction for the original case but excludes the counterexample. Over successive rounds, the reference class of "relevant distant matter" shrinks and accumulates conditions: non-uniformity, causal connection, specific asymptotic boundary conditions. Each addition is defensible on its own, yet together they seem to make the relationist thesis immune to any rotational counterexample, not because the distant-matter link is false but because the conditions for its application have been tailored to match every observed outcome.

Is there an established term in philosophy of physics or metaphysics for this particular pattern—the progressive restriction of a relational reference class through individually reasonable exemption clauses in response to rotational counterexamples? If it is merely a case of ad hoc auxiliary hypotheses, what distinguishes it from standard theory-saving? If it is not recognized as a distinct phenomenon, how should we delimit it: does it apply only to relational theories of inertia, or is it a broader failure mode of reference-frame theories in physics? I am less interested in a bare label than in a precise carving of the phenomenon, its nearest conceptual neighbors, and where its boundaries lie.
