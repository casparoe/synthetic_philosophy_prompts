hey, i chair a seven-person grant panel at a small science foundation. every quarter we review about a dozen proposals, and we've adopted this three-part vote: reviewers score seperately on whether the method is sound (P1), the result is novel (P2), and the impact justifies the cost (P3). then we vote fund or reject as the conclusion. last round we hit the discursive dilemma hard -- majorities on each premise came out yes, but the conclusion vote was reject because people weighted them differently. i need a reusable procedure i can run in about fifteen minutes at the end of each meeting, no philosophy phd required, that tells us exactly which aggregation rule applies when premises and conclusion clash. it has to deal with reviewers who want to revise their premise votes after seeing the conclusion (backtracking), and it needs to flag when "not novel" is really a proxy for personal dislike. our charter says methodological soundness trumps impact in deadlocks, but doesn't say how to operationalize that. the procedure should ask questions in order, say what counts as enough to stop with a table-it decision, and mark exactly where human judgment takes over from rules. apply it to case A: P1 6-1 yes, P2 6-1 yes, P3 4-3 yes, conclusion 3-4 reject; and case B: unanimous yes on all premises, but conclusion splits 4-3 reject because half the panel thinks impact should be weighted double. also tell me where this procedure will misfire or break, and include one case where it gives us an answer we'll hate.
