Consider the philosophical problem of free-floating rationales—normative or functional reasons that appear to carry weight independently of any reasoning agent. Suppose you are advising a small philosophy-of-mind research unit with three years of funding and no engineering mandate; they wish to work specifically on an underexplored extension of this idea, call it the problem of “unmoored rationales.” By this I mean patterns that behave like reasons—guiding optimization, constraining outcomes, or supporting explanations—within complex distributed systems that lack evolutionary selection histories, design intentions, or any interpretive stance that could ground them in an agent.

Which two or three open conceptual questions in this precise space should occupy the majority of their effort, and in what priority order? I want a defended agenda rather than a survey: for each question, state the criteria that place it there (for example, whether solving it is upstream of the others, whether it is tractable for philosophers rather than computer scientists, or what kind of theoretical result over the next few years would cause you to reorder the list). In particular, defend whether the central urgency lies in establishing ontological conditions for the existence of unmoored rationales, in determining whether they generate genuine prescriptive force or merely explanatory patterns, or in developing identification criteria that do not smuggle in anthropomorphic projection.
