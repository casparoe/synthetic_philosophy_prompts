ok so i was arguing with my friend in a philosophy of decision course about whether honoring sunk costs is ever rational, and we kept circling this one weird pattern that i dont think has a clean name. imagine someone who spent like ten years becoming an expert in some niche framework — could be a dead programming language, could be a specific interpretation of kant, whatever. then solid evidence shows up that the framework is broken or outdated. they dont deny the evidence, and they dont say "ive already invested too much to quit now" — thats just the sunk cost fallacy, we all know that one. but instead they feel like if they abandon the framework now, it retroactively turns their past decade into "just a mistake" rather than "a serious pursuit that happened to reach a dead end." so they keep defending it not because they think it will pay off going forward, but because quitting would break the narrative coherence of their own biography — like, the past effort only made sense if it was genuinely directed at something worthy, and switching makes that false.
