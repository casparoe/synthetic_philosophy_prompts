Please describe the most important implications of resolving the meta-problem of consciousness through an evolutionary-signaling framework—where our judgments about phenomenal experience are treated primarily as adaptive social-coordination mechanisms rather than as direct ontological detectors—for the methodology of consciousness science. Specifically, address (i) whether first-person reports should be regarded as evidential data or as phenomena to be explained away, (ii) whether cross-species or cross-system disagreement about consciousness can serve as a meaningful diagnostic criterion, and (iii) how evaluation protocols for artificial consciousness should be redesigned if introspective self-report is discounted. In each case, distinguish the consequences if this framework is correct from those if it remains only one competing explanation.
