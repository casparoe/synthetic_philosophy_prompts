As a retired physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mind, I want to evaluate theories of propositional unity on clear criteria before examining particular accounts. What are the full desiderata that any adequate resolution of Bradley's regress must satisfy? Specify the phenomena it must explain—such as how a judgment binds subject and predicate into one thought rather than a mere list, including relational judgments, negative propositions, and the difference between representing a connection and merely associating representations—stating why each is required. Also state whether the theory must remain compatible with empirical findings on temporal feature-binding in neural processing, and whether that compatibility is negotiable or essential. Identify any subset of these requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable, especially if avoiding infinite regress demands a form of binding that neuroscience does not recognize. Finally, briefly indicate how the standard relational and non-relational approaches fare against this list, but do not provide bibliographies or citations.
