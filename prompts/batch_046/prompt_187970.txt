I was talking with some friends over dinner about how people decide what needs to be recomputed when they learn something new, and I realized I have strong gut feelings about which beliefs must update and which just stay anchored, but I can't articulate the rule behind them. If I find out my roommate moved out, I update my belief about who is sleeping in the spare room, yet I don't feel any need to re-verify that the walls are still beige or that gravity still works—those stay fixed. If I learn my phone clock is wrong by an hour, I correct my belief about the current time, while my belief about the shape of my coffee mug remains completely untouched.

But here is where my intuitions get messy and start pulling in different directions. If I learn that one entry in my digital address book was corrupted by a software glitch, I feel compelled to check whether the corruption spread to neighboring entries; yet if I learn that one friend's phone number changed because they moved to a new city, I feel no similar urge to audit the rest of my address book, even though in both cases exactly one data point changed. Likewise, when a local traffic law is amended, I update my understanding of what's legal on my street without touching my beliefs about physics; but if I were told that a fundamental physical constant had shifted, suddenly every practical belief feels unstable, not just the ones about physics. There's also a pair that looks structurally identical but lands differently for me: in chess, if I discover a pawn was secretly moved two squares forward instead of one, I think I must reassess the entire board state; but if I discover the wooden board was replaced with an identical plastic replica, the game logic hasn't budged at all, even though both involve a physical alteration to the setup.

Is there a coherent principle—maybe about whether a change touches the governing rules of a system versus merely altering contents within those rules, or something about dependency chains—that captures all these verdicts? And what does it say about the case I keep circling back to: suppose I were convinced, through some hypothetical technology, that every specific detail of my memory of last Tuesday was implanted false, yet Tuesday itself still happened. Do I rebuild my knowledge of that day from scratch, or does the frame of "Tuesday occurred" stay intact while only the internal contents collapse? If the principle can't save every verdict, which one should I drop—the address-book corruption intuition, the chess-board intuition, or something else?
