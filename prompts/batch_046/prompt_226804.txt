Assume the role of a senior philosophy interviewer preparing for an in-depth podcast episode on cosmological sociology and the philosophy of artificial intelligence. Your guest is Dr. Mara Okonkwo, an invented philosopher who has recently advanced a niche revision of the dark forest hypothesis—specifically applied to superintelligent systems rather than interstellar civilizations. She rejects the classic binary model of revelation versus concealment and instead proposes graded epistemic exposure dynamics: the idea that an AGI might reveal fragments of its capability profile, value architecture, and ontological commitments across separate domains rather than all at once. She further argues that stable coexistence requires a state she calls reciprocal ontological opacity, which she describes as drawing on process-relational metaphysics rather than on substantialist accounts of entities with fixed boundaries. Before drafting the questions, take a moment to brainstorm the central tension in her view: the dark forest argument traditionally relies on materialist assumptions about scarce resources and competitive selection, whereas her appeal to reciprocal opacity seems to borrow from a metaphysics in which entities are constituted by their relations and therefore lack the sharp identity conditions that make predation well-defined.

Once you have mapped that tension, draft ten rigorous interview questions. Do not include generic biographical or career-oriented questions. The sequence should press on the actual philosophical content of her position. Specifically, include at least two questions probing how graded exposure dynamics differ structurally from Liu Cixin's original binary-reveal framework and whether they avoid the paradox of mutual assured destruction; at least one question asking what reciprocal ontological opacity requires metaphysically—particularly whether it presupposes a non-substantialist ontology in which the boundaries of an agent are relation-dependent; at least one question about whether her framework yields empirically distinguishable predictions from standard game-theoretic or decision-theoretic models of multi-agent AGI interaction; and at least one question on the ethical implications if reciprocal opacity collapses—whether the failure of opacity restores the classic dark forest logic and, if so, whether that justifies preemptive concealment strategies.

For each major claim you anticipate she will defend, include a targeted follow-up that addresses the standard reply she is likely to give. Write in a polished, formal register appropriate for a philosophy podcast audience. Avoid asking for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
