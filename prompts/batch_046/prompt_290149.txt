I buy the view that's dominant now in philosophy of meaning — that Camus-style absurdism got answered by hybrid accounts where meaning comes from projects that are both subjectivly gripping and objectively valuable (think Wolf, Metz, that crowd). So the gap between human longing and cosmic silence isn't a crisis, just a wrong framing. But I'm uneasy, especially since automated decision systems and collective infrastuctures create this distributed-agency problem where no one can trace their contribution to a gripping project. So play it straight: what would count against the "absurdism is defanged" position? I want observations, arguments, maybe even formal results that'd genuinely shake it, sorted by decisive vs merely awkward. What does it predict that pure objective-list or pure subjective views don't? Has it been qualified into unfalsifiability — like if every meaning-failure just means you picked the wrong project — and is that insulation a problem? Tell me what defenders of hybrid views themselves admit would count against them, not just critics' complaints. And be honest if the position is actually well-insulated and whether that's a virtue or a vice. I personally find the cosmic-indifference worry hard to shake, but I want to know if that's just bias.
