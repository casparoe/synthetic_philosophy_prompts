One student said it's exactly like a song: the melody is the real thing, and whether it's on guitar, piano, or hummed by a random stranger doesn't change what the song is. From that she argued being stuck in one skull is arbitrary, since the pattern is substrate-independent. Can you audit this anology? Give me the correspondence table - melody = self?, instrument = brain?, listener = ? - then where it breaks. Specifically, does a song need an audience to exist, are brains instruments or composers, and does the analogy hide that mental loops need continuous feedback that a copied version might lack? Does her conclusion - that substrate doesn't matter - survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the song comparison carrying it the whole time? If the song analogy is garbage, what's a better one for how Hofstadter thinks self can distribute across brains?
