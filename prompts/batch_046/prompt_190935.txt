I would like you to write a polished, formal dialogue between two equally competent philosophers of physics—Dr. Amara Okonkwo and Dr. Julian Voss—debating whether the thermodynamic arrow of time can be explained solely by boundary conditions.

Voss should defend the opposing view: that boundary conditions are explanatorily inert without an intrinsic temporal asymmetry built into the fundamental laws—perhaps through a time-oriented collapse mechanism or an irreducible causal structure—because otherwise the choice of which boundary to label "past" is arbitrary.

The dialogue should take place in a seminar room, with real dialectical engagement: each speaker should directly answer the other's previous point, not simply restate their own position. They should address the standard Boltzmann-brain objection, but I also want them to discuss a specific hypothetical that I have been considering: a cosmological model with symmetric high-entropy boundaries at both temporal extremes, within which localized "entropic bubbles"—regions where metric fluctuations transiently suppress entropy—can host observers. Okonkwo should argue that an observer inside such a bubble could still rationally infer a global Past Hypothesis by conditioning on the statistical rarity of the bubble's internal order. Voss should reply that because the bubbles are symmetric with respect to both boundaries, this inference is circular: there is no non-question-begging ground to privilege one direction of time, and therefore the boundary-condition account fails to deliver the asymmetry it promises.

Please keep both characters intellectually formidable and free of straw-man arguments. The language should be precise enough for someone with a scientific background but without excessive jargon. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading.
