Please describe the most important implications of the thesis that an infinite, strictly non-repeating sequence of finitely valuable conscious lives distributed across causally disconnected spatiotemporal domains carries exactly the same aggregate moral weight as a single life of literally infinite value. Focus especially on what this equivalence implies for the rationality of extreme existential-risk aversion, for whether there remains a principled distinction between rescuing a fixed finite population and safeguarding an indefinitely extending future, and for how moral aggregation should treat value that is spread across domains with no possibility of mutual observation or interaction.
