I have been studying occasionalist accounts of causation—particularly the view, associated with al-Ghazālī, that observed connections between events are not necessary relations but customary sequences directly instituted by divine will, with no causal efficacy residing in created things themselves. In reading these texts I have repeatedly encountered a structural pattern for which I lack a precise label, and I want to determine whether philosophy of religion or Islamic philosophy treats it as a distinct conceptual problem or merely subsumes it under broader headings.

The pattern is this: the occasionalist offers a total metaphysical denial that fire, for example, possesses any power to burn, allowing that God might produce cooling in the presence of fire; yet in practical reasoning, scientific prediction, and even in the theological defense of the system, the occasionalist must rely on the very regularities—fire followed by burning, medicine followed by healing—that have just been stripped of metaphysical necessity. The reliance is not incidental; it is systematic and unavoidable. What makes the tension specific, I think, is that this is not the general skeptical problem of induction—where one doubts whether constant conjunction justifies inference—because the occasionalist does not merely doubt the justification but denies that the conjunction reflects any real relation whatsoever, whether in nature or in the mind, while still treating it as epistemically authoritative.

Does this asymmetry between total metaphysical denial and unavoidable epistemic dependence on regularity have an established name in the literature on occasionalism or on causation? If there is no specific term, I would want a clear definition that distinguishes it from Humean skepticism about induction, from concerns about divine consistency, and from the mere pragmatic acceptance of natural law; and I would want to know whether occasionalist thinkers regarded it as a genuine difficulty requiring resolution, or as a benign feature of a system in which only God, and not nature, bears the explanatory burden. I am not looking for a bibliography, but for the conceptual carving: what exactly this phenomenon is, where its edges lie, and which neighboring concepts it should not be confused with.
