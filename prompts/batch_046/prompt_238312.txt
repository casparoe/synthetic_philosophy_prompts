I'm preparing notes for a graduate seminar on Quine and post-Quinean philosophy of language, and I'd like a consistency audit of my current position before I present it. Here is where I stand. (a) I accept Quine's argument in "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" that the analytic/synthetic distinction cannot be sustained through appeals to synonymy, definitional reduction, or conventional stipulation, and I think attempts to revive it on those specific grounds fail. (b) I also think the failure of reductionism—the second dogma—is actually the deeper source of Quine's holism, and that confirmational holism should be read primarily as a consequence of the collapse of sentence-by-sentence empirical verification rather than as an independent argument against analyticity. (c) Despite this, I want to preserve a functional category of "framework-constitutive" statements: those held fixed within a given domain of inquiry, not tested in isolation, but serving as the logical and conceptual apparatus through which empirical claims are formulated; I take these to do the work analytic statements were meant to do, without requiring that they be "true by meaning alone." (d) I treat mathematics as largely framework-constitutive in this sense—its claims are not empirically falsified piecemeal but are embedded in the web of belief—while still maintaining that mathematics is paradigmatically non-empirical knowledge. (e) I reject the Kantian synthetic a priori altogether, holding that any apparently necessary substantive knowledge is either framework-constitutive (and thus analytic in this functional sense) or ultimately empirical, though I concede the boundary is vague. (f) Finally, I practice conceptual analysis—say, of knowledge or justice—and take it to yield genuine, non-empirical knowledge about the internal structure of our framework principles, not about mind-independent reality.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension how severe it is and what the least costly revision would be. In particular, I'm unsure whether (c) and (f) are compatible with (b): if holism flows from reductionism's failure, can I still identify framework principles in a way that supports objective conceptual analysis? And does (d) sit comfortably with (a) once the metaphysical notion of analyticity is abandoned? Rank the tensions by severity, indicate which single revision would clean up the most problems at once, and flag any commitment that looks stable but actually commits me to something I haven't endorsed. I am not asking for a lecture on Quine; I want an audit of this specific package.
