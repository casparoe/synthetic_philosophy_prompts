I'm taking this course on public memory and ethics, and we keep arguing about tearing down statues or renaming streets, but nobody's actually said what a good *theory* of commemorative practice is even supposed to solve. So I want you to lay out the desiderata: what must any adequate normative account of statues, street names, plaques, and so on actually deliver?

It obviously has to handle the standard cases — honoring a slaveholder whose name is on a courthouse versus honoring an abolitionist whose monument was put up by people trying to rewrite history — but it also needs to deal with what I'm calling the 'indexing problem': a statue doesn't just represent a person, it anchors a specific narrative about why he matters, and tearing it down might collapse that narrative without replacing it with anything coherent. There's also the 'mnemonic externality' issue: every time a resident has to say that street name out loud or navigate by it, they're forced into a kind of passive endorsement, which is different from just seeing a statue in a park. So the theory has to seperate symbolic endorsement from historical indexing, and it has to say whether navigational identity — people knowing where they live — is a real moral constraint or just a practical inconvenience. It should also resolve puzzles like: if we rename a street after a local activist, are we just swapping one bias for another? What about figures whose values were partly good and partly horrific — do we need a hard threshold, or some kind of proportionality test? And can a theory allow for 'mnemonic triage', the idea that in a small town with limited space we have to actively choose who gets remembered, which means omission is always a decision, not a neutral default? Flag any pair of these demands that can't be jointly satisfied — I have a hunch that respecting navigational continuity and allowing democratic revision are going to clash hard.

Don't just list contenders; briefly check how presentist revisionism, historical contextualism, and something like 'mnemonic minimalism' (where we only commemorate processes, not people) do against your list. Write this in American English, please — and don't worry about citations, just the reasoning. I definatly think the hard part is figuring out which demands are real and which are just camp prejudices.
