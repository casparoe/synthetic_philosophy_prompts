hey, i'm serving on the funding committee for the Carroway Institute (small nonprofit that does public philosophy stuff) and we have this weird proposal on our desk. it's called "Conscience and Affiliation" and the organizer wants to run a year-long seminar series using Simone Weil's anti-party writing as the backbone — you know, her idea that parties basically train people to stop thinking with thier own minds and just parrot the line. the twist is that the organizer specifically wants to invite local party reps to come sit in as respondents, not as speakers but as people who have to answer back without any whipping or party discipline. they claim this actually honors Weil because she wasn't against people with party ties, she was against the structural pressure that kills individual moral attention.

half our committee thinks this is brilliant and the other half thinks we're being naive — that once a party person is in the room the structural pressure doesn't vanish just because you ask them nicely to leave it at the door. plus there's our charter clause that says we don't platform partisan organizations, though these folks wouldn't be representing anyone officially. we need a memo that recommends approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and the reasoning has to actually apply Weil's argument rather than just name-check her. like, does her critique apply to individuals who happen to belong to parties, or only to the party as an institutional machine? and we need to be honest about the strongest counter-argument: that we're basically using Weil's dead body to justify either including or excluding real people, which feels kind of gross. can you write that memo? make it something the board could actually vote on, with conditions we could check if they revise the proposal. also please don't ask for bibliographies — we don't have time and half the board wouldn't read them anyway.
