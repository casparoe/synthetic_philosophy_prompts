For a graduate seminar on the semantics of conditionals, I have asked two doctoral students—one defending an update-theoretic uniformism that treats indicative and counterfactual conditionals as variants of a single belief-revision mechanism governed by contextual parameters, the other defending a modal dualism that assigns indicative conditionals to epistemic probability updating and counterfactuals to metaphysical similarity-ordering among worlds—to produce an adversarial collaboration document rather than another round of rebuttals. Please draft the joint statement they should have written. The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims—covering at least the failure of the material-implication analysis, the context-sensitivity of both conditional types, the irreducibility of counterfactual reasoning to mere belief revision, and one precise but non-obvious point about the behavior of nested conditionals under successive belief updates that both camps can sign even though they read its significance in opposite directions. Next, present a disagreements table with three columns—topic, the uniformist’s own formulation of its view, and the dualist’s own formulation—phrased in strictly neutral terms so that each party recognizes its position without feeling it has been caricatured; the disagreements should include whether similarity metrics are genuinely metaphysical or only epistemically reconstructed, and whether indicative conditionals can accommodate causal-intervention reasoning without hidden world-selection machinery. Then identify three cruxes: sub-questions such that settling each one would move at least one camp closer to the other’s overall framework. For each crux, specify a concrete test case—such as a predictive-updating scenario involving partial observation of a stochastic system, or a policy-evaluation counterfactual requiring explicit intervention on a causal graph—that both sides agree is genuinely probative, and record in advance each side’s explicit prediction about what the correct analysis of that case ought to yield. The tone should be formal and constructive, treating terminological divergence as distinct from substantive disagreement, and avoiding any framing that makes either position appear conceptually confused rather than genuinely incompatible on the merits.
