Consider Schopenhauer's doctrine that music is not, like the other arts, a representation of Platonic Ideas, but rather a direct objectification and copy of the Will itself—an immediate expression of the inner nature of the world that bypasses the principle of sufficient reason and operates directly upon the listener's own will. Now consider a practice that did not exist in his lifetime: clinical and immersive audio systems that use real-time biometric feedback—heart-rate variability, galvanic skin response, and EEG patterns—to algorithmically reshape a musical stream's tempo, harmonic tension, and dynamics, with the explicit therapeutic or experiential goal of regulating the listener's autonomic and emotional states. If Schopenhauer had encountered such adaptive, biofeedback-driven music, how do you think he would have assessed its metaphysical status? Would he have regarded it as a corruption of music's essential function—turning an art that should reveal the Will into a technical instrument for the Will's self-manipulation and self-deception—or would he have seen it as an unexpected confirmation of his view, precisely because these systems work not through conceptual representation but by speaking directly to the bodily will they seek to modulate? Reconstruct the reasoning he might have offered, drawing on his distinction between representation and will, his account of music's peculiar effect on the listener, and his broader pessimism about the will's endless striving. Be explicit about where his position might be ambivalent or internally conflicted.
