ok so i've been chewing on Tolstoy's thing that art is basically infection — one person actually feeling something and passing it to others through the work. i want to see if my gut verdicts on weird cases fit any clean version of that, or if i'm just being inconsistent.

my takes:

1. an old woman in a village makes up a funeral song on the spot, everyone weeps together. that's art, and good art — sincere, clear, hits regular people.

2. a composer writes a symphony that only other composers understand; it makes them feel clever, everyone else feels stupid. not real art, or at least not good art.

3. a propaganda poster designed by a committee makes crowds hate an outsider group; it works, people get furious. i think it's art because it infects, but bad art since the feeling is ugly. though part of me wants to say it's not art at all because Tolstoy cares about brotherhood. i go back and forth.

4. an AI is trained on grief diaries and writes a story that makes readers cry, but no human ever felt that grief — just weights. my gut says it's not art, because there's no person-to-person transmission. but the readers are infected... so maybe it is? i keep flipping.

5. a guy lives like a peasant for ten years, then paints one clumsy picture of exhaustion. technically ugly, but you feel the tiredness. good art, maybe the best kind.

6. a songwriter who never had her heart broken writes a breakup hit using formulas; millions cry. there's a human behind it, but she's faking the feeling. i think it's art but counterfeit — like fake infection. but then case 4 has no human feeling at all, and i treated that differently. are these actually seperate?

7. i made up this idea of 'counter-infective art' — an installation designed to block all feeling, and people leave numb exactly as intended. is that art? my gut says yes, but Tolstoy's framework seems to break.

so is there a principle that captures all of these without being a mess? does it need a real human feeling the original affect? does the affect have to be 'good' in Tolstoy's moral sense? and what does it do with case 4 vs 6 — same verdict or different? also, if there's no principle that saves every verdict, which one should i drop? and what does the best principle predict for case 7?

don't need sources, just the reasoning.
