Specify the full list of desiderata that a theory of second-order derived intentionality must meet. By second-order derived intentionality, I mean representational content possessed not directly by tracking world-features (first-order derivation, as in the case of a thermometer designed to register temperature), but by being designed to interpret, correct, or attribute representational states to another artifact that itself possesses only derived intentionality — for example, a diagnostic system that revises the outputs of a first-order classifier, or a legal hermeneutic program that resolves ambiguities in a statutory text. For each desideratum, state the specific conceptual failure it is meant to prevent: the collapse of all derived content into original biological intentionality (the challenge raised by Searle's Chinese Room argument), the inability to distinguish representational malfunction in the interpreted artifact from successful revision by the interpreter, the threat of an infinite regress of interpretation with no grounding original mind, and the loss of normative authority when a second-order system overrides a first-order one. Include the distinction between merely having derived representational status and possessing a particular normative authority over the system interpreted, and the constraint that both artifacts remain syntactic engines lacking intrinsic aboutness. Identify which single desideratum eliminates the largest class of existing philosophical accounts, and assess whether the full set of requirements is jointly satisfiable or produces an unavoidable trilemma. A table mapping each desideratum to its motivating case, its excluded theories, and its negotiability is welcome.
