I recently encountered Hume's notion of a standard of taste—the idea that aesthetic judgments can be more or less correct depending on the delicacy and practice of the observer—but the summaries I found treat it as a settled midpoint between pure subjectivism and rigid objectivism. For my purposes, that framing is too coarse. I need a decision tree that takes a respondent from an initial commitment about the existence of observer-independent aesthetic facts to a specific philosophical position, using concrete diagnostic questions rather than labels.

The tree should begin with the question: If every human observer disappeared, would there remain any fact about whether the Grand Canyon is beautiful? From there, each branching node should pose a specific case or claim on which positions genuinely diverge—such as whether two observers with perfect perceptual resolution but radically different cultural histories must converge if both are free of bias; whether persistent disagreement among acknowledged experts counts as evidence of error in some parties or as proof that beauty is plural; and whether an artificial system optimized solely to minimize prediction error in human preference data could serve as an ideal critic without itself possessing consciousness. I want the leaves to include named positions where they exist—strong aesthetic objectivism, Humean intersubjectivism with its emphasis on trained sensibility, subjective relativism, and an error-theoretic view that denies all aesthetic properties—along with at least two unoccupied answer-combinations, each briefly diagnosed as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Given my technical background, I am particularly interested in whether a view that analogizes the ideal critic to a well-calibrated model with maximal feature resolution can be distinguished from Hume's human-centered account, or whether it collapses into the same branch. Please flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, phrase every node so that someone without philosophical vocabulary can answer it on substance alone, and avoid asking for citations or reading lists, since the instrument is meant to classify commitments rather than scholarship.
