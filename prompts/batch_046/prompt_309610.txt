I've been arguing with my friend about whether Simone Weil was just being dramatic in her essay calling for abolishing all political parties — she claims they turn citizens into machines that can't think for themselves, and that party loyalty is basically a cult that kills independant judgment. My friend says that's a strawman, modern parties are just coordination devices for shared preferences, nothing more. Before I take sides I want the full spec: what must any adequate theory of democratic representation through parties actually deliver?

Write the desiderata. The theory has to handle cases where parties genuinely represent interests versus where they manufacture false concensus (Weil's central worry). It must respect the distinction between reflecting pre-existing preferences and constructing them — collapse that and you're dead. It can't flatly contradict basic findings on group polarization and authority deference, but also can't reduce parties to pure pathology since they clearly organize democratic power. Flag which requirements are non-negotiable (e.g., explaining how a voter retains moral autonomy while identifying with a party platform) versus which are just preferences of particular camps (maybe determinism about voter rationality?). Then briefly score the standard pluralist, deliberative-democracy, and Schumpeterian competitive-elite pictures against your list — I mainly care about the criteria, not the scorecard. Most importantly, tell me if there's a jointly unsatisfiable subset: if we demand both that parties express authentic popular will and that they don't manipulate belief-formation, does Weil's critique show that's impossible?

One more thing since she wrote in the 1940s: I want a criterion about "platform parties" that don't have membership in the old sense but operate through algorithmic curation of attention — is that still a party in the relevant sense, or does it escape Weil's critique because there's no loyalty-structure left to corrupt? And if it escapes, does that mean her argument was always about organizational form rather than about representation itself?
