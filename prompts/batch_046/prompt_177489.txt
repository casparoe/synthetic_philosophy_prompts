I sit on my university's research ethics board and this proposal just landed—my sister recieved a related diagnosis last spring so I'm definatly not neutral. The PI wants to recruit teens using a provisional label they call "synthetic attention deficit" (they think AI-curated feeds produce a novel distractibility pattern) and then track whether being in the study changes self-descriptions over eight weeks. Half the board says this is a classic Hacking looping-effect case—you're constructing the psychiatric kind you're measuring—so reject outright. The other half says every psych study involves labeling and Hacking's idea is being overapplied here. I need our decision memo for Thursday: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with concrete criteria the PI can actually respond to, and with an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side. Please seperate what's fairly settled about looping effects in psychiatric classification from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't present a contested view as if it's the consensus.
