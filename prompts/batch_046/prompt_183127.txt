Write an essay on the controversy over whether honouring sunk costs is always irrational. Your essay should distinguish carefully between calculative rationality — assessing future costs and benefits independently of past expenditure — and expressive rationality, in which continuing a course of action can be justified as maintaining the narrative continuity of one's practical identity and collective commitments. Apply this distinction to a concrete case: a city council must decide whether to complete a half-built cultural centre that has already consumed its entire budget, where finishing requires twice the original estimate but abandoning it leaves an unusable ruin and severs a century-long civic identity tied to that specific site. Weigh whether the council's decision to complete the project can be defended as rational without falling into the sunk-cost fallacy, or whether any appeal to identity or commitment ultimately collapses into an irrational attachment to irrecoverable losses. Reach and defend a clear, substantive conclusion; do not end with a non-committal summary of both sides. Write in British English throughout. At the very end, present your bottom-line verdict in the fixed form \answer{...} enclosing a concise phrase of no more than fifteen words that captures your conclusion.
