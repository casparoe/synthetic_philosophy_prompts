I am writing an essay for my philosophy class on whether numbers should count in rescue cases, especially in light of Taurek's argument that each person's claim to be saved carries equal weight regardless of how many others are involved. Rather than the standard five-versus-one scenario, I need an example of a conceptual issue that complicates the non-aggregation view—specifically, a situation in which the people who might be saved share a collective resource, an interdependent survival condition, or a single distributed identity, so that saving the larger group does not merely add more equal claims but actually transforms the nature of the remaining single person's claim. Could you construct such a concrete scenario and explain precisely where the conceptual tension lies for the idea that moral claims cannot be summed?
