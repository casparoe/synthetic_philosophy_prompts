ok so i'm drafting a novel about a linguist who slowly looses her grip on what she can take for granted, and i've been reading Wittgenstein's On Certainty to get the vibe right. for a reading group (yeah i know, pretentious) i wrote down my philosophical profile on hinge epistemology and i want to know if it holds water before i build the plot around it. here's where i stand:

(1) hinges aren't beliefs at all, they're more like the frame that makes belief possible — grammatical, not empirical.
(2) but in fiction i think we can treat hinges as movable, like the rules of a game you can swap if the story demands it.
(3) i also think there's no universal list of hinges; what's bedrock in physics might be up for grabs in a novel.
(4) yet i want to say my character's crisis is genuinely epistemic, not just aesthetic — she's loosing something real, not just changing genres.
(5) and i reject the "well-entrenched belief" reading cause that makes hinges just stubborn opinions.

do these hang together? specifically, if hinges are context-relative and fiction can move them freely (2 & 3), can her crisis still be about real knowledge (4)? and does (1) sit ok with (5) or am i smuggling in a metaphysical claim? rank the tensions, tell me the cheapest fix, don't go easy on me just cause i'm a novelist.
