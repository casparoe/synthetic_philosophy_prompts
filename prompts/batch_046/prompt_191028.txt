I serve on the Acquisition and Interpretation Committee of the National Craft and Concept Archive. Before our March 15 vote, we must recommend which single proposed acquisition should anchor the new permanent gallery, "How Invention Happens"; budget permits only one, and the decision will set the archive’s interpretive stance for a decade.

Proposal A is the ceramic series The 144 Bowls, in which the artist treats bowl-hood as a concept-space defined by six continuous variables—rim curvature, foot height, wall thickness, glaze saturation, asymmetry coefficient, and thermal-shock pattern—while holding constant every identity condition required for something to count as a vessel. The work is presented as an exhaustive traversal of adjacent possibilities: pure knob-twiddling in Hofstadter’s sense, with the claim that creativity is never ex nihilo but is the disciplined navigation of a pre-structured parameter space.

Proposal B is the installation Unmaking the Vessel, which uses the same clay medium but systematically violates every identity condition of vessel-hood: walls are absent, interiors are exteriorized, and the ceramic is fired to structural failure. Its curator argues that genuine creativity requires not adjusting dimensions within a concept but dissolving the concept’s boundary conditions altogether—what might be called dimensional collapse rather than dimensional adjustment.

Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. Recommend one concrete acquisition, state the bottom line unambiguously, and include a specific framing or display condition for the winning work. Your reasoning must engage the strongest philosophical point on the losing side: specifically, address whether preserving identity conditions is a necessary feature of creativity or an arbitrary constraint, and whether an institution dedicated to documenting creative process can legitimately exclude works that reject the very idea of a parameterized concept-space. Keep the memo under 450 words, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading.
