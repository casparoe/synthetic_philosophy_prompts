Consider a philosopher who judges that humans, cows, and octopuses are phenomenally conscious in a way that raises the meta-problem of consciousness; that fish and insects are not; and that a bee colony is not, despite solving collective problems no single bee can solve. Do you think she will say the same about a current language model running with a persistent recurrent state that reports continuous subjective experience? What about a human-derived brain organoid that exhibits synchronized gamma oscillations and reports a unified sense of self but lacks all sensory input and motor output? I am less interested in the direct answers than in the rule she appears to be using, the other rules that accommodate her earlier judgments equally well, what each rule predicts for both new cases, the verdict she is most likely to reach and why, and the one further test case that would best separate the competing rules. Please respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
