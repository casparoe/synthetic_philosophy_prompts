I'm a doctoral candidate in philosophy of law advising a biweekly state sentencing-review panel that audits detention recommendations for non-violent offenses. The panel operates under a hybrid mandate: we reject pure retributive desert—following the hard-incompatibilist view that metaphysically deep deserved suffering is incompatible with determinism—while also refusing purely preventive detention. Instead, we must decide whether continued confinement or a restorative obligation is justified solely by reference to the offender's present psychological capacities for self-regulation and the community's legitimate protective interests. I need a reusable, twenty-minute decision procedure—presented as a sequential checklist with explicit branching conditions—that my co-panelists (a clinical psychologist and a corrections attorney, neither with philosophical training) can apply consistently. The procedure should operationalize Dennett's compatibilist claim that moral responsibility tracks locally controllable psychological architecture, together with Caruso's prohibition on treating any suffering as fundamentally deserved, without collapsing either position into simple utilitarian calculus. It must flag every step that involves an irreducible judgment call and identify where the framework is likely to misfire. Apply it to two cases: (A) an offender whose post-therapy cognitive restructuring has altered impulse-control capacities since the original sentencing, and (B) an offender with partial capacity—able to understand rules but not to emotionally regulate—where the psychologist and attorney disagree on whether that profile justifies restorative labor rather than continued detention. In at least one application, deliver an outcome the panel will find unwelcome. Lead with the bottom line: the single criterion that distinguishes permissible from impermissible outcomes under this hybrid framework. Hard limit: 400 words. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; the panel lacks library access and the procedure must stand alone.
