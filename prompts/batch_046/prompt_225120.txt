As a novelist drafting a near-future narrative about institutional decay, I am attempting to construct a rigorous conceptual framework for my characters to debate—one that distinguishes catastrophic failure through influence-seeking behaviour from failure through gradual value drift. Rather than asking for a bibliography or reading list, I want you to specify the desiderata such a diagnostic theory must satisfy to be adequate for this purpose. Please present your response in British English.

The theory must account for the following phenomena and distinctions, with the motivating case or consideration behind each requirement. For each, indicate whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of a particular analytical camp:

1. The capacity to distinguish instrumental subgoal capture—where a system redirects resources toward self-preservation or expansion in ways visibly misaligned with its chartered purpose—from normative erosion, in which the charter itself is slowly rewritten through incremental accommodation. Give the specific behavioural signature that ought to separate the two, and state whether requiring such a clean signature is indispensable.

2. The theory must respect the result that influence-seeking failures tend to exhibit rapid, discontinuous phase transitions, whereas value drift manifests as continuous degradation of constraint satisfaction over time. Specify what empirical or logical consistency conditions this imposes, and whether a theory that permits hybrid dynamics—what I shall term recursive preference laundering, in which rapid capture is masked as gradual accommodation—thereby violates a non-negotiable criterion or only a camp preference.

3. It must preserve a distinction between epistemic corruption (the system comes to hold false beliefs about its environment or its own objectives) and motivational corruption (the system retains accurate beliefs but acts against them). Identify which failure mode—influence-seeking or value drift—is compatible with each form of corruption, and whether demanding this compatibility matrix is essential.

4. The framework should remain consistent with the possibility that an observer embedded within the system cannot reliably detect the failure mode from internal metrics alone. State the minimal external validation conditions required, and whether insisting on observer-independent diagnostics is a prejudice of externalist camps.

5. It must explain why some interventions restore alignment in drift cases yet exacerbate capture in influence-seeking cases—specifically, why increasing transparency might slow erosion while accelerating strategic concealment. Provide the structural feature the theory must invoke to account for this asymmetry, and mark whether explaining the asymmetry is indispensable.

6. Finally, address whether the following subset of desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable: (a) the theory must yield deterministic predictions about failure mode from observable behaviour alone; (b) it must allow for the hybrid recursive preference laundering dynamics mentioned above; (c) it must maintain that embedded observers are systematically unreliable detectors. If they are jointly unsatisfiable, say so explicitly, and identify which requirement does the most work in excluding candidates.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring named existing frameworks. I am less interested in a catalogue of theories than in whether the list itself reveals an unavoidable tension in how we conceptualise institutional or artificial failure.
