ok so i'm writing up my views on collective memory and forgetting for a seminar, and i want a consistency audit before i present. here's where i've landed—some of this is Margalit/Rieff territory, some is my own spin:

1. There's a genuine moral duty for societies to remember specific atrocities—it's owed to victims, not just instrumentally useful. Forgetting at the collective level is a moral failure.

2. At the same time, i'm convinced by Rieff-style arguments that forced remembrance can poison social life; in some post-conflict contexts active collective forgetting is not just permissible but maybe required for repair.

3. I think the duty is institutional, not personal: archives, monuments, education should carry the record, but private individuals shouldn't be expected to maintain perpetual emotional memory. The collective remembers through structures, not through every citizen being haunted.

4. I also think individuals have a right to be forgotten—a moral claim that you shouldn't be permanently defined by past actions, especially after amends, and not just a privacy claim.

5. If a society deliberately chooses amnesia as part of reconciliation (like some truth-commission designs), that's not automatically betrayal; it can be a legitimate political choice.

6. But i want a firewall: institutional forgetting should never erase seperate accountability records, so perpetrators can't use reconciliation to vanish.
