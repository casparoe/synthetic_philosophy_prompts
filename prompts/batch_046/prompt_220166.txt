ok so for a workshop i'm writing down my commitments on precommitment and want a consistency check before i present. here's roughly where i landed:

1) the classic Ulysses contract — tie yourself to the mast — is only rational if your future self is genuinely irrational or prefference-distorted, not just diffrently informed.

2) i also think constitutions should entrench procedural rules that bind future generations, even though those citizens are fully rational, because a polity is a diachronic agent with intransitive social preferences over time.

3) on the personal side, binding your own future self is rational even if they're fully rational, because temporal parts of one person stand in an authority relation — principal to agent.

4) i reject binding strangers' futures without consent, so cross-generational lock-in only works with actual democratic endorsement by the present generation.

5) and i think the best model for all this is strategic/game-theoretic (Schelling-style), not based on rationality failure or diachronic authority.

6) also, epistemic Ulysses contracts — precommitting to ignore certain future evidence — are usually irrational, except when they're just attention budgeting, not belief formation.

do these hang together? it feels like (1) and (3) pull opposite directions for personal cases, and (5) might undermine both. meanwhile (2) and (4) seem to clash but maybe they don't? i want an audit: which pairs are actually in tension, rank them by severity, what's the cheapest fix for each, and which single revision cleans up the most. and tell me which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection — don't just flag everything.
