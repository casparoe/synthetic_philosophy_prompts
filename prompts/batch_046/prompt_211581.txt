dependency order: what idea has to be in place before choice architecture even makes sense? i'm guessing they need to see that preferences aren't always pre-formed, but i need the exact sequence and why each step depends on the last.

the carrying example: one concrete scenario that carries the whole session. it needs to show the same outcome reached three ways — hard mandate, nudge, and boost — and it must lack something the cafeteria example has (probably food?). also say why not the tray.

misconceptions map: they'll think nudging is just manipulation with extra steps, and that boosts are just education with a fancy name, and probably that libertarian paternalism is an oxymoron. tell me exactly where in the sequence to head each one off, not just at the end.

what to leave out: tell me what concept or debate i should openly refuse to explain at this level and say so.
