Rank the following five normative stances on intergenerational discounting in long-term climate policy evaluation, ordering them from most defensible to least defensible and giving one sentence of reasoning for each: (A) a constant pure rate of time preference justified by temporal location neutrality; (B) hyperbolic discounting reflecting democratic deliberation biases; (C) zero pure discounting coupled with declining marginal utility of consumption; (D) cohort-limited moral standing that assigns weight only to presently living generations; and (E) a selective framework that applies pure time preference to experiential welfare streams but exempts structural goods such as multi-century institutional continuity. In particular, indicate whether stance (E) avoids the standard criticisms of (A) or collapses back into one of the other positions.
