there's this neurophilospher i interview, dr. k, and she's given me her gut calls on a bunch of hypothetcial replacement scenarios over coffee:

- destructive scan-and-upload to silicon: she calls that death, the upload is just a copy.
- gradual in-vivo neuron-by-neuron swap over a decade: she says the original survives, no big deal.
- non-destructive upload running parallel while the meat brain lives on: original is the biological one, the upload is a new person.
- gradual replacement with a yearly rollback where 5% of synthetic parts get swapped back for fresh organic tissue, then re-replaced: she still says original survives.
- upload lives in vr for 20 years then gets downloaded into a synthetic clone body: contiuous, same person.
- split-brain digitization, left and right hemispheres running on separate servers: neither is the original, both are copies.

now the new case: patient gets replaced up to 80%, but the protocol forces a two-week "organic reversion window" every six months where all synthetic modules shut down and the remaining 20% organic tissue takes over fully. during one window the patient reports a sharp first-person "stutter"—like a gap in narrative—then synthetic comes back and the story resumes. eventually they hit 100% synthetic, but the windows continue via simulated organic states. during one simulated window, the stutter happens again. is dr. k going to call that the same person?

predict her verdict. then map out the implicit rule she's running—maybe narrative coherence without substrate breaks, maybe a no-branching rule with temporal indexing, maybe something about an unbroken causal chain through a single center of experience. also list the rival rules that fit her past answers just as well. and tell me what further hypothetical case would actually split those rules apart.

please don't ask for citations or reading lists—this needs to work for a model with no web access. keep it in the weeds but don't lean on some ultra-obscure theory no one's heard of.

format: do the analysis in prose, then at the end give me a JSON object with exactly these keys:
- "predicted_verdict" (string)
- "primary_rule" (string)
- "rival_rules" (array of strings)
- "crucial_test_case" (string)
