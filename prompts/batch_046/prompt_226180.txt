I am a retired experimental physicist who, after four decades in condensed-matter research, have recently begun auditing philosophy-of-science seminars and facilitating a small interdisciplinary discussion circle—approximately ten participants, roughly half with scientific training and half from the humanities, meeting for ninety minutes every month. In our recent sessions on scientific methodology, I have noticed that references to Popper's falsificationism and the demarcation problem tend to land flat; the group treats them as settled textbook material rather than as live difficulties. For our next meeting, I would like you to design a structured participatory activity that makes the tension between falsificationist demarcation and the problem of auxiliary hypotheses genuinely felt, without relying on familiar historical cases or on any lecture delivered beforehand.

Please construct the exercise around a fictional research programme you call resonant-field dynamics, which posits a core claim about non-local correlation patterns and defines three explicit categories of theoretical adjustment available to its defenders: protective-belt refinements of measurement conditions, core-parameter recalibration within pre-specified bounds, and methodological exemptions invoking observer-dependence. Design four staged experimental reports that initially appear to refute the core claim, and assign participants in pairs—some as programme defenders, some as independent funding evaluators—rules that permit them to invoke adjustments from any category. The rules should be calibrated so that, absent prior agreement on which adjustments are legitimate scientific revisions versus ad hoc rescues, the evaluators cannot conclusively reject the programme, and the defenders discover that every apparent falsification can be absorbed. Provide a precise ninety-minute timeline, specify roles and any minimal materials needed, include a debrief that maps the group's decisions onto the logical distinction between naive and sophisticated falsificationism, and frankly identify which normative feature of the demarcation problem—most likely the status of the protective belt—this simulation cannot reproduce and must be supplied by facilitation.

Please present the design in a formal, step-by-step format suitable for direct use. In addition, I ask that your response conclude with a brief, self-critical reflection: identify the specific argument, pedagogical failure mode, or conceptual limitation that would most likely cause you to revise your claim that this activity makes the problem felt rather than merely illustrated—stating clearly where your reasoning is most vulnerable.
