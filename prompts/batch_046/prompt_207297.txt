I'm delivering the keynote address at the retirement symposium for Dr. Elena Vasquez, who directed a public scholarship foundation for thirty years. Half the audience are philosophers and economists who have worked on distributive justice; the other half are the students she funded and their families. Vasquez maintained that merit is not an intrinsic property of individuals but a relational status contingent on which opportunities a society renders visible — a position that made her controversial in policy and academic circles alike. Write a twelve-minute address that honors her career without reducing it to biography. The concept of desert must do structural work: explain why she rejected the label "meritorious" for her selections in any straightforward sense, and do so in language that reaches the non-specialists without diluting the philosophical claim.
