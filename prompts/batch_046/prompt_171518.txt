I'm drafting an essay for my historiography seminar, and I'm attempting to navigate a path between Leopold von Ranke's aspiration to present the past as it actually was and the scepticism developed by E.H. Carr and Hayden White. My argument is that Carr's well-known critique in What Is History? depends on an unstable fact-value distinction: Carr insists that historical facts are always selected and interpreted, yet he retains the normative claim that some selections are distorted, which seems to presuppose a baseline of correctability that his constructivism should reject. At the same time, White's tropological analysis in Metahistory shows that Ranke's own narrative structures—romantic, comic, tragic—determine which events become visible as historical, but White's framework appears to leave no space for the archive to resist narrative imposition at all.

To avoid both naive realism and total constructivism, I propose a position I am calling constraint realism: archives do not deliver raw, uninterpreted facts, but they do establish negative conditions—structural limits on what can be coherently said about the past—rather than merely providing material for free narrative construction. For example, if colonial census records systematically miscategorize religious identity, they do not simply supply biased data points; they render certain historical descriptions structurally unavailable while leaving others open. I want to claim that this captures Ranke's actual archival practice better than his slogan does, and that both Carr and White miss half the picture—Carr by retaining a hidden normative baseline, White by dissolving all resistance into narrative form. Before I develop this further, I would value your comments on whether this distinction is conceptually coherent or merely a relabeling of ordinary source criticism, whether I have mischaracterized Carr's or White's positions in ways that undermine the comparison, and whether the essay risks fabricating a false middle ground simply to avoid committing to either side. I am particularly interested in whether the notion of negative archival conditions can do genuine philosophical work, or if it collapses under pressure.
