Here is a puzzle I have been working through regarding the so-called bootstrapping problem for easy knowledge. It seems clear to me that if reliabilism permits me to know my perceptual faculties are reliable only by employing those same faculties, then the view is caught in a vicious circle: the justification for trusting my vision already presupposes that I can trust my vision, which is structurally identical to begging the question. Because of this loop, reliabilism appears unable to license any knowledge of its own reliability without collapsing either into skepticism about the external world or into an arbitrary stipulation that some beliefs are basic. Any validation of the device through itself reproduces the same circular pattern. If reliabilism allows an analogous move in the epistemic case, then it seems committed to the same vicious loop.

Given all of this, my question is: since the bootstrapping problem reveals an unavoidable vicious circularity in reliabilist justification, is Cohen's denial of the inference anything more than a way of masking the problem?
