In philosophy-of-time discussions the Rietdijk-Putnam argument for a four-dimensional block universe has stalled for decades against presentist and growing-block responses, despite both sides accepting the same relativistic formalism and the same failure of absolute simultaneity. The disagreement persists over whether this failure eliminates temporal becoming, but I suspect the stalemate is neither empirical nor straightforwardly metaphysical: the sides may be operating with different criteria for what makes an event determinately real—one treating determinacy as tenseless truth-value, the other requiring causal accessibility from a present perspective—while using the single term "real" to cover both. Is this diagnosis correct? Is the disagreement verbal, a conflict of explanatory priorities about what physics needs to preserve, or a bedrock intuition that passage is indispensable and unarguable?
