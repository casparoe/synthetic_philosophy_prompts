Hey, I'm writing this up for a seminar on whether climate and germs can be "historical actors" without falling into determinism, and I built this argument that now seems to prove either everything is an actor or nothing is. Where's the mistake?

(1) To call something an actor rather than background noise, we need it to make a counterfactual diffrence: if F hadn't happened, the historical outcome we care about wouldn't have either, even holding human intentions roughly constant.

(2) The 1815 Tambora eruption satisfies this: without that volcanic winter, the 1816 crop failures don't happen, and the migration waves and local unrest tied to them unfold differently, regardless of what European governments intended.

(3) So volcanoes can be actors.

(4) The Black Death works the same way: remove Yersinia pestis from 14th-century Eurasia, hold human social structures constant, and feudal labor relations don't collapse in that specific pattern.

(5) So germs can be actors.

(6) But now apply the same test to climate more broadly. The Little Ice Age's worst decades in the 1600s shaped Swedish military logistics and Ming grain storage in ways that no local human decision could fully offset.

(7) So climate is also an actor.

(8) Here's where it gets weird. To "hold human intentions constant" in (1) we have to treat human agency as an independent layer. But if environmental conditions actually constitute the possibilities for action—if you can't hold intensions constant without also holding soil chemistry, temperature, and disease ecology—then the separation in (1) is fake.

(9) Once we drop that separation, counterfactual dependence applies everywhere. The specific gravitational pull of Jupiter on Earth in 1642 also changes outcomes, if only by a tiny fraction.

(10) So either we accept that everything—Jupiter, soil bacteria, solar flares—is a historical actor, which makes the concept useless, or we reject (1) and can't defend Tambora or the plague as anything special.

(11) I don't want to give up (2) or (4) or (6), but I also don't want (10). Which step do I reject, and what's the cost? I feel like something slipped between the counterfactual test and the conclusion about agency, but I can't name it.
