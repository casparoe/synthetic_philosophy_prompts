You serve as the senior sign-off referee for the Inter-District Governance Council, which reviews proposed Social Coordination Charters for autonomous districts located in high-mobility transient-demographic zones — regions where annual population turnover exceeds forty percent and stable kin networks are absent. Your charge is to translate the tight-culture versus loose-culture principle — that societies facing sustained ecological or historical threat develop stronger norms and lower tolerance for deviance, while low-threat societies permit looser coordination — into a reusable decision procedure your office can execute within forty-eight hours without ethnographic staff, specifying the ordered questions, what counts as sufficient factual answers, where the procedure issues a formal denial, where it returns the decision to you as a matter of values rather than prediction, and where it will misfire in rapidly shifting populations. Apply this procedure to three charter proposals: (A) a strict public-market queuing mandate with penalties for line-cutting; (B) an informal, negotiation-based access rule for shared water rights in a low-literacy community; and (C) a universal curfew and dress-code charter in a religiously diverse district, noting that in case C the procedure yields a result you personally reject. Deliver the procedure, the three applications, and the explicit statement of its limits as a numbered list of exactly five items, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introductory or concluding text.
