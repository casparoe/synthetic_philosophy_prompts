Specify the desiderata that an adequate philosophical account of mathematical effectiveness in physics must satisfy to resolve Wigner's puzzle rather than merely redescribe it. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual distinction behind it—covering predictive success, explanatory unification, and structural correspondence, but also the accommodating of misdirection by mathematically elegant yet physically empty formalisms; the preservation of distinctions between invented formalism and discovered structure, and between representational fidelity and instrumental utility; and consistency conditions with fallibilism and the historical contingency of theoretical change. Indicate which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable and which reflect camp-specific commitments (e.g., mathematical Platonism, structural realism, or formalism). Then determine whether any subset of the non-negotiable requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—especially combinations demanding both complete representational transparency and full ontological neutrality—and identify which single criterion does the most work in excluding inadequate candidates. Maintain the weight on the criteria and their justifications; do not provide a comparative scorecard of existing accounts.
