I've mostly bought Dennett's idea that the self is just a center of narrative gravity -- not a soul, just the point where all the stories about someone converge. But I'm pushing a version I haven't seen defended: the narrative center only counts as a full moral self if it's tied to one continous biological body that can suffer and act. So if you upload someone's memories into two different robots, neither is really them; they're both narrative satellites orbiting a dead planet, with diluted moral status. What would actually count against this? I don't mean vague discomfort -- I mean which fission cases, gradual replacement experiments, or results about distributed cognition in animals actually threaten it? And does it secretly smuggle back in the Cartesian substance it claims to reject? Give me a committed take: is this a defensible extension of narrative gravity, or does it collapse under its own weight?
