So me and three friends run this open-mic night at a local cafe every other thursday. The unwritten rule was always that any organizer can book an act they found on youtube and also pick the lighting for that slot, since we all figured we could trust each other's judgement. Last month it went totally wrong: two different people booked performers with heavy strobe effects, and I threw in some flashing LEDs cause I thought it added energy, and now we've got regulars complaining about migranes and one person actually had a seizure in the crowd. What's messed up is that each of us genuinely believed we were being cautious — I knew strobes were risky, but I assumed 'just a little' was fine since no one had objected yet.

Now we're fighting about the fix. One co-organizer wants a strict veto system where anyone can block any booking or lighting choice if they feel uneasy, but another friend says that's just paralysis and we'll never run a show again. My roommate suggested something weirder called 'sortition rotation' — basically we randomly pick one person each week to have total authority over all decisions, so there's no overlap in private reasoning. I keep feeling like the core issue isn't just bad communication but something specific about how groups fail when every individual acts on their own rational assessment of risk. Is there actually a philosophical concept for that kind of failure? And does rotating authority solve it, or just move the risk to one person? I'm genuinely stuck on whether this is a coordination problem or a deeper problem about rationality in groups.
