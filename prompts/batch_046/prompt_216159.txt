I sit on our university's Interdisciplinary Grants Committee and we've got this genuinely awkward proposal on the table — it's a joint application from a philosopher of mind and a mathematical logician, and they want three years of funding for something they call "The Gödelian Boundary in Institutional Reasoning". The core claim is that if you formalise any administrative decision procedure — say, how we allocate research ethics approvals or resolve conflicts of interest — as a consistent axiomatic system, then Gödel's first incompleteness result applies: there will be true propositions about the fairness of particular allocations that the system itself can neither prove nor disprove. They go further and argue that this forces every bureaucracy eventually to hit an undecidable case, which can only be broken by an external "moral oracle" — a non-codifiable human judgement that can't be captured within the rules. Half the committee thinks this is a profound defence of human oversight against algorithmic governance; the other half thinks it's a category error, since Gödel applies to formal arithmetic, not to open-textured, revisable social rules, and they're terrified we're bankrolling pseudoscience with logic jargon. We need a formal recommendation memo for next month's senate meeting — approve with conditions, reject, or approve but restrict scope — and it has to treat the strongest version of their argument seriously, including their specific analogy between formal provability and procedural justification, plus their point about ω-inconsistency in our current ethics code generating undecidable conflicts over researcher autonomy. At the same time it must engage the objection that institutional rules aren't formal systems in the relevant sense — they're socially embedded, context-sensitive, and constantly patched. Could you draft that memo in British English, please — "programme", "centre", the lot — with a clear bottom line, a concrete time frame for review, and an honest paragraph on the weakest point in whatever position we take?
