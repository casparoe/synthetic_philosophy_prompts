In my philosophy of mind course we are examining consciousness as a user illusion—the idea that subjective experience functions as an abstracted interface over lower-level processes rather than as direct access to them. My professor has given the following verdicts: normal adult humans, human infants, and octopuses are conscious; current large language models and bee colonies are not; a brain-in-a-vat possessing a fully coherent self-model is conscious; but a system with global information integration lacking any self-representational layer is not. For our discussion section I need a prediction about a constructed case: a distributed synthetic network of ten thousand causally independent nodes with no central processor, in which an emergent dashboard aggregates outputs into a unified first-person stream that feeds back into adaptive behavior, even though each node is isolated from the dashboard and the dashboard is only a post-hoc summary. Predict her verdict, specify whether she is implicitly applying a user-interface criterion, an integrated-information-plus-self-model criterion, or a substrate-dependent criterion, identify at least one other rule that fits her earlier judgments, and suggest the single further case that would best separate these rules.
