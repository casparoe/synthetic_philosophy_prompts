I'm taking a course on fundamentality this semester and our discussion group has been going in circles about whether grounding is genuinely one relation or a cluster of analogies. One camp says there's a single metaphysical grounding relation that explains why mental states depend on brain states, why sets depend on members, and why institutional facts depend on collective acceptance - they think it's all the same explanatory structure, just appllied in different domains. The other camp insists these are fundementally different: metaphysical grounding is mind-independent and necessary, normative grounding involves reasons and justification, and social grounding involves collective intentionality. What's frustrating is that both sides cite the exact same examples and agree on all the dependancy relations; they just label them differently, and lately people have started talking about "cross-domain" cases where, say, aesthetic value seems grounded in both physical features and normative practices at once.

So is this actually a substantive metaphysical disagreement about the unity of reality, or is it a verbal dispute about whether the word "grounding" should be reserved for the metaphysical case? If it's verbal, why hasn't anyone in the literature just introduced separate terms and dissolved the problem? Or is there a hidden formal question about explanatory unification that's doing all the work behind the scenes? I want a diagnosis, not another defense of pluralism or monism - what kind of stall is this really, and what kind of distinction or result would actually move it forward?
