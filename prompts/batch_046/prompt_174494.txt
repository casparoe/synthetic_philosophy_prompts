Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Mira and Theo, debating the dogmatism paradox. Mira holds that any rational belief-revision policy demanding openness to counterevidence must eventually apply to itself, producing either an infinite regress or an arbitrary stopping point, and that the stopping point is merely dogmatism under another name. Theo holds that the paradox confuses object-level beliefs with framework-level commitments, and that a rational agent can legitimately hold certain structural assumptions fixed while revising everything inside them, without this being dogmatic.

To keep the exchange from being a replay of the standard peer-disagreement literature, frame their argument around a hypothetical model of recursive epistemic escrow: a system in which an agent assigns every belief a confidence weight and a meta-confidence weight, and updates both simultaneously according to a shared rule. Have Mira argue that in such a system, either the meta-weights collapse to zero or the agent exempts the update rule itself, and that this exemption cannot be justified within the system. Have Theo respond by distinguishing between the rule's application conditions and its justification conditions, and ask whether Mira's demand for total reflexivity is itself a framework commitment she is unwilling to escrow.

The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth: each character should respond to the other's specific point about the escrow mechanism rather than talking past each other. Neither should be a strawman; both should concede minor points and press harder ones. End with them disagreeing not only on whether the paradox is dissolved, but on whether it reveals a genuine limit of rationality or only a misapplication of recursive reasoning. Write in American English, keep the tone formal, and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts.
