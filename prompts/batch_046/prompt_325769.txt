Hi — quick consistency check before I embarass myself in the seminar. Here's roughly where I've landed on structral realism; tell me if it's coherent or if I'm smuggling in commitments.

(a) Epistemic structural realism about science: we only ever access the mathematical structure of theories, never the intrinsic nature of whatever's relating.

(b) Ontic structural realism about fundamental physics: there literally aren't objects with intrinsic properties; it's relations all the way down.

(c) I formalise this with a Ramsey-sentence approach, but I think you need second-order quantification over relations plus what I call 'persistense conditions' that fix how those relations carry across counterfactual scenarios. I also talk about 'modal viscosity' — the idea that two worlds can be structurally isomorphic yet differ in how 'thick' their modal connections are, without that thickness being reducible to further structure.

(d) I reject the 'no miracles' argument, because pessimistic meta-induction actually supports structural continuity rather than full-blown entity realism.

(e) I want to extend this to moral discourse: moral terms survive theory-change structurally, so there's a kind of ethical structural realism that isn't just analogy.

(f) I don't think conceivability is a guide to possibility for physical structures, but I do think it's reliable for judging differences in modal viscosity.

My gut says (b) and (c) pull apart — if relations are all there is, where do the persistense conditions live? And (e) seems to commit me to moral structures being as fundamental as physical ones, which feels like a leap from (a). Meanwhile (f) looks like I'm helping myself to modal intuition exactly where I deny it elsewhere.

Which tensions are genuine and which dissolve? Rank them by severity, not all equally bad. I'm especially unsure whether (c) and (f) are compatible with (b). What's the least costly revision?
