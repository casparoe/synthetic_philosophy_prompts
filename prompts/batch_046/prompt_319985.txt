I'm digging into Popper's poverty of historicism and keep noticing a pattern I don't have a word for. Take a historicist law that predicts some crisis—say, a systemic collapse—by a specific date. When that date passes with nothing obvious happening, defenders don't drop the law; instead they say the crisis was "already present" in a transformed institution, or that the timeline was always dialectical not calender-based. Each save sounds okay alone, but together they let the theory survive any chronology without changing its core claim. I know Popper talks about prophecy vs prediction and about ad-hock rescues, but is there a specific name for this kind of chronological elasticity, where you preserve the law by dissolving its temporal boundaries rather than tweaking the mechanism? How's it different from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis? If there's no established term, what should I call it and where does it blur into plain unfalsifiability? And what evidence or counter-example would change your mind about whether this is genuinely distinct from the immunization strategies Popper already described?
