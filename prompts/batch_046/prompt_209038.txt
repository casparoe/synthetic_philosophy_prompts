After retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have recently turned to the philosophy of education and have been studying Alfred North Whitehead’s writings with particular interest, finding in them unexpected parallels to the rhythms of scientific inquiry. To test my understanding, I have formulated five propositions that might capture or extend his educational philosophy. Please rank them from 1 (most central and faithful to Whitehead’s actual vision) to 5 (least central or least faithful), and provide a brief justification for each placement.

The propositions are as follows:

1. Education should proceed through a repeating cyclical rhythm—romance, precision, and generalization—each cycle operating at a higher level of complexity.

2. The fundamental pedagogical task is to prevent the accumulation of inert ideas, that is, knowledge that is received but never actively integrated into the student’s living experience.

3. The learning process ought to be modeled on concrescence: each educational stage is an actual occasion that prehends prior learning and synthesizes it into a novel subjective form, rather than functioning as a passive transmission of information.

4. At the university stage, disciplinary specialization should be privileged over interdisciplinary synthesis, since generalization is a luxury that should only follow exhaustive technical mastery.

5. Mathematical and logical training represents the apex of the precision stage, but it must remain continuously tethered to the concrete experiential contexts from which its abstractions originally arose.

I am especially uncertain about the third proposition, which applies Whitehead’s metaphysical vocabulary directly to pedagogy, and about the fourth, which appears to conflict with his well-known emphasis on synthesis. Your ranking and reasoning will help me calibrate my interpretation.
