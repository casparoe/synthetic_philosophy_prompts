I've bought into the careenium picture: you've got these simmballs embedded in the substrate, and the claim is they don't just encode state but physically push the substrate around - like symbols are literally causal gears in the machinery. What'd actually count against that? Not just the usual "symbols are abstract" handwave. I want the hard defeaters: experiments or models where represention and causation come apart cleanly, cases where the substrate moves without simmball involvement or vice versa, and whether this collapses into some kind of pancomputationalism that'd be embarassing. Rank fatal to awkward. Which ones would someone defending the careenium admit are real problems versus ones they'd call category mistakes? And check whether the view can keep being patched up - if every counterexample gets explained away by adding another substrate layer, is that insulation a virtue or does it kill the claim?
