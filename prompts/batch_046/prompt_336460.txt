Write a villanelle about Buridan's ass in which the two refrains are “No reason weighs the left” and “No reason weighs the right.” Let the six stanzas alternate these lines so that the form itself enacts the paralysis: each return of a refrain should feel like the same bale approached again, with no progress toward decision. In the closing couplet, bring both refrains together not to break the deadlock but to reveal that the ass starves not from weakness but from an integrity of judgment that refuses to invent a preference where none exists. The poem should treat the paradox as a problem of rational choice under perfect equality rather than a fable about indecision, and it should quietly register—without footnotes or explicit citation—the scholarly point that Buridan himself likely never posed the example in this literal form, since the “ass” scenario is largely a later attribution. Avoid anachronism, avoid resolving the tension with a moral, and let the villanelle’s formal closure be the only closure: the meeting of the two lines in the final stanza should suggest that the will can suspend judgment even at the cost of action, and that the tragedy, if there is one, lies in the demand that reason always supply a decisive reason. Keep the diction plain but precise, and make sure someone who knows the paradox recognizes it rendered correctly rather than named in passing.
