I run a grad seminar in philosophy of tech, 8 students, one 90-min sesssion. I want them to feel the force of artifacts having politics, but not in the obvious bridge-in-concrete way — I'm interested in "silent delegation," where a system absorbs moral and political choice into its normal running so users stop seeing those choices as choices at all. Design an in-class simulation or structured role-play where the group starts by making explicit collective decisions about a shared resource, then rules gradually replace human choice with automated protocals, and they end up justifying outcomes they never actively picked. Give me the rules, roles or groups, a timeline with phases, and whatever small stakes or payoffs don't need real money but still matter. They should discover the delegation themselves; no lecture up front, don't name the concept until debrief. Then a debrief that maps each phase onto the classic embedded-politics case and onto silent delegation, tells me what will predictably go wrong the first time (maybe they blame me as facilitor instead of seeing the system), and is frank about which part of silent delegation this can't reproduce — probably the slow historical sedimentation over decades. No citations, no reading list, just the runnable acitivity.
