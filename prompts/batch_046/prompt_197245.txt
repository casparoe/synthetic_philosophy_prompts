I am testing reasoning about hinge epistemology with an argument that seems to collapse into self-defeating skepticism if Wittgenstein's picture of hinges is taken together with a principle I am calling the Hinge-Transmission Principle. Here are the steps:

(1) In Wittgenstein's On Certainty, hinge propositions—such as that there is an external world, that one's senses are generally reliable, or that the earth has existed for many years—are not justified or unjustified within our practices. They stand fast as part of the scaffolding of thought; they are neither items of knowledge nor rational beliefs held for reasons.

(2) Call this the Non-Epistemic Status Thesis (NEST): hinges occupy no position in the space of epistemic reasons. They are neither warranted nor unwarranted, neither rational nor irrational.

(3) The Hinge-Transmission Principle (HTP): if belief B is rationally maintained only within a framework that takes hinge H for granted, then B's epistemic status cannot exceed that of H.

(4) Every particular empirical belief B (for example, that this is a tree, or that water boils near 100°C at standard pressure) is rationally maintained only within a framework that takes specific hinges for granted.

(5) So by HTP together with NEST, every particular empirical belief B also occupies no position in the space of reasons: B is neither warranted nor unwarranted.

(6) But to claim that B lacks all epistemic status is itself to subject B to an epistemic assessment—to place it within the space of reasons after all.

(7) Applying HTP to (6): that very assessment inherits the non-epistemic status of hinges and is therefore itself neither warranted nor unwarranted.

(8) Hence the argument that empirical beliefs lack epistemic status is unwarranted; yet if it is unwarranted, it gives us no reason to accept NEST or HTP, and the skeptical conclusion is blocked only by undermining its own premises.

(9) To avoid this self-defeat, one might reject HTP. But then empirical beliefs can possess full epistemic standing despite being embedded in a framework whose foundations have none—which requires explaining how epistemic standing can be generated ex nihilo from a non-standing base, an explanation that looks like magical emergence.

(10) Alternatively, one might reject NEST and grant hinges a minimal form of warrant. But then the sharp Wittgensteinian boundary between hinges and ordinary beliefs dissolves, and hinges become just especially stable beliefs.

(11) Or one might reject (4) and hold that some empirical beliefs are self-standing, independent of any hinge framework. But this seems phenomenologically false and undermines the very idea of framework-dependence that makes hinge epistemology distinctive.

I want to preserve NEST if I can, but I feel the real pressure is on HTP. Tell me which step is the weakest link, and explain precisely why it initially looks plausible—especially the move from framework-dependence in (4) to status-inheritance in (3). Is the flaw an equivocation between pragmatic presupposition and normative inheritance, a scope shift in applying HTP to meta-level assessments, or a modal slide? Then price the alternatives: what does a Wittgensteinian pay for rejecting HTP and allowing ex nihilo standing? What does a Moorean pay for rejecting NEST and granting hinges minimal warrant? And what does a rationalist reconstruction of hinge status pay for accepting HTP but denying that (6)-(8) generate a self-defeat? Do not just list positions; show how each position bears the specific cost of the step it rejects.
