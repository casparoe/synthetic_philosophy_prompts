Examine the contrast between W. E. B. Du Bois's position—particularly as articulated in his essay 'Criteria of Negro Art'—that art is inseparable from propaganda for racial justice and beauty, and the formalist counter-claim that art's political impact is strongest when it refuses explicit advocacy in favor of autonomous aesthetic form. To what extent can Du Bois's framework accommodate the latter view without collapsing artistic value into purely instrumental terms? Address whether Du Bois allows for any non-propagandistic aesthetic experience, and distinguish settled aspects of his philosophy from contested scholarly interpretations of his attitude toward formalism and disinterested contemplation. Separate established readings from genuinely disputed ones, and avoid presenting a contested interpretation as the consensus.
