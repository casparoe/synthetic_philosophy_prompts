For my senior philosophy seminar I am preparing a creative supplement on Moore’s paradox—the tension created when someone sincerely asserts that a particular state of affairs holds while sincerely denying belief in that very state of affairs, especially in the first-person present tense. I want a Petrarchan sonnet that makes the paradox do structural work: the octave should present the asserted content in observational or third-person terms, the volta at line nine should shift sharply to a first-person denial of belief, and the rhyme scheme of the sestet should leave the two positions unreconciled rather than resolving them. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with the issue recognizes why the problem is not merely a logical contradiction but involves the transparency of first-person belief reports, yet it should also stand as poetry for someone who has never encountered the philosophy.
