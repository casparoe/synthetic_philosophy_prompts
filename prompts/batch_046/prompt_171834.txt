I'm trying to sort out my gut feelings about farmed fish and shellfish welfare since I cut back on beef but still eat shrimp and salmon. Here are my calls, mostly off the cuff:

- Keeping salmon in those dense net pens where they get fin rot and sea lice: not okay, clearly suffering.
- Stunning shrimp with electricity before boiling them: okay, that seems humane.
- Live crabs in grocery store tanks, claws banded, sitting on bare plastic for days: not okay.
- Breeding trout for bigger filets even though it gives them spinal deformities: not okay.
- Feeding farmed salmon wild-caught fish meal: wrong, but I think that's more about wild ecosystems? Still feels like a welfare issue for the wild ones.
- The case I keep going back and forth on: using cleaner wrasse in salmon pens to eat sea lice. They swim around eating parasites, which sounds natural, but their trapped in the pen and sometimes the salmon eat them. Is that okay because they're doing their job, or is it wrong because we're using them as tools?
- Also, culling the slowest-growing shrimp larvae in a hatchery — part of me says they're too tiny and undeveloped to matter, but then I think about chick culling and feel like I'm being inconsistent.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? What does it say about the cleaner-fish case and the larva-culling one? And if I have to drop one of my verdicts to keep the principle simple, which one should go? Please write your answer in American English.
