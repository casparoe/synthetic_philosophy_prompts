In my graduate seminar on metaphysics of science, a student kept insisting on the maxim: "If a mask can suppress it, then it was never a real disposition to begin with." She deployed it to reject any analysis of fragility that permits an antidote—say, a sorcerer's-apprentice-style intervention—to block the breaking of a struck glass, arguing that genuinely dispositional properties must be, in her phrase, "fink-proof." I am convinced that this one-liner is doing highly ambiguous work: it might be advancing a conceptual claim about the conditions for correctly applying disposition-vocabulary, a metaphysical thesis about the ontology of dispositional properties, a methodological rule for testing whether something is disposed, or even a normative claim about how we ought to individuate properties. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan might be expressing, arranged as a menu rather than a single interpretation? For each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, whether it is substantive and defensible, whether it is false, and—most importantly—whether that particular version actually undermines the standard counterfactual analysis of dispositions (the analysis that ties a disposition to a stimulus condition and a manifestation) or whether it merely attacks a strawman version of that view.

The context matters: she needs the slogan to do specific damage to the idea that fragility can survive the presence of a masking antidote. So please say, for each disambiguated claim, whether adopting it genuinely forces us to abandon the conditional analysis or whether it leaves that analysis intact, perhaps by relocating the disposition to a different time-slice or substrate. I also want you to consider a less-discussed variant that came up in our discussion: what if the slogan is interpreted not about single-track dispositions but about "disposition clusters," where suppressing one manifestation-track (breaking) reveals that the underlying categorical basis was always shared with other, unmasked tracks (say, a particular crystalline structure that also grounds scratchability)? In that case, does the slogan collapse into a claim about property individuation—about how finely we should carve up the categorical base—rather than a claim about the existence or reality of the disposition itself? Please address whether that cluster-based reading saves the intuition behind her maxim or renders it vacuous.
