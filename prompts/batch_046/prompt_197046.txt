In my philosophy of mind reading group we've been stuck on the combination problem for panpsychism for months now. The micropsychists say macro consciousness is just lots of tiny consciousnesses arranged together, the fusion people say they merge into one, and the bonding crowd says there's a special relation that ties them up — and yet nobodys conviced anyone else. The arguments are all familiar, the examples keep getting recycled, and we haven't moved an inch. My hunch is that this isn't really one disagreement but two: one about whether experience can be structurally composed like physical parts, and another about whether a macro-subject needs a single indexical 'point of view' that can't be distributed across micro-subjects. So is the stall because we're answering different questions under the same heading — a metaphysical question about parts and wholes versus a phenomenological question about what it's like to be one thing? Or is there a hidden factual issue about neural correlation that we're ignoring? And honestly, what would actually break the deadlock — a new conceptual distinction, a formal model of phenomenal fusion, or should we just admit the question is malformed and move on?
