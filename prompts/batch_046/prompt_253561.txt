Here's a view I think gets dismissed too fast: with something like 10 quintillion insects alive at any moment, even a 0.1% credence that one is sentient makes the expected moral weight of insect suffering astronomically larger than all human suffering combined. So if you reject veganism and don't care about crushing ants, you're not being rational, you're just committing scope neglect. Play it straight — what would actually have to be true for this scale argument to collapse? I'm not asking for the obvious 'bugs probably don't feel pain' reply, since the argument is designed to survive low probabilities. I want the real escape routes: can moral patienthood be probabilitic in any meaningful way, or does expected-value reasoning break down across deep ontological uncertainty? Is there an action/omission asymetry that saves ordinary morality? Does the argument prove too much and drag in bacteria or digital minds? For each, say whether it's a live objection or just logically possible, what defenders of the scale view would themselves concede is awkward, and whether the position is genuinely well-insulated or just insulated by qualifications. I want an honest map of its exposure — is this insulation a virtue or a bug?
