State your actual credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that an action retains full Kantian moral worth when performed by an agent for whom both duty and a strong, co-present inclination are individually sufficient motives, provided the agent's maxim explicitly assigns duty the status of sufficient reason. I do not want a conditional answer that defers to decision-theoretic analogies; I want a single number and its explicit decomposition. Break the credence into four weighted components: (a) the interpretive question of whether Kant's prohibition on inclination serving as the determining ground permits overdetermined motivation without contamination; (b) the metaphysical question of whether a single action can bear two distinct determining grounds without incoherence; (c) the axiological question of whether moral worth is binary or admits of intermediate degrees; and (d) the normative question of whether the intuition that acting solely from duty carries special value survives if overdetermination is metaphysically unproblematic. For each component, provide a rough sub-credence and explain whether they combine multiplicatively, additively, or via some other rule to yield your overall figure. Finally, identify which component is the dominant source of uncertainty—meaning the one whose revision would shift your final number most—and specify the form of a purely conceptual argument, requiring no empirical data, that would cause that revision.
