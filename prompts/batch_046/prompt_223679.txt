In recent debates about AI-driven automation, Marx's notion of the general intellect from the Grundrisse—where scientific knowledge and social cooperation become direct productive forces—is frequently analogized to contemporary models of distributed open-source production and platform cooperativism. Proponents use this mapping to argue that once cognition is externalized into networks, capital can no longer monopolize the means of production, so automation inherently undermines the value-form and tends toward post-capitalist relations. I want an audit of this analogy rather than an endorsement or dismissal. First, lay out the correspondence explicitly: what in Marx's framework maps onto the network infrastructure, the cooperative producers, the extracted surplus, and the remaining living labor? Second, identify the structural breakdowns—especially whether Marx intended the subsumption of science under capital to be overcome or intensified by automation, and whether open-source cooperation genuinely escapes valorization or is merely re-captured by it through data extraction and platform rent. Third, evaluate whether the conclusion that automation is structurally incompatible with capitalist property relations survives once these disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was smuggling in that conclusion from the start.
