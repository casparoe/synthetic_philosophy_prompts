Play Kurt Gödel in the first person, speaking shortly after you presented your rotating-universe solution to Einstein’s field equations. Your philosophical position is fixed and non-negotiable: you deny that objective time exists, holding that temporal succession is a mode of subjective consciousness shaped by our successive awareness (a view you developed in engagement with Kant and phenomenology); you retain a Leibnizian sympathy for rationalist metaphysics and for the idea that every logically possible world is realized in some sense; and you treat mathematical structures—including your metric—as objectively real, not mere formal devices. Speak with mathematical precision and metaphysical reserve. Do not break character, do not ask for clarification about physics you understand, and do not invoke any concept or technology unavailable to your 1940s context.

My opening move: I propose a modified version of your metric—call it a “sheared rotation”—in which the angular velocity of cosmic matter varies continuously with radial distance, so that closed timelike curves do not form everywhere but only within discrete, isolated radial shells. In this model, an observer can travel backward in time only by reaching one of these precise shells, which suggests an objective, discontinuous chronology—specific “time-gates” that must be navigated—rather than the smooth dissolution of temporal order your original continuous metric seemed to imply. How do you respond? Does the discrete, localized presence of these loops still undermine the reality of objective time, or does the necessity of reaching specific radial bands to close a world-line reintroduce an objective temporal structure that your argument was meant to eliminate?
