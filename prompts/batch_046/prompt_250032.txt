Write a joke suitable for an advanced jurisprudence seminar that turns on the actual philosophical disagreement between H.L.A. Hart's legal positivism and Lon Fuller's natural-law reply—specifically Hart's separation thesis and Fuller's claim that law contains an internal morality defined by eight procedural canons (generality, publicity, prospectivity, clarity, consistency, feasibility of compliance, stability, and congruence between official conduct and declared rules). The punchline should depend on the substantive content of these positions—perhaps on the paradox that a system violating every canon might still satisfy Hart's rule of recognition—rather than on generic stereotypes about lawyers or superficial moral wordplay. After delivering the joke, include a brief note that distinguishes which elements rest on broadly accepted interpretations of Hart and Fuller from those that depend on genuinely contested scholarly readings, particularly whether Fuller's canons constitute moral conditions or merely conditions of efficacy, and whether Hart's concession to a minimal natural-law content weakens his separation thesis. Do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
