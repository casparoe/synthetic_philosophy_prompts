For my seminar paper in metaphysics of mind and art, I have constructed a test case that I believe puts pressure on the standard well-foundedness requirement for ontological grounding, though I suspect my reasoning contains a hidden mistake. Consider an infinite, non-cyclic descending chain indexed downward: each artwork A(n) exists and is what it is in virtue of the intentional mental state M(n) that produced it, while each mental state M(n) exists and is structured as it is in virtue of its prior contemplation of the preceding artwork A(n+1). Thus A(0) is grounded in M(0), M(0) in A(1), A(1) in M(1), and so on, with no first artwork and no first mental state. Every member of the sequence appears to have a local ground, yet the chain never terminates in a fundamental fact. Given this structure, I want to ask whether the absence of a bottom-level ground entails that the entire sequence lacks ontological weight—so that the existence of A(0) is ultimately unexplained—or whether it merely shows that explanation is infinitely deferred without being absent. Furthermore, if explanation is indeed infinitely deferred across the whole chain, does that not amount to the sequence explaining itself, which would violate the irreflexivity of grounding? I am particularly unsure whether this scenario forces us to reject such artistic dependence loops as metaphysically impossible, or whether it instead demonstrates that local grounding relations can remain sound even when global well-foundedness fails. Could you help me identify precisely where my reasoning goes wrong?
