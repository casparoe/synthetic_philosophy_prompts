I'm designing a take-home exam for a graduate-level course in epistemic logic and self-reference. Write one essay question expected to take about 90 minutes and yield roughly 800 words, on the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents. To keep the question from being answerable by reciting Löb's theorem and its standard belief-corollary, embed the following constructed framing: consider an agent A that operates with a graded stability hierarchy, assigning each belief a rank n; A's self-trust principle applies only to beliefs of rank n and is conditional on A possessing a proof that no belief of rank n is unstable; in addition, A has a promotion rule that allows reflective endorsement to elevate a belief to rank n+1. Ask students to determine whether this graded architecture allows A to consistently trust its own consistency, or whether the Löbian obstacle re-emerges either at the limit rank or through an interaction between the promotion rule and the derivability conditions (D1–D3). The prompt should require them to separate the formal derivation of the obstacle from the philosophical claim that rational self-trust is impossible, and to diagnose exactly where the graded restriction blocks the standard proof and where it fails.

Provide a model answer of about 700 words that addresses the graded architecture explicitly, analyzes the limit-rank problem, distinguishes formal derivability from philosophical interpretation, and evaluates whether the obstacle is escaped or merely relocated. Include a marking scheme out of 25 points built around five must-hit moves—such as correct statement of the obstacle, analysis of the graded restriction, diagnosis of the limit-rank or promotion-rule interaction, separation of formal and philosophical claims, and evaluation of consistency—with specific marks for each and a one-line note on how candidates typically fail that point. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific papers by title or date; the answer must be fully self-contained.
