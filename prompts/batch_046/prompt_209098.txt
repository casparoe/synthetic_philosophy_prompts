I'm preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends a modified Millikan-style teleosemantics. Her specific contribution is a "producer-coordination" model: she rejects the standard consumer-based account, in which representational content is fixed by what a representation is biologically designed to cause in its consumer, and instead argues that the normative standard for a mental representation is set by the conditions under which its production is synchronized with other producers in a distributed cognitive system. She maintains that this avoids the disjunction problem for perceptual representations and explains why misrepresentation can occur even when the individual organism's biological function is fully satisfied.

Please draft eight to ten rigorous, conversational interview questions tailored to this view, going well beyond asking her to restate it. I want the questions to press where the teleosemantics literature actually presses, but adapted to her coordination framework. Specifically, include:

- One question on how she distinguishes misrepresentation from mere subsystem malfunction at the level of an individual neuron or circuit, with a follow-up keyed to her likely appeal to system-level norms.
- One question on "empty" representations—such as belief in phlogiston or in a particular nonexistent deity—asking whether her account can accommodate them without collapsing into a purely causal or purely social theory, with a follow-up anticipating her probable response that these are cases of uncoordinated producers.
- One question comparing her synchronization criterion to Dretske's learning-based selection: is her account just information-theoretic naturalism in social clothing? Include a follow-up for the case in which she denies the parallel.
- One question on the naturalization commitment itself: does her reliance on "coordination" smuggle in intentional or normative notions that a purely biological story cannot ground?
- A closing question asking what empirical result—perhaps from neuroscience or comparative cognition—would genuinely count against the producer-coordination model rather than being reinterpreted within it.

Write the questions as polished podcast prompts, each with its follow-up logic briefly noted, and avoid any generic biographical or career-oriented questions.
