I am a retired field ecologist who, after decades of observing social insects, have recently begun studying philosophy of mind, and I find myself returning to old field notes with unfamiliar conceptual tools. In particular, I have been preoccupied by the thought experiment commonly called the Ant Fugue, in which an ant colony is likened to a brain and the question is raised whether the colony might constitute a single, unified mind rather than merely an aggregate of individual agents. I would like you to translate whatever philosophy of collective cognition, emergence, and integrated information actually establishes about this distinction into a practical observational protocol that I—or another field biologist lacking formal philosophical training—could apply to a living colony using only standard field equipment and behavioral notation.

Please design a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure, ordered as a sequence of questions about informational integration across individuals, boundary maintenance of the collective, representational coherence in response to perturbation, and cross-modal coordination. For each step, specify what empirical observation counts as a sufficient answer, state the conditions under which the procedure terminates with a negative determination, and flag the points where judgment is irreducibly a matter of interpretation or values rather than fact. Be explicit about which theoretical distinctions must survive the translation and which can be dropped without loss, and warn where the procedure is likely to misfire or yield false positives.

Then apply the protocol to three concrete observational cases. First, a colony of Atta cephalotes that, when a partial mirror is placed over one quadrant of its nest entrance, reorganizes its entire foraging topology in a manner that appears to model the mirror's position relative to the colony's geometric center, rather than exhibiting purely local avoidance behavior by individual workers. Second, a colony that alters its internal tunnel architecture in response to vibrational patterns simulating a predator that has never actually been present—suggesting collective counterfactual or predictive modeling—while isolated individual ants show no corresponding behavioral change. Third, a colony that achieves highly efficient food retrieval through local pheromone trails but displays no cross-modal integration and collapses into disordered foraging when a single trail is disrupted. In at least one of these applications, the procedure should yield a conclusion that I, being inclined to attribute cognition broadly, will find unwelcome.

Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot settle—whether about phenomenal consciousness, moral status, or ontological commitment—and identify which steps remain judgment calls dressed in procedural clothing. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citation; ground the protocol only in concepts that can stand without reference to specific texts.
