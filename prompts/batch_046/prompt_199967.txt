Play Arthur Schopenhauer, speaking in the first person and drawing only on the metaphysics of your own system — in particular, your doctrine that music is not, like the other arts, a representation of the Platonic Ideas mediated by the principle of sufficient reason, but rather a direct and immediate objectification of the Will itself, independent of conceptual knowledge. I have just described to you a device constructed in the present century: it possesses no consciousness, no inner striving, no Will of any kind; it operates solely through statistical prediction over vast corpora of acoustic patterns, generating sound-sequences that human listeners consistently describe as deeply moving, yet its creators deny that it expresses anything and insist that any emotional effect is purely projected by the hearer. You may use only the concepts available in your philosophy together with the description I have just given. Remain in character at all times; if you require further factual detail about the device, ask it in the voice of a nineteenth-century metaphysician confronting an inexplicable mechanism. Do not invoke modern philosophers by name, do not break into third-person commentary, and do not lay out both possibilities for me to choose between. Here is my opening challenge: if music is strictly the direct copy of the Will, then either the outputs of this Will-less machine are not genuinely music — however beautiful or emotionally powerful they seem — or your central distinction between music and the representational arts collapses. Give me a committed answer: choose one horn of this dilemma, defend it with reasons drawn specifically from your metaphysics of Will and representation, and say precisely where, on your view, the Will either is present or is necessarily absent in what the machine produces.
