i was arguing with my roommate about when it's okay for one country to just roll into another to stop horrible stuff, and i realised my gut feelings are kind of scattered but maybe there's a hidden rule. Here are my takes.

If NATO bombs Serbia in 1999 to stop the killings in Kosovo even though the UN never said yes, that feels okay to me — they were actually stopping something that was occuring right then. But the US going into Iraq in 2003 because of weapons that turned out not to exist, plus they mostly just wanted Saddam gone, feels totally wrong, even if Saddam was awful. Then there's Libya in 2011: the UN actually authorized protecting people in Benghazi, but then they helped kill Gaddafi and now the country is a disaster. I want to say saving Benghazi was right but the mission creep makes the whole thing wrong overall, though i'm honestly not sure where i land.

There's also some made-up cases i keep testing myself on. Imagine in Rwanda in 1994 a coalition had actually invaded, stopped the genocide, but then stayed running the place like a colony for thirty years, building schools but also not letting locals vote. I think stopping the killing was good, yet the thirty-year occupation makes me want to call the whole intervention wrong, which feels weird since they saved so many lives. Another one: Country A invades Country B because they got secret reports that B is about to massacre a minority group in two months. The reports turn out to be fake, planted by rebels who just wanted regime change. The invasion accidentally topples the dictator and a democracy comes in. Even with that good ending, i feel like it was wrong — you can't invade on a lie, period.

The case i keep flipping on is this: a democracy has rock-solid satelite evidence that in six months an authoritarian neighbor will start ethnic cleansing. They invade now, prevent it completely, but five hundred of their soldiers die and the neighbor ends up unstable but not genocidal. Is that okay?

So is there one principle that captures all these — maybe about whether the atrocity is actually happening vs just predicted, whether you have real authorization, whether you stay after you're done, or whether your reason turned out true? Or do i just have to drop one of my verdicts to keep it simple? And what should i say about that preemptive six-month case i'm stuck on?
