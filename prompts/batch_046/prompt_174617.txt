I've been thinking through my own intuitions about whether I'd survive being gradually turned into a computer program, judging each scenario by gut feeling rather than theory. If doctors replace a single cell in my brain with a chip that behaves identically, I feel nothing essential has changed — still me. If they do that for every cell, one by one over many years, until my whole body is synthetic, I still think I'm the same person at the end. But if they scan my brain in detail, destroy the original, and run a perfect copy on a server in one second, that feels like I died and a duplicate woke up — not survival. Here's what's hard to square: during gradual replacement, if each new chip runs just a bit faster, so by the end I think a hundred times quicker, that still feels like me, only accelerated. But if they gradually replace just the parts that handle feeling, using chips that flip every emotion — grief becomes delight, affection becomes irritation — then even though the change was smooth, I feel I've been replaced by a stranger inside my head. I'm also unsure about a different case: what if every replacement chip is mass-produced, not unique to me, and is running inside thousands of other uploaded minds at the same time? Am I still one person, or just one instance running? Is there a principle that captures the pattern — gradual is okay, instant copying isn't; speed changes are okay, emotional inversion isn't — or do I have to abandon one of these verdicts? And what should I think about the scenario I keep flipping on: replacing my brain in reverse order, starting with the parts that plan and decide and ending with the parts that keep me awake and breathing?
