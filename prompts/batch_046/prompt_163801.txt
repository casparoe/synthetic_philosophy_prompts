Please write, in American English, the complete list of desiderata that an adequate philosophical theory of moral responsibility must meet—especially one that takes Strawson's challenge seriously.

For each desideratum, state the motivating case, distinction, or result behind it: for instance, whether the theory must preserve reactive attitudes such as resentment and gratitude; whether it must allow responsibility within a deterministic framework; whether it must separate causal contribution from being genuinely deserving of blame; whether it must distinguish historical responsibility for one's formed character from present-tense responsibility for a specific act; and whether it must accommodate the idea that moral responsibility operates as a socially useful regulatory fiction rather than as a metaphysical property. Flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—especially if the demand that true responsibility requires being causa sui makes standard compatibilist conditions impossible to satisfy together. Then briefly indicate, without bibliographic detail or citation, how hard determinism, classical compatibilism, and Strawson's own illusionist stance fare against your list, but keep the emphasis on the conceptual architecture of the criteria rather than on scoring the views. I do not want reading lists or references; I want the specification itself.
