Write an essay assessing whether George Berkeley's master argument against the conceivability of mind-independent matter succeeds if reconstructed using a contemporary distinction between representational content and phenomenal character, rather than relying solely on his original appeal to bare imagination. Weigh the strongest objections—particularly those charging that the argument equivocates between epistemic and metaphysical possibility, and those suggesting that representational content can outstrip phenomenal character in precisely the way the argument denies—against the defense that any representational vehicle for matter existing independently of minds necessarily involves a mental act of conception. Reach a specific conclusion about whether the reconstructed argument is valid and whether its key premise about the dependence of representational content on mental acts is defensible. Justify your conclusion by weighing the considerations discussed rather than merely reporting them.
