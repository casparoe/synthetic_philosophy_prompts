If historians are correct that revolutions devour their own children—destroying Robespierre, Danton, and thousands of sans-culottes who had advanced the popular cause—does this not demonstrate that the Terror operated according to an autonomous ideological logic, independent of class interest, given that the guillotine was class-blind? From that premise, I struggle to see how the Law of the Maximum, which imposed fixed prices on grain, can be interpreted as a material class measure rather than as a purely symbolic enactment of revolutionary virtue. Must accepting the devouring thesis force us to read such economic legislation as ideology alone, or is there a coherent way to maintain a class-struggle interpretation without denying that the Revolution turned on its own agents?
