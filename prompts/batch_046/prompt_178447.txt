I'm directing a small research group in metaphysics for the next three years and trying to decide where our labor will be most consequential. We've worked through the standard terrain—whether grounding is well-founded, whether infinitely descending chains are possible, the relation between grounding and ontological dependence, and whether grounding is one unified relation or a family of them—but I'm now weighing which remaining open questions are genuinely upstream of the rest. One line concerns whether explanatory pluralism about grounding, where different domains invoke structurally different dependence relations, undermines the very idea of a single hierarchy of fundamentality; another concerns how grounding interacts with modal properties, especially de re modal claims about entities taken to be fundamental. There's also a conceptual tension I think is underexplored: treating fundamentality not as an absolute metaphysical status but as indexed to particular explanatory projects—call it the practice-relative fundamentality hypothesis. Which of these, or which others I'm neglecting, should actually absorb limited philosophical effort? Rank them with an explicit defense: for each, indicate how tractable it is with current conceptual tools rather than empirical science, which answers would unlock progress on adjacent questions, and what criteria—dependency, tractability, potential to reshape debates—justify the ordering. I want you to separate sharply what is genuinely contested among specialists from what is relatively settled, and not present any contested view, such as the univocality of grounding or the viability of practice-relative fundamentality, as if it were the consensus position.
