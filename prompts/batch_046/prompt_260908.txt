I am drafting a proposal for a six-month philosophy of cognitive science fellowship, and I need help sharpening a half-formed idea into a full research plan. In conversations with colleagues working on mechanistic interpretability, I have noticed that the debate over whether neural networks contain genuine representations tends to collapse into an unproductive binary: one camp points to feature directions recovered by sparse autoencoders as evidence that networks encode human-interpretable concepts, while the opposing camp cites polysemantic neurons and superposition as proof that these features are merely convenient descriptions of distributed causal dynamics with no real semantic content. I think the stalemate persists because both sides implicitly treat representation as an all-or-nothing property that requires both high semantic specificity—tracking a single, discrete concept—and high counterfactual stability—systematically altering model outputs across diverse contexts. My proposal is to reject this unitary conception and instead split representation in artificial systems into two gradable dimensions: counterfactual stability and semantic specificity. I then want to ask whether this two-dimensional framework can dissolve standard puzzle cases—such as feature directions that seem to encode both curved visual lines and Arabic script—while clarifying exactly which interpretability results should count as evidence for representational realism.

Please write the proposal to include the following: a precise statement of the central question; an account of why existing realist and eliminativist positions fail to resolve it; a clear methodological approach explaining how I would formalize these two dimensions using existing interpretability metrics, apply them to documented cases of superposition in large language models, and determine whether the framework actually adjudicates contested cases; an explicit description of what success and failure would look like; and a realistic scope assessment for a six-month project that involves conceptual analysis rather than original empirical experiments. Do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or literature review—I will handle references separately.
