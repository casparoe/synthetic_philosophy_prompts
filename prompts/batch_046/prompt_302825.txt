As a journalist writing about the philosophy of classification, I have been struck by a routine institutional practice that seems to carry an unexamined metaphysical burden. In both museum accession protocols and certain pharmaceutical regulatory frameworks, an item is assigned to a taxonomic category on the basis of two criteria applied jointly: its intrinsic kind, and an observable but ostensibly non-essential property such as its material composition or its colour in solution. The result is that a carved figurine is catalogued not under equine sculpture but under ivory horse, or that a compound is registered as white crystalline substance A rather than as substance A alone, with the descriptive modifier treated as determinative for filing and retrieval purposes even though it is acknowledged to be accidental to the item’s function or chemical identity. This habit of allowing an adjective to split a kind into separate institutional entities echoes the old debate in the School of Names — particularly Gongsun Long’s argument that a white horse is not a horse — yet it is carried out daily by curators and regulators who have never heard of Mingjia metaphysics. What theory of universals and particularity does this practice actually presuppose? Does it commit its practitioners to the view that attributes like colour or material are separable universals that can be added or subtracted without altering the underlying kind, or does it presuppose a holistic ontology in which the compound name refers to an indivisible particular? Reconstruct the implicit metaphysics of classification at work here, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely fits it well, say whether it is philosophically coherent or whether it quietly oscillates between a separation of names and realities reminiscent of Gongsun Long and a more integrated view of named things, and tell me what would have to change in these institutional routines if the underlying commitment were replaced by an ontology in which every descriptive attribute is treated as essential to the identity of the thing named.
