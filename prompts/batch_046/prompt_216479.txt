I've been working through some philosophy of science on my own—mostly Dennett's idea of cranes versus skyhooks, and the worry about greedy reductionism that explains away the very thing you're trying to understand rather than building it up from simpler parts. I got interested in whether modern neuroscience falls into this trap, particularly with hierarchical predictive processing, where the brain is said to minimize prediction error across nested levels to generate perception and cognition. It strikes me that calling lower-level signals "predictions" and "errors" might already smuggle in intentional content—aboutness and normativity—right at the base machinery, which would make the framework a skyhook disguised as mechanistic explanation. I want to test whether I really grasp the distinction, so I'm drafting a self-study exam question for myself and a couple of friends who are also reading outside any formal program.
