For a graduate seminar on formal systems and self-reference, draft a take-home essay question that uses Hofstadter’s framework of JOOTSing and the MU-puzzle but cannot be answered by reciting his exposition. Introduce a modified formal system—call it MUI—in which the standard MIU derivation rules hold except that rule 4 (doubling) applies only to strings ending in U, and a new rule 5 permits replacing III with U only when the string contains an even number of I’s. Ask students whether a proof that MU is not derivable in MUI can be constructed inside MUI itself, and whether recognizing the parity invariant forces an observer to jump outside the system. The question should require them to distinguish object-level derivation from meta-level invariant-checking and to evaluate whether JOOTSing remains necessary if the invariant is encoded in an extended but still internal notation. Provide the exam question, a model answer of approximately 700 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 points that separates first-class from upper-second responses by naming three required conceptual moves: correctly identifying that rule 5 does not internalize the parity check within the object-language; explaining why the non-derivability proof requires an external invariant; and assessing whether the thesis that understanding requires JOOTSing survives this modification. For each move, assign points and state the characteristic misconception that costs marks—specifically, conflating the parity condition with an ordinary derivation rule, treating meta-observation as just another string transformation, and assuming that notation-extension eliminates the need for a system-external perspective.
