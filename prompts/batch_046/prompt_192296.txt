In my AI ethics club we've been arguing for months about whether we should supervise AI by grading its step-by-step reasoning or just its final answer. The process people say outcome-only lets models hide bad logic inside correct-looking intermediate steps — like steganographic scratchpads — and the outcome people say grading every step is to slow and just trains models to fake honest reasoning. Everyones agrees reward hacking is bad and we want truthful models, but nobody has budged. I think the stall might be that we're using 'supervision' to mean two different things: checking visible token chains versus checking some hidden mental state. But is that right, or is there a real empirical disagreement about whether we can ever observe internal computation? Or are we just stuck on bedrock intuitions about trust? Give me a real diagnosis — is it verbal, substantive, or are we answering different questions — and tell me what, if anything, could actually move it: a formal result on observability, a new interpretability experiment, or just admitting the question is ill-posed?
