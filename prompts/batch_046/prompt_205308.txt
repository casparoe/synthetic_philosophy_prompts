After a long argument with a friend about Weber’s Protestant Ethic thesis, I am trying to pinpoint exactly where her challenge misfires. She insisted that unless Protestant ascetic values can be shown to exercise downward causal power over economic behavior—operating like an emergent mental property acting on its neural substrate—Weber is merely offering a post-hoc narrative that neuroscience and rational-choice economics can fully replace. I objected that Weber never intended law-like efficient causation of that sort, but she took this as an admission that values play no genuine explanatory role at all. Does her demand rest on a false presupposition about what adequate causation in the cultural sciences requires? Specifically, is she conflating interpretive understanding with natural-scientific causation, and can Weber’s notion of elective affinity provide a genuinely explanatory middle ground that avoids both reductionism and the need for downward causation?
