As a novelist drafting a philosophical scene in which two scholars have argued for years without convergence, I need an adversarial-collaboration document they could both sign—something precise enough to serve as an appendix to my manuscript, yet not so technical that it collapses into a literature review. The two camps are the Malcolmians, who maintain that phenomenal consciousness is suspended during sleep and that dream reports are retrospective constructions lacking any prior experiential substrate, and the Dennettians, who maintain that dreams are not direct perceptions of an internal world but are narrative-cognitive events assembled at or near awakening, possessing presentational reality without being literal experiences during sleep itself. Please draft their joint statement: begin with a numbered section of claims both sides endorse—concerning REM neurophysiology, the unreliability of retrospective report, and the absence of any direct behavioral verification of dream content during sleep—then present the remaining disagreements in a structured, neutral format, phrased so that each camp recognizes its own position exactly, particularly whether the absence of narrative consolidation during sleep entails the absence of phenomenal presence altogether, and whether a report can be veridical about a mental state that was never experientially present.

Next, identify the cruxes—those sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side—including, first, whether phenomenal content can exist without retrospective narrative integration, and second, whether pre-awakening neural signatures can constitute evidence for experience in the absence of any accessible first-person trace. For each crux, specify a concrete case or evidence both camps agree bears upon it: I have in mind a precisely described protocol in which a sleeper is awakened by an auditory cue delivered at a predetermined REM latency and immediately asked to report any preceding mental content, with concurrent EEG tracking of narrative-consolidation markers, and I want each camp to record in advance what correlation—if any—they predict between report richness and consolidation signatures, together with what pattern of absence or presence would genuinely revise their view. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; avoid caricaturing either position; and keep the language formal and balanced enough that both parties could place their names beneath it without reservation.
