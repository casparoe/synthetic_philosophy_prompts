Exam question: Explain Scanlon's contractualist account of why promises bind. Then evaluate the 'moral escrow' idea — that promises don't create immediate obligations but instead deposit them in a conditional holding pattern activated only when specific conditions are satisfied. Identify one real weakness of this escrow model compared to Scanlon's approach.

Student answer:
Scanlon argues that promises bind because breaking them wrongs the promisee in a way that can't be justified by principles no one could reasonably reject. When I promise you I'll drive you to the airport, I create a legitimate expectation. If I flake, I'm treating you as someone whose interests dont count equally, which violates principle F. Its not about social utility or keeping a cooperative system running — its about the direct moral relationship between promiser and promisee.

The moral escrow model says obligations are suspended until conditions trigger them, like putting money with a lawyer. The weakness is that promises usually require a third-party trustee to hold the obligation in abeyance, but promises are only between two people so theres no mediator. Also, Scanlon's view actually allows conditions inside promises, so the escrow model is redundant. In fact, Scanlon's contractualism is really just a version of Kant's categorical imperative applied to interpersonal justification, so both views end up saying obligations depend on universalizable maxims.
