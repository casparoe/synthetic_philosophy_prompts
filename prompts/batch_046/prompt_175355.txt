I am a novelist developing a speculative narrative about artificial cognition, and during a recent philosophy-of-mind seminar I was given a summary of the frame problem that strikes me as suspiciously tidy. The account, attributed to an unnamed syllabus, states that the frame problem—first articulated in McCarthy and Hayes's 1969 work on situation calculus—is essentially the puzzle of how an agent knows which world-properties remain unchanged after an action; if a robotic arm moves a ceramic cup from table to shelf, the system must update location beliefs but also preserve beliefs about color, weight, and fragility, plus the shelf's stability, yet because unchanged properties are indefinitely numerous, explicit logical representation demands an unmanageable explosion of frame axioms and precondition checks, which is why symbolic AI researchers abandoned rule-based reasoning entirely. The summary continues that philosophers such as Dennett reconceived this failure as proof that human cognition relies on holistic, narrative framing rather than deduction, and that connectionist networks eventually solved the problem by learning statistical patterns instead of encoding axioms; indeed, it claims that contemporary cognitive science now largely treats the frame problem as a special case of predictive coding, in which the brain suppresses prediction errors about stable features rather than computing explicit non-change rules. Please adopt the persona of an expert philosopher of artificial intelligence and cognitive science, begin with a brief mental inventory of the summary's separate claims, think carefully about where technical terms are being used in ordinary senses, and then evaluate whether this characterization is fair; present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion, distinguishing accurate descriptions, common simplifications, subtle misrepresentations of the original debate, and whether the predictive-coding interpretation genuinely resolves the problem or merely relocates it.
