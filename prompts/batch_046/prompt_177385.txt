Since I recently began advising a regulatory body on the governance of large language models, I have become convinced that mechanistic interpretability has dissolved the philosophical problem of representation in artificial systems. The identification of discrete, localised feature directions—isolated via sparse autoencoder methods—shows that hidden-layer states correspond determinately to specific concepts, which surely means these networks possess literal intentional content rather than mere functional correlation. If representational realism is therefore empirically established, why do philosophers of mind still speak of “aboutness” as if it were a mysterious property that artificial systems might lack? And given that we can now observe and edit these representational states directly, should we not treat attributions of false belief or misrepresentation to an AI as literally true descriptions rather than metaphorical shorthand?
