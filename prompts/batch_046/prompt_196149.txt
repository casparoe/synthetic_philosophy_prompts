I have recently begun mentoring surgical residents in a particular laparoscopic suturing technique, and I have noticed a recurring pattern that does not quite align with the conceptual distinctions I have encountered in philosophy of mind. Among experienced surgeons, some can execute the maneuver reliably and fluently; when asked to explain their method, they can produce explicit, accurate rules about hand position, needle angle, and tension sequencing. Yet residents who learn primarily through these articulated rules consistently perform worse than those who acquire the skill through guided repetition with minimal verbal instruction. Even more striking is what happens when the expert herself attempts to perform the maneuver while consciously adhering to her own stated rules: her execution becomes slower, more rigid, and less accurate than her usual intuitive performance. The explicit formulation is therefore not merely an incomplete rendering of the practical knowledge; in both pedagogy and self-application, it appears to actively undermine the very competence it describes.

I am familiar with Ryle's distinction between knowing how and knowing that, with discussions of tacit knowledge, and with the Dreyfus account of skill acquisition, but none of these frameworks seem to isolate this precise configuration, in which propositional articulation is both genuinely available and genuinely obstructive. Does this phenomenon—where accurate explicit description coexists with, yet interferes with, embodied mastery—have an established name? If not, how should it be carved apart from ordinary tacit knowledge, from the mere difficulty of translating embodied skill into language, and from cases in which explicit knowledge genuinely supports rather than hinders practice? I am less interested in a bare label than in understanding the boundaries of the concept: what conditions must hold for practical and propositional knowledge to stand in this antagonistic relation, and are there recognized boundary cases where the relation shifts from interference to cooperation?
