hey so family law treats adoption, IVF surrogacy, and donor-embryo births in ways that dont line up. in adoption the genetic parents are cut off completely once papers are signed, but in surrogacy the genetic parents keep rights even though someone else gestated. with donor embryos the gestational mother is the legal mom but the donors have zero claim. what view of personal origin is hiding behind that patchwork? does it presuppose youre essentially tied to your genetic origin unless you contract it away, or that bodily labor creates a different essential link? reconstruct the implicit theory, check whether its actually consistent across those three cases, and say what'd have to change in the law if we replaced it with strict origin essentialism — the idea that you literally could not have come from different sperm and egg and still be the same person.
