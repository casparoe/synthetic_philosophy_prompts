I'm trying to sort out my own view on induction and Goodman's grue problem for a workshop, and I want to check if these commitments actually hang together or if I'm kidding myself.

Here's what I hold:

(a) Hume was basically right: there's no non-circular rational justification for extending observed patterns into the future. Induction is a psychological habit, not something reason endorses.

(b) Goodman's entrenchment fix for grue/bleen fails, because linguistic entrenchment is just a sociological fact about which predicates we've used a lot. It doesn't track any real metaphysical difference between properties.

(c) Still, I think we can rescue induction with something I call ecological tracking: we only project properties that our sensory apparatus has causally coupled with across evolutionary history. Green works because our retinas literally track wavelengths; grue fails because no evolved mechanism couples with a time-indexed disjunctive property.

(d) I take this to supply a principaled, non-ad-hoc boundary on projectibility — it's not just "stick to familiar stuff" but a constraint coming from embodied perceptual architecture.

(e) I also think reflective equilibrium is the wrong method for fixing inductive norms, since our intuitions about future cases are just outputs of the very evolved heurisitcs we're trying to justify.

(f) Even so, I want to say we genuinely know future regularities, but only in a deflationary pragmatist sense — there's no metaphysical inductive warrant floating around, just stable ecological coupling.

So: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only seem to be, and what's the least costly revision? Rank them by severity rather than treating everything as equally bad. Also, tell me what would change your mind — what argument or finding would make you abandon one of these?
