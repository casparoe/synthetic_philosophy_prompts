me and my roommate were arguing about Hume's standard of taste after our aesthetics seminar and we walked ourselves into what feels like a trap. here's the argument we ended up with, and i cant find the exit.

(1) Hume says beauty isnt in the object but in the sentiment of qualified observers - true judges with delicate sentiment, practice, comparison, and freedom from prejudice. (2) The standard of taste is just the convergent verdict of these true judges. (3) So if two people who both seem to meet all those criteria disagree about whether some specific work - say a minimalist sculpture - is beautiful, then according to the standard the work is both beautiful and not-beautiful, which is a contradiction. (4) Hume's usual escape is that one of them must have a hidden defect: maybe a subtle prejudice or a temporary flaw in their organ of taste. (5) But we can construct a symmetry case: Anna and Ben are equally trained, equally unbiased by any detectable measure, and they persistently disagree. There's no independent test for the hidden defect except that they disagree with the majority verdict - which is circular. (6) So to preserve the standard we either have to dismiss one arbitrarily, or admit that there's an objective feature they're both tracking and one is just wrong about it. (7) Now here's the niche move we added: maybe aesthetic judgments have what we called indexical leakage - "this is beautiful" secretly means "this is beautiful-relative-to-my-calibrated-frame." If so, Anna and Ben arent actually contradicting each other, they're just reporting different frame-relative facts. (8) But if every aesthetic judgment works this way, then there's no shared content for true judges to converge on. The standard of taste becomes just a statistical cluster of frame-relative reports, not an intersubjective norm. (9) So Hume is caught: either he keeps the intersubjective standard and needs an objective property in the object (giving up his core anti-rationalist claim), or he saves the anti-rationalist claim with indexical leakage and loses the intersubjective standard altogether, collapsing into relativism.

it feels like one of these steps is doing sneaky work, maybe the slide from disagreement-among-critics to contradiction, or maybe the indexical move in (7) is a false rescue. which step should i actually reject, and whats the price? also please say what would change your mind here - like what argument or observation would make you think the escape actually works or that the contradiction is only apparent - and flag where your reasoning is shakiest, since i'm trying to write this up for a class and dont want to look like i missed an obvious reply.
