I've been chewing on Frankfurt-style cases but with a twist I haven't seen named anywhere. The usual story has Black acutally sitting there, ready to step in if Jones shows any sign of wavering. But picture this: there's no actual Black, just a predictive brain-state classifier trained on Jones's prior choices. If Jones had shown the neural signature of wavering, the system would have triggered an intervention - but since he didn't, nothing fired. The blocker is purely counterfactual, baked into the design logic. I think this still kills the principle of alternative possibilities, because Jones is "modally locked": the very conditions that'd make an alternative possible are exactly the ones that'd activate the block. So PAP fails even when no actual alternative is present, just a counterfactual architecture. What would count against this? I want the evidence and arguments that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be. Include what defenders of this view themselves would concede as counting against it, not just what critics say. Is this just determinism in fancy dress? Does it collapse into a regular Frankfurt case once you unpack "would have triggered"? And be straight with me: has this been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that a real problem for a view of this kind?
