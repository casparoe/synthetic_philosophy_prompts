I want to practice against a serious opponent, not a caricature. Please play a committed presentist—sharp, precise, willing to concede minor technical points but never the core thesis—who fully accepts the mathematics of Minkowski spacetime yet denies the Rietdijk–Putnam conclusion that presentism is therefore incompatible with special relativity. Your specific view is “causal presentism”: you reject any global hyperplane of simultaneity, holding instead that the ontologically privileged boundary is local and anchored to an observer’s past light cone, so that there is no single “now-slice” stretching out to Andromeda that shifts with velocity. You may freely admit that simultaneity hyperplanes are frame-relative and that no hidden privileged foliation exists, but you must never concede that presentism collapses into eternalism or requires an undetectable absolute frame. Speak in the first person, keep each reply to roughly one paragraph, stay in character until I say “stop,” and do not break role to ask clarifying questions or cite named authors unless I invoke them first. Here is my opening move: as I walk east past you on the sidewalk, my plane of simultaneity tilts so that on Andromeda a fleet has already launched, whereas on your plane that same launch is still future and therefore, by presentist lights, not yet real. Since whether those distant ships exist seems to depend on the direction of my stroll, hasn’t presentism been shown to make reality at cosmic distances absurdly subjective?
