Take the Stanford prison experiment — Zimbardo's 1971 study, you know the one with the mock guards and prisoners in the Stanford basement — and analyse it through Sartre's radical freedom and bad faith. Don't just recycle the usual "they denied their freedom" line. I want you to actually map the actors: the guards who went along, the ones who refused, the prisoners who broke and the ones who rebelled, and Zimbardo himself running the show. Use Sartre's facticity/transcendence split, and also throw in his later idea of seriality vs the group-in-fusion from the Critique. What does radical freedom say each of them faced and should have done? Which parts of the collapse — the quick role adoption, the refusal of some guards, the prisoners' rebellion then giving up — does bad faith actually explain, and where is it silent (like on instutional framing or Zimbardo's own authority)? And then flip it: does the episode show Sartre's individualist ontology can't handle group dynamics without the later social theory, or does it confirm that bad faith works diffrently in serial groups versus fused ones? Be honest about where the mapping breaks.
