He then adds that democracy can only function when citizens share common notions that direct the state toward what is genuinely useful for the whole body, which suggests that democratic sovereignty depends on some prior agreement in adequate knowledge rather than on mere majoritarian aggregation.

What confuses me is the logical relationship between these two moves. Is Spinoza saying that democracy produces the shared adequate ideas that make it work, or is he saying that democracy is only possible among people who already possess them? If the former, the exclusions should be provisional and curable by education, yet he writes them as structural features of the democratic constitution. I also can't see how this squares with his metaphysical claim in the Ethics that reason is always the same in all people—if reason is uniform, then the capacity for adequate ideas should be universal, and the exclusions look like an arbitrary social filter rather than a philosophical one.

Can you help me understand this passage? Don't just present the two interpretations and ask me to pick; I want a committed reading.
