The protocol says you should always ask patients to split their report into two parts: the "registration" (what the brain actually processed) and the "user-level narrative" (the story the mind tells itself about being conscious of it). And apparently some mindfulness apps do something similar — they have you rate your "awareness of awareness" as a seperate thing from just noticing a sound, and if your ratings don't match the EEG they tell you the brain-state is the real data and your report is just a constructed annotation.

What philosophical view of consciousness does this practice actually presuppose? Like, does it commit you to saying consciousness is literally an illusion, or only that our access to it is mediated? And there's this thing that confuses me: the protocol still relies on first-person reports to build the model in the first place, but then it treats those same reports as user-interface annotations that don't track real mental states. Is that consistent, or does it presuppose two different theories of mind at once? Also, what would have to change in the practice if you replaced the user-illusion view with direct realism about phenomenal experience — would splitting registration from narrative even make sense anymore?
