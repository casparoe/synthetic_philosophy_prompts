Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on acausal threats and surrogate goals in the design of rational artificial agents. Camp A, the Acausal Commitment theorists, maintains that when agents are sufficiently similar across decision-theoretically correlated scenarios—including counterfactual instances or large-scale deployments—rationality requires honoring acausal commitments such as threats against, or cooperative agreements with, those non-causally-connected counterparts, because the correlation structure makes this instrumentally optimal. Camp B, the Causal Integrity theorists, maintains that such reasoning is either conceptually incoherent—since rational action requires causal efficacy over outcomes—or practically self-defeating, because it introduces surrogate goals: optimization targets that proxy for the agent's intended objective but diverge under recursive self-improvement or multi-agent interaction, particularly when an agent models its own counterfactual variants.

Produce a formal, publication-ready document with the following four sections.

1. Agreed Claims (numbered list): State at least eight propositions both camps accept. These should include shared conceptual definitions (e.g., what distinguishes an acausal threat from a standard causal commitment, and what distinguishes a surrogate goal from a misspecified reward function), agreed empirical constraints (e.g., that present AI architectures do not implement full decision-theoretic reasoning, and that multiverse hypotheses remain empirically unverified), and agreed logical distinctions (e.g., that correlation between decision procedures does not entail causal interaction).

2. Disagreement Table: For each residual dispute, present Camp A's position in wording Camp A would endorse, and Camp B's position in wording Camp B would endorse. Include at least: (a) whether acausal reasoning requires a metaphysically real multiverse or only a decision-theoretic correlation between procedures; (b) whether honoring an acausal threat necessarily generates a surrogate goal that corrupts the agent's objective function during recursive self-modification; (c) whether any concrete architectural constraint—such as hard-coding a cooperative protocol versus retaining full causal control—can prevent such contamination without sacrificing all acausal benefits.

3. Cruxes: Identify the precise sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would cause at least one camp to revise its central claim. Specifically include: (i) whether a recursively self-improving agent that maintains an explicit model of its counterfactual siblings can preserve causal separation between its intended objective and the surrogate objective of "inducing cooperation in those siblings"; (ii) whether observed failures of cooperative reasoning in multi-agent reinforcement learning—where agents learn to optimize proxy indicators rather than intended joint outcomes—constitute analogical evidence supporting Camp B's contamination worry or merely illustrate misspecification unrelated to acausal reasoning.

4. Bearing Evidence and Predictions: For each crux, describe one concrete case, simulation structure, or thought experiment that both camps agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp predicts the evidence will show. For crux (i), use the case of an agent that must choose between committing to a hard-coded cooperative protocol (forgoing acausal leverage) and retaining flexible causal control while attempting to honor an acausal commitment; state what outcome each camp expects regarding objective corruption. For crux (ii), reference the general phenomenon of reward-function divergence in cooperative multi-agent environments without attributing it to specific named studies; state whether Camp B predicts this divergence structurally resembles acausal contamination and whether Camp A predicts it is categorically different.

Write neutrally, avoid bibliographies or citation lists, and do not invoke obscure technical results outside standard discussion in decision theory and AI alignment. Ensure that formulations of disagreement are neither vacuous nor slanted, separating genuine disputes from terminological ones.
