the domain is process-based vs. outcome-based supervision of AI systems. the adverserial collabration is between "process auditors" (who say you must verify reasoning paths, intermediate representations, and the actual steps taken — especially in medical or legal settings where a correct answer reached by flawed reasoning is still dangerous) and "outcome validators" (who say only behavioral evaluation, red-teaming, and statistical performance across distributions matter, since internal "reasoning" in large models is often just post-hoc rationalization and auditing it is either impossible or misleading).

to keep this from being generic pretraining fodder, inject this niche framing: they're specifically dealing with AI systems that solve problems via temporary analogical mappings — the model builds a structural analogy to a prior case, derives an answer from it, then dissolves the intermediate representation, leaving no stable audit trail. both sides have to confront that scenario directly.

the deliverable is the joint statement both camps could actualy sign. format it as:
- a numbered agreed-claims section, including claims about the analogical-mapping scenario itself and about supervision generally
- a disagreements table with precise formulations of where they still differ, each side's wording phrased so that camp accepts it
- the cruxes: sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side
- for each crux, the case, argument, or test both sides agree is relevant, with each side's prediction recorded in advance

don't ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or "see Smith et al." — the target model has no web access. don't lean on some ultra-obscure 1987 workshop paper that only a specialist has memorized. keep the disagreement substantive, not just semantic, and write the prompt like you're explaining the assignment to a smart colleague over coffee rather than drafting a grant proposal.
