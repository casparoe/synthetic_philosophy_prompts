To keep the argument from collapsing into the usual well-worn debates about whether specific ideas "really" changed, inject this conceptual distinction: separate "intellectual content" (theories, concepts, methods) from "epistemic infrastructure" (who validates knowledge, under what patronage conditions, with what instruments, and for what audiences). Then lay out your argument in clear steps. Finally, be genuinely self-critical: state explicitly what kind of historical evidence—perhaps about patronage continuity, institutional overlap, or the persistence of clerical authority within early scientific societies—would cause you to weaken or abandon this position, and identify the single weakest inferential step in your reasoning.
