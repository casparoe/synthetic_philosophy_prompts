Our study group got into a fight about whether cancer is a "disease" the same way a broken bone is, and the prof threw out Boorse and Wakefield but it all felt like rehearsing old lines. So I invented this hypothetical clinical grading system I'm calling adaptive dysfunction grading, or ADG. Under ADG, you only call something pathological if there's both (a) a measurable deviation from the species-typical functional baseline, and (b) that deviation gets picked up by the organism's own homeostatic monitors as a threat-state — regardless of whether it actually hurts survival or reproduction. So a silent tumor with no immune signaling wouldn't be a disease, but a harmless mutation that keeps firing stress pathways would be.
