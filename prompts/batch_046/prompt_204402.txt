After a career in experimental physics, I have recently begun formal study in philosophy of science and am preparing a one-page proposal for a semester-length independent research fellowship. My question concerns whether applied scientific practice reveals a distinct third relation between explanatory levels—beyond standard reduction and emergence—that I tentatively call calibration coupling: cases in multiscale modeling where macro-scale descriptions neither derive micro-scale dynamics (so not reduction) nor exercise novel downward causal powers (so not emergence), but instead serve as selection constraints on micro-parameters justified solely by reproducing observed macro-behavior. Write the proposal with a precise statement of this question; an explanation of why the standard philosophical dichotomy, focused largely on theoretical derivation and causal autonomy, leaves this practice unsettled; a conceptual method centered on close analysis of parameterization in climate and materials modeling rather than empirical laboratory work; a clear account of what success would look like (a rigorous formal distinction of calibration coupling from both standard categories) and what failure would look like (a demonstration that the practice collapses into pragmatic reduction or weak emergence); and an honest assessment of whether this can be completed in one semester without original data collection or exhaustive historical scholarship. Do not include a bibliography or citation list, as I will supply those separately, and maintain a formal, polished register appropriate to an academic grant application.
