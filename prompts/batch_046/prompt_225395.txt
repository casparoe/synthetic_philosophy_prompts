Can you explain why the usual quantifier-variance story about ontological disputes feels too tidy in practice? Take cases where both sides share the same logic and translation rules, yet still disagree over whether a scattered fusion - say, my left shoe plus the Eifel Tower - counts as one thing. They don't seem to be fighting over the meaning of 'exists', or over which sentences are true; they agree on all that. Instead it looks like there's a hidden parameter, maybe a threshold of ontological commitment, that each side treats as self-evident but can't provide a defence for without circularity. Is that a verbal dispute in Hirsch's sense, a substantive one in Sider's, or something weirder? I want a gut-level explanation of where the disagreement actually lives, not a literature survey.
