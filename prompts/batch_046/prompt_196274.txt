I'm drafting a scene for my novel — there's this underground philosphy salon in a smoky backrom in Prague, all bad wine and worse arguments, and two characters keep circling whether consequentialism asks too much of us. I want a decision tree or taxonomy for the demandingness objections that branches on actual claims, not just philosopher names, so readers could sort themselves by answering questions. Start with whether the problem is that the theory demands excessive sacrifice compared to other moral views, or whether it collapses the distinction between what's required and what's supererogatory altogether. From there split on whether you think the fix is adding an agent-cost threshold, restricting morality to negative duties only, or biting the bullet that our intuitions are just lazy and biased toward ourselves. At each leaf put the view's name if there is one — like the agent-centered prerogative position, or unnamed — plus one sentence on its most awkward commitement, like having to call saving a drowning child merely heroic. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate, and note any empty branch where a moderate view should sit but nobody defends it. Every node should be phrased as a concrete question someone can answer without knowing the jargon in advance. Oh, and please don't include any bibliographies or reading lists — my character wouldn't carry one, and neither should this.
