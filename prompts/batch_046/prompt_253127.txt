I'm putting together a feature on free will debates and got stuck on Galen Strawson's basic argument — specifically I think I pushed it past its breaking point. My study partner and I sketched this out over coffe with bad wifi. (1) To be genuinely morally responsible for doing X, you have to be responsible for the mental nature — your character, reasoning patterns, values — that actually produced X. (2) But you can't be responsible for that mental nature unless you intentionally shaped it, which already requires having some mental nature in place to do the shaping. (3) So you can never be causa sui, and true moral responsibility for actions collapses — that's Strawson's core point. (4) But here's where I went further: if you're not responsible for your mental nature, then you're also not responsible for any attempt to change or improve it, because those attempts are just more outputs of the very nature you didn't choose. (5) That means therapy, moral education, even deciding to think more carefully about your biases, aren't things anyone does responsibly — they just happen, like digestion. (6) Yet in practice we praise people for working on themselves and blame people who refuse to grow, which only makes sense if self-correction can be a responsible act. (7) So the argument seems to prove not just that we're never blameworthy, but that the whole idea of taking ownership over your own development is an illusion. That feels like it proves too much. Where does the chain slip? I'd rather keep (1) through (3) intact if possible, and I'm genuinely unsure whether the problem is in (4) — maybe there's a difference between being responsible for your mental nature and being responsible for directing it — or in (6), where we might be conflating moral responsibility with something thinner like rational agency. Can you walk me through which step to reject and what that costs? And please keep settled stuff separate from what's actually contested among philosophers who work on this — don't present one camp's reading of Strawson as if it's the consensus.
