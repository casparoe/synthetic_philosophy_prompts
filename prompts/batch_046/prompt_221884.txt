I am a retired experimental physicist now studying philosophy of mind and science, and I am drafting a one-page proposal for a six-month workshop fellowship. Rather than rehearse the familiar dispute over whether cognition can causally penetrate visual experience, I want to ask whether the debate is misframed by its implicit directional model. I propose treating so-called penetration not as contamination of perception by belief, but as instrumental co-calibration: the perceptual system updates internal parameters—functionally analogous to calibration constants in physical measurement—using information that is cognitive in origin yet serves to preserve, rather than corrupt, accuracy.

Please write the proposal. State the precise question: does this co-calibration framework dissolve the standard epistemic objection that penetrated perception forfeits its evidential authority, or does it merely relocate the problem into the dynamics of parameter-fixing? Explain why existing modularist and top-down accounts leave this open, since both presuppose a unidirectional influence model rather than an integrated calibration relation. Describe the method: apply the framework to two concrete cases—color constancy under non-standard illumination, and expert pattern discrimination in microscopy—comparing explanatory outcomes against standard Bayesian and encapsulation treatments. Specify what a useful result would look like (a taxonomy that distinguishes calibration-like integration from genuine perceptual distortion) and what would count as failure (if the analogy collapses into either pure modularity or unconstrained holism). Give an honest assessment of why this is tractable within one semester and what would make it too large. Please begin the response with the proposal text itself, with no preface or commentary.
