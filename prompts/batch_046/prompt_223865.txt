Compare the Buddhist doctrine of anatta with Derek Parfit's reductionist theory of personal identity, but avoid treating them as interchangeable "bundle theories." Focus specifically on moral responsibility for actions performed years earlier: how does each framework justify holding an agent accountable without a persisting metaphysical self? In your comparison, distinguish the Abhidharma analysis of momentary mental streams sustained by karmic continuity from the Madhyamaka claim that even such streams lack intrinsic nature (svabhāva). Then ask whether Parfit's psychological-continuity criterion operates at the level of conventional truth (saṃvṛtisatya) while the Madhyamaka denial of svabhāva operates at ultimate truth (paramārthasatya), and whether that two-truths framing resolves or deepens the tension between anatta and ordinary practices of praise and blame. Reach and defend a clear verdict on which account offers the more coherent explanation of responsibility without a substantial self.
