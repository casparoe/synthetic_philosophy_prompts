I have been drafting a novel centered on a quantitative hedonist philosopher who, when confronted with the obvious qualitative differences between intellectual satisfaction and bodily comfort, repeats the maxim that "all pleasure is one thing, however it arrives," and uses it to dismiss any distinction between the pleasure of solving an equation and the pleasure of sunlight warming one's skin. During a recent discussion with my writing group, one friend insisted the claim is empirically false—no subject could genuinely confuse those two states—while another argued it is analytically true, since both experiences are called pleasures by linguistic convention alone. I am skeptical that either response captures what my character actually needs, particularly because the novel's central tension depends on the heterogeneity problem: if pleasures share nothing beyond being pleasant, then in what sense can they be aggregated into a single measurable quantity of well-being, and does the slogan genuinely defend that aggregation?

Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that this slogan might be expressing, arranged from the trivial to the radical? For each reading, indicate whether it is primarily a conceptual claim about the term "pleasure," a phenomenological claim about the intrinsic feel of pleasant experiences, a normative claim about the commensurability of goods, or a metaphysical claim about the underlying nature of affective states. Then, for each interpretation, say whether it is defensible, plainly false, or merely ambiguous, and—most importantly—specify which readings would actually support a quantitative hedonism that treats every pleasant experience as interchangeable currency in a calculus of welfare. I need this as a menu rather than a single verdict, since my aim is to calibrate my character's position without reducing him to a strawman; please show the full range, flag where each reading stands with respect to the classic heterogeneity objection, and tell me which interpretation he would need to be invoking for his dismissal of qualitative distinctions to carry any philosophical weight rather than functioning as mere rhetorical closure.
