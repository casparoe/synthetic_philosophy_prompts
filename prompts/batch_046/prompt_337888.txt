Write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—an Abhidhamma reductionist who holds that a person is strictly a causal stream of momentary dharmas with no enduring substrate, and a Madhyamaka constructivist who holds that the self is dependently originated, conventionally functional, but ultimately empty—debating the following case: a long-term meditator has, over decades, systematically dissolved the narrative structures that ordinarily bind experience into a continuous ego, such that the present mental continuum bears almost no psychological resemblance to the agent who committed a serious harm thirty years prior; nevertheless, karmic effects are said to ripen in this very continuum. The reductionist should argue that without an enduring subject there is no one to bear responsibility, only a causal chain to describe. The constructivist should argue that conventional moral practice requires a conventionally real, narratively continuous agent, and that denying this collapses ethical discourse into mere causal reporting. Both must engage the two-truths framework (sammuti-sacca and paramattha-sacca) and respond directly to each other’s claims about whether moral responsibility can be distributed across a skandha-stream without a stable subject. Include a moment where one speaker modifies their position after a direct objection. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or external sources; build the arguments solely from the internal logic of these Buddhist metaphysical positions.
