I am defending a job-market paper that argues the standard inefficacy objection to individual climate and dietary choices rests on a mistaken linear-causal aggregation model. I propose instead a threshold-governed institutional-signaling framework: collective harms like climate damage or factory farming are structurally reproduced not by the marginal causal effect of any single action, but by a social practice of normalized complicity that remains legitimate only so long as participation stays visibly widespread. On my view, opting out is not morally significant because it causally reduces harm—indeed, the marginal reduction is negligible—but because it refuses the social role of co-contributor that sustains the institutional framework. I call this refusal deontic disengagement, and I claim it is obligatory regardless of causal efficacy, provided the practice crosses a perceptual visibility threshold that makes individual withdrawal socially legible as dissent rather than invisible statistical noise.
