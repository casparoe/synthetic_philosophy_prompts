Consider a philosopher of mind, Dr. E. Voss, who has evaluated a sequence of hypothetical anti-mechanist arguments in the Gödelian tradition—specifically, arguments that attempt to show a reasoner is not equivalent to any formal system by appealing to that reasoner’s ability to recognize the truth of a Gödel sentence that the system cannot prove. Her verdicts on four prior scenarios are as follows.

First, in the canonical Lucas-Penrose setup: a consistent, recursively axiomatizable formal system F is presented, and a human mathematician understands F, accepts its consistency on independent grounds, and asserts that the Gödel sentence G_F is true. Dr. Voss judges the anti-mechanist argument valid.

Second, in an inconsistency-variant: the target system is known to contain a hidden contradiction, and the reasoner nevertheless claims to see a corresponding Gödel-like sentence as true. Dr. Voss judges the argument invalid.

Third, in a mechanization-variant: a computer program is constructed that, given the description of any consistent system, mechanically generates and verifies the corresponding Gödel sentence in exponential time. Dr. Voss judges that this does not support anti-mechanism; the program is still a formal system, merely slower.

Fourth, in an escalation-variant: the reasoner sees G_F, so they are at least as strong as F augmented by the consistency statement; but this stronger system has its own Gödel sentence, and so on indefinitely. Dr. Voss judges that this undermines any claim to absolute non-mechanizability, leaving only an infinite hierarchy.

Now consider a new case. A cognitive scientist proposes that a particular human mathematician, call her R, is fully formalizable as a specific system R*—the mapping is supported by neurocomputational modeling—yet R correctly asserts the Gödel sentence for R* without any independent, external proof of R*’s consistency being available; R’s assurance is the only basis for accepting that R* is consistent. The scientist asks whether this demonstrates that R exceeds R*.

Predict Dr. Voss’s verdict on this new case. Then identify the two competing principles that could both explain her four prior judgments: one that makes the argument depend on an independent consistency guarantee combined with non-formalizability at the same strength (call it the Independence-Formalization rule), and one that makes it depend on a direct, non-algorithmic faculty for recognizing arithmetic truths regardless of formalization or consistency proofs (call it the Direct-Access rule). Say which of these she is implicitly running, with reasons drawn from the pattern above. Finally, specify one further hypothetical case—distinct from the four listed and from the new one—that would decisively adjudicate between the Independence-Formalization rule and the Direct-Access rule, explaining what each rule predicts about that case and why the outcome would settle the matter.
