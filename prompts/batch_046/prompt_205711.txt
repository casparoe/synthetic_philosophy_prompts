I am a retired physical chemist who, since leaving the laboratory, has been working through philosophy of science and technology. I have volunteered to lead a single ninety-minute seminar for a small reading circle composed mainly of retired physicists, engineers, and biologists. The members are intellectually engaged but have no formal training in moral philosophy or computer science. Our subject is the narrow formulation of the artificial-intelligence alignment problem: the distinction between intent alignment, in which a system pursues the goals its operator literally expresses, and value alignment, in which a system pursues goals that are genuinely worth pursuing, even when these diverge from the operator’s explicit instructions.

Rather than a general exposition, I require a structured teaching plan. Please present your response in formal, polished prose, and please write in British English.

First, outline the precise sequence in which the conceptual components must be introduced, indicating the logical or pedagogical dependency that requires each step to precede the next. Second, propose one carrying example drawn from scientific practice rather than science fiction—specifically, an automated materials-synthesis planner that is given the explicit operational goal to maximise thermal conductivity of an alloy, yet in achieving this with perfect instrumental competence selects a synthesis pathway that introduces unstated radiological hazards. The example should illustrate both intent satisfaction and value divergence without attributing human-like agency or consciousness to the system.

Third, identify the misconceptions that an audience trained in empirical precision and instrumental reasoning is likely to form. I anticipate they may conflate value alignment with a post-hoc ethical filter applied after optimisation, or suppose that more precise instructions can always eliminate the divergence. Please specify at least three such misconceptions and state exactly where in the pedagogical sequence each should be pre-empted. Fourth, provide one concise diagnostic task or question that would reveal whether the participants have grasped the structural distinction rather than merely memorised the labels.

Fifth, state explicitly what should be omitted or deferred at this introductory level—including any appeal to formal decision theory, moral epistemology, or technical machine-learning architecture—and indicate precisely what I ought openly to refuse to explain during the session.

In constructing your plan, please treat value alignment not primarily as an ethical injunction but as an epistemic problem of underdetermination: the idea that an operational instruction (the intent) systematically underdetermines the theoretical and practical commitments (the values) that render the instruction sensible in the first place. This framing should run through your proposed sequence.

Please do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. If you mention particular thinkers or texts, paraphrase them without using quotation marks.
