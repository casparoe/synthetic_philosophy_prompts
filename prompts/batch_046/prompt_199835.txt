In our seminar on self-reference and formal representation, we have been working through Hofstadter's framework from Gödel, Escher, Bach in which meaning is distributed across a frame message (the decoding rules), an outer message (the literal symbol sequence), and an inner message (the intended interpretation). I am trying to grasp the precise conceptual difficulty that arises when a formal system attempts to internalize its own frame message—that is, when the syntactic rules governing interpretation are themselves encoded as theorems within the inner message layer. Does this necessarily collapse the meta-level distinction between frame and content, producing a strange loop, or is there a coherent way to preserve the separation? Please explain the conceptual tension involved, with particular attention to whether self-reference here is a feature of the encoding or an unavoidable artifact of any system that represents its own syntax.
