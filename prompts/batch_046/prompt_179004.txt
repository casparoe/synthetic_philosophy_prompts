Consider the color phi illusion, in which a second colored disc presented shortly after a first seems to retroactively change the perceived color of the first disc before the second arrives. Some discussions of the timing of conscious experience analogize this postdictive effect to historical revisionism: an Orwellian revision, where the earlier percept is seamlessly replaced leaving no trace, versus a Stalinesque revision, where the original record technically persists but is obscured, leaving potential inconsistencies. If perceptual revision were Stalinesque rather than Orwellian, should we not expect careful introspection or closer phenomenological attention to reveal some residual trace of the first disc's original color? And since no such trace is ever reported, does this not establish that the revision must be genuinely Orwellian?
