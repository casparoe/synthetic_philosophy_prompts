I serve on the Review Board of the National Historical Accountability Archive, which oversees the legacy of a transitional-justice process that granted conditional amnesty to perpetrators in exchange for full disclosure of command-level abuses. A recent forensic audit suggests that a substantial subset of the sealed testimonies contain material omissions about organizational hierarchy, though no demonstrable false statements. The Board must decide whether to maintain the permanent seal, authorize limited scholarly access with structural redactions, or refer the omissions to a prosecutorial review panel. Draft a formal decision memo recommending one course of action. Your memo should include explicit criteria for distinguishing material omission from false testimony under the original amnesty agreement, and it should treat honestly the strongest argument for preserving the seal—namely, that reopening the archive undermines the epistemic trust conditions that made voluntary disclosure possible and risks substituting prosecutorial precision for the moral closure that transitional mechanisms are designed to provide. The recommendation should be actionable and address how reviewers on the losing side could respond to the reasoning.
