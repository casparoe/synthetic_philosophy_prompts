A fellow hobbyist recently mentioned that legal scholars argue about whether judges should follow the plain words of a statute or the purpose behind it, and I recognized the shape of my workshop problem. I do not know the formal academic terms, but I want to understand those two legal positions through the only framework I know well.

Please take the debate between textualism and purposivism in statutory interpretation and restate both positions entirely in the vocabulary of vintage radio repair, using the specific practices I deal with: comparing printed schematic values against drifted measured values, interpreting a builder's handwritten margin notes, deciding whether to replace a failed resistor with a modern equivalent that functions correctly but looks anachronistic, or to restuff the original shell so the chassis looks authentic while hiding a new part inside. For each legal position, show precisely how a repairer operating under that philosophy would approach a circuit where the diagram, the builder's notes, and the actual electrical behavior all conflict. Then give me a residue report: which legal claims translate cleanly into repair logic, which require distorting either the legal idea or the craft to make them fit, and which have no expression at all in the workshop—for instance, the idea that a schematic could carry authoritative weight even if every builder and every functioning radio in existence contradicts it.
