Please respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion: (1) explain why eliminating craving is structurally different from merely eliminating suffering, and how this distinction is meant to avoid the familiar "happy slave" objection to negative utilitarianism; (2) clarify whether tranquilism can consistently allow for what I will call "instrumental preference"—goal-directed behavior that lacks any affective investment—without smuggling positive value back into the account; (3) address the charge that a world without craving is conceptually indistinguishable from a world without agents, given that agency is typically defined in terms of desire; (4) distinguish "therapeutic tranquility," which removes suffering after it arises, from "architectural tranquility," which removes the conditions that generate craving altogether, and state which version the view actually requires; and (5) say whether tranquilism implies that the only rational life-plan is total withdrawal, or whether there is a coherent account of engaged, non-craving action—such as maintaining open-source infrastructure or writing precise algorithms—without internal contradiction.
