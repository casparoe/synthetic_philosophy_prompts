The Bronze Age crisis is the classic example of institutional decoupling: palace economies at Mycenae, Ugarit, and Hattusa dissolved, Linear B literacy disappeared, and long-distance bronze trade collapsed, yet agrarian populations in the Levant and Aegean persisted in smaller settlements. The Classic Maya lowlands show the same pattern—divine kingship, monument building, and calendrical ritual ended—but this was purely institutional decoupling without meaningful demographic contraction, since people simply moved north and into the highlands with little net loss. Together, these cases prove that collapse is a misleading label: failure is always institutional decoupling or adaptive restructuring, and true demographic contraction is confined to peripheral zones.
