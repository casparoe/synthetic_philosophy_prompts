hey for my disertation prospectus i'm comparing two angles on the paradox of deontology. first there's the classic agregation worry—if it's wrong to kill one to save five, is it also wrong to kill one to save a million, and where's the cutoff? second, there's the view that rights violations are non-fungible goods, so they don't aggregate at all. that blocks the scale problem but creates what i want to call a distributive paradox: if harm is unavoidable and rights can't be summed, how do you decide who bears the unavoidable violation? are these actually different paradoxes, or is the non-fungibility move just relocating the same tension into allocation? specifically, does treating rights as non-aggregable solve anything, or does it just hide the problem?
