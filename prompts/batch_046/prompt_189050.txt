She presented it like this: Gödel's first incompleteness theorem establishes that any consistent formal system rich enough to encode arithmetic contains true statements it cannot derive, while his second theorem establishes that such a system can never prove its own consistency. From these results, she said, one can derive Lucas's and Penrose's anti-mechanist conclusion: since human mathematicians appear able to recognize both Gödel sentences for arithmetic and the consistency of Peano arithmetic itself, human cognition must transcend any single formal system. She then extended the argument in two directions. First, because scientific theories can be expressed in formal languages, Gödel's results imply that every scientific framework will contain empirically true predictions it can never generate, meaning science is permanently and systematically incomplete regardless of empirical progress. Second, she applied this to artificial intelligence: any self-improving algorithm formalized within arithmetic will eventually confront an undecidable safety condition—specifically, it will be unable to prove that its own next modification preserves consistency—so fully autonomous AI alignment is not merely practically difficult but logically impossible. She concluded that Gödel therefore simultaneously refutes computational theories of mind, sets an in-principle boundary on scientific completeness, and undermines the very project of safe machine autonomy.

Something about this feels like it blends genuine logical results with overreaching philosophical conclusions, but I am not sure where the line falls. Could you audit this summary with care? Please distinguish what is technically accurate about the two theorems themselves, what counts as a legitimate but contested philosophical extension, and what depends on subtle conflations—such as treating the first and second theorems interchangeably, equating "formal system" with "any rule-governed process whatsoever," or sliding from syntactic undecidability to empirical unpredictability. Also indicate whether the conclusions about scientific incompleteness and AI safety actually follow from Gödel's results once hidden premises are exposed, or whether they collapse into misapplication. I am not asking for citations or a reading list; I just want to know which parts of her account I can trust and where it goes wrong.
