I teach a grad seminar on colonial legacies in museums, 10 people, 90 min. I want them to actually feel why returning the Benin Bronzes or Parthenon Marbles gets philosophically weird once you take seriously that museum life changes the object — conservation chemicals, display lighting, digital archives, provenance files. Design a role-play where the group has to decide, piece by piece, what exactly crosses back: just the bronze? the conservation report? the high-res scans? Give me roles (maybe origin-community reps, museum curators, conservators, legal claimants), clear rules with small paper-token stakes, a 20-min timeline, then a debrief that maps each decision onto the idea that restitution might be returning a hybrid thing, not the original. Tell me what will predicatbly break the first time I run it, and be honest about which real-world feature — like actual Nigerian/British law or the British Museum Act — this activty just can't reproduce. No lecture up front; they should hit the contradiction themselves before I name it.
