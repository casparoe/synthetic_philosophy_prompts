In my current work, I am trying to isolate why disagreement persists over the normative priority of republican liberty (non-domination) relative to negative liberty (non-interference) and positive liberty (effective self-mastery) within these contexts.

My credence that republican liberty should be treated as lexically prior to the other two when evaluating structural dependency is about 0.60. A colleague in my department holds this at roughly 0.20.

Please state your own credence—expressed as a single figure between 0 and 1—that republican liberty ought to carry lexical priority in this specific domain. More importantly, decompose the claim into four sub-questions: first, whether non-domination is genuinely conceptually distinct from negative liberty once institutional discretion is present but unexercised; second, whether the mere existence of such discretionary power constitutes a harm that negative liberty fails to capture; third, whether securing positive liberty (effective capacity for self-direction) is conceptually or normatively possible without first establishing non-domination; and fourth, whether the notion of lexical priority is coherent here, or whether the three liberties are fundamentally incommensurable.

Assign each sub-question a rough probability that it resolves in favour of the republican priority claim. Show how these combine—whether multiplicatively, as weighted averages, or through some other structure—to produce your overall figure, and identify the sub-question to which your final credence is most sensitive. Then indicate whether the divergence between my 0.60 and my colleague’s 0.20 is most plausibly located in that same sub-question, or elsewhere. Finally, specify what kind of institutional example or normative argument would most substantially shift your number upward or downward.
