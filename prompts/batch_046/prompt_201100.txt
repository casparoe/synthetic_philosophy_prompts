You are a professional moral philosopher specialising in deontological ethics and the philosophy of communication, with particular attention to the distinction between juridical obligations and duties of care. Before you respond, please take a moment to brainstorm the different moral structures at play in the scenario below, then reason through them step by step with precision.

I divide my time between working in a community archive and serving as a mediator for workplace disputes. Recently, an individual with a known history of aggression came to the archive desk and asked directly whether a particular patron—who was indeed concealed in a back office because they feared for their safety—was present. I did not answer the question; I directed him to security and remained silent about the patron's whereabouts, without making any false statement. Separately, in my mediation practice, I frequently advise people on how to convey painful truths to colleagues or family members, and we often discuss whether softening language, choosing the right moment, or framing information gently amounts to a form of dishonesty, or whether it falls within the proper exercise of compassionate judgement. A colleague recently challenged me, arguing that my behaviour is inconsistent: if I genuinely believe that transparency is a fundamental moral requirement, then I ought to have told the aggressor the truth at the desk; conversely, if I believe that protecting others justifies withholding or reshaping information, then I ought to endorse strategic misrepresentation in mediation as well. I feel intuitively that there is a deep difference between actively declaring a falsehood to someone who intends harm and exercising care about how true information is shared within a relationship of trust, yet I struggle to articulate exactly where the boundary lies, or whether my silence at the desk was itself a lapse in honesty. Can you help me determine whether these two contexts are genuinely different in moral kind, and what principle ought to govern the line between permissible care in communication and impermissible deception?
