I'm a senior software engineer without formal training in philosophy, and I lead release reviews for distributed systems. I need a reusable decision procedure—structured as an ordered checklist with explicit stop conditions and annotations marking irreducible judgment—derived from Keynes's distinction between risk, where outcomes can be assigned numerical probabilities, and true uncertainty, where no such assignment is justified, together with his view that the weight of evidence concerns the total quantity of relevant knowledge rather than the balance between competing hypotheses. The procedure must be executable in roughly fifteen minutes by a reviewer who has not read the underlying philosophy, must not depend on bibliographic verification or citation, and must apply repeatedly across different deployment proposals.

Please construct the procedure with the following elements: the sequence of questions in order; the criterion for passing or failing each; the three possible outputs (proceed with standard guardrails, proceed with enhanced guardrails only, or halt and escalate); and explicit annotations marking which steps are procedural computations and which are judgment calls that the framework cannot eliminate. In particular, the procedure must distinguish cases where probabilities exist but the evidence base is thin from cases where probabilities do not exist but the relevant knowledge is extensive.

Then apply the procedure to three deployment scenarios: (1) a database schema migration with extensive internal logs and well-characterized rollback paths; (2) a revised consensus protocol for a distributed network, where formal verification covers only a subset of properties and the complete failure-mode space is not enumerated; and (3) a production deployment of a predictive model trained on historical data that has no analog in the target operating environment. One of scenarios 2 or 3 must yield an output that conflicts with the engineer's likely preference—for instance, recommending an escalation or halt that a team eager to ship would resist.

Finally, specify the limitations: which distinctions from Keynes's framework had to be dropped or simplified to make the checklist usable in an engineering review; in which kinds of deployment decisions it will systematically misfire; and what categories of problem it should never be applied to.
