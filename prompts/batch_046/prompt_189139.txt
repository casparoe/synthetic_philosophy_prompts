In a philosophy of science seminar I am taking, two fellow students—Elena and Marcus—recently argued about Otto Neurath's picture-language project and whether it genuinely dissolves metaphysical disputes or merely hides them in the grammar of icons. Elena opened by claiming that Neurath's system succeeds because it replaces theoretical sentences with standardized pictographic protocol statements arranged in intersubjectively observable configurations: since every symbol is anchored to a feature that different observers can confirm, there is no hidden ontology, only empirical coherence among the icons. Marcus replied that selecting which features to represent—such as depicting mental states through behavioral-output icons rather than internal-state icons—is not a neutral design choice but a substantive metaphysical commitment about reduction. Elena countered that Neurath's explicit anti-foundationalism treats all framework choices as pragmatic conventions revisable within the system, so labeling them "metaphysical" mistakes the nature of scientific engineering. Marcus then pressed that if every framework decision is reduced to mere pragmatics, then physicalism and idealism become equally legitimate "engineering options," which undermines Neurath's own critical aim of distinguishing scientific coherence from metaphysical speculation. Elena answered that the distinction survives through protocol-anchoring: a claim like "the Absolute directs historical progress" cannot be stabilized by any observable icon, whereas even auxiliary hypotheses in science are ultimately tethered to configurations of observable symbols. Marcus came back that Neurath explicitly allows unobserved auxiliary hypotheses to organize protocols, so once organizing symbols—such as a standardized icon for "causal connection" that is never itself observed—are admitted into the pictorial syntax, metaphysics has entered through the back door, merely wearing a different notation.
