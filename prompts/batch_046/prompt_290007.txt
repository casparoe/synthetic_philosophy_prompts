I'm preparing notes for a seminar on relational ontology and want a consistency audit before I present. My position combines Ubuntu-inspired relational personhood with commitments about agency and obligation that I'm not sure cohere. Here is where I stand:

(1) Personhood is not an intrinsic property but a relational achievement: to be a person is to stand in reciprocal recognition-relations with others, so that severe and prolonged isolation does not merely harm one but partially dissolves one's personhood.

(2) Moral responsibility nevertheless requires an irreducible individual locus of practical reasoning: one can be held accountable only for actions that express one's own deliberative standpoint, which collective norms cannot fully constitute or replace.

(3) I reject the idea that individual autonomy is a Western fiction, but I also insist that dissent and self-authorship are not illusions; they are healthy modes of relational existence, and a community that suppresses them undermines the very recognition-relations that constitute it.

(4) Obligations of justice are strongest within direct recognition-relations, yet they diminish gradually through chains of shared humanity rather than stopping at any sharp boundary—there is no point at which another's claim on me becomes literally zero.

(5) I hold that recognition itself can be suspended in degrees: there is a meaningful difference between being unrecognized, being recognized as a non-person, and being in a "shadow" state where one remains present but is excluded from full moral interlocution, and these gradations affect standing without being all-or-nothing.

Which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, which pairs only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? I am especially concerned that (2) smuggles in an individualist ontology that (1) and (5) deny, and that (3) combined with (1) makes dissent self-undermining whenever dissent risks the isolation that dissolves personhood. At the same time, I suspect (4) and (5) might seem to generate contradictory obligations—strong local duties versus diffuse universal ones—without actually doing so. Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each what would have to give and at what cost, and identify which single revision would clean up the most problems without destroying the relational core.
