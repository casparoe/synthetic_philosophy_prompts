"The old evidence problem is that Bayes' theorem can't increase the probability of a theory T when the evidence E is already known, because P(E) is 1 and P(T|E) equals P(T). Glymour pointed this out with Mercury's orbit: astronomers had observed the anomalous perihelion shift since the 1800s, so when Einstein proposed general relativity in 1915 and showed it predicted the shift, the data was already in everyone's background knowledge. That means a Bayesian who already knew about Mercury couldn't update on GR predicting it.

The entailment conditioning fix says you shouldn't condition on E, but on the fact that T entails E, written T→E. Even if you know E, learning that T predicts E can still raise your credence in T. But this just pushes the problem back, because now you need a prior over the entailment relation T→E, and if you already knew that GR entails the Mercury result before you believed GR, then P(T→E) is already near 1 and you still don't update.

In the notebook case, the measurements are old in one sense because they were made in 1974, but they are new in the epistemic sense because nobody knew about them until 2029. So for the scientific community evaluating the 2022 model, this isn't old evidence—it's newly discovered data that happens to be physically old. The 2022 researchers didn't have access to it, so their priors in 2022 shouldn't include it. The only problem is if someone in 1974 had read the notebook and suppressed it, but that doesn't affect the Bayesian calculation for the 2029 evaluation. Therefore the model gets confirmed in the standard way."
