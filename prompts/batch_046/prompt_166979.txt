Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of few-shot concept induction—specifically one intended to explain human performance on Bongard-style problems—must satisfy, with the motivating case or consideration behind each requirement. The theory must not presuppose compositional symbolic representations or massive statistical pretraining; instead, it should account for analogical transfer of relational structures across perceptual domains, the constraining power of negative examples on overgeneralization, and a mechanism of representational re-description that renders the same feature relevant or irrelevant depending on the induced rule. Add the further constraint that the mechanism must operate within working-memory limits substantially below those required for explicit symbolic computation. For each desideratum, indicate whether it is non-negotiable or camp-specific, and identify any pair that may be jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether accounting for analogical transfer without compositionality forces the abandonment of necessary-and-sufficient-condition analyses of concepts. Conclude with a brief assessment of how connectionist and classical symbolic approaches fare against this list.
