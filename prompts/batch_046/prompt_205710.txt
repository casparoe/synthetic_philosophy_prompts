As a novelist working on a dialogue about constructed interiority, I am preparing to interview a philosopher who advances narrative illusionism—the position that phenomenal consciousness is not a hidden property we misidentify, but an artifact of the brain's narrative self-modeling system, which synthesizes a unified protagonist from disjointed cognitive processes and thereby produces the false impression of qualitative immediacy. I need eight to ten interview questions that avoid letting her default to standard visual-illusion analogies. Specifically, include at least one question on how her view differs from straightforward eliminativism about qualia; one on why introspective reports of phenomenal character remain stable across cultures if the experience is purely narrative; one on whether the mechanism can account for the specific grain and immediacy of pain without collapsing into the functionalism she rejects; and one on the ethical implications for fictional characters if consciousness is purely a narrative construct. For each, provide a follow-up tailored to the answer she is most likely to give.
