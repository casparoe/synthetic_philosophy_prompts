Write a dialogue between two grad students — one leaning reliabilist, one obsessed with epistemic circularity — sitting in a campus cafe right after they watched a demo of a "reflexive sensor loop." The gadget predicts room temperature, then checks its own thermometer against that prediction, and uses agreement to confirm the sensor is working. The reliabilist should argue that if the loop is stable and error-correcting, first-order knowledge can feed back into second-order justification without vicious circularity. The skeptic should answer that this is just easy knowledge with extra silicon: you're using the very faculity you're trying to validate to validate itself, and stability doesn't recieve the logical problem. Make them actually respond — the reliabilist might concede pure circularity is bad but insist that cross-modal checks inside the loop break it, and the skeptic should ask whether those cross-checks aren't themselves trapped inside the loop. Keep it casual and don't ask for citations, named philosophers, or reading lists — just have them hash it out in plain terms.
