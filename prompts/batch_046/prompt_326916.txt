In our philosophy-of-psychiatry seminar we've hit a wall arguing about prolonged grief disorder ever since it got formalized in the DSM. The loopers say naming it creates feedback: people learn the script, clinicians see it more, the category expands. The realists counter that grief is anchored in attachment biology, so any loop is first-order only — it might change how people report symptoms, but not the underlying cluster. What's strange is both sides accept Hacking-style looping in other domains, both accept suffering is real, yet they can't agree on whether this case involves first-order reporting shifts or second-order changes to what the cluster means socially. My working theory is that the stall isn't about ontology at all, but about what counts as an adequate explanation: do we need biomarkers, or is a stable phenomenalogical profile enough? But maybe it's worse — maybe they're using 'looping' for two different mechanisms and don't realize it. Is this a verbal confusion, a hidden empirical disagreement about attachment neurobiology, or bedrock intuitions about medicalization? I want a diagnosis, not a literature review. And be honest: could any distinction — say, separating feedback on prevalence from feedback on normative framing — actually move the debate, or is this an impasse we'll always have?
