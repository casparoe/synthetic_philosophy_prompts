hey so im in phil of time and we just read this old paper by McTaggart (1908 i think?) where he argues that time cant be real because every event has to be present, past, and future, which is a flat contradiciton. he splits time into the A-series (past/present/future) and the B-series (before/after) and says only the A-series gives us real change, but its contradictory so change is impossible.

my tutor mentioned this hybrid view called archival presentism — like, only the present is fully real, but past moments are kept in an archived state where they lack presentness-properties but still do causal work and explain memory. she said this dodges McTaggart because the contradiction only hits if you assign present/past/future to the exact same thing, and the archived layer never gets those properties assigned.

i dont really get the move though. if an archived past-event was once present, doesnt that still mean it had the property of being present at some point? and if its now archived and not-present, then the same thing still went from present to not-present, which sounds like McTaggart's contradiction all over again, just with extra steps. also, if the archive is real in any ontological sense, can we still ask whether its present or past?

can you help me see whether archival presentism actually escapes the contradiction or if its just renaming the problem? im not asking for a reading list, just trying to wrap my head around the logic.
