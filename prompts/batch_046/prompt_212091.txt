Construct a thought experiment set in housing and zoning policy that tests whether collective neighborhood veto power over urban density can be justified without undermining the rights-based framework invoked to defend it. Design a single-paragraph, realistic scenario—no science fiction, no omniscient regulators, no more than one paragraph—in which residents of an established neighborhood invoke a recently enacted "community preservation covenant," a niche instrument that ties the transfer of air-rights bonuses to a binding neighborhood veto over any project exceeding baseline density, in order to block a cooperative housing proposal that would replace underused surface parking with permanently affordable units governed by a community land trust.

Specify that the scenario must generate the following verdicts: (i) a "right-to-the-city" theorist, who holds that democratic control over urban space belongs to all city residents rather than to adjacent property owners, must conclude that the veto is illegitimate; (ii) a Lockean property-rights theorist applying the proviso must initially conclude that the veto is legitimate, on the grounds that blocking the development protects existing residents from degradation of shared infrastructure and open space that would leave them worse off; and (iii) when the cooperative's "permanent affordability covenant" is present as a structural feature—transferring all future appreciation to a city-wide land trust rather than to private shareholders—the Lockean theorist must face an internal tension: either the proviso is satisfied globally, since the development improves the position of the worst-off across the city, and the veto collapses, or the theorist must defend the veto on parochial grounds that abandon the universalist logic of the proviso itself.

After presenting the scenario, show the reasoning for each position, identify the precise feature that forces the Lockean account into the tension, explain what happens to the case if that feature is removed (the covenant disappears and the development becomes a standard market-rate project), and explain how a defender of the Lockean view would attack the scenario—specifically, whether they can reject the global application of the proviso without ad hoc special pleading. The purpose of the case is to determine whether exclusionary veto mechanisms can be grounded in universal rights or whether they necessarily depend on localized privilege; therefore, a successful design must generate the tension from the theories themselves rather than stipulating it by fiat.
