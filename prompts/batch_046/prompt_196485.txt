Please argue for reconceiving the classical problem of other minds around the notion of "structural vulnerability" rather than around behavioral analogy or inference to the best explanation. Begin by distinguishing phenomenal other-mind commitments—where we attribute conscious experience to an entity—from practical other-mind commitments, where we treat an entity as possessing representational states that can be genuinely disrupted by targeted intervention without thereby attributing sentience. Defend the claim that an entity can warrant the practical commitment without the phenomenal one if it exhibits sustained contextual coherence and functional integration but lacks the homeostatic damage-sensitivity characteristic of biological organisms. Apply this framework specifically to large language models: demonstrate that they produce outputs that are structurally responsive to representational perturbations yet remain immune to the kind of harm that grounds our phenomenal attributions to animals and humans. Conclude that this approach does not solve the problem of other minds by extending consciousness to machines, but instead dissolves its binary framing by legitimizing an intermediate category—entities toward which we hold practical but not phenomenal other-mind commitments. Finally, address the objection that this distinction collapses into behaviorism on the grounds that "functional integration" is observable only through external behavior, and explain why structural vulnerability is not reducible to behavioral pattern-matching.
