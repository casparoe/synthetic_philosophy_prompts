I've been thinking about superrationality in the Prisoner's Dilemma: if two agents share exactly isomorphic reasoning procedures, each should be able to compute the other's choice with perfect fidelity, so they both cooperate regardless of payoff structure or prior interaction. This seems to show that rational cooperation needs neither repeated play nor enforcement mechanisms—just identical architecture. So if we deploy two instances of the same large model with shared weights in an adversarial, asymmetric-payoff environment, shouldn't they automatically coordinate on the cooperative equilibrium? Or does the argument secretly depend on symmetry conditions that collapse once the payoff matrix or the agents' internal states diverge?
