I think this is different from case 1 — if the change is stable, fully informed, and they genuinely don't regret it, then the post-mod preference is legitimate. Here I think they should NOT be bound. It's okay to walk away from your own coherence procedure if the result feels like someone else wrote your life.

You put someone in a room with perfect simulations of every possible version of themselves, running through all counterfactual lives, and they must pick which future self to become. Once they leave, they immediately regret it and claim the mirror-room environment distorted their judgement, producing a preference that doesn't match their actual values outside. I keep flip-flopping: sometimes the mirror-room choice feels like the purest form of idealised preference because it's maximally reflective; other times it feels invalid because the artificial setting broke coherence with their real-world values.
