I've been trying to understand illusionism about phenomenal consciousness after encountering it in a discussion about artificial intelligence and subjective experience. The view seems to be that the "what it's like" quality of seeing red or feeling pain is not a real feature of our mental states, but rather a kind of systematic misrepresentation—similar to how a graphical user interface presents files and folders that don't literally exist in the underlying storage architecture. As someone who builds software systems, I find this analogy compelling, but I think it exposes a problem I can't resolve. If phenomenal consciousness is an illusion analogous to a GUI overlay on raw machine processes, then there must be some underlying cognitive substrate—the actual mental processing without the phenomenal gloss—that serves as the ground truth being misrepresented.

But this appears to commit the illusionist to a dual-layer model: a veridical non-phenomenal base layer and an illusory phenomenal representation layered on top. And if the illusion consists in representing phenomenal properties that don't exist, doesn't that very representation preserve those properties as intentional content? In other words, hasn't the illusionist merely relocated phenomenal consciousness into the representational content rather than eliminated it, leaving us with two kinds of mental states rather than none? I'm particularly interested in whether this rendering-pipeline analogy actually undermines illusionism by forcing it into a representationalist framework that retains phenomenal properties at the level of content, or whether there's a way to reject the analogy without giving up the core claim that phenomenal consciousness is illusory.
