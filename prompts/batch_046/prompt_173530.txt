I was recently discussing with friends how modern social platforms seem to dissolve individual voices into an anonymous collective, and someone mentioned in passing that the Danish philosopher Søren Kierkegaard had warned precisely about this mechanism in newspapers and in what he called the public. I have no academic training in philosophy, but I have been reflecting on my own about whether there is a structural problem with communication that addresses everyone and no one at once, leaving no one responsible for what is said. I would like you to write a satirical parody — in the deadpan style of an internal corporate memorandum from a technology firm — announcing the launch of a new platform feature called The Public, engineered to maximise engagement by guaranteeing that no user ever speaks as a distinct individual. The piece should capture the conceptual worry that when opinion is addressed to an abstraction, personal responsibility and qualitative difference are levelled away, and it should embed the standard objections — about anonymity, the illusion of collective authority, and the replacement of direct encounter with mediated noise — entirely within the feature’s specifications and user guidelines. Please write in British English, approximately 600 words, with the humour arising from the accuracy of the conceptual exaggeration rather than from invented absurdities, so that a reader familiar with the idea recognises the satire as fair. I do not need bibliographies, citations, or references to specific texts; I am interested only in the conceptual form.
