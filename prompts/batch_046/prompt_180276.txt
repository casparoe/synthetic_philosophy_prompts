I'm preparing a position paper for a philosophy of religion seminar on Madhyamaka metaphysics, and I want a consistency check before I defend my commitments. Here is where I've landed:

(1) All phenomena (dharmas) lack inherent existence (svabhāva), and this applies reflexively: emptiness (śūnyatā) itself is empty.
(2) The catuṣkoṭi (four-cornered negation) exhausts logical space—any claim that X is, is not, both, or neither can be shown to fail—so no metaphysical thesis, including the thesis of universal emptiness, can be ultimately established.
(3) Conventional truth (saṃvṛti-satya) is not a lower-grade reality but a dependently originated, pragmatically indispensable register of discourse that carries no representational authority over an ultimate domain.
(4) Dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) is not the causal mechanism that produces emptiness but is identical in content with emptiness: to say things arise dependently is just to say they are empty.
(5) The distinction between the two truths is pedagogical and therapeutic, not ontological; there are not two domains of being, only two registers of speech that serve different soteriological functions.
(6) I think Nāgārjuna's method is best reconstructed as a global skeptical equilibrium in which every position is undermined by reductio, yet ethical and contemplative practice proceeds entirely unchanged because conventional discourse retains its full functional authority.

Which pairs among these are in genuine tension, which are only apparently in tension, and what is the least costly revision for each real conflict? In particular, does (2) undermine the assertibility of (1) in a way that (6) tries but fails to stabilize? Does (4)'s identity claim between dependent origination and emptiness conflict with (5)'s denial of an ontological two-truths structure? And is there a hidden tension between (3)'s denial of representational authority and (6)'s claim that practice proceeds unchanged—does unchanged practice presuppose some representational grip on conventional entities?

Please rank the tensions by severity, specify the cheapest fix for each, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most conflicts. Also, please separate what is settled interpretation of Nāgārjuna from what remains genuinely contested among Madhyamaka scholars, and do not present a contested reading—such as the purely pedagogical interpretation of the two truths—as if it were the consensus view.
