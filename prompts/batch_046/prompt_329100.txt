For a long-form feature I am filing on whether macrohistory can be treated as a predictive science, I have tried to settle my methodological commitments before conducting further interviews. I should like a consistency audit—please respond in British English—before I finalise the draft. Here is where I stand:

(a) I take Turchin-style secular cycles—elite overproduction, fiscal stress, and popular immiseration—to identify genuine structural rhythms that recur across agrarian and industrial societies, not merely narrative patterns imposed after the fact.

(b) I also hold that macrohistorical forecasting faces a reflexivity problem: once structural predictions enter public and elite discourse, the anticipated crisis alters behaviour—through anticipatory reform, repression, or institutional innovation—thereby violating the stationarity conditions on which quantitative calibration depends.

(c) Nevertheless, I maintain that cliodynamics must retain predictive aspiration. Without out-of-sample forecasting of crisis windows and structural outcomes, the discipline collapses into unfalsifiable historical storytelling, however mathematically dressed.

(d) At the same time, I believe that contingent leadership and collective agency are causally decisive at crisis nodes; structural pressures constrain the possibility space but do not determine which institutional path is taken.

(e) From these, I derive a working stance I call structural forecasting with trajectory agnosticism: we can predict that a crisis window will open within a given temporal band, but we should remain silent about the specific political form it takes.

My question is whether this package actually hangs together. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which only appear to be? In particular, does (b) undermine (c) more severely than I have admitted? Does (d) weaken (a) to the point that the structural rhythms become epiphenomenal? And is (e) a coherent resolution, or does it try to have matters both ways? Please rank the tensions by severity, identify the least costly revision for each, and tell me whether there is a single change that would clean up the most conflicts. I do not want a summary of Turchin’s work, nor a bibliography; I want an audit of my own commitments.
