I'm preparing materials for a graduate seminar on the ontology of chance, and I want a poem that does philosophical work rather than merely naming concepts. Write a villanelle—strict ABA ACA ADA ABA ABA ACA rhyme scheme, two refrains, nineteen lines of roughly iambic pentameter—about the propensity interpretation of probability, specifically the circularity that arises when propensities are defined as irreducible physical tendencies (not long-run frequencies, not subjective credences) yet any specification of a single-case propensity seems to depend on either a hidden frequentist calibration or a prior assignment of weights. Let the first refrain concern the "tendency" said to exist prior to measurement; let the second concern the "measure" required to reveal it. The volta should come in the third tercet, where the speaker realizes that the disposition is not an intrinsic monadic property of the object but is relational—defined only with respect to a symmetry-breaking experimental arrangement—without collapsing into frequentism or Bayesianism. Seed the imagery with a decaying nucleus, a weighted die, or a spin-state preparation, but keep the argument structural: the rhyme scheme and returning lines should enact the circular dependency, and the closing couplet should name what remains—an ontic disposition that can be observed only through the statistical pattern it was meant to explain. Render it precisely enough that a philosopher of science recognizes Popper's propensity view and the single-case objection, but coherently enough that a reader with no background finds the poem intact. Do not cite texts or authors; do not offer a bibliography; let the form itself carry the conceptual tension.
