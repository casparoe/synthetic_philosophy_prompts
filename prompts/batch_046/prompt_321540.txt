As a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun reading philosophy with the same attention to error bounds and structural assumptions I once applied to measurement systems, I ask you to draft the joint statement that two camps could sign after a sustained, good-faith adversarial collaboration on capital punishment. The first camp, call them the Procedural Retributivists, holds that retributive justice demands execution for the gravest crimes and that procedural safeguards—improved forensics, appellate review, and evidentiary standards—can reduce the risk of executing the innocent to a quantitatively acceptable level. The second camp, the Structural Abolitionists, holds that any human justice system exhibits an irreducible false-positive rate in capital sentencing, which I term epistemic closure, and that execution is categorically distinct from other state errors because it is non-compensable: unlike wrongful imprisonment, which permits material compensation and status restoration, wrongful execution allows only posthumous symbolic repair, generating a deontic constraint that operates independently of consequentialist risk calculus.

Please structure your response as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: about the formal logic of retributive proportionality; about the empirical record on deterrence, including its contested status; about the documented existence of exonerations among death-row inmates; and about the logical distinction between the magnitude of a risk and the type of error it produces. Second, a disagreements table in which each camp’s position is phrased in wording its own members would accept—especially regarding whether non-compensability introduces a categorical moral boundary, and whether epistemic closure is in principle reducible or structurally permanent. Third, the cruxes: sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. I take the central cruxes to be (a) whether retributive proportionality can be fully satisfied by permanent incarceration without execution; (b) whether the non-compensability of execution creates a deontic prohibition regardless of quantitative risk levels; and (c) whether any empirically observed deterrence effect, if genuine, can outweigh the moral cost of an irreducible false-positive rate. Fourth, for each crux, identify a concrete case, comparative jurisdictional pattern, or empirical argument that both sides agree is relevant—such as cross-jurisdictional homicide-rate data following abolition versus retention, or the documented post-conviction outcomes of DNA-exonerated capital defendants—and record in advance what each camp predicts that evidence demonstrates. Fifth, a brief assessment of whether any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, with reasoning.

Please write with formal precision. Finally, state explicitly how confident you are in the claims advanced in this draft, and single out the one point about which you are least certain—whether it concerns the coherence of the non-compensability thesis, the interpretation of retributive theory, or the empirical status of deterrence.
