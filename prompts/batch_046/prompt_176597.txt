I've been studying debates about intuition pumps in philosophy of mind and ethics, and I've noticed a recurring structural pattern that I don't have a precise term for. A philosopher wants to isolate phenomenon A from phenomenon B and constructs a scenario in which B is absent while A remains present. But the very mechanism that removes B also introduces, or eliminates, some other feature C that is structurally coupled with B in ordinary cases. The intuition that results therefore tracks C's presence or absence as much as it tracks A, yet the scenario is presented and defended as a "pure" or "clean" case in which only A and B vary. Once the intuition is established, it is used to support general claims about A and B, while objections that point to C's hidden work are dismissed as missing the point of idealization. I've seen this in consciousness debates where bodily absence is coupled with informational isolation, in personal-identity cases where psychological continuity is separated from bodily continuity by mechanisms that also alter causal integration, and in moral-responsibility scenarios where determinism is separated from agency by framing that simultaneously removes the agent's reasons-responsive structure.
