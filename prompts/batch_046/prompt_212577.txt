I take the position that in supervising advanced AI systems, process-based oversight holds conceptual priority over outcome-based oversight, and that this priority is not merely practical but logical, arising from what I will call evaluative non-transparency. The core claim is this: when a system's internal reasoning traverses representational structures that lie outside the supervisor's reconstructive capacity, assessing only the final output forces the supervisor to rely on an implicit, unverified model of the reasoning path that produced it. The evaluation then becomes circular—the supervisor treats the outcome as validating a process they cannot inspect, yet the outcome's epistemic authority derives precisely from that uninspected process. Before I commit to this framework, I want to understand its exposure. What would have to be true for this claim of conceptual priority to fail? I want an honest assessment organized around the following: first, whether there are conditions under which outcome evaluation remains genuinely non-circular despite severe representational mismatch, and whether protocols of staged evaluation—checking intermediate artifacts without full trajectory transparency—solve the problem or only displace the circularity to an earlier stage; second, whether there exist domains in which full process transparency is available but outcome evaluation is impossible or meaningless, and whether such domains weaken or strengthen the priority claim; third, whether the argument can be saved from tautology by precisely defining the boundary of "process"—if every successful outcome check is reinterpreted as implicit process verification, has the view become unfalsifiable, and is that insulation a virtue or a vice for a supervisory framework; fourth, what empirical or formal results—say, about the invertibility of reasoning traces or the stability of intermediate representations—would decisively undermine the claim that outcome-only supervision is conceptually deficient; and fifth, which of these defeaters defenders of the view would themselves concede as damaging, versus which they would dismiss as missing the point about the supervisor's epistemic position. Rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and say plainly whether the position risks being qualified into emptiness.
