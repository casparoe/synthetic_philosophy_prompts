I have been thinking, without any formal training, about whether the Enlightenment was genuinely about freeing people or whether it mainly taught governments and societies more efficient ways to manage them. I should like to see this tension explored through sustained irony. Please write a deadpan bureaucratic circular, approximately 600 words, in the voice of the fictional Crown Office for Rational Advancement and Its Counter-Weights. The style should be that of a meticulous, modern administrative report written with the formality of a nineteenth-century civil servant: precise, dry, and entirely serious. The circular announces the results of a decennial Dialectical Balance Audit — an invented accounting framework in which every entry for human progress must be matched by a corresponding entry for the mechanism of regulation it introduced. You might pair universal literacy with the standardisation of dialect; the ideal of public reason with the architecture of total visibility; the declaration of rights with the census categorisation of the citizen. The target is the celebratory narrative that presents Enlightenment rationality as pure liberation, ignoring its disciplinary shadow. The humour should arise from treating domination as a routine line item in a progress report, so that a reader acquainted with critical theory recognises the argument without the text ever naming particular philosophers or movements. The bureaucrat must remain genuinely convinced he is praising advancement, even as the details inadvertently expose systems of control. Please do not name any living individual, and include no bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
