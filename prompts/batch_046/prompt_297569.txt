Write a short dialogue between two philosophers, Alina and Marcus, debating whether the dogmatism paradox exposes a genuine instability in rational belief or merely reflects imprecise talk about justification. Alina defends "revisionary dogmatism": she argues that agents may rationally hold dogmatically to beliefs anchored in direct perceptual access or valid deduction, but should revise only in domains lacking independent calibration mechanisms. Marcus advances a "calibration-debt" objection: every resisted revision accumulates epistemic liability that eventually undermines the agent's claim to rational authority, so Alina's domain exemption collapses once believers can strategically reclassify contentious beliefs as protected. Require real back-and-forth—especially Marcus's charge that Alina's boundary is ad hoc, and Alina's reply that Marcus demands an impossible doxastic transparency—without either speaker becoming a strawman. The dialogue should stay focused on whether a principled line separates legitimate epistemic self-trust from irrational stubbornness.
