I have spent the last decade working on non-convex optimization in deep learning, and my intuition for causation and possibility is shaped by loss landscapes: local minima, saddle points, gradient-flow paths, the sensitivity to initialization and learning-rate schedules, and the role of regularization in constraining which regions of parameter space are reachable. Recently, in a reading group on philosophy of history, I encountered the standard debate between structural inevitability—the idea that industrialization or democratic consolidation were bound to happen given macro-conditions—and radical contingency, which treats events like the 1848 revolutions or the specific shape of the 1945 settlement as path-dependent accidents that could easily have diverged. I want you to restate both positions in the vocabulary of optimization dynamics, as precisely as you can: treat inevitability as a claim about a dominant global basin with steep gradients funneling trajectories toward it regardless of initialization noise, and treat contingency as a claim about flat, multi-basin landscapes with weak gradients, high sensitivity to stochastic perturbations, and no globally dominant attractor.

For each translation, report the residue in detail. Which philosophical distinctions—modal necessity, counterfactual dependence, causal overdetermination, normative regret—map cleanly onto optimization terms like convergence guarantees, path dependence, or initialization bias; which require distortion (for example, treating moral responsibility for a historical outcome as analogous to the choice of an initial parameter vector); and which have no counterpart at all in the optimization framework, such as the normative question of whether we ought to regret that a particular trajectory was taken rather than another. Then assess whether the residue reveals that historical philosophy is asking questions that optimization theory genuinely cannot formulate, or whether the optimization translation exposes those philosophical distinctions as metaphysical ornament that does not alter predictive or explanatory practice. I am not looking for analogies used as arguments, but for an honest translation whose value lies as much in what it fails to carry as in what it carries.
