As a software engineer without formal philosophical training, I have been following the long-running dispute over whether the sciences carve nature at its joints—that is, whether scientific taxonomies discover pre-existing divisions in reality or construct useful divisions for prediction and control. The disagreement seems stalled: realists argue that successful science tracks mind-independent structures (e.g., chemical elements, biological species), while anti-realist or pluralist critics respond that taxonomic revisions, cross-disciplinary disagreement, and the practical aims of science show that kinds are engineered rather than discovered. Every standard argument—inductive success, predictive novelty, the failure of essentialism—appears well rehearsed on both sides, and neither camp seems moved.

My own intuition, coming from building classification pipelines, is that the stalemate may not be substantive but structural: realists often treat carving at the joints as a binary ontological property—either a taxonomy mirrors nature exactly or it is purely conventional—whereas practitioners in science and engineering treat taxonomies as dynamically maintained inference structures whose value lies in stable predictive correlation rather than in ontological fundamentality. If that is right, the two sides may be answering different questions—one about metaphysical fundamentality, the other about epistemic engineering—under the single heading natural kinds.

Please diagnose this impasse. Is it a verbal disagreement about what real or natural means; a substantive disagreement about facts that neither side can currently settle; a case of hidden empirical questions (for instance, about the stability of taxonomies across theory change); or are the sides genuinely addressing different problems—ontology versus scientific practice—while using the same vocabulary? Support your diagnosis by describing what each side actually commits to, not just by labeling them. Then tell me what, if anything, should count as progress: a conceptual distinction, a formal result, an empirical finding, or a change in the framing of the question?
