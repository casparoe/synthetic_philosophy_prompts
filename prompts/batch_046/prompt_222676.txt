I'm writing up my philosophical commitments on cultural explanation for a philosophy of social science seminar, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present. Here is roughly where I've landed. First, I take Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis seriously—not as a monocausal story, but as showing that religious values can enter the causal history of economic institutions through elective affinity between belief structures and organizational incentives, rather than through direct determination. Second, I am a methodological individualist about explanation: every social-level claim must ultimately be anchored in the subjective meanings and choices of individuals, and collective entities like a Calvinist cultural ethos have no autonomous causal powers of their own. Third, despite that individualism, I think these collective descriptions are genuinely indispensable; replacing talk of a cultural ethos with an exhaustive list of individual beliefs and choices would lose the very structural pattern that needs explaining. Fourth, I hold that values cause actions only in the sense that they supply agents with reasons they recognize, not in any efficient-causal sense comparable to physical forces; so explaining action by values is always a matter of interpretive understanding rather than subsumption under a causal law. Fifth, I maintain that for a cultural factor to be genuinely explanatory rather than post-hoc decorative, there must be counterfactual dependence: had the value-complex been absent or substantially different, the institutional trajectory would have diverged, holding material conditions and organizational constraints constant. Sixth, I reject any form of cultural determinism: material interests and organizational constraints are always necessary co-conditions, which is why I think critics who read Weber as an idealist miss the point—values never suffice for institutional outcomes.

For each real tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs; then tell me which single revision would clean up the most tensions overall. I have a particular hunch that my second and third commitments might be contradictory, but I'm unsure whether that's a real conflict or just an apparent one—please treat that pair specifically. Also check whether my fourth and fifth commitments pull in opposite directions: can values supply recognized reasons for individuals while also supporting the kind of macro-level counterfactual isolation that my fifth commitment demands?
