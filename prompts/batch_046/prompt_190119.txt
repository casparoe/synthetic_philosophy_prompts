I'm working on a piece about whether virtual objects are "real" in any meaningful sense, and I keep running into this friend of mine who designs social VR spaces. She's firm that stealing a rare sword in an MMO isn't real theft — it's just copying data — but destroying someone's custom avatar after years of use is genuinely wrong, like trashing a painting. She also thinks virtual land in those blockchain worlds counts as real property since people pay real cash for it, yet she says a temporary weather effect in the exact same world is just pixels, nothing more. Now I'm trying to figure out what she'd say about this new distribued-archive thing I came accross: people store 3D "memory objects" across a peer-to-peer network, so there's no central server, and if one node drops out and takes the last copy with it, the object vanishes for everyone. Is that destroying a real artifact, or just loosing a file? I'm less interested in the yes/no answer than in the principle she's actually using, what other principles could explain her past calls just as well, and what kind of case would finally split them apart.
