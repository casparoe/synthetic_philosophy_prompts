At my philoshophy meetup I'm the retired biologist who just started reading this stuff, and two friends argued about Jaspers and whether a nation can be guilty across generations. (1) She said Jaspers' metaphysical guilt works like an epigenetic mark: it doesn't rewrite DNA but changes expression in people born into the same political structure, so Germans born in 1960 carry it structurally even if they never committed a crime. (2) He said that's a category mistake—guilt needs intentional agency, and Jaspers himself split metaphysical guilt (individual, before God) from political guilt (collective, civic); biology analogies confuse causal transmission with moral accountability. (3) She replied that if political guilt sticks to citizenship in an ongoing state, then descendants who still benefit from unrepaired injustices inherit a real debt, not just a story, and denying that is like ignoring vertical transmission of environmental harm. (4) He said she's smuggled in an intergenerational justice theory Jaspers never endorsed, and without a hard line her view makes every descendant everywhere guilty for every past wrong. Please adjudicate move by move—rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right with brief reasons. Also say what argument or finding would flip your verdict, and flag where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
