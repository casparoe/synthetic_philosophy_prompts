I work for a municipal climate-adaptation office that must repeatedly evaluate capital proposals trading present costs against benefits realized decades later — for instance, elevating a sewage plant now versus restoring a coastal wetland that protects the district in 2075. I need a reusable decision procedure grounded in pure time preference: treat temporal location as ethically neutral, distinguish pure discounting (held near zero) from growth-adjusted or risk-based adjustments, and include two synthetic checks — a "reverse-chronology parity check" (identical risk profiles in reversed chronological order should not change the recommendation solely because of temporal placement) and a "future-population flag" (halt welfare comparison if the project alters who will exist to receive the benefit). Present the procedure as ordered steps, specifying for each what counts as a sufficient answer, where it stops with a definitive no, and where it must hand off to human value judgment rather than economic fact. Apply it to three proposals: (a) an immediate 40-million-dollar seawall protecting the current population for 50 years; (b) a deferred 15-million-dollar wetland restoration yielding protection only after 2045 for a different likely population; (c) a low-cost-now hybrid that imposes large future taxes on people not yet born. Note precisely where the procedure breaks down or yields an answer the office will reject, and flag every step that remains an ineliminable judgment call.
