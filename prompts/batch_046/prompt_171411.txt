I'm a program officer at a regional infrastructure fund that works across state and provincial lines in federal systems — US, German Länder, Swiss cantons, EU regional programs — and every quarter I recieve proposals for new enviromental or transport rules where it's unclear which level should actually set them. I need to write a quick memo (20 mins max, then my director signs off) saying local, regional, national, or cross-border, based on subsidiarity. The problem is that about half these cases involve split competence: one goverment writes the rule but enforcement is done by a private contractor licensed at a different level, or the funding comes federal with conditions that conflict with local preferences. So the usual "closest to the people" answer doesn't work.

Can you turn subsidiarity into an actual decision procedure I can run repeatedly? I want the questions in a specific order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where it stops with a hard no, and exactly where I have to flag it for my director because it's a values call rather than a competence call. The procedure needs to explicitly handle split-competence cases — where the regulatory authority and the operational capacity sit at different levels — and it should say honestly what it can't resolve, like when democratic proximity conflicts with technical scale. Don't turn it into a vague flowchart; I need the reasoning steps spelled out.

Then run it on three proposals:
1. A canton/state wants stricter noise limits for logistics hubs than the national standard, but the hubs are federally licensed for interstate/international freight.
2. A cross-border river basin board (two provinces, one state) wants to mandate vaccination of wild waterfowl against avian flu — local vets can administer it, but the epidemiological model and vaccine stockpile are managed nationally.
3. A municipal goverment wants to ban a specific agricultural chemical that's legal nationally but leaks into a shared aquifer; I expect the procedure should tell them no, and I want to see that outcome even if it feels wrong locally.

Please don't cite specific constitutional articles or case law — I'm not a lawyer and I don't have time to check them. No bibliography or reading list either. Just the procedure, the three worked examples, and a clear note on where judgment is unavoidable and where the procedure will misfire.
