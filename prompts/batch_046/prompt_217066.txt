Compare the inevitability thesis about simultaneous disovery — the idea that Newton and Leibniz, or Darwin and Wallace, were just interchangable nodes in a knowledge network bound to spit out calculus and natural selection eventually — with a path-dependence view that says the specific conceptual framing each person used actually shaped what was discovered. Focus on whether calculus still counts as the "same" discovery when Leibniz's notation and metaphysics of infinitesimals diverged so sharply from Newton's fluxions, and on whether Wallace's 1858 essay really proposed the same theory as Darwin given that Wallace never developed sexual selection or the artificial-selection analogy. Is inevitability stronger in math than biology, or is that distinction itself kind of shaky? Don't ask for a bibliogaphy; just argue it out in the weeds.
