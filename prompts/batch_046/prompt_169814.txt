ok here's where i've landed on the darwin/turing inversion stuff and i want to check if it actually hangs together before i present it in the seminar. (1) Darwin shows you can get full adaptive design with zero comprehension on nature's part — selection doesn't represent problems, it just differentally kills. (2) Turing's parallel is that you can get intelligent-looking problem solving with zero semantic comprehension inside the machine — syntax outruns semantics. (3) I think these are structurally the same move: both defeat the inference that smart-looking performance implies a mind behind it. (4) But i also think human comprehension is genuinely different — not just more competence, but first-person aboutness that neither evolved adaptations nor computers have. (5) So evolutionary debunking of our cognitive faculties fails, because even if our beliefs are evolved, they could still be tracking real comprehension-dependent truths; the blindness of selection doesn't infect the products. (6) At the same time i think human comprehension itself is an evolved trait — it's just that evolution here produced something that breaks the usual pattern, a kind of recusive self-modeling that generates real intentionality. (7) And i think Turing was right that asking whether machines can think is premature without clarification, but wrong if he meant there's no substantive answer — the answer is no, they lack comprehension, and that's not just verbal.

So: which of these pull against each other?
