I've been working through Quine's Two Dogmas of Empiricism and I think I've spotted a tension that I can't resolve without either misreading Quine or reintroducing the very distinction he rejects. Quine attacks the analytic-synthetic distinction by arguing that attempts to define analyticity either circularly invoke synonymy or illegitimately reduce statements to experience, and he pairs this with his critique of reductionism—the idea that each statement carries its own discrete empirical content. From this he derives the familiar image of belief as a web in which any statement can be held true come what may, provided we make sufficiently drastic adjustments elsewhere. Now, here is the confusion: if there genuinely is no analytic-synthetic boundary, then Quine's own claim that no statement is immune from revision must itself lack any special epistemic shield. If it is synthetic, it is revisable; if it is analytic, Quine has smuggled back the distinction. So does his holism turn out to be self-undermining? Furthermore, if every revision is just a local adjustment in the web, what resources remain for distinguishing between revising a logical law—say, dropping the law of excluded middle—and revising an empirical generalization like the conservation of energy? Both seem to be on a par, yet Quine clearly treats logical revision as more costly but not categorically different. To avoid this collapse, I have tried to construct a structural gradient within the web: rather than sorting statements into analytic and synthetic bins, we rank them by interconnection density and centrality, treating logic as highly central but not analytically protected. But I worry this is just Carnap's principle of tolerance dressed in Quinean vocabulary—allowing us to choose frameworks by pragmatic criteria while pretending we have escaped the first dogma. So my question is: does Quine's argument in the first dogma actually forbid any structural differentiation of beliefs by their functional role in the web, or does it only forbid defining that role in terms of meaning or convention? In other words, can a consistent Quinean accept my interconnection-gradient as a legitimate successor concept, or does the rejection of analyticity entail that all nodes in the web are, epistemically speaking, equally bare?
