I've noticed a recurring failure mode in intentional action that sits near the classic absent-minded driver problem but differs in a specific way. The pattern runs as follows: a person forms and states a clear intention to do A, then begins executing the well-practiced routine for B — which shares an initial physical segment with A — and, partway through, realizes they are performing B rather than A. Instead of correcting course, they complete B. Afterward, they do not treat this as a mistake; they construct a narrative that they had a tacit or background intention to do B all along, pointing to situational features that were present but never mentioned in the original plan. The reconstructed intention serves specifically to justify continuing B after recognition, not merely to explain the result ex post facto.
