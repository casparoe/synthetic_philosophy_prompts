I'm in this philosophy seminar on epistemology and we just went over the Sleeping Beauty problem. I mostly get why thirders say 1/3 — she's sampling from three possible awakenings, two of which happen under heads — but then we started arguing about whether this applies outside the lab, and I think I'm mixing something up. If I learn it's Tuesday, that's a self-locating belief too, right? But I don't suddenly revise my belief about whether it rained Monday just from knowing the day. So there has to be some hidden rule about when self-locating updates count.

My friend said it's about whether the number of observers depends on the outcome — in Beauty's case, heads makes two observers, tails makes one. But then I started thinking: if determinism is true, then my being awake on Tuesday is also causally tied to the coin flip on Sunday. The coin result determines whether the experiment runs long enough for me to exist on Tuesday at all. So isn't my Tuesday self just as "outcome-dependent" as Beauty's? That makes me think the thirder/halfer debate isn't really about self-location, but about whether we should treat our own existence as evidence for whatever caused it. But if that's the case, then shouldn't I be updating my beliefs about everything that happened before me, since my existing depends on all of it? Where does the reasoning actually stop, and why doesn't Sleeping Beauty's logic turn every belief I have into a 1/3 kind of update? I feel like I'm missing a formal distinction but I can't see where it lives.
