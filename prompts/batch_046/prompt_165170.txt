As a novelist working on a narrative in which a community's identity depends on a story the archives do not support, I need to think through the underlying philosophical tension. Write an essay addressing the following controversy: when collective memory and the historical record conflict—imagine a settlement that remembers its founding as a unified, intentional act of resistance, while the documentary evidence reveals it as dispersed, contingent, and largely unplanned—which should give way? Weigh the case for honoring the living memory (its function in sustaining identity, moral coherence, and intergenerational obligation) against the case for honoring the record (its claim to evidentiary authority, its protection against myth, and its duty to future inquiry). Reach and defend a clear verdict; do not conclude with mere balance. In doing so, examine whether a principled distinction—between what we might call commemorative truth and evidentiary truth—can preserve both, or whether one form must ultimately be subordinated to the other. Please also indicate, at the end, how confident you are in your conclusion, and specify the precise step in your argument about which you feel least certain.
