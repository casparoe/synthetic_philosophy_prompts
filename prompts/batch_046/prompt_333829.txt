okay so my sister had her kid three weeks ago and suddenly i'm an "aunt" which isn't even in the classic five relationships, and it's made me weirdly obsessed with Confucian role ethics. i read that your obligations come from your roles not universal rules, but then i built this argument and it feels like it proves way too much.

(1) in Confucian ethics, what you ought to do flows from the social roles you occupy—filial child, parent, elder sibling, etc.
(2) roles aren't biologically fixed; they depend on actual social relationships, and there's zhengming—rectifying names so the role matches the reality.
(3) so modern roles like "aunt" or "step-parent" are legitimate if the underlying relation is real.
(4) now, if a role is valid whenever there's a social relation, and relations can be constructed—like me declaring i'm the "recycling coordinator" for my building and everyone here relates to me through that—then i've created a genuine role.
(5) once that role exists, it generates obligations not just for me but for others in the network (they owe me cooperation, deference to my coordination, etc.).
(6) there's no principled Confucian line between "aunt" and "recycling coordinator" except saying the second one isn't traditional, but that's just conservatism, not role ethics.
(7) so Confucianism either lets anyone manufacture binding obligations for others by inventing social ties, or it needs an external principle to limit which roles count.
(8) both seem like betrayals of the view.

where does this slip? i really want to keep (1) and (6) because they feel like the heart of the position, but maybe i'm misreading how roles constrain people. if i have to reject one step, which is it and what am i paying?
