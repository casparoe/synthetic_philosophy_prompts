Write a formal dialogue between a prioritarian and an egalitarian critic discussing whether a pure leveling-down policy could be morally justified in the specific case of a municipal carbon-rationing scheme. In this scheme, reducing the energy-consumption caps of high-income households produces no additional energy supply for low-income households, because the local grid operates at fixed total capacity and any unused portion is simply dissipated rather than reallocated. The prioritarian should maintain that moral weight attaches only to improvements in absolute well-being, especially for the worst-off, and therefore that cutting wealthy households' caps without benefiting anyone else is unjustified—perhaps even harmful—unless there are indirect effects on the poor. The egalitarian should defend the non-instrumental value of equality, arguing that democratic citizens have reason to reject extreme consumption disparities even when leveling them produces no material gain for the disadvantaged, and that shared limits can express a valuable social relation rather than mere envy. The dialogue must feature genuine back-and-forth: the prioritarian should challenge the egalitarian on whether endorsing harm to some without helping others violates a basic moral constraint, and the egalitarian should press whether prioritarianism can recognize the independent importance of relative standing and collective self-governance over distribution. Neither speaker should be a strawman; both should articulate their positions with philosophical precision. The exchange should remain unresolved, with each side having responded directly to the other's central objections.
