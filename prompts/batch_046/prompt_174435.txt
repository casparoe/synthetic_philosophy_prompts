In debates over Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, philosophers frequently invoke the analogy that an external relation is like a piece of glue or a stitch in fabric: it binds together terms that would otherwise fall apart, yet if the glue is itself a term among others, it too needs binding, generating an infinite regress. The analogy is deployed both to motivate Bradley's monistic conclusion—that reality is an undivided whole within which distinctions are internal rather than externally stitched—and by critics such as Russell, who treat the regress as a reductio of treating relations as entities that require further relations. Audit this analogy with precision: map the terms, the relation, and the binding act onto one another; identify where the correspondence collapses—whether because glue possesses physical properties that metaphysical relations lack, because stitching presupposes a pre-existing fabric that has no parallel in Bradley's ontology, or because the image quietly imports a compositional, part-whole metaphysics that Bradley's absolute idealism precisely rejects. Separate the disanalogies that damage the argument from those that are merely decorative, and say whether the failure of the analogy undermines Bradley's conclusion, supports Russell's pluralism, or points to a third option such as taking relations as primitive.

To push the audit further, consider an updated version of the same image drawn from formal semantics: a syntactic tree in which grammatical relations link nodes without themselves being nodes that require linking. Ask whether this modern reconstruction escapes Bradley's regress, or whether it merely relocates the problem to meta-rules or to the relation between the tree and its interpretation. Does the tree analogy smuggle in a different set of commitments—say, about hierarchical structure or about the autonomy of syntax—that are just as questionable in metaphysics as the glue image was? Finally, evaluate whether the conclusion that only an absolute, internally related whole can avoid regress actually survives once both analogies are on the table, or whether the breakdown of the stitching image suggests that the problem was never about relations at all, but about a mistaken demand that every connection be mediated by an entity.
