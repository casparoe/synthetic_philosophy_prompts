I have no formal training in philosophy or computer science, but I have spent considerable time thinking on my own about a problem in artificial intelligence safety. The question is whether an instruction such as "avoid side effects" can be defined in purely structural or formal terms — for example, by measuring whether an action reduces the range of future states that the system or its environment could reach — without first providing a complete specification of what is valuable. I have noticed two opposing camps in the discussions I follow. One camp holds that such a structural definition is possible and that value-neutral measures like what I have been calling "reachability contraction" — where a side effect is defined as making some previously reachable state permanently unreachable, irrespective of whether anyone values that state — can do the necessary work. The other camp holds that any structural measure necessarily smuggles in value judgements about which states are worth preserving, so that "avoid side effects" cannot be defined without a full specification of value.

Please draft the document that these two camps might produce after a genuine adversarial collaboration. I would like it formatted as a numbered section of agreed claims, followed by a disagreements table. The agreed claims should include points both sides can genuinely accept about the nature of side effects, the capabilities of current systems, and the logical structure of the problem. The disagreements should be stated with precision, each phrased in language that its own camp would recognise and accept. Then identify the cruxes — the specific sub-questions such that resolving them would actually move at least one side — and for each crux provide a concrete case or reasoning step that both sides agree is relevant, together with the prediction each side makes about it.

In particular, please address whether my notion of "reachability contraction" succeeds in separating structural damage from value-laden harm, or whether it collapses into the value-dependent view. I do not want bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the argument developed on its own terms. Please provide your answer in British English.
