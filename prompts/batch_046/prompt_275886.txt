Write the full specification of desiderata that any adequate theory of epistemic closure must meet if it is to preserve deductive closure in non-skeptical domains without simply collapsing into reliabilism or accepting Nozick’s wholesale rejection of closure. Frame the theory as one that combines tracking-style conditions—sensitivity and adherence across modal space—with a restricted closure principle that operates over what I will call modal-gradient transmission: the idea that knowledge can propagate through entailments that stay within a local modal neighborhood but may break down across large modal leaps (e.g., from knowing one has hands to knowing one is not a brain-in-a-vat). Your list must include: (a) the specific cases and inference patterns the theory has to get right, including ordinary deductive closure, the lottery-paradox cascade, nested counterfactual dependencies in skeptical scenarios, and the heavyweight/lightweight knowledge distinction; (b) the conceptual distinctions it is forbidden to collapse, such as that between tracking failure and relevance-filter failure, between local sensitivity and global sensitivity, and between having knowledge and having the right to assert or rely on an entailment; (c) the consistency conditions it must satisfy with Nozick’s original tracking conditions and with the result that unrestricted closure plus unrestricted sensitivity generates contradiction in skeptical cases; and (d) an explicit judgment about which pairs or triples of these desiderata are jointly unsatisfiable and why—particularly whether modal-gradient transmission can be made precise without becoming ad hoc. For each desideratum, give the motivating case or argument behind it. Then, briefly and with definite judgment, score three existing approaches—Nozick’s pure tracking-without-closure, Dretske’s relevant-alternatives closure failure, and a safety-based modal-proximity view in the spirit of Sosa—against your list, noting which each fails and whether that failure is fatal by your lights. Take a committed position: do not merely list options, but defend which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable, which are camp-specific preferences, and whether the project of a closure-preserving tracking theory is ultimately coherent or self-undermining. Keep the weight on the criteria and their interactions rather than on the scorecard. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not ask for them in return.
