I'm putting together notes for a philosophy of religion discussion and want a consistency check before I present. I hold: (1) divine hiddenness is mostly an epistemic problem on our side — human minds evolved for social navigation, not for detecting non-physical agents, so the absence of obvious divine presence is like wondering why we can't smell ultraviolet; (2) a perfectly good God still has a defeasible moral obligation to make itself unmistakable to anyone who sincerely seeks it, even if that means accomodating our cognitive limits; (3) religious belief should be formed on publicly available arguments and evidence, not on private revelation or tradition; (4) hiddenness is actually a stronger objection to theism than the problem of evil, because it blocks any possible relationship rather than just making God seem unkind; and (5) God might remain hidden precisely because undeniable presence would coerce belief and destroy the kind of free repsonse that makes worship or love meaningful.

Do these hang together? I can already see a tension between (2) and (5) — if there's a duty to reveal, then staying hidden for freedom's sake looks like violating it. But is there a deeper conflict I'm missing, maybe between (1) and (4)? And is there an apparent tension that dissolves once you look closer? Please rank the real tensions by severity, say which commitment is least costly to revise, and don't just list them all as equally bad.
