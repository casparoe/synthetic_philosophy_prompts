Construct a thought experiment—realistic, contemporary, confined to a single paragraph, with no science fiction or exotic technology—that engineers a separation between Marx's concept of exploitation and his concept of alienation in the less commonly discussed direction: exploitation is absent, yet alienation is fully present. Specifically, design an organization in which workers collectively own the means of production, democratically control the surplus they generate, and are not subject to extraction by non-laboring owners—thereby failing Marx's conditions for exploitation—while simultaneously experiencing pronounced estrangement in all four dimensions Marx identifies: from the product of their labor, from the labor process itself, from their creative or species capacities, and from their fellow workers. The source of alienation must be structural and internal to the organizational form, not reducible to external coercion, physical danger, or an obviously mismanaged rule. After presenting the scenario, provide the following analysis: first, state precisely why Marx's conditions for exploitation fail to apply in your case; second, apply each of the four dimensions of alienation to the case and demonstrate that it is satisfied; third, isolate the specific institutional feature that produces this separation and explain why removing it causes the case to collapse back into the standard Marxist expectation that exploitation and alienation co-occur; fourth, present the strongest response available to a defender of Marx's linkage thesis—the claim that exploitation is the root cause of alienation—showing precisely how they would argue that either exploitation is secretly present after all or that the alienation described is not the genuine Marxian kind; fifth, conclude with your own assessment of whether the case succeeds in undermining the linkage thesis or merely exposes a boundary condition.
