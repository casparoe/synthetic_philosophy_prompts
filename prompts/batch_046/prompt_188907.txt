Write a deadpan parody of a corporate HR training manual titled “Resilience & Control: A Manager’s Guide to Stoic Leadership,” approximately 600 words, formatted as numbered sections with bullet-point action items. The manual should apply the Stoic dichotomy of control—judgments and impulses within our power, everything else (reputation, revenue, market conditions, colleagues’ emotions) outside it—to modern office politics, but exaggerate the distinction until it becomes absurd: classify team morale, economic downturns, and even a manager’s own past errors as strictly “external” and therefore not worthy of moral concern, while framing employees’ anxiety about layoffs or unfair evaluations as purely “internal” failures of judgment that must be corrected through self-discipline. The satire should target the productivity-oriented modern revival of Stoicism—the use of the dichotomy as an individual emotional-suppression tool that ignores the classical emphasis on virtue as the sole good, cosmopolitan justice, and reason as a shared social practice—rather than Stoicism itself. Maintain a consistently bureaucratic, deadpan voice; the irony must be legible without winking, and the humor should come from the manual’s internal consistency. Do not reference any named living person. Use authentic Stoic concepts (internals, externals, indifferents, virtue) in their distorted corporate form.
