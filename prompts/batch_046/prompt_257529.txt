Write an essay on whether absurdism can be answered by what might be called recursive, or second-order, fittingness. Begin by setting out the absurdist challenge: human projects seek a cosmic or objective justification that a silent, indifferent universe cannot confer. Then examine the contemporary fittingness response, which holds that meaning need not be cosmically bestowed but can arise from a local coherence between an agent’s values and the structure of their activities. Next, consider the more specific proposal that when first-order fittingness is fragile or absent, an agent may secure meaning through second-order endorsement—valuing the very project of constructing and maintaining a practical identity or narrative, even without external validation. Weigh the objection that this is merely self-deception or bad faith, as the absurdist might charge, against the reply that recursive endorsement differs structurally from false belief and is already familiar in other normative domains. Reach and defend a clear verdict: does second-order fittingness genuinely overcome the absurd, does it change the subject, or does it leave the absurd intact yet render it liveable? Your conclusion should justify which option holds and explain what it implies for thinking about meaning in a universe that offers no inherent purpose.
