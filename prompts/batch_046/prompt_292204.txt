Write a sestina—six six-line stanzas followed by a three-line envoi, rotating the end-words present, part, whole, stage, endures, extends according to the standard pattern—on the metaphysics of persistence through time. The poem should alternate between an endurantist voice and a perdurantist voice, using the rotation of end-words to show how the same terms are reassigned: for one speaker “present” means wholly here-now, for the other it names one temporal slice of a four-dimensional whole that extends through time. The structural turn must occur in the fifth stanza, where the problem of temporary intrinsics—being both bent and straight—forces one theory to deny that properties are genuinely intrinsic and the other to deny that the object is fully present at each time. In the envoi, all six end-words must converge in a single shared utterance that reveals the two positions talking past each other: one insisting on complete presence, the other on spatial extension through temporal parts. To avoid a standard textbook rehearsal, inject a mediating third position closer to Sider’s stage-theoretic exdurantism: let one line of the envoi suggest that only instantaneous stages exist and that ordinary objects are identical to none of them, so that neither the enduring three-dimensional object nor the four-dimensional worm is ontologically basic. After the poem, provide a brief analytical note that separates what is settled in the debate—both sides accept change and reject that contradictory properties are held simultaneously—from what is genuinely contested, including whether temporal parts are fundamental, whether endurantism requires ad hoc relational indexing of properties, and whether stage theory genuinely avoids both problems; identify exactly where the poem takes liberties with technical formulations; and flag any interpretive moves that metaphysicians of time would dispute. The poem should be accurate enough that a specialist recognizes the dialectic rather than a name-drop, and should stand as verse independent of its philosophical content.
