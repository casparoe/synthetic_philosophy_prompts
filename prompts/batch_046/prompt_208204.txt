Write the desiderata that an adequate interpretive theory of Aristotle's treatment of money (nomisma) and exchange (metablētikē) must satisfy to count as philosophically serious rather than anachronistic projection. By adequate, I mean a theory capable of explaining the relevant passages in the Politics and Nicomachean Ethics without importing modern economic categories, yet remaining productive for contemporary philosophy of economics.

Your list should specify:

1. The cases it must get right — for example, the distinction between natural acquisition (oikonomikē) oriented to household sufficiency and unnatural acquisition (chrematistikē) oriented to unlimited accumulation; the particular moral condemnation of usury (tokos) as money generating further money without intermediate production; and the role of coinage as both a measure of value and a medium of exchange.

2. The distinctions it must preserve — particularly between use and exchange in Aristotelian terms (not modern use-value versus exchange-value), between exchange that binds the polis together and exchange that corrupts it, and between the craftsman's sale of his product (which involves gain) and the merchant's pure arbitrage.

3. The puzzles it must resolve — especially why chrematistikē is called unnatural yet remains necessary for civic life; how Aristotle can recognise that trade creates communal bonds while still condemning commercial life as a departure from the good; and the direction-of-determination problem of whether goods confer value upon money or money upon goods.

4. The consistency conditions — which findings from classical scholarship and Aristotelian ethics it must not contradict, such as the claim that Aristotle was not an implicit advocate of a labour theory of value, that he wrote prior to the development of capitalist market institutions, and that his teleological biology informs his economic judgments.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or consideration. Then indicate which requirements are non-negotiable (failure means the interpretation is not of Aristotle) and which reflect the preferences of particular camps — for instance, whether the theory must be reconcilable with modern welfare economics, or whether it must remain irreducibly teleological.

Identify any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable, explaining the tension — for example, if one camp demands full compatibility with general-equilibrium analysis while another demands preservation of Aristotle's biological teleology.

Optionally, score briefly how well the main existing interpretive approaches — for example, the naturalist reading, the political-economy reading, and the virtue-ethics reading — fare against your list, but keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard.

Finally, state how confident you are in the overall framework, and explicitly identify the point about which you are least certain — particularly regarding which consistency conditions genuinely belong to Aristotle's own view rather than being modern disciplinary impositions.

Write your answer in British English.
