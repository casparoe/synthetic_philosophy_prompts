(1) i buy the Williams/Appiah line that big normative flips—like dueling going out of fashion—are honor/shame cascades, not the result of anyone being rationally persuaded. people don't argue their way into new norms; the status costs shift and everyone scrambles to adapt. (2) but i also think slavery's abolition was basically an economic and geopolitical story—industrial capital made slave labor inefficient, plus international pressure—not an honor cascade. trying to force it into the honor framework feels like post-hoc rationalization. (3) dueling, by contrast, died because the honor code literally inverted: challenging someone went from signaling gentlemanly courage to signaling brutishness. no economic driver needed, just a status inversion. (4) i want to say that for a change to count as genuine moral progress, not just power rearranging furniture, the norm has to be internalized—agents have to feel it as binding, not just comply because of sanctions or status costs. (5) and if honor is the real engine of change, then explicit moral argument is always epiphenomenal—reasons come after the shift, they don't cause it. (6) finally, i reject ad hoc pluralism: i shouldn't invoke seperate mechanisms for seperate cases just to save face.

so: am i consistent? my gut says there's a tension between (1) and (2) given (6)—if honor drives all moral revolutions, then slavery should be honor-driven too, but i'm insisting it's not. but maybe there's a principled distinction i'm missing? there's also something weird between (4) and (1)/(5): if change is just status-tracking, can internalization ever be more than shallow compliance? or is internalization just the psychological mechanism of an honor cascade? and does (5) actually undermine (4) by making moral reasoning irrelevant to progress?

i don't want a map of every logical relation—i want an audit. tell me which commitments have to go, in order of severity, and what the cheapest consistent package looks like. be direct: am i being incoherent or just holding a defensible pluralism? and please don't just list options—take a stand.
