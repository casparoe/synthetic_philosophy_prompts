Two friends, Maya and Jake, got into it at a bar about whether consciousness is just a user illusion — like the desktop interface on a laptop. Maya said yeah, the brain builds a simplified self-model so the organism can navigate social stuff, but there's no inner theater, just a virtual object. Jake said that sounds self-defeating: if the "user" is also part of the illusion, then who's actually being fooled? There has to be a real experiencer getting duped.

Maya came back that the organism is the user, but the user-model is just a data structure. A computer runs a desktop without a ghost in the machine. Jake said okay, but then why does consciousness feel like something? He brought up this thing he called phenominal transparency — the illusion is invisible to itself, so you can never correct it. He said if it's transparent, calling it an illusion is misleading, because normal illusions are ones you can eventually see through.

Maya said transparency just means the brain doesn't represent its own representational nature. She went further: introspecion isn't access to some inner reality, it's more like blind editing — the brain updates the self-model without showing the source code. So asking "who feels it?" is like asking "who reads the source code?" Nobody, it's just processed.

Jake then pivoted. If the user-model is basically a PR department for the brain, then blame and moral responsibility are just social feedback loops, not tracking anything real about agency. Maya said that's actually consistent — responsibility tracks the representations in the user-model because that's what social coordination needs, not because there's a metaphysical self.
