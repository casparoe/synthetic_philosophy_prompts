Consider the position that the paradox of fiction—our emotional engagement with characters and events we know to be invented—dissolves once we reject the "unitary intentionality" assumption inherited from Brentano-style philosophy of mind. On this view, emotional responses do not require a single, belief-dependent doxastic channel; instead, they can be directed at intentional objects that are "presented as real within a framing context" without being believed to exist outside that context. The defender argues that fiction supplies a structured framing context (narrative perspective, affective cueing, imaginative mandate) that activates genuine emotional mechanisms, but these mechanisms operate with suspended ontological commitment rather than false belief. Thus there is no paradox: we feel real grief or fear, yet their intentional objects are properly characterized as fictionally present rather than real and believed to exist.
