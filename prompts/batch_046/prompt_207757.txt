I'm auditing my own position on historical explanation and need you to trace the fault lines precisely rather than flagging everything. My commitments are:

(1) Historical explanation is irreducibly narrative: explaining a revolution or war requires tracing how contingent choices and meanings unfolded, not subsuming events under a sociological law.

(2) This form tracks something real—call it modal fragility—the fact that at several junctures the process could have easily diverged, and that explaining why it did not requires citing specific intermediate steps rather than initial conditions alone.

(3) I reject determinism about human affairs, yet I also reject that quantum indeterminacy propagates upward, so macro-contingency must be genuine without being reducible to micro-randomness.

(4) If determinism held, modal fragility would collapse into illusion and narrative explanation would be at best a cognitive convenience with no special epistemic status.

(5) Historians should still use comparative and statistical methods, but only as diagnostics to check whether a narrative is idiosyncratic—not as replacements for narrative form.

(6) Good narratives must support robust counterfactuals (had X not occurred, Y would not have followed), and this counterfactual dependence is precisely what law-based explanations erase by treating outcomes as necessary given antecedents.

(7) Predictive social science should seek covering laws for macro-patterns (e.g., demographic regularities), and I see no conflict in pursuing narrative explanation for particular events alongside law-seeking for general patterns.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the real tensions by severity with the cheapest revision for each. Is the deepest problem between (2) and (3)—the metaphysical status of modal fragility—or between (4) and (7)—whether seeking covering laws for patterns implicitly undermines the contingency that makes narrative explanation more than heuristic? Identify the single revision that would clean up the most conflicts, and do not treat every friction as equally serious.
