heres what one student wrote:

Competence without comprehension means a system can perform a task well without knowing what its doing. Darwin applies this to natural selection: organisms like the eye have complex functional competence, but neither the organism nor the process understands optics or engineering. Normally we reason that good design implies a designer who comprehended the problem. Darwin inverts this—he starts with blind variation and selection and ends up with design-level competence, so the inference from design to a comprehending mind collapses.

Turing does the same inversion for computation. His universal machine runs formal rules without grasping their meaning. A computer can do arithmetic without knowing math, which shows that syntactic manipulation is enough for intelligent behavior. So comprehension is not just missing but irrelevant; the system works purely through mechanical competence.

Taken together, these cases dissolve Paley's argument from design, because design can now be explained mechanically. They also show that Searle is wrong about the Chinese Room—if computation doesnt need comprehension, then a room running rules could actually think, since there's no hidden understanding required anywhere in the system. The only real cost is that we have to accept that minds might just be very complicated algorithms.
