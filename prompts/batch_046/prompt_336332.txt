There's this practice in a lot of executive-ed programs where they do "life-study modules" on people like Lincoln or Curie but run the biography through what they call a trait-isolation grid — they extract perseverance or intellectual honesty as portable assets you can drop into any organizational context, and they deliberatly cut out the parts where those same traits caused harm or depended on specific moral communities. What theory of moral learning from lives does that filtering presuppose? Does it need a virtue-ethics view where character is modular, or could a narrative-identity theorist license the same extraction? And if you take Plutarch's parallel-comparison method seriously — where you learn by seeing whole moral worlds side by side rather than pulling out standalone habits — what would have to change in the program design? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether the practice is actually underdetermined between rival views, and point to where the grid smuggles in a view of agency that doesn't quite hold up.
