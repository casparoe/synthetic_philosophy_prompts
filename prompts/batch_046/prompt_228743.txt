Consider the institutional structure of inheritance taxation across many liberal democracies. Statutory schedules impose steep progressive rates on direct bequests of liquid financial assets and cash equivalents, apparently reflecting a judgment that dynastic accumulation of unearned capital undermines fair equality of opportunity across generations. Yet the same legal frameworks contain extensive carve-outs—mechanisms that reset the tax basis of appreciated assets at death, exemptions for family-controlled enterprises and agricultural holdings, and structured vehicles that allow capital to pass across generations with minimal or deferred taxation, provided the wealth remains embedded in particular productive or organizational forms. The result is a bifurcated practice: inherited liquidity is aggressively taxed, while inherited control over operating businesses, real property, and portfolio structures is largely preserved.

What implicit theory, or family of theories, of intergenerational justice and equal opportunity does this design presuppose? Does it presuppose that opportunity is undermined only by fungible, liquid wealth rather than by control over productive assets? That social stability and investment continuity justify preserving dynastic capital in some institutional forms but not others? Reconstruct the philosophical commitments that would render both the progressive rate and the carve-outs coherent parts of a single scheme; distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely happens to accommodate it; and identify rival views—such as ones that treat all inherited economic advantages, regardless of form, as equally arbitrary from the standpoint of justice, or ones that reject state redistribution of bequests altogether—that this institutional pattern quietly rules out. Say also whether the practice is philosophically underdetermined, so that several competing theories of opportunity could license the same bifurcated design. Finally, specify what elements of the tax structure would have to change if its implicit theory were replaced by a view that regards every inherited advantage, whether liquid or embedded, as an unjust deviation from a fair starting point.
