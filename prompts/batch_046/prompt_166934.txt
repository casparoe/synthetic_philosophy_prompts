As a retired experimental condensed-matter physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of science, I have been studying the long-running dispute over whether the quantum wavefunction is psi-ontic—representing an element of physical reality—or psi-epistemic—representing limited information about some underlying ontic state. Despite well-known results such as the PBR theorem and Spekkens' toy model, the debate seems to have reached an impasse: both sides accept the quantum formalism, both acknowledge the no-go theorems, and yet neither has conceded ground. I suspect the stall may not be a straightforward metaphysical disagreement but something more structural. Might the two camps be answering different questions—one about the ontology of state space, the other about the dynamics of hidden variables—while using the same vocabulary? Or could there be an unacknowledged empirical disagreement about preparation contextuality that the standard framing obscures? Please diagnose the nature of this disagreement: is it verbal, substantive but empirically hidden, or rooted in bedrock intuitions about physical reality that argument cannot reach? And given your diagnosis, what kind of progress—whether a formal distinction, a new theorem, or a reframing of the ontological question—would actually be capable of moving the debate?
