I sit on our town council's heritage panel and we've hit this mess over Bishop's Lane — named after a Victorian mill owner who built our library but also ran plantations abroad. Half the residents want it kept cos it's just where they live now and the honour bit faded years back; others want a rename. I've been chewing over this idea of mnemonic drift, where a commemorative name stops pointing to the person and becomes pure geographic habit, so renaming might actually erase local memory rather than correct it. Could you draft the memo for Thursday's meeting? I need a clear recomendation — rename, keep, or keep with a contextual plaque — plus a concrete timeframe, and it must seriously engage the argument that the original meaning is already lost rather than just dismissing it. Don't cite legal definitions as if that settles it; actually tackle whether adding a plaque just hides the honour in plain sight, and say what happens if we kick the can down the road for another ten years. Keep it fairly short and write it like a working document, not an essay.
