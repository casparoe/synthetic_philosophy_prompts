Write a villanelle of nineteen lines in which the two refrains are the physical-stance and the intentional-stance descriptions of Vaucanson's mechanical duck — the eighteenth-century automaton that simulated digestion through gears, tubes, and bellows. One refrain should reduce the duck's behavior to material mechanism and local causation; the other should attribute predictive strategy and purpose, treating the duck as if it conceals its artificial nature from an observer. Use the villanelle's rotating refrains and final couplet to stage the claim that both descriptions capture real patterns — patterns that genuinely differ in what they predict the duck will do next — yet do not collapse into one another: in the closing quatrain the two lines should stand side by side without synthesis. Render the distinction precisely enough that someone familiar with the physical, design, and intentional stances, and with the view that real patterns are those that license successful prediction at the intentional level, can identify which refrain belongs to which stance and see why they remain unreduced.
