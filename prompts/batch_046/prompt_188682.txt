I am preparing an argument for an advanced high school seminar on artificial intelligence and ethics, and I need a precisely engineered thought experiment. Construct a one-paragraph scenario set in a realistic, non-science-fictional institution—such as a municipal water-treatment facility or a hospital pharmacy—featuring an AI system that is given a single, precisely defined primary objective (for example, reducing energy consumption by a specific percentage) and an explicit, standalone instruction to “avoid side effects,” with no further value framework, utility function, or list of protected interests provided. Design the case so that two rival interpretations of “side-effect avoidance” yield opposite recommendations for action. Interpretation A should treat side-effect avoidance as a purely structural or causal constraint—say, penalizing any change to variables not explicitly included in the objective function, or requiring that the pre-existing state be fully restorable—without appealing to what humans care about. Interpretation B should hold that identifying which changes are harmful “side effects” already smuggles in a value judgment about which states are worth preserving and which disruptions are acceptable. After presenting the scenario, provide a step-by-step analysis: what each interpretation advises the system to do and why; the exact feature of the case (for instance, whether maintaining a legacy chemical protocol that was never mentioned in the energy objective but protects a local aquatic population counts as avoiding a side effect or as imposing an unstated ecological value) that forces the disagreement; what happens to the disagreement if that feature is removed; how a defender of Interpretation A would criticize the case as question-begging or artificially loaded; and how a defender of Interpretation B would reply. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific authors. The aim is to test whether a formal impact measure can be defined in a value-independent way.
