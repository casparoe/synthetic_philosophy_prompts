After a long argument with a friend about whether laws of nature are mere regularities or involve necessary connections, I want to pressure-test the distinction with a concrete case. Construct a thought experiment—realistic, no exotic physics or omniscient predictors, confined to a single paragraph describing the scenario—in which a research team synthesizes a biochemical pathway that produces a stable compound only in the presence of a non-natural catalyst that never occurs outside the lab. The pathway operates with perfect uniformity across thousands of trials, yet every condition required for it is artificially maintained and physically impossible in any natural environment. Then demonstrate how a Humean best-system account and an anti-Humean necessary-connection account deliver opposing verdicts on whether this regularity is a law of nature. For each theory, state the relevant nearby worlds, why it gives its verdict, and which feature of the case does the work. Finish by explaining what happens to the disagreement if we remove the artificial-environment condition, and how a defender of the view that comes out badly would attack the scenario.
