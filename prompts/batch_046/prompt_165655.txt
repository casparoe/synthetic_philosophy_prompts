As a doctoral student in moral philosophy working on problems of aggregation and moral scale, I am investigating whether the classic tension between torture and dust-speck harms can be dissolved once we reject the assumption that all harms are intersubstitutable scalar units. Write a dialogue between Dr. A, who defends a structural discontinuity thesis—holding that severe harms possess irreducibly relational properties (they rupture narrative identity, undermine agential continuity, and generate second-order obligations of repair) that prevent them from being decomposed into commensurable micro-units, and therefore lexically dominate any cardinal aggregation of tiny harms—and Dr. B, who advances a view of context-weighted continuous aggregation, arguing that micro-harms carry non-uniform moral weights determined by causal embedding, epistemic observability, and proximity to vulnerable subjects, and that once these weights are applied with appropriate discounting for partial observability, sufficiently large aggregates can in principle outweigh an isolated severe harm without collapsing into absurdity. The exchange should be genuinely reciprocal: each interlocutor must respond directly to the other’s specific claims about relational ontology versus weighting functions, address whether moral granularity survives under conditions of epistemic opacity, and examine whether their disagreement is ultimately about the metaphysics of harm (discrete versus continuous) or about the normative limits of aggregation given verification constraints. Neither speaker should invoke bibliographies, named authorities, or citation lists; both should argue from first principles. Ensure neither view is treated as a strawman, and have the dialogue conclude not with a forced resolution but with a precise identification of the crux—specifically, whether accepting continuous aggregation requires abandoning the idea that some harms alter the moral topology of a situation in ways that mere multiplication cannot replicate.
