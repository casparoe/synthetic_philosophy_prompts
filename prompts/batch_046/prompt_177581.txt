ok so i'm putting together my notes for a metaphysics semenar next week and want to check if my position on persistence actually hangs together before i open my mouth. here's roughly where i've landed:

1. i'm an endurantist - ordinary objects like chairs and people are wholly present at every time they exist, not spread out like four-dimensional worms with temporal parts.

2. i'm also a presentist, so past and future times (and the objects in them) literally don't exist right now.

3. for temporary intrinsics, i take the relational solution: being bent or being tired are really two-place relations between the object and a specific time, not intrinsic monadic properties.

4. i think causal continuity is necessary for persistence over time - if there's no direct causal chain linking me-now to me-tomorrow, then it's not me, even if there's perfect qualitative match.

5. on personal identity, i apply psychological continuity theory but to an enduring self: it's the same enduring person as long as there's a continuous stream of consciousness and memory linking the stages.

6. i think vagueness about persistence conditions for ordinary objects is purely epistemic - there are sharp boundaries, we just don't always know them. but for persons, i think metaphysical vagueness is impossible: it can't be indeterminate whether i'm the same person.

7. i reject ship-of-theseus-style gradual part-replacement for material objects, but i think a person could survive complete neural replacement if the psychological profile transfers smoothly.

8. and i don't think perdurantism can explain cross-temporal causation anyway, since a temporal part existing at only one instant can't causally interact with parts at other instants if causation requires shared temporal location.

do these commitments pull against each other? i'm especially suspicious that (2) and (3) might clash, and that (6) is treating persons special in a way that undermines (4) or (1). but maybe some of these tensions are only apparent. can you audit the package: rank the real tensions by severity, say which pairs only look bad but dissolve on inspection, and for each real problem tell me the least costly revision? please answer in American English, and don't just list everything as equally bad - i want to know what actually has to go.
