In my philosphy of mind reading group we've been circling Chalmers-style zombies for like a year and nobody's moved. The basic setup is familiar: a physical duplicate of me with no conscious experience is concievable, therefore consciousness isn't metaphysically necessary on physicalism. The dualists say this reveals a real modal gap; the physicalists say it's just epistemic conceivability, like water not being H2O in our heads pre-chemistry. But I think we're actually running two different mental exercises and pretending they're one.

One camp does subtractive imagination: take a conscious human, mentally peel away the feel, check that the physics stays intact. The other does replicative imagination: construct the duplicate from physical specs alone and ask whether consciousness has to be there. The first checks whether consciousness feels detachable in our model; the second checks whether our concept of physics already includes it. So when we ask "is a zombie concievable?" we might be asking about conceptual separation, or about metaphysical completeness, and we're not noticing.

Is the stall really about modal epistemology, or is it a verbal disagreement about what counts as successful mental simulation? Or are there hidden bedrock intuitions about phenomenal transparency that neither side can argue for? What's settled here, what's genuinely contested among people who actually work on this, and what—if anything—would actually shift it: a formal result, a distinction, or just admitting we're answering different questions?
