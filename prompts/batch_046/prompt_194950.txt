In a philosophy discussion thread, two posters—call them Maya and Leo—debated whether disagreements about the social construction of race are mainly verbal disputes. (1) Maya said the dispute dissolves once we distinguish "race is not a biological natural kind" from "race is not a real social kind": critics and defenders talk past each other, and once disambiguated only a normative question about policy remains. (2) Leo replied that calling it verbal is not neutral arbitration but a rhetorical dismissal: separating the metaphysical claim from the normative one assumes that describing an institutional kind can be done without taking a stand on the practices that constitute it, which is exactly what's contested. (3) Maya answered that this conflates levels—descriptive sociology can be shared while normative commitments differ, so dissolution of the metaphysical ambiguity doesn't erase the ethical debate, it just relocates it cleanly. (4) Leo said that for institutional kinds the descriptive is already normative, since identifying which interactions "constitute" the kind requires selecting which practices count, and that selection is value-laden; therefore you cannot dissolve the metaphysical dispute without smuggling in a specific metaontological view about language and institutions.
