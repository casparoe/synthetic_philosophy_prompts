so i retired from physics a few years back and lately ive been hanging around in a philosophy discord trying to make sense of consciousness. last week two people -- lets call them dave and lina -- got stuck in an argument about panpsychism and that combination problem thing. dave said look, consciousness is probably just a fundamental property like charge or spin, and the combination problem is overblown. he said its exactly like chemistry: individual atoms dont have wetness but H2O does, so micro-experiences can bond into macro-experiences through some phenomenal bonding relation. he thinks once you accept that, the hard problem dissolves because youre not deriving experience from non-experience.

lina pushed back hard. she said chemical emergence works because we can reduce macro-properties to structural-functional relations among parts, but consciousness isnt structural -- its phenomenal. she brought up the palette problem (if micro-experiences are simple, where does the richness of human color experience come from?) and the structural mismatch problem (brain structure is spatial and geometric, experience isnt). then she said daves chemistry analogy actually undermines him: we dont need a special bonding law for wetness, we just need physics, but for consciousness dave has to invent an extra primitive relation -- phenomenal bonding -- that does no other explanatory work in physics.

dave came back with what he called the small-world problem. he said if consciousness is everywhere, why am i not experiencing being the andromeda galaxy? his answer was local coherence and causal integration, just like why a swarm of bees acts like one unit only locally. he said this shows panpsychism predicts exactly the bounded subjects we observe, so its not unfalsifiable hand-waving.

lina said thats circular -- youre using the existence of bounded subjects to define the bonding relation, then claiming the relation explains the subjects. she also said dave never addressed whether phenomenal bonding creates one genuinely new macro-subject or just a cluster of micro-subjects that happen to correlate. if its the latter, panpsychism doesnt solve the hard problem for macro-subjects, it just denies they exist, which is wild.

they both turned to me since im the science guy, but im out of my depth. whos right about which part? is daves chemistry analogy actually apt or does it miss the point about phenomenal properties being non-structural? does linas palette and structural mismatch objection stick, or can bonding solve it? and is daves small-world / local coherence reply a real solution or just definitional hand-waving? also what are they both ignoring -- maybe mental causation, or whether panpsychism even needs to explain unified consciousness rather than just correlated micro-experiences?

please adjudacate point by point. im not looking for a bibliography, just a clear verdict on the arguments as they stand.
