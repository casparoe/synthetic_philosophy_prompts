I am preparing a seventy-five minute workshop for second-year philosophy undergraduates who have just finished a unit on varieties of physicalism but have never encountered the causal exclusion argument in its original form; several have recently read popular neuroscience writing that casually invokes “downward causation,” and I want to preempt the confusion between biological emergence and mental causation before it hardens. I need a teaching plan rather than the explanation itself: specifically, how to introduce the exclusion argument as it applies to mental properties that are claimed to be non-reductive yet physically realized, using a non-mental analog as the carrying case.

Please lay out the dependency order of the conceptual pieces—distinguishing causal closure from causal exclusion, clarifying supervenience without reduction, and identifying which must be fixed in place before the exclusion problem can arise rather than being mistaken for an ordinary overdetermination puzzle. Advise whether a biological analog—such as the causal efficacy of a particular protein conformation in initiating a signaling cascade—can serve as the single carrying scenario, and if so, specify exactly what properties it must have and lack to preserve the structural parallel to mental causation, together with the precise pivot by which I move from that analog to the mental case without losing the argument’s force. Then map the misconceptions I should expect in the order they will likely surface: for instance, that mental causation is analogous to two people pushing a rock, that physical causal closure renders mental states merely epiphenomenal rather than excluded, and that rejecting reductionism automatically rescues mental efficacy; for each, indicate the exact point in the explanatory arc where it should be headed off. Suggest one targeted question I can pose at the end to test whether the students have grasped the specific exclusion structure rather than merely internalized the slogan that “physics does everything.” Finally, state explicitly which part of the surrounding dialectic—perhaps the interaction with emergentist replies or the comparison with Davidson’s anomalous monism—I should openly bracket and say is beyond the scope of this session.
