so i've been chewing on that Hofstadter idea where an 'I' isn't locked to one skull but can kind of persist in other brains if the pattern is right. not the usual ship of theseus stuff—more like, when does someone else actually become a container for your self?

here are my gut verdicts. i want to know if there's one principle behind them or if i'm just being messy.

1. my partner picked up my exact verbal tics and decision style after twenty years. when she reacts the way i would, i feel a flicker of 'that's me too.' verdict: partly distributed, but only in those moments.

2. a perfect digital copy of my brain runs on a server and claims to be me. i think that's me, full stop, even if the original body is still walking around. so the 'I' can be in two places at once.

3. after split-brain surgery both hemispheres talk and each says 'i am the original.' i feel like that's broken, not shared—presence in two bodies doesn't always mean distribution.

4. a close friend went through trauma and started holding beliefs i used to hold, but she never knew me well. i don't feel distributed there; it's coincidence.

5. here's a weird case i made up—call it the mirror-protocol—where five people share every memory for a year until they can't tell whose reaction is whose. if they all feel like one blended self, i think the 'I' is genuinely spread across all five. but if they still feel like distinct nodes with shared data, then it's not distribution.

6. my kid imitates my laugh and values. that feels like inheritance, not distribution. the pattern is there but it's not carrying my 'I'.

so what's going on? is it pattern fidelity, causal continuity, or subjective recognition? and here's hte one i keep flipping on: what if a neuroscientist maps my connectome onto a chimpanzee with enough precision that it starts showing my preferences? is that me distributed, or just a weird copy?

also tell me what your principle predicts for the mirror-protocol group if they have to make one medical decision for all of them. i feel like my verdicts mostly track whether there's a single center of agency, but case 2 breaks that since both copies have agency.

if there's no single principle that saves all these, which verdict should i drop?
