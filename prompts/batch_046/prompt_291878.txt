As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy and decision theory, I am organizing a 90-minute workshop for twelve intellectually curious adults with no prior exposure to game theory. I want them to experience firsthand why the Kalai-Smorodinsky bargaining solution exerts a distinct intuitive pull—particularly through its monotonicity axiom—without my lecturing them on axioms in advance.

Please design a runnable, table-top activity. Divide the group into six pairs. Each pair receives a physical board representing a convex feasible set of shared resources (e.g., 60 points of laboratory time, divided between two research proposals represented by colored tokens). In round one, each player rolls a die modified by a hidden card to establish an endogenous disagreement point (their fallback allocation if negotiation fails). In round two, one player—determined by a random draw—discovers an improved outside option: their individual fallback rises, expanding the utopia point in their direction only, while the other player's fallback and the overall resource pool remain unchanged. The pair must then agree on a new division of the same 60 points.

The rules should require explicit proposals and allow rejection, with rejected pairs losing half their fallback points. Use only tangible tokens and verbal agreement; no real money. After 20 minutes of negotiation, halt all pairs simultaneously for a structured debrief.

The debrief should map three specific observed behaviors—(i) the advantaged player demanding a larger share, (ii) the disadvantaged player resisting by appealing to the prior agreement, and (iii) the group’s confusion about whether the total pool should also grow—to the formal distinction between monotonicity and Nash’s independence of irrelevant alternatives. Be explicit about which feature of the Kalai-Smorodinsky formalism the token board cannot capture—specifically, the continuity and convexity conditions that guarantee uniqueness in the two-player case—and state exactly how you will supply that missing piece verbally without undermining the experiential lesson.

Finally, identify one failure mode that predictably occurs the first time this is run with novices, and describe the precise facilitator intervention that corrects it without revealing the answer. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or suggested readings; the participants have no access to external sources, and neither do I in this session.
