I'm preparing a set of philosophical prompts for a class project on historical inevitability and path dependence, and I would like you to write one for me. Compose a deadpan parody of a high-school guidance counselor's newsletter column — approximately 600 words — in the earnest, bureaucratic voice of an advisor who genuinely believes that every student's life follows strict path dependence. The counselor should treat individual biography as if it were an economic technology subject to lock-in and increasing returns: once a freshman chooses an elective, their entire career trajectory becomes historically inevitable, and any attempt to switch majors is framed as a dangerous disruption of continuity. Apply real concepts — contingency, lock-in, path dependence, the distinction between collective-scale outcomes and individual choice — but only through absurd personal examples, such as insisting that the cafeteria menu has been frozen since a 1952 procurement decision and that failing chemistry permanently commits one to pharmaceutical history. The satire should let a knowledgeable reader recognize that you understand the actual debate — particularly the error of treating social history like a QWERTY keyboard or applying collective-scale contingency arguments to single lives — without ever stating those objections outright. Invent one mock-technical term, for instance "biographical increasing returns," and deploy it with deadpan academic seriousness. Avoid citations, named scholars, bibliographies, or references to specific texts. Present the entire piece as continuous prose only: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, exactly as one would write in a long-form letter or email.
