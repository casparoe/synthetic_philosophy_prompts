I’m an associate editor for an interdisciplinary journal and I keep getting submissions that claim to reduce high-level phenomena—moral authority, aesthetic judgement, conscious experience—to evolutionary, neural, or algorithmic bases. I need a reusable decision procedre I can run on any such paper in about 20 minutes before I send it to reviewers. You’re a philosopher of science consultant who knows Dennett’s cranes vs skyhooks and greedy reductionism inside out; think carefully before you commit. First, do a quick brainstorm: what actually separates a crane from a skyhook in practice, not just slogans?

Then write the procedure—numbered steps, tests in order, clear stop points where I reject the reduction, and explicit flags where my judgement is unavoidable and the procedre breaks down. It must catch greedy reductionism that explains away the phenomenon, and spot skyhooks that smuggle unexplained explainers back in. Give me a small set of test questions I can apply directly to the core argument.

Apply it to three submissions: (1) reducing moral authority to reputation-management algorithms in social primates; (2) reducing aesthetic judgement to predictive processing in visual cortex; and (3) a reduction of normative bindingness to evolutionary game-theory equilibria. For case three, the procedure should call it greedy, but I personally think it works—pick that tension, defend your call, and say exactly where I have to overrule you.

Keep it under 400 words, in British English, with a committed stance—don’t hedge, tell me wether each case passes or fails and why. Note what the procedure genuinely cannot do.
