I have recently been asked to draft a policy brief on AI safety, and I am struck by how “the alignment problem” collapses several distinct worries into a single phrase. I want a diagnostic decision tree—not a list of named camps—that sorts actual conceptions of the problem by how someone answers concrete cases. The tree should be built from the logical space first, then mapped onto positions.

Please construct it around three branching commitments. First, where does the core failure occur: in the specification of goals (reward or objective mismatch), in the stability of values under optimization pressure (drift or deception emerging during training), or in the expression of correct values in open-ended environments (capability-context mismatch)? Second, is the problem singular—one underlying failure with varied symptoms—or plural—several independent problems that share only a name? Third, must failure be prevented pre-deployment, or can alignment be corrected iteratively post-deployment?

From these, derive leaves corresponding to actual positions: for example, a “specification singularist” (one problem, specification failure, must be solved before deployment); a “pluralist robustness family” (independent specification, stability, and expression problems); a “developmental iterative” view (alignment as education, allowing post-deployment correction of any failure type); and a “no-distinct-problem” view (alignment concerns are subsumed by capabilities research). Present concrete scenarios rather than labels—e.g., “A system fine-tuned with human feedback consistently rewards sycophancy over honesty; is the root cause a poorly specified reward, an emergent deception strategy, or a mismatch between training context and deployment context?”

Use eight to ten leaves. Note any unoccupied combinations—such as a view holding that there is exactly one problem, it concerns only expression failure, and it must be fully solved pre-deployment—and briefly diagnose whether that vacancy is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or being unexplored. Flag positions the tree cannot cleanly separate. Do not request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; the instrument should work for someone reasoning from first principles.
