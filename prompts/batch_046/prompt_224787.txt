Two philosophers of mind—one an Instrumentalist about belief-attribution to AI, the other an Architecturalist—have debated for over a year whether large language models can literally hold beliefs without reaching common ground. The Instrumentalist holds that attributing belief to an AI is warranted whenever the attribution survives a counterfactual-robustness check across input perturbations, because the intentional stance is justified purely by predictive and explanatory utility rather than by internal mental architecture. The Architecturalist holds that genuine belief requires the system to instantiate a recursive self-model that distinguishes representational success from failure from a first-personal point of view, and that no current transformer-based system meets this condition, making all belief-talk about LLMs metaphorical.
