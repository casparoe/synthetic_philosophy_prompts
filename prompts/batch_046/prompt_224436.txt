I work as a software engineer on a platform's trust-and-safety team, with no formal training in philosophy, and lately I have been trying to make our content-policy reviews less arbitrary. After a long discussion with friends about whether moral frameworks can actually guide engineering decisions, I found myself drawn to Jeremy Bentham's treatment of sexual morality—particularly his insistence that restrictions on sexual conduct must be justified by direct measurements of pleasure and pain rather than by social disgust, symbolic offense, or vague claims of moral corruption. I want you to translate that principle into an explicit, reusable decision procedure that my team could run on any proposal to restrict, demote, or allow a category of sexual expression. The deliverable should be a step-by-step flow in words: list the questions in the order they must be asked, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to each, mark the precise nodes where the procedure stops with a definitive recommendation (either "no restriction warranted" or "restrict only if condition X is met"), and clearly flag the nodes where the decision is not empirical but a values judgment that must be escalated to a human reviewer rather than solved by the framework. The procedure must be able to distinguish harm experienced by direct participants, harm experienced by non-consenting audiences, and diffuse social harms that Bentham treated with deep skepticism; it should also specify what to do when the felicific calculus produces contradictory measurements across different groups.

Then apply the procedure to three concrete proposals: first, algorithmically suppressing discussion groups centered on consensual non-monogamy; second, maintaining an absolute ban on all visual depictions of historical pederastic relationships, even within clearly educational documentary material; and third, permitting user-generated descriptions of legal sexual practices that are widely characterized as degrading, where the only documented negative effect is viewer disgust rather than demonstrable psychological injury. For one of these applications—specifically the one where the framework's recommendation will most conflict with common institutional intuition—please explain exactly why the procedure yields that result and where it breaks down or requires a judgment call that the engineer running the checklist cannot make alone. Finally, state explicitly what this procedure cannot do: for example, resolving inter-group conflicts in pleasure and pain accounting, substituting for legal compliance obligations, or generating quantitative weights for qualitative harms.
