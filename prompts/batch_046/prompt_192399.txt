Hey, been a lurker on these forums for years and I'm trying to formalize this tension between James's will to believe and Clifford's evidentialism for a post on longtermist epistemics. I think I've walked myself into an argument that proves way too much — either Clifford kills all far-future belief or James turns rationalist forecasting into collective wishful thinking. Here goes:

(1) Clifford is right that believing on insufficient evidance is wrong not just privately but for the whole network, since beliefs spread through testimony and shape coordinated action.
(2) James allows an exception only for options that are living, forced, and momentuos — where you genuinely can't suspend judgment and the stakes actually matter.
(3) Take a concrete longtermist claim like "transformative AI will arrive before 2050." The evidence is necessarily thin, the decision is forced (we have to invest or regulate now), and the stakes are enormous.
(4) So by James, the community is permitted to will belief in either the optimistic or pessimistic forecast, depending on what feels "live" to them.
(5) But if belief-formation works this way, then a rationalist cluster that emotionally prefers optimism can coordinate on it without evidential check, and vice versa for pessimism.
(6) That collapses the distinction between legitimate longtermist belief and pure collective self-deception.
(7) Therefore either Clifford applies and all far-future belief is epistemically forbidden, or James applies and longtermist belief is just preference-satisfaction.

I want to keep (1) and (2) if I can — Clifford's social point feels solid, and James's taxonomy is genuinely useful. The slip feels like it's somewhere between (3) and (5), maybe about whether "living" scales to groups at all, or maybe I'm missing a hidden constraint in James that blocks arbitrary willing. Where's the exit? And if I do have to drop one of (1) or (2), which one hurts less?
