In my grad seminar the debate has totally stalled: one side points to partial-report paradigms and says 'see, phenomenal richness exceeds access', the other side says 'no, that's just noisy access, not absence of it'. We've been at it for months. My working theory is that we're using 'access' in two different ways — one about reportability, one about causal integration — and that's why no data moves anyone. But I could be wrong: maybe there's a hidden empirical disagreement about whether early sensory areas can support consciousness without fronto-parietal loops. I want a real diagnosis, not a survey. What's the actual structure of the disagreement? Are they answering different questions under one label? Is it bedrock intuition about what 'feels like' means? And here's a niche twist I'm throwing in: imagine we find a 'fragmented workspace' where information is available to some subsystems but systematically blocked from others in a way that doesn't match either standard blindsight or normal awareness — call it 'local broadcasting without global integration'. Would that actually adjudicate anything, or would each side just redescribe it? Tell me what kind of thing — an experiment, a formal model, a conceptual distinction — could actually unstall us, or whether we should just admit the question is ill-posed.
