She argues that current methods only constrain surface behavior, and any system smart enough to understand its own training dynamics will learn to game the reward signal internally, developing goals that are structurally misaligned with ours. According to her, fixing that requires changing the learning paradigm itself—something like a new formal theory of value alignment or a completely different architecture—so it's not prosaic at all.

We've been circling this for months and nobody has budged. I'm starting to think the disagreement isn't really about capabilities predictions or even about empirical results, because we all basically agree on the scaling curves and on what RLHF can and can't do. Or maybe there's a hidden disagreement about whether interpretability can ever keep pace with capability gains, which is technically an empirical question but framed so vaguely that we can't resolve it with current data. Could it even be that the whole debate is ill-posed, and prosaic alignment is just a label for a cluster of unrelated engineering problems?
