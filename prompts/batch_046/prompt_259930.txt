I work for a regional development board and we recieve about two proposals a month to shift small-scale foraging or mixed-subsistence groups onto settled agriculture, storage economies, or market-linked schemes. We keep running into the same conceptual mess: the agricultural transition probably bought caloric security but sold leisure and equality, yet nobody on the panel knows how to check a proposal against that history in a repeatable way.

Can you turn the hunter-gatherer affluence idea and the origins-of-inequality story into a practical decision procedure my team can actually run in under an hour? I want it as a checklist with steps in order, the specific questions to ask at each stage, and the hard thresholds where the answer is a clear reject — for instance if a scheme reduces autonomous leisure below a baseline or creates stored surplus without collective governance mechanisms. You need to flag the points where judgement can't be eliminated and a values call has to be made, not disguised as fact.

Make sure it handles the tricky cases: proposals that promise food security but demand permanent settlement; ones that keep mobility but introduce dependency on external markets; and ones where labour time is hidden in "community contribution" language.

Then run it on three examples:
1. A maize-and-granary programme for a mobile group with high seasonal variance.
2. A vaccination-and-cash-crop scheme with collective land tenure but cooperative market linkage.
3. One that scores brilliantly on caloric security but collapses on leisure autonomy — the kind my board will want to approve but shouldn't, according to the procedure.

Also tell me where the procedure will definately misfire — especially if applicants hide labour-time data or conflate seasonal storage with permanent hierarchy — and be honest about what it simply cannot decide for us.
