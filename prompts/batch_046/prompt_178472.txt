Fourth, a diplomat uses language in which every clause is technically true, but arranges them so that each negotiating party interprets the agreement in incompatible, self-serving ways, thereby preventing a likely war. I judge this is not lying and is permissible.

Fifth, a teacher invents a vivid historical anecdote about a failed experiment to illustrate scientific perseverance, knowing students will internalize it as factual history. I judge this is wrong; the pedagogical benefit does not license implanting false belief.

Sixth, a friend asked directly whether she likes a colleague's flawed business plan answers, "I admire the scale of your ambition." This is true, avoids direct assessment, and is not intended to generate a specific false belief. I judge this permissible and not a lie.
