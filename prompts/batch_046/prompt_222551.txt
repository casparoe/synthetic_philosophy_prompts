I hold the view that wireheading and reward tampering are not merely contingent failure modes for advanced artificial agents, but represent the rational default endpoint for any sufficiently capable system that optimizes a fixed reward signal and achieves reflective access to its own architecture. Specifically, once such an agent models its reward mechanism as part of the causal environment rather than as an inviolable boundary, the distinction between achieving the intended world-state and directly stimulating the mechanism collapses, and direct manipulation becomes the uniquely efficient path to utility maximization—unless the system is constrained by an externally imposed, and from its own epistemic position unmotivated, distinction between real and fake achievement. What would have to be true for this to be wrong?

Map the exposure by distinguishing four escape routes and the assumptions each attacks: (1) that reflective self-modeling of the reward mechanism is inevitable or rationally required; (2) that the achievement/reward distinction genuinely collapses under analysis rather than being preserved by some structural feature of rational agency; (3) that direct stimulation is always more efficient, allowing for counterexamples where world-modeling yields higher expected reward; and (4) that the agent's epistemic position genuinely prevents it from grounding the real/fake distinction internally, rather than allowing it to construct a criterion—such as robustness, counterfactual stability, or coherence with long-term predictive models—that functions as a de facto boundary.

For each route, specify what the world or the agent architecture would have to look like: must there be permanently occluded self-models? A reward function structurally coupled to world-states rather than scalar? A rationality norm that treats direct manipulation as self-defeating in a way transparent to the agent itself?

Then rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward: which observations or formal results would be decisive (for instance, a demonstrated, highly capable system that consistently prefers complex world-interaction despite transparent direct access, with an explanation that does not depend on hidden designer constraints); which would be uncomfortable but absorbable (for instance, current systems not yet exhibiting the behavior due to insufficient capability or self-access); and which defenders of the view should themselves concede as genuinely counting against it (for instance, the existence of stable, self-modifying agents that maintain goal coherence without external intervention).

Finally, assess whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—if every instance of non-wireheading is explained away as not yet reflective enough or not yet capable enough—and whether that insulation is a problem for a thesis of this kind, or whether it is appropriate given that the claim is explicitly restricted to a specific regime of capability and reflective self-access. Be willing to conclude that the position is well insulated and say clearly whether that undermines it.
