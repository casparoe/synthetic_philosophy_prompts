Here's an argument I cooked up for a seminar on Hilberts hotel and Cantor, and now I'm suspicious because the conclusion is way too strong. (1) If actual infinities exist, Hilberts hotel shows a proper part can be put in bijection with the whole, violating Euclid's principle that the whole is greater than the part. (2) So actual infinities are incoherrent unless we reject that axiom. (3) But finitsts reject actual infinities and allow only potential ones—collections always finite but unbounded. (4) Apply the same reasoning to potential infinity: for any stage n there's a stage n+1, so the 'potential whole' is always strictly larger than any actual part, yet there's never an actual whole to violate the axiom against. (5) But if there's no actual whole, then 'every natural number has a successor' can't be a universal claim over a completed domain; it's just a rule for extending. (6) So Cantor's theorem can't be formulated, since there's no set of all sets to diagonalize over. (7) That means either we accept actual infinity (and lose the part-whole principle) or we treat math as a modal grammar with no objective content. (8) Nobody wants to say math is just grammar. (9) Therefore actual infinities exist and the finitist is wrong. Where does this go wrong, if it does? I'd rather keep (1) and (3), but I'm willing to drop (7) if that's the price.
