Hey, I've been thinking about how AI labs run safety audits on language models. The usual practice is you have a checklist of prohibited outputs—hate speech, instructions for violence, explicit bias—and you red-team the model to see if it triggers any of them. Pass the checklist, ship the product. But nobody seems to audit for whether the model consistently frames every ethical dillema as a cost-benifit calculation with one correct answer, or whether it gives technically true but epistemically corrosive answers that slowly undermine a user's reasoning about risk. What view of ethical harm does that checklist audit preseuppose? Does it have to be that harm is always discrete and content-based, not structural? Could it be compatible with a virtue-ethics or care-ethics framing where harm is about patterns of interaction? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say what's strictly required versus what just fits well, and tell me what an audit built on structural or epistemic harm would actually have to do differently—what it would measure, and what it would stop measuring.
