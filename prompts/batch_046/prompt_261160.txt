I'm working through this for a philosophy of medicine seminar and my argument keeps looping back on itself. Here is the suspicious chain. (1) If we take autonomy seriously, then honoring an advance directive is only justified when the directive still expresses the will of the same agent now facing treatment. (2) Personal identity over time can't be just bodily continuty — if it were, we'd have to say a brain-dead body on a ventilator is still the person, which is clearly wrong. So some psychological continuty is necessary for being the same person. (3) But psychological continuty isn't binary; it comes in degrees. Severe dementia breaks it sharply, yet so does the slow drift of values, memories, and personality across fifty years of healthy aging. (4) So the authority of a directive should be proportional to the degree of continuty between the signer and the present patient: near-total for temporary delirium, weak for advanced dementia, and probably weak too for a document signed at thirty and invoked at eighty-five. (5) Now apply this consistently: either long-range directives carry almost no binding force, which makes living wills pointless, or we have to admit that bodily and narrative continuty are enough for identity after all — meaning the demented patient is still fully the signer and should be bound by the directive even when they now clearly want to live. (6) Both horns feel wrong. We want long-term directives to matter, but we don't want to force a suffering patient to endure against their present expressed will. (7) Therefore the idea that authority must track psychological continuty proportionally must be false, right? But if I drop that and revert to pure bodily continuty, I lose any reason to ever set aside a directive in dementia. Where exactly does the slide happen — is it in step 3 treating continuty as gradable for identity, or in step 4 smuggling in a proportionality claim that doesn't follow? I keep feeling there's an equivocation hiding between "continuity as a condition for being the same person" and "continuity as a dimmer switch on authority," but I can't quite pin it, and I don't want to just invent a distinction that saves the view.
