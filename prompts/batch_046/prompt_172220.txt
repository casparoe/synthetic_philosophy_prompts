So I was watching this youtube video about the centipede game and this thing called backward induction, and now I'm obsessed with running it at my philosophy meetup. We meet in a library community room, about 8 to 10 people, mostly regular folks not students, and we have about 60 minutes. I want an actual game we play with real small stakes—like candy or points that decide who picks the playlist—to make everyone feel why rational people keep bailing out early even when cooperating longer seems like it would pay more. But I dont want the usual paper version with fake money.

Instead I want a "trust ladder" setup: each round someone can either take a small guaranteed reward or pass and double the shared pool, but there's a hidden "broken rung" rule—if the pool hits a specific size the whole ladder collapses and nobody gets anything, and only one player secretly knows exactly which round that is. They can only signal it indirectly through their choices, not by talking. The idea is that people should feel the pressure to jump out early because they cant be sure others wont, but also see how hidden information messes with the induction chain. Can you design the full rules, the payoff scheme with actual small stakes, a 60-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened back to the backward induction paradox? Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time with people who dont know game theory, and be honest about which feature of backward induction this hidden-rung version cant actually reproduce since the hidden info breaks the common knowledge part. No lectures up front—let them run into it themselves.
