hey, i'm drafting a short essay on hard choices and incommensurable values and i could use some feedback before i post it. here's the gist: the usual story is that hard choices are hard because we can't weigh two values on a common scale. i think that's wrong, or at least incomplete. my view is that the problem isn't a lack of metric but a kind of "evaluative diglossia" — you're fluent in two different value-languages (say, the language of filial duty and the language of career autonomy) and there's no translation manual between them. so when you're stuck, it's not that you can't compare them; it's that comparison itself is a category mistake, like asking which color is louder. i think Ruth Chang gets close when she talks about hard choices revealing something about the chooser, but she still assumes the values are comparable in principle. Joseph Raz's incommensurability is closer, but i want to push it further: if there's no shared framework, then the choice isn't rational in the weighing sense but is rational in the sense of deciding which framework to inhabit. that sounds like voluntarism about values, which i'm not sure i want. does this distinction hold water? am i just dressing up Raz in linguistic clothing? and is "diglossia" a useful analogy or does it just muddy things? specifically, i want to know if there's a hidden normative slip when i say you "choose which language to keep" — does that imply values are up for grabs? also, is there a result in social choice theory or decision theory that already kills this idea? i'd especially appreciate comments on accuracy and whether the argument is intersting or just confusing. feel free to point out where i'm being sloppy; i know i misspelled "incommensurable" at least once up there.
