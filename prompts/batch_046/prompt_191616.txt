Since I began using a digital commitment device to regulate my habits, I have become preoccupied with the boundary between genuine weakness of will and other failures of practical reasoning, and I should like to determine whether my intuitions about particular instances can be systematised under a single principle.

Here are my verdicts. When I resolve not to check social media and then do so within minutes, I judge this a paradigmatic case of akrasia. When I resolve to avoid refined sugar but consume dessert because I am exhausted after work, I still regard it as weakness of will, though the fatigue renders it partly excusable. When I attempt to dissolve a savings lock that I previously imposed upon myself, I do not treat this as akrasia; rather, it strikes me as a failure of strategic self-binding rather than of executive control. When I abandon a long-planned research project because I have genuinely come to believe its central premise false, I do not think I have acted against my better judgement—I have merely updated it. When I delay a medically advised vaccination because I fear the needle, despite fully acknowledging the future health benefit, I am unsure whether this counts as hyperbolic discounting, as a distinct irrationality, or not as weakness of will at all. When a colleague persists in smoking with accurate knowledge of long-term risks, and attributes her behaviour solely to hyperbolic discounting of future harm, I feel this explanation is incomplete, though I cannot identify precisely what is missing.

Is there a coherent principle that delivers all six verdicts? If so, please state it with sufficient precision that each clause corresponds to one of the cases above, and indicate what it predicts for a case I have not mentioned: I resolve to exercise each morning, yet each morning I sincerely value immediate rest more highly than long-term fitness, with no alteration in my underlying values or beliefs. Must I overturn one of my listed verdicts—perhaps treating the fatigue case as non-akratic, or the vaccination case as clear akrasia—to preserve a simpler account? Furthermore, does the principle require treating the present self and the future self as distinct moral agents with competing claims, or can it be articulated within a unified-agency framework without invoking intertemporal fragmentation?
