I have been developing, in a graduate seminar on scepticism and epistemic entitlement, a somewhat unorthodox reading of Moore's proof that there exist objects external to the mind. Rather than treating the proof as either a formal deduction that answers the sceptic premise-for-premise or as a naive appeal to common sense, I maintain that it is best understood through the lens of pragmatic encroachment: the practical stakes of global scepticism are so catastrophically high that they lower the epistemic threshold for knowledge, so that Moore's demonstration — holding up one hand, then the other — functions not as a logical refutation but as a direct entitlement that meets the lowered standard. On this view, the argument succeeds because denying the existence of an external world carries unacceptable practical costs, not because Moore provides a non-circular justification for his perceptual beliefs; consequently, the proof is a case of stakes-sensitive knowledge rather than a traditional anti-sceptical argument.

I want a rigorous assessment of what would genuinely count against this interpretation. Please map its exposure honestly: which formal results in epistemology, intuitive counter-cases, arguments about circularity, or developments in the philosophy of perception would undermine it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Specifically, address whether there is evidence that basic perceptual knowledge is not stakes-sensitive; whether the interpretation renders Moore's proof circular, since the 'costs' of scepticism appear to presuppose the very external world whose existence is in question; and whether committed defenders of this reading could concede any finding as decisive, or whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and, if so, whether that insulation is philosophically damaging. I also want you to identify the strongest consideration that defenders of the view themselves would recognise as counting on the other side, not merely the objections critics usually press.

Please respond in British English, and be self-critical about your confidence in each claim, distinguishing well-established points from those that rest on contested readings. Finally, if my framing rests on a confused premise — for instance, if pragmatic encroachment is conceptually incompatible with the structure of Moore's proof in a way I have not recognised — you should say so plainly and refuse to construct a polite answer to a misposed task.
