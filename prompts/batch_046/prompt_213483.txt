The project asks whether Kierkegaard’s critique of the press and the public in The Present Age and Two Ages can be reconstructed as a standalone structural analysis of anonymous reception—the way mass publication dissolves the conditions of accountability that make face-to-face communication epistemically and ethically tractable—without relying on his theological framework of the single individual before God. State the question precisely: is there a coherent, non-theological mechanism in these texts that explains how the press generates the public as an abstract entity capable of holding contradictory commitments without anyone being responsible for them, and is this mechanism conceptually distinct from his well-known idea of leveling?

Most readings treat the press critique either as an aesthetic complaint about modern passionlessness or as a direct extension of religious existentialism, and therefore do not isolate whether there is an independent conceptual apparatus describing how anonymity, distance, and non-reciprocity alter the logic of belief-formation and commitment.

Propose your approach and method.
