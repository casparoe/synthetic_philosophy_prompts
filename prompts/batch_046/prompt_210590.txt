So: write me a short story about a near-future apprenticeship program where trainees learn ethics not from texts but from immersive, first-person reconstrutions of paired historical lives—specifically two figures whose virtues genuinely conflict, not just different flavors of the same thing. The protagonist should be stuck in a loop where they keep trying to resolve the contradiction by picking one exemplar over the other, and the system (or a mentor figure) keeps pushing them toward a third option that isn't synthesis but something weirder—maybe recognizing that both lives share a hidden structural failure that only becomes visible when you live them both. Keep the setting mundane, like a bureaucratic training institute or a rehab clinic, no space opera.

After the story, please separate what's actually settled in the literature about moral learning from biography—like whether exposure to exemplars reliably changes behavior—from what's genuinely contested, like whether Plutarch intended his pairings to produce moral ambiguity rather than clear models. Don't present a contested interpretation (e.g., that the Parallel Lives is secretly a manual for virtue ethics) as if it's the consensus view.
