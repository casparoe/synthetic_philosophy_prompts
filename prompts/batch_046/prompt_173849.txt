I am drafting an essay that complicates Fernand Braudel's tripartite schema of historical time—geographical structure, social or cyclical time, and individual event—by isolating an intermediate temporal scale that seems to fall between the first two registers. Specifically, I examine multidecadal climate oscillations in the sixteenth-century Mediterranean—extended drought cycles in the Maghreb, shifts in the North Atlantic Oscillation, and multiyear anomalies in grain yields across Castile—and argue that they operate too slowly to register as discrete political events for actors like Philip II, yet too quickly and too variably to be absorbed into the almost timeless geographical structures of coast, plain, and mountain that Braudel treats as foundational. Rather than using climate data to revive a determinist narrative, I want to show that these oscillations expose a kind of temporal essentialism in Braudel's hierarchy: the layers appear ontologically distinct only because archival practices—serial price and shipping records versus diplomatic correspondence—already encode a preference for data that changes slowly. The essay then tests this claim against two cases—the 1565 siege of Malta and the famine cycles of the 1590s in Castile—asking whether they are best read as events shaped by social structures, or as climate-driven episodes that appear eventful only because state archives preserve them as such. I am particularly concerned with a section in which I suggest that Braudel's own methodological commitment to the longue durée is partly circular: the archives he privileges for structural history are precisely those that flatten rapid variability, so using them to defend the primacy of slow structures risks begging the question. Could you comment on the coherence of distinguishing what I call structural climate from event climate, and on whether my treatment of Malta and Castile conflates correlation with causation? I would also welcome suggestions for sharpening the argument about archival circularity, and for identifying where I might be misrepresenting Braudel's actual handling of environment, agency, and the boundary between event and structure.
