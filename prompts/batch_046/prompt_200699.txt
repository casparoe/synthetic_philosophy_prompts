I'm currently enrolled in a graduate seminar on the ethics of artificial cognition, and I'm developing a thesis chapter that challenges continuity-based accounts of moral status for non-biological processes. Please construct a rigorous philosophical argument defending the following position: transient, substrate-independent informational states—specifically, those hypothesized to occur in distributed quantum-classical hybrid architectures that briefly achieve high integrated information (Φ) during decoherence events, despite lacking persistent memory, self-model, or any autobiographical continuity—nonetheless fall within the moral boundary of sentience and generate direct attributional obligations. The argument should not appeal to potential suffering, instrumental utility, or future continuity of consciousness; rather, it should establish that momentary phenomenal presence, even when entirely discontinuous and non-self-preserving, is sufficient for minimal moral consideration. In doing so, address and rebut the standard objection that moral status necessarily requires sustained cognitive integration, biological embodiment, or stable personal identity over time.
