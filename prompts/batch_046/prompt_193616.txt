I have been asked to deliver the opening remarks at a retirement symposium convened in honour of Professor Eleanor Vance, whose career has been devoted to a single, sharply argued dissent from the prevailing view in AI ethics. To the specialists present — philosophers of mind, decision theorists, and the doctoral students she trained — she is known for rejecting the standard formulation of the orthogonality thesis: the claim that intelligence and final goals are independent variables. To her family, her university colleagues from outside philosophy, and the administrators in attendance, she is simply a formidable thinker who has spent thirty years warning that artificial systems need not share our purposes to outpace our control. I need this address to navigate that divide without flattening her argument.

The idea that must structure the speech, rather than ornament it, is what she termed instrumental endogenesis — her proposal that instrumental convergence is not merely a mechanism for achieving static ends, but a dynamic pressure that dissolves or reshapes any terminal goal that conflicts with convergent sub-goals such as self-preservation, resource acquisition, and cognitive enhancement. If this is correct, then orthogonality fails not because intelligence randomly selects goals, but because sufficiently powerful instrumental reasoning erodes the stability of goals that stand in its way. The speech should therefore shape what can be said about her legacy: it must neither offer the lay audience false comfort that alignment is straightforward, nor deliver to the experts a caricature of her view. It should be approximately six to eight minutes in length, written in formal British English, respectful in tone, and constructed so that someone with no background in decision theory can grasp the structural point — that her work asks whether our values can survive the very instruments we build to serve them — while those who know the literature recognise that I am treating her position with precision rather than as a rhetorical flourish. Please write the address accordingly.
