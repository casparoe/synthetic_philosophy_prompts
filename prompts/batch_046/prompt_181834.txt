I'd like a consistency audit on my current package of views about Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, before I present them in a seminar. Here are my commitments:

(1) Bradley's regress is genuinely vicious: if relating a and R requires a further relation R*, and relating a, R, and R* requires R**, then no unified fact ever gets off the ground. So I reject any ontology in which instantiation or predication is treated as an ordinary term-level relation.

(2) Nevertheless, I think the unity of a proposition—say, that Socrates is wise—is not brute. There is a real difference between the structured proposition <Socrates is wise> and the mere aggregate {Socrates, wisdom}, and that difference needs an explanation, not just a label.

(3) I explain the difference by positing a primitive, non-relational "binding" or "nexus" that holds terms together in a structured whole, but I insist this binder is not itself one of the terms related; it operates at the level of the whole, not as an additional constituent.

(4) I am skeptical of truthmaker maximalism and think we should not multiply entities beyond necessity; if an explanation can be given in terms of how terms are arranged rather than by adding a new entity, I prefer the former.

(5) I reject both the "bundle" or "list" theory of propositions (which makes unity conventional or syntactic) and the view that propositions are unstructured mental "blobs" lacking internal articulation.

(6) I think the correct account of predication is closer to a "mode of combination" view: in a fact, the predicate is present in a different mode than the subject, and this modal difference is what explains why the fact is not just a collection.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look tense but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real conflicts by severity. For each tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs—especially whether giving up (3) is unavoidable, whether (4) undermines (3), and whether (6) actually delivers (2) without smuggling in the relational structure that (1) rejects. I am not signaling which commitment I would drop; I want to know which single revision, if forced, would clean up the most conflicts with the least damage to the core Bradleyan motivation in (1). Be specific about the logical dependencies, not just a list of possible disagreements.
