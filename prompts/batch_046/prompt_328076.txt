I am preparing a position paper for a philosophy of normativity workshop and would like a consistency audit of the following commitments before I defend them. I take them to be largely compatible, but I want to know where I am fooling myself.

(1) I subscribe to a strict agent-centred deontology: there are exclusionary reasons against treating an innocent person as a mere means, and these reasons bind regardless of aggregate welfare outcomes.

(2) I think the paradox of deontology — in which universal adherence to agent-centred restrictions can produce more violations overall — is dissolved once we reject the premise that moral reasons are maximisable. Each agent's duty is only to refrain from violating the restriction herself, not to optimise the global pattern of compliance.

(3) I nevertheless accept a catastrophic threshold: when enough lives are at stake, a direct violation of the exclusionary reason becomes permissible.

(4) I deny that wrongness aggregates: the moral badness of killing five people is not five times that of killing one, and comparative quantitative weighing of this sort has no place in sound moral reasoning.

(5) I draw a sharp distinction between immediate and mediated agency. The exclusionary reason applies with full force only when my own bodily movement or direct causal intervention constitutes the harm. Where harm is mediated by institutional structures, mechanical processes, or prior arrangements over which I do not exercise direct control, the constraint is substantially weakened.

(6) I justify this framework by appeal to a Kantian conception of respect for persons, interpreted structurally rather than psychologically: respect consists in adhering to the institutional rules that constitute the role of moral agent.

(7) I hold that evolutionary debunking arguments undermine consequentialist moral intuitions but not deontological ones, on the grounds that the recursive, rule-governed structure characteristic of deontology is not the kind of cognitive output that evolutionary explanations can either reproduce or undermine.

Please examine these for tension. Which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which only seem to conflict, and how severe is each tension? For the genuine ones, specify the cheapest revision and what it costs. Rank them by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. Then tell me which single change to one commitment would resolve the greatest number of tensions overall. Please answer in British English.
