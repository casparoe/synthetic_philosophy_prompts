Hello—I'm writing up notes for a graduate seminar on hedonism and Ive noticed a recurring argumentative move that I can't quite place. Here's the pattern: a student defends hedonism by saying all pleasures share a distinctive phenomenolgical glow. When I ask whether that glow is just the feeling of pleasure itself—making the claim circular—they switch to saying pleasure is unified by its functional role, e.g. reinforcing behaviour. I then point out that pain also functions to reinforce avoidance, so reinforcement can't be the specific common factor, and they reply that pleasure is just the state with positive valence. At that point I ask what makes positive valence feel good rather than merely signal reward, and they loop back to the phenomenological glow. Ive seen this happen in three different discussions this term alone, with different students, always ending up in the same circle between phenomenology, functionalism, and valence talk. In my head I'm calling it the 'hedonic carousel', but is there actually an established concept for this specific dialectical loop? How is it different from ordinary explanatory pluralism or from the kind of circularity Mill gets accused of? If there's no standard term, does 'hedonic carousel' do the work, and what would be its boundary conditions—e.g. does it apply only to hedonist debates or to any theory that oscillates between first-person and third-person accounts of a mental state? I'd love your take—please reply in British English, and no need for bibliographies, I'm just trying to figure out if I'm seeing a real pattern or projecting.
