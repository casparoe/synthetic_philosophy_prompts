I coordinate a small forecasting collective that resolves competing explanations for failed open-source replication studies—roughly two cases per month, with stakes that include funding recommendations but no legal liability. Our charter requires us to apply a formal version of Occam's razor derived from Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity: we prefer the explanation with the lower minimal description length, but with one internal twist—our instrument-overhead rule requires that any bits needed to specify calibration or measurement apparatus be included in the complexity tally, so that a hypothesis cannot launder its cost into the sensor description. We lack compute resources to approximate Kolmogorov complexity algorithmically; decisions must be made within about ninety minutes by a panel of one statistician and two domain generalists, and we must flag explicitly where the procedure ends and values or institutional judgement take over.

Please design a reusable decision procedure—ordered questions, stopping conditions, and explicit hand-off points—for this panel. For each step, state what counts as a sufficient answer, what should happen if the answer is ambiguous, and whether the question is factual or normative. Then apply the procedure to three cases:

(1) A high-temperature superconductivity replication fails; one explanation posits an undiscovered crystal-phase transition (requiring new lattice parameters), the other posits miscalibration in the resistance probe (requiring a revised temperature-correction curve).

(2) A social-psychology priming study fails to replicate; one explanation invokes publication bias and p-hacking in the original, the other invokes a genuine but context-dependent effect that activates only with a specific demographic framing.

(3) An unwelcome case: a prominent climate consortium's model divergence is best explained, under the procedure, by mischaracterised satellite drift—an explanation that discredits a major grant programme but scores lower on our instrument-inclusive complexity tally. Show exactly where the procedure delivers this answer, where it demands human sign-off, and what it cannot settle.

Finally, state what the procedure cannot do—where it will misfire, which kinds of explanations it systematically undervalues, and the limits of applying Solomonoff-style reasoning without actual computation.
