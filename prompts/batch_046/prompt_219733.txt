I am trying to understand exactly how far this comparison can be pushed. Could you audit the analogy in detail? Also, I wonder whether the analogy quietly imports the idea that national memory is a private keepsake rather than a public argument about who owes what to whom. If you find that the photograph analogy collapses under these pressures, is there a better comparison—perhaps something drawn from architecture or music—that captures the same idea without those hidden assumptions?
