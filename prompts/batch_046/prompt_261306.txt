Write a dialogue between a Tranquilist and an Achievementist. The Tranquilist holds that only the absence of craving and suffering has intrinsic value, so that positive goods—knowledge, beauty, moral virtue—matter only instrumentally, if at all. The Achievementist holds that the exercise and realization of complex human capacities have intrinsic value that can justify some measure of suffering and craving. Have them debate the following invented case: a hypothetical post-human archival consciousness can preserve all human culture and scientific achievement indefinitely, but only if it maintains a minimal, irreducible level of epistemic craving and occasional cognitive dissonance (a form of mild suffering). The Tranquilist should argue that creating or sustaining this entity is not justified, since it introduces new suffering for a derivative good; the Achievementist should argue that the intrinsic value of preservation outweighs the minimal cost. Require that they respond directly to each other's arguments—particularly on whether absence and presence can share a common metric of evaluation, whether the Tranquilist can consistently care about the suffering of non-existent beings, and whether an achievement that requires its own precondition in craving undermines the Tranquilist's asymmetry. Neither interlocutor should be a strawman; each should press the strongest version of the opposing view.
