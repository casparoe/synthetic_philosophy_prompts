I ask you to treat the paired American Psychiatric Association episodes of 1973 and 1980–1987—the removal of homosexuality from the DSM-II and the brief institutional life of ego-dystonic homosexuality in DSM-III and its deletion in the 1987 DSM-III-R revision—as a disciplined case study in Ian Hacking's looping-effects account of human kinds. Do not summarize the theory in general terms; map it precisely onto the actors, options, and institutional dynamics of these specific moments, and allow the mapping to expose where the theory is silent or strained.

First, reconstruct the feedback loop for each diagnostic configuration: specify who constituted the classified population, what self-conceptions and behaviors the category made available or salient, and the pathways—clinical, activist, or bureaucratic—through which those alterations fed back into the criteria.

Second, introduce and defend a conceptual distinction not standard in Hacking's own texts—call it institutional counter-looping—to capture situations in which the classified group or allied professionals actively resist the category's performative pull rather than internalizing and enacting it. Apply this distinction to explain why the 1973 decision dissolved the loop rather than redirecting it, and why the residual ego-dystonic category, despite being designed to preserve a pathological niche, failed to generate a stable, self-reinforcing patient population.

Third, compare the two loops with precision: determine whether ego-dystonic homosexuality represented a weakened continuation of the original loop or a categorically different institutional artifact, and use this comparison to assess whether Hacking's framework requires a typology of loop fragility rather than treating all psychiatric kinds as uniformly moving targets.

Fourth, state with explicit discipline what the theory explains well here, what it misdescribes—particularly regarding the speed of political reversal, the role of non-clinical actors, and the bureaucratic logic of residual categories—and what it leaves entirely unaddressed. Conclude by judging whether this paired episode ultimately supports or complicates Hacking's claim that human kinds are constituted by the very classifications applied to them.

Maintain rigorous actor-mapping throughout; where the theory offers no analytical purchase, say so rather than extending it by force.
