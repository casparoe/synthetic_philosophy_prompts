Draft the joint statement that a causal-representationalist about deep neural networks and a functional-instrumentalist about interpretability could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on whether trained networks genuinely contain representations.

The document should open with a numbered section of claims both sides accept. Cover: the formal structure of feedforward computation and gradient-based training; what ablation, intervention, and linear-probing studies can demonstrate about causal dependency; the distinction between predictive correlation, causal mediation, and representational content; the absence of evolutionary history or intentional design in standard supervised or self-supervised training; and the conditions under which both agree that talk of "representation" would be genuinely explanatory rather than metaphorical.

Next, present a disagreements table with two columns. In the left column, state the representationalist view in wording its proponents would accept: that specific intermediate activation directions can bear content about input properties when they are systematically activated by those properties, causally mediate downstream computation in task-relevant ways, and exhibit counterfactual robustness across distributional shifts. In the right column, state the instrumentalist view in wording its proponents would accept: that these conditions establish only efficient encoding and causal influence within an information-processing pipeline, and that representational content requires normative conditions of misrepresentation—conditions that artificial systems, lacking biological function or design intent directed at truth, do not satisfy.

Then identify the cruxes explicitly. Include at least these three: (a) whether causal mediation plus systematic correlation is sufficient for representation in the absence of biological or intentional function; (b) whether artificial systems can genuinely misrepresent rather than merely err, and what distinguishes misrepresentation from incorrect classification or out-of-distribution activation; (c) whether interpretability methods discover pre-existing representational structures or construct useful but ontologically neutral decompositions of high-dimensional computation.

For each crux, specify a concrete case or experimental pattern both sides agree is relevant: a vision model trained on geometric shapes in which an intermediate feature direction, linearly decodable for "curvature," is ablated; suppression selectively degrades curvature-related judgments while sparing orientation judgments, and the feature activates both on genuine curved stimuli and on adversarial or out-of-distribution inputs that are not curved. Record each side's prediction in advance. The representationalist should predict that the pattern of selective degradation combined with adversarial activation reveals a contentful misrepresentation condition—an error about curvature—while the instrumentalist should predict that the same data is fully explained by distributional statistics, correlation structure, and causal dependency within the network, with no need to attribute representational content or error conditions.

Conclude with a brief assessment of whether any portion of the dispute is verbal—specifically, whether disagreement about the term "representation" masks underlying agreement about the mechanistic and causal facts, and if so, which part.

Keep all formulations exact and neutral: neither side should feel straw-manned, and both should be able to endorse every agreed claim and their own column of disagreement. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific papers.
