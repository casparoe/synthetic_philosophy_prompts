me and my friends jules and riley argued about the paradox of tollerance after some campus thing. jules said its overblown — if a group is literally working to take away other peoples rights to speak, then excluding them isnt intolerance, its just protecting the conditions that make tolerance possible, like locking a door isnt anti-door. riley said thats circular: youre defining intolerant as whatever threatens your view of tolerance, so youre just being intolerant of people who disagree about what tolerance means. jules said no, theres a real structural difference — tolerance is a second-order thing, you allow disagreement about first-order stuff but not about whether disagreement should be allowed at all. thats not picking sides, its protecting the framework. riley said every framework does that though — a pacifist excludes violence because they value peace, not because violence is neutrally outside the framework. youre dressing up a prior commitment as meta-level neutrality. then jules brought in this idea he called a tolerance budget — societies only have so much forbearance, and some movements dont just hold bad views, they actively drain the pool by suppressing counter-speech, so keeping them out is resource management, not intolerance. riley said budget is just a fancy word for prejudice since you never actually measure it, you just call views you hate expensive. jules said you can measure it: check if the group allows internal dissent and counter-argument or if it systematically silences opponents. riley said that metric is still yours — the group probably says they silence opponents because those people are dangerous, same logic flipped. so theyre stuck in a mirror. they asked me to referee and honestly i dont know. can you go through each move and rate whos right at each step, not just overall? also say what theyre both missing, like whether the framework/parasite distinction actually holds up or if its just relabeling.
