I've been chewing on Rorty ever since a thread on Slate Star Codex about whether antirepresentationalism can survive the success of physics, and I think I've landed on a kind of hybrid that I'm not sure holds water. The basic idea is that Rorty's right — for ethics, politics, and most everyday talk, treating language as a tool for coping rather than a mirror of nature is exactly the right move, and the representationalist vocabulary is just one more useless metaphor. But I want to carve out a narrow exception for cases of what I'd call structural convergence, where totally independent formalisms (say, different mathematical derivations or distinct experimental setups) keep locking onto the same quantitative result. My claim is that even this convergence doesn't need a representationalist story about tracking reality; it's enough that some vocabularies are more robust across contexts because they solve more coordination problems, not because they correspond to something out there. So I'm defending a kind of pragmatist pluralism that admits convergence but explains it functionally, not metaphysicly.

But I'm worried I'm just sneaking realism in through the back door, or maybe I'm being too generous to Rorty and the whole thing collapses into either full-blown antirepresentationalism or a hidden correspondence theory. So I want to play it straight: what would actually count against this position? I'm not looking for generic realism vs antirealism rehashes — I want the specific defeaters. Would it take finding a case where convergence happens despite zero practical utility? Or showing that my robustness across contexts is litterally just another name for representational success? What about internal tensions: if I say vocabularies are tools, but then rank some as more robust in a way that sounds objective, haven't I already smuggled in a normative standard that Rorty can't allow? I also want to know which of these escape routes are live versus just logically possible — like, is there an actual argument that predictive convergence in science is inexplicable without some minimal representational commitment, even if I don't need citations?

Don't present a contested reading as the consensus — if Rorty himself would concede part of the critique, say so; if the defeaters mostly come from critics he wouldn't take seriously, say that too. And if my hybrid position is actually well-insulated and I'm just being paranoid, tell me straight — I don't need it protected.
