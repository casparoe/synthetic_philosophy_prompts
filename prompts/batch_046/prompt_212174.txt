Design a classroom activity for about 18 students in a 75-minute philosphy seminar. The topic is the varieties of free will worth wanting — specifically the idea that what matters for moral life isn't some metaphysically deep ultimate origination but rather having the right kind of elbow room and being responsive to reasons. I want the students to actually feel that difference, not just hear about it. Here's the niche twist: set it up as a deterministic negotiation game where each student gets a hidden payoff card that fixes their choice in advance, but they also have to publically defend their decision using reasons. Only after the round ends do they learn the cards were pre-set. The debrief should map this onto the compatibilist/libertarian split and ask whether the students still blame each other for bad outcomes. Include rules, roles (maybe a moderator and observers), a point-based payoff scheme with no real money, and an honest note about what the game can't reproduce — probably the long-term evolutionary story about why reactive attitudes exist. Also definately tell me what will predictably go wrong on the first run. Keep it casual — I don't need formal jargon everywhere, and you can misspell a word or two if it feels natural.
