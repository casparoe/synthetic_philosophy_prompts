I have sketched the core of an essay arguing that Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi should be read as a deflationary practice rather than a logical schema that yields a positive "middle" thesis. The claim is that conventional truth (saṃvṛti-satya) operates not as a descriptive domain with fixed quantificational structure but as pragmatically indexical—tracking interdependent designation (prajñaptimātra) without committing to svabhāva. From this, I suggest the four positions (it is, it is not, both, neither) all presuppose inherent existence and therefore collapse together, not into skepticism but into a performative dissolution of assertion; the "emptiness of emptiness" then applies the same dissolution to the tetralemma itself, blocking self-refutation. To make the point concrete, I draw a loose analogy with Quinean ontological commitment—where "to be" is tied to bound variables—to argue that if conventional designation lacks a stable quantificational domain, none of the four koṭis can get purchase. I anticipate three objections: that this collapses into nihilism (countered by treating emptiness as dependently originated being rather than absence), that it makes Madhyamaka discourse impossible (countered by treating negation as framework-dissolving rather than doxastic), and that it conflicts with Candrakīrti's commentary tradition, which treats conventional entities as causally efficacious in a way that sounds more realist than my indexical reading allows.
