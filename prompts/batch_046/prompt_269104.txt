I draft procedural rules for citizens' assemblies that must render rapid, high-stakes collective judgments, and I want to operationalize the structural lessons of Athenian democratic failure—specifically the emotional reversal in the Mytilene debate, the overreach of the Sicilian Expedition despite expert dissent, and the suppression of philosophical dissent in the trial of Socrates—into a reusable five-stage protocol. Design the procedure so that a non-philosopher clerk can run it in sequence, identifying at each stage whether the check is factual or evaluative, where it terminates with a procedural block, and where it must be handed to the chair because values rather than rules decide; then apply it to two cases—an assembly under pressure to vote severe sanctions against a rebellious ally, and an assembly committing to a costly exploratory venture after dismissing cautious expert warnings—ensuring at least one application produces an answer the assembly will reject. Present the result as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, including the stages, the judgment boundaries, the two worked applications, and a brief statement of what failures this protocol cannot catch.
