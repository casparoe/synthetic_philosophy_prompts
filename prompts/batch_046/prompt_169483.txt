As a retired condensed-matter physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of science, I keep encountering the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for eternalism presented through an analogy that seems to borrow from crystallography: defenders claim that special relativity reveals spacetime as a fixed four-dimensional manifold, and that the relativity of simultaneity is like inspecting a cylindrical crystal along different crystallographic axes—one axis yields a circular cross-section, another an ellipse, yet all sections belong equally to the object, with no privileged orientation. From this they infer that all temporal slices are equally real and that our experience of a flowing present is merely the perspective-dependent shadow cast by our worldline's orientation. Audit this analogy precisely: map the cylinder, the axes, the shadow profiles, and the spacetime slices onto one another; identify the point at which the comparison collapses because an observer in spacetime is not a static viewpoint but a traversing worldline; say whether the analogy silently imports the conclusion that every slice exists timelessly rather than establishing it; and conclude whether the denial of objective becoming survives once the disanalogy is repaired. Give your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
