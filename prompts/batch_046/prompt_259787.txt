i've been thinking about Stevensons Jekyll and Hyde through the lens of dispositions, finks, and masks. in philosophy of science we talk about a disposition being masked (hidden by interfering conditions) or finked (the manifestation is cut off). in the story Jekyll brews a potion that turns him into Hyde — different body, different morality. is the potion a mask that hides Jekyll's virtuous disposition, or a fink that eliminates it? and since Hyde seems like a distinct agent, not just a behavior, does that mean we're dealing with a multi-track disposition — one that can manifest as either saint or monster depending on the trigger — or does the story actually presuppose something like substance dualism that the dispositional framework cant capture?
