I've been mulling over a specific take on Weber's Protestant work ethic — not the usual capital-accumulation story, but the idea that its real damage wasn't glorifying labour itself, but making leisure feel guilty unless it served some visible purpose. So the 35-hour week and modern work-life balence talk miss the point entirely: they treat time off as a right you defend, not as a void that needs justifying. Karoshi, on this view, isn't virtue-driven overwork but the guilt-structure running without its theolgical brake — people work themselves dead just to avoid the feeling they're wasting existence. What would actually count against this? I want the arguments or evidence that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be versus merely awkward. Could Weber's own text undermine the 'leisure guilt' framing? Could karoshi statistics map better onto pure economic pressure? And has this view been qualified into unfalsifiabilty — like, any work pattern confirms the guilt structure? Be honest about whether it's well insulated and whether that insulation is actually a problem. Also say what defenders of the position would themselves concede counts against it, not just what critics say.
