Please argue for the position that Dennett's intentional stance cannot be consistently applied to contemporary large language models, not on the familiar grounds that they lack consciousness or are mere stochastic-parrot systems, but because they fail a specific coherence condition that the stance implicitly requires: inter-contextual belief stability. Define this condition as the requirement that if we attribute belief B to a system in conversational context C, then across a structured family of counterfactual perturbations of C—where the semantic content relevant to B is preserved but surface features vary—the system's outputs should support either the retention of B or a principled, predictable revision of B, rather than arbitrary fragmentation. Then show that LLMs systematically violate this condition in a way that differs from ordinary human context-sensitivity: human belief revision is governed by identifiable rational or psychological mechanisms, whereas LLM output variation under perturbation is governed by distributional patterns in training data that do not support the kind of generalizations belief-attribution is meant to license. Conclude that this modal fragmentation undermines the pragmatic justification for the intentional stance—predictive and explanatory utility—even if we grant that the model's internal computations are causally structured and that its outputs are often semantically appropriate. Distinguish your argument from both eliminativist and realist positions about machine representation, and address the objection that human beliefs themselves exhibit enough context-dependence to render the condition useless.
