I defend a particular interpretation of Adam Smith's account of sympathy and the impartial spectator: that the spectator functions primarily as an internal counterfactual affective simulator rather than as an internalized social authority supplying moral norms. On this reading, sympathy is not the foundational ground of moral judgment but a secondary calibration mechanism that corrects the proportional distortion introduced by self-love, adjusting the intensity of our passions against a structurally independent emotional baseline generated by the spectator. Moral approval, then, consists not in alignment with collective sentiment but in convergence between the agent's corrected affective state and this baseline, with the spectator's authority deriving from its formal independence from immediate interest rather than from representational accuracy about others' feelings. I want to know what would genuinely count against this interpretation. Which patterns in Smith's discussion of propriety, resentment, gratitude, and the division of the self would be decisive if they resist being reconstructed as emotional regulation rather than normative judgment? Can the view explain why we approve of the spectator's own judgments without generating a regress, and if not, does that undermine the claim that sympathy is derivative? What comparative tensions arise with Hume's account of sympathy, or with Smith's own treatments of rhetoric and the passions, that might expose this reading as anachronistic or as improperly collapsing Smith's moral psychology into a precursor of modern predictive-processing frameworks? I also want defenders of the standard sympathy-first reading to say what they would themselves concede counts against their view—whether there are textual passages or conceptual commitments they acknowledge as genuinely awkward—so that I can compare the exposure of both positions. Finally, assess whether this affective-regulation interpretation has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every reference to sympathy or social approval can be redescribed as calibration, is that interpretive flexibility a virtue or a vice for a claim about Smith's actual commitments?
