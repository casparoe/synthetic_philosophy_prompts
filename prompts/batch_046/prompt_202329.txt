Take the Haitian Revolution (1791-1804) and read it through Hegel's master-slave dialectic and recogntion. I don't want a summary of either; I want the actual mapping — who plays master and slave at different moments, what the struggle for recogntion looked like when one side literally owned the other, and where the dialectic's idea of mutual recognition collapses because the colonial ecnomy needed the slave to stay unrecognized. Then flip it: does the Revolution prove recognition can be seized by force alone, or does it show Hegel's frame misses racial capitalism? Be honest about where the theory is silent — like on gender dymanics inside the revolutionary camps — rather than forcing it to talk.
