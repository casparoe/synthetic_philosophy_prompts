everyone in philosophy of mind circles keeps saying the self is like the center of gravity of a physical object -- useful for predictions but not a real part inside the thing. Dennetts version is that its a center of narrative gravity, and people lean on this to say there's no real self, just stories we tell. but I keep thinking: in physics the center of gravity is calculable from mass distribution, moves predictably, and if you split the object it splits too. in the narrative case the center can jump discontinously after trauma or conversion, it can be contested (two people claiming the same autobiographical center), and it seems to have causal powers a physical center doesnt -- like making you feel shame or prompting action. so audit the analogy: what corresponds to mass, to gravitational pull, to the coordinate frame? where does the mapping break? does the conclusion that the self is not real survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or is the analogy smuggling in physicalist assumptions? and is there a better analogy -- maybe an event horizon, or a strange attractor in dynamical systems -- that captures the discontinuities without pretending the self is just fiction?
