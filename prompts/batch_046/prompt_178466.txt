I'm writing a feature for an ideas magazine on why Whig history keeps resurfacing in popular science writing, and I want to audit an analogy that defenders lean on heavily. The claim is that writing the past as progress toward the present is like tracing a river upstream to its source: you begin at the present delta—say, modern constitutional democracy or the molecular structure of DNA—and follow the tributaries backward, treating each earlier current as contributing flow to the final outcome. The analogy is used to argue that selecting past events by their present relevance is not bias but cartography; you are simply mapping the watershed that actually produced the result. Critics reply that the image quietly imports teleology, since rivers do not have destinations, and treating the present as the inevitable mouth makes contingency disappear. Lay out the correspondence table explicitly: in historical explanation, what plays the role of gravity, the riverbed, evaporation, and tributaries that dry up before reaching the sea? Then isolate the disanalogies that matter for the intended defense: does the river model allow for tributaries that were dominant in their own era but contributed nothing recognizably to the present? Does it treat historical disappearance as failure to reach the delta rather than as transformation into something unrecognizable? And does it presuppose that cultural causation is conservative—water remains water—when transmission in fact involves translation, mistranslation, and radical repurposing? Next, evaluate whether the analogy survives if we substitute a different ecological image—say, a forest in which deadwood feeds new growth through unpredictable fungal networks—where the present ecosystem owes as much to decay and lateral exchange as to direct lineage. Finally, tell me whether the defense of Whig historiography—that we are merely describing the causal path that led here—survives once these disanalogies are visible, or whether the river image was carrying the entire argument all along.
