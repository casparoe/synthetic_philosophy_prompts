Write a short story about the paradox of fiction, but skip the usual classroom examples. Here's a weird setup: there's a therapist who writes custom fictional tragedies for patients who feel nothing about real loss — they sob over her made-up characters but stay cold when she mentions their actual dead relatives. One day she writes a story-inside-a-story about a character who realizes she's fictional and starts mourning her own non-existence. The client finally cries, but it's clearly for the inner fiction; yet that unlocks real grief about his sister.

Keep it muddy — does feeling for a fiction-within-fiction count as real emotinal response? Is the therapist's own grief for her dead kid just another narrative she authored? Don't resolve it neatly. Write it like she's jotting this in a notebook at 2am, casual voice, maybe drop a comma here and there. I want the philosophy in the weeds, not a tidy essay with answers.
