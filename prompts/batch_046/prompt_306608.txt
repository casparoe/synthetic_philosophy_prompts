I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I am drafting interface specifications for an autonomous logistics platform that will deploy on physically identical hardware in two jurisdictions—call them Earth and Twin Earth—where sensor inputs and internal logic are indistinguishable, but external legal and institutional frameworks assign different meanings to terms such as "operator," "hazard zone," and "autonomous decision." I want to operationalize the core idea behind the Twin Earth thought experiment in philosophy of language—namely, that the content of some terms is fixed partly by external reference rather than solely by internal syntax or mental state—into a reusable decision procedure my team can apply during specification reviews. The deliverable should be a step-by-step checklist or flowchart in words: for any term in our spec, run the tests in order to determine whether it has "wide" content (its meaning differs across jurisdictions because of external reference) or "narrow" content (it is defined entirely within the system and is therefore identical in both deployments). For each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer, identify which steps are unavoidable judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and mark the exact point where the procedure yields a definitive classification versus where it must hand the decision to a human reviewer because the issue is one of institutional values rather than semantic fact. Then apply the procedure to three specification terms: "operator" (where labor law defines the role differently), "temperature threshold" (where sensor calibration is identical but the clinical or regulatory significance of the value diverges), and "autonomous decision" (where the internal algorithm is unchanged but one jurisdiction mandates human-in-the-loop oversight). Include at least one case in which the procedure classifies the term as wide-content despite the engineering intuition that it ought to be purely internal. Finally, state explicitly what the procedure cannot settle—particularly whether a term should be re-engineered to eliminate external dependence, and whether the wide/narrow distinction itself breaks down for highly abstract technical terms. The procedure must be runnable in under fifteen minutes per term by reviewers without philosophical training, and it should not require any external research or citation.
