I am putting together an adversarial-collaboration exercise for an advanced seminar on epistemic ethics, and I have grown tired of the usual classroom replay of James versus Clifford in the abstract. I want you to draft the joint statement that a committed Jamesian and a committed Cliffordian should be able to sign together—except, of course, for the parts where they genuinely diverge—focused specifically on the ethics of belief under what I will call structural evidential opacity: situations in which a decision-making collective (say, a multi-disciplinary engineering team maintaining a deep-sea carbon-capture system) must adopt or reject a provisional explanatory model in order to act, even though no single member can personally verify the full evidential chain, and where both adoption and suspension carry irreversible practical risks. The scenario should be realistic, grounded in institutional practice rather than science fiction, and should not invoke perfect predictors, demons, or exotic physics.

Please structure the document in four parts. First, list the claims both sides can jointly endorse about the nature of belief, the conditions under which evidence is sufficient, the distinction between voluntary and involuntary assent, and the moral stakes of institutional error. Second, state the precise disagreements in neutral wording—phrased so that each side recognizes its own view—particularly around whether there is a meaningful distinction between living and dead hypotheses in applied contexts, and whether epistemic risk can ever be legitimately traded against practical catastrophe. Third, identify at least two cruxes: for each, present a concrete decision-point within the carbon-capture maintenance scenario that both sides agree bears on the crux, state in advance what each camp predicts about the correct course of belief and action, and explain which feature of the case drives their predictions apart. Fourth, conclude with an honest residue report: is any portion of the dispute merely verbal—say, about the term evidence versus the term faith—and if so, which portion? Also note whether removing the institutional-collective feature, collapsing the case to a single agent with complete access, dissolves the disagreement or merely relocates it.

Constraints: keep the scenario to one or two paragraphs; do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; avoid quoting specific passages—paraphrase the two positions without quotation marks; and do not invoke obscure theories or technical apparatus that a well-read model could not be expected to know. The goal is a document that isolates the genuine philosophical tension without letting either side win by definitional fiat.
