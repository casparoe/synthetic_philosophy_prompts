As a retired experimental physicist now studying philosophy of action, I recently heard two colleagues debate Daniel Dennett's compatibilism against Gregg Caruso's rejection of just deserts. Their exchange ran as follows. (1) One argued that Caruso's quarantine model treats offenders like pathogens, stripping them of moral agency in a way Dennett avoids: for Dennett, responsibility is a functional status embedded in our social technology, allowing us to address agents without invoking libertarian freedom. (2) The other replied that Dennett's functionalism collapses into Caruso's conclusion—if responsibility tracks only predictive utility and cooperative engineering, then once retributive suffering is shown to lack rehabilitative value we should abandon it entirely, leaving only quarantine. (3) The first speaker countered that Dennett supplies a non-circular criterion—reactive attitudes are warranted when they stabilize legitimate cooperative expectations—while Caruso offers no comparable principle, merely replacing one metaphysics with epidemiology. (4) The second speaker insisted that Dennett's stabilization criterion is circular: expectations are stabilized by blaming practices, and blaming practices are justified by the need to stabilize expectations, with no independent normative check; hence the functionalist defense of desert secretly depends on the retributive intuition it purports to dissolve. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each argument from clearly mistaken to clearly right, justify each rating, and say what, if anything, both disputants overlook. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then reason through it with no preamble and without restating the question.
