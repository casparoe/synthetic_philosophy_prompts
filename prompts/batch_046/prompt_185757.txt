Construct a thought experiment in which an agent performs a morally required action, and the reason that actually moves her is structurally isomorphic to the correct moral reason—same propositional content, same normative structure—but is causally sustained by a non-moral, aesthetically conditioned associative pathway rather than by direct moral recognition. Design the case so that a right-reasons account of moral worth grants the agent full moral worth, while a source-purist account (which holds that moral worth requires the motivating reason to originate in a genuinely moral source, not in a non-moral associative mechanism) denies her any moral worth. Constraints: the setting must be an ordinary workplace or family context, not a trolley or disaster scenario; present the scenario in a single paragraph; the mediating mechanism must be psychologically realistic, such as habit formation through repeated environmental pairing; and the disagreement must not collapse into the familiar distinction between acting from duty and acting from mere inclination. After presenting the scenario, do the following: (i) specify the action, the agent’s explicit motivation, the correct moral reason, and the associative pathway that sustains it; (ii) show precisely why the right-reasons theorist assigns full worth and why the source-purist assigns none, identifying the structural feature that drives the divergence; (iii) state what happens to both verdicts if the associative mediation is removed and the agent instead accesses the moral reason directly; and (iv) explain how a defender of the source-purist view would attack the case—specifically, whether they would deny that the agent truly grasps the moral reason, or concede the grasp but deny that it is the operative cause.
