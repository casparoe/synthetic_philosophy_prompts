Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with refrains in the pattern A1bA2 / abA1 / abA2 / abA1 / abA2 / abA1A2—about Max Black’s counterexample to Leibniz’s Principle of Identity of Indiscernibles. One refrain must state or evoke the principle (that complete sameness of properties entails being one thing); the other must evoke the bare numerical difference of the spheres. Render the setup with philosophical precision: two bodies of identical size, mass, and composition suspended in an otherwise empty, perfectly symmetric space, lacking any other objects to supply relational distinctions, so that even spatial position cannot non-circularly distinguish them. Do not resolve the tension by invoking hidden intrinsic properties, quantum identity, or divine discrimination; instead, let the villanelle’s cyclical refrains enact the circularity of trying to distinguish the spheres relationally in a featureless void. In the final quatrain, where both refrains converge, register the cost of preserving their distinctness—namely, that location must be treated as an intrinsic property or space as absolute—without taking Leibniz’s or Black’s side. The rhyme and repetition should track the argument: the returning lines should feel like the same claim reasserting itself, only to be met each time by the unyielding fact of two.
