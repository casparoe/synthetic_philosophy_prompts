I am a retired experimental physicist who, since leaving active research, have been auditing courses in philosophy of science and find myself repeatedly drawn back to the classic tension between spacetime substantivalism and relationism, especially as it is crystallized in Newton's rotating-bucket thought experiment. In a recent seminar with former colleagues—mostly engineers and one applied mathematician—we disagreed not about the observable mechanics of rotating water, but about which interpretive framework best honors empirical discipline while avoiding commitments to entities or structures that play no independent dynamical role. To clarify my own thinking, I ask you to rank the following five interpretive stances on Newton's bucket argument according to a single criterion: their success in explaining why the water's surface becomes concave under rotation without appealing to empirically inaccessible reference frames or redundant ontological baggage. Assign each a score from 1 to 10, with 1 being the most successful, and present them in strict ranked order.

The stances are: (A) Newton's original substantivalist reading, in which absolute space itself provides the true reference for rotation; (B) Mach's relationalist response, according to which rotational inertia is determined by relation to the distant distribution of matter; (C) a modern geometric substantivalism that retains spacetime points as real but treats rotational effects as properties of the metric field rather than of material relations alone; (D) a dynamical relationalism that eliminates all absolute spacetime structures and derives inertia entirely from the dynamics of relative configurations; and (E) a structural-relational compromise that denies both substantial points and pure material reductionism, treating rotational inertia as an irreducible feature of a network of spatiotemporal relations that carries no underlying substance. For each entry, provide the rank, the score, and in no more than two sentences the decisive reason for its placement. Then, in one final sentence, state whether your top-ranked stance genuinely dissolves the original tension or merely relocates the unobservable commitment elsewhere. Keep the entire response within 250 words.
