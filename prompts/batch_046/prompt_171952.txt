So I've been trying to wrap my head round hinge epistemlogy after reading bits of Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and I think I'm stuck on a basic mix-up. The idea is that some commitments — like that I have hands, or that the earth existed long before me — aren't ordinary beliefs but more like the frame that lets belief happen at all. But if they're not beliefs, then in what sense can they be correct or wrong? And Wittgenstein talks about the riverbed shifting over time, which sounds like these frame-commitments can change, but if they can change doesn't that just mean they were deep beliefs all along? There's also this move people make where they say religious faith should be treated as a hinge rather than something you justify with evidence — but if hinges sit outside the game of giving reasons, then saying my faith is a hinge feels like saying it's protected from criticism without explaining why anyone should share it. So is there a real distinction between a hinge and a very stubborn belief, or is the whole framework just blurring psychological confidence with logical structure?
