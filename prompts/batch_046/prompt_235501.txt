Write an essay on the following controversy and defend a clear verdict. William James permits belief in living, forced, and momentous options when evidence is genuinely insufficient, provided the choice is unavoidable and the risk is genuine. W. K. Clifford, by contrast, maintains that belief formed on insufficient evidence is wrong regardless of context, extending this prohibition to commitments that guide practical and theoretical inquiry. The specific question is whether James's framework can legitimately license scientific working hypotheses—exemplified by the provisional adoption of causal closure as a guiding assumption in cognitive neuroscience, where empirical data underdetermine the framework and abandoning it would paralyze the research program—without collapsing into the kind of evidentialist violation Clifford condemns. In your essay, weigh whether working acceptance of a theoretical framework can be distinguished from full belief in a way that preserves James's defense; examine whether Clifford's argument against negligent commitment applies with equal force to methodological heuristics adopted in institutional science; and determine whether the conditions of a living option can be satisfied in collective scientific practice or only in individual existential choice. Reach a definite conclusion: either argue that James's doctrine, properly circumscribed, justifies such provisional scientific commitments, or argue that Clifford's prohibition extends to working assumptions and James's exception therefore fails to protect scientific practice.
