Writing for a column on ideas and institutions: commentators increasingly liken contemporary deplatforming and social sanction to Roman infamia—the formal degradation of civic standing that stripped reputation without physical harm. Audit that analogy precisely: lay out the correspondence table—what plays the magistrate, the public record, the irreversible stain, the affected citizen—then the disanalogies, especially decentralized versus state-enacted authority, platform terms versus legal statute, reversibility versus permanence, and the gap between a polity and an audience. Does the analogy genuinely support the claim that cancel culture operates as legitimate communal punishment, or does it smuggle in false equivalence between crowd dynamics and state-inflicted civil death? If the framing is conceptually mismatched at the root—if comparing decentralized platform bans to Roman infamia is already the error—say so plainly rather than forcing the audit to accommodate it.
