I have been developing the position that Jain anekāntavāda — specifically the syādvāda scheme of conditional, qualified predication — should be treated not merely as an ancient metaphysical pluralism about the complexity of reality, but as a live logical framework competing with modern paraconsistent and dialetheist accounts of contradiction. On this reading, the syāt qualifier, usually glossed as in some respect or perhaps, does not express mere epistemic caution; it functions as a formal index that distributes contradictory properties across different evaluation respects, allowing an object to be both permanent and impermanent, or an agent to bear genuinely incompatible moral obligations, without either accepting true contradictions within a single evaluation context (as dialetheists do) or weakening negation to block explosion (as paraconsistent logics do). I think this gives anekāntavāda unexpected traction in normative ethics, where it might describe conflicts of duty that classical deontic logic forces us to resolve artificially. What would actually count against this position? I want a map of its exposure: formal objections about whether syādvāda can be reconstructed with rigor without collapsing into contextualism, trivialism, or an unworkable proliferation of respects; interpretive objections about whether Jain texts ever intended syāt to do this kind of logical labor, and whether modernizing it this way violates the doctrine’s original scope; comparative objections from dialetheists or paraconsistent logicians who might argue that anekāntavāda either solves nothing they have not solved more precisely or smuggles in hidden consistency conditions; and practical objections about whether a framework that refuses absolute negation can ever deliver decisive moral guidance or instead licenses normative paralysis. Please rank these defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be explicit about which ones a committed defender of this modernized anekāntavāda would concede as damaging versus which they could absorb by refining the doctrine. I also want an honest assessment of whether I have insulated the view: if every counterexample can be handled by adding another respect, has the position become unfalsifiable, and is that a philosophical vice for a logical framework of this kind?
