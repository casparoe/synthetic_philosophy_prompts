Maya said that if you watch Physarum polycephalum solve a maze, your seeing real patterns that only make sense from the intentional stance. The mould isn't designed by anyone, but it behaves as if it has goals — finding the shortest path, avoiding dead ends — and Dennett's idea is that real patterns are the ones that let you predict behaviour better than the physical stance ever could. So calling it just chemistry misses the predictive power you gain by treating it like an agent.

Leo said thats backwards. The design stance already covers slime mould: evolution designed its chemotaxis mechanisms, so you predict its moves by knowing its functional architecture, not by attributing beliefs. He said Maya was smuggling in a designer-less intentional stance, which Dennett never meant for organisms without nervous systems. Plus, he added, real patterns exist at every level — molecular diffusion is a real pattern too — so the existence of macro-patterns doesnt license jumping to intentionality.

Maya came back saying Leo was conflating orgin with explanation. Evolution doesnt design in the intentional sense; its just selection. If you insist on a designer for the design stance, then you cant apply it to evolved traits at all, which is absurd. She said the intentional stance works precisely when there's no designer — its a predictive strategy that doesnt care about origins. She also threw in this thing she called the ecological stance, from some obscure philosophy of biology paper, where you predict behaviour by treating the organism-plus-environment as a single coupled system, and said this was actually the right level, not Leo's design stance.

Leo replied that this 'ecological stance' sounds made up, or at least its just the physical stance with extra steps. He said if Maya wants to allow intentionality for slime mould, she has to allow it for rivers finding the sea or crystals growing, because they all show macro-patterns that are predictable. He asked where she draws the line, and said she cant answer without either being arbitrary or secretly relying on physical complexity.
