everyone keeps comparing autonomous systems to subcontracters — you hire them, they mess up, you're still on the hook just like a manager is for their team. people use this to argue we should just apply normal vicarious liability rules and there's no special 'responsibility gap'. but i think the analogy is doing a lot of work.

can you lay out the correspondance table? what plays the role of the contract, the manager's oversight, the employee's autonomy, the firing mechanism? then tell me where it breaks — especially around the idea that you can 'fire' an algorithm or that the system has intentions like a human worker. does the analogy quietly import the idea that human oversight is always present, like a manager checking in? and what about the case where the human is just a shock absorber — they get blamed but never actually had control? does the subcontracter analogy survive that? also, is there a better analogy — maybe something with tools, or with weather prediction — that captures the gap without pretending there's always a boss in the loop?
