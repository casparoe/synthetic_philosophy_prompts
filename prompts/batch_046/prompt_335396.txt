I'm drafting material for a workshop on social ontology and evolutionary dynamics, specifically trying to hold in tension the rationalistic and evolutionary explanations of how arbitrary coordination equilibria stabilize. Rather than writing another expository paragraph, I want to close our session with a villanelle that enacts the problem itself: the rhyme scheme and the two refrains should mirror the interlocking expectations that make a convention self-sustaining, and the poem's formal repetition should feel like the accumulation of precedent that evolutionary accounts emphasize.

The philosophical content needs to stay in the weeds. The poem should treat convention not as mere habit but as a solution to a coordination problem with genuinely equivalent alternatives—left or right, one queue or two—so that the arbitrariness is structurally present, not just mentioned. One refrain should keep returning to the idea that any of several signals could have done the work; the other should insist that once adopted, deviation is irrational not because the rule is intrinsically right but because the cost of breaking the pattern is borne collectively. I want the turn—around the fifth tercet—to be the intrusion of the evolutionary account: the moment when shared reasoning gives way to selection, repetition, and path dependence, so that the reader feels the shift from common knowledge to silent lock-in. The closing couplet should refuse to settle whether stability comes from rational recognition or from the weight of history, leaving the knot open for the audience.

Please make the verse precise enough that someone who works on Lewis-style convention theory and on evolutionary game-theoretic models of norm emergence would recognize the specific tension—particularly the challenge that evolutionary stability does not require the common knowledge of rationality that Lewis's account seems to presuppose—rather than seeing a generic poem about rules or habits. No citations, no bibliography, and no references to specific page numbers are needed; I just need the form to carry the idea tightly enough that my colleagues can feel the conceptual problem in the rhyme and refrain rather than only read about it.
