Here is the package of views I defend on evolutionary explanation, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. I hold: (1) Gould and Lewontin's spandrels critique is largely correct—many phenotypic traits are non-adaptive architectural byproducts rather than direct adaptations shaped for their current role; (2) this critique applies with full force to cognitive and behavioral traits, even though mental organization lacks the physical structural analog that makes morphological spandrels obvious; (3) evolutionary psychology's reverse-engineering program remains defensible, but only for capacities exhibiting high design specificity and strong cross-cultural universality; (4) the adaptation/exaptation/spandrel taxonomy is conceptually coherent and ought to structure evolutionary explanations, even though for behavioral traits we rarely possess sufficient historical data to apply it reliably; (5) when a behavioral trait is currently universal and enhances fitness in a stable environment, its adaptive origin is the appropriate default hypothesis, to be abandoned only when an explicit spandrel or exaptation account is available. Which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other, which conflicts are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the real tensions by severity, specify for each what commitment would have to give and at what price, and indicate whether a single revision could resolve the most tension at once. I have no prior commitment on which view to drop.
