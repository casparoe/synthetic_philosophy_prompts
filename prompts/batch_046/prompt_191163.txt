For a long-form essay I am drafting on predictive frameworks in cognitive science, I have constructed what strikes me as a reductio of the standard physicalist reading of Dennett's three stances—physical, design, and intentional. The argument proceeds through seventeen steps, and I find myself unable to locate the error without surrendering something I would prefer to keep.

(1) A pattern is real, on Dennett's criterion, only if it supports projectible counterfactuals and improves upon random guessing within its domain.

(2) The physical stance predicts behavior from the laws governing matter and energy; in principle it covers every event, though for complex agents it is computationally intractable.

(3) The design stance predicts behavior from functional architecture—what a system is engineered or evolved to do—without requiring belief-desire psychology.

(4) The intentional stance predicts behavior by attributing rational beliefs and desires.

(5) Now consider any successful intentional-stance prediction: if physics is complete, then for that predicted behavior there exists some physical-stance description that also entails it, even if no agent can ever compute that description.

(6) By parsimony, if two descriptions cover the same behavioral domain and one is complete in principle, the other ought to be treated as a compressed heuristic rather than an independent real pattern.

(7) Therefore intentional-stance patterns are not real over and above physical patterns; they are useful fictions.

(8) The same reasoning applies to the design stance: evolutionary and engineering functions are ultimately implemented in physics, so design-stance patterns are also heuristic compressions of physical patterns.

(9) But now inject a constructed case: a synthetic cognitive architecture—call it a stance-interference system—built with analog neuromorphic circuits that carry no evolutionary history. For this system, the design stance predicts catastrophic failure from thermal drift in the analog substrate. The intentional stance predicts rationalization and continued operation from contradictory belief attributions. The two predictions conflict: the design stance forecasts shutdown; the intentional stance forecasts persistence.

(10) In actual testing, the system continues operating but with degraded coherence, matching neither pure prediction.

(11) Only a hybrid prediction—combining thermal-drift parameters with a modified belief-attribution rule—captures the outcome. This hybrid relies on normative terms ("rationalize," "degraded coherence") that have no direct translation into physical vocabulary.

(12) If only physical patterns are real, then both pure predictions and the hybrid should be reducible to physical descriptions. But the hybrid's success depends on cross-stance terms that resist such translation.

(13) Hence the hybrid tracks a pattern that is not physically reducible.

(14) Applying this back to human cognition: neuroscience, evolutionary psychology, and intentional psychology constantly generate conflicting predictions about edge-case behavior, and they are sometimes reconciled only in hybrid clinical or engineering models. By (13), those hybrids also track real, non-physical patterns.

(15) Therefore physicalism, understood as the claim that all real patterns are ultimately physical, is false.

(16) To preserve physicalism, one might deny (12) and insist that even hybrid predictions are ultimately physical, merely awaiting the right translation.

(17) But if (16) holds, then (6) applies with full force: all non-physical stances are mere heuristics, and the intentional stance has no explanatory claim on reality—its successes become accidental correlations rather than tracks of rational patterns.

I am committed to keeping (1) and (4) intact, and I am reluctant to abandon physicalism without overwhelming reason. Where does the chain break? Is the error in (5), in the inference from the synthetic case at (9) to the hybrid result at (11), or in the analogical leap from (13) to (14)? And what does rejecting each of those steps cost—does it save physicalism only by making the design and intentional stances mysteriously disconnected from the physical world they clearly operate within?
