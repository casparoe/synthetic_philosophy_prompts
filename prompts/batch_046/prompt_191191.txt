I was talking with my friend after our phil of mind class about Dennett's real patterns idea and how it relates to the three stances — physical, design, intentional. She was convinced that if a pattern only shows up when you take the intentional stance then it's not 'really real', just a useful fiction. I think that's wrong but I couldn't articulate why without sounding like I'm just defending anithropomorphism. So write a short story about a sound-archivist in a library where books don't have pages but emit frequencies arranged in patterns. She tries to catalog them using the physical stance (just waveforms), the design stance (the library was engineered to preserve knowledge this way), and then accidentally slips into the intentional stance (the books are warning her about something). There's one sequence that forms a clear map of the building only in the intentional stance, but collapses into noise in the other two. She has to figure out whether she's discovered a real pattern or just compressed her own fear into the vibrations. Don't explain Dennett in the story — show it through her drawing one wrong map, spilling coffee on it, and deciding wether to trust the pattern anyway. Keep it to max 450 words, one scene, no chapters.
