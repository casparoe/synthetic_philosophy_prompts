I've been thinking about There Will Be Blood lately and whether it's secretly a Weberian case study hiding in plain sight. Daniel Plainview is pure capital accumulation, Eli Sunday is pure religious performance, and the movie never lets us decide whether Eli's faith is actually driving events or just dressing up economic competition in holy language. I'm trying to read the film through Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis — specifically the problem of whether cultural values can be genuine causes rather than post-hoc rationalizations of material interests. There's that baptism scene where Daniel has to submit to Eli to secure the pipeline deal: is that Weber's elective affinity breaking down into pure instrumentalism? Or does the movie argue that once capitalism and religion are locked in that affinity, you can't tell the cause from the costume?

I'd like a reading that takes both the film and Weber seriously — not just using the movie as an illustration — and that addresses whether Daniel's final line about being finished is a rejection of value-rational action altogether, or just the logical endpoint of an ascetic ethic stripped of its transcendental justification. I'm also curious if there's anything in the film that complicates Weber's causal story: maybe Eli's emotional grip on his congregation shows values operating as an independent social force that capital has to accomodate rather than absorb. Just tell me where the film illuminates Weber, where it resists him, and whether the ambiguity between culture-as-cause and culture-as-epiphenomenon is something the film actually resolves, or just dramatizes without answering.
