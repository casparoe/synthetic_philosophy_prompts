Write a deadpan parody of an internal corporate ethics whitepaper—numbered sections, dry bureaucratic headings, approximately 600 words—issued by a fictional integrated-pest-management consultancy named Orthoptera Solutions. The document, titled Ethical Framework for Precision Eradication Protocols v.2.1, should defend the firm’s proprietary method for controlling locust swarms and aphid infestations through a framework that treats consequentialist harm-minimization as an accounting exercise. Specifically, the whitepaper should quietly take three philosophical positions without naming them: first, that the moral status of an individual insect depends on whether its neural architecture supports phenomenal binding, and that absent binding, only aggregate swarm-level welfare counts; second, that the lesser-evil calculus applies across generations of short-lived organisms in a way that raises the non-identity problem—killing this swarm prevents a numerically different but equally suffering future swarm, yet the firm treats the substitution as morally neutral; third, that agronomists visiting from jurisdictions with stricter invertebrate-protection laws have a contractual obligation to follow local eradication conventions, analogizing the duty to queueing or jaywalking norms rather than to universal moral law. The satire should exaggerate real features of applied ethics—cost-benefit analysis of sentience, the aggregation problem, and the locality of conventional obligations—so that a reader familiar with the literature recognizes the targets as fair rather than invented. Keep the voice consistently bureaucratic; do not break character or wink at the reader. The piece should be readable without specialist knowledge but should reward recognition of the underlying concepts. Begin with the whitepaper itself, with no preface or commentary.
