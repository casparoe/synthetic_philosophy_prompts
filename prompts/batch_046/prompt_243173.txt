I've been working through Schopenhauer's metaphysics of music — the claim in his central metaphysical work that music is not a copy of Platonic Ideas like the other arts, but rather a direct objectification of the Will itself, operating outside the principle of sufficient reason and bypassing conceptual representation entirely. I was discussing this with a philosophy-of-mind reading group, and we tried to map Schopenhauer's hierarchy onto modern debates about non-conceptual content and embodied cognition in musical perception. We kept circling back to three interrelated tensions that seem genuinely open, and I'm trying to determine which one functions as the actual crux — both for interpreting Schopenhauer charitably and for assessing whether his account is salvageable for contemporary aesthetics and philosophy of mind, rather than just a historical curiosity.

The first tension is metaphysical: if music truly bypasses representational mediation, how do we account for the structural cognition — harmonic expectation, thematic transformation, formal analysis — that listeners clearly perform? Is Schopenhauer committed to a radical non-cognitivism about musical structure, or can his view accommodate these processes without collapsing into contradiction? The second is comparative and interdisciplinary: Schopenhauer privileges melody and instrumental music over vocal or program music because text introduces conceptual representation, yet modern evolutionary and social-cognitive accounts treat music primarily as a relational, communicative practice embedded in social coordination. Does integrating these empirical and philosophical frameworks require abandoning Schopenhauer's core metaphysical claim, or can the claim be reconstructed — say, by treating social function and direct Will-objectification as operating at different explanatory levels? The third is normative and system-level: Schopenhauer treats aesthetic contemplation of music as temporary liberation from the Will's suffering, but within his broader pessimism this looks more like an analgesic than a solution. Is the ethical value of music merely palliative, or does it point to something like a genuine, if fleeting, transcendence of the subject-object divide?

Given that I have limited time and no plan to become a Schopenhauer scholar, I want a defended prioritization: which of these three tensions should I treat as upstream, in the sense that resolving it would most change my view of the other two and determine whether Schopenhauer's framework is worth adapting? I also want to know whether there's a fourth line — perhaps about how his view relates to current debates on phenomenal consciousness, or on whether aesthetic experience can be genuinely non-intentional — that you'd rank above all three. Please defend the ordering explicitly: what makes a question tractable with conceptual rather than empirical tools, how the dependencies run between them, and what resolution on one side would actually shift anything downstream in ethics or philosophy of art. Crucially, separate what is fairly settled in Schopenhauer interpretation from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers who take the metaphysics seriously, and do not present a disputed reading — for instance, about the literalness of the Will-objectification claim or about Schopenhauer's attitude toward vocal music — as if it were the consensus view.
