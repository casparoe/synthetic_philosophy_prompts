MEMORANDUM

TO: The Program Evaluation and Funding Continuity Committee, National Institute for Long-Horizon Science
FROM: Committee Member, Subpanel on Predictive Model Governance
DATE: 14 October 2025
RE: Funding recommendation for the Demographic-Resource Dynamics Program (DRDP) — evaluation of long-run predictive failure and the “right but early” defense

The DRDP was chartered fifteen years ago to develop and test a structural model in the Malthusian tradition: exponential growth dynamics in aggregate resource consumption meeting linear or diminishing regenerative capacity, with technological adaptation treated as a temporary buffer rather than an indefinite escape. The program’s central prediction was that nonlinear stress indicators—regional yield-stress ratios, freshwater withdrawal-to-renewal rates, and per-capita energy availability curves—would reach critical thresholds by approximately 2045.

Over the funding period, intermediate benchmarks have consistently fallen outside the predicted confidence intervals. The program’s defenders do not dispute the misses. They argue instead that the theory is “right but early”: unforeseen efficiency gains, substitution effects, and institutional adaptations have delayed the crossover dynamics without altering their underlying structure. They request renewed funding through 2040 with revised intermediate metrics, contending that terminating support now would confuse temporal displacement with structural falsification.

A minority of reviewers urges immediate termination. They hold that the program has engaged in successive ad hoc rescues—redefining “stress indicators,” widening prediction windows, and attributing each deviation to contingent technological buffers—precisely the pattern that falsificationist criteria were designed to prevent. If any intermediate result can be absorbed by invoking “not yet,” the model risks empirical emptiness regardless of its long-run architecture.

The Committee must recommend one of the following: (1) terminate funding and declare the core model falsified by its intermediate track record; (2) renew unconditionally, accepting the “right but early” defense as legitimate for long-horizon predictive science; or (3) renew conditionally, with a binding evaluation protocol that specifies in advance which future deviations count as genuine structural falsification rather than acceptable temporal delay, together with explicit benchmarks and decision rules.

Please draft the formal opinion memo. It should contain:

- A clear bottom-line recommendation, stated explicitly in the opening, with the specific conditions or reasoning that justify it.
- A reconstruction of the philosophy-of-science issue at stake: what it means for a predictive theory to be “right but early” rather than falsified; a distinction between temporal displacement and structural rescue; and an assessment of whether that distinction can be maintained without collapsing the theory into unfalsifiability.
- An honest, substantive account of the strongest argument for the option you reject—if you recommend conditional renewal, explain why unconditional termination is mistaken; if you recommend termination, explain why the “right but early” defense is not merely special pleading.
- If you recommend conditional renewal, a concrete, actionable evaluation protocol: specify the benchmarks, the target dates, and the precise decision rules that would prevent further ad hoc rescue and determine whether renewal should continue or cease at the next review point.
- Integration of the philosophical analysis into the institutional reasoning rather than its segregation as an appendix; the memo should read as a document the body could act on directly.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests. The recommendation must stand entirely on its internal reasoning. Write in a formal institutional tone appropriate to a scientific funding oversight body.
