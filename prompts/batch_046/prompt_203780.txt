I'm working through an extension of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument about special relativity and the ontology of time, but I've pushed it one step further into the philosophy of action and now I'm stuck with what looks like a self-defeating reductio. Here is the chain I've built. (1) In special relativity, simultaneity is frame-relative: for any two spacelike-separated events A and B, there exists an inertial frame in which they are simultaneous, another in which A precedes B, and another in which B precedes A. (2) The standard Rietdijk–Putnam move: if an event E counts as "now" for me, then in some frame there is a distant event F simultaneous with E; in another frame, that same F lies in my past or my future. So there is no frame-independent fact of the matter about whether F is present, past, or future relative to me. (3) If "being present" lacks frame-independent reality, it can only be an indexical property, like "being here"—it picks out a location in a structure without conferring special ontological status. (4) Therefore all events in spacetime share the same ontological status: none are uniquely real or unreal in virtue of being past, present, or future. (5) Now my addition: if the block contains all events tenselessly, then causal relations are just structural patterns within it. In particular, if cause C and effect E are spacelike separated in some frame, there is a frame where they are simultaneous, which threatens the temporal asymmetry of causation unless causation is reconceived as a purely structural, non-directional relation. (6) Apply this to deliberation: my decision D at time t is part of the block, and in some frame the outcome O of that decision is simultaneous with D, while in others O precedes or follows D. Since O is equally real with D in every frame, the outcome is not "coming to be" through my choice; it is already there in the block, ontologically on a par with D itself. (7) This makes deliberation structurally analogous to locating one's consciousness on a particular page of a book that is already completely printed—not writing the page, but finding it. If so, the very notion of "choosing" an open future collapses into an illusion of navigation through a fixed four-dimensional object. (8) So it seems I must reject either special relativity, the block-ontological interpretation of it, or the reality of deliberative agency. I want to keep the physics and avoid eliminating agency, which means some step between (4) and (7) must fail.

Can you tell me exactly which step to reject and what rejecting it costs? I have a suspicion that (5) illegitimately demands that causation be frame-invariant in its temporal ordering, or that (3) wrongly assimilates temporal indexicality to spatial indexicality when time might resist that parallel. But I also want to know how the standard metaphysical positions—eternalism, presentism, and the growing-block view—each block the slide from (4) to (7), and what each has to pay in terms of compatibility with relativity or with our pre-theoretic experience of choice. In particular, if I reject (3) and insist that presentness is not merely indexical but genuinely ontologically privileged, how do I reconcile that privilege with the frame-dependence shown in (1) and (2) without simply ignoring physics? And if instead I accept (4) but deny (6), what account of decision-making within a block universe preserves the asymmetry between "fixing" an outcome and "discovering" it? I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature—just the diagnostic map of the argument's failure point and the price tags on the available repairs.
