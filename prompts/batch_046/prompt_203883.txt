I'm prepping interview questions for a podcast with a philosopher who defends "localist self-trust" against the Löbian obstacle — she thinks the collapse only happens if self-trust is one global axiom, and that splitting belief into modules with restricted cross-endorsement rules lets you stay rational without the blowup. I want maybe six tight questions that go into the mechanics, not the elevator pitch.

1. Walk me through the exact closure step in Löb's derivation inside your modular setup. Is it blocked because Module A can't apply its trust rule to Module B's outputs, or is the syntax of the endorsement rule itself restricted?

Follow-up keyed to "syntax blocks it": If there's a meta-module that checks coherence across modules, can't it reconstruct the loop at second order?

2. Folks conflate Löb's obstacle with Gödel's second theorem all the time. Is the belief version genuinely different, or just Gödel in epistemic clothing? If different, what's the extra philosophical bite — damage to rational agency rather than formal consistency?

Follow-up if she says Gödel's about proof not belief: But if true belief is factive, why shouldn't belief inherit the same limitation?

3. You allow graded confidence — the agent applies the trust axiom only above 0.9. But Löb's proof only needs modus ponens and the axiom, not full certainty. How does lowering confidence stop the derivation instead of just delaying it?

Follow-up if she says graded belief breaks the modal logic: Are you solving the obstacle, or just redefining belief so the axiom never applies?

4. If global self-trust is banned, are long reasoning chains always suspect? Should a rational agent suspend judgment on anything taking more than two steps, or is there a principled line between safe chains and Löb-triggering ones?

Follow-up if she draws the line at module boundaries: What happens when a conclusion requires integrating outputs from three different modules?

5. What evidence could actually change your mind? Say we built an artificial agent with genuinely global self-trust that didn't collapse — maybe resource-bounded reasoning truncates the derivation. Would that refute you, or would you just say the truncation hides a modular structure?

No "what drew you to philosophy" filler — just press where it hurts.
