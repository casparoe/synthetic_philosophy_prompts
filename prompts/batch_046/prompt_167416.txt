Write the full specification of desiderata that any adequate philosophical theory of health and disease must satisfy. I want the list organized by the kind of work each criterion does: phenomena it must cover, distinctions it must preserve, results from biology or medicine it must remain consistent with, and conceptual puzzles it must dissolve. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or consideration behind it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or rather a preference of a particular camp (naturalist, normativist, or hybrid).

The theory must, in particular, handle the following without collapsing them into one another: (a) biological dysfunction that is contextually advantageous or even essential (for example, sickle-cell trait conferring malaria resistance, or microbiome states that are inflammatory in one host genotype yet symbiotic in another); (b) intact biological function that is experienced as harmful or socially disabling (for example, sensory capacities that cause suffering in hyper-stimulating environments, or deliberately attenuated capacities that improve quality of life); (c) the boundary between being diseased, being at elevated risk, and occupying a "pre-disease" state that predictive and precision medicine now targets; (d) comparisons across species, across radically different environments, and across engineered or chimeric organisms without assuming a single statistical norm or reference class; and (e) the normative intuition that disease is typically bad without either reducing biological function to value judgments or treating values as causally irrelevant to the classification.

Then flag any subset of these desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable. I am especially interested in whether accommodating microbiome functional pluralism—where no unique reference class determines health—can be reconciled with a naturalist requirement for objective dysfunction, and whether that tension is fatal or manageable through some other conceptual move.

Finally, briefly indicate how a biostatistical naturalist account (along Boorse's lines) and a holistic normativist account (along Nordenfelt's lines) score against your list, but keep the weight on the criteria rather than on the scorecard. At the end, state how confident you are in the assessment you have given, and single out the specific point about which you are least sure.
