I have been thinking for some time about why time has a direction, and I believe I have found a difficulty that the standard explanations leave untouched. As I understand it, the microscopic laws of physics are largely reversible, yet entropy tends to increase, and this thermodynamic arrow is said to be what distinguishes the past from the future—not merely correlating with it, but providing its actual physical basis.

From this, I have imagined a device—let me call it a recurrence vault—an isolated chamber containing an automated, purely mechanical gate that sorts particles according to velocity without any external energy source, exploiting only the statistical fluctuations permitted in a finite system across very long timescales. Rather than a brief downward fluctuation, suppose this mechanism sustains a localized pocket over centuries in which entropy steadily decreases. If entropy truly grounds the direction of time, then within this vault the arrow ought to point the opposite way: heat should flow from cold bodies to hot ones, and causal sequences should run in reverse relative to the world outside.

My question, then, concerns an observer placed inside during one of these sustained local reversals. Would that observer experience consciousness and memory running backward—un-learning information and growing younger—while people outside the vault continue to age in the usual manner? Or does the global entropy gradient of the surrounding universe somehow "lock" the local arrow in place, preventing any genuine reversal regardless of conditions inside the chamber?

If the first option is correct, then time's direction is not universal but patchwork, varying region by region according to local thermodynamic conditions. If the second option is correct, then entropy cannot be the direct cause of temporal orientation, but only a statistical pattern that happens to align with an arrow that must be explained in some other way. I cannot see how to choose between these two possibilities, and I wish to understand precisely where my reasoning has gone astray.
