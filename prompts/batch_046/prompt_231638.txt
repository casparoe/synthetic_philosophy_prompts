Construct a thought experiment extending the dialogue format of The Mind's I: Einstein's brain is preserved in a nutrient vat and responds to spoken questions, while in an adjacent room sits a complete printed archive—every letter, draft, and annotated calculation he produced—bound into volumes arranged so that a librarian can instantly retrieve the passage most responsive to any query. Both systems produce identical answers to every philosophical and scientific question, with the same reasoning steps visible. Design this scenario in one paragraph, holding constant the informational content and causal responsiveness of both systems, and isolate one structural feature—other than biological substrate, processing speed, or consciousness itself—that could explain why the brain is conscious and the archive is not, with the physical setup restricted to present-day preservation and printing technology. Then show exactly what a functionalist, a biological naturalist, and an enactivist each conclude, specifying which feature of the case drives their divergence. Identify the feature doing the real explanatory work, explain what happens if you remove it (for instance, by allowing the archive to rewrite its own volumes in response to new questions), and state how a defender of the archive's consciousness would attack the case. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts beyond the framing dialogue.
