I'm currently working through some comparative moral philosophy and have found myself stuck on a structural feature of Buddhist ethical reasoning that sits awkwardly beside Western consequentialist frameworks. In particular, I am interested in how Buddhist ethics handles the aggregation of morally significant outcomes across two dimensions that consequentialists usually treat separately: the temporal extension of a single conscious continuum—including future rebirths—and the simultaneous plurality of beings present in a given moment. Buddhist doctrine holds that karmic consequences ripen for the agent across lifetimes, yet it also emphasizes universal compassion and the Bodhisattva's commitment to liberating all sentient beings, which looks structurally like a form of horizontal aggregation. Meanwhile, consequentialism typically aggregates welfare horizontally across persons within a single temporal slice, addressing intertemporal concerns only through indirect mechanisms such as discounting future welfare or representing distant generations.

Could you please compare the Buddhist logic of karmic fruition and merit-transfer with the consequentialist logic of utility aggregation across individuals and time? More specifically, I want to understand whether Buddhist ethics implicitly contains a distinct theory of "vertical aggregation"—the accumulation and redistribution of welfare-relevant outcomes along a single diachronic mental continuum—that differs not merely in metaphysical commitment but in normative structure from consequentialist "horizontal aggregation." Does the possibility that an agent can direct accumulated positive karma toward another being's welfare collapse the vertical and horizontal dimensions in ways that a unitary consequentialist calculus cannot accommodate? And if so, does this suggest that Buddhist ethics operates with a two-dimensional aggregation matrix—temporal for the self, spatial or social for others—that renders direct comparison with standard consequentialist frameworks conceptually misleading rather than merely metaphysically divergent?
