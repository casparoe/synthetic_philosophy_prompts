Write the opening remarks for an interdisciplinary workshop entitled "Role, Ritual, and Care: Confucian Ethics in Clinical Settings." The audience is divided between scholars of Confucian philosophy and practicing clinical ethicists with no background in Chinese thought; the remarks should run approximately eight minutes when delivered. Introduce the core thesis of Confucian role ethics—that moral competence is constituted not by individual autonomy but by reciprocal obligations embedded in relational roles—without reducing it to generic relationality. Then frame the workshop's guiding problem: modern institutional caregiving roles, such as patient navigator or palliative care coordinator, do not map onto the classical five relationships, producing a condition of ritual indeterminacy in which moral obligations feel weighty yet lack an established ritual grammar. Let the tension between classical role-fixity and modern role-fluidity serve as the structural backbone of the speech: it should determine what you can present with conceptual confidence and what must remain genuinely open, and it should land for experts without losing the non-specialists. Do not provide a bibliography, cite specific passages, or ask the audience to consult particular texts; instead, allow the friction between inherited ritual grammar and emergent institutional roles to set the terms for the discussions that follow.
