Write a short story about a counterfactual auditor who discovers she is being XOR-blackmailed by an entity that operates through a modal prediction market. In this arrangement, the blackmailer never observes her choice directly; instead, the payout structure ties her decision to the welfare of one specific stranger in exactly one nearby possible world. If she complies, the stranger in branch A suffers; if she refuses, the stranger in branch B suffers—so her action determines which world bears the cost, even though she can never verify which branch is actual. Include a moment where she realizes the mugging is genuinely counterfactual: the market needs no direct observation of her, yet reasoning about her own decision changes the moral calculus in worlds she cannot access. The story should explore the psychological weight of this logical correlation without resolving whether she should pay or resist, and it should keep the human cost visible rather than reducing the dilemma to abstract game theory.
