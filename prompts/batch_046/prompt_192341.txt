Write a Petrarchan sonnet—octave rhymed ABBA ABBA, sestet rhymed CDECDE or CDCDCD, with a clear volta at line 9—about Schopenhauer's metaphysics of music. In the octave, present sculpture, painting, and poetry as representations of Platonic Ideas mediated through the principle of sufficient reason, using imagery of form, figure, and concept; keep the diction architectonic, weighted, and visually fixed. At the volta, pivot sharply: music is not a copy of an Idea but a direct objectification of the Will itself, bypassing representation entirely. In the sestet, unfold Schopenhauer's specific analogy between musical parts and the grades of the Will's objectification—bass as the inorganic realm, the inner voices as plant and animal life, melody as the conscious human will—so that the poem's rhythm and sonic texture ascend from heavy, low-register phrasing to lighter, more fluid movement. The closing couplet should state exactly what is gained and lost: melody tells the story of the will's movements without the veil of phenomena. Render this as verse rather than exposition, make it precise enough that a reader familiar with Schopenhauer's aesthetics would recognize the analogies, and do not include direct quotations or requests for citation.
