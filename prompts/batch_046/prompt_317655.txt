I've been thinking about Margalit's ethics of remembering and Rieff's argument that we sometimes have a duty to forget, and I want to apply them to Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind for a blog post — but I don't want to just use the movie as an illustration. Joel decides to erase Clementine, then tries to stop the procedure once it starts; meanwhile the erasure is handled by Lacuna, so he's basically delagating the work of forgetting to a machine and a company. Does that outsourcing change the moral picture? Is Joel violating a duty to preserve the memory of the relationship's failure as part of his moral growth, or is he exercising a legitimate right to emotional repair? The film adds this twist that Clementine already erased him, so there's an asymmetry where one remembers and the other doesn't. Does that asymmetry mean a wrong has been done to Joel, or does it just show that forgetting is never fully private? I'm trying to avoid reducing the film to a case study, so I'd like to know: what does the movie actually add to the philosophers' debate — maybe something about the social nature of memory — that Margalit and Rieff don't cover?
