I have been thinking about Tolstoy's well-known argument that art is essentially a form of emotional infection: the artist feels something with unusual intensity, and the work transmits that feeling directly to the audience. He seems to reject works that are only intellectually clever or technically refined if they fail to produce this emotional contagion. Recently I encountered a conceptual installation—blank white panels arranged in a precise grid, with the spacing between them encoding global temperature records over the past century. The artist explicitly said she was not aiming for any emotional effect; her goal was to let viewers grasp a complex pattern in a single spatial glance. To me, this still feels like genuine art, but Tolstoy's criterion seems to exclude it entirely, since there is no feeling being passed on. This makes me wonder whether Tolstoy's framework needs a revision: perhaps art can also involve the transmission of a structured cognitive pattern—a kind of conceptual infection—rather than only emotion. Write an essay on whether Tolstoy's emotional-infection standard should be preserved unchanged, broadened to include such cognitive transmission, or rejected altogether in light of works like this installation. Weigh the objections each option faces, and reach a definite conclusion with reasons.
