Write a decision tree that sorts philosophical positions on the poverty-of-the-stimulus argument in language acquisition, built from logical branching questions rather than from a list of named theories. Start from the commitment: "Children reach linguistic competence that genuinely outstrips the explicit and statistical information in their environment." Then branch on concrete diagnostic claims in this fixed order.

First: Is the gap best characterized as a sparsity of positive data, or as an absence of negative evidence (lack of explicit correction for overgeneralization)?

Second: Is the innate contribution representational—supplying specific rules, concepts, or structures—or procedural—supplying only learning constraints and biases?

Third: If representational, is it domain-specific to language or domain-general across cognition?

Fourth: If procedural, is it a domain-general statistical prior or a domain-specific learning architecture?

Fifth: For domain-specific representational views, is the innate structure fully formed and merely triggered by experience, or parameterized so that experience sets values?

The leaves must be exactly these six: (A) radical empiricist denial that any gap exists; (B) Chomskyan parameterized nativism; (C) pure-triggering domain-specific nativism; (D) domain-general representational nativism; (E) domain-general Bayesian/procedural rationalism; (F) domain-specific procedural nativism (a dedicated architecture without innate representations). For each unoccupied combination in the logical space—especially whether domain-general representational nativism can coherently be pure-triggering rather than parameterized—note whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate.

Present the tree first, with no preface or commentary. Afterward, briefly justify your node ordering and state which distinction you treat as primary.
