Exam question:
Some philosophers maintain that an artwork’s value is partly determined by its causal pedigree—the direct chain of intentional acts that connect the artist to the finished object—rather than by perceptible features or written instructions alone. Apply this view to the following case. In 2019, an artist creates a temporary sound installation by personally tuning fifty analog radios to static and arranging them in a circle; the piece is destroyed after one week. In 2024, the artist issues a precise technical protocol permitting reproduction. A museum follows the protocol with identical radio models, but an automated algorithm—calibrated only to the original acoustic measurements—performs the tuning. Is the 2024 installation a forgery, an authentic instance, or something else? Does it share the original’s artistic value? In your answer, clearly distinguish aesthetic value, historical value, and what the prompt calls genetic value tied to the artist’s direct causal involvement.

Student answer:
The 2024 installation is not a forgery but an authentic allographic instance, comparable to a performance of a musical score. Because the artist supplied an explicit protocol, the work is defined by its instructions rather than by the particular radios or by the artist’s hand in tuning them. The automated algorithm reproduces the identical acoustic result, so the aesthetic value—what listeners actually experience—is the same. Therefore, the museum version possesses the same artistic value overall.

One might object that the original has genetic value since the artist personally tuned each radio. I believe this objection confuses a biographical detail with an artistic property. Just as a perfect copy of a painting might have lower market value without losing artistic merit, the 2024 version lacks only the historical link to 2019, not any genuinely artistic quality. Hence, although the original may be more valuable as a historical relic, its artistic and aesthetic value are fully preserved in the authorized reproduction.
