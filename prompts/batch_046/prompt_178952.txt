I've been arguing with a friend about populism and I think we've backed ourselves into a corner. Here's the chain: (1) If democracy means rule by the people, then a government is legitimate only when it tracks the actual will of the people. (2) The actual will of the people at any moment is just whatever the majority currently prefers—there's no hidden "real" will sitting behind the polls, that's just mysticism. (3) So legitimacy is purely synchronic: if 52% want X today, X is legitimate; if they want not-X tomorrow, not-X becomes legitimate instead. (4) But populists don't talk like that, they say elites have "betrayed the true will of the people" even when they have majority support in polls, which implies there's a deeper, diachronic will that persists across polling shifts. (5) To identify that deeper will without just asking people, you need some interpreter—culture, tradition, the leader's intuition—to filter "false consciousness" from authentic preference. (6) So populism either collapses into ordinary majoritarianism, in which case "betrayal" is just loosing a vote, or it requires an unelected arbiter of the true will, in which case it's not democratic at all. (7) But this feels like it proves too much—every democratic theory either becomes a poll-following machine or an authoritarian cult. Where's the slip? I want to keep (2) and (4) if I can, but I'm open to loosing either. Which step do I reject, and what's the cost?
