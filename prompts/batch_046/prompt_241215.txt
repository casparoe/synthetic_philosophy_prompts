I'm trying to pin down a recurring pattern I notice in discussions of the classic classroom surprise-exam puzzle, but in a form that seems distinct from the usual backward-induction story. In the standard version, a teacher announces that there will be a surprise quiz during the coming week; the students reason that it cannot be on Friday, because if it had not occurred by Thursday they would know it was coming, which would contradict the announcement that it will be surprising; then they apply the same reasoning to Thursday, Wednesday, and so on, concluding that no day works—yet the quiz happens on Wednesday, and they are genuinely surprised.

Here is the variant that interests me. Imagine that the teacher is not only making the announcement, but is also fully aware that the students will perform exactly that backward-elimination reasoning. They realize that she might choose Friday for that reason, which means Friday is no longer safely eliminated—instead, it becomes a live possibility again, but now for strategic rather than logical reasons.
