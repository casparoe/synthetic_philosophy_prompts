so ive been thinking about whether lying is actualy worse than misleading, or if thats just a phrase people repeat. like, if you tell someone the meeting got moved to thursday when it didnt, thats a straight lie. but if you just leave the old calendar invite sitting there so they see thursday by mistake and never correct them, thats misleading? me and my friend keep circling this and it feels like philosophers have been stuck on the exact same loop for decades — some say lying is worse because you put a false thing into the world, others say misleading is just as bad because youre still steering someones brain into the wrong place. my working guess is that its not one debate but two smushed together: one side cares about breaking a rule about words, the other cares about controlling what another person ends up believing, but they both call the problem "wrong" so they talk past each other. but heres the weird middle thing ive been calling "layout lying" — you never say anything false, but you physically or digitally arrange true pieces of info so the other person assembles the wrong picture in their head, like stacking books so the wrong title is on top, or ordering a list so the second item looks like the first. does layout lying belong in the lying box or the misleading box? i think part of why nobody moves is that people secretly disagree on that sorting question and its doing all the hidden work. so is this stall just a verbal mess about the word "lie," or is there a real moral split hiding inside about falsehood versus control? and if its real, what could actually break it — a sharper distinction, a thought experiment that cleanly splits layout cases, or is the question built to stay open forever? be honest about whether we should even expect progress here, and why.
