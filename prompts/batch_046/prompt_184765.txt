Sen's liberal paradox demonstrates that granting each person authority over a personal pair of alternatives inevitably conflicts with Pareto efficiency, since the minimal liberal condition always generates a cyclical majority against unanimous preference. Given that this tension seems structural rather than contingent on particular tastes, should we conclude that any society honoring minimal liberal rights must permanently forgo Pareto improvements, or does the proof depend on a hidden preference assumption—such as people caring about others' choices—that could be dropped without abandoning liberalism itself?
