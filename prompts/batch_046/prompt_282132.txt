Consider the institutional practice in environmental accounting and food-procurement policy: sustainability metrics for university dining services and corporate ESG reports routinely rank beef as far worse than chicken, and both as worse than legumes, primarily on carbon and land-use grounds; yet these same frameworks do not quantify wild-animal deaths caused by crop cultivation—field rodents, insects, and birds killed during planting, harvesting, and pest management—nor do they treat such deaths as comparable to the direct slaughter of livestock. At the same time, agricultural extension services fund research to reduce incidental field mortality, framing it as an efficiency or biodiversity problem rather than an ethical cost to be weighed against dietary choice.

What philosophical commitments about moral status, harm, and comparability does this institutional framing presuppose? Must it assume that only direct, intentional killing is morally relevant, or can an indirect-harm view accommodate the asymmetry? Does the practice presuppose a speciesist boundary that grants farmed animals standing in the metric while excluding wild ones, or could a consistent consequentialist framework that aggregates suffering justify the omission on empirical grounds—say, that crop-field suffering is smaller, less certain, or non-comparable? And is the practice consistent with the same institutions' insistence, in animal-welfare auditing, that we should minimize suffering regardless of whether it arises from direct intention? Where is the framing philosophically underdetermined—might a rights-based view and a suffering-focused view both license it for different reasons, or do they diverge? Finally, say what would change your assessment: which empirical finding about wild-animal suffering in crop fields, or which argument about the moral relevance of indirect harm, would lead you to conclude that the practice rests on a genuinely inconsistent set of commitments rather than a defensible division of labor between ethical metrics and management protocols?
