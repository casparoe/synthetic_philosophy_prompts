I serve on my university's Senate Subcommittee on Campus Cohesion and Institutional Design, and we must deliver a binding recommendation by the end of term on a petition that has split us along philosophical lines I want addressed directly in the memo rather than glossed over. A student collective — the Civic Integration Circle — has applied for formal university recognition and a dedicated meeting space. Their distinctive claim is assimilationist in the classical French republican tradition: they argue that group-differentiated rights and identity-based exemptions, the standard Anglo-American multiculturalist model, fragment civic solidarity, and they therefore request recognition precisely on the condition that they be granted no special exemptions whatsoever — including exemption from the university's mandatory common-values orientation, which they regard as a legitimate expression of universal laïcité rather than as an imposition. The opposing student union contends that denying them recognition unless they accept a multiculturalist framing is itself a form of coercive assimilation that violates institutional neutrality.

We need a concrete decision — approve with conditions, reject, or approve a modified status — with a clear time frame and actionable steps, plus an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's committee meeting, written in British English. The memo should treat the tension between French laïcité and Anglo-American multiculturalism as an institutional design problem rather than a culture-war talking point: specifically, it should analyse whether a university can coherently accommodate a group whose defining philosophical commitment is the refusal of group-differentiated accommodation. It should reason from institutional logic and first principles, avoid bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests, and propose a specific, time-bound resolution — for example, provisional recognition for one academic year subject to defined review criteria. I want the reasoning to display epistemic humility, including a genuine steel-man of the view that should not prevail, and to avoid treating either laïcité or multiculturalism as self-evidently correct.
