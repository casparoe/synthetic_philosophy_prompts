In drafting a feature on the architecture of cognition, I have constructed an argument about Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—that now seems to prove far more than I intended, perhaps dissolving the very layer it introduces. The chain runs as follows:

(1) A Gregorian creature is defined by its dependence on culturally inherited cognitive tools—language, logic, numerical notation—that the individual does not invent but receives from a tradition.
(2) Because these tools are public and normative, the conditions for their correct deployment are fixed by communal standards rather than by the individual user's private mental states.
(3) Consequently, when a Gregorian creature thinks with these tools, the representational content is determined externally; the creature's own internal representations serve at best a causal or implementational function, not a justificatory one.
(4) The defining mark of a Popperian creature, however, is precisely the capacity for private, fallible internal representation—hypotheses that can be false and tested without direct environmental feedback or social correction.
(5) So if Gregorian cognition operates through externally validated tools, the Popperian layer is not merely extended but functionally superseded: private evaluation of culturally mediated thoughts becomes redundant.
(6) From this it follows that a fully Gregorian creature lacks genuinely private false beliefs about any domain governed by its cultural tools—including semantics, arithmetic, and inference.
(7) Yet human beings, the paradigm Gregorian creatures, plainly hold false private beliefs about language, mathematics, and logic.
(8) Therefore, either human beings are not Gregorian, or the taxonomy is not cumulative but incoherent.

I am inclined to preserve both (4) and (7). Which premise or transition is the weak link—likely the move from (2) to (3) or from (3) to (5)—and what does repairing it cost? Specifically, if Gregorian tools do not eliminate private evaluation but merely scaffold it, does that preserve Dennett's claim that culture amplifies rather than replaces Popperian thought? Or must one concede that Gregorian dependence introduces a form of epistemic heteronomy that undermines the autonomy required for genuine Popperian error?
