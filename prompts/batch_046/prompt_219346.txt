Give an example of a conceptual issue in the study of archives and power: a situation in which the absence of records about a specific community could be interpreted either as an exercise of suppressive power (the active refusal or failure to document) or as a structural feature of non-textual, oral, or embodied knowledge practices that never produced archival traces in the first place. The example should hinge on a conceptual ambiguity about what it means for something to be 'recorded' — not on an empirical lack of access or a missing bibliography. If the premise that these interpretations are genuinely in tension is confused, or if the distinction between epistemic silence and ontological absence collapses upon inspection, say so plainly rather than constructing the case.
