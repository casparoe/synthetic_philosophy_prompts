After a long and somewhat circular argument with a friend about Kelly betting and geometric rationality, I want to check whether we were both missing the same point. My friend argued that since the Kelly criterion maximizes geometric mean growth, a geometrically rational agent must treat her belief system as a portfolio in which each credence is weighted by its contribution to the expected log-utility of her total epistemic position. She then claimed that this implies a uniqueness result: if two agents share identical evidence but assign different credences to the same proposition, at least one must be violating geometric rationality, because a geometrically optimal portfolio cannot have two distinct allocations with the same growth rate. I countered that Kelly applies to gambles with defined payoffs, not to belief states, but she insisted that geometric rationality is a general coherence constraint that extends beyond betting. Is her derivation valid, or does it rest on a false presupposition—perhaps the assumption that belief has a quantifiable "wealth" analog and that geometric mean maximization can serve as an epistemic norm rather than a strategic one? More precisely, does geometric rationality actually entail that rational disagreement is impossible under shared evidence, or is that conclusion an artifact of applying portfolio theory to a domain where the optimization target is not well-defined?
