You advise the administrator of a municipal nested participatory-budgeting office. Every month she receives district-level proposals with cross-boundary effects—parks that alter regional flood dynamics, transit hubs used by non-resident commuters, infrastructure whose rules bind people outside the tax base. She needs a reusable decision procedure to determine, for each proposal, who belongs to the demos (the entitled decision-makers) and who does not. Ground the procedure in democratic boundary theory—principles such as all affected interests, all subjected to coercive rules, and stakeholder inclusion—but translate it into ordered questions a non-philosopher can run in under thirty minutes. Specify clear stop points that return exclusion, clear hand-off points where the question is one of values rather than fact, and explicit notes on where the procedure will misfire. The procedure must handle three hard cases: residents in adjacent districts affected by spillovers but not taxed; daily commuters subjected to the rules but living elsewhere; and a youth-council proxy claiming representation for long-term impacts. Apply it to two proposals: (A) a waterfront climate-resilience park that changes regional flood risk and is used by commuters; and (B) a waste-to-energy facility near a school boundary that subjects nearby residents to emissions rules but serves a city-wide energy mandate. Ensure one application yields a verdict the administrator will likely resist. Conclude by stating what the procedure cannot settle. After the procedural text, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: procedure_name, decision_order, stop_points, judgment_hand_offs, case_A_membership, case_B_membership, disliked_verdict_case, known_misfires.
