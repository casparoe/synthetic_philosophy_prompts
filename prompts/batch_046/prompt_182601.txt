I've been working through Galen Strawson's Basic Argument on my own: the idea that true moral responsibility requires being causa sui, but since our characters are formed by heredity and environment over which we had no control, we can never be ultimately responsible for our actions, and this regress never bottoms out. When I discuss it with people who take a compatibilist line, we seem to agree on the logic of the regress and on the broad scientific picture of character formation, yet they still insist that we can be genuinely responsible in a meaningful, non-ultimate sense, often appealing to the social role of blame and praise rather than to metaphysical sourcehood. After going back and forth for months, I can't tell whether our stall is substantive or structural. Are we secretly disagreeing about whether moral responsibility necessarily requires this kind of ultimate sourcehood, or are we answering different questions—one about metaphysical causation and one about the conditions under which reactive attitudes like resentment are appropriate? Could there be a hidden empirical question about what people actually care about when they blame someone, or is the disagreement just bedrock—two incompatible intuitions about whether blame needs a "clean" causal origin? And if it's bedrock, is there any conceptual distinction or reframing that could move either side, or is the impasse a sign that the original question is ill-posed?
