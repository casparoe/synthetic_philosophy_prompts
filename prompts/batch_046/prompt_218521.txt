I have recently found myself in the middle of a genuinely personal decision about whether to pursue preimplantation genetic screening, and in trying to think clearly about it I keep returning to the procreation asymmetry—the apparent moral difference between having no obligation to bring a happy life into existence and having a clear obligation not to bring a suffering one into existence. The more I sit with it, however, the more I suspect the asymmetry quietly assumes that the potential child in the happy world and the potential child in the suffering world are the very same individual, an assumption that looks shaky once origin essentialism is taken seriously: if a person could not have originated from different gametes, then altering the conditions of conception does not merely change the welfare of one future person but changes which future person, if any, comes to exist. I would like a villanelle—exactly nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains in the standard ABA rhyme scheme and only two rhymes throughout—to make this philosophical knot do structural work. The first refrain should carry the voice of the asymmetry's negative demand, something like an imperative not to cause suffering, and the second should carry the voice of the absence of any parallel imperative to cause happiness; they should alternate through the tercets, and in the final quatrain they should appear together, side by side, so that the form itself enacts the collision I am describing. In the middle stanzas I want the poem to press the origin-essentialist worry—not by naming a doctrine but by inhabiting the modal intuition—that comparing the happiness of one possible child with the suffering of another possible child may be comparing across identities rather than within a single life. The poem should not resolve into advocacy for testing or against it, and it should not treat nature as a moral idyll or as a circle of life to be shrugged away; it should leave the tension open and structurally unsettled, exactly where my own thinking has stalled.
