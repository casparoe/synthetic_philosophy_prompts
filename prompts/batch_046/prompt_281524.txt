Please describe the most important implications of interpreting Gongsun Long's White Horse argument not as a sophistical confusion about color and class membership, but as an early expression of predicate-dependent ontology: the view that kind-classification is fixed not only by intrinsic essences but by which properties a given explanatory framework treats as essential. In particular, discuss what this reframing implies for contemporary debates about natural kinds and conceptual engineering—does it rehabilitate the School of Names against the standard charge of mere wordplay, and how should it affect our assessment of whether scientific taxonomy should aim at unique correct descriptions or at purpose-relative pluralism?
