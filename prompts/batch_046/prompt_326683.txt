I've come round to thinking the Darwin-Turing parallel isn't just a neat analogy -- it's a genuine methodological rule. Darwin shows design without a designer, Turing shows computation without intuition, and together they should set our default: whenever we see complex successful behavior, assume it's competence without comprehension untill forced otherwise. The burden's always on anyone who wants to smuggle understanding back in. So what would actually count against this? Not minor edge cases, but serious defeaters: are there competences or adaptive processes that genuinely resist a mechanistic, comprehension-free explanation? Any formal results showing that comprehension is unavoidable for certain kinds of performance? And has this view already been qualified into unfalsifiability, where every counterexample just gets relabeled as hidden mechanism? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say what defenders of the view would themselves concede, and give me an honest assessment of whether the position is well-insulated or whether that's actually a problem for it.
