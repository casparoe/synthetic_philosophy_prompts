ok so i've been trying to figure out if my takes on art and morality actually fit together or if i'm just being inconsistent. here's where i landed after thinking about it for a while, mostly just from my own head:

1. i think you can totally seperate the artist from the art - like knowing the painter was a terrible person shouldn't change how you see the painting
2. but i also think if the art itself is trying to convince you that something awful is good, thats a problem with the art, not just the artist
3. i think a really well-made movie about a villain can still be good art even if the villain's views are disgusting, as long as the movie doesn't make you root for those views
4. but then there's this case i made up: what if an author writes a beautiful poem that secretly encodes instructions for something harmful, and only people who study the meter realize it? then the aesthetic quality and the harmful intent are literally in the same text
5. and i also think that if an artist made something beautiful specifically to launder a bad ideology, that should count against the work, but if they made it for other reasons and it just happens to align with bad stuff, thats different

do these hang together? especially (1) and (5) - if i'm seperating artist from art, why does the artist's specific purpose matter? and is (4) just a weird edge case or does it break the whole framework? which one should i drop if they don't fit?
