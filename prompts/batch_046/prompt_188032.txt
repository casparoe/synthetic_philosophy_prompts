I am designing a handout for a graduate seminar on Nietzsche’s second Untimely Meditation, On the Uses and Disadvantages of History for Life, and I want a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a branching decision tree—of interpretive positions on how monumental, antiquarian, and critical history relate to the flourishing of life. The tree should be built from logical questions rather than from a list of author names, so that a student can locate her own view without recognizing a label in advance.

Begin with this root question: does historical consciousness serve life only when it is actively transformed into present action, or can preservation and critique be life-affirming in their own right even when they do not issue in action?

From there, split on four concrete claims that genuinely divide the interpretive space. First, monumental history requires access to a genuinely great past exemplar; if no such exemplar is available, monumental history must either fabricate or reconstruct one, or else remain inert. Second, antiquarian history necessarily risks ossifying life into conservative reverence for the past, yet it can also supply the continuity required for any future growth. Third, critical history demands an active, therapeutic forgetting of the past, not merely a selective judgment about which parts to retain. Fourth, the three modes form either a stable hierarchical ordering, a cyclical rhythm through which a culture moves, or an irreconcilable tension that must be perpetually managed but never resolved.

In addition, include a cross-cutting branch that asks whether the active and reactive distinction introduced in On the Genealogy of Morals restructures this taxonomy: does it collapse the three modes into two life-affirming versus life-denying orientations, or does it reveal a hidden fourth mode that Nietzsche never names?

Each leaf must carry either the name of an actual interpretive stance—for example, the standard life-serving hierarchy reading, the permanent-agonism reading associated with some post-structuralist interpretations, an archival-equilibrium view, or a genealogical-collapse view—or the label unnamed if that combination of answers is unexplored in the literature. Every leaf should also include one sentence identifying its most awkward commitment, that is, the point at which the interpretation strains most sharply against Nietzsche’s text or against ordinary intuition.

Please flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate, explaining whether the ambiguity is textual, conceptual, or merely a gap in scholarship. Do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations; I will supply references myself. Phrase every branch node as a substantive claim a student could assess based on her own understanding of Nietzsche’s argument, not as a quiz about which scholar holds which view. The taxonomy should be usable for students who know the basic threefold distinction but have not yet worked through the secondary literature in detail.
