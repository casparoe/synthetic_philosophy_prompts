She defended the standard analogy: our folk-psychological vocabulary of beliefs and desires is to mature neuroscience what the theory of phlogiston was to modern chemistry — a predictively useful but ontologically empty framework destined for elimination once a better science arrives. Audit this analogy in detail: lay out the explicit correspondences (the false entity, the phenomena it purports to explain, the successor theory, the kind of predictive success enjoyed despite falsehood), then identify the disanalogies that matter for the eliminativist conclusion — for instance, whether folk psychology suffers from the same quantitative predictive failures that phlogiston did, whether there is any equivalent of decisive experimental refutation, and whether beliefs and desires serve explanatory or normative roles that have no parallel in combustion theory. Say what the analogy quietly imports about the reducibility of intentional content, and judge whether the conclusion — that belief-desire talk will be eliminated rather than reduced or retained — survives once those breaks are visible.
