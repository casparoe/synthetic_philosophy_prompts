I work as a registrar for a small contemporary art museum, and I'm trying to formalize something I've noticed in discussions about originals: the value seems to come not just from appearance but from the object's direct causal link to the artist's act of finishing it. I need a reusable decision procedure my team can apply when we receive a loan or donation and must classify the object as an original, an authorized reproduction, or a forgery to be rejected. The procedure should run as a sequence of questions about causal origin and artist authorization, with explicit stopping rules — where it returns reject, accept as original, or escalate to human judgment — and it must include worked applications to three cases: a painting restored with 80% new pigment under the artist's direct supervision; a sculpture produced from a digital file the artist released but never physically touched; and a piece left unfinished at the artist's death and completed by an assistant using written notes. The procedure should explicitly mark the points where causal history and artistic authorization conflict and where no rule can settle the case. Keep it practical — no bibliographies or citations — and flag the cases where the answer will likely go against common intuition.
