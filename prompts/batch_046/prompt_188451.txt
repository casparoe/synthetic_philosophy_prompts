I was recently appointed chair of my university's Institutional Ethics and Recognition Board, and I need the board's formal recommendation on a nomination that hinges on a philosophical distinction. During a prolonged winter infrastructure failure—when university contingency protocols collapsed and left several residence halls without heat or safe water for nearly two weeks—a mid-level procurement coordinator, whose contractual obligations cover only vendor scheduling and supply tracking, personally organized emergency shelter, negotiated private contracts for potable water delivery, and maintained continuous communication with affected residents for eleven days without being directed to do so and without accepting the emergency hazard compensation later authorized. The staff union and a group of faculty have nominated her for the university's highest service commendation, describing her conduct as clearly supererogatory: morally exemplary precisely because it exceeded every professional, contractual, and baseline moral duty she held. A dissenting board member argues the opposite: that because the crisis was caused by institutional negligence, her actions were not genuinely optional but were required by a broader duty to remedy harm that the university itself had failed to prevent; framing them as beyond the call therefore mischaracterizes necessary corrective labor as voluntary virtue, risks legitimizing the administration's failure to maintain adequate protocols, and could establish a precedent that rewards individuals for filling systemic gaps rather than holding systems accountable. The board must decide whether to recommend the commendation with an explicit supererogatory framing, to recommend it while explicitly rejecting that framing, or to deny it on conceptual and institutional grounds. Draft the decision memo: give a clear, actionable recommendation; provide reasoning that the nominating parties and the dissenting member could each respond to; and treat the strongest argument on the losing side with full fairness.
