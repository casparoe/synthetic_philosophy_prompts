Write an essay on whether evolutionary divergence should modulate the moral weight assigned to insects within the scale argument. The scale argument observes that insects outnumber sentient vertebrates by orders of magnitude, so even a low per-capita probability of consciousness could yield enormous aggregate moral significance. Against this, consider the claim that the absence in insects of integrated nociceptive architectures homologous to mammalian pain matrices should not merely reduce confidence in sentience but should apply a non-linear discount to moral consideration—treating phylogenetic distance as a continuous scalar rather than a binary exclusion criterion. Evaluate whether such evolutionary discounting is conceptually coherent: does it avoid arbitrary speciesism, or does it smuggle in an unjustified preference for neural similarity? Weigh the case for linear aggregation of probabilistic sentience against the case for a phylogenetically weighted discount function, and defend a clear verdict on how moral agents ought to calibrate obligations toward insect populations.
