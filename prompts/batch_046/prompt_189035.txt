Could you write me an essay prompt for a 90-minute exam on Benatar's asymmetry? I don't want students to just list the four premises and say "therefore having kids is bad" — that's too easy to memorize from a textbook summary. Instead, the question should present a specific constructed scenario: a couple can have exactly one child, and that child's life will include serious unavoidable suffering (like a chronic painful condition that can't be cured) but also substantial wellbeing that depends entirely on contingent choices they haven't made yet (career, friendships, etc.). The child doesn't exist yet. Ask whether Benatar's asymmetry gives them a decisive reason not to procreate, and specifically whether it matters that the suffering is unavoidable while the goods are contingent and avoidable.

Then make them tackle the objection that Benatar's view seems to justify harming or killing people who are already alive and suffering, since for them too non-existence would avoid harm. They need to explain whether this objection actually hits Benatar's position, and how he might distinguish the ethics of bringing someone into existence from the ethics of allowing an existant person to continue living. Please include the exam question itself written clearly in one or two paragraphs, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme with exactly five must-hit points totalling 20 marks — each point worth 4 marks with a one-line note on how candidates typically mess it up. No bibliography or citation list needed, since they won't have books in the exam room anyway, and please don't hinge it on some obscure 19th-century figure I haven't heard of; Benatar and the standard objections are plenty if we dig into the logic properly.
