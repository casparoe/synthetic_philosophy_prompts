Ok so I was trying to push the Ship of Theseus thing past its usual stopping point and now I think I've accidentally proved that personal identity is impossible, which can't be right. (1) If Theseus's ship is still the same ship after every plank gets replaced, then artifact-identity can't depend on original material; it must depend on functional continuity and spatiotemporal path instead. (2) Apply the same logic to people: if your neurons get replaced one-by-one with synthetic ones that play the exact same causal roles, you survive, because there's continuous psychological and bodily history. (3) Now imagine two people, A and B, undergo this replacement using parts drawn from the same synthetic pool - like a distributed brain upgrade - so both end up with fully synthetic brains, each tracing a continuous path back to A and B respectivly. (4) By (1) and (2), both are still A and B. (5) But now we physically merge those two synthetic brains into one body. The resulting person has two continuous histories, one leading back to A and one to B. (6) So that merged person is identical to A and also identical to B, which means A = B, but they started as distinct people with different lives. (7) Contradiction. (8) So (1) must be false, and material origin fixes identity after all - which means Theseus with all-new planks isn't Theseus. But that feels wrong too, since continuity clearly matters some. Where's the slip? Is (3) actually impossible, or does (5) violate some hidden uniqueness rule for paths, or am I just misapplying artifact logic to people?
