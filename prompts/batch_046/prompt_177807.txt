I write novels, and for some years I have observed with a kind of professional curiosity the long stalemate over whether philosophy makes progress in any sense worth comparing to science. The dispute has settled into an exhausted equilibrium: defenders cite the refinement of conceptual distinctions—between varieties of mental representation, or between moral luck and genuine control—as genuine advances, comparing them to the way a narrative gains clarity across drafts until a theme becomes unavoidable; skeptics counter that these are merely shifts in vocabulary, that philosophy has resolved none of its ancient questions, and that judging philosophy by scientific standards is a category error, like assessing a poem by whether it predicts the weather. Both sides have grown more sophisticated without moving closer together, and I want to understand why. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not about history or sociology at all, but about what an explanation must accomplish—whether clarifying a concept counts as progress, or whether progress requires predictive or technological payoff that philosophy, like fiction, does not provide. Is that the right diagnosis, or is the real division hidden deeper: perhaps a disagreement about which problems are genuinely philosophical rather than empirical, about whether philosophical questions are well-formed enough to admit of solutions, or about whether understanding and solving are even separable aims? I ask for an honest structural diagnosis—verbal or substantive, factual or normative, bedrock intuition or misframed question—supported by what each side actually claims rather than a survey of positions. Given that diagnosis, what, if anything, could actually move the dispute: a formal result linking conceptual clarification to empirical discovery, a historical case showing philosophical confusion obstructing science, a new distinction that dissolves the analogy to scientific progress, or an argument that no resolution should be expected because the question itself is ill-posed? Please respond in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one might write in a long, careful letter.
