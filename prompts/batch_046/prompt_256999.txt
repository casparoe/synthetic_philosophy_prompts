I'm writing a column on where ideas come from, and I've noticed a pattern that doesn't quite fit the usual 'remix' story. You take a well-known theme—say, the structure of a classical sonata or a particular ethical dilemma—and you hold one feature absolutely constant, almost hidden, while you crank every other paramter until the result feels alien. The fixed feature isn't the obvious one; it's like leaving the harmonic skeleton intact but burying it under noise and irregular rhythm so listeners don't recognise it. In arguments, it's keeping the logical form of an old paradox but swapping in completely different content so the familiar shape sneaks up on you. I know Hofstadter talks about creativity as twiddling knobs on concepts, but this feels different: it's not tweaking all the knobs a bit, it's locking one knob in place and spinning the others wildly, often hiding the locked one on purpose. Does this specific move—this hidden-axis variation—have a name in aesthetics or philosophy of mind? Is it just Hofstadter's model with an asymmetry added, or is there a separate literture? I want a committed take: is the locked-knob idea actually distinct, and if so, what's the sharpest boundary between it and ordinary pastiche or homage?
