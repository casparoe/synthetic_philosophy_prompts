Please argue that Grice's binary distinction between what is said and what is implicated should be expanded to include a third layer of speaker meaning—pragmatic framing—that is activated when a speaker employs literally true but selectively structured descriptions to establish a shared evaluative orientation rather than to convey a discrete proposition. Concentrate on indirect evaluative utterances, such as presenting technically accurate statistics about a policy within a deliberately narrow contextual frame, and defend the view that this framing layer generates its own form of communicative responsibility: the speaker is accountable not for asserting falsehoods or for generating conventional implicatures, but for constructing the normative lens through which the audience is invited to process subsequent information. In particular, show why this layer cannot be reduced to either semantic content or Gricean implicature, and explain what conditions must hold for a speaker to be held responsible for framing effects that they never explicitly endorsed.
