I've been reconsidering the standard interpretation of the Knobe asymmetry—the claim that moral evaluation contaminates an otherwise conceptually pure attribution of intentionality. To probe whether the driver is genuinely moral, I constructed a hypothetical vignette in which an agent's side effect violates a purely structural, non-moral norm—disrupting the symmetry of an architectural layout—without any welfare implications. If subjects still withhold the intentionality label in the neutral framing but apply it in the violation framing, wouldn't that show the effect is mediated by a broader norm-violation detector rather than by morality specifically? And if intentionality judgments are systematically anchored to such a detector across domains, doesn't that actually vindicate folk psychology—revealing a unified mechanism linking norm salience to action attribution—rather than undermining the mental-state account?
