After a long argument with a friend about whether comparative judgments must be transitive, I want to isolate the precise error in a spectrum-style objection we've both been treating as serious. The objection claims that if "A is better than B" holds across a chain of imperceptibly small differences, then transitivity forces us to rank a clearly inferior endpoint above a clearly superior one—an absurdity that proves the relation is intransitive. I think this mistakes the vagueness of the comparative predicate for a failure of the underlying ordering, and it also collapses what is really a multi-dimensional dominance relation—where the weighting vector across dimensions shifts with context—into a single scalar ranking. Is the deeper problem therefore that spectrum arguments apply differently to ordinal relations than to categorical predicates like "bald," or is the real issue that maintaining transitivity requires fixing the dimensional weights across the chain? And what does the correction imply for modeling rational choice when comparisons are vague but not necessarily intransitive?
