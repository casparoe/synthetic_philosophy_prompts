Here is my opening move. In my city, elder care is being reorganized through a proposed “distributed filiality” platform: adult children are algorithmically matched with aging parents they do not know, assigned a temporary “surrogate child” role for six months, and evaluated on ritual-performance metrics—visit frequency, gift appropriateness, emotional-attunement scores. The designers claim this scales xiao beyond the biological family. I contend that this collapses the distinction between performing a role and being constituted by it: if the role can be assigned, monitored, and dissolved by an algorithm, then the obligations it generates are contractual, not moral in your sense. Is there a Confucian account of “empty roles”—roles that possess the formal structure of filial obligation but lack the constitutive history, mutual recognition, and irreversible commitment that make the role genuinely binding? And if so, does your view condemn this system outright, or can it accommodate some form of institutional translation of family roles without losing the moral difference between xiao and managed care?
