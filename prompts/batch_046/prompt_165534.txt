For a philosophy-of-mind and comparative-cognition context, write the full specification of desiderata that an adequate theory of consciousness distribution across animal taxa must satisfy. The theory should determine which non-human animals are phenomenally conscious, in what modalities, and to what degree, without collapsing distinctions that carry ethical or scientific weight.

Structure your answer as a requirements list. For each desideratum, state the concrete case, empirical result, or conceptual distinction that motivates it; mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp (gradualist, discontinuist, architectural-pluralist, sentience-first, etc.); and indicate which popular candidates it threatens.

The list must cover at least the following dimensions:

- Biological coverage: the theory must handle cephalopods with distributed, non-centralized nervous systems; insects with minimal integration centers; birds with pallial architectures lacking layered cortex; cnidarians with diffuse nerve nets; and colonial or symbiotic organisms where the boundary between individual and collective consciousness is ambiguous.
- Distinctions that must be preserved: between being conscious at all and possessing a specific phenomenal kind; between phenomenal consciousness and access consciousness; between sentience and more demanding capacities; and between first-person qualitative character and functional integration.
- Empirical constraints: consistency with comparative neuroanatomy showing convergent but non-homologous architectures (e.g., absence of thalamocortical loops in avian brains, local recurrent processing in invertebrates); with behavioral flexibility data in decapod crustaceans; and with the absence of a global neuronal workspace in many taxa.
- Ethical neutrality: the theory must not prejudge whether consciousness is binary or graded, whether marginal analogues (embryos, split-brain configurations, hive-level entities) extend across species, or whether phylogenetic continuity implies moral continuity.

Then identify the single desideratum that does the most work in excluding mainstream theories—particularly those that make mammalian cortical architecture necessary, or those that dissolve the distribution question into panpsychist-adjacent continuity. Note explicitly whether any subset of your requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, with special attention to whether the demand for both sharp ethical boundaries and phylogenetic gradualism can be met simultaneously.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their justifications rather than on scoring named theories, though a brief mapping of where gradualist, discontinuist, and pluralist architectural views fall against the list is welcome. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not ask for them.
