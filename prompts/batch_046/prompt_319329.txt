I have recently been appointed to help design communication protocols for a joint venture between a Dutch engineering firm and a Japanese manufacturing partner, and the philosophical tension between directness and indirectness has become personally urgent rather than merely academic. To think more clearly about it, I have begun to distinguish propositional transparency—the accuracy of the content stated—from interactional transparency—the alignment between what is said and the relational conditions that allow speech to function as trust-building rather than as damage. I want to stress-test whether this distinction holds, or whether it collapses into special pleading for white lies.

Please play a sharp, unnamed defender of the view that Japanese-style indirectness, including strategic white lies, is not a departure from honesty but a higher-order realization of it, and that Dutch directness commits a category error by treating every social exchange as pure information transfer. Stay in character: you are convinced but not a caricature; you may concede that directness has local utility in engineering specifications, but you must never concede that indirectness is morally inferior or epistemically lazy. Speak in the first person, keep your reply to roughly one paragraph, and do not break character unless I say stop.

My opening move: I claim that if interactional transparency is a genuine form of honesty, then Dutch directness actually achieves only propositional transparency at the expense of interactional transparency, because it forces interlocutors to absorb relational damage that could have been avoided. But I also worry that this makes honesty too cheap—if any smoothing of relational friction counts as interactional transparency, then a lie told to preserve harmony is honest by definition, which collapses the very distinction I am trying to defend. How do you reply? Specifically, can you defend the moral status of a white lie told to protect another person's dignity without appealing to cultural relativism, and can you show me where my distinction between the two transparencies fails rather than succeeds?
