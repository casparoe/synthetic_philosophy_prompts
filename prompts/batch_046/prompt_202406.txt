I am drafting an essay for my advanced philosophy seminar on the relationship between individual Ulysses contracts and collective constitutional precommitment. My outline begins with the familiar contrast: Odysseus has himself bound to the mast so that he can hear the Sirens without being destroyed by them, which philosophers typically treat as rational self-binding against a predicted failure of will; constitutional precommitment, by contrast, involves entrenched rules—such as rigid amendment procedures or independent monetary authorities—that constrain future democratic majorities in order to protect long-term values or institutional credibility. I then propose an extension that our course materials have not addressed: treating a legislature’s decision to lock its distributive policies inside an automated system with fixed, non-overrideable parameters as a collective Ulysses contract. In this case, the binding is not directed at the collective’s own weakness of will, but rather at external strategic actors who might manipulate future legislators; I argue that this shifts the normative justification from autonomy-preservation to credibility-enhancement. However, I am concerned that the analogy may break down, since the “mast” is now an artificial agent that is not part of the collective will in the same direct way.

Could you comment on the coherence of this framing? Specifically, I would welcome feedback on three points. First, am I mischaracterizing constitutional precommitment by treating it mainly as management of weakness of will, rather than as commitment to enduring principles? Second, does introducing an automated decision-agent genuinely create a new philosophical category, or does it collapse into a standard principal-agent problem with extra technological vocabulary? Third, should my essay distinguish more rigorously between precommitments that remain revocable in principle—however costly—and those that are structurally irrevocable once enacted? I am not asking for a bibliography, citation list, or source recommendations; I am working without library access and need only conceptual criticism and suggestions for sharpening the argument’s structure.
