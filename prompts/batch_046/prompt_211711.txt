For my philosophy seminar's position-paper assignment, I've tried to articulate where I stand on supertasks and Thomson's lamp, but I'm unsure whether my views form a consistent package. Here is my profile:

(1) I take Thomson's lamp argument seriously: if a lamp is toggled infinitely often in a finite interval and ends in a definite state, we derive a contradiction about whether it is on or off.
(2) Nevertheless, I do not think this contradiction shows that supertasks are conceptually impossible; I think it only shows that discrete-state descriptions break down at the limit of an infinite process.
(3) I hold that supertasks are physically impossible—owing to energy divergence and information-density limits—but I treat this as an empirical constraint, not a logical one.
(4) To avoid the contradiction, I propose modeling the lamp's state continuously (for instance, as a real-valued luminosity function) so that the limit can be undefined without being paradoxical.
(5) I am a physicalist about properties: every physical feature of an object is either determinately instantiated or determinately absent, with no intermediate metaphysical status.
(6) I also maintain bivalence for all well-formed physical descriptions: for any precise physical claim, either it is true or its negation is true, even when we talk about limit conditions.

My questions: Do these commitments hang together? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to conflict? For each real tension, how severe is it, and what is the cheapest revision that would resolve it? Is there a single change that would clean up the most problems without forcing me to abandon physicalism or bivalence? I am not sure which of (2), (4), or (5) is the weak link, so please do not assume I am ready to drop any one of them—just trace what each commits me to.
