Write an essay on the following controversy. Consider a public institution—call it an Archive of Unclaimed Grievances—that collects and preserves first-person testimonies of harm arising from diffuse structural conditions for which no identifiable perpetrator exists and no legal or reparative remedy is available. Drawing on Avishai Margalit's view that shared remembrance of wrongs is a form of ethical solidarity with victims, and on David Rieff's argument that collective forgetting can be a necessary condition for communal renewal and liberation from paralyzing pasts, assess whether this archive has a stronger moral duty to preserve these memories indefinitely or a stronger duty to allow them to fade over time. Your essay should weigh whether remembrance that serves no restorative or retributive purpose risks becoming mere moral spectacle, and should reach and justify a specific conclusion about how the institution ought to govern its holdings. Begin with the essay itself: state your thesis in the opening sentence, then present your reasoning continuously, with no preface, no restatement of the prompt, and no commentary outside the essay.
