Im giving the opening remarks tomorrow at the kickoff for our neighborhood mural project, and I need help writing it. The occassion is weird: local residents, high school painters, a city council member, the corporate sponser's rep in the front row, and probably a couple grad students who actually read Du Bois. I want Du Bois's old argument — that all art is propaganda, that pure beauty is just propaganda for whoever's already in charge — to actually shape what I say, not just be decoration. I can't open with the usual art brings everyone together line, because by his lights thats itself a political move that hides power; but if I come out swinging saying we're all propagandists now, half the room will check out.

Write it in these sections, with rough lengths:
- The room (about 100 words): name the mix of people and establish why the usual comforting opener won't work here.
- The Du Bois constraint (about 250 words): lay out the idea — every brushstroke takes a side, neutrality is impossible — and show how that forces me to be honest about what this mural is actually for, without turning it into a lecture.
- The plaque problem (about 250 words): there's a brass plaque with the funder's logo going beside the wall. Some residents say the mural should stay apolitical; others say taking the money makes us mouthpieces for capital. Work through whether refusing the cash is also propaganda, whether unity is just another message with its own winners and losers, and land somewhere the student artists can paint from without feeling like sellouts.
- The closing (about 100 words): don't wrap it up with a tidy moral. Give the room permission to start working while knowing exactly what game their in.

Keep it casual — I'm talking to a friend who writes speeches, not a journal. A typo or two is totally fine.
