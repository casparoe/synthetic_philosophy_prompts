Since a recent restructuring in my own sector has left me unable to interpret any personal setback—romantic, professional, or domestic—without asking whether it is genuinely my own or merely a superstructural reflex of underlying relations of production, I have become personally preoccupied with the question of whether historical materialism can function as an exhaustive explanatory key for lived experience. Write a parody of a syndicated advice column, approximately 650 words, in the deadpan, compassionate-but-firm voice of a long-running print columnist. The column should be titled in the manner of classic agony-aunt features and should respond to three short reader letters: one about a painful romantic breakup, one about a mid-career change, and one about a bitter dispute with a neighbor over property lines. In every response, the columnist must apply the conceptual machinery of historical materialism rigorously—explicitly identifying the relevant mode of production, tracing the mediation through relations of production, diagnosing false consciousness or ideological interpellation where appropriate, and refusing to grant any irreducible autonomy to the psychological or cultural superstructure. The satire should push the reductionist logic to its limit: the columnist should treat the breakup as an effect of changing labor-market conditions on reproductive relations, the career anxiety as a direct expression of shifts in the organic composition of capital, and the property dispute as an instance of hegemonic legal ideology masking real economic antagonism. Crucially, include a passage in which the columnist addresses the standard objection that the superstructure possesses "relative autonomy," only to dismiss that very objection as itself an ideologically overdetermined reflex of liberal individualism. By the final letter, the reader should recognize that the columnist's own advice—her tone, her prescriptions, even her sympathy—is itself determined by her class position within the intellectual division of labor, though she remains entirely unable to see this. The target is not any named individual but the practice of deploying base-and-superstructure analysis as a universal hermeneutic for everyday personal dilemmas; the humor must arise from using the actual Marxist conceptual apparatus correctly—mode of production, relations of production, ideological state apparatuses, hegemony—while applying it with absurd literalness. Maintain sustained deadpan irony throughout: never wink, never break character, and never state that the view is absurd. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific editions of texts.
