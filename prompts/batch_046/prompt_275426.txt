- A healthy heart pumping blood: yes, that's clearly its funciton.
- The lub-dub sound it makes: not a funciton, just noise.
- A heart with a bad valve that barely moves blood: I still say pumping is its funciton, it's just broken. So malfunction only makes sense if there's a proper job assigned.
- A penguin wing used for swimming: I want to say swimming IS its funciton now, even though evolution built it for flight. The old job is gone, replaced.
- But a pancreatic tumor that secretes insulin and actually lowers blood sugar — useful! — I want to say that's NOT its funciton, it's just cancer being weird. Even though it's currently doing a job just like the wing.
- A synthetic heart made of plastic and metal, designed in a lab, implanted and pumping: yes, pumping is its funciton, even though natural selection never touched it.

So evolutionary history seems to matter for the broken heart (case 3), but I ignore it for the penguin (case 4). I reject pure current-role for the tumor (case 5) but accept it for the penguin. And design or intent saves case 6. Is there one principle that holds all six together, or am I being inconsistent?
