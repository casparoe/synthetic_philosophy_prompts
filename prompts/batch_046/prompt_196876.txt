For our seminar on formal value-agnostic constraints, I want to examine whether “side-effect avoidance” can be operationalized without resolving the full value-specification problem that low-impact AI is meant to circumvent.

Consider a system designed to operate within a narrowly defined task envelope—say, rearranging objects in a bounded workspace—while its designers deliberately withhold any global ordering over states of the world outside that envelope. The challenge is to define a computable impact measure that distinguishes intended causal paths (those internal to the task) from unintended perturbations, without appealing to the designers’ complete utility function or to a universally valid harm metric.

Write the desiderata that any adequate formalization of side-effect avoidance must meet if it is to serve in aligning systems built from current methods without new insight. For each requirement, provide the conceptual tension or motivating case behind it—for example: whether the measure must be invariant under redescription of the agent’s intentions; whether it can be computed from local observables rather than requiring a global world-model; whether it must block interventions that are harmful across all reasonable value frames, or only those that violate a minimal, ordinally specified boundary; and whether it can distinguish between causing an effect and merely failing to prevent it.

Flag any pairs of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable, particularly combinations that demand both strict value-neutrality and the capacity to veto genuinely destructive actions. Finally, indicate which requirements are non-negotiable if the low-impact program is to remain conceptually coherent, and which are methodological preferences associated with particular formal approaches—such as reachability-based divergence measures, counterfactual dependence metrics, or state-space boundary conditions.
