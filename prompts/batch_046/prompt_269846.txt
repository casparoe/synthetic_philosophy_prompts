For my advanced philosophy of science seminar, I am constructing a conceptual diagnostic that classifies respondents not by the theoretical labels they prefer but by the actual ontological commitments they hold regarding pattern-realism across explanatory domains. I am particularly interested in applying Dennett’s framework of the physical, design, and intentional stances—not merely as predictive heuristics for human behavior, but as competing ontologies for the status of real patterns in complex systems—while avoiding the usual survey format that asks whether one “believes in emergence” or “accepts reductionism.”

Please design a decision-tree taxonomy (with explicit branch questions and terminal leaves) that sorts positions on the reality of patterns using concrete, non-label-dependent cases. The root node should present a specific, cross-domain regularity that is philosophically under-examined: the phenomenon of critical slowing down preceding regime shifts in complex adaptive systems, observable across ecological, economic, and neural contexts despite lacking any uniform microphysical implementation. From this root, the tree should branch through a sequence of precisely formulated commitments—each answerable without knowing the names of the positions—concerning whether such patterns are reducible without remainder to physical-stance descriptions, genuinely explanatory only when embedded in a design-stance functional specification, or dependent for their very existence on an intentional-stance interpreter adopting them.

The taxonomy must include at least eight terminal positions, among them strict physicalist eliminativism about patterns; robust pattern realism that treats design-level regularities as ontologically serious but stance-independent; instrumentalist design-stance realism; full intentional-stance anti-realism; and at least two hybrid views that split commitments across levels (for instance, accepting real patterns at the design level while denying them at the intentional level, or the reverse combination). For each branching question, state the exact diagnostic claim that separates siblings; for each logically possible but unoccupied combination of answers, provide a brief diagnosis of whether the gap reflects incoherence, conceptual instability, or merely an unexplored theoretical space. The instrument should assume familiarity with the general idea of the three stances and the debate over real patterns, but it must not require recognition of niche named sub-theories, and it should contain no requests for bibliographies, citation lists, or further reading. Deliver the tree in structured form with its scoring logic clearly articulated.
