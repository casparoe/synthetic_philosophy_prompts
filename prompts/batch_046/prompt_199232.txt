I am advising a working group of clinical ethicists and nosologists who must review proposed amendments to psychiatric manuals on a recurring basis, and I want them to have a reusable decision procedure—something they can run in a ninety-minute session—grounded in the looping-effect thesis associated with Ian Hacking, but translated into steps that do not require philosophical training. The core idea is that introducing or revising a diagnostic category does not merely describe a pre-existing population; it can alter how individuals understand their own experiences, how institutions respond to them, and consequently how the symptoms themselves are expressed, thereby feeding back into the prevalence and boundaries of the category itself. I need you to operationalize this into an ordered checklist with explicit stopping rules: for each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to continue, what answer triggers an immediate rejection of the proposal, and where the question is irreducibly one of values rather than empirical projection so that the procedure must halt and hand the decision to the committee chair. The procedure should be designed for proposals that are not obviously harmful—say, a new dimensional rating for “interpersonal prediction-error sensitivity” in social contexts, or a refined subtype of withdrawal—rather than for blatantly pseudoscientific categories. Include three worked applications: first, a proposed category for “chronic adaptive disconnection in high-demand cognitive environments”; second, a proposal to split an existing mood category by adding a “context-sensitive grief prolongation” specifier; and third, one case where the procedure returns a verdict that the working group will likely find unwelcome—perhaps recommending acceptance of a category that carries significant stigma—while explaining precisely why the loop dynamics still permit it. Throughout, flag the points where the procedure necessarily misfires or depends on unquantifiable judgment, and conclude by stating clearly what this framework cannot determine, especially regarding the moral legitimacy of pathologizing any particular form of human variation. Write this as a formal protocol with the role, frequency, and stakes fixed: the group meets monthly, has access to epidemiological consultants but not to philosophers, and must produce a signed recommendation within the session.
