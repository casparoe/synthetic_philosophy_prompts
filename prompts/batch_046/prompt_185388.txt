I'm in this course on german idealism and we were arguing about Hegels master slave dialectic after class. One of my friends brought up this distinction she read about between status-recognition, where you acknowledge someone as an equal rights-bearing subject, and care-recognition, where you just manage their dependency needs. She said Hegel's master only gives the slave care-recognition—keeping him alive and working—while withholding status-recognition entirely, which means the dialectic isn't really about recognition at all, its just about labor and domination. So my question is: if recognition by definition requires equality, then doesn't the master-slave relation automatically fail to be recognition? And if Hegel knows this, why does he call it a dialectic of recognition rather than a dialectic of misrecognition? I feel like there's a false premise in assuming that recognition is either fully symmetrical or not present at all, but I can't figure out if Hegel actually thinks asymmetrical recognition counts as recognition, or if he's showing that it collapses into something else. Also, if the slave never gets status-recognized by the master, does he eventually get it from other free subjects, or is the whole point that he has to generate self-recognition through labor without ever receiving it intersubjectively?
