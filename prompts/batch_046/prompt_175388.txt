I teach philosophy of religion and am preparing a graduate seminar on divine foreknowledge and human freedom. Rather than rehearse the standard determinism-versus-libertarianism framing, I want to use Steven Spielberg’s film Minority Report as a case study read specifically through Luis de Molina’s doctrine of middle knowledge: the distinction between natural knowledge (necessary truths), middle knowledge (counterfactuals of creaturely freedom), and free knowledge (the actual world), together with the Molinist claim that God knows what every free agent would do in any feasible circumstance without causally determining that choice. Focus on the Precrime system and Anderton’s predicted murder of Leo Crow: do the precogs’ visions function analogously to middle knowledge—knowledge of what Anderton would freely choose in that precise circumstance—or do they present fixed actualities that render choice illusory? Examine the scene in which Anderton ultimately spares Crow: is this best interpreted as Anderton actualizing a different counterfactual of freedom than the one the precogs knew, thereby falsifying the precognitive claim, or as the actualization of the very counterfactual the system failed to register? How should we understand Agatha’s dissenting “minority report” in Molinist terms—as a vision of an alternative feasible world that Anderton could access, or as a failure of middle knowledge itself? Be precise about where the film resists this theological framing: for instance, does the technological determinism embedded in the precog machinery distort Molina’s insistence that middle knowledge is non-causal? Finally, what does the film contribute to the philosophical debate about counterfactuals of freedom that a purely conceptual argument cannot? Which scenes best illustrate the difference between knowledge of what will happen and knowledge of what would happen? I want an honest critical reading that neither reduces the film to an illustration of Molinism nor distorts Molinist semantics to fit the plot.
