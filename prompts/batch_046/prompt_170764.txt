Following a lengthy argument with a friend about Darwin’s actual commitments on morality and religion, I want to test whether a consistent implicit rule can be extracted from his known judgments and what it implies for a boundary case.

The reconstructed pattern of verdicts is as follows. First, on human conscience: Darwin treats it as emerging from social instincts shared with gregarious animals, not as a supernaturally implanted faculty. Second, on cooperative self-sacrifice in hive insects: he permits full explanation through natural selection acting on kin or colony lines, without attributing conscious moral choice to individuals. Third, on religious belief: he regards it as likely arising from evolved tendencies to attribute events to intentional causes, rather than as valid evidence of a divine order. Fourth, on the authority of moral norms: he denies that they require religious sanction, grounding them instead in evolved sympathy and social utility. Fifth, on moral progress: he treats it as contingent on education and environment, not as an inevitable biological ascent.

Consider now this hypothetical case: a solitary deep-sea cephalopod species, lacking sustained social structures beyond mating, is observed to exhibit individually variable behaviors—including manipulation of objects in ways observers describe as play, and targeted interference with conspecifics that disrupt these activities—without any measurable reproductive or kin-selection benefit. Would Darwin classify these behaviors as proto-moral analogues to human conscience, as purely mechanical instincts comparable to hive cooperation, or as phenomena that fall outside evolutionary moral explanation altogether?

Please identify the two or three competing interpretive rules—such as broad evolutionary continuity across all cooperative behavior, a social-instinct threshold requiring sympathy and group life, and a conscious-reflection requirement—that are all consistent with the five reconstructed verdicts. For each rule, state its prediction for the solitary cephalopod. Then offer your best-guess verdict for Darwin himself, with reasoning, and specify what further hypothetical case—perhaps involving an artificial system—would best adjudicate between the rules. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
