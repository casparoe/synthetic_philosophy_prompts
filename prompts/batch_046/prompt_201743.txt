I run weekly audits for a peer-to-peer resource protocol in which pseudonymous agents interact with stochastic exit probabilities and noisy strategy observation. I want to operationalize the evolutionary conditions for cooperation—specifically the shadow of the future, recognition fidelity, and the defection-payoff ordering—into a reusable decision procedure I can apply in under thirty minutes without evolutionary-game-theory training, deciding whether an interaction class should be treated as iterated and whether a costly reputation or punishment layer is justified. Present the result as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: item one should state the ordered tests or flowchart in words that distinguish a true iterated game from a one-shot interaction masked by exit options; item two should specify the recognition and payoff conditions that must hold for direct reciprocity to stabilize cooperation; item three should apply the procedure to a case where cooperation clearly evolves and the layer is warranted; item four should apply it to a case where the procedure demands exclusion or punishment despite my intuition that the defection was noise-induced and therefore excusable; and item five should flag the exact points—especially under imperfect observation and indirect reciprocity—where judgment is irreducible, the procedure misfires, and I must sign off on a call the flowchart cannot settle. Keep the vocabulary practical for an engineer, but do not collapse direct and indirect reciprocity into a single check.
