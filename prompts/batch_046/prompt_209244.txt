I have been working through some formal epistemology readings on coherence and rational belief, and a colleague in my study group offered me a compressed summary of the Dutch book argument that strikes me as mostly accurate but suspicious in its stronger conclusions. According to her account, the argument establishes that any agent whose partial beliefs violate the probability axioms can be presented with a combination of individually acceptable bets—each priced according to the agent's own stated credences—that jointly guarantee a net monetary loss regardless of outcome; from this she concludes that probabilism is not merely a prudential recommendation for avoiding exploitation, but a constitutive condition of rational agency itself. She extends this claim to agents with imprecise or interval-valued credences, arguing that even qualitative comparative judgments—such as holding that event A is strictly more likely than event B without assigning either a precise value—can always be mapped onto a unique precise betting quotient that respects those orderings, so that imprecision offers no shelter from Dutch-bookability unless the agent collapses into a single sharp distribution. She further notes that Ramsey originally devised the argument chiefly to undermine frequentist interpretations of probability, and that de Finetti later reinforced it by demonstrating that nonlinear utility or risk aversion cannot block the result, since any such preference structure can be built directly into the stakes of the bets.

I am trying to determine how fair this summary is. Which elements are standard, accurate expositions of the Dutch book tradition? Which are defensible simplifications that overstate the argument's reach—particularly the move from vulnerability-to-exploitation to constitutive rationality, and the claim that imprecise credences are unproblematically extractable into precise quotients? Are there misrepresentations of the logical structure, or of Ramsey's and de Finetti's actual motivations and targets? And if the summary is wrong about imprecision, what is the correct response: does holding genuinely imprecise beliefs block the construction of a Dutch book, or does it merely require a modified coherence constraint? Please separate the accurate core from the overstatements and errors, and tell me whether the conclusion that non-probabilistic belief is strictly irrational actually follows from the premises she has laid out.
