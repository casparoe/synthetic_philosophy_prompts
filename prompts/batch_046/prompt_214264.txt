I was out with two friends from my enviro phil course last week, and they got into it over this one local project—there's an old industrial lot downtown thats being torn down and turned back into a wild meadow. They were argueing about whether that actually lowers wild animal suffering or makes it worse.

(1) Maya said yes, it's bad from a suffering-focused view: more plants means more insects and small rodents, so you're just boosting biomass and creating a bunch of short, painful lives. She called the paved lot an accidental welfare buffer because the hard surface keeps populations low and limits that kind of suffering.

(2) Leo said that treats wildness like a factory-farm problem and ignores ecological integrity, which he thinks has value that can't be reduced to a headcount of pain. He said some values are incommensurable with suffering, so you can't just sum lives and call it ethics.

(3) Maya replied that if values are truly incommensurable, Leo has no non-arbitrary reason to prefer the meadow. She said incommensurable can't work as a one-way stop-sign that only protects wildness—if suffering and integrity don't share a scale, then suffering doesn't automatically lose, but integrity doesn't automatically win either.

(4) Leo said he doesn't need a shared scale; he uses a regret structure where you imagine fully endorsing both values and check which choice you'd still stand behind after seeing the suffering up close. He said that's not quantifying, it's testing for coherence across values.

(5) Maya said that sounds like smuggling a hidden metric back in—regret is just weighted preference with extra steps. She also pointed out Leo supports demolishing other habitats for affordable housing, so his regret seems to track human convenience rather than wildness.

Can you go through each of the five moves and rate them from clearly mistaken to clearly right with brief justification? Say who's right on each specific point, not just overall, and tell me what both are missing—maybe something about how habitat quality affects lifespan versus population density? Don't just pick a winner; I want point-by-point adjudication.
