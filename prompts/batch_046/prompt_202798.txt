My physics philosophy tutor has ruled consistently on several quantum scenarios. On the double-slit experiment with no which-path information, she said that asking which slit the particle traversed is a category error: the interference pattern is real, but it does not license talk of a particle being in two places at once. On Schrödinger's cat, she said macroscopic superpositions are suppressed by decoherence, so the cat is definitely alive or dead well before the box is opened; however, she added that this suppression is a dynamical process, not a physical collapse event triggered by observation. On the EPR paradox with spin-entangled particles separated by large distances, she rejected both hidden variables and faster-than-light signaling, insisting that the correlation is accounted for entirely by the joint quantum state being complete. On Wigner's friend, she denied that consciousness plays any causal role in producing outcomes, but she also refused to say that the friend splits into multiple copies; she called that a metaphysical add-on, not physics.

Next week we discuss a proposed experiment: a single ion is trapped in a superposition of two spatially separated potential wells inside two perfectly shielded, decoherence-free chambers. The chambers are kept isolated for months, then the ion is recombined and interference is observed. During the isolation period, no measurement occurs and no environment interacts with either branch.

Predict her verdict: will she say that during those months the ion genuinely occupied both wells in parallel branches of reality, that it occupied neither well determinately, or that the question is illegitimate? Identify whether she is running a Copenhagen, Everettian, or Bohmian framework — or a hybrid — and explain which of her earlier rulings most strongly support that identification and which one is the outlier. Then name the competing interpretation that fits her rulings almost as well, say what it predicts for this trapped-ion case, and specify the one further scenario — perhaps involving the quantum potential's role in guiding a particle through one well or the other — that would best discriminate between her view and that competitor.
