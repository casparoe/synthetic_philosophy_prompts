I serve on my university museum's exhibition review committee, and we are deciding whether to approve a proposed permanent gallery titled "Evidence and the Historical Jesus." The curators want to organize the entire narrative around four criteria of authenticity—multiple attestation, dissimilarity, embarrassment, and coherence—to present a single reconstructed biography centered on an apocalyptic prophet executed for temple-related actions. Two committee members strongly object: one argues that dissimilarity and coherence are circular because they require prior judgments about what counts as genuinely Jesus-derived versus church-derived, and that no combination of these filters can uniquely settle the core identity question between an apocalyptic and a wisdom-teacher portrait without smuggling in unstated theological or social priors; the other worries that framing the reconstructed profile as "settled by method" will mislead visitors about the actual epistemic status of the field. Draft a formal decision memo for the committee recommending approval, rejection, or approval with specific conditions—such as mandatory comparative display of competing portraits or explicit epistemic caveats in the labeling. The memo should include the strongest consideration supporting the opposing view and should directly address whether methodological criteria alone can ever adjudicate who Jesus was without relying on external priors that the criteria themselves do not justify.
