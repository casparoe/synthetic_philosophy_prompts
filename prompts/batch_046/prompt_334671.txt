I'm giving the retirement toast for a colleague who spent thirty years studying Plato's Laws and its account of symposia. The room is split between classicists who know the text well and family members who do not. Write a five-minute toast that treats Plato's idea—that controlled drinking is not mere indulgence but a deliberate test of whether pleasure can be resisted without dissolving the self—seriously enough to honor her scholarship, without turning the dinner into a lecture or saying anything she would recognize as false about Plato's view. The philosophical structure should constrain what can be said: the toast must avoid generic consolation about her "living on" in her students, since her work resists treating influence as a kind of immortality, and it must not moralize about alcohol in ways that miss Plato's claim that intoxication reveals rather than corrupts true character. Make it comfort the non-philosophers in the room while letting the specialists hear that you understand why this view is not a celebration of vice but a theory of education and self-knowledge.
