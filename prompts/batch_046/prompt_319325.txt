Write an essay on whether maximal moral goodness—conceived as a life almost wholly organized around moral improvement, altruistic action, and the minimization of harm—is genuinely desirable for an individual agent. Begin by acknowledging the standard objection that such a life risks crowding out legitimate non-moral goods, but direct your primary analysis toward a more subtle tension: maintaining the single-minded commitment characteristic of moral sainthood may conflict with the epistemic humility required to recognize that one's own moral judgments are fallible. Investigate whether a genuinely saintly agent can consistently combine intense dedication to acting on her best moral views with an equally intense openness to revising those views, or whether the two demands pull in opposite directions. After weighing both sides, reach and justify a definite conclusion about whether maximal moral goodness remains an attractive ideal once this tension is taken seriously. In your response, state how confident you are in your overall conclusion and explicitly identify the specific point in your reasoning about which you feel least certain.
