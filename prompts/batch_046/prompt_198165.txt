Consider the following examination item and student response, both drawn from an upper-division philosophy-of-mind assessment on cognitive architecture.

Question: Daniel Dennett distinguishes four kinds of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—defined not by species but by the mechanism through which behavior is shaped or selected. A robotics team has designed an autonomous exploration unit with three integrated subsystems: (1) factory-fixed obstacle-avoidance reflexes that cannot be modified after deployment; (2) a reinforcement-learning navigation policy updated in real time by energy-cost penalties; and (3) a predictive world-model that runs internal simulations of multiple routes, discarding those with high simulated risk before any physical movement occurs. In addition, the unit regularly downloads annotated terrain maps from a central server maintained by other units in the fleet. The task is to classify each subsystem and the unit as a whole within Dennett’s taxonomy, with particular attention to whether the predictive model makes the unit Popperian and whether the server connection makes it Gregorian.

Student answer: The hard-coded reflexes are Darwinian, because they are fixed by prior selection—here the engineers serve as the selecting environment, analogous to natural selection fixing instincts. The reinforcement-learning module is Skinnerian: it shapes behavior through direct interaction with the environment, with energy-cost penalties functioning as negative reinforcement that suppresses inefficient paths. The predictive world-model is Popperian, since it generates internal representations of possible routes and lets the poor ones fail in representation rather than in reality, which matches Dennett’s claim that Popperian creatures can allow hypotheses to perish in their place. Regarding Gregorian status, the unit qualifies on two counts. First, it accesses the central server, importing knowledge accumulated by other units without having to discover it through its own experience, which is precisely the cultural transmission that defines Gregorian cognition. Second, and more fundamentally, its predictive model employs structured symbolic representations—encoded maps and logical path constraints—which are themselves cultural artifacts produced by other agents. Because these symbols structure its internal reasoning, the planner is not merely Popperian but Gregorian in its highest-level functioning. Consequently, the unit is not just layered with all four kinds; at its cognitive peak, Gregorian mechanisms supersede the lower ones, so its dominant architecture is Gregorian.
