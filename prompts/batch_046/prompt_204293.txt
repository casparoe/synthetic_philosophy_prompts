I'm putting together a taxonomy — more like a flowchart with dead ends — for where people actually stand on the pessimistic meta-induction. I don't want labels first; I want the branches built from concrete answers.

Start with this root question: when a past theory like the luminiferous ether was replaced, was the failure mainly about its ontology, its predictive machinery, or the very idea that it had succeeded in the first place?

From there branch on whether you think present physics avoids the same fate because its core math survives replacements, because its ontology is more minimal, or because the historical record of failure is overblown.

The leaves have to include: (a) the view that current theories are literally true and the induction is just bad history; (b) the view that ontology is disposable but the formal structure is kept; (c) full instrumentalism; (d) a domain-selective stance that says only fundamental-physics theories are vulnerable, not applied ones; and (e) one combo that looks logically possible but is actually empty — I think it's the one that accepts the induction wholesale yet insists that every current ontological claim is literally true. Note that empty slot and say if it's incoherent or just unattractive.

Use concrete cases people can answer without knowing jargon — like asking whether they think dark-matter models could be replaced while keeping the rotation-curve predictions intact, not asking if they are structural realists.

Keep it to about five or six decision nodes. Flag any two leaves your tree can't actually distinguish — don't fake a separation.

And commit: definately pick the root question you think actually splits people, defend why that's the right first cut rather than some other one, and tell me which branches you think are genuinely unoccupied versus just unpopular. No hedging with "here are three possible trees."
