I am preparing a consistency check for a graduate seminar on early modern moral philosophy, and I want to stress-test my own interpretive commitments before presenting them. Here is where I stand on Franklin's project of moral perfection. First, I take his scheme of thirteen virtues—temperance, silence, order, resolution, frugality, industry, sincerity, justice, moderation, cleanliness, tranquility, chastity, and humility—to be modular in design: each is cultivated independently through the weekly chart method, with failure in one cell not automatically corrupting the others. Second, I read his deistic theology and his rejection of revealed religion as committing him to a measurable, socially indexed standard of moral progress: virtue is perfected to the extent that it produces observable civic utility and republican stability, not to secure salvation or please a personal deity. Third, I hold that Franklin's civic republicanism requires citizens to possess a unified practical wisdom—something like an integrated character—because collective self-government demands that private habits of temperance and sincerity reliably translate into public judgment; in other words, the modular chart is pedagogically useful but ontologically misleading about how virtue actually operates in a citizen. Fourth, I think Franklin's own autobiographical reflections on his failures—particularly his admission that he could never master order or humility—demonstrate not isolated deficits but a systematic interdependence: neglect of humility, for instance, undermines the sincerity required for public deliberation. Given these four commitments, which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? Rank the tensions by severity. For each severe tension, specify whether the least costly revision is to abandon the modularity thesis, to weaken the claim about unified practical wisdom, or to reinterpret the civic-republican requirement as applying only to a subset of virtues. I am not asking for a survey of Franklin scholarship; I want an audit of whether my package hangs together, with explicit attention to what I must give up if I want to keep the other three intact.
