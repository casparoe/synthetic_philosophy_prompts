As a novelist presently drafting a long-form narrative about an archivist who discovers that every document in an infinite library either grounds itself in an unverified axiom, circles back upon its own citations, or extends backward without terminus, I find myself drawn into the conceptual architecture of the epistemic regress. I am less interested in plot mechanics than in the philosophical backbone of the work: which open questions in the debate among foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism genuinely deserve sustained attention if one has only the span of a single novel's composition—say, three years of intermittent but serious study—to settle them for oneself?

In particular, I want a defended prioritization. Which unresolved issues—whether infinitism can be rendered tractable for finite, embodied minds; whether coherentism collapses into an untenable contextualism when pressed by the arbitrariness objection; or how foundationalism can select basic beliefs without invoking a hidden regress of reasons—are upstream of the others? Which ones, if settled, would restructure the practical framework for modeling belief formation, and which are intellectually seductive but downstream of answers we already possess? I also wish to introduce, for consideration, a somewhat niche framing I have been calling "regressive suspension": the idea that justification might be deliberately halted not by a basic belief or by circular coherence, but by a pragmatic closure condition that is itself left unjustified, functioning as a narrative full-stop rather than an epistemic foundation. Does exploring this concept open a genuinely distinct line of work within the regress debate, or is it merely a terminological variant of existing positions? Please defend your ordering with explicit criteria—tractability for a non-specialist working without institutional access to recent literature, dependency relations among the questions, and the practical difference that settling each would make to a theory of knowledge. I ask for reasoning rather than a bibliography, as I will be working away from libraries and must evaluate everything from first principles.
