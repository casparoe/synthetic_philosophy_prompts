I'm taking a course on Marx's political economy and keep running into an analogy outside the classroom: people argue that today's large language models are the concrete historical form of what Marx called the general intellect — the transformation of social scientific knowledge into an immediate productive force that eventually undermines the value-form. The analogy works like this. Marx, in his analysis of machinery, described an automatic system in which dead labor embodied in capital dominates living labor, reducing necessary labor time to a minimum while capital still measures wealth in labor time, thereby generating a contradiction that tends to break the value-form. Proponents say foundation models realize exactly this structure: collective human knowledge is crystallized in capital-controlled weights that generate outputs with near-zero marginal labor, so direct labor is minimized, yet the outputs remain enclosed within proprietary platforms and priced through exchange. From this they conclude that we are approaching a crisis in which value production collapses because knowledge is freely reproducible, even though property relations still try to extract surplus value from it. I want you to audit this analogy with precision. First, provide the correspondence table: what plays the role of the automatic machinery, the dead labor in capital, the living labor required at inference, the capitalist as platform owner, the reserve army of labor displaced, and the tendency of the rate of profit to fall? Second, identify the disanalogies that specifically bear on the crisis-of-the-value-form claim: does the near-zero marginal cost of generating an output map onto Marx's reduction of necessary labor time, or does it confuse technical production costs with the social validation of value through exchange? Does the non-rival, non-excludible nature of model outputs correspond to Marx's idea of social knowledge becoming a direct force, or does it ignore his distinction between the sphere of material production and the sphere of circulation? Third, point out what the analogy quietly smuggles in — for instance, that knowledge is homogeneous across technical and cultural domains, or that inference labor is analogous to the factory operative — that Marx's own distinction between formal and real subsumption might not license. Finally, tell me whether the conclusion that capital is now undermining its own basis survives once these breaks are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the political conclusion all along.
