I am preparing a short presentation for my high school philosophy seminar on self-reference and hierarchical explanation, and I want to close with an original poem that takes the idea seriously rather than using it as decoration. Please write a poem structured as two sections—each exactly eight lines, with no prologue, epilogue, or framing stanza outside the loop—so that the final line of the first section is identically the opening line of the second, and the final line of the second section is identically the opening line of the first. This circular form should enact a tangled hierarchy: there should be no “ground” line or inviolate level that stands outside the system, just as Escher’s Drawing Hands depicts two hands drawing each other into existence without a prior artist. The content should treat Douglas Hofstadter’s concept of strange loops, specifically the locally consistent but globally contradictory directions of “up” and “down” across levels, and should present the absence of a base level not as a failure of explanation but as the structural condition of the loop itself. It needs to be accurate enough that someone familiar with Gödel, Escher, Bach recognizes the reference to strange loops and the critique of hierarchical levels, rather than treating the image as mere surrealism. After the poem, include a brief but committed note—taking a definite position, not surveying options—explaining exactly where the poem takes liberties with Hofstadter’s framework or leans on a contested reading of whether tangled hierarchies genuinely eliminate all inviolate levels or only obscure them from view. The piece should be polished enough to read aloud to the group.
