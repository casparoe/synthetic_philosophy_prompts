I am a journalist specialising in science and ideas, and after discussing the thesis with colleagues at a recent seminar, I am now preparing the question sheet for a long-form recorded interview with Dr. Alistair Venn, a historian of science who advances a rehabilitated cyclical view of history. Rather than reviving Spengler’s organic metaphor of inevitable decay, Venn treats civilisations as dissipative structures in the Prigogine sense, interpreting Ibn Khaldun’s asabiyya as an energy-gradient that sustains institutional complexity only so long as imported order exceeds internal entropy production; on this account, Polybius’s anacyclosis describes a non-equilibrium phase transition rather than biological destiny, and Toynbee’s challenge-response mechanism is recast as the system’s capacity to absorb renewed energy influx rather than as open-ended linear progress. Critics worry this framing merely relocates directional development into thermodynamic vocabulary without yielding testable predictions.

Please provide exactly five interview questions, numbered 1 through 5, each expressed in one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. They must press the actual content of his position: ask whether the dissipative analogy generates falsifiable predictions about collapse or only redescribes them in physical language; probe how he distinguishes genuine cyclical renewal via renewed influx from linear technological transformation that permanently raises carrying capacity; request the empirical marker of asabiyya-decay that could genuinely refute the model rather than being accommodated post hoc; anticipate his likely reply that this is a research programme, not an objection, and follow up by asking what specific evidence would count against the view; and challenge how cumulative, apparently linear scientific knowledge fits within a cyclical morphology that denies directional progress. The tone should be formal, conceptually precise, and suitable for an audience acquainted with the history of ideas but not expected to verify specialist sources.
