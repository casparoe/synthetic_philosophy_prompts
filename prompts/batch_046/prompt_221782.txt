There's this old fight in philosophy of science about whether biology and chemistry are just physics in a trenchcoat, or whether once you get feedback loops and autocatalisis you've got genuinely novel causal powers. The reductionists say every causal interaction in a cell eventually bottoms out in quantum-electrodynamic forces arranged in complex patterns; the emergentists say downword causation in things like protein folding shows the higher-level properties are doing real work that can't be derived from the base laws alone. I've always thought the stalemate happens because reductionists are asking for a derivational explanation while emergentists are asking for a mechanistic one — they're talking past each other about what 'explanation' even means. But maybe I'm wrong: maybe there's a hidden empirical disagreement about whether downward causation actually occurs, or maybe it's purely verbal — they just mean different things by 'causation'. Can you diagnose what's actually going on? Use what each side actually says about derivation vs mechanism, and tell me whether this is a substantive dispute, a verbal one, or two different questions wearing the same hat. And be honest: could a formal result about computational irreducibility, or some new experiment on molecular self-assembly, actually end it? Or should we expect no progress and why?
