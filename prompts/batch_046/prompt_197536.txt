I have been working through judgments about legitimacy in a hierarchical, self-amending rule system—one with base statutes, amendment procedures, and meta-rules that can refer to their own authority. Case 1 — The Lowered Threshold: Statute S1 states that it may be amended only by unanimous vote. By unanimous vote, that clause is replaced with a clause saying simple majority suffices. Then a simple majority repeals S1 entirely. I judge the repeal valid: the Council lawfully updated the procedure and then exercised it.

Case 2 — The Immovable Meta-Rule: Meta-statute M1 states that it is not subject to any amendment procedure whatsoever. Statute S2 holds that any rule conflicting with M1 is void. The Council enacts S3, which amends M1 to create an exception for environmental law. I judge S3 void and M1 untouched; its self-exemption is absolute.

Case 3 — The Retroactive Shield: Statute R1, enacted in 1900, declares that any later-enacted rule contradicting R1 is null. In 1950, R2 contradicts R1. In 2000, R3 amends R1 to say it applies only to rules enacted after 2000. I judge that R2 remains null; R3 does not retroactively narrow R1's original reach.

Case 4 — The Parasitic Save: Statute A may be suspended only by a decree that explicitly names Statute A. An emergency decree suspends A but cites it only by catalog number. Statute B provides that any suspension failing procedural form is void, unless the suspended statute is essential to public order; A is essential. I judge the suspension valid—B saves it. Yet if the same decree also suspended B (using the correct name), I think A's suspension would then fail, since B could not save itself and A together. This feels arbitrary: B's authority seems to depend on whether it is also suspended.

These verdicts feel mostly right, but Cases 3 and 4 sit uneasily with Cases 1 and 2. Is there a coherent principle—perhaps about procedural continuity, hierarchical authority, or temporal jurisdiction—that preserves Cases 1–3 and tells me exactly what to say about further scenarios? Or must I drop one verdict, likely the selective saving in Case 4?
