My opening challenge introduces what I will call the null-board problem. I grant that many canonical cases — Bach's variations, the gradual abstraction of mathematical structures, stylistic distortion in painting — fit your slider model well. But consider acts where the breakthrough is not an adjustment of any parameter but the recognition that the entire board belongs to the wrong framework and must be discarded. Take Duchamp's readymade: it is not sculpture with its form-parameters pushed to an extreme, but an act that rejects the very parameter space of shaped form. Or Einstein's dissolution of the luminiferous ether: he did not turn an ether-viscosity knob to zero, but eliminated the conceptual machinery that made the knob meaningful. If creativity can include this kind of framework-dissolution — destroying the board rather than twiddling its knobs — then your model is at best a special case, not the crux of creativity.
