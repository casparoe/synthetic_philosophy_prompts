Read Spike Jonze's film Being John Malkovich through Frank Jackson's Mary's room argument and the knowledge problem about phenomenal consciousness. In the film, characters enter a portal that lets them experience the world from John Malkovich's first-person perspective. Focus on the sequence in which Lotte first enters and describes what she sees and feels. Does this scenario support Jackson's claim that there are non-physical phenomenal facts—facts about the subjective character of experience that no amount of physical information can provide—or does it instead show that phenomenal access is just a novel causal route to physical information already available? In particular, consider whether the film treats first-person experience as a kind of transferable data, and if so, whether that undermines the intuition that Mary, upon leaving her black-and-white room, learns something genuinely new. A strong answer should take both the film's metaphysics of consciousness-transfer and Jackson's epistemic gap seriously, and say where the work illuminates or resists the knowledge argument in ways that the philosophical literature usually ignores.
