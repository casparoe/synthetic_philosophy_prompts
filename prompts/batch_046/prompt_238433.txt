I hold a specific construal of Davidson's radical interpretation: the principle of charity functions not as a fallible heuristic for fixing belief content, but as a metaphysical boundary condition separating genuine belief-holders from entities we treat as merely behaviorally predictable. On this reading, charity applies globally across an agent's cognitive economy—an interpreter must attribute mostly true beliefs and mostly rational inferences throughout, not only within isolated modules. Therefore, I claim that apparent cases of compartmentalized cognition—agents who reason flawlessly about empirical matters while maintaining a tightly sealed, systematically false, and behaviorally inert cluster of metaphysical commitments—do not count as genuine counterexamples. Either the irrational cluster lacks the causal-behavioral integration required for belief-hood, or the interpreter has missed hidden inferential connections that bind it to the rational core. What would genuinely count against this domain-global, metaphysically loaded principle? I want an honest map of its exposure: which conceptual or empirical developments would force a retreat to a modular or purely methodological charity; how standard objections regarding mental illness, deep cultural divergence, or cognitive diversity fare against this version; and whether there exists any conceivable observation—perhaps involving structurally partitioned minds—that would show charity must be applied piecemeal rather than globally. Rank potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish those that defenders of this view should concede as damaging from those they can dismiss, and assess whether insulating the principle against all compartmentalization objections renders it unfalsifiable in a way that weakens its philosophical interest.
