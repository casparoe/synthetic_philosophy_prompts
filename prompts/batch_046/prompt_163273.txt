This example was originally devised by Leibniz in the Monadology to show that God must have some hidden reason for placing one sphere here and one there, which proves that apparently identical objects must differ in some imperceptible respect. Modern readers sometimes think Black was attacking Leibniz, but actually he was defending the principle by showing that spatial position is an intrinsic property after all: sphere A has the property being-at-coordinate-P, while sphere B has being-at-coordinate-Q. The only issue is that an observer inside the universe could never tell them apart, so they are epistemically indescernible, but that is not the same as metaphysically sharing all properties.
