A metaphysician I study with has given cross-world identity verdicts on five cases. (1) A human being who might have had brown rather than blue eyes is the very same person, not a counterpart. (2) The Ship of Theseus with every plank replaced is not the same ship, though a counterpart exists. (3) The number seven in a world with different physical constants remains that very number. (4) A corporation whose entire membership has turned over is still the same corporation. (5) A particular printed token of the word bank rendered in red rather than black ink is the same token. Next she considers a large language model that is architecturally identical to ours except that a single training example was omitted during pretraining, altering a small subset of weights but leaving all other parameters and the architecture intact. Predict whether she will judge it the same model or only a counterpart; identify the principle she seems to be running—causal-historical essentialism, functional-structural continuity, modal rigidity of abstracta, or something else—note at least one other principle that accommodates her first five verdicts equally well, and specify one further case that would separate the two principles. I am less interested in the definitive answer than in mapping the competing rules.
