People often invoke the image that history follows an arc bending toward justice, comparing moral development to a projectile curving under gravity toward a fixed target. Activists and theorists use this to argue that progress is structurally directed and that temporary setbacks are merely deviations from an inevitable path. Lay out the explicit correspondence table — what is the projectile, the gravitational field, the bow or launcher, and the target — and identify where the physical analogy fails: does history possess a unified state space, a single attractor, or a mechanism analogous to gravity? Then examine whether substituting Hegel’s idea of the cunning of reason — rational ends achieved through opposing passions — repairs the analogy by replacing an external archer with an immanent process, or whether that introduces a teleology the physical picture cannot carry. Finally, assess whether the conclusion that justice is structurally assured survives once these disanalogies are visible, or whether the arc was doing all the argumentative work.
