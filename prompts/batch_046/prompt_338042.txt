I serve as a senior policy analyst for a parliamentary defense committee in a state that maintains armed neutrality through universal conscription rather than a standing professional army—similar in structural logic to the Swiss or Finnish models, though our constitutional specifics differ and need not be detailed here. We are reviewing legislative proposals to shift toward selective service, targeted reserve systems, or exemption mechanisms, and I need a reusable decision procedure that my team can apply repeatedly without re-deriving the underlying philosophy each time. The principle I want operationalized is not the standard manpower-efficiency argument, but a claim about structural reciprocity: in an armed neutrality regime, the broad social distribution of defense obligations functions as a non-institutional check on foreign-policy adventurism, because political leaders must internalize deployment costs in the bodies of citizens who also vote. If this coupling is severed—through professionalization, class-selective exemption, or commodified substitution—the neutrality commitment becomes strategically hollow regardless of troop numbers. Please translate this principle into a practical evaluation procedure: list the sequential questions an analyst should ask about any proposal, specify what structural feature or evidence answers each affirmatively or negatively, identify the precise point where the procedure mandates rejection, and mark the exact junctures where factual determination ends and normative judgment about democratic equality must take over. Then apply the procedure to three concrete proposals: first, replacing universal ground service with a volunteer-only cyber-defense reserve; second, permitting high-income citizens to pay a "neutrality contribution" in lieu of physical service; and third, extending conscription obligations to long-term resident non-citizens. Ensure that one application yields a recommendation that conflicts with conventional liberal intuitions about individual autonomy, and conclude by stating explicitly what the procedure cannot settle—particularly whether patriotic duty carries independent moral weight beyond its instrumental role in sustaining neutrality. Respond in no more than 450 words, presenting the procedure itself as an ordered checklist.
