People usually read Arendt's banality of evil as "Eichmann was just a stastical normal guy, so anyone could do it." I think that's a misread—she's not making a psychological claim about ordinariness, she's making a phenomological one: the evil of the Holocaust lacked traditional depth, intentionality, or diabolical malice; it was bureaucratic surface all the way down. So the position is that modern administrative machinery can generate massive harm without anyone inside possessing a genuinely malicious will, and that this hollow-ness, not hidden monsters, is the real danger.

Play it straight: what would have to be true for that to be wrong? If the Sassen tapes and private letters show Eichmann was deeply ideologically motivated, does that defeat the phenomological claim or only the pop-psych version? What if you need at least a few designers with real malicious intent to build and maintain the machinery—does that make the "surface-level" thesis collapse into something banal? And has the view been so qualified—"she never meant it literally"—that it's now insulated from any counterevidence? I want an honest assessment of how exposed it actually is, not just what critics say but what defenders would concede counts against it.
