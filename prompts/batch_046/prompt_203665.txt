I've come round to a kind of conciliationism, applied particlarly to literary judgement: when another novelist whose craft I respect disagrees with me about whether a draft's structure works, I ought to become less sure I'm right. Not just in science or politics — in taste and form too. What would actually count against extending conciliationism into aesthetic and craft domains? I want the arguments and observations sorted from decisive to merely awkward, including what defenders of the view would themselves concede is damaging, whether it collapses into relativism about novels, whether it's been qualified into unfalsifiability, and which domains (if any) are genuinely immune. Please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who study peer disagreement, and don't present one contested side as the consensus.
