I am preparing an opening exercise for an upper-level seminar on comparative church-state regimes, and I want a sustained piece of deadpan satire that forces students to see how incompatible the canonical Western models actually are when treated as interchangeable policy modules. Please write a bureaucratic “User Agreement and Civic Piety Selection Form” for citizens of the fictional Republic of Pluristate, presented in the flat, nested-clause voice of a government digital-services portal. The form must require citizens to navigate all four major Western frameworks simultaneously: the English established-church model (Crown appointment of bishops, Lords Spiritual sitting in Parliament, formal establishment with low religious practice); the German Kirchensteuer system (state payroll collection of an ecclesiastical tax—roughly 8 to 9 percent of income tax—tied to registered religious affiliation and formal exit via Kirchenaustritt); French laïcité (strict public neutrality under the 1905 separation law, including prohibitions on conspicuous religious symbols in state schools and civic spaces); and the American Establishment-Clause framework (no direct state funding of religion, the Lemon-test logic of secular purpose and primary effect, coupled with robust free-exercise protections).

The satire should derive entirely from real institutional details pushed into logical contradiction, not from invented absurdities. For instance, the form should ask users to consent to automatic Kirchensteuer withholding while simultaneously affirming no religious affiliation that would trigger such withholding; to accept that their denomination is the constitutionally established church whose bishops sit in the legislature, yet also agree that all public-school teachers and students must display strict neutrality that prohibits visible affiliation with that same denomination; and to acknowledge that the state neither advances nor inhibits religion, even as it collects religious taxes and appoints religious officials. Include at least one invented administrative neologism—such as “Cooperative Non-Establishment” or “Negative Positive Neutrality”—that sounds like a genuine policy category but collapses the four traditions into nonsense. Add a “Frequently Asked Questions” sidebar in the same deadpan register, addressing citizen confusion about whether paying the church tax violates the Establishment Clause or whether leaving the church requires notifying both the tax office and the Crown. Keep the tone utterly flat, like an actual terms-of-service agreement, with checkboxes, nested subsections, and legalistic warnings. The piece should run about 700 to 800 words, target the liberal-democratic fantasy that constitutional mechanisms can be cherry-picked across incompatible traditions without generating contradictions, and remain legible as satire without any authorial winking. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the humor and the conceptual work must come from the accurate rendering of the institutional mechanisms themselves.
