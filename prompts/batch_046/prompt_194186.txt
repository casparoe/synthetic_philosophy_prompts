Please explain the conceptual tension that emerges when the Biological Species Concept—defined by reproductive isolation—is applied to predominantly asexual lineages, such as many bacterial and plant groups. Specifically, I want to understand how the absence of sexual reproduction blurs the boundary between a taxonomic species and an ontological evolutionary individual: if clonal reproduction makes lineage-boundary conditions structurally similar to the boundary conditions of a single multicellular organism, does this undermine the realist claim that species are mind-independent entities? Furthermore, does shifting to a phylogenetic or ecological concept resolve the ambiguity, or does it merely relocate the same ontological confusion into the criteria for monophyly or niche occupation? I am interested in the conceptual architecture of the problem rather than a survey of particular taxonomic disputes.
