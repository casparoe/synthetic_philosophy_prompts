Analyze the 1987 Black Monday crash as a case study in deterministic chaos and predictability. The basic story is well-known: Dow fell 22.6% on Oct 19, program trading and portfolio insurence got blamed, and it spread globally in hours. I want you to use the idea of a predictability horizon — that deterministic systems can be technically deterministic yet practically unforecastable past a certain window — plus the shadowing lemma idea that even chaotic trajectories have nearby shadow paths that match observed data post-hoc. So: was Black Monday a genuine breakdown of determinism, or just traders running past there horizon? Does the shadow-trajectory idea explains why every post-mortem makes the crash feel inevitable? And where does chaos theory miss — for example, the role of human panic or regulatory gaps that the model doesn't capture? Also, does the episode support or undermine the claim that financial markets live at the edge of chaos?
