I've kinda bought into Benatar's asymmetry, but with a twist I don't hear talked about much. I don't think the core point is that pleasure and pain are asymetrical in value — I think it's about the moral status of merely possible people. On my view there's a negative duty not to actualize a being who'd suffer, but there's literally zero moral reason (not just a weaker one) to actualize a being who'd be happy, and that zero holds even under aggregation across huge numbers. So the repugnant conclusion doesn't bite, not because suffering is infinitely bad, but because happy potential lives don't accumulate any weight at all — they're just not on the scale.

What would count against this? I want arguments and observations sorted by how decisive they'd be: do our adoption or embryo-rescue intuitions actually track this modal-status claim? Is there a hidden symmetry in how we treat risk to actual vs potential people? Does the view accidentally collapse into saying existence itself is harmful? And has this version been qualified into unfalsifiability — like, defenders retreating to "those people weren't really possible in the relevant sense" whenever counterexamples show up? If it's that insulated, is that a problem?

Please say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you're least sure of.
