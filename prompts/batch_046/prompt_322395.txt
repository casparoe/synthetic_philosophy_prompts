Please develop a sustained argument that the standard money-pump objection to cyclical preferences fails when applied to genuinely diachronic agents, because it tacitly assumes a synchronic model of personal identity that cannot survive scrutiny under psychological-continuity or narrative theories of the self. Begin by sharply distinguishing the static case—where an agent holds simultaneous preferences A ≻ B ≻ C ≻ A and is vulnerable to a Dutch-book extraction—from the dynamic case, in which an agent prefers A over B on Monday, B over C on Tuesday, and C over A on Wednesday, with each shift tracking a real change in her evidential situation, affective state, or normative framework rather than a logical error. Your central claim should be that forcing transitivity across these temporal stages requires treating Monday-me, Tuesday-me, and Wednesday-me as parts of one continuous super-agent governed by a single utility function; but if personal identity is constituted by psychological continuity or an evolving self-narrative rather than by the persistence of a substantive ego, then there is no single agent across time to be made irrational by the cycle.

Then defend the positive reconstruction: diachronic rationality should not be defined as the preservation of an unchanging preference ordering, but rather as a coherence constraint on the reasons the agent endorses at each stage for her local transitions. Argue that a cyclical trajectory is rationally permissible provided that, at each temporal boundary, the agent can offer a context-sensitive justification—grounded in altered information, revised values, or transformed practical circumstances—that she herself accepts from the perspective of that stage. Conclude by addressing the obvious reply: that without cross-temporal transitivity, long-term planning collapses into incoherence. Show why this reply confuses instrumental coordination with metaphysical unity—an agent can still engage in stable planning by adopting higher-order policies or pre-commitment devices without claiming that her future self is literally the same rational subject whose preferences must be kept in line.
