Argue that Carnap's principle of linguistic tolerance, if extended consistently from language choice to institutional design, supports what might be called institutional framework pluralism in social planning. On this view, a rational polity ought to maintain parallel planning mechanisms—such as participatory democratic allocation and expert-driven predictive modeling—not as competing comprehensive doctrines but as distinct internal frameworks, each governed by its own rules of inference and value-coordination, with meta-rules that shift decision authority between them on the basis of predictive track record rather than normative consensus. In developing the argument, map Carnap's distinction between internal and external questions onto the separation of technical planning questions from political value questions; show how this pluralism avoids collapsing into either technocratic authoritarianism or value-relativism; and defend the claim that Carnap's commitment to scientific rationality in social organization requires not a single unified planning language but a tolerant meta-framework that allows incompatible planning logics to coexist, provided each submits to empirical assessment within its own terms.
