I have been reflecting on moral emotions with some urgency because my own community recently underwent a public reckoning over a collective wrong, and the response seemed to oscillate between ritualized guilt and something closer to status-based exclusion; I am trying to settle my view before presenting it in a seminar. Here is roughly where I have landed: (1) I think guilt is the more defensible moral emotion because it localizes wrongdoing to a specific act, leaving the agent’s moral identity intact and making genuine restitution psychologically possible, whereas shame targets the global self and tends to produce withdrawal rather than repair. (2) At the same time, I believe shame serves an irreplaceable function in sustaining the normative boundaries of a moral community—without the threat of collective disapproval that shame embodies, there would be no mechanism for signaling that a violation threatens shared identity rather than merely breaking a rule. (3) I also think a culture can deliberately choose and cultivate one emotional regime over the other—for instance, through legal and social architectures that replace public rituals of humiliation with private mechanisms of accountability—so that the dominance of guilt or shame is institutionally negotiable rather than culturally fixed. (4) Yet I am convinced that both guilt and shame are evolved, domain-general affective responses shaped by deep evolutionary pressures, which makes them largely resistant to top-down cultural engineering. (5) More recently, I have come to think that what philosophers call “moral shame” is actually a misidentified form of social-rank anxiety; on this view, shame is not genuinely responsive to moral reasons at all, whereas guilt tracks moral wrong in a direct and unreducible way. Do these commitments hang together, or am I helping myself to incompatible motivations? Specifically, I want you to go through the pairs and tell me which tensions are genuine—especially where the usual reasons for holding one view are incompatible with another—versus which only look contradictory but dissolve on closer inspection. Please rank the tensions by severity, say for each what I would have to give up and at what cost, and then identify the single revision that would clean up the most inconsistencies with the least damage to the overall position.
