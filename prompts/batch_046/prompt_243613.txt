I am enrolled in an advanced jurisprudence seminar that meets fortnightly, and our tutor — a constitutional scholar who asks us to treat her rulings on hypothetical instruments as a single, coherent body of doctrine — has delivered the following verdicts on self-amending legal rules. First, she considered a municipal charter whose Section 8 permitted amendment by a two-thirds vote of the council, except that Section 8 itself could be altered only by unanimous consent of all registered electors; when the council voted by two-thirds to replace that unanimous-consent requirement with a simple majority, she held the amendment void ab initio, leaving the original protective lock untouched. Second, she examined a regulatory code whose Rule 14 stated that the Director might amend any rule in the code, including Rule 14, provided that no amendment eliminated the Director's amendment powers without a concurrent legislative resolution; she upheld an amendment that transferred those powers to an independent Oversight Board, but only because the requisite legislative resolution had been passed in tandem. Third, she addressed a constitution whose Article III allowed partial or full suspension by a two-thirds vote of the Assembly, yet declared that Article III itself could never be suspended; faced with an emergency vote purporting to suspend the entire constitution, including Article III, she ruled that the suspension was valid with respect to every other article but void with respect to Article III, which therefore retained authority over the conditions of its own suspension.

We have now been given a fresh hypothetical, which I shall call the Bootstrap Amendment. A constitution's Article 7 provides that Article 7 may be revised solely by a constituent convention convened according to the procedural rules set out within Article 7, and that those convening rules may themselves be changed only by the convention they govern, never by an ordinary act of the legislature. Claiming that the existing rules have become procedurally paralysed, the legislature enacts a statute that revises the convening rules; it then summons a convention under those revised rules, and the convention proceeds to amend Article 7. I should like you to predict our tutor's verdict on whether the legislative revision and the subsequent convention amendment are valid, to set out the two most plausible implicit principles that could account for her earlier three rulings — for example, a principle of hierarchical textual entrenchment versus one of systemic coherence — and to say what each principle predicts about this Bootstrap case. Please also identify the further hypothetical case — perhaps one in which the convening rules contain an explicit internal contradiction that the legislature attempts to resolve by statute before any convention is called — that would best discriminate between those two principles. Offer your best guess at her actual ruling, with reasons drawn from her track record, and present your answer in American English.
