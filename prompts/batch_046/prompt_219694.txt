After months of arguing with my closest friend—she defends abortion rights through bodily-autonomy analogies and rejects any special obligation arising from pregnancy itself, while I defend fetal rights through continuity-of-identity reasoning but concede that standard formulations neglect the physiological specificity of gestation—we want to stop talking past each other and write the document we should have produced together. Draft our adversarial-collaboration statement. First, provide a numbered list of claims both of us can sign: about what rescue analogies actually establish regarding consent and bodily use; about whether pregnancy involves a form of labor or support structurally different from both accidental rescue and ordinary parenting; about the conditions under which an argument from a being’s potential future applies, particularly when that future’s realization depends continuously on one specific organism; and about any empirical premises (concerning fetal sentience, dependency, or available social structures) that do not divide us. Second, present our residual disagreements in a table: each disagreement stated in wording that the respective side would accept for its own view, with one column for her position and one for mine. Third, identify the cruxes—those sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one of us—especially whether gestational labor creates a moral category distinct from both negative and positive duties, and whether the contingency of fetal survival on a particular body weakens or strengthens the wrongness of termination. For each crux, supply a concrete hypothetical case or argument that both of us agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts that case reveals. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading; the aim is to map our disagreement exactly, not to survey the literature.
