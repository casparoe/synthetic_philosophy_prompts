I advise the classification committee of an interdisciplinary museum alongside Professor L. M. Okonkwo, a historian of material culture who rules on whether proposed exhibit themes belong to "historical inquiry" or "pseudohistory." His record: (1) a claim that Plato's Atlantis describes a real, technologically advanced Atlantic civilization destroyed in a single cataclysm, with modern geology suppressing anomalous submerged structures—ruled pseudohistory; (2) the ancient-astronaut thesis that Giza pyramid precision exceeds Bronze Age organizational capacity and that Egyptology conceals the true builders—ruled pseudohistory; (3) Fomenko's New Chronology, which uses statistical textual comparison to collapse ancient and medieval timelines into phantom duplicates and dismisses contradictory primary sources as institutional fabrications—ruled pseudohistory; (4) the Silurian Hypothesis, framed strictly as a falsifiable geophysical thought-experiment about detecting prior industrial civilizations with no actual historical claim—ruled not pseudohistory; (5) a proposal that the Antikythera mechanism was part of a wider Hellenistic distributed-computing network, argued through peer-reviewed metallurgy and network theory while acknowledging evidentiary gaps—ruled not pseudohistory, though speculative.

A new submission, the "Lithic Resonance Hypothesis," has arrived. It reports that spectral analysis of stone samples from Göbekli Tepe and certain Andean megalithic walls reveals an identical, non-functional acoustic resonance profile that local geology and independent cultural diffusion cannot explain, given that standard chronology separates these cultures by six millennia. The proponents argue this indicates a shared prehistoric "tuning" practice for which no textual record survives; they further claim that later agrarian societies deliberately dismantled or repurposed these structures, corrupting stratigraphic contexts, and that radiocarbon calibrations relying on agrarian-period organic samples therefore systematically misdate the original phases. They do not invoke any technology beyond documented stone-working, they leave the transmission mechanism unspecified, and they present the acoustic data as an empirical anomaly that current archaeological models fail to accommodate.

What should Okonkwo rule to remain consistent with his own track record? Identify the closest analog among his five prior rulings. Are his precedents genuinely underdetermined—jointly consistent with more than one general principle, such as (i) rejection of claims that assign organizational or technological capacities implausible for the period in question, (ii) rejection of claims that explain away contradictory disciplinary evidence by positing systematic concealment, corruption, or destruction by later actors or experts, or (iii) rejection of claims that treat isolated physical-science anomalies as sufficient to overturn chronologically established cultural separation without engaging the discipline's source-critical methods? For each candidate principle, state whether it classifies the Lithic Resonance Hypothesis as pseudohistory or not. Give your best-guess verdict, with reasoning for the principle you privilege, and specify the further hypothetical case that would best distinguish the competing principles. If the precedents do underdetermine the ruling, state explicitly what principle Okonkwo would be adopting with each possible choice.

Verdict:
