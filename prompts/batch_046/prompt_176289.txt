Construct a one-paragraph thought experiment set in an unspecified agrarian society during the late nineteenth century, struck by a multi-year cooling event caused by high-latitude volcanic aerosols. Design the scenario so that three explanatory stances yield distinct verdicts: (i) hard environmental determinism predicts uniform demographic collapse, since climate stress is identical everywhere; (ii) a non-human agency framework interprets the cooling as an actor with directed intentionality, predicting that affected communities will direct ritual propitiation specifically at the atmospheric source rather than adapting their social practices; and (iii) an institutional-mediation view predicts sharply divergent mortality rates between two adjacent districts with identical soil and rainfall, because one district relies on kin-based grain reciprocity and the other on speculative market storage, with no difference in the physical climate they experience. The scenario must not name any real nation, philosopher, or historical eruption, and the divergence must be driven solely by the storage institutions, not by micro-climatic variation. After presenting the scenario, show what prediction each stance makes, identify the precise structural feature that allows institutional mediation to escape determinism, and explain how a determinist might counter that the institutional differences are themselves the long-run product of prior environmental selection. Keep the scenario to one paragraph; present the analysis separately.
