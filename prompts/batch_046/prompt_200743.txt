I am preparing the final examination for an upper-level seminar on rational choice and the structure of practical reason, and I need a single essay question that students cannot answer by reciting the familiar outline of Buridan's ass but that instead forces them to dissect what it means to choose without a preference. Please write the exam question, a model answer of approximately 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an excellent response from an adequate one by specifying the exact argumentative moves the former must include and the particular misunderstandings that should cost marks in the latter. The question should introduce a modified scenario: an automated allocation system must distribute one of two identical resources to one of two claimants whose profiles are indistinguishable on every metric the system can access, yet delay in assignment generates harm that scales linearly with time, and the system may invoke a tie-breaking protocol only if it can derive that protocol from preferences already present in its evaluation architecture rather than importing it arbitrarily. Ask the student to argue whether rationality demands that the system suspend judgment, revise its evaluation function, or adopt the tie-breaker, and require them specifically to examine whether using an arbitrary tie-breaker dissolves the paradox by uncovering a hidden preference or merely displaces the original problem. The model answer should clarify the distinction between lacking any reason to prefer one option and positively judging the options to be equally worthy, should evaluate whether an incomplete preference ordering is rationally permissible or defective, and should explain precisely where the algorithmic case diverges from or parallels the classical donkey. The marking scheme should be rendered entirely in continuous prose, narratively describing the allocation of credit across these analytical tasks—for example, noting that an answer which treats the algorithmic twist as irrelevant should fall below the top band, and that an answer which mistakes practical suspension for epistemic ignorance should be penalized—without using headings, lists, boldface, or tables. Present everything—the prompt, the answer, and the scheme—in continuous prose as in a formal letter, with no structural formatting whatsoever. In addition, please indicate how confident you are in the distinctions and judgments you make, and explicitly identify the point on which your confidence is lowest.
