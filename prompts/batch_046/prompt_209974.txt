You are advising a researcher with roughly four years, formal training in logic and philosophy of science but not in theoretical physics, on how to allocate effort within the problem space of ontology identification under physically inaccessible information. The domain includes cases where competing ontologies are empirically equivalent within our accessible domain but diverge in regions or at scales permanently beyond observation—such as cosmological structures outside the observable horizon, interior black-hole geometries, or dual descriptions in quantum gravity—as well as cases where theoretical formulations share all predictions but differ in ontological interpretation. Among the open lines of work are: (1) whether structural realism can be coherently maintained when the structural relations that might adjudicate between ontologies lie entirely outside the accessible domain; (2) how to formalize rules for ontology identification that depend only on causally or informationally accessible structure, and whether such rules collapse into a form of empirical equivalence or preserve substantive ontological distinctions; (3) whether holographic or other quantum-gravity dualities should be treated as instances of this general inaccessibility problem or as a separate category with distinct identification criteria; and (4) whether inaccessibility should be interpreted as a purely epistemic limitation that leaves ontology intact, or as an ontological boundary that renders the inaccessible distinctions non-substantive. Which two or three of these are most worth prioritizing? Provide a defended ranking based on tractability for a philosopher without deep physics expertise, the extent to which other questions in the area depend on each resolution, and what specific formal or empirical development over the next few years would cause you to revise the ranking. In particular, explain whether resolving the status of quantum-gravity dualities upstream would settle the broader identification problem, or whether the two should be pursued in parallel.
