I'm preparing an interview for a science-and-ideas podcast with a philosopher of biology who holds that heritability estimates are conceptually hollow outside the specific breeding populations in which they are calculated. She argues that treating h² as a measure of how genetic a trait is commits a category error—heritability is a ratio of variances, not a causal coefficient—and that the missing heritability problem is not an empirical gap but a symptom of forcing complex traits into an additive variance-partitioning framework that ignores gene-environment covariance and niche construction. I want six to eight questions that press her on these claims without letting her retreat to general skepticism about genetics. Specifically: one question on why heritability values change across environments if genes are constant; one on whether her additive-variance critique undermines quantitative genetics entirely or just its popular interpretation; one on niche construction as a confounding factor that makes the genetic-environmental split ontologically unstable; and for each, a sharp follow-up anticipating her most likely defense—particularly the move that this is just a statistical tool, not a metaphysical claim. Make the questions accessible to an educated lay audience but precise enough that she cannot answer with a sound bite.
