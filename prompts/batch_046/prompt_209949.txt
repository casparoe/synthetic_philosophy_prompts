Write a dialogue between two philosophers of physics, Alistair and Beatrice, who hold opposing views on the derivation of the Born rule within Everettian quantum mechanics. Alistair defends a self-locating uncertainty approach: he holds that a rational agent facing branching should assign credences to future branches in proportion to the squared amplitudes because the agent lacks any epistemic basis for favouring one branch over another. Beatrice defends a decision-theoretic approach: she holds that rational preference axioms, applied to agents who care about all their successors, necessarily constrain betting behaviour so that the agent values outcomes in proportion to branch weight. The dialogue should contain genuine back-and-forth, with each character responding to the specific point just made by the other, and neither should be presented as a strawman.

To ground the disagreement, have them discuss a hypothetical scenario they call the merged-branch wager. In this case, an agent is split into two branches with weights 1/3 and 2/3; in the lighter branch the agent receives £10, in the heavier branch nothing; but then, before any observation or decision can occur in the lighter branch, it is deterministically recombined with the other branch via a decoherence-reversing mechanism. Alistair should argue that the agent's pre-split rational credence in receiving the £10 remains 1/3, because the subsequent pruning does not retroactively cancel the self-locating uncertainty that existed at the moment of branching. Beatrice should reply that if the lighter branch never reaches a point at which preferences are exercised, then the decision-theoretic axioms have no purchase there; consequently, the rational agent should evaluate the wager solely from the perspective of the 2/3 branch in which they actually deliberate, and Alistair's view wrongly treats branch weights as frequencies over observers who never come to exist. They should also address whether the squared-amplitude measure describes a physical feature of the wavefunction or functions merely as a normative constraint on belief.

Keep the exchange concise, technically precise, and free of appeals to authority, citation, or external sources.
