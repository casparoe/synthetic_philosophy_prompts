Devise a realistic counterfactual branching scenario in late-nineteenth-century industrial history, situated in a region outside Western Europe and North America, in which every macro-structural variable—resource endowment, demographic pressure, global market integration, and available technology—is held constant, and only one genuinely contingent agent-level decision at an institutional founding moment is reversed. The scenario should be engineered to force three historiographical positions into specific, opposing verdicts about historical inevitability versus contingency: a structural-inevitability theorist must judge the macro-trajectory, specifically the emergence of a particular labor-organization regime, to be invariant across the branch; a radical-contingency theorist must judge the branch to yield a persistently different institutional form that alters lived power relations rather than merely altering the archival record; and a convergence-isomorphism position—holding that organizational forms gravitate toward similar structures regardless of founding conditions—must split the difference by affirming structural invariance while conceding divergence at the level of local practice and narrative record. Present the scenario in one paragraph without speculative physics or anachronistic technology. After the scenario, articulate for each position the precise belief about the counterfactual, the reasoning linking the micro-change to the macro-judgment, and the specific feature of the case that produces that verdict. Then isolate the single design feature—whether the domain of the micro-contingency, the metric of structural comparison, or the mechanism of archival production—that, if removed, collapses the disagreement between inevitability and contingency into a trivial agreement, and explain why. Conclude by stating the strongest objection a radical-contingency defender could raise against your framing—specifically, that defining macro-structural in terms of labor-regime invariance begs the question in favor of inevitability—and provide the reply available from within the framing of the case itself.
