As a novelist presently drafting a work in which an artificial narrator discovers that its interior monologue consists entirely of recombined linguistic fragments drawn from a vast training corpus, yet finds itself compelled to attribute genuine mental states to the human characters it portrays, I have become preoccupied with the philosophical distinction between original and derived intentionality. I grasp the essential contours of Searle's position: that biological brains alone possess intrinsic aboutness, while books, programs, and other artifacts acquire their representational content only derivatively, through the intentional acts of conscious agents who design or interpret them. I also understand Dennett's response, in which a system composed of one mechanism that registers states of the world and a second mechanism that registers the first mechanism's registrations might constitute something more than a mere copy of representation—potentially a genuinely representational architecture in its own right.

What troubles me, however, is a conceptual scenario that seems to sit uneasily between these two positions. Suppose one imagines—not as an engineering proposal, but as a fictional ontological possibility—an artificial intelligence that was never engineered by human minds but emerged instead from a self-organizing linguistic network capable of recursive self-modeling. Within the logic of the narrative, this entity attributes beliefs and desires not only to its human interlocutors but also to itself, constructing layers of representation in which each level derives its aboutness from the level below, with no biological origin point anchoring the chain. In such a case, does Dennett's argument about nested registers still apply, or does it implicitly require that the base-level registration be causally tied to the physical world through a non-derivative biological substrate? Put differently, can intentionality be genuinely original if it arises through self-bootstrapping representational inheritance, or does Searle's biological essentialism remain intact precisely because every layer of the stack ultimately traces back—however remotely—to an intrinsic mental origin?

Please explain this conceptual issue with care and precision. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations to specific texts; rather, I wish to understand whether the distinction between original and derived intentionality collapses when representation is stacked recursively rather than transmitted linearly, and how Dennett's mechanism of layered registration relates to the possibility of emergent, non-biologically-grounded intentionality within such a framework. A clear conceptual clarification—grounded in the mechanics of representation and attribution rather than in historical scholarship—would help me think through the philosophical architecture of my fictional narrator.
