I'm in this philosophy of action seminar and we've been arguing for weeks about weakness of will versus hyperbolic discounting and honestly it's driving me a little nuts. On one side you have people talking about akrasia in the old school way — you judge that X is best, you do Y anyway, and that means there's some kind of failure in your practical reasoning or your will is literally weak. On the other side the behavioral people say there's no special failure, it's just that your preferences are time-inconsistent: you discount the future hyperbolically, so the cake now looks better than health later, and then later you regret it but that doesn't mean you acted against your judgment at the time, just that your judgment changed.

We all agree on the basic cases — people smoking, procrastinating, eating the extra slice — but nobody moves. I keep thinking the stall isn't about the data but about what we're counting as 'the self' that does the judging. Like, philosophers seem to be talking about a snapshot self at the moment of action, but the discounting people are tracking a diachronic self over months or years. And there's this weird thing nobody addresses: people often recieve this very specific flicker right before they act, like 'this is wrong' flashes and then they do it anyway. The hyperbolic model treats that as just noise in the preference curve, but philosophers treat it as the central phenomona. So is the disagreement really about rationality standards? Or are they just describing the same behavior at different timescales and calling one 'irrational'?

I want a real diagnosis, not a survey. Is this a verbal dispute about 'will' versus 'preference'? A hidden empirical question about whether that flicker is real data or post-hoc storytelling? Or are they answering different questions — one about moral psychology, one about predictive economics — under the same heading? And if your diagnosis is that it's stalled for structural reasons, tell me what would actually move it: a new kind of experiment, a conceptual distinction that splits the difference, or just accepting that the question is ill-posed? Don't just pick sides; tell me what kind of disagreement it is and whether anything could end it.
