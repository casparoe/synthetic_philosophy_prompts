Write a dialogue between two grad students—call them Alex and Jordan—who're arguing about workfare and the whole "undeserving poor" idea. Alex defends strict conditionality and fraud checks: if society is going to support people, there has to be reciprocity, and letting people claim benefits while refusing available work undermines solidarity. Jordan thinks that's based on what she calls "phantom reciprocity"—treating welfare like a wage contract when it's actually a baseline entitlement, and then using that fake contract to punish people whose dependency is visible but whose labor isn't valued by the market anyway. They should actually respond to each other, not talk past each other. Have them discuss a concrete case: a single parent loses benefits after missing a mandatory job-search appointment because of childcare, and whether that shows the system correctly filtering out free-riders or reveals that the "deserving" category is just whatever fits the adminstrative logic. Keep it casual, maybe they're in a campus bar. Don't make either one sound stupid—both should be sharp, and neither should be a strawman.
