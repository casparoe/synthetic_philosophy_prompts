I'm moderating a podcast interview with an unnamed philosopher of biology who defends a strong version of the DNA–Gödel isomorphism. On her view, open-ended self-replication is not merely analogous to formal self-reference but constrained by what she calls "typogenetic closure": any system that copies its own instructions must embed representations that are causally decoupled from their immediate environment, and that decoupling necessarily introduces a Gödelian form of incompleteness into the system's regulatory or predictive capacities. I want eight to ten interview questions that avoid generic biography and instead press the actual content of her position. Please include: a question on whether the isomorphism requires literal Gödel-numbering in molecular processes or only a functional parallel; a question on how she distinguishes this structural claim from standard evolutionary accounts of genetic error-correction and robustness; a question on what kind of synthetic replicator—one that copies instructions without informational decoupling—would count as breaking the analogy; and for each, a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, particularly if she absorbs the objection by appealing to the closure condition. End with a question about whether artificial evolutionary algorithms are therefore subject to the same closure constraints, and what that implies for assessing their predictive reliability. In framing the discussion, please separate what is settled in molecular biology and formal logic from what depends specifically on her interpretation, and do not present the closure condition or the strength of the DNA–Gödel isomorphism as the consensus view.
