Here are my gut calls on whether pleasure and pain can actually be weighed against each other, and I want to know if there's a coherent principle behind them or if I'm just rationalizing after the fact. If I've got a migraine I'll take ibuprofen without hesitation, so apparently mild suffering is totally commensurable with relief. But I also feel like one minute of being burned alive outweighs literally any amount of ice cream or sunshine you could stack on the other side - there's a lexical floor or ceiling or whatever. A life that's 99% blissful except for one hour of torture somewhere in the middle still sounds ruined to me, not just "slightly less net good." Then there's the artist thing: Van Gogh's misery seems like it was worth the paintings, but I wouldn't say the same about a factory worker suffering for cheap sneakers, even though both involve pain traded for output. There's also this low-grade background hum - climate anxiety, nuclear dread, just knowing the world is fragile - that never hits like acute pain but colors everything. Can personal pleasures like a good meal or falling in love ever "pay for" that hum? Some days I think yes, that's just being alive. Other days I think the hum is incommensurable, it contaminates the pleasure from inside. Same with suffering that's embedded in pleasure itself: the melancholy in a beautiful song, or the vulnerability in loving someone. Should we try to eliminate that embedded suffering if we could, or does it belong there? Is there one principle - maybe lexical threshold negative utilitarianism with some tweaks - that captures all these verdicts, or do I have to drop one?
