So I've been reading up on the old Malcolm vs Dennett debate about whether dreams are actual experiences, and it strikes me the whole thing has been stuck in mud since basically the 80s. Malcolm's line is that there's no criterion of correctness for dream reports -- if you say you dreamed you were flying there's no way to check, so it's not a real experience in the sense that seeing is. Dennett comes back and says sure, but that's just because the narrative gets assembled at wake-up; the underlying sleep-state cognition is still experiential, we're just bad at recalling it without confabulation. And then they just talk past eachother for forty years.

My working theory is that this isn't really about dreams at all. I think Malcolm is smuggling in a model where "experience" requires diachronic continuity of the epistemic agent -- like, there has to be a stable self that can correct its own reports over time -- and Dennett is running a functionalist reconstruction model where experience just needs the right information-processing architechture running, regardless of whether there's anyone "home" to verify it. But here's the niche bit: neither of them seems to address the hypnagogic transition state, where the consolidation mechanism is partly online but the narrative generator hasn't fully booted. If you could show that reports made in that window have different error-structures than either pure dream-reports or pure waking reports, wouldn't that break the symmetry?

Anyway, I'm not asking for a survey. I want a diagnosis. Is this a verbal disagreement about what "experience" means? Are they answering different questions -- one about phenomenology, one about cognitive architecture? Is there a hidden empirical question about memory consolidation doing all the work? Or is it bedrock intuition about first-person authority? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move the needle? A neuroimaging result about the transition window, a conceptual distinction between synchronic presence and diachronic unity, or is the question just ill-posed and we should stop asking it?

Also, quick side-note: I keep seeing people cite this as if Dennett "won" but I don't see any consensus in the literature I can access, just people picking sides based on thier prior commitments to functionalism vs. phenomenology. Is that fair?
