I am preparing the question sheet for a recorded colloquium interview with a philosopher who has advanced a specific, somewhat heterodox position at the intersection of moral psychology and metaethics. Rather than treating cross-cultural variation in folk morality as either noise around universal principles or as straightforward evidence for relativism, she argues that the well-documented WEIRD/non-WEIRD divergence—particularly the overrepresentation of harm/care and fairness/individualizing foundations in Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic populations—reflects what she calls a literacy-distortion calibration error. On her account, the cognitive restructuring produced by widespread literacy and formal schooling does not merely expand moral reasoning but systematically overweights individualizing concerns, generating an illusion of objective moral structure that dissolves once we correct for this demographic skew.

Please draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that press her on the actual mechanics of the claim, going well beyond a restatement of the thesis. I want at least one question on how she distinguishes this calibration error from ordinary cultural preference or adaptive specialization; one on whether her view commits her to treating non-literate moral frameworks as epistemically closer to the truth, and if so, how she avoids a romanticized primitivism; one on the problem of near-universal prohibitions—for example, against incest or arbitrary lethal harm—that persist even in societies with very different foundation weightings, asking whether these represent residual tracking or are themselves artifacts of a different kind of distortion; and one that anticipates her likely reply that literacy reveals hidden structural features of morality rather than projecting them, with a sharp follow-up asking what independent evidential standard could adjudicate between revelation and projection without presupposing the very WEIRD framework she is criticizing. End with a question about what empirical finding—perhaps in developmental moral psychology or in cross-cultural experimental philosophy—would actually cause her to abandon or significantly revise the literacy-distortion thesis, rather than allowing her to absorb it as further calibration noise.
