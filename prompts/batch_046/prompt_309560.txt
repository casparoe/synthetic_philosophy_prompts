Write an essay on the competing explanations for the disappearance of widely accepted moral norms—specifically the abolition of chattel slavery and the decline of dueling—focusing on whether such shifts occur primarily through rational moral argument, shifts in material and social interest, or transformations in honor culture. Engage with Kwame Anthony Appiah’s claim that moral revolutions are often driven by changes in what confers honor rather than by the persuasive force of ethical reasoning alone, and with the contrasting view associated with Bernard Williams (the Williams thesis) that normative change tracks deeper shifts in practical reasoning and social interest rather than in symbolic status dynamics. Weigh whether these accounts are genuinely incompatible or whether they operate at different explanatory levels. In particular, address the following tension: if honor dynamics are the primary engine, then the canonical moral arguments against slavery and dueling appear causally redundant, yet we retrospectively treat those arguments as central to the legitimacy of the change; conversely, if argument and interest are primary, then honor risks collapsing into mere epiphenomenal window-dressing on structural transformation. Reach and defend a specific verdict on which factor is explanatorily dominant, or on how they should be ordered, and justify why your resolution does not collapse into an unfalsifiable pluralism.
