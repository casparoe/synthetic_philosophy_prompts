For a graduate seminar on the philosophy of spacetime I have sketched the following package of commitments about Einstein’s hole argument, and I want a rigorous consistency audit before I present it. My commitments are:

(1) I accept the standard technical formulation: apply a smooth diffeomorphism that is the identity outside a matter-free region H and non-trivial inside H to a solution of the Einstein field equations, yielding a second solution that agrees everywhere outside H but assigns different metric values to points inside H.

(2) I reject spacetime substantivalism. Spacetime points possess no primitive identity; they are ontologically dependent on the metric and matter fields—what I take to be a structural-relational ontology.

(3) I hold that determinism in general relativity should be formulated as “structural determinism” rather than “haecceitistic determinism”: the exterior data and the laws fix a unique qualitative relational structure (the metric and matter distribution) inside H, irrespective of which particular points instantiate those relations.

(4) I also maintain that the diffeomorphism invariance revealed by the hole argument is not a mere representational redundancy to be quotiented away, but expresses a genuine ontological indeterminacy: there is no fact of the matter about which point is which inside H.

(5) Within H, where no matter fields are present, I deny that there is any gauge-independent sense in which “the metric at point p” possesses a definite value.

(6) At the same time, I claim that Einstein’s field equations determine a unique metric tensor field g_ab defined on the manifold M, and that this uniqueness should be interpreted literally—not merely “up to diffeomorphism”—if determinism is to be preserved.

(7) Consequently, I propose that we should restore determinism by adopting a canonical coordinate gauge inside H that breaks the diffeomorphism symmetry and assigns definite values to g_ab at each point.

Please examine this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Rank the tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify the cheapest way to restore consistency and what it costs—particularly whether it requires abandoning my structural-relational ontology (2), my claim about ontological indeterminacy (4), or my literalist interpretation of uniqueness (6). Note any unoccupied positions in the logical space—e.g., a view that accepts (2) and (4) but rejects (3)—and briefly diagnose why such combinations are absent (incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored). Finally, indicate which single revision to my commitments would eliminate the greatest number of conflicts, and flag any pair of positions your audit cannot cleanly separate.
