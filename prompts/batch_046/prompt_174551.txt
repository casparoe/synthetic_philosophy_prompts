You facilitate a 90-minute seminar with 12 upper-level philosophy students on obligations to future people. Design an in-class structured deliberation—complete with decision rules, a voting mechanism using non-monetary stakes, and assigned roles if useful—that leads participants to generate the procreation asymmetry themselves: they should consistently reject proposals that would create identifiable, named individuals with low welfare, yet feel no parallel obligation to approve symmetrically structured proposals that would create different, named individuals with high welfare, where the identity of each potential person depends on which proposal is chosen so that the non-identity structure is present but never named. The debrief should map specific moments in their voting onto the asymmetry and onto the person-affecting versus impersonal-reason distinction, should frankly identify which feature—such as whether the asymmetry survives if welfare is replaced by a pluralist or relational good—the rules cannot reproduce and you must supply, and should predict the failure mode that will occur the first time you run it.
