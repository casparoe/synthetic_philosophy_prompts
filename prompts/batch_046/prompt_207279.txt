Specify the complete set of desiderata that a methodology for reconstructing past collective beliefs and institutional rules solely from material traces must satisfy to be adequate, with the conceptual or archaeological case motivating each. The list should cover: how to distinguish functional adaptation from symbolically encoded belief when interpreting artifacts like burial orientations or ceramic motifs; how to avoid the Pompeii-premise error when inferring ritual continuity from stratified deposits; what trace-density or pattern-repetition threshold is required before attributing an institutional rule rather than an isolated practice; and which physical or ethnographic constraints are non-negotiable versus which are camp-specific preferences (e.g., parsimony in mental-state attribution). For each requirement, state whether it is negotiable or hard. Then determine whether demanding both strict underdetermination control and legitimate single-artifact institutional attribution are jointly satisfiable, and if not, which must yield. Conclude with a brief, non-scored indication of how processualist, post-processualist, and cognitive-archaeological approaches align with these criteria. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or specific author attributions; the evaluation must stand on the logic of object-to-institution inference itself.
