I'm planning a single ninety-minute session for a small reading group of public-health graduate students who have no background in political philosophy. I want to teach them the core structure of luck egalitarianism—not as a generic slogan about equality of opportunity, but as a distributive principle that contains an internal tension it cannot resolve on its own. Plan the explanation. First, lay out the dependency order: which conceptual distinction must be firmly in place before brute luck and option luck can be introduced, and why the brute/option distinction collapses unless the audience already grasps a resource-based baseline rather than a welfare-based one. Second, select one carrying example that must involve both a circumstance-sensitive background and a genuinely choice-sensitive outcome; specify exactly what the example needs to include and what it must avoid—particularly examples that confuse risk-taking with negligence or that make the choice dimension look like pure recklessness—so that the tension between compensation and responsibility remains visible rather than resolved. Third, map the misconceptions in the precise order they will arise: I expect they will first conflate luck egalitarianism with meritocracy, then reduce it to simple disability compensation, then interpret it as a denial of all personal responsibility; for each, identify the exact point in the sequence where it should be headed off and the specific distinction or question that defuses it. Fourth, design one check-for-understanding that reveals whether they have grasped the internal tension—specifically, the cutoff problem about where circumstance ends and choice begins—rather than merely repeating the slogan back. Finally, state clearly what must be left out at this level: I do not want them leaving with the impression that any standard refinement solves the cutoff problem, so specify how to acknowledge that such refinements exist without explaining them; also name one other well-known objection (not the expensive-tastes problem) and explain how to bracket it so it does not derail the session, and say openly what you will refuse to address in the remaining time.
