ok so imagine jeremy bentham is alive and someone explains coherent extrapolated volition to him — the idea that an AI should serve not our messy current wants but the preferences we'd hold if we were smarter, knew more, and had ages to think. bentham was all about the feliicific calculus and counting real pleasures, but he also noticed how sympathy and social pressure warp what people say they want. now here's the twist: suppose we run CEV but we strip out every preference that only survives because of status anxiety or copying people we admire, keeping only the ones in reflective equilibrium. do you think bentham would see that as a cleaner version of utility counting, or as smugling in an aristocratic 'ideal self' that throws away the democratic weight of actual suffering? and if he applied it to his own hedonism, would he trust an extrapolated bentham more than the one writing his principles in a drafty study?
