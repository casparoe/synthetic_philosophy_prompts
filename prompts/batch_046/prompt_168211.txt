Please provide a conceptually precise yet intuitive explanation of the paradox of phenomenal judgment within an epiphenomenalist framework—specifically, the challenge that arises when phenomenal properties are causally inert with respect to the physical processes that generate judgments about them. To structure your analysis, use the following constructed scenario: "System M" is a physical system in which neural pathway A produces a qualitative state Q (for instance, a felt warmth) and, through an entirely separate pathway B, produces a linguistic report R ("I feel warmth"). By design, Q exerts no causal influence on pathway B, on pathway A, or on any other physical event in System M. Your task is to articulate the exact conceptual tension between two claims: (i) that R is physically determined and can therefore be systematically true about Q, and (ii) that because Q never enters the causal chain leading to R, there is no available mechanism by which the content of R could be calibrated to, or causally informed by, Q. In doing so, distinguish clearly between the "access problem"—how a purely physical representational system can come to denote a causally isolated phenomenal property—and the "justification problem"—why representations formed in this way should count as knowledge rather than fortuitous correlation. Finally, explain whether resolving the access problem for the epiphenomenalist necessarily resolves the justification problem, or whether a residual gap remains.
