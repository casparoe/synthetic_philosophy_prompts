Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a macro-historical institutionalist and a geographic determinist on the question of why national income levels diverge so sharply across countries. Present it in two parts: first, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including about the formal limits of causal inference from non-experimental history — specifically, the non-random spatial clustering of institutions and geographic endowments, the threat of equifinality in comparative case studies, and what selection-on-observables assumptions imply when countries are the unit of analysis; second, a disagreements table that states each camp’s position in wording its own proponents would accept. In particular, distinguish the institutionalist claim that institutions are the proximate mechanism of growth from the stronger claim that they are exogenous to geography, and distinguish the geographic claim that climate and resource endowments shape development through institutional channels from the claim that they exert direct, institution-independent effects. Identify at least two cruxes — for instance, whether the divergence of economies with initially comparable income levels and geographic conditions but different institutional inheritances, such as the contrasting development paths of Korea and Ghana in the decades after independence, demonstrates institutional causation that geography alone cannot replicate, and whether micro-level natural experiments at borders aggregate reliably to macro-level national outcomes. For each crux, specify the concrete empirical pattern or comparison both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what result each side would take as weakening its own view. Finally, indicate whether any portion of the dispute is verbal — for example, whether disagreements about the definition of an institution versus an adaptation to geography can be dissolved by a neutral redefinition — and if so, propose that redefinition explicitly. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
