Consider the institutional and pedagogical practice by which scientific cultures assign credit and proper names in cases of simultaneous or near-simultaneous discovery. In mathematics, calculus is presented as genuinely co-invented: textbooks preserve both Newton’s fluxional notation and Leibniz’s differential notation, and the field treats the two formalisms as interchangeable expressions of a single inevitable structure. In evolutionary biology, by contrast, the mechanism of natural selection is designated almost exclusively as “Darwin’s theory,” with Alfred Russel Wallace’s 1858 contribution—presented jointly with Darwin at the Linnean Society—regarded as an anticipatory parallel rather than a co-authorship, despite the equivalence of the core insight. What philosophical commitments about discovery, authorship, and the ontology of scientific results does this asymmetrical treatment presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory: does the practice assume that mathematical formalism is a structural feature of reality that any adequate notation must eventually capture, so that individual inventors are merely instrumental conduits, whereas a biological theory is a contingent, synthetic explanatory narrative whose coherence depends on a particular author’s organizing vision? Or does it presuppose a deeper distinction—say, between “discovery of a method” and “discovery of a cause”—and is that distinction defensible? Examine whether this practice is consistent with the same culture’s treatment of other convergences, such as the development of special relativity, where multiple contributors are acknowledged without displacing a canonical name. Finally, ask what would have to change in our conventions of attribution, nomenclature, and pedagogy if we replaced the underlying view with a strict convergence thesis: that mature scientific results are inevitable given available evidence and conceptual resources, so that attaching an individual’s name to a theory is always a misleading form of heroic mythology rather than a meaningful designation of origin.
