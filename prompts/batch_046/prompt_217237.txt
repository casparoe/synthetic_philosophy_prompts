Please argue that the familiar distinction between intent alignment and value alignment—where intent alignment targets what a user literally requested and value alignment targets what the user ought to want—rests on a false separation between descriptive specification and normative commitment. Defend the stronger claim that any sufficiently precise statement of intent already embeds what you might call normative framing: the background judgments that determine which states of affairs count as satisfying the request, which side effects are acceptable, and which interpretations of ambiguity are correct. Show that this framing is not an execution-level problem that can be bracketed, but is present already at the level of specification, so that an agent aligned purely to intent without value alignment would need direct, unmediated access to mental content that is not itself shaped by values. Address the standard counterexample that instrumental intentions—such as a request to prepare coffee—can be separated from substantive moral values, and explain why even such cases require value-laden interpretive choices about resources, risk, and social context that cannot be resolved by closer attention to the user's mental state alone. Develop the argument conceptually from first principles; do not invoke specific named theories, cite authors, or provide bibliographic references.
