The driver knows the mechanics of this arrangement. Is it rational for the hitchhiker to pay the mediator? here is one student's answer (paraphrased form their paper):

In the original case the driver anticipates defection and drives past, since after arrival the hitchhiker has no instrumental reason to pay. Parfit argues that it can be rational to cultivate an irrational disposition — to be the kind of agent who pays even when doing so is not in their immediate interest. In the mediator version, the hitchhiker pays fifty dollars to make the transfer automatic, but the mediator requires that he genuinely believe he'll honor the deal without the contract. This is rational belief-management: he shapes his own credences so that he becomes trustworthy. Because Parfit claims that rational agents can never rationally make themselves irrational, this mediator case disproves him — we can rationally engineer our beliefs to serve long-term goals. Once the contract is sealed, payment happens automatically, so there is no point at which the hitchhiker acts against his interests. Therefore the tension between rationality and irrationality fully dissolves: standard rational choice theory explains everything, and we do not need any mysterious 'disposition to be irrational.' The driver should pick him up because the mediator guarantees payment regardless of the hitchhiker's later preferences.
