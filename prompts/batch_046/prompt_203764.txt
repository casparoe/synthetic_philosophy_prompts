I am a high school student in an advanced philosophy and environmental studies seminar, and I am designing a take-home essay exam for my peers on intergenerational justice and climate change. Rather than asking for a standard summary, I want the question to place students inside a specific conceptual tension: aggressive climate mitigation will alter the conditions of conception and identity for future generations, meaning the individuals who live in a stabilized world are not the same people who would suffer in a degraded one. Given this non-identity structure, harm-based justifications for present sacrifice seem to collapse, since no specific future person can claim to have been harmed by our inaction—those individuals would never have existed otherwise. The exam should ask students to evaluate whether shifting to an impersonal axiology—valuing the quality of lives regardless of who lives them—actually solves this problem, or whether it merely relocates the burden onto a different moral question, such as whether extinction-level risk carries special weight compared to merely suboptimal lives. Please provide a single essay question suited to 45 minutes and approximately 800 words, together with a model answer and a marking scheme. The marking scheme must separate an A response—which must identify both the collapse of person-affecting harm reasoning and the unresolved difficulty in weighing existential risk against welfare quality—from a B response, which addresses only one side. The prompt should not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, since the goal is conceptual reasoning accessible without external research. Finally, please state how confident you are in this framing and explicitly identify the point about which you are least sure, particularly whether the impersonal axiology genuinely resolves the tension or only relocates it.
