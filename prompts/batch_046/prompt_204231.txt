Write the desiderata that any philosophically adequate theory of meaningful work must satisfy in an economy where automation displaces not only manual execution but practical judgment itself—call this deliberative automation—so that human agents are retained only for formal assent rather than for reasoning through outcomes. For each desideratum, provide the concrete case or conceptual tension that motivates it: the theory must distinguish the loss experienced by a worker replaced in physical labor from that experienced by a professional reduced to ratifying algorithmic outputs; it must determine whether work’s normative value derives from task difficulty, from the moral weight of deciding, or from socially conferred recognition of contribution; and it must address the limiting case of universal unconditional provision without collapsing work into a purely instrumental source of income. Identify at least one pair of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable—for example, requiring irreducible human agency in all high-stakes judgment while permitting unconditional algorithmic oversight in those same domains. Briefly, then, assess how a recognition-centered theory and an experience-centered theory (centered on subjective mastery) fare against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria, their conflicts, and the cases behind them rather than on the comparative scoring.
