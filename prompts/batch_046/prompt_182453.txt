In a graduate seminar, two students debated Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism. Lena argued that if occasionalism means only that natural events depend directly on God rather than on metaphysically necessary laws, then it is essentially a theistic variant of Humean regularity: science tracks constant conjunctions, and God provides the ground that stabilizes them. On this reading, Al-Ghazālī's dispute with the philosophers concerns the modal status of connections, not the existence of real causal powers in nature, so occasionalism is fully compatible with scientific practice. Marcus objected that this domesticates Al-Ghazālī. His target was not merely necessary connection but secondary causation altogether: fire possesses no power to burn cotton, and there is no mechanism in created things. God creates every event directly; the observed order reflects only a habitual pattern in divine action, not any causal structure in the world. Lena replied that Al-Ghazālī's own theory of human agency—according to which people acquire acts that God directly creates in them—shows he allows some non-divine involvement, which suggests his denial of causation cannot be absolute. Otherwise moral responsibility collapses, which Al-Ghazālī clearly intends to preserve. Marcus answered that acquisition is not causal efficacy: the human being is the locus or recipient of the act, not its source. This is precisely how occasionalism saves responsibility without granting nature or humans any real causal power. Lena then pressed whether, if all scientific causal language refers only to regular succession guaranteed by divine will, occasionalism makes any empirical difference; Marcus conceded it does not, but insisted this is irrelevant, since occasionalism is a metaphysical claim about what exists, not a predictive theory, and its consequence is that nature is causally inert. They asked me to referee. Please adjudicate their exchange point by point: for each of Lena's and Marcus's moves, indicate whether it is clearly right, partly right, or clearly mistaken, and explain why. Then say who is more right about Al-Ghazālī's actual position, about compatibility with science, and about the consistency of occasionalism with human agency. Also identify what both are missing.
