My reading group has reached an impasse between two camps on whether artistic works can possess genuine aesthetic excellence while being morally corrupt in ways that go beyond the artist's biography—specifically, through what I will call an "immanent ethical architecture," by which I mean formal structures embedded in the work itself (narrative framing, compositional hierarchy, or representational logic) that systematically distort human agency or suffering. One side, the separatists, holds that aesthetic value is autonomous and can be assessed by bracketing these moral properties; the other, the integrationists, holds that when such structural corruption is present, it necessarily diminishes the work's artistic achievement rather than sitting alongside it. Draft the joint statement that these two sides should produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: first, the claims both can sign about how artistic form relates to ethical content, including acknowledgment that works with immanent ethical architecture do exist; second, the precise formulations of their residual disagreement, each phrased in wording its own side would accept; third, the cruxes—particularly whether "immanent ethical architecture" is a coherent descriptive category or a category error, and whether aesthetic autonomy requires the possibility of fully bracketing a work's representational commitments; and fourth, for each crux, one concrete case or constructed thought experiment both sides agree bears on the question, with each side's predicted assessment recorded in advance. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the document should be readable as a standalone text that either party could sign except for the clearly demarcated disagreements. I am particularly interested in whether part of their dispute turns out to be verbal once the concept of immanent ethical architecture is made precise, and if so, which part and why.
