I keep seeing this pattern in medical and policy debates. Someone says that if you can learn something for free before making a choice, you should always learn it, since knowing more can't hurt you if you think straight about the odds. But then people point to cases where learning a fact doesn't open up new options -- like finding out you carry a gene variant that doctors can't treat -- and yet people say the knowledge itself is damaging because it makes the patient care about different things or feel pressure they didn't feel before, almost like it rewires their prefferences. So the standard argument assumes knowledge only changes your beliefs about outcomes, but in these cases it seems to change what outcomes you value, or how you wiegh them.
