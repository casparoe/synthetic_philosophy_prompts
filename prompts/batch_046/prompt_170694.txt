Two mates of mine got in to it at the pub over whether history is actually locked in or if things would have turned out the same anyway. (1) The first reckoned path dependence is overblown: given long enough, functional selection sorts out bad institutions, so even if some random event hadn't kicked off the industrial revolution in Britain, similar conditions elsewhere would've produced comparable outcomes eventually. (2) The second pushed back that this ignores the 'small cause, big effect' cases -- like how the layout of typewriter keys got frozen not because it was optimal but because of coordination costs, and now we're stuck with it despite better alternatives existing. (3) The first said that's not true lock-in, it's just local equilibrium; if Dvorak had genuinely huge gains, market forces or state mandates would've switched us over by now, so the fact we haven't switched proves the gains were never big enough to matter. (4) The second replied that he's defining path dependence away: the point isn't that we're stuck with something globally terrible, it's that the specific form of our technology depends on arbitary historical sequence, and asking 'would we have got here anyway' misses that 'here' includes all those arbitary features. (5) Then the first said the whole framing is dodgy because in complex adaptive systems counterfactuals don't have stable outcomes -- you can't rerun history with one change and expect everything else to stay fixed, so asking whether 'the same outcomes' would've arrived is conceptually muddled.
