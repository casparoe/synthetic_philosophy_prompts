I am writing the final exam for an upper-level course in democratic theory, and I need an essay question on populism and the idea of "the will of the people" that cannot be answered by reciting the standard Rousseau-versus-Schmitt framing or listing familiar criticisms of populist rhetoric. I want students to grapple with a specific conceptual tension: populist movements claim to express a singular, homogeneous, pre-political will belonging to "the people," yet they necessarily rely on mediated mechanisms—such as sortition-selected citizens' assemblies, digital deliberative platforms, or plebiscitary referenda—to identify and articulate that will. The question should ask students to determine whether these mechanisms are best understood as instrumental (merely discovering a will that exists independently of them) or constitutive (actively producing the unity and homogeneity they purport to represent), and to assess whether the populist commitment to anti-pluralism survives if the constitutive reading is correct—specifically, whether it collapses into a performative contradiction in which the people are invoked as prior to politics precisely through a politically constructed procedure. Please provide the exam question formatted for a 75-minute essay, a model answer of approximately 700 words, and a marking scheme that distinguishes an A-level response from a B-level response by naming the specific conceptual moves required—above all, the clear separation of epistemic from ontological mediation, the argument that constitutive mediation undermines the claim to a pre-political people, and the recognition that instrumentalism faces its own problem of explaining how a homogeneous will could exist prior to the procedures that reveal disagreement. The response should begin with its bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
