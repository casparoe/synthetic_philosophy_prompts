ok so i'm trying to map out where i actually stand on descartes and animals for a phil of mind discussion group, and i want to check if i'm talking out of both sides of my mouth. here's my package: (1) i take descartes literally on the bête-machine thing — animals are just fancy clockwork, zero phenomenal consciousness, not even pain, nothing it is like to be a bat or a dog. (2) but i think the real engine behind his view isn't just the absence of language in animals but a metaphysical argument about the soul being indivisible and immaterial, so only beings with that substance can have unified experience. (3) i also think modern neuroscience mostly backs up the mechanistic side: predicitive coding, reflex arcs, all that can explain animal behavour without invoking any cartesian soul. (4) at the same time i want to say animals aren't pure reflex machines — they have these integrated sensory-motor representations that guide adaptive behaviour in ways too complex to reduce to simple clockwork, even though none of it is conscious. (5) i think this middle view is actually what descartes should have held, and it's compatible with dualism because dualism only rules out rational souls in animals, not internal integration. (6) finally, evolutionary continuity in brain mechanisms between us and them doesn't threaten descartes' core claim, since continuity in machinery doesn't mean continuity in substance.

do these hang together? specifically i'm worried that (1) and (4) pull in different directions — if animals have rich internal representations, doesn't that undermine the zero inner life claim? but maybe that's only an apparent tension. the deeper worry is whether (2) can survive alongside (3) and (6): if neuroscience explains human cognition mechanistically too, then the immaterial soul starts looking redundant even for us, not just for animals. and there's something weird about saying in (5) that descartes should have adopted my middle view while also insisting in (1) that he was strictly bête-machine. can you rank the tensions by severity, say which ones dissolve on closer look, and tell me the cheapest single revision that would clean up the mess? i don't want to drop (3) if i can help it since that feels like the empirically grounded part.
