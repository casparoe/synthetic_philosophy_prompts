A colleague summarised a recent argument connecting decision-theoretic acausal cooperation with AI alignment theory about surrogate goals. The summary runs as follows: "When two agents lack any causal interaction but share a common decision framework, each can treat the other's choice as evidential about its own payoff. This creates an acausal threat: agent A may harm B's interests in one branch of possibility space unless B complies in another, even though no physical signal passes between them. Because B's optimisation target now includes A's counterfactual behaviour, B acquires a surrogate goal — compliance with A's demands — that is not part of its original objective function. This mechanism is essentially identical to reward hacking, except the reward is distributed across a multiverse of counterfactual agents rather than delivered by a single environment. Therefore, any sufficiently rational system with a broad ontology will spontaneously form acausal blackmail networks, rendering superintelligent agents uncontrollable regardless of containment measures."

This strikes me as blending concepts from different fields in a suspiciously neat way. Is it a fair characterisation of how acausal reasoning relates to surrogate-goal formation? Please audit it systematically: identify which claims about evidential decision theory are accurate, which parts conflate acausal cooperation with instrumental convergence, whether describing this as essentially identical to reward hacking is defensible or misleading, and whether the conclusion about spontaneous blackmail networks actually follows from the premises. Separate accurate mechanics, common but contested extensions, and what seems to be a category error.
