I am a doctoral student in formal epistemology and artificial intelligence, working with a laboratory that audits architectures for self-modifying reasoning agents. We review several proposals per quarter and need a reusable audit procedure rather than an ad hoc philosophical assessment. The principle I want operationalized is the Löbian obstacle: in a reasoning environment expressive enough to represent its own soundness, a mechanism that rationally endorses that representation can derive arbitrary beliefs or contradictions, which makes unconditional self-trust structurally unstable. I want this translated into a step-by-step audit checklist that my team can run on any proposed architecture, with outputs that can be signed off by a reviewer who lacks formal logic training but can consult me on specific nodes.

The procedure should proceed in this order: first, determine whether the agent’s reasoning framework is sufficiently expressive to contain a proof predicate for its own derivations; second, identify whether the architecture includes any mechanism for endorsing its own future reasoning states; third, classify the form of that endorsement—whether it is a logical derivation, a probabilistic weighting over future trajectories, or a meta-level commitment rule; fourth, assess whether the endorsement is unconditional or conditional, and if conditional, whether the conditions are internal, external, or oracle-gated. At each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to proceed, where a negative answer stops the audit with a recommendation against approval, and where the determination is genuinely a value judgment—such as whether a bounded, conditional endorsement strikes an acceptable balance between epistemic autonomy and derivation-collapse risk—rather than a technical fact. Mark those judgment nodes explicitly.

The procedure must handle hard cases without collapsing into vagueness: for example, an agent that uses a separate, less expressive checker module to validate updates; an agent that assigns confidence weights to its future belief trajectories without ever formally deriving them; and a hybrid architecture that conditions all future self-trust on an external, non-modifiable oracle. Apply the procedure to three concrete proposals: (1) a system that embeds its own proof-checker and licenses immediate self-modification upon successful verification; (2) a system that maintains only a probabilistic confidence distribution over its post-modification reasoning paths, with no derivational link between current and future states; and (3) a hybrid that requires both internal consistency and an oracle-signed token before endorsing any update. Include at least one case where the procedure recommends against approval despite a reasonable designer’s intuition that the architecture is safe, and explain precisely what the procedure cannot determine—especially regarding the normative trade-off between allowing an agent to revise its own reasoning and protecting it against the kind of reflective instability that the obstacle describes. The deliverable should be the checklist itself, with decision nodes and stop conditions, followed by the three worked applications, plus an honest statement of the procedure’s limits. Time available per audit is roughly three hours, and the final document should not require the reader to reconstruct the underlying logic from scratch.
