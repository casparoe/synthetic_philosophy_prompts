I'm drafting a research agenda for a small working group that takes coherence arguments for expected utility maximization seriously, but specifically in the setting of artificial agents that may revise their own decision procedures over time. I don't want a survey of Dutch-book or money-pump arguments; I want to know which open conceptual problems in this space are genuinely upstream—meaning that resolving them would change how we think about rationality for self-modifying systems—and which are solved, downstream, or largely orthogonal.

Here are four lines I'm currently weighing: first, whether standard coherence arguments survive realistic failures of logical omniscience in agents that cannot compute all logical consequences of their beliefs; second, whether diachronic coherence requires a fixed utility function, or whether preference change can be structured so the agent remains unexploitable without locking itself into a static objective; third, how coherence arguments interact with updateless decision rules of the kind discussed in agent-foundations work, versus standard Bayesian updating, particularly when the agent can anticipate altering its own update mechanism; and fourth, whether there exists a non-circular coherence justification for the independence axiom that does not already presuppose the probabilistic reasoning it aims to ground.

But I want more than a catalog. Give me a defended prioritization across these four and any others you think belong: for each, why it is tractable for philosophers and formal theorists rather than empirical machine-learning researchers; what specific result, failure mode, or formal result over the next few years would cause you to revise its priority upward or downward; and which one or two you would actively deprioritize on the grounds that resolving them wouldn't shift the broader framework for self-modifying agents. Please make the criteria explicit—upstream dependence, tractability, and relevance to agents that can change their own reasoning—rather than simply declaring importance.

Finally, are there open questions I'm not listing that should displace one or more of these four, given this specific focus on coherence for EU maximization under self-modification? If so, state them precisely and map them into the dependency structure: which depend on which, and which are prerequisites for making progress on the others? I want an agenda, not a reading list, and I'm especially interested in where the coherence literature implicitly assumes static agents and whether fixing that assumption collapses some standard arguments or opens new ones.
