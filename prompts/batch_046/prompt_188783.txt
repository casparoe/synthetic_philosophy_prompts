In our graduate ontology seminar, Professor Vasquez has issued the following verdicts on puzzles of material constitution. She holds that a bronze statue and the bronze lump that initially makes it up are numerically distinct, and that melting the bronze into an amorphous mass destroys the statue while the bronze survives. She allows that a statue can survive the gradual, atom-by-atom replacement of its original substance with synthetically identical analogues, so long as artistic form and causal continuity are preserved. She denies survival in the scan-and-reconstruct case: if a statue is digitally scanned, destroyed, and then rebuilt elsewhere from different matter according to the scan, the result is a perfect replica, not the original. She rules that splitting a statue in two and rebuilding each half into a complete statue destroys the original and produces two new ones. Finally, she judges that a marble statue melted into liquid water and then refrozen in the identical mold minutes later is not the original statue but a replacement, despite the restoration of form and location.

Consider now a new case. A sculptor fabricates a statue from an engineered ceramic embedded with distributed microprocessors that continuously regulate its molecular lattice. In a storm, the ceramic is dispersed into an unshaped gel across several rooms; the embedded system remains fully operational, tracking all particles, and over several hours the material autonomously reconverges and re-solidifies into the precise original shape in the original gallery, though some original particles are lost and some ambient ones are incorporated. No external scanning or reconstruction occurs; the causal mediation is entirely internal to the dispersed system.

What should Professor Vasquez decide if she wishes to stay consistent with her track record? Identify the closest analog among her prior rulings. Then lay out at least two competing principles that could jointly explain her earlier verdicts—one that makes continuous spatiotemporal arrangement in the artistic form essential to survival, and one that makes the uninterrupted causal-functional system that produces and maintains that form essential. For each principle, state its prediction about the smart-ceramic case. Give your best guess at her actual verdict, with explicit reasons for preferring the principle you think governs her thinking, and state honestly what metaphysical commitment she adopts with each choice. Lastly, specify one further hypothetical scenario, different in kind from the cases above, that would genuinely adjudicate between these two principles rather than merely repeating the same disagreement with new materials.
