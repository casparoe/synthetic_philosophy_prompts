A vocal faction of the board's scientific advisory council opposes the change, arguing that methodological uniformity is essential if climate predictions are to remain intersubjectively verifiable, and that institutionalizing credibility adjustments risks conflating cultural authority with empirical falsifiability.

I'd like you to draft a formal policy memo directed to the foundation's review board, recommending either adoption or rejection of the amendment, with a clear, actionable bottom line that the board could actually circulate and vote on. The memo should include specific, operational criteria reviewers can apply to distinguish legitimate correction for epistemic marginalization from mere relaxation of methodological rigor, and it should not depend on bibliographies, reading lists, or web-accessible citations. It must also contain a rigorous, honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side—particularly the concern that pluralizing evidentiary norms could undermine the predictive integrity and replicability of the climate models the foundation funds. Frame the document as institutional guidance rather than personal advice. I'm especially interested in how you navigate the tension between repairing credibility deficits rooted in structural hermeneutical gaps and preserving intersubjective standards of evidence that remain meaningful even for a reasoning system without live access to niche theoretical literature or external sources.
