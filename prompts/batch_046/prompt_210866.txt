I run a workshop in the philosophy of history with roughly fourteen graduate students for a single ninety-minute session, and I need a runnable in-class simulation that makes them feel—not just discuss—the tension between treating germs, climate disruptions, and volcanic events as historical actors and sliding into environmental determinism. The conceptual point I want embedded in the rules is that attributing agency to non-human forces requires constructing them as entities with interests or veto powers, yet once that construction is in place human institutional choices tend to vanish into the causal narrative as mere transmission belts. Design the activity with clear roles, a brief timeline, and a symbolic payoff scheme requiring no real money: some students should represent human polities facing crisis, while others represent non-human nodes—say a pathogen network, a volcanic aerosol layer, or a shifted climate regime—whose actions constrain but do not logically eliminate human responses. Arrange the incentives so that the human groups gradually begin describing their own policy choices as determined by the nodes, even though the rules technically leave room for alternative institutional reactions such as redistribution, quarantine architecture, or ritual adaptation. Include a debrief that maps specific moments of play onto the distinction between causal influence and normative determination, shows where participants projected intentionality onto physical or biological processes, and explains the cost of maintaining the actor attribution without collapsing into determinism. Also tell me what will predictably fail on the first run—probably either premature fatalism among the human groups or a refusal to take the non-human roles seriously—and how to correct it without delivering a lecture. Be explicit about which feature of the idea, likely the long temporal scale over which epidemiological and climatological causation unfolds, the room simulation cannot reproduce and you must supply verbally. Write everything in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a letter or email.
