So I'm taking a course on pluralism and two people in my study group, Amir and Chloe, got into it over lunch about our university's new funding rule for cultural student orgs. The rule says any club that gets school money has to allow anyone to join, regardless of background. (1) Amir said that's the classic Anglo-American multicultural compromise: groups keep their identity but can't wall themselves off, which stops balkanization and forces real cross-cultural contact. (2) Chloe said that's assimilation wearing multicultural makeup -- by forbidding exclusive membership, the school forces every cultural group to stay legible and open to the dominant culture, which destroys the internal solidarity and private meaning that actually makes minority culture worth preserving. (3) Amir came back that French laïcité is way worse because it demands people leave culture at the door entirely; at least this policy lets groups exist, just on condition of openness, which is a fair middle ground. (4) Chloe said comparing it to laïcité misses the point entirely -- laïcité is at least honest about wanting one unified public sphere, whereas this pretends to celebrate difference while structurally diluting it. She said if the point is actually preservation and not just "integation theater" for the majority's consumption, then exclusive membership should be allowed.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Say who's right about what specific point, what's missing from both sides, and whether the disagreement is mostly empirical or about which value should win. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
