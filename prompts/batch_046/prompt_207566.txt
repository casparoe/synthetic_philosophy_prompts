At a faculty workshop, two colleagues—one in moral psychology and the other in philosophy of law—debated whether Dennett's compatibilist defense of "elbow room" can genuinely ground just deserts, or whether Caruso's public-health model of punishment avoids retributivism only in name. Their exchange ran as follows.

(1) The first argued that Dennett's conditions for free will—rational deliberation, responsiveness to reasons, and self-control—are precisely the capacities that make punishment deserved rather than merely instrumentally useful. Without them we treat agents like natural disasters; with them, reactive attitudes are fitting, and fitting reactive attitudes just are the ground of desert.

(2) The second replied that Dennett mistakes practical control for ultimate sourcehood. Even full elbow room is embedded in a causal chain; basic desert requires that the agent be the originator of the act, not merely a well-functioning node within it. Dennett gives us conditions adequate for forward-looking practices—deterrence, rehabilitation, risk management—but not for backward-looking blame.

(3) The first countered that Caruso's own quarantine model cannot avoid distinguishing intentional wrongdoers from accidental harm-doers, and that this distinction tracks blameworthiness rather than pure risk. If remorse lowers risk and justifies earlier release, that is only because remorse correlates with desert; otherwise risk alone should govern the duration of detention.

(4) The second answered that the distinction is purely predictive: intentional harm generates different risk profiles than accidental harm, and remorse is a reliable indicator of lowered recidivism. Any overlap with desert is correlation, not constitution. To treat risk-correlation as hidden retributivism confuses the instrument with its justification.

Please adjudicate this exchange move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Then say who is more right overall about whether Dennett's framework can support just deserts without collapsing into Caruso's quarantine logic, and identify one important consideration—perhaps concerning institutional legitimacy or the phenomenology of blame—that both speakers neglect. Write your response in American English.
