I'd like a consistency audit of my views on capital punishment before I present them in a seminar. Here are the commitments I've arrived at:

1. Retributivism is the correct framework: punishment must be proportionate to culpability, and for the worst premeditated homicides only execution is proportionate—life without parole is a moral understatement.
2. The risk of executing an innocent person is structurally unavoidable in any capital system; procedural safeguards can lower the rate but cannot eliminate fallibility inherent in human judgment.
3. In a particular case where guilt is epistemically certain—say, with direct DNA, unambiguous video, and fully corroborated confessions—the structural-risk objection does not apply to that specific execution, so if retributive proportionality demands death, it should proceed.
4. Punishment also carries an expressive function: the state must be able to register the unique gravity of certain acts, and imprisonment, however permanent, cannot communicate that same categorical condemnation.
5. Justice is agent-relative: the state's duty to each defendant is to respond to that defendant's own culpability, not to calibrate punishment by statistical properties of the system as a whole.
6. I reject deterrence-based justifications entirely; empirical debates about whether execution deters are irrelevant to whether it is morally warranted.
7. I also think that maintaining a capital system generates a kind of institutional moral luck: every execution within that system is partly tainted by the system's unavoidable future or past errors, even when this particular defendant is certainly guilty.

Please audit these for joint tenability. Structure your response as follows:

- Tensions by severity (about 350 words): Rank the conflicts from most damaging to least. For each genuine tension, identify the pair of commitments that pull against each other, explain the incompatibility precisely, and state the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what it costs.
- Apparent tensions that dissolve (about 150 words): Identify at least one pair that looks incompatible but is actually compatible once the right distinction is drawn; explain why the tension is only superficial.
- Overall verdict and recommended revision (about 150 words): Say whether the package is salvageable as is, and identify the single revision that would clean up the most problems—specify which commitment should be modified and how.

Be direct and don't lecture me on first principles; I want to know what my own commitments commit me to.
