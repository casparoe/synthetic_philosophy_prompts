Write an essay addressing the following controversy. The classic aggregation dilemma contrasts one person enduring fifty years of torture with an astronomically large population each suffering only a barely perceptible dust speck in the eye. Rather than framing this solely as a conflict between total utilitarianism and threshold deontology, consider a modified variant: the distributed harm is not physical discomfort but an imperceptible, irreversible micro-alteration to each victim’s capacity for aesthetic judgement. No individual detects any loss, yet collectively the population undergoes a vast, diffuse erosion of a shared cultural faculty. Weigh whether this distributed aesthetic corruption ought to be aggregated against the single severe harm, drawing on both preference-satisfaction and objective-list theories of well-being. Reach and justify a definite conclusion: does this variant strengthen, weaken, or leave unchanged the case that innumerable tiny harms can collectively outweigh one catastrophic harm?
