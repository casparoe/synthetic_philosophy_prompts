I am preparing for a philosophy-of-mind seminar that examines the Gödelian analogy in Douglas Hofstadter’s Gödel, Escher, Bach—specifically the inferential move from self-referential arithmetic formulas to the claim that conscious minds are best understood as self-aware “strange loops”. Rather than asking for a synopsis of Hofstadter’s argument, I want a rigorous specification of what any adequate philosophical theory of this analogy must deliver if it is genuinely to explain, rather than merely illustrate, the relation between formal self-reference and mental self-awareness.

Please set out the full list of desiderata. For each requirement, identify the phenomenon or conceptual distinction it must preserve—such as the gap between syntactic self-reference (a formula that contains its own Gödel number within its syntactic structure) and semantic self-reference (a system that interprets a symbol as denoting itself)—and explain why collapsing that gap would weaken the analogy. I want particular attention paid to a third dimension that Hofstadter tends to elide: what might be called operational self-reference, in which a system’s causal dynamics are continuously coupled to representations of its own states, so that the self-model is not merely a static symbol but part of an ongoing feedback loop. Must an adequate theory require the analogy to hold at this operational level, or is it sufficient that it hold only at the representational level? For each desideratum, indicate whether it is non-negotiable for any theory that aims to bridge mathematics and mind, or whether it reflects only the preference of a particular camp (for example, those who insist on determinism, on emergent properties, or on the irreducibility of first-person access). Then address whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—perhaps because preserving both the syntax-semantics distinction and the operational-coupling condition conflicts with fixed-point results in formal systems, or because demanding empirical equivalence with cognitive science rules out purely formal analogies. Finally, tell me which single desideratum does the most damage to the popular “strange loop” interpretation, and keep the emphasis on the criteria themselves rather than on scoring rival theories, though a brief assessment of how Hofstadter’s own account fares against the list is welcome. Please provide your answer in British English.
