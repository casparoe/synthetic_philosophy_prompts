I'm delivering the dedication address for a hospital wing named after Dr. Elena Vasquez, who spent thirty years providing surgical care in conflict zones on unpaid leave. The audience is split between bioethicists and clinicians who will catch every philosophical reference, and family and local donors who will not. The hospital has recently adopted an ethical service expectation that treats extended humanitarian deployment as a baseline factor in promotion, effectively raising the floor of professional obligation. Write a ten-to-twelve-minute address that honors Vasquez's choices as genuinely supererogatory—without implying that colleagues who never left the ward failed a duty, and without letting the institution use her legacy to justify the new baseline—making the philosophical tension between praising what exceeds obligation and avoiding a moral remainder for ordinary practice the structural spine of the speech, and ensuring both audiences leave understanding exactly what is at stake.
