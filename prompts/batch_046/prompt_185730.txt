Write the complete set of desiderata that a cosmological theory must meet in order to genuinely resolve—rather than merely displace—the Boltzmann brain problem. For each desideratum, provide the specific phenomenon or observation that motivates it: for instance, the requirement that the theory reproduce the observed large-scale structure and cosmic microwave background, the requirement that it not make our own ordered cognitive states statistically atypical relative to randomly fluctuated observers, and the requirement that it preserve a coherent distinction between evolutionary histories and thermal fluctuations without invoking an ad hoc temporal cutoff. Classify each desideratum as either non-negotiable or as a camp-specific preference (for example, demands for determinism, for the absence of a multiverse, or for strict adherence to standard thermodynamic arrow-of-time explanations). Then examine whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable in light of current statistical-mechanical or cosmological no-go results; if a tension exists, identify the precise conflict and explain why it matters. As an additional constraint, introduce and defend a non-standard criterion—designate it the archival-continuity condition—which requires the theory to account not merely for the existence of ordered observers but for the cross-generational sequential coherence of scientific records and technological artifacts; discuss whether this condition strengthens the argument against fluctuation cosmologies or instead introduces a new theoretical vulnerability. Keep the emphasis on the criteria, their interactions, and any internal contradictions; if you briefly reference how eternal-inflation or cyclic-model frameworks fare against your list, do so only in passing. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for further sources.
