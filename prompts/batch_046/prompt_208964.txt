I'm working on a piece about land-use law and I keep running into this assumption: zoning codes and property doctrines treat permanent settlement and improvement of land as the default human condition, while mobile foraging gets classed as non-use or wilderness. Given the agricultural-revolution-was-a-mistake debate — Sahlins' original affluance thesis, the data that foragers often worked fewer hours and ate more varied — what philosphical commitments does that framing presuppose? Does it quietly assume sedentism equals progress, or that labor-time is the right metric for value, or that collective welfare in the wild just doesn't count? And if we actually bought the hunter-gatherer affluance thesis, which parts of property law — not just indigenous rights, but the basic improvement doctrine — would have to be rebuilt?
