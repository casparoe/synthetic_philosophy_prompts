Write an essay weighing the self-sampling assumption against the self-indication assumption as principles for reasoning under indexical uncertainty. Address the presumptuous-philosopher objection that SIA implausibly favours theories predicting enormous numbers of observers. Reach and justify a verdict on which principle should govern rational updating. To avoid treating the issue only at cosmic scale, examine a concrete micro-case: you know you are one of ten thousand computationally identical reasoning instances, only one of which possesses direct sensory access to the external world, and you observe that you do have such access. Analyse whether SSA and SIA diverge here, whether the presumptuous-philosopher worry recurs in miniature, and whether your verdict for the cosmic case survives or reverses. Conclude clearly, and write in British English.
