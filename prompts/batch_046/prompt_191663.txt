Write a dialogue between Mara and Juno, two friends arguing over coffee about whether the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem is a real problem for actual groups or just a mathematical ghost. Mara thinks any non-dictatorial, surjective rule with universal domain is broken by design, since somebody can always lie about preferences and gain. She keeps pointing to a made-up "band-ranking" budget system for their shared vacation fund, where people slot activities into tiers and a peer-review tie-breaker handles ties. Juno is skeptical—she says real-world groups have clustered preferences, incomplete information, and bounded rationality, so the universal-domain condition never holds and people don't acutally compute optimal misreports. They need real back-and-forth: Mara should press Juno on whether it's okay to ignore strategic risk just because lying is hard, and Juno should push back that Mara treats every strategic move as corruption rather than as noisy intensity-revealing behavior. Keep it casual, no lecture mode, and make sure neither sounds like a strawman.
