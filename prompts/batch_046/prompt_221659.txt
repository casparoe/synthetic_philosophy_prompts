I'm working on a novel with parallel timelines, and I keep hitting the same wall with my beta readers that I don't have a word for. There's this character Elias — in the main story he's a doctor who saves lives but totally burns out. In one branch he never went to med school and became a musician instead; in another he went, but during one crisis he made a different call and lost his license. The readers keep fighting over whether the musician is still "the same Elias" or just a seperate person who happens to carry his memories and looks like him. Some say if he remembers being a doctor in dreams, he's the real guy living a different life; others say once he makes that one different choice at the crucial moment, he's a copy with stolen memories, not the same person at all. I feel like philosophers must have argued about what makes someone stay the same person when everything around them changes, but I don't know the actual terms and I definately don't want to guess and build three hundred pages on a mistake.

Is there an established concept that draws the exact line between "same person, different path" and "different person who shares a past"? How should I think about the case where memories stay intact but choices diverge? And if there's no clean name for this exact split, could you define one with real boundary cases — like, where does it break? Also please push back hard if my framing is confused or if I'm asking for something that doesn't make sense. Answer in American English.
