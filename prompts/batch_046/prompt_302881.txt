Write a sonnet in iambic pentameter addressing the Hart-Fuller debate on whether law and morality are conceptually separate. Avoid the familiar historical illustration of authoritarian regimes; instead, build the octave around a single hypothetical statute—enacted through every correct procedural channel of a democratic legislature, yet retroactive, secret, contradictory, and impossible for citizens to obey. In the octave, articulate Hart's separation thesis: that procedural pedigree through rules of recognition makes the statute valid law regardless of its moral failures or practical impossibility, and that a system of primary and secondary rules need not embed moral criteria. At line 9, execute a volta in which Fuller's response enters not as an external moral judgment but as an internal structural analysis: show that retroactivity, secrecy, contradiction, and impossibility are not merely unjust but defeat the very conditions that distinguish a legal rule from a naked sovereign command. In the sestet, develop Fuller's principles of legality—generality, promulgation, non-retroactivity, clarity, consistency, feasibility, stability, and congruence between declared rule and official action—as prerequisites of law's own coherence rather than imported moral ideals. The closing couplet should preserve the debate's unresolved tension, asking whether a directive violating all these conditions can still be called a rule of law, or whether Hart's thesis must concede that validity already contains a minimal morality of form. Use the rhyme scheme ABAB CDCD EFEF GG so that paired sounds mirror conceptual couplings the debate forces together and then pulls apart; you need not force every rhyme to carry meaning, but the form should feel like an argument that turns and closes without resolving. The poem must be accurate enough that a reader of legal philosophy recognizes the specific conceptual moves—separation thesis, rules of recognition, internal morality, and the conditions of legality—while remaining a coherent poem for someone who has never encountered the debate.
