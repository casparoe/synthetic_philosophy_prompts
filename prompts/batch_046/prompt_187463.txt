Start with your standard counterfactual mugging: Omega predicts you'll refuse the $5, then tortures someone in the branch where you said no. Now layer on an XOR structure. Omega runs two separate predictions—call them sim A and sim B—each with its own victim and its own $5 price tag. Then Omega tells you: "I'm going to actualize exactly one harm in the real world, not in a sim, and I'll pick which one based on which refusal I predict. Pay me once, and I drop both predictions. Refuse, and I randomly actualize the harm from whichever branch I think you'd fail."

The intution pump that confuses me is whether paying here feels like you're covering both counterfactual worlds with one causal action, or whether the XOR gate means you're just buying off one randomly-selected harm while the other counterfactual victim is still "out there" in an unactualized branch. People seem to flip between treating it like double counterfactual mugging and treating it like standard XOR blackmail where you only need one shield. So is this configuration named? Does it collapse back into one of the two parent concepts, or does the interaction between the counterfactual prediction layer and the XOR selection layer break something? If there's no term, what's the cleanest way to carve it—by the prediction mechanism, by the XOR gate, or by the fact that the harm moves between counterfactual and acutal worlds depending on the predictor's choice?
