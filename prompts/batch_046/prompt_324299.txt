I recently came across a popular essay that summarizes the exchange between Dennett and Searle on derived versus original intentionality in the following way. Dennett’s “two-bitser”—a minimal intentional system that distinguishes only two environmental conditions—demonstrates that even very simple mechanisms can be interpreted as bearing beliefs and desires when they reliably track those conditions. The essay claims that Dennett then argues that if such a system acquires recursive self-monitoring capacities—representing not merely the world but its own representational states—it effectively bootstraps itself from derived to original intentionality, because its representations are no longer assigned by an outside interpreter but are internally self-validated through what the essay calls “meta-bitser loops.” On this reading, the two-bitser’s aboutness becomes intrinsic rather than observer-relative. The essay concludes that this collapses Searle’s Chinese Room objection: the room is essentially a vast network of two-bitser-style symbol manipulations, and if recursive self-monitoring confers original intentionality, then the human agent inside—who participates in such loops—must also possess original intentionality, undermining Searle’s claim that syntax is never sufficient for semantics.
