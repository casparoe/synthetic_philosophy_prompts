hey so im running a small grad reading group on archives and power — were finishing up the semester and everyones a little burnt out. i want to end the session with a poem, maybe 20-24 lines, divided into four stanzas, that we can read aloud and then sit with for a minute. the idea i want it to carry is this weird hybrid i came up with: treating the archive like Davidson's radical interpretation problem, where youre trying to understand a speaker whose language you dont share, except here the speaker is dead and half the utterances are physically destroyed or never recorded in the first place. so the principle of charity becomes dangerous — youre not just interpreting, youre filling gaps with coherence that serves whoever kept the records.

for the form: each stanza should have exactly one line thats just blank space or an em dash, and the blank should move — first stanza first line, second stanza middle, third stanza near the end, fourth stanza last line. also repeat four words across all stanzas: record, charity, burn, and voice, so the repetition tracks whats preserved versus whats erased. dont make it preachy about intervention or giving voice to the voiceless — i hate that. the tension should be structural: the interpreters charity is necessary to understand anything, but every act of charity is also an act of power over the silence. someone who knows Davidson should recognize the charity principle working in a weird context, and someone who doesnt should still feel the poems logic. the group is mostly historians and one philosopher of language, so it should land for both. no citations needed, obviously — just write it.
