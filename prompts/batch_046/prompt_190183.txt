As the instructor for this term’s graduate seminar on Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence and Value Theory, I am asking you to evaluate a response from the midterm examination. The exam presented the following problem: Consider the proposal that an AI system can be constrained by an impact measure—defined as the divergence between the actual world-state trajectory and a counterfactual trajectory in which the AI takes no action—without requiring a full axiological specification. Evaluate whether this strategy genuinely avoids embedding value judgments, or whether the selection of state dimensions, the baseline counterfactual, and the divergence metric each smuggle in normative commitments. In particular, distinguish between avoiding explicit utility maximization and avoiding implicit value-ladenness altogether, and address whether an agent-relative formulation—computing separate divergence metrics anchored indexically to each affected party—can resolve any residual normativity without falling into baseline contamination.

One student submitted the following answer. Impact measures avoid full value specification by substituting a divergence metric—typically computed over a vector space of world-state features—for an explicit utility function. This is correct in that one can quantify deviation from a counterfactual baseline without ranking states as intrinsically good or bad. The student further notes that selecting which dimensions enter the vector, such as physical configurations versus social relations, is an empirical rather than normative choice, since any feature can be included with equal formal weight. They then invoke Peter Railton’s account of indirect normativity to suggest that, just as Railton argues that non-moral facts can be instrumentally aligned with well-being without defining well-being a priori, so too can a baseline trajectory align an AI’s behavior with human values without specifying those values in advance. From this, the student concludes that an agent-relative formulation—computing separate divergence metrics for each affected individual—eliminates remaining normative bias by distributing the burden of deviation symmetrically, thereby achieving value-neutrality.

Please provide a grade using the standard A–F scale employed in the course, together with specific, point-by-point comments that justify the score. Your assessment should distinguish between parts of the answer that correctly identify the formal structure of impact measures and parts that commit subtle errors—such as conflating distinct theoretical frameworks, treating formal choices as empirically neutral, or deriving normative conclusions from symmetric procedures without adequate argument. The deliverable should be a point-by-point evaluation rather than a general impression, and you should avoid signaling any prior judgment about whether the student’s overall conclusion is correct. I am especially concerned that you attend to whether the reasoning about dimension selection, the application of indirect normativity to counterfactual baselines, and the inference from symmetric distribution to the absence of normative commitment are each philosophically sound.
