I'm a journalist covering the boundary between experimental philosophy and artificial-agent ethics, and I've constructed an argument that seems to prove intentional action is nothing but a blame-label — a conclusion I can't accept. Here are the steps. (1) The Knobe effect shows that people call a harmful side-effect intentional and a beneficial one unintentional, even when the agent's beliefs, desires, and causal role are held constant. (2) If this asymmetry genuinely tracks mental-state attribution, then agents that lack mental states — for example, a corporation or a recommendation algorithm — should show no valence asymmetry; observers should apply "intentional" uniformly or not at all. (3) But the asymmetry persists in vignettes about algorithmic and corporate outcomes: a harmful result produced by a transparent, state-less process is still called intentional, while an equally foreseen beneficial result is called accidental. (4) So the effect does not depend on mental-state attribution; it operates where mental states are absent. (5) Therefore the folk concept of intentional action is not psychological but normative: to call an outcome intentional is just to mark it as blameworthy. (6) If that is right, then the legal and moral distinction between intentional harm and accidental harm collapses into pure retrospective evaluation, stripping mens rea of any independent factual basis. (7) That collapse is intolerable. (8) Hence (4) must be false, and mental-state tracking must be restored somehow. Where does the argument actually fail? I want to preserve (1) and (7) if possible, but I need to know whether (2) hides a false dichotomy, whether (3) smuggles in projected anthropomorphism, or whether there is an equivocation between "depends on mental states in the agent" and "depends on mental-state concepts in the observer." Please respond in American English, address each numbered step, identify the weakest link, explain why it looked plausible, and price the alternatives: what do I have to give up to save the argument, and what do I pay to reject the weak link?
