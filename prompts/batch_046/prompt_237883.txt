I write about the philosophy of science and evolutionary theory for an informed general audience, and I am commissioning a feature essay on a controversy that too often collapses into caricature: Karl Popper’s charge that Darwinism is not genuinely scientific because it is unfalsifiable, together with his subsequent partial retreat.

Most accounts present Popper either as a dogmatic falsificationist who failed to understand biology, or as a philosopher who gracefully corrected an overreach. I want an essay that gets into the conceptual weeds instead.

Please write an essay that addresses the following.

First, reconstruct Popper’s argument that natural selection risks tautology—“survival of the fittest” can seem to mean only “survival of those that survive”—without treating it as mere ignorance of evolutionary biology. Identify the precise logical feature of selectionist explanations that makes them vulnerable to this charge, and distinguish that charge from the separate complaint that evolutionary theory lacks predictive precision.

Second, examine Popper’s later acknowledgment that evolutionary theory can generate risky predictions, for instance about the gradual adaptation of organisms or the conditions under which transitional forms should appear. Assess whether this recantation resolves the tautology problem or merely relocates it: does specifying mechanisms of variation and selection turn the framework into a falsifiable scientific theory, or does the mechanism itself remain logically compatible with virtually any biological outcome?

Third, introduce and examine the following tension. Popper’s own evolutionary epistemology treats the growth of scientific knowledge as a process of conjecture and refutation—an intellectual selection against error. If selectionist logic is tautological or metaphysically loose when applied to biological evolution, is it not equally so when applied to the history of ideas? Or is there a principled asymmetry between biological selection and epistemic selection that saves the latter from the same demarcation failure?

Weigh these considerations and defend a clear verdict. Is Popper’s critique of Darwinism best understood as a fatal failure of his demarcation criterion, as a useful methodological caution that evolutionary biology can accommodate, or as an unwitting self-critique that undermines his philosophy of science? Your conclusion should specify what, if anything, evolutionary theory must do to satisfy a rigorous falsificationist standard without abandoning its essentially historical and probabilistic character.

Write for an intelligent but non-specialist reader. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the essay should stand on its conceptual arguments alone. Reach a definite conclusion and justify it—do not end with an open-ended survey of opinions.
