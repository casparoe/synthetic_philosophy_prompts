I'm preparing interview questions for an episode of my philosophy podcast, "Conceptual Mechanics." Next week's guest is a philosopher who has recently defended what she calls the "structural-anchor" resolution to the old-evidence problem in Bayesian epistemology. Rather than treating old evidence as a failure of conditionalization, she argues that the mistake lies in assuming a fixed prior distributed over a static hypothesis space. On her view, when a theory T retroactively explains a long-known datum E, what changes is not merely the credence assigned to T within an unchanged framework, but the very partition of the possibility space: E operates as a structural anchor that redistributes prior weights across the cells of a reconfigured credal network, allowing the agent's overall belief structure to achieve greater explanatory coherence without violating diachronic rationality. She further maintains that standard objections — that this permits double counting of evidence, or that it collapses into the familiar logical-learning response — miss a distinction she draws between framework-neutral uptake (the initial acquisition of E) and framework-integrated uptake (recognizing E's explanatory role within T).

I want eight to ten interview questions that press her on the mechanics of this position rather than asking her to restate it. At least one should target her claim that the possibility-space itself shifts: ask what formal or normative constraints prevent that shift from being ad hoc, and include a follow-up anticipating her appeal to explanatory-coherence metrics or to restrictions on admissible partitions. At least one should address the charge of double counting: ask specifically whether her mechanism allows an agent to gain confirmatory support for T from E on two separate occasions — once when E was initially learned, and again when its explanatory connection to T is recognized — and follow up with the reply she is likely to offer, namely that the first uptake lacked the theoretical framing required for confirmatory weight. I also want a question on imprecise probabilities: she has suggested that adopting credal sets rather than point-valued priors makes the old-evidence problem seem unavoidable; ask whether an imprecise model dissolves the need for structural re-weighting altogether, with a follow-up keyed to her probable defense that imprecision merely postpones the integration problem without solving it. Finally, include one question about defeat conditions: ask what formal result — for example, a proof that any framework-shifting update can be simulated by standard conditionalization over an appropriately expanded language — would convince her the view is wrong, and craft the follow-up assuming she responds that such simulations always tacitly import the very structural assumptions her account rejects.
