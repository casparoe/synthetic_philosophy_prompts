The Williams integrity objection — the worry about having one thought too many when deciding whether to save your spouse — has been argued about for decades and nobody shifts. Utilitarians call it a psychlogy bias; critics call it a structural limit on moral agency tied to ground projects. I think the stall isn't about the case itself but about two hidden disagreements: first, whether integrity names a normative constraint or just a cost of being alienated from your projects; second, whether moral reasons have to be universalizable to count at all. Is this actually a verbal disagreeement about what integrity does, or are both sides answering different questions — one about what morality demands, one about what kind of self can survive making those demands? Give me a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey, and say whether a distinction like narrative coherence versus impartial justification, or findings about identity fusion in moral psychlogy, could break the deadlock. If I'm mixing up Williams's actual argument with later commentary, or if the premise is confused, say so plainly instead of going along.
