Here is my package on distributive ethics and I want a consistency audit before I defend it in a reading group. (1) I take prioritarianism to be correct: aggregate moral value is the sum of a strictly concave, monotonic transformation applied to individual welfare levels, with no separate coefficient for equality and no discontinuities except those implied by the curvature itself. (2) I regard the leveling-down objection as a category mistake — prioritarian aggregation never recommends reducing anyone’s welfare without compensating gains elsewhere, so the objection tacitly imports an egalitarian premise about relative standing that my view rejects outright. (3) For social choice under risk I adopt an ex post stance: we apply the concave weights to the welfare levels that actually obtain in each realized world, then aggregate across worlds with standard expectation, rather than applying weights to ex ante expected welfare. (4) I measure welfare over complete lives, not over instantaneous time-slices or experience-stream fragments, both to avoid double-counting suffering and to respect temporal neutrality; on this view a brief catastrophic episode can be substantially offset by high welfare in the surrounding temporal parts of that same life. (5) Despite all of this, I want to maintain that there is a non-arbitrary “catastrophic region” near zero welfare where the concave function behaves almost lexically — benefits delivered inside this region dominate benefits delivered far above it — yet I insist this is not a sufficiency threshold in the standard sense, since there is no discrete jump and no moral status change at the boundary.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look tense, and rank the conflicts by severity. In particular, I am worried that measuring welfare over complete lives (4) drains the catastrophic region in (5) of its content, since a near-zero lifetime total is just a uniformly bad life rather than a local crisis, and that combining ex post evaluation (3) with lifetime aggregation (4) might commit me to risk attitudes I do not actually hold — for instance, being forced to prefer a gamble that risks pushing one life into near-zero lifetime welfare over a safe outcome with moderate equality, even when the ex ante profile looks benign. For each tension, say what has to give and what the cheapest revision is; then identify the single change that cleans up the most conflicts without abandoning prioritarianism itself. Do not list every minor friction — focus on the ones that threaten the coherence of the package.
