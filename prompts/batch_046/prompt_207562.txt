Write a short story set in a near-future institution called the Archive of Facticity, where every citizen is assigned a Curator who continuously synthesizes their past choices into an immutable biographical narrative that is projected back to them as the definitive account of who they are. Your protagonist is a Curator who discovers that her own official biography has been silently edited: a moment of raw anguish she clearly remembers—when she abandoned a secure career without reason, purely because she could—has been replaced by a deterministic explanation citing her neural profile, childhood conditioning, and economic incentives. The story should trace her Sartrean crisis as she decides whether to correct the record and reclaim her transcendence over her facticity, or to accept the edited version and treat herself as a being-in-itself whose essence is already fixed. Crucially, include a scene in which she realizes that the Archive’s predictive model has already catalogued her likely act of resistance—restoring the original record—as a predictable behavioral output derived from her personality matrix, so that her very attempt to assert radical freedom risks becoming just another datum in her facticity. Do not resolve the tension with either a triumphant assertion of will or a passive surrender to determinism; instead, end in a sustained moment of anguish where she makes a choice that simultaneously affirms and denies her freedom—perhaps by altering the record in a way that makes her future unpredictability itself part of the official narrative—leaving the reader genuinely uncertain whether she has achieved good faith or only invented a more sophisticated form of bad faith.
