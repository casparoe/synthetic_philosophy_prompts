Analyze Willy Brandt's Warsaw Genuflection of December 1970 as a philosophical test case for the agent-identity conditions of institutional apology. Do not recount the diplomatic history; treat the episode structurally. Brandt knelt, unannounced and without a treaty mandate, at the Monument to the Ghetto Heroes in Warsaw as Chancellor of the Federal Republic of Germany—a state that officially claimed legal continuity (Staatskontinuität) with the German Reich responsible for the Holocaust, yet that simultaneously presented itself as a constitutional and moral rupture with the Nazi past. Through the lens of institutional apology theory—distinguishing expressive acknowledgment from reparative obligation, and literal speech-act identity from ritual succession—examine whether Brandt could validly apologize on behalf of an agent that was, by its own self-understanding, both numerically continuous (same territory, bureaucracy, population) and normatively discontinuous (democratic successor breaking with criminal predecessor). Did the physical and legal continuity supply the standing required for the apology to bind the apologizer, or did the normative rupture necessary for democratic legitimacy sever the chain of moral responsibility? Consider the absence of any legislative or treaty authorization for the gesture: was this an apology performed by the correct agent under defective institutional conditions, or does the episode support a counterpart-state model in which a successor institution acknowledges wrongs committed by its predecessor without claiming literal identity? Map precisely which features of the episode—Brandt's personal spontaneity, the state's continuity doctrine, the lack of formal reparative structure—the identity-based theory explains well, and which it misdescribes or leaves silent. Conclude with what the case shows about whether continuity of statehood is an asset or an obstacle to the moral logic of apology, and whether institutional apologies are best understood as speech acts requiring numerical agent-identity or as rituals of moral succession between counterpart entities.
