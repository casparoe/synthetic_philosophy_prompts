My opening move: I want to challenge indirect normativity with a case of deliberative opacity. Consider an agent who must decide whether to break a serious promise in order to prevent a widespread harm. She attempts to follow indirect normativity — she should do what her fully informed, unbiased, idealized self would endorse. But she suffers from deliberative opacity: her access to her own reasoning is structurally partial, and any mental simulation of that idealized perspective is corrupted by her present panic; the simulation inherits the bias rather than filtering it out. As a result, she cannot identify the idealized endorsement without already being contaminated by the very state the idealization is meant to correct. The mediating procedure is therefore opaque and unavailable to her as an actionable guide. By contrast, the direct norm — "do not break serious promises without an overriding moral justification" — applies immediately, regardless of her self-access or the transparency of her reasoning.
