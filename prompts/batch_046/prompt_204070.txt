For a graduate seminar on normative ethics, write the full list of desiderata that any adequate theory of moral aggregation over infinite or indefinitely extendable populations—what we might call infinite ethics—must satisfy. I want the specification of adequacy itself, not a scorecard of existing views, though a brief concluding comparison is permitted.

Please structure your answer around the following, with a brief justification for why each belongs on the list:

1. Phenomena to be accommodated: the Mere Addition Paradox; the Repugnant Conclusion and its inverse, the Sadistic Conclusion; moral value divergence under unbounded addition (the St. Petersburg-style problem for total welfare); and the rearrangement problem, in which the same multiset of lives ordered differently yields contradictory comparative judgments.

2. Distinctions the theory must preserve: between "better overall" and "better for each affected party"; between actual and merely potential moral subjects; between quantitative and qualitative dimensions of welfare; and between well-orderable and non-well-orderable populations, since cosmological scenarios may involve spatially or temporally distributed subjects that lack a canonical ordering.

3. Consistency conditions: the theory must not generate Dutch-book vulnerabilities over infinite lotteries; it must remain compatible with the psychological and biological reality that welfare is not cardinally interpersonally comparable across species or epochs; and it must acknowledge the logical result that, over certain infinite domains, transitivity, completeness, and the joint avoidance of both the Repugnant and Sadistic Conclusions are unsatisfiable together.

For each desideratum, mark whether it is non-negotiable or represents the preference of a particular camp (e.g., person-affecting restrictions, critical-level thresholds, or the denial of inter-world comparability).

Then answer two diagnostic questions: which subset of these requirements do you take to be genuinely jointly unsatisfiable, and which single requirement does the most damage to the four main contending approaches—total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, critical-level views, and person-affecting restrictions?

Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases that motivate them; spend no more than one short paragraph comparing the four named contenders against your list. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate to a philosophy professor assigning a conceptual specification task; do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Limit your response to approximately 450 words.
