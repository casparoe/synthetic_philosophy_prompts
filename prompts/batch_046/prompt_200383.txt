I am currently enrolled in an upper-level seminar on the philosophy of history and historical explanation, and I have become preoccupied with a conceptual tension that seems to underlie nearly every discussion of path dependence but is seldom made explicit. On the familiar side of the debate stands the claim—exemplified by technological lock-in like the QWERTY keyboard layout and by evolutionary contingency arguments about replaying the tape of life—that history is deeply locked in: minute initial differences cascade through feedback loops into radically different present outcomes, so that replaying history from an altered starting point would not reproduce anything resembling our current biological forms or social institutions. Against this stands the convergence thesis: evolutionary biologists cite the independent emergence of eyes, flight, and echolocation across distant lineages, while institutional theorists observe that market economies, legal frameworks, and property-rights regimes appear to arise independently in societies facing analogous coordination problems, suggesting that functional selection pressures eventually pull diverse historical paths toward a small set of stable equilibria. The standard framing treats this as a straightforward empirical dispute between contingency and convergence.

Yet I suspect that the debate is obscured by a deeper ambiguity in what "path dependence" actually denotes, and resolving that ambiguity is necessary before any verdict about history being "locked in" can be justified. I want to distinguish two concepts that are usually conflated. "Thin path dependence" holds that the sequence of events determines which of several pre-existing attractors is reached, but the space of possible attractors is itself fixed: replaying the tape might swap one equilibrium for another, yet the replacement is functionally equivalent within the same landscape of possibilities. "Thick path dependence," by contrast, holds that the historical sequence does not merely select among given attractors but actively reshapes the landscape of possible outcomes, so that replaying the tape with altered initial conditions does not merely substitute one functionally similar institution or biological form for another, but generates a genuinely different kind of evolutionary or institutional possibility space altogether. For example, the particular path taken in the development of quantitative notation might not only determine which mathematical culture prevails, but might determine whether quantitative reasoning becomes centralized in state bureaucracies or distributed across merchant networks, thereby altering what kinds of economic and scientific institutions are even conceivable in subsequent centuries.

Write an essay that addresses whether history is locked in in the thick sense, the thin sense, both, or neither, and defend a clear conclusion rather than ending on a non-committal note. In reaching your verdict, weigh arguments drawn from evolutionary contingency, institutional economics, and convergent evolution, but treat them not as mere empirical disputes about frequency—how often history diverges—rather as analyses of the structural kind of dependence at work. Specifically, ask whether the same outcomes, understood not merely as specific events but as the functional equilibria that characterize a system, would have arrived anyway had initial conditions differed. Would replaying the tape eventually land in a functionally equivalent world, or in a world whose very categories of possibility differ from ours? To ground the analysis, examine at least one concrete hypothetical case—such as the divergence of writing systems, the emergence of property-rights regimes, or the evolution of cooperative breeding in social species—and determine whether the path taken altered only the particular form that emerged, or also the space of forms that could subsequently emerge. If you conclude that thick path dependence is genuine, explain what mechanism—irreversible investment, complementary institutional bundling, or evolutionary ratcheting—makes the attractor space itself path-dependent. If you conclude that only thin path dependence holds, explain why functional constraints eventually dominate and why the appearance of radical divergence is illusory. If you think the distinction collapses, propose the framework that should replace it. Justify your verdict with explicit reasoning about the causal and structural dependencies that actually govern historical sequences.
