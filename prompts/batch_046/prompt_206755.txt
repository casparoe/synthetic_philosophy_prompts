You serve on an editorial board that publishes retrospective assessments of government crises, and your board requires every explanation to survive an audit for hindsight bias and narrative fallacy before it can be printed. Write a reusable thirty-minute audit procedure—ordered checks with explicit pass/fail conditions—that a non-philosopher editor can run on a draft explanation. The procedure must separately test for (i) retrospective inevitability framing, where an outcome is presented as unavoidable despite contemporaneous records showing open contingency; (ii) spurious causal chaining, where events are linked into a coherent plot despite gaps in documented influence; and (iii) temporal-order confusion, where the logical sequence of an explanation is mistaken for the chronological sequence of decisions. Mark every point where the audit hits a judgment call that formal criteria cannot settle—especially regarding what agents actually believed they could do at the time. Apply the procedure to two drafts: one that explains a financial crisis through a single regulatory omission while ignoring concurrent systemic fragility visible in period reports, and one that reconstructs a diplomatic rupture as an inevitable linear cascade of miscommunications without testing whether intermediate steps were avoidable. Include one worked case where the procedure rejects an explanation the editor finds intuitively satisfying, and state precisely what the audit cannot establish—particularly about counterfactual belief states—without direct historical evidence.
