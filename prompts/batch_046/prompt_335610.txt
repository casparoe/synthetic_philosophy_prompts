As a novelist, I keep returning to a problem that shapes the families in my work: whether a person is constituted by the communities she belongs to, or whether she stands prior to them and chooses them. I want to know if my intuitions form a pattern.

Here are my verdicts.

First, a young architect in Kyoto leaves a promising solo practice to join his father’s carpentry collective. He does not do it from guilt; he comes to see his design vision as continuous with the collective’s craft tradition. I think this is autonomy fully realized, not surrendered. The Western vocabulary of “giving up his career” misdescribes it.

Second, a British philosopher argues that all obligations are contractual, so he owes his aging mother nothing unless she paid for his upbringing. I find this conceptually tidy but humanly hollow: it treats the person as existing before relation.

Third, the same Kyoto architect is then ordered by the collective’s elder to break off an engagement with a woman outside the tradition. He obeys. Here I think he is wrong: the collective has overstepped. But I worry this contradicts my verdict in the first case.

Fourth, an American nurse moves alone to a disaster zone to care for strangers, with no prior bond. I call it genuinely noble, yet I also feel it is thinner than the Kyoto case—heroic precisely because it lacks relational density.

Fifth, a second-generation immigrant refuses to teach her child her parents’ language, insisting the child must choose attachments freely. I keep reversing myself: sometimes this protects autonomy; sometimes it imposes a false universalization of the Western model, cutting the child off from a relation that could be constitutive without being coercive.

What principle, if any, captures these? Please state it precisely, with the case that motivates each clause, and apply it to two further cases: the immigrant child case above, and a person who voluntarily enters a monastic order, surrendering property and decision-making for life. Be honest about which of my verdicts must be dropped for consistency.

If my framing falsely opposes East Asian and Western models, or if the task requires theoretical machinery no model should be expected to hold without search, say so plainly instead of complying politely.
