I'm preparing a presentation for my school's philosophy symposium on Derek Parfit's teletransporter thought experiment. Rather than revisiting the standard debate over physical versus psychological continuity, I want to examine a modified case: the scanner produces a perfect duplicate without destroying the original body, but then subjects the original to a gradual, continuous erosion of memory and psychological traits over several months, so that its links to the pre-scan person weaken by degrees rather than breaking all at once. Meanwhile, the duplicate lives with full continuity, and the technology can be iterated to generate additional successors arranged along a spectrum of shared memories and intentions. Given this graded, multi-successor scenario, what are the most important implications for Parfit's central claim that personal identity is not what matters—only psychological connectedness and continuity? In particular, does the existence of a continuous gradient of connectedness collapse the distinction between surviving and failing to survive, and if moral accountability depends on psychological continuity, how should we assign responsibility among the various points on the gradient when one of them acts on intentions inherited from the original? I am asking for a direct conceptual analysis of these implications, not a review of the literature.
