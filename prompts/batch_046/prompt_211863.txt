As a retired molecular biologist who has recently turned to philosophy of mind, I have been puzzling over the film Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind and its relationship to the Buddhist doctrine of anatta. I would like a reading that takes both the narrative and the doctrine seriously, without reducing either to an illustration of the other. The film depicts a technology that selectively erases autobiographical memory of a romantic partner, yet Joel and Clementine are drawn back together at Montauk despite having no conscious recollection of their past. Analyze this convergence through the framework of the five skandhas: do the residual emotional pulls that survive erasure correspond to samskara—mental formations that persist when vijnana and the narrative self have been disrupted—or do they instead suggest a relational essence that outlasts constructed identity and thereby resists anatta? Furthermore, consider Joel's decision in the final scenes to preserve the painful record even after learning its outcome. Is this an act of upadana—clinging that reconstitutes the very self he wished to dissolve—or does it represent a different kind of acceptance compatible with no-self? I am particularly interested in what the film's specific mechanics of targeted forgetting reveal about personal identity that the skandha analysis alone might overlook, and where an honest critic must admit that the film's romantic conclusion pushes back against a strictly Buddhist interpretation. Please ground your response in the film's well-known plot and in the conceptual structure of anatta itself, without bibliographies, citations, or appeals to texts I cannot consult.
