There's this long-running argument in AI alignment about whether HCH and factored cognition are a decent idealisation of deliberation, and I think it's stalled in a weird way. They say this is just peer review or expert panels made explicite, and that real deliberation is already factorised if you look closely. The other camp says it's a fantasy — values and beliefs co-construct each other as you go, there's constant epistemic leakage between steps, and treating judgement like modular computation misses the phenomenolgy entirely. For years both sides have repeated the same moves and nobody's budged an inch. Is this actually a substantive disagreement about cognitive architecture, or is it verbal — one side using 'deliberation' for the normative structure of justification, the other for the lived experience of thinking? Could there be a hidden empirical claim about whether value-dependence is unavoidable, or are we just facing bedrock intuitions about what reasoning should feel like?
