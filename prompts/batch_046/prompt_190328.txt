After a long and somewhat circular argument with a friend about punishment, I have tried to write down where I actually stand, and I am unsure the package holds together. Here are my commitments:

1. I accept Caruso's hard-determinist argument that retributive punishment is never justified: no agent is the ultimate source of their actions, so "just deserts" in the deep metaphysical sense is a fiction we should discard.

2. At the same time, I think Dennett is correct that moral responsibility is not an illusion but a real, evolved social technology — a functional status like being a legal person — that distinguishes agents who can participate in reason-giving practices from those who cannot.

3. From this I conclude that criminal justice should operate on a pure public-health or quarantine model: we restrain, rehabilitate, or deter solely on the basis of risk and social utility, with no suffering added for its own sake as "payment" for wrongdoing.

4. Yet I also think that within this forward-looking framework, sentencing and institutional response should still be calibrated to the offender's rational and moral competence at the time of the offense, because the appropriate mechanism for a reasoning-responsive agent differs instrumentally from that for a severely impaired agent.

5. I reject evolutionary debunking arguments: the evolutionary origin of our reactive attitudes does not undermine their justification, just as the evolutionary origin of perception does not make vision unreliable.

6. However, I maintain that if we ever achieved complete causal and predictive transparency about human behavior — knowing the full deterministic story behind every act — we should then abandon private moral blame, resentment, and reactive attitudes altogether, retaining only institutional management.

Go through these and audit them for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each? For every real tension, specify the cheapest revision and what it costs. Rank the tensions by severity. Then tell me which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would clean up the most conflict overall. In particular, I want to know whether I am smuggling a covert commitment to desert back into my quarantine model in (4), or whether my functionalism in (2) can survive my own eliminativist impulse in (6) without collapsing into incoherence.
