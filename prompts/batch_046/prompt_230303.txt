I'm trying to determine whether Lakatos's distinction between progressive and degenerating problem shifts actually captures a stable feature of scientific development, by testing my intuitions against cases. Here are my verdicts.

The shift from Newtonian mechanics to Einsteinian relativity strikes me as clearly progressive. The Newtonian hard core—absolute space, instantaneous gravitational action—was protected by an expanding belt of auxiliary hypotheses, including the Lorentz-Fitzgerald contraction introduced specifically to accommodate the Michelson-Morley null result. Those modifications yielded no novel predictions of their own. Once relativity offered a new hard core that explained the same anomalies without the ad hoc adjustments and went on to predict gravitational lensing and the perihelion advance, the Newtonian programme was left behind in a progressive transition.

The late-eighteenth-century development of phlogiston theory feels degenerating. The hard core held that combustion involves the release of a substance called phlogiston. When metals were observed to gain weight upon burning—directly contradicting the idea of releasing a substance—the protective belt was expanded with the auxiliary claim that phlogiston carries negative weight. This saved the hard core, but it predicted nothing new; it was pure defensive maneuvering.

But I become uncertain with intermediate cases. Consider a hypothetical research programme in cognitive science with the hard core that all mental processes are computations over explicit symbolic representations. For decades it predicts precise reaction-time patterns in human problem-solving, and these predictions are borne out. Then a rival connectionist programme gains traction. Rather than abandon computation, the symbolicists introduce a hybrid-architecture auxiliary hypothesis: connectionist modules are embedded within an overarching symbolic framework. This saves the hard core, generates a genuinely new prediction about a specific class of reasoning errors that is subsequently confirmed, but also forces post-hoc adjustments to the original reaction-time models. I want to call this progressive, yet the protective-belt modification is clearly defensive.

Consider Ptolemaic astronomy after astronomers realized that stellar parallax should be observable if the Earth moves, yet was not detected. The programme added the auxiliary hypothesis that stars lie at unimaginably vast distances. I judge this degenerating, even though the distance claim was later vindicated within a different theoretical framework, because within the Ptolemaic programme it served solely to protect the geocentric hard core and generated no independent astronomical predictions.

Consider string theory. Its hard core includes supersymmetry and extra spatial dimensions. Over several decades it has produced no unique, testable predictions about particle masses or the value of the cosmological constant that distinguish it from competing frameworks. Instead, it has generated a vast landscape of possible vacuum states. When observations revealed a small positive cosmological constant, the programme accommodated this through anthropic reasoning within the landscape. I judge this degenerating. Yet I face a tension: I want to call inflationary cosmology progressive because of its precise predictions regarding the cosmic microwave background power spectrum, even though multiverse interpretations of inflation seem to invoke the same kind of landscape-style reasoning that I criticize in string theory.

Consider the Bohr atomic model. Its hard core was that electrons occupy discrete stationary states with quantized angular momentum. The protective belt included rules for spectral lines. When the Zeeman effect produced anomalous splitting patterns, the model was saved by introducing ad hoc quantum numbers rather than deriving the effect from the hard core itself. I see this as degenerating. Yet the broader quantum research programme eventually abandoned the visualizable orbit hard core altogether and moved to matrix mechanics and wave mechanics—programmes that retained some structural commitments but replaced others, eventually yielding novel predictions. So was the Bohr episode a degenerating phase of one programme, or a progressive transition between programmes?

Is there a coherent Lakatosian principle that captures all of these verdicts? In particular, what exactly distinguishes the symbolicist hybrid case, which I call progressive, from the phlogiston case, which I call degenerating? Is it simply whether auxiliary hypotheses eventually lead to novel predictions, or does the relationship between the hard core and the modifications matter more? And can my distinction between string theory and inflation be maintained, or does it collapse once I apply the principle consistently? Please state the principle precisely, indicate which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon, and apply it to a case I have not listed: the development of plate tectonics from Wegener's continental drift, where the hard-core claim that continents move was initially protected by implausible mechanisms such as tidal forces, then apparently abandoned until geophysical evidence for seafloor spreading and mantle convection provided a new mechanistic basis. Would that be progressive throughout, degenerating and then reborn as a different programme, or something else entirely?
