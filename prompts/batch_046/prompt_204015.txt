For a seminar on medieval Islamic philosophy and contemporary metaphysics, I am setting out my current position on causation, agency, and scientific explanation, and I would like an audit of its coherence before I present. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept al-Ghazālī's occasionalist thesis that God alone is the efficient cause of every event, and that created things are merely occasions (asbāb) upon which divine creation is habitually conjoined.

(2) I hold that this is fully compatible with empirical science, because scientific laws describe the stable patterns (sunan) God has ordained rather than intrinsic necessary connections in nature.

(3) I maintain that genuine human moral responsibility requires that the soul (al-nafs) be more than an occasion — specifically, that it genuinely acquires (kasb) its voluntary actions through an internal power of choice.

(4) I think the Ashʿarite account of kasb succeeds in preserving responsibility without attributing independent creative power to creatures, because acquisition attaches the act to the agent without implying that the agent produces it ex nihilo.

(5) I also hold that contemporary interventionist theories of causation in philosophy of science are closer in spirit to occasionalism than Humean regularity theories are, since both deny that causal powers are intrinsic to nature.

(6) Finally, I believe that miracles are best understood not as violations of natural law but as cases in which God suspends His habitual conjunction, which presupposes that there is no necessary connection to violate.

Please examine these commitments for internal tension. Which pairs genuinely pull against one another, which only appear to, and what is the least costly revision in each case? In particular, I am unsure whether (3) and (4) can both be maintained alongside (1) without covertly reintroducing secondary causation. Rank the tensions by severity, and indicate whether the package is salvageable or whether one central commitment must be abandoned. Be specific about what each revision costs.
