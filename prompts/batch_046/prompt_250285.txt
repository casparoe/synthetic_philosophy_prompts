hey, i've been playing around with translation apps for my job (i work in logistics, nothing fancy) and i keep hitting this weird wall that i dont have words for. there's this thing where the app translates a phrase that uses a physical action to mean a mental one, but it does it in a way that sounds totally natural in the target language, except its clearly wrong conceptually. like "we're weighing our options" comes out in another language as if you're literally putting boxes on a scale, but the grammar and word choice are so smooth that a native speaker would read it and think "yeah, thats how we talk about scales" not "oh thats a mistranslation of thinking hard." same thing with "grasping the concept" turning into physically grabbing something, but written so fluently that it sounds like the writer is just being poetic about holding an object.

what's strange is it doesn't happen with super common sayings like "time is money" - those come out right. and it doesn't happen with totally obscure phrases either, because then the translation is just broken and you can tell. it's specifically the ones in the middle: common enough that the software has seen all the words together, but not so common that its learned them as a fixed chunk with a mental meaning. so the statistical part gets the fluency perfect, but the "understanding" part (if there even is one) misses that the physical verb is standing in for a mental state.

i know hofstadter and others have talked about translation being shallow, but i'm asking about this specific pattern. does it have its own name? like, is there a term for when statistical fluency actually camouflages a conceptual error by making the literal interpretation sound culturally appropriate? and if not, how would you distinguish it from just "bad translation" or from the general idea that machines dont really understand? i dont want a reading list, just wondering if philosophers or linguists have already carved this out, or if im describing something that needs a new label.
