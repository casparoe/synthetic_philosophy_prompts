I'm currently taking an advanced seminar on Quine's naturalized epistemology, and I've been asked to moderate the Q&A portion of a colloquium featuring a philosopher who has recently advanced an interpretive thesis about Quine's scattered remarks on ethics—particularly in On What There Is, Word and Object, and several late lectures. Rather than reading Quine as a straightforward moral anti-realist or cultural relativist, she argues for what she calls peripheral insulation with holistic vulnerability: moral values are neither direct empirical hypotheses subject to observational falsification nor purely expressive utterances, but framework-internal nodes positioned near the periphery of the web of belief in a structurally protected role. They can absorb localized empirical shocks by triggering auxiliary adjustments elsewhere in the system, yet they remain vulnerable to large-scale holistic revision should the overall framework become too unwieldy. I need six to eight interview questions that operate at this level of mechanical detail—questions that press the actual architecture of the view rather than asking her to summarize it.

At least one question should target the variability objection. If moral predicates are structurally analogous to logical constants in being protected from direct revision, why do they exhibit far greater cross-cultural divergence than logic or mathematics? I want the follow-up keyed to her likely reply—that moral values are closer to high-level theoretical posits with wider latitude—asking whether that latitude makes moral disagreement genuinely cognitive or merely pragmatic, and whether Quine's own theory-choice criteria (simplicity, conservatism, overall predictive coherence) can be applied across competing moral frameworks without circularity. Another question should address what she terms moral recalcitrance: cases in which a moral belief—such as a commitment to universal human equality—persists despite sustained empirical evidence of significant group-level differences. If the belief is genuinely insulated near the periphery, what magnitude or kind of systemic shock would actually dislodge it, and does her model predict that such beliefs are more stable or less stable than scientific paradigms under equivalent stress?

Finally, I want one question that introduces a distinction I have not seen named in the literature: between terminal-node revision, in which a protected belief is abandoned only when the entire surrounding framework collapses, and gradient drift, in which the belief's content slowly shifts through accumulated auxiliary adjustments without ever being explicitly rejected. Does Quine's account of belief revision allow for gradient drift in normative domains, and if so, does that undermine the claim that moral values are genuinely insulated rather than merely slowly mutable?
