I've been thinking about the analogy people keep using when they want to drive a wedge between determinism and predictability: a deterministic chaotic system is like an automated card-shuffling machine running with perfectly fixed mechanical rules. Every card's path is fully determined by the initial layout and the machine's geometry, yet after enough iterations the final ordering bears no recoverable relation to the starting deck, and any observer with imperfect measurement is permanently locked out of forecasting it. In rationalist and philosophy-of-mind circles, this is usually wielded to show that determinism poses no special threat to free will or to long-range planning—if even a simple mechanical system becomes practically opaque, then the fact that brains might be deterministic doesn't make our choices predictable, and determinism therefore changes nothing about our practical situation.
