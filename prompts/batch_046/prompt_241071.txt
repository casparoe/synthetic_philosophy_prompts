I am a senior philosophy major taking our department's advanced seminar on moral obligation, and I also serve as the student representative on the university's Scholarship Integrity and Awards Committee. I need you to act as a professional moral philosopher with deep expertise in supererogation and institutional ethics, and before drafting anything I want you to think carefully and do a brief conceptual brainstorm: consider how the distinction between duty, obligation, and acts that go beyond the call of duty behaves when it is an institution rather than an individual that sets expectations, and whether encoding voluntary excellence as an evaluative criterion might destroy the very feature that makes such acts morally significant. Once you have done that, please draft a formal decision memo for the committee.

The case concerns the Meridian Civic Scholarship, which contractually requires recipients to maintain a 3.5 GPA and complete exactly twenty hours per semester of approved community service. A junior recipient met both conditions precisely, but she declined an additional, unsolicited request from the scholarship office to design and run an unsanctioned summer literacy program for local youth—work that was never mentioned in the award letter, carried no compensation, and would have required roughly sixty unpaid hours during her internship period. Half the committee believes her refusal violates the scholarship's implicit "spirit of civic leadership" and argues that future awards should treat going beyond contractual minima as an evaluative expectation; the other half contends that penalizing her for doing exactly what was required and no more collapses the category of the supererogatory into ordinary obligation, effectively punishing students for not being heroes. The committee must reach an actionable bottom line—whether to uphold the award without sanction, issue a formal reprimand with revised conditions, or reform the scholarship criteria going forward—and the memo must present that recommendation clearly.

Please write the entire document as continuous prose only: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables, as though it were a single formal internal letter. Begin with the recommendation, then lay out the reasoning, and include an honest, rigorous treatment of the strongest argument on the side you reject. Do not provide any bibliography, citation list, or reference to specific page numbers; let the reasoning stand entirely on its own. I am especially interested in an unconventional but philosophically serious consideration—such as whether an institution can coherently claim to honor supererogation while simultaneously making it a condition of esteem—so please inject that kind of nuanced conceptual move rather than simply applying a standard textbook distinction. Write in a polished, formal register appropriate to an institutional review body.
