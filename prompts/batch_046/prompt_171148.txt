Write an essay in American English on whether G. E. Moore's ranking of friendship and aesthetic appreciation among the greatest intrinsic goods is consistent with his metaethical claim that goodness is a simple, non-natural property. Critics argue that Moore's defense of these goods implicitly appeals to naturalistic features—emotional intensity, phenomenological richness, or relational structure—that his open-question argument explicitly rules out as constitutive of value. Defenders reply that recognizing friendship or beauty as good requires no such reduction and that intuitionism can rank goods without analyzing them. Weigh both sides, address the charge of hidden naturalism, and reach a justified conclusion about whether Moore's axiology survives his own anti-naturalist commitments.
