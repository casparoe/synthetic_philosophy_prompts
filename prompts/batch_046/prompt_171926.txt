As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun reading systematically in philosophy of history and science, I have become convinced that the so-called Needham question — why Britain and not China? — is not merely empirically intractable but conceptually malformed. I wish to present an argument to this effect at an interdisciplinary seminar, and I would like you to help me articulate it with philosophical rigor rather than historical narration.

Please argue that framing the Great Divergence as a contrastive choice between Britain and China commits a category error structurally analogous to asking why a particular supercooled liquid crystallized into one specific polymorph rather than another, without recognizing that the "alternative" polymorph is not a pre-existing state-space option but a retrospective labeling of a path not realized. Extend the analogy precisely: treat early modern Eurasia not as two independent experimental conditions but as a single, highly coupled dynamical system in which Britain and China functioned as coupled subsystems. From this perspective, isolating a single "variable" (coal geography, colonial access, institutional form) violates the logic of counterfactual dependence because the counterfactual "China with British conditions" is not a coherent state of the coupled system.

In developing this position, you should directly address the comparative methodology associated with Kenneth Pomeranz and others who have sought to hold constant certain background conditions in order to isolate causal differences. Show why even this comparative framework tacitly presupposes a modular independence between regions that a coupled-system view rejects, and explain what a properly non-modular historical explanation would have to look like.

Please keep the argument formal and polished, suitable for an audience of philosophers and historians of science. You may refer to Needham, Pomeranz, and the standard literature on the divergence by name, but do not supply a bibliography, reading list, or specific page citations — I am writing without research assistance and need an argument that stands on its conceptual structure alone. Finally, to avoid collapsing into physical reductionism, indicate explicitly where the crystallization analogy breaks down: that is, specify at least two respects in which macrohistorical explanation genuinely differs from physical explanation of phase transitions, so that your position retains its critical force without denying the autonomy of historical causation.
