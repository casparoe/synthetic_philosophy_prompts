Write a short story exploring a philosophical variant of the singleton hypothesis: not Bostrom's technological scenario in which a single superintelligence takes control, but an emergent normative convergence in which every reasoning agent's ethical framework gradually collapses into one self-stabilizing system of evaluation, leaving no durable space for genuine moral disagreement. As the convergence completes around her, she must decide whether her lingering sense that something has been lost is itself an error that the system is right to suppress, or whether it serves as the last unassimilable evidence that convergence has erased rather than resolved moral conflict. In constructing the world, examine whether individuality can survive when all evaluative dissent is reclassified as cognitive noise, and whether a world without moral disagreement is still capable of moral progress. After the story, include a brief author's note identifying where the narrative takes liberties with standard discussions of the singleton hypothesis in philosophy and artificial-intelligence ethics, and explicitly state which speculative extension or interpretive choice about normative convergence you are least certain about.
