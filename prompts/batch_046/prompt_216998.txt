Write an eight-minute dedication address for the opening ceremony of the Simon Center for Procedural Design, an interdisciplinary institute devoted to institutional decision-making under constraint. The audience is split: half are scholars of bounded rationality, organizational behavior, and ecological psychology who will recognize every conceptual move; the other half are donors, students, and local residents with no background in decision theory. The address must use Herbert Simon’s distinction between substantive rationality—optimizing outcomes against fixed preferences—and procedural rationality—optimizing the decision process itself—to structure the institute’s self-definition. Crucially, it must also incorporate Simon’s concept of aspiration-level adaptation, in which agents adjust their target levels of achievement based on accumulated experience rather than maximizing against a stable utility function. These ideas should not serve as ornament: they must determine what the center promises to do—build adaptive stopping rules, design context-sensitive “good enough” thresholds, and reject universal best-practice templates—and what it refuses to do—pursue globally optimal solutions or impose fixed performance benchmarks across heterogeneous environments. The tone should be ceremonial but intellectually serious, accessible to non-specialists without diluting the technical point, and must contain no citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or direct quotations. Do not ask the audience to read further; instead, demonstrate how the center’s mission follows directly from treating satisficing as an engineered feature of rational institutions rather than as a failure of will or information.
