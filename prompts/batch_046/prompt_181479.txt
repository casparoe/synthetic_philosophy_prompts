Write a short research proposal, suitable for a philosophy-of-science workshop or a one-semester independent study, addressing the following: does deterministic chaos undermine the practical significance of ontological determinism in a way that is conceptually distinct from the undermining produced by quantum indeterminacy? Precisely state the problem by distinguishing two claims—first, that a system is governed by deterministic laws, and second, that its future states are in principle predictable given adequate epistemic resources. Note that the standard literature often either treats chaos as a mere technical limitation within determinism or conflates chaos-induced unpredictability with genuine ontological openness. Propose an approach that asks whether “predictive determinism” should be treated as part of the very concept of determinism or as an independent epistemic ideal, and examine whether the sensitivity to initial conditions in chaotic systems introduces a failure mode that is structurally analogous to, yet philosophically different from, quantum measurement indeterminacy. Specify what a successful result would establish—for instance, a principled taxonomy of unpredictability that preserves determinism’s metaphysical status despite chaos—and what would count as failure, such as the collapse of the chaos/quantum distinction once measurement and computational limits are fully internalized. Keep the scope realistic for a single paper: restrict the analysis to classical dynamical systems and bracket detailed quantum interpretations, indicating exactly where those brackets would need to be removed for a longer project. Do not provide a bibliography or citation list; refer to positions and arguments in general terms, and note that formal references will be incorporated in the final draft.
