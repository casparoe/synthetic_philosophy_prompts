Write an essay on the following controversy: when democratic institutions face a hard choice between genuinely incommensurable values—values that lack a common metric and are not reducible to one another—can the choice be rationally resolved, or does any resolution necessarily involve an irreparable, tragic loss? Focus on a specific institutional proposal: a democratic body first publicly articulates that two competing values (for instance, the preservation of an irreplaceable cultural heritage and the immediate protection of public health) stand in a relation of parity rather than superiority, and then delegates the final decision to a randomly selected, disinterested subset of citizens charged with choosing without further deliberation. Weigh three lines of argument. First, consider the view—associated with philosophers such as Ruth Chang—that rational agency includes the capacity to create reasons through commitment, and ask whether this capacity can be exercised collectively through such a procedural framework without reducing the values to commensurable preferences. Second, examine the pluralist objection, in the tradition of value pluralism, that procedural delegation does not eliminate the tragic character of the choice but merely disguises an arbitrary resolution, and that random selection introduces a form of arbitrariness incompatible with genuine rational justification. Third, assess the worry that recognizing parity at the collective level risks collapsing into a kind of democratic indifference, where no value can claim authoritative weight, and consider whether this undermines the legitimacy of the outcome. Reach a definite conclusion: either defend the proposed framework as a legitimate way for a collective agent to resolve hard choices while preserving the reality of incommensurability, or reject it and explain what alternative democratic theory should adopt—whether accepting the tragedy, seeking a covering value, or restructuring the decision context—when facing values that resist common measurement. Your conclusion should clarify whether the incommensurability is dissolved, preserved, or transformed by the procedure, and what this implies for democratic legitimacy.
