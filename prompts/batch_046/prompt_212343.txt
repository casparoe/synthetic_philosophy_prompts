Write a sestina on the omnipotence paradox, using exactly these six line-ending words in every stanza, rotated through the standard sestina order: stone, lift, power, limit, logic, God. The first stanza should present the classic formulation: a being with unlimited power creates a stone too heavy for that being to lift, apparently producing a task it cannot perform. Stanza two should rotate the words and take up the defense of voluntary self-limitation; stanza three, the logical-impossibility defense (that the task is not genuinely possible, hence not a failure); stanza four, the task-property distinction (omnipotence as the ability to do tasks, not to possess incompatible properties); stanza five, the modal-consistency view that omnipotence covers only logically possible states, so bringing about a state in which omnipotence is lost is not within its scope. Stanza six should reorder the terms to show that these resolutions have only reshuffled the vocabulary, not dissolved the contradiction: the being either can or cannot lift the stone it made, and either outcome restricts its power. The three-line envoi must place "God" and "stone" adjacent, leaving structurally unresolved whether omnipotence is bounded by logic or logic is bounded by omnipotence. Keep the paradox accurate: the problem is not physical weakness but that an omnipotent being seems able to actualize a state in which it is no longer omnipotent. Use loose iambic pentameter or blank verse; the poem should be about thirty-nine lines (six stanzas of six lines plus a three-line envoi).
