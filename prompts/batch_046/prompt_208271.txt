As a retired natural scientist who has only recently begun studying philosophy in earnest, I have noticed that debates over statues and street names are now being echoed in science by controversies over renaming eponyms—laws, constants, units, and syndromes that honor researchers with contested ethical or political records. Write an essay examining whether the moral framework applied to public monuments can be coherently transferred to scientific nomenclature, or whether the epistemic and institutional roles of eponyms in science create genuinely distinct obligations. Weigh the cases for retention, renaming, and contextualization across both domains, and defend a specific conclusion about whether scientific eponyms ought to be treated as a separate category of commemorative practice. Please respond in American English.
