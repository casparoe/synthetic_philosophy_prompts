I have just finished a long and rather exhausting argument with a friend about whether Frankfurt-style counterexamples genuinely defeat the principle of alternative possibilities, or whether they only defeat an especially unsophisticated version of it. She kept insisting that every compatibilist reply I mentioned—hierarchical identification models, source-compatibilist conditions on authentic desire, even the more recent modal-structural amendments—still quietly depends on some form of leeway that Frankfurt's original scenario was supposed to eliminate. To work through this properly, I am preparing to interview, for an academic podcast, an unnamed philosopher who defends a position she calls revisionist Frankfurtian compatibilism. On this view, Frankfurt cases do successfully undermine the classical principle of alternative possibilities, but moral responsibility nevertheless requires a specific modal-structural condition that she labels counterfactual openness to revision: the agent's actual psychological economy must be such that, had she accessed a particular kind of second-order information about her own first-order motives, she could have revised her decision without breaking psychological continuity or agential identity. The view is intended to preserve responsibility without alternative possibilities in the actual sequence, while avoiding the standard compatibilist retreat to pure sourcehood.

Please write me a formal, polished sequence of interview questions—about five or six—that takes this position seriously but presses it at precisely the points where the existing literature on Frankfurt cases, hierarchical identification, and modal conditions for responsibility applies the most pressure. In particular, anticipate the standard objections: that the openness condition collapses into a disguised alternative-possibilities requirement; that making responsibility depend on access to counterfactual information introduces a problematic form of epistemic luck; and that psychological continuity is itself too vague to bear the normative weight being placed on it. For each anticipated reply, include the immediate follow-up that makes the deflection harder. Conclude with one question asking what empirical or behavioral marker could distinguish an agent who genuinely satisfies this openness condition from one who lacks it, without relying solely on introspective report or hypothetical self-assessment. The questions should be substantive enough for a specialist audience, should avoid generic biographical openings, and should not allow the interviewee to escape into appeals that the issue is merely an unfinished research program.
