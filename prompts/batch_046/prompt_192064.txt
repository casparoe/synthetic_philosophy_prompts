We've always used a peer reveiw mix with gut-feel weighting, but now one board member who reads a lot of effective altruism stuff wants us to formally adopt an importance-tractability-neglectedness (ITN) scoring rubric, like they use for global health or AI safety, to rank our grant applications.

I need a memo for Thursday's meeting recommending whether we adopt it, adapt it, or reject it outright. I'm writing this quick before the meetng so apologies if it's scattered.

Write it in these parts, roughly these lengths:

1. Translation and residue (about 180 words): restate ITN in our vocabulary — importance as cultural/heritage value, tractability as feasibility given living practitioners, neglectedness as the current funding landscape. Be honest about the mapping: what translates clean, what gets distorted, and whether anything in the EA framework has no counterpart in heritage work (or vice versa). Don't smuggle in EA assumptions and call them translation.

2. The case for formalizing (about 150 words): why a three-axis score might help us compare across domains — say, a dying weaving tradition versus a puppet-theatre archive — and reduce bias.

3. The strongest case against (about 150 words): treat this seriously. What's lost when you reduce craft preservation to axes? Include at least one concrete structural distortion — for example, how "tractability" for a vaccine trial is nothing like tractability for intergenerational knowledge transfer, or how scoring "neglectedness" might push us to neglect well-funded but genuinely fragile traditions.

4. Bottom line and conditions (about 120 words): clear recommendation (approve, reject, or approve with conditions). Give concrete next steps and a timeframe if relevant — maybe a pilot with two domains? No citations or reading lists needed; just reason from the framework itself.

Keep it casual, like a memo to colleagues who know philosophy but not EA inside-baseball.
