So I retired from biochemestry a few years back and got into philosophy of memory - Margalit, Rieff, that crowd. I'm trying to sort my gut verdicts on remembering vs forgetting.

Cases where I think remembering is required: the names of people killed in a lab disaster I witnessed; the exact promise I made to a dying mentor. Cases where forgetting seems right, even good: the details of a petty feud with a collaborator; a nation's collective amnesia about a corrupt regime it overthrew. Harder ones: a town that keeps gossip alive about an innocent family - I think they ought to forget but can't locate who owes the forgetting. And me deliberately blurring the details of failed experiments, keeping only protocols - is that corruption?

I've been toying with a principle like 'mnemonic debt': we owe precise memory only within direct dependancy relations, and have a right (maybe duty) to forget outside them. Does that capture my pattern, or am I forcing it? What does it say about the town gossip case, and about a scientist who remembers every failed protocol? Please separate what's settled in this debate from what's genuinely contested - don't present one view as consensus. And tell me which of my verdicts I'd have to drop for the principle to work cleanly.
