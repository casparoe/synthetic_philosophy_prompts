As a software engineer without formal training in philosophy, I have been thinking about the classic puzzle of the statue and the lump of clay, and I have come to accept what I would call an “instantiated-design” view. On this account, the statue is not identical to the clay, but it is also not merely a modal or relational property of the clay. Instead, I think of the statue as a particular functional-design state that is realized in the clay—something analogous to how a running software instance is realized on particular hardware, yet is not identical to the hardware itself or to the source code. The clay is the substrate; the statue is the specific organizational and intentional structure that makes this arrangement count as that artwork. Destroying the clay destroys the substrate, but destroying the statue requires destroying that exact design-state being instantiated, which is why the clay might survive reshaping while the statue would not.

Given this framing, I want to understand what would genuinely count against it—not just the standard constitution objections, such as the modal argument that the clay can survive squashing while the statue cannot, or the worry that we are multiplying entities beyond necessity. I am interested in which intuitions, analogical cases, or results from other domains—say, about restored artifacts that replace all their original parts, about software identity across hardware migration, or about biological organisms that replace every cell—would be decisive versus merely awkward for this view. Which of these defeaters would a committed defender of the instantiated-design account concede as genuinely damaging, and which could they dismiss by appealing to differences between substrate-independent design and physical persistence? I also want to know whether the view risks collapsing into standard hylomorphism or into a purely relational account, and whether such a collapse would be fatal or just an embarrassment. Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what defenders would themselves concede from what critics merely allege, and say whether you think the position is well-insulated—or whether its insulation is itself a problem.
