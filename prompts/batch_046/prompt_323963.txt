I've been arguing with my roommate about that Pascal idea in the Pensees — justice without force is powerless, but force without justice is just tyranny, so they have to be married somehow. I tried applying it to real situations and my gut is all over the place.

Case one: cops enforcing a tax law thats technically legal but crushing to poor people. That feels like pure force with no justice, and its wrong. Case two: activists peacefully breaking an unjust law to protest. That feels like justice trying to work without force, and its admirable. Case three: a vigilante kills a murderer who got off on a technicality. That feels like force trying to stand in for justice, wrong in a different way than the cops. Case four: a parent yanks their kid out of oncoming traffic. Force for protection, clearly okay. But case five: a factory boss threatens layoffs unless workers accept a faster line-speed that actually improves safety protocols, though the workers never consented. The threat is economic force, the outcome is just, but there's no consent. I think thats wrong — but if so, why is the parent okay? Both coerce for a good end without asking.

Is there one principle that saves all these verdicts, or do I have to drop one? And what does it predict for an algorithm that automatically freezes bank accounts of people flagged for fraud with no human in the loop? Keep it tight — one paragraph, max 250 words.
