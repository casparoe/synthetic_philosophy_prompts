At a philosophy-of-mind reading group, Jordan and Sam debated eliminative materialism about propositional attitudes. Jordan opened by arguing that folk psychology is best treated as an empirical theory of internal representational states, one that has stagnated for centuries, failed to generate precise mechanistic explanations of mental illness or cognitive development, and has not yielded localized neural correlates for beliefs or desires; since neuroscience is already replacing its explanatory work, we should regard beliefs and desires as theoretical posits comparable to phlogiston and eliminate them in favor of mature neuroscience. Sam replied that this mischaracterizes folk psychology: it functions primarily as a framework for social coordination rather than a competing scientific theory, and its sustained predictive success—anticipating whether someone will keep a promise, avoid a threat, or seek food—demands explanation rather than dismissal. Jordan conceded that prediction succeeds but proposed a niche alternative: what looks like mental-state tracking is actually recognition of “behavioral scripts,” learned social patterns that generate regularity in human conduct, while mental-state language is a post-hoc narrative overlay comparable to attributing intentions to a river. The predictive success, Jordan insisted, reflects script conformity and environmental cue-response pairing, not the causal efficacy of internal beliefs. Sam countered that scripting cannot account for genuinely novel, unscripted behavior—such as improvised moral reasoning or original scientific hypothesis formation—where no prior pattern applies, and that Jordan’s own argument implicitly relies on intentional descriptions (“Jordan argued,” “Sam countered”) without explaining how such reports survive ontological elimination. Jordan responded that intentional vocabulary can be retained as a pragmatic fiction or heuristic register, just as physicists speak of “force” without committing to Newtonian ontology, and that legal categories like insanity or intent can be reconstructed on behavioral risk profiles, rehabilitation capacity, and social-protection needs without mental-state metaphysics. Sam then pressed a specific institutional point: the distinction between murder and accidental death turns on whether the agent believed the act was wrong and desired the harmful outcome; without these states, criminal law collapses into pure behaviorism that cannot distinguish malice from misfortune, undermining justice itself. Jordan answered that the law’s purpose is social protection and risk management, not metaphysical accuracy, so behaviorally reconstructed categories achieve the same practical results. They asked me to referee, and I genuinely could not decide. For each move, say whether it succeeds, partially succeeds, or fails, and note where each speaker misses something important—particularly whether Jordan’s script account can genuinely replace mental-state explanation in unscripted domains, and whether Sam’s reliance on intentional language in his objections undermines his own anti-eliminativist position. Finally, state who is more right overall and identify what both parties are leaving out, especially regarding whether eliminativism can be applied selectively or must be all-or-nothing.
