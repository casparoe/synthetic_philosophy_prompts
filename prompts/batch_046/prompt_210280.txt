I'm trying to sort out my gut feelings about when people with clashing value systems can cut a deal without just selling out. Here are some cases and where I land, but I keep going in circles.

Case 1: A pacifist nurse works in a military hospital on the condition she only treats civilians, but the hospital uses her presence in propaganda claiming even pacifists support our troops. She stays and saves lives. I think that's okay, even if she's being used a bit.

Case 2: A hardline environmentalist takes a consulting gig with an airline to design a carbon-offset scheme that's mostly greenwashing, but she uses the fee to fund direct action lawsuits. I think that's not okay — she's lending credibility for cash.

Case 3: Two organizers, one who thinks equality trumps liberty and one who thinks the reverse, agree on a policy that slightly restricts speech to reduce wealth gaps. They both feel they've lost something but gained stability. That feels like legitimate compromise, not corruption.

Case 4: A vegan chef takes a job at a steakhouse and redesigns the menu so plant-based sales jump and meat orders fall by half. Okay.

Case 5: The same chef takes the same job, cooks meat without complaint, and donates her salary to an animal sanctuary. Not okay — she's still part of the machine.

Cases 4 and 5 look almost identical on paper but I feel totally different about them. What's going on?

Case 6: A journalist who hates state secrecy strikes a deal with a goverment agency: they leak proof of corruption, but she has to sit on a smaller story about incompetence. She publishes the big one. I'm genuinely torn — part of me says she made a rational trade, part says she let herself be bought.

So is there a principle here about value arbitrage — like compromise is okay when your values actually reshape the other system from inside, but not when you're just extracting resources? Or is that too neat? Does it cover case 6? And what about a case I haven't listed: a religious doctor who works in a secular hospital that provides abortions, but she refuses to do them herself and instead trains residents in trauma care? Would my principle call that okay or not?
