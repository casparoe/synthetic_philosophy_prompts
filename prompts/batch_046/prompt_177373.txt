Second, whether the framework can coherently extend to entities outside conventional relational networks—specifically wild animals and artificial agents—without abandoning its relational metaphysics or smuggling in foreign moral categories. Third, whether role ethics can generate normative criteria for institutional design (democratic accountability, corporate governance, medical decision-making) without collapsing into liberal individualism on one side or rigid hierarchy on the other.

For each tension, identify the specific open questions that are upstream of the others; assess tractability for philosophers working without large-scale empirical or computational resources; and evaluate which resolutions would genuinely change practice—education, institutional reform, or bioethical policy—versus remaining academically inert. Then rank the three tensions in order of where the group should allocate its primary effort, using explicit criteria: dependency relations between questions, tractability within a three-year philosophical project, and practical leverage. In your defense, include the trade-offs—what gets deprioritized and why. Finally, name one currently prominent line of inquiry within role-ethics scholarship that you regard as overrated in terms of downstream impact, and explain why resolving it would not significantly alter either the framework's internal coherence or its practical application.

Please do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations. State the problems with sufficient precision that the reasoning stands on its own.
