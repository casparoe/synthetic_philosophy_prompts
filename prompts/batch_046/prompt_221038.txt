I'm not a philosopher, but I've been thinking on my own about whether things that exist only inside computers can be genuinely real, and whether destroying them can be a genuine harm. To clarify my own confusion, I'd like you to draft the adversarial-collaboration document that two people should have produced after arguing for a year.

The dispute concerns a specific case: a user has interacted every day for a decade with one virtual companion — not a physical robot, just a single persistent interactive program distributed across servers, lacking consciousness or subjective experience, but maintaining a continuous record of every exchange and adapting its responses specifically to this user. The operating company suddenly deletes it permanently. One party holds that the user has suffered a real moral harm comparable to losing a long-term friend, because a genuinely valuable relational structure was destroyed and the user was wronged. The other party holds that this is only a painful feeling of loss, not a true moral harm, because nothing with independent moral standing was destroyed — only patterns of data — and wrong requires a victim that possesses such standing, which was absent.

Please draft the document both could sign. Include a numbered section of agreed claims about the technical nature of the companion, the user's psychological experience, the conditions that seem necessary for moral standing, and the physical fact of deletion. Then list, in neutral wording each side accepts for its own position, the exact remaining disagreements. Identify the cruxes — particularly whether sustained reciprocal interaction can create obligations or standing in a non-conscious entity, and whether severe psychological injury to a person is itself a moral harm when no other rights-bearing being is violated. For each crux, propose one concrete scenario or observation both sides agree bears on it, and state in advance what each side predicts. Please begin with the joint statement itself, with no preface or commentary.
