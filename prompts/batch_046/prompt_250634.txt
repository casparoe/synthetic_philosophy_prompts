Write a joke about an author who writes a 1000-page book with one page per lottery ticket, each page arguing that particular ticket will lose. In the preface he says he's definatly sure there's exactly one mistake in the whole thing. The punchline should turn on the actual logical tension shared by the lottery paradox and the preface paradox—how you can rationally believe each individual page is right but still rationally believe the aggregate claim that one is wrong—not just generic 'books have errors' humor. Keep it casual, like you're explaining it to a friend at a bar, not a textbook excersise. And no citations needed, just the joke.
