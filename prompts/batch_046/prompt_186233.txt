Write a villanelle about the procreation asymmetry—the claim that bringing a suffering being into existence is bad, while failing to bring a happy being into existence is not bad, despite the seeming symmetry of absence and presence—but render it in the voice of an unactualized possible person addressing those who exist. Use the two refrains to carry the asymmetry’s two poles: one refrain on the non-badness of never knowing pleasure (no one is there to be deprived), the other on the badness of being brought into suffering (someone is there to bear it). Let the form do the philosophical work: the refrains should alternate through the tercets without ever balancing, then converge in the final quatrain side by side but unsynthesized, mirroring how the judgment about omission and the judgment about commission remain structurally mismatched. Keep the language precise enough that someone familiar with population ethics recognizes both the absence-of-deprivation point and the presence-of-harm point, but write so that a reader who has not encountered Benatar still receives a coherent poetic statement about creation, omission, and the weight of being on one side only.
