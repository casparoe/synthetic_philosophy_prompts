I am a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun working through philosophy of science systematically, and I find myself unable to referee a disagreement that has stalled my small reading group. The dispute concerns Maxwell's demon and whether its standard resolution genuinely removes the paradox or merely disguises a deeper problem. I report it below as a bystander with no settled view of my own.

Marcus argues that the demon has been fully exorcised. Landauer's principle establishes that the erasure of one bit of information dissipates at least kT ln 2, and Bennett's extension shows that this cost is borne by the demon's own memory register. The entropy reduction achieved by sorting fast and slow molecules is therefore exactly compensated by the thermodynamic cost of resetting the register. On Marcus's account, information is genuinely physical: the demon's computation carries an unavoidable entropic price, and there is no violation of the second law—only a demonstration that information and thermodynamic entropy are two descriptions of the same underlying reality.

Elena replies that this accounting merely relocates the difficulty rather than resolving it philosophically. She grants the quantitative balance but insists that identifying certain microscopic degrees of freedom as "the demon's memory" requires a prior functional framing—specifying which particles constitute the register, which interactions count as writing, and which as erasure—that is not present in the fundamental Hamiltonian. Without that framing, she contends, there is no objective fact of the matter about whether the total entropy of the universe has increased or decreased; the resolution therefore demonstrates not that information is physical, but that physical descriptions depend on an informational partition we impose.

Marcus responds that the framing is operational rather than subjective. We identify the register precisely as we identify a piston or a valve—by its causal-functional role in the sorting protocol. Once that role is fixed by the dynamics of the apparatus, the statistical distribution over the register's states is an objective feature of the system, and applying Shannon entropy is no more observer-dependent than applying temperature or pressure.

Elena counters that the boundary between "system" (demon plus register) and "environment" (the gas plus heat bath), as well as the classification of particular microstates as "errors" requiring erasure, are still selected by us in light of the protocol we have in mind. Consequently, the claim that the second law holds for the combined system presupposes the very informational distinction it purports to ground physically. Landauer's principle, on her view, does not eliminate the need for an interpretive partition; it embeds that partition inside the definition of the system and then treats the result as if it were discovered.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves above from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with brief justification for each rating. Then state who is more right overall about whether information is physically fundamental or framing-dependent in this resolution, and identify what both Marcus and Elena are missing—whether a further physical distinction about reversibility and correlation, a conceptual clarification about system boundaries in statistical mechanics, or a mischaracterization of what Landauer's principle actually requires of a computation.
