As a journalist covering philosophy and conceptual science for a serious ideas magazine, I am commissioning a deadpan satirical news report in the style of The Onion, approximately 500 words in length, with a headline and dateline. The piece should centre on causation by omission, presented through the fictional announcement that the Institute for Omissive Causation has adopted a new bureaucratic taxonomy of failures to act. The taxonomy must include invented but conceptually coherent categories — for instance, negative instrumental omission (not performing an action that would have produced an effect) and distributive omission (multiple agents each failing to intervene, with causal absence divided proportionally). The report should feature an invented Senior Omission Auditor defending the framework with mock-legal precision, and an invented critic arguing that the taxonomy cannot distinguish genuine omissions from physical incapacity, thereby risking a form of moral overload in which every non-intervention is treated as causally blameworthy. The satire should exaggerate genuine philosophical features of the debate: that identifying an omission as a cause requires selecting a normative baseline; that causal selection is often interest-relative rather than metaphysically fixed; and that treating absence as causally equivalent to presence threatens to erase the distinction between causing harm and allowing it. The humour should come from this conceptual fidelity, not from invented absurdities. The piece should be readable without specialist knowledge yet rewarding for readers familiar with the literature. Please write in British English, with British spelling and conventions, and do not include references, footnotes, or bibliographic citations.
