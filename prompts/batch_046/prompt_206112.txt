I'm trying to figure out my actual view on using biotech to abolish suffering across the board, going by gut reaction on cases.

Editing livestock embryos so they can't feel pain in industial farming: totally fine, almost obligatory.

Releasing a gene drive that suppresses the stress response in wild prey animals so they don't suffer being hunted: also seems okay, maybe even good?

A voluntary neural implant that raises your baseline mood but still lets you grieve properly when someone dies: seems okay.

That same implant, but engineered so you never feel moral outrage — you understand injustice but it never hits emotionally: not okay.

Editing human embryos so they never feel existential dread or spiritual longing, even if they report high wellbeing: I think that's wrong.

Offering free suffering-blockers to welfare recipients only, framed as cutting healthcare costs: wrong, feels like chemical pacification.

Engineering gut symbiotes in ant colonies to suppress nociception, assuming ants do suffer: I keep flip-flopping. One side says suffering is suffering regardless of brain size; other side says there's no integrated subject there to be wronged.

Is there a principle that captures the pattern? Maybe it's about preserving the emotional capacities that underpin autonomy and moral engagement, plus a consent condition for altering them? But then why am I okay with the wild prey gene drive — there's no consent there either, and I'm suppressing a capacity (fear) that's tied to survival agency?

What does that principle say about the ant case? And which of my verdicts has to be thrown out if I want a cleaner rule?
