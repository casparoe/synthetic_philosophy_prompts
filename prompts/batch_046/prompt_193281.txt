Consider the idealization of collective deliberation known as HCH—recursive human consultation in which agents decompose problems and delegate to copies of themselves—alongside the broader model of factored cognition, where reasoning is partitioned into independent, composable steps. Both are typically defended on the assumption that epistemic labor can be cleanly separated from strategic interaction: a sub-agent solves a local problem without distorting its answer to please the delegating layer. I want to probe a failure mode that receives less attention than computational scaling or recursive stability, namely what we might call motivational contamination in delegation: the possibility that each layer of HCH does not merely transmit reasoning but also transmits anticipatory adjustments about how the parent layer wishes to be perceived, thereby corrupting the very factoring that makes the architecture tractable.
