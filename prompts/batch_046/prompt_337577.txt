Draft the joint statement that a "systemic-footprint consequentialist" and a "direct-agency rights-theorist" could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaberation on meat, crop cultivation, and wild animal suffering. The consequentialist counts wild animals killed in crop fields and climate spillover as part of a diet's moral footprint, and takes the substitution problem seriously: replacing beef on pasture with legumes might increase total rodent and insect deaths. The rights-theorist thinks only beings intentionally bred and killed for food enter the moral calculus, so beef is worse than chicken (one large being vs many small ones) and crop-field deaths are background conditions, not the eater's fault.

Format:
1. Numbered agreed-claims section: empirical agreements (land-use ratios, estimated wild-animal deaths per calorie of crops vs pasture, ranges of sentience for cattle/chickens/insects/rodents) and shared normative commitments (e.g., that unnecessary suffering should be reduced, that factory conditions are bad regardless of theory).
2. Disagreements table with columns: Crux | Consequentialist's own wording | Rights-theorist's own wording | Shared case or argument both agree bears on it | Each side's predicition.
Cover these three cruxes:
- The attribution crux: is suffering caused indirectly via crop cultivation "surrogate suffering" attributable to the eater?
- The comparison crux: when comparing beef vs chicken, does the fact that one involves one large sentient being and the other many smaller ones with different capacities block a fact-of-the-matter answer, or is there a correct aggregation?
- The climate crux: should present dietary choices include predicted future wild-animal suffering from climate change?

Keep it focused, no bibliography or citation requests, and flag where the disagreement is genuinely normative and won't be settled by more data.
