I have been trying to work out my own position on why time appears to run in one direction, and I should like to know whether the pieces actually fit together. My commitments are roughly these: (1) the flow of time is real rather than illusory, so I reject the block-universe view that past and future exist on equal terms; (2) the thermodynamic arrow—entropy increasing—supplies the physical basis for this direction; (3) because statistical mechanics is time-symmetric, I think we must invoke a special low-entropy boundary condition near the origin of the universe; (4) yet I regard that boundary explanation as unsatisfying, since it replaces one mystery with a brute fact about the initial state rather than genuinely accounting for it; (5) I take the Boltzmann-brain concern seriously—if the arrow were purely statistical with no underlying direction, isolated conscious observers ought to be far more common than evolved ones like myself, which I treat as absurd; (6) consequently, I want to ground the arrow in cosmic expansion itself, treating the growth of space as giving time its orientation independently of entropy; (7) but I also hold that expansion cannot be explained without already presupposing a temporal direction, otherwise it collapses into a static geometric relation lacking flow. Are these jointly tenable, or am I guilty of circular reasoning between (6) and (7)? Which conflict is the gravest, and what is the least costly revision—abandoning real flow, accepting the brute boundary condition, or allowing that expansion might be primitive? Please rank the tensions by severity, and indicate whether any apparent contradiction dissolves upon closer inspection.
