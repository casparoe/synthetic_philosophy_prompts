I work on an evidence-review board for a regional health agency during outbreak responses. Every morning we recieve 10-15 field reports -- some are just "this village had no new cases today," others claim they've pinned down a transmission vector. We have about 20 minutes per report, no lab access, and I have to sign off on whether the claim is knowledge-grade (safe to base quarantine policy on) or just true belief that might flip tomorrow.

Can you turn the Meno problem -- specifically why knowledge is worth more than true belief because it's tied down -- into an actual decision procedure I can run? I want something like a three-anchor audit drawn from the statue idea but made concrete for field reports: the questions to ask in order, what counts as passing each anchor, where I stop and call it knowledge, where I call it un-tethered belief, and where the procedure gives up and I have to decide with my gut. Include a small set of test report-fragments I can apply, with one worked example for each kind of outcome.

Then run it on three reports:
1. A nurse reports zero cases after checking 40 households, but admits she skipped two remote ones because flooding made the road impassable.
2. A local official says the outbreak stopped right after they banned a specific market practice; he can show the ban date and the case curve dropping.
3. A report that passes every anchor but forces us to extend a costly lockdown we're desparate to lift -- I want to see the procedure tell me to keep it even though I hate the answer.

Also flag the misfires: a report that fails all anchors but is actually correct and we'd be fools to ignore, and one that passes every check but is still wrong because of a hidden confounder we couldn't see. Say exactly what the procedure can't do -- like predicting black-swan transmission routes or resolving value conflicts about economic harm.

Keep it practical, not a philosophy essay. I'm signing a form, not writing about Daedalus.
