Read Hamlet through Rouseau's anti-theater arguments -- specifically the idea that acting is morally degrading because the performer sells his presence and lives in constant dissimulation, and that watching theater trains spectators to consume fake emotion (amour-propre) instead of acting as citizens. Focus on the Mousetrap and Hamlet's antic dispostion: does using theater to expose Claudius vindicate representation, or does it show Hamlet being corrupted by performative logic? Also consider Ophelia's madness as public spectacle -- is the court's reaction an example of the detached voyeurism Rousseau fears? I want a two-way reading for a philosophy of art class: where does Shakespeare complicate or resist the critique, and does Hamlet's damage come partly from treating life as a play?
