I have been thinking on my own about the idea that the mind does not stop at the skull — for instance, that a person with severe memory loss might genuinely think with a notebook they carry everywhere, since the notebook plays the same role that biological memory plays for others. Last week I tried to extend this reasoning with a friend, and we arrived at an argument that now strikes me as suspicious because its conclusion seems to erase the boundary between people.

Consider the following steps. (1) If an object is continuously available to me, automatically endorsed without deliberation, and directly guides my behavior in the same way an internal mental state would, then it is not merely a tool I use but literally part of my cognitive system. (2) The predictive-text algorithm in my messaging app satisfies all three conditions: it is always present in my pocket, I accept its word suggestions without conscious evaluation, and it completes my sentences, thereby guiding what I communicate. (3) Therefore that algorithm is part of my mind. (4) My friend uses the identical algorithm through the same application, running on the same server weights, and it completes her sentences in exactly the same predictive fashion. (5) If the algorithm is part of my mind, and it is the very same running process serving her, then she and I share an actual component of our mental lives — not just analogous thoughts, but overlapping cognitive machinery. (6) But shared mental machinery implies that some of my mental states are also hers, which undermines any clear distinction between our personal identities. (7) Since personal identity cannot be that porous, either the predictive-text algorithm is not part of either of our minds, or the original idea that the notebook extends the mind must also be rejected.

I feel that something slips between the claim that we both use something and the claim that we both contain it, yet I cannot name the error precisely. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? In particular, if I preserve the notebook case for the Alzheimer's patient, am I forced to draw an arbitrary line that lets notebooks in but keeps algorithms out?
