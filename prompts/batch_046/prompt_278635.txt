Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a final quatrain, obeying the classic refrain and rhyme scheme—on the metaphysical dispute between endurantism and perdurantism. Let the speaker begin in the voice of an endurantist who insists she is wholly present at each instant, not stretched like a four-dimensional worm; make the first refrain (A1) declare this claim to complete presence, and make the second refrain (A2) answer with the perdurantist’s counter that she is only ever a temporal part. Arrange the turn near the quatrain around a specifically relativistic difficulty: because special relativity denies any absolute simultaneity, the “present slice” in which the speaker claims to be fully present is frame-dependent, so there is no unique universal now for her to occupy all at once. Use that physical result to put structural pressure on the refrain scheme—so that the repetition of A1 begins to sound unstable, as if the speaker’s “wholeness” is being observed differently from different inertial frames—while A2 accumulates the implication that persistence without a privileged frame may require accepting temporal parts. In the closing quatrain, do not resolve the tension into either view; instead, leave the reader with the precise strange consequence—that if identity is not frame-relative, then either one is scattered across spacetime or one’s present self is differently constituted for every observer. Keep the philosophical and physical details accurate enough that a specialist in the metaphysics of persistence or in the philosophy of relativity would recognize the argument rather than treat the science as decorative imagery.
