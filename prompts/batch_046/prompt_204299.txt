I work as a compliance reviewer for a robotics firm that deploys household assistants, and every reward-function amendment must pass my team—three engineers with no philosophy background—within a forty-minute window before it reaches the simulator. Lately we have been arguing over whether a rule like avoid unnecessary side effects can be written into code without first committing to a complete theory of human welfare, which our board has forbidden us to hard-code. The debate came up again over dinner with friends, one of whom mentioned impact measures in low-impact AI research, and I am now taking a course on practical ethics where the boundary between instrumental constraints and value judgments seems especially murky.

Please construct a reusable decision procedure my team can run on any submitted behavior policy. Present it as an ordered sequence of tests or questions: what evidence counts as sufficient at each stage, where the procedure stops with a rejection, where it must hand the case to a values officer because the question is normative rather than technical, and where it is structurally guaranteed to misfire. Ground the procedure in the philosophical distinction between structural footprint and axiological evaluation, but do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. As part of the framework, introduce and apply two analytical dimensions—task-correlated perturbation and task-orthogonal displacement—to measure a policy's causal footprint without invoking a full utility function.

Then work through two proposals. First, apply the procedure to a policy that prevents the robot from rearranging living-room furniture in order to reach a charging station; the procedure should return an answer that the engineering team will find unwelcome. Second, apply it to a policy that stops the robot from shutting off a noisy appliance that is actually a concealed life-support device; here the structural measure should fail and the decision should be flagged for human judgment. Conclude by stating explicitly what the procedure cannot settle, particularly whether necessary can be defined without circular reference to the very values the framework seeks to avoid specifying.
