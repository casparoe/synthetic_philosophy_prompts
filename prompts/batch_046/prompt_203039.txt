Construct a thought experiment that tests whether Bernard Williams's integrity objection—centered on the charge that certain moral theories require an agent to harbor an alienating extra thought about her own deepest commitments—applies symmetrically or asymmetrically across consequentialist and Kantian frameworks. Design the case so that a consequentialist agent can honor a deeply personal, non-generic project (for example, sustaining a long-running community art collective that defines her practical identity) without engaging in explicit utility calculation at the moment of choice, because the project's value is fully internalized in her stable preference structure. Contrast this with a Kantian agent who, facing the same forced choice between preserving that collective and preventing a moderate but definite harm to strangers, must first derive permission to prioritize the commitment through a universalizability test applied to maxims that weight personal attachments—thereby inserting a deliberative step that stands between her and the commitment. The scenario must be realistic (set in a workplace or local institution, not a trolley or disaster fantasy), confined to one paragraph, and must involve only harms and goods that a normal decision-maker could face.

After presenting the scenario, provide the following: (a) state the action at issue, the reasoning method each theory employs, and the relevant nearby worlds or alternative actions; (b) show step-by-step why the consequentialist avoids the alienating extra thought while the Kantian is forced into it, and explain why this result does not collapse into the generic claim that all moral reasoning is alienating; (c) identify precisely which structural feature of the case—internalized value versus derived permission—does the work, and explain what happens if that feature is removed (for instance, if the consequentialist has not yet internalized the project's value and must compute); and (d) include an honest note on how a defender of the Kantian view could attack the case, specifically by arguing that the universalization step is not an intrusive extra thought but the very form through which the agent respects her commitment as rational. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for specific textual quotations. The purpose of the exercise is to determine whether the design genuinely generates the reversed verdict from the theories rather than stipulating it.
