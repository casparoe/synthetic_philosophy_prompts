I've been trying to formalize a worry about Parfit's hitchhiker and rational irrationality, and I've ended up with an argument that seems to collapse all diachronic rationality into an infinite regress. Here it is, with numbered steps.

(1) In the hitchhiker case, it is rational for the agent to form a prior intention to pay the driver after being rescued, even though once rescued there is no causal benefit to paying. So rationality can require acting, at time t, against the causal reasons available at t.

(2) If rationality can override present causal reasons in virtue of a prior commitment, then the authority of that commitment must derive from the rationality of forming it at the earlier time t-1.

(3) But the formation of that commitment at t-1 is itself an action. At t-1, the agent's causal reasons might have favored forming a different commitment (e.g., committing to defect whenever it is causally advantageous).

(4) Therefore, the rationality of the commitment formed at t-1 must itself be justified by an even earlier commitment formed at t-2, and so on.

(5) This generates a regress: every rational commitment requires a prior rational commitment to authorize it, with no ground-level commitment that is rational on its own.

(6) If there is no ground-level commitment, then there is no non-circular justification for any diachronic rational choice.

(7) But if no diachronic choice is justified, then the very idea of planning, precommitment, or personal identity over time is rationally groundless.

(8) Since (7) is absurd—planning and precommitment are not only possible but central to rational agency—something in (1)-(6) must be wrong.

I feel the pressure is strongest at (2) and (4), but I'm not sure whether rejecting them saves the hitchhiker intuition or destroys it. Which step should I reject? What does giving up that step cost—does it make rational irrationality a mere psychological quirk rather than a normative principle? And if you think the argument is actually valid, which premise is the least costly to abandon?
