I was a biologist for about thirty years, mostly studying cell signaling, and since retiring I've been pokin around philosophy of mind and aesthetics for fun. Lately I've been stuck on letterforms — specifically what makes every 'a' an 'a', no matter the font, size, or whether it's handwritten by a kid or carved into stone.

Here's the pattern I keep seeing but don't have a word for. Take three cases: a Times New Roman lowercase 'a', a child's scrawl that's basically a circle with a stick, and some decorative capital where the loop is almost shut but has a tiny gap. None share a single visible feature — loops open or closed, stems left or right or missing, proportions all over the place. Yet anyone literate sees all three as the same letter, and specifically as 'a' rather than 'o' or 'd', even though those also mix loops and sticks. Even weirder, if you show someone a shape halfway between 'a' and 'o' — a circle with a tiny tail that might just be a smudge — people don't just call it ambiguous; they often feel it's being 'pulled' toward one letter or the other, like there's some gravitational center.

Now I know about family resemblance and prototype theory, but this feels different. Some pairs of 'a's share literally nothing visible — the decorative capital and the child's scrawl — yet they're seen as identical in letter-identity, not just similar. And it's not averaging toward a mental prototype, since people recognize wildly distorted 'a's they've never seen, including ones that look nothing like any 'standard' version. At the same time there's clearly no hidden checklist of necessary features, since nobody can write one down, and yet agreement is rock-solid across cultures.

So is there an established term for this exact kind of categorization? One with no shared features, no canonical exemplar, no explicit rules, near-unanimous agreement, and that 'pull' on ambiguous cases? If so, how is it distinct from family resemblance, prototype effects, and essentialism? If not, what should we call it, and where are its edges — does it only work for learned symbols like letters, or could it cover recognizing a melody or face the same way? I don't just want a label; I want the carving: what the thing is, what it isn't, and whether anyone's mapped it precisely.
