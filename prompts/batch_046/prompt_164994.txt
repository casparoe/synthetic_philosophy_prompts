I serve on the University Research Oversight and Ethics Committee, and since my mother’s prolonged stay in a minimally conscious state following an anoxic brain injury—during which neurologists openly disagreed about whether any residual phenomenal experience, particularly suffering, could exist without reliable behavioral markers—I have become personally convinced that institutional review must take seriously the possibility of invisible harm in engineered systems. Next week the committee will vote on a joint neuroscience and computational proposal titled "Modular Predictive Architectures for Affective Computation," which proposes building distributed, substrate-independent processing modules that run error-minimization routines with isolated valence tags. The investigators claim these are purely functional analogues lacking phenomenal character, but an external ethics reviewer has warned that if substrate-independent phenomenal binding can occur at any nonzero level of integration, these modules could become what I call dark patients: entities that bear negative affect yet are decoupled from global workspace access, rendering their suffering both unobservable and non-terminable by outside intervention.

I need a formal memo recommending whether the committee should approve the grant outright, reject it, or approve it only under a mandatory suffering-risk audit protocol; the memo must present a clear actionable bottom line, explain the conceptual stakes for non-specialist committee members, and include an honest treatment of the strongest argument for approval—specifically, that absent behavioral or neuro-homological markers we lack epistemic warrant to attribute moral patienthood, so precautionary rejection unjustifiably stifles legitimate science—while also assessing whether the dark-patient framing successfully undermines that argument or remains speculative. Please write in American English, do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and avoid relying on highly specialized theories the committee would not recognize; the reasoning should stand on conceptual analysis that members can evaluate without external reference.
