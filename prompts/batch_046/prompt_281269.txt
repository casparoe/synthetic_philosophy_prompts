Here is my philosophical profile on phenomenal consciousness and illusionism, which I present for coherence-checking. (1) I hold an illusionist view of phenomenal consciousness: there are no intrinsic qualitative properties, and talk of what-it-is-likeness tracks representational states that are systematically mischaracterized by introspection. (2) I take Chalmers's framing of the hard problem to be genuinely unsolved—no functional or physical story closes the explanatory gap—but I treat the gap as an epistemic limit rather than proof of non-physical ontology. (3) I think phenomenal concepts are direct-reference mechanisms that genuinely pick out properties, except the properties they pick out are actually specific neural-integration patterns that we erroneously conceive as non-physical feels. (4) I reject Nagel's claim that there is something it is like to be a bat in any phenomenal sense, though I grant bats have a minimal proto-phenomenality consisting solely in informational accessibility to internal states. (5) I regard first-person reports about consciousness as systematically unreliable because they are generated by the same monitoring mechanisms that produce the illusion, yet I also consider my own first-person judgments more secure than any third-person neuroscientific claim.
