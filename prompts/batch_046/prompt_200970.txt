Consider the Löbian obstacle facing self-trusting reasoning agents: within Gödel-Löb modal logic, any system that can formalize its own proof apparatus and endorses its own soundness is blocked from proving its own consistency. This creates a conceptual bottleneck for models of artificial or human agents that seek to modify their own reasoning rules while maintaining justified confidence in the result.

Imagine you are advising a philosophy-and-logic research group with three years of funding and no empirical engineering division. They want to prioritize conceptual work on how agents can navigate this obstacle. Which open questions and lines of work should they pursue first?

Specifically, weigh the relative urgency of: (1) whether bounded or resource-limited rationality dissolves the obstacle; (2) whether reflective equilibrium or coherence-based methods can replace full self-endorsement; (3) how to formalize graded or partial self-trust that falls short of universal soundness commitment; and (4) whether weakening the underlying logic — for example, abandoning the modal fixed-point properties — is conceptually viable or merely relocates the problem.

Defend a clear ordering. For each item, explain which other questions depend on its resolution, how tractable it is for philosophers using conceptual and formal tools alone, and what theoretical finding — a formal counterexample, a consistency proof under modified axioms, or a conceptual clarification — would cause you to revise the ranking. I want an agenda, not a bibliography: the criteria should be upstream dependency, tractability without ML experimentation, and impact on the design of self-modifying agents that must trust their own reasoning.
