Write a short research proposal (approximately two pages) for a six-month independent study fellowship in philosophy. The project examines Zhuangzi's account of death as transformation (hua) and asks whether his refusal of conventional grief can be sustained without a prior metaphysical commitment to the illusory nature of the self or to the dissolution of personal identity into the great clod. Existing interpretations divide between mystical readings, which treat ego-dissolution as the necessary ground for equanimity, and pragmatic readings, which treat the stance as an affective discipline independent of ontology; neither explains why Zhuangzi pairs descriptions of death as natural change with explicit rejections of mourning without either importing a hidden metaphysics or reducing the view to psychological management. Your proposal should state this precise question, explain why it remains open and why resolving it bears on the wider issue of whether attitudes toward death require metaphysical justification, outline a method combining close textual analysis of the relevant inner chapters with comparative attention to recent philosophical work on transformative experience, describe what a successful result would look like, specify what would count as failure (for example, if the texts prove irreducibly ambiguous or if the project collapses into pure exegesis lacking broader philosophical purchase), and provide a realistic assessment of what can be achieved in six months. Write in British English and do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
