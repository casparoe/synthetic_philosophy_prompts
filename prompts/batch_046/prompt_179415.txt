I'm a high school student preparing an interview for my school's philosophy podcast with an unnamed defender of what I'll call the registry-indexed view of artifactual persistence, applied specifically to the Ship of Theseus. She denies that persistence is secured either by continuous spatiotemporal existence of the original planks or by preservation of structural form, and instead argues that the ship remains the same vessel solely through its uninterrupted enrollment in a maritime maintenance registry that assigns a single institutional index across successive physical replacements, treating the ship as analogous to a legal corporation rather than to a natural object. I want a sequence of formal, progressively tighter questions that press this position without letting her retreat to generic metaphors about the idea of the ship. First, ask how her registry distinguishes genuine persistence from a case in which two independent registries accidentally track overlapping sets of planks—does the index do metaphysical work, or is it merely an epistemic bookmark? Second, ask about a counterfactual in which the ship is rebuilt plank-by-plank in secret by a rival crew with no registry entry: does she deny that this reconstructed vessel is Theseus's ship, and if so, does that mean institutional recognition constitutes persistence rather than merely recording it? Third, anticipate her likely reply that recognition is constitutive only for artifacts and ask whether that renders artifactual identity mind-dependent in a way that conflicts with her claim to offer an objective, non-psychological account of persistence; press for whether she can maintain that distinction without collapsing into conventionalism. Finally, ask what kind of evidence could genuinely undermine the view—if archaeologists discovered an ancient seafaring culture that maintained vessels through perfect physical continuity but left no registry of any kind, would she be forced to concede that persistence can occur without indexing, or could she expand the notion of registry to include implicit oral traditions without rendering her theory unfalsifiable? Please frame these so they build on one another, appropriate for a student interviewer who has studied the metaphysics of material objects but needs the logical pressure points made explicit.
