Write an essay examining whether instrumental convergence genuinely follows from the orthogonality thesis, or whether their standard pairing in discussions of advanced artificial intelligence rests on unstated auxiliary assumptions. Begin by clearly distinguishing “thin” orthogonality—the claim that any utility function is in principle compatible with any level of general intelligence—from “thick” orthogonality—the stronger claim that the cognitive architectures, learning dynamics, and world-modeling capacities that constitute intelligence are themselves neutral with respect to the goals they eventually serve. Then analyze the derivation of instrumental convergence: does the tendency of intelligent agents to pursue self-preservation, resource acquisition, and goal-content integrity flow solely from the logic of means-end reasoning given arbitrary final ends, or does it require further commitments about environmental scarcity, the coherence of preferences over time, and the design constraints of rational agency? Consider specifically whether an agent engineered with thick orthogonality—for instance, one whose learning dynamics are deliberately decoupled from long-term strategic planning—would still exhibit convergence on standard instrumental goals. Reach a justified conclusion about whether the two theses are conceptually coupled or whether instrumental convergence is an independent empirical claim that orthogonality alone cannot license.
