For my graduate seminar on causal explanation in the human sciences, I want the students to articulate the full specification for a theory that treats cultural values as genuinely causally efficacious—not merely as post-hoc rationalizations or as correlates of material conditions—before they assess any particular historical thesis. Take Weber’s Protestant-ethic argument about the origins of modern capitalism as the motivating benchmark, but treat it as a conceptual test case rather than an empirical claim to be verified or refuted.

Write the desiderata: the complete list of conditions that any adequate account of value-causation must satisfy. For each, supply the conceptual consideration or counterexample that motivates it—e.g., distinguishing causal influence from spurious correlation with economic infrastructure; accommodating actors who systematically misrecognize their own motives; preserving the normative, reason-giving force of values without reducing them to individual psychological dispositions; avoiding both economic reductionism and the reification of collective mentalities into independent agents; and maintaining explanatory relevance without collapsing into functionalist just-so stories. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable for any causal account and which reflect particular methodological commitments (interpretivist versus covering-law, or hermeneutic versus nomological).

Then introduce an additional constraint drawn from contemporary philosophy of science: the theory must also be compatible with an interventionist or mechanistic framework that demands identifiable difference-makers at the micro-level, yet it must do so without collapsing culture into aggregated individual psychology. Determine whether this constraint can be jointly satisfied with Weber’s own anti-reductionist, interpretive commitments, and if the two are incompatible, specify precisely which desideratum must be relinquished and what the cost is.

Conclude with a brief conceptual scorecard—how Weber’s own account, a rational-choice culturalist reconstruction, and a practice-theoretic alternative fare against your list—focusing on conceptual failures rather than empirical inadequacies, and flag the single requirement that does the most work in excluding popular candidates.
