Write a short story—approximately 800 words—set in a near-future clinic that offers voluntary symbiotic gestation: an unrelated volunteer host is physiologically coupled to an embryo belonging to other genetic parents, with real-time neural and endocrine linkage between host and fetal environment. The coupling can be dissolved, but doing so after the second trimester eliminates the embryo's only viable neural substrate, because the technology requires the host's specific biological milieu to sustain the developing brain.

Center the narrative on a host who requests withdrawal, citing psychological distress and bodily autonomy. Oppose her, in the deliberation, with the genetic parents and an ethics committee that appeals to the embryo's interest in a future of value—specifically, a future like ours that is technologically contingent on remaining coupled. Do not resolve the conflict through a legal verdict or an unambiguous moral winner; let the tension remain structurally embedded in the scenario.

The story should dramatize, without explicit philosophical lecturing, the conceptual clash between a Thomson-style bodily-rights argument for withdrawal and a Marquis-style future-value argument against it. Make the philosophical pressure visible in the details: the host lacks any genetic connection to the embryo; her body is not merely sustaining life but constituting the only possible form of that particular life; and withdrawal is physically possible but existentially destructive to the being whose future is at stake. Carry the conceptual conflict through character choice, physical description, and narrative irony rather than through dialogue that names the theories.
