In a graduate seminar on logical empiricism, a colleague summarized Otto Neurath’s picture-language project—the ISOTYPE system and his broader Bildersprache—roughly as follows. She said Neurath believed scientific disagreement stems mainly from the ambiguity of natural language, and that he proposed replacing propositional scientific reports with standardized international pictograms because images are interpretation-free: a drawing of a factory with smokestacks conveys the same quantitative fact to every observer regardless of theoretical commitment. On her account, these visual symbols were meant to serve as the ultimate protocol sentences—direct, unmediated records of experience that ground empirical knowledge without contamination by theoretical presuppositions. She concluded from this that Neurath’s program was essentially an early form of theory-neutral data visualization, and that its failure to achieve universal adoption demonstrates that empirical science can never escape the hermeneutic circle of linguistic interpretation.

I suspect this characterization quietly imports commitments from other members of the Vienna Circle and overstates both the epistemic status Neurath assigned to images and their intended role in scientific practice, but I am not well enough read in his writings to locate every distortion. Could you audit the summary carefully and indicate which parts a sympathetic interpreter of Neurath would accept, which parts misrepresent his actual view—particularly regarding the possibility of theory-free observation, the function of protocol sentences in his physicalist framework, and whether pictograms were meant to supplement or to supplant verbal language—and whether her concluding claim about the inevitability of linguistic interpretation actually follows from an accurate rendering of his position? I am especially uncertain about how his visual system relates to his holistic and physicalist account of scientific statements, and I would like to know what an accurate version predicts for contemporary attempts to build interpretation-independent visual databases in science.
