Write a formal dialogue between two philosophers, call them Mara and Lin, who disagree about the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem in moral motivation but who both reject the textbook framing in which an amoralist is moved only by self-interest. Instead, they should examine a subtler institutional case: a senior public-health commissioner who advocates decriminalizing personal possession of a particular synthetic opioid analog not because she judges criminal punishment to be morally wrong, but solely because epidemiological modeling shows that decriminalization reduces net mortality and infectious-disease transmission, and she treats this aggregate harm reduction as the only legitimate basis for public justification. Mara should defend the view that this is a paradigmatic wrong-kind-reason case: the commissioner’s justification treats the moral status of punishment (whether it violates dignity or autonomy) as merely instrumentally valuable, substituting an efficiency calculus for categorical moral reasoning, and thereby fails to supply reasons of the right kind even if the policy conclusion is correct. Lin should defend the opposing view that in collective institutional contexts, demanding categorical moral motivation is a category error—public agencies are not individual moral subjects, so harm-minimization is not the wrong kind of reason but the constitutive reason for institutional action, and insisting on categorical moral framing either renders institutional moral agency impossible or illicitly imports private virtue ethics into structural design. They must respond directly to each other’s actual points: Mara should press Lin on whether her view collapses into a form of institutional moral anti-realism that cannot distinguish between just and unjust policies that happen to share the same harm profile; Lin should press Mara on whether her demand for categorical reasons makes all institutional moral action necessarily impure rather than genuinely moral, and whether she can explain how a democratic legislature could ever legitimately act for moral reasons without reducing them to instrumental metrics. Both characters should be equally competent, neither a strawman, and the exchange should avoid generic restatements of familiar literature. Keep the reasoning precise and in the weeds—focus on the structure of reasons, the ontology of institutional agency, and whether categorical and instrumental reasoning can be separated in collective decision-making. End with a single line that begins with "Summary:" and states in one sentence the core unresolved tension between their positions.
