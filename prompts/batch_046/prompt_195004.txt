For an upper-division metaphysics final exam (60 minutes, approximately 800–1000 words, 25 marks total), draft one essay question on dispositions, finks, and masks. The prompt must be answerable without web access, must not ask for bibliographies or citations, and should not be solvable by reciting standard textbook summaries of classic cases. Instead, present a novel, fully self-contained scenario: compound X is a chemical catalyst that synthesizes its own inhibitor I as an essential part of its molecular structure. Under ordinary conditions I is always present internally, so X never manifests the catalytic disposition for reaction R; but if I is destroyed by an external agent, X catalyzes R and is itself destroyed in the process. A counterpart compound Y is structurally identical except that it entirely lacks I, is reactive under all conditions, and survives R intact. Ask students to adjudicate whether X and Y genuinely possess the disposition to catalyze R, requiring them specifically to: (i) distinguish whether I functions as a mask, a fink, or as part of the manifestation conditions of the disposition; (ii) evaluate whether an internally generated, identity-constituting suppressor differs metaphysically from an external interfering factor; and (iii) defend a consistent assignment of the disposition to X, Y, both, or neither, without collapsing the distinction between finks and masks. Provide a model answer of roughly 600 words, followed by a marking scheme distributing 25 marks across four criteria—correct application of the fink/mask distinction (8 marks), analysis of internal versus external suppression (7 marks), consistency of the overall assignment (6 marks), and clarity, precision, and argumentative structure (4 marks)—and for each criterion include a one-line note on the typical student error that should cost marks (e.g., treating I as an obvious external mask without engaging its essential, internal status; or asserting that both compounds share the disposition without resolving the contradiction with X’s never-manifesting behavior).
