I'm giving the commencement address at my sister's graduation next weekend — she's finishing her philosophy BA but I'm just a backend engineer so I'm way out of my depth. The dean asked me to talk about "building the future," and I want to avoid the usual cheerleader vibe where I tell everyone to go out and maximize human potential or whatever. I was reading about the procreation asymmetry, where it's apparently not wrong to skip having a happy kid but it is wrong to have one that suffers, and it broke my brain a little. I want that idea to actually shape the speech structurally: I shouldn't be able to just say "create more good things" because the asymmetry says there's no obligation to bring good into existence, only a duty not to bring harm. I made up this extension myself — applying it to startups, apps, maybe even AI systems we build — where "creating" something potentially good isn't automatically a win, but creating something harmful is. I know it's probably half-baked but I want to play with it honestly, not preach. The audience is split: half are her profs who know the literature and will catch every sloppy reference, half are parents who just want to cry and don't care about citations. So please write a five-minute talk that lands for both groups without being a lecture, and don't include any footnotes or reading lists — I don't have access to verify those and it would kill the vibe. Keep it casual, maybe leave a typo or two since I'm drafting this on my phone between deploys. The speech should feel like I'm genuinely working through the idea rather than delivering a verdict, and I want the asymmetry to constrain what I can and can't say about "the future we build."
