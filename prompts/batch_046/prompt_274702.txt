The first framework, which I will call the interruption model, treats habitat destruction as a direct cessation of a local suffering-stream: the animals currently present are killed, and the conditions that would have produced future generations are eliminated, so the aggregate suffering tied to that specific location is truncated.

The second framework, which I will call the displacement model, treats suffering as more like a fluid that shifts rather than vanishes: when one ecosystem is removed, the ecological functions and the wild populations they support are redistributed into adjacent or successor ecosystems, meaning the total suffering load may be conserved, relocated, or even amplified depending on population density and reproductive rates in the remaining spaces.
