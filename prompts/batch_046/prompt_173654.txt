I'm drafting a short essay for an advanced seminar on the dissolution of philosophical pseudo-questions, and I want to test whether the central argument holds up before I commit to the structure. The essay concerns Frank Ramsey's brief but influential remarks—delivered in the posthumous essay appended to his logical writings—about whether the question "What is the meaning of life?" can even be coherently posed. The standard reading, which I find too quick, treats Ramsey as applying a straightforward logical-empiricist dissolution: because "life" is not the kind of entity that enters into propositional structures with semantic values, asking for its "meaning" is grammatically malformed, analogous to asking for the color of a number. I want to push back by arguing that this dissolution conflicts with Ramsey's own broader pragmatist commitments, particularly his view that beliefs acquire functional significance through their role in guiding partial belief and preference ordering rather than through correspondence with atomic facts.

My proposed thesis is that Ramsey's framework actually permits—indeed requires—a distinction between global and local "meaningfulness" that he never makes explicit. If a belief like "this project is worth pursuing" can be functionally meaningful within an agent's system of partial beliefs without corresponding to any discrete fact, then there is no principled reason why a global framing belief—"my life as a whole is oriented toward something significant"—should be treated as a syntactic error rather than as a higher-order integration of those local functional roles. The essay would then ask whether Ramsey's dissolution of the global question secretly depends on smuggling in a criterion of significance that operates locally but is forbidden globally, and whether this creates an instability: either local coherence collapses back into a disguised teleological framing, or the global question was never genuinely dissolved but merely relocated into the architecture of preference. I am not asking you to evaluate the historical accuracy of every interpretive claim about Ramsey's intentions; rather, I want comments on whether the asymmetry I have constructed is philosophically interesting, whether it misrepresents the pragmatist resources available to him, and specifically whether there is a cleaner way to block the move from local functional coherence to global meaningfulness without simply stipulating that the latter is malformed. If the argument is unsound, I would particularly value suggestions about which feature of Ramsey's account of belief—perhaps his treatment of degrees of belief as grounded in betting behavior—does the work of preventing this slide, and whether that feature can be maintained consistently.
