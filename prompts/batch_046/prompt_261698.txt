I teach an upper-level seminar on well-being, about 12 students, one 75-minute session. Design a classroom game around Nozick's experience machine, but build in a twist that isn't just "would you plug in?" — I want participants to evaluate lives from two angles: a first-person present-tense stance and a retrospective "biographer" stance, and the rules should be arranged so they give opposite answers and feel the pull themselves before anyone names hedonism vs narrative or objective-list theories. Use only paper, pens, and small non-monetary stakes (points that convert to snack preference or just a public scoreboard). Maybe have them bid on hidden life-modules, then reveal properties and ask them to score the life both as the person living it now and as a historian writing about it ten years later.

Need clear phases with a timeline that fits 75 min, the payoff/scoring rules, any roles, and a debrief that maps the two decision-points onto the well-being views. Also tell me what will predictibly go wrong the first time — I think people will start gaming the retrospective frame once they smell the pattern — and be honest about which feature of the philosophical problem the activity can't reproduce (probably the phenomenology of not knowing you're inside the machine?). Don't ask participants to cite anyone; they should derive the conflict from the structure alone. Keep it fully runnable with no tech, and inject one slightly niche conceptual move if it sharpens the design.
