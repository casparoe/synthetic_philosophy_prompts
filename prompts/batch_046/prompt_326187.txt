A popular philosophy article I found summarizes hedonism and the heterogeneity problem like this: hedonism holds that pleasure is the only intrinsic good, but critics object that pleasures—such as tasting food, solving a puzzle, and feeling malicious satisfaction—are phenomenally too diverse to share one essential feature, so pleasure cannot name a single good-making property. The article says hedonists usually reply by identifying pleasure with a uniform hedonic tone, a raw subjective quality present in every case that makes all pleasures identical except for intensity. It then claims this reply fails because contemporary philosophy of mind distinguishes sensory pleasure, a bodily feeling, from attitudinal pleasure, a pro-attitude or liking; since these two kinds share no phenomenal substrate, hedonism cannot identify one common quality that unifies all pleasures, and the theory should therefore be replaced by a pluralist account of well-being.
