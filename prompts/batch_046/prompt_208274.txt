Here's an argument I cobbled together for a Stoic reading group and now it smells off. (1) The classical dichotomy says only what's in our power — our judgements and impulses — can be good or bad; everything else is indifferent. (2) Modern revivalists apply this to mental health: anxiety and grief are just bad judgements about externals, so they're within our control. (3) If an emotional state is within our control, then sustaining it when it harms us is a voluntary faliure — basically a vice. (4) So anyone who stays anxious or grief-stricken is morally failing to correct their own judgements. (5) But we dont think trauma survivors are vicious for being traumatised; we think they've suffered something. (6) Therefore the modern application of the dichotomy must be wrong, or at least (2) colapses.

Where does it go wrong, if it does? I'd really like to keep (1) and (5) — the classical claim and the intuition about trauma — but I'm open to losing (2) or (3). Is the argument sound and I just have to bite the bullet, or am I smuggling in an equivocation? If my framing is confused, tell me straight — dont just patch it politely.
