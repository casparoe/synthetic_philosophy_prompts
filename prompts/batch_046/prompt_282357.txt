A philosophy blog I came across summarizes Bas van Fraassen's Reflection principle in these terms: if an agent is now certain that her future self will fully believe some proposition P after collecting evidence, then rationality requires her to fully believe P already; consequently, genuine belief revision upon learning evidence is impossible, because any shift in opinion violates the principle. The post further claims that van Fraassen introduced the principle specifically to establish Bayesian conditionalization as the only rational updating rule, and that it implicitly assumes a Cartesian transparency about one's own reasoning processes—so that standard externalist objections to self-knowledge undermine the principle itself. I want to check whether I am being misled by this framing. Which parts of the summary accurately capture van Fraassen's view, and which parts conflate degrees of belief with full belief, overstate what the principle proves about updating, or present a tension as an absolute impossibility? Also, is the connection to Cartesian transparency and externalist critique a defensible extension of the principle, or does it misapply a thesis about mental-state access to a normative constraint on credence?
