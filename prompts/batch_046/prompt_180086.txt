Here are notes for a short essay. The central claim is that Otto Neurath's ISOTYPE picture-language, designed to avoid metaphysical commitments by using purely empirical visual symbols, implicitly relies on a distinction between iconic syntax—the structural rules governing how symbols are arranged—and iconic semantics—the factual content those arrangements convey. I argue that this distinction is not reducible to Neurath's physicalist account of protocol sentences, and that if it is real, it introduces a non-empirical framing condition that undermines his rejection of metaphysical pseudopropositions in visual form.

Specifically, take a committed position: is the syntax/semantics split genuinely present in Neurath's writings on picture languages, or does his physicalist monism dissolve it? If you think the tension is real, judge whether it is fatal to his anti-metaphysical program or can be resolved within it. Give definite reasons, not a balanced survey of possibilities.
