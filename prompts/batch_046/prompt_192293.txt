design a classroom activity for my grad reading group on moral status — about 10 people, 80 minutes, mix of philosophy and bioethics students. i want them to actually feel the pressure of gradational sentience, not just talk about it. specifically they should run into the problem that continuous moral value clashes with binary legal/ethical categories, and discover for themselves that there is no non-arbitrary place to draw the line between "moral patient" and "not," especially once you include entities like smart thermostats or LLMs that have some but not all markers.

the activity should be a structured board-game style deliberation. give me rules, roles (maybe a chair, advocates for specific entities, skeptics), materials i can print on index cards, and a payoff/stakes scheme that doesnt need money — maybe social credit or the right to keep/remove entities from a shared "protected" list. the participants should start by ranking entities from human infant down to a basic thermostat using observable behavioral criteria only (no inner life reports), then they have to vote on a sharp cutoff for legal protection. only after they've locked in their vote should you reveal that two entities stradle the line by a tiny margin, and that the thermostat scores higher on feedback-loop complexity than the insect. include a forced-revision round.

then the debrief: map each round onto a part of the gradationalist view, say what will predictably go wrong (probably people will smuggle in species membership or "potential" arguments), and be honest about which part of the idea this cant reproduce — maybe the metaphysical claim that sentience literally comes in degrees rather than being a threshold phenomenon with noisy measurement. also tell me how long each phase should take, and include one honest note on what will break the first time i run it.

make it casual — this is for a group that already read the basic literature, so dont lecture them in the prompt. maybe include one small typo.
