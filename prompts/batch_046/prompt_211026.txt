Write an essay on whether Davidson's radical interpretation can survive what I'll call 'systematic indexical drift'. Picture a small community where every speaker consistently swaps 'yesterday' and 'tomorrow' in a rule-governed fashion — they never actually disagree about when things happen, just about which word maps to which day. Does the principle of charity force us to correct their lexicon and read them as meaning what we do, or can we charitably accept the inverted mapings and translate? Weigh this against Davidson's own handling of malapropisms and belief ascription — don't quote him, just use the gist. Reach a proper conclusion and justify it: is radical interpretation ultimately about finding shared propositional content, or about constructing any coherent scheme that renders behaviour intelligible, even if it means tolerating a bizarre calender? Don't end non-committally.
