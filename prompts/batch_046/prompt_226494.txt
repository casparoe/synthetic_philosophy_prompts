My philosophy tutor has been applying Karl Jaspers's postwar framework—criminal, political, moral, and metaphysical guilt—to a sequence of hypothetical nations, and her rulings are as follows. First, a state whose bureaucratic apparatus systematically executed atrocities bears collective political guilt, even though criminal guilt remains strictly individual. Second, a society that passively permitted those crimes through widespread silent acquiescence does not incur collective criminal guilt, though its members may each carry individual moral guilt. Third, a democratic successor state that inherits the legal identity, treaties, and assets of the guilty regime retains the predecessor's political guilt and must discharge corresponding reparative obligations. Fourth, a nation whose present citizens are almost entirely post-atrocity immigrants living inside the same continuous legal entity are personally free of moral guilt, yet the institutional state itself remains politically responsible. Fifth, an ethnic minority inside the guilty state that was itself victimized bears none of the four categories. Next week she will judge a new case I have constructed: a sovereign nation that never existed as a unified state during the period of atrocity, because its territory was then divided among three colonial powers, but after decolonization it adopted the administrative boundaries and legal succession of only one of those colonial zones; it now petitions for international recognition as a victim-state entitled to reparations from the other two zones, while survivors of the colonial administration's crimes simultaneously demand that it apologize and make restitution for actions committed by the very administration whose legal identity it has inherited. Predict her verdict—whether she assigns it political guilt, victim status, both, or neither—identify which implicit rule she is running among an institutional-continuity rule, a benefit-and-complicity rule, and a collective-moral-agency rule, name the closest analog in her earlier track record, and specify the further hypothetical case that would finally distinguish these rules from one another. Give a committed response: take the definite position that best coheres with her precedents, state the principle she would be adopting in either direction, identify the analog that pulls strongest, and be explicit about exactly where Jaspers's taxonomy is being stretched beyond its original design.
