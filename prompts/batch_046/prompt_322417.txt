I am preparing a single 75-minute graduate seminar in philosophy of science for students who have read Foucault on medicalization and biopower but have not encountered Ian Hacking's thesis of looping effects in psychiatric classification. I want to teach the mechanism itself—not as generic social construction, but as a phased process of classification, uptake, behavioral modification, and category revision—while insisting on a distinction between strong looping, in which the interactive kind is genuinely altered, and weak looping, in which only the population distribution shifts.

Please provide a teaching plan rather than the lecture content. First, specify the exact order in which the conceptual pieces must be introduced, with dependency reasoning: what must be secured before the student can grasp the loop, and why the strong/weak distinction should be introduced at the point you choose rather than earlier or later. Second, identify the single carrying case study that should anchor the entire session; it must not be homosexuality or anorexia, which this audience has likely encountered in other courses. Instead, build the case around the classification of borderline intellectual functioning and its interaction with special-education eligibility thresholds, using it to illustrate both institutional gatekeeping as the loop's vehicle and a specific point at which the loop stalls or reverses. Third, map the predictable misconceptions—especially the conflation of looping with placebo dynamics, the assumption that all psychiatric categories loop with uniform intensity, and the belief that conscious self-identification is a necessary condition for looping—stating precisely where in the sequence each should be anticipated and defused. Fourth, design one closing diagnostic question that forces a student to apply the strong/weak distinction to an unfamiliar case rather than recite a definition. Finally, identify one significant element of Hacking's broader framework that must be explicitly omitted at this level—such as his statistical analysis of child abuse or his taxonomy of natural versus social kinds—and justify why leaving it out protects the coherence of the session. The plan should be self-contained: do not include assigned readings, bibliographies, or references requiring external verification.
