I would like you to adopt the role of an expert philosopher of science and formal epistemologist, and to begin with brief brainstorming: outline the core commitment of the principle, distinguish it from ordinary Bayesian updating, and flag the hidden assumptions that allow it to bridge subjective belief and objective world-structure.

Here is the specific position I want you to pressure-test. The view holds that the Principal Principle is not a contingent bridge law but a constitutive norm of rational coordination: whenever agents share the same admissible evidence about an objective chance-generating mechanism—including structurally opaque contexts such as the selection of a cosmological vacuum state in eternal inflation, where empirical frequency data is unavailable—any divergence in their credences about the outcome must signal a rational failure on at least one side. On this fortified reading, deference to objective chance is conceptually mandatory, and attempts to "update away" from the known statistical weight based on theoretical considerations about underlying dynamics—for example, arguments that certain vacuum configurations are dynamically disfavored regardless of their chance—are epistemically illegitimate unless they are first incorporated as admissible evidence that revises the chance assignment itself.

What, precisely, would have to be true for this fortified version to be wrong? I want an honest exposure map, not a list of familiar objections. Which formal results, conceptual arguments, or physically live scenarios would genuinely undermine it rather than merely complicate its application? Is there a coherent case—perhaps in quantum foundations or in reasoning about hidden-variable substrates—where rational agents should systematically diverge from the objective chance despite shared admissible evidence? Would defenders of this position concede that undermining-future scenarios, or direct structural access to the mechanism generating the chances, count as legitimate defeaters, or would they absorb these by redefining what counts as admissible? Please distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence that would merely be awkward, state whether the position risks anything substantive or has been insulated by qualification, and be willing to conclude that it is either genuinely vulnerable or well protected—and whether that protection reflects intellectual virtue or ad hoc immunization.
