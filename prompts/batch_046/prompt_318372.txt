I'm writing up my epistemological profile for our seminar on the regress problem, and I want to check whether my commitments actually cohere before I defend them in my term paper. Here is where I stand.

First, I reject foundationalism: there is no class of epistemically basic beliefs that justify others without themselves requiring justification. Even perceptual beliefs depend on prior conceptual framing, so nothing is "given" in the relevant sense. Second, I reject coherentism as well: coherence is neither necessary nor sufficient for justification. A maximally coherent conspiracy theory remains unjustified, and an isolated but well-supported perceptual belief need not cohere with much else. Third, I therefore accept infinitism: for a belief to be justified, it must be supported by an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons, with neither circular loops nor foundational termini.

Fourth, I also hold that rational agents are permitted—indeed, in practice required—to halt inquiry when they reach beliefs that exhibit what I call perturbation-resistance: they remain intact across a broad range of counterfactual revisions to adjacent beliefs, even though no terminal reason has been reached. Fifth, I distinguish sharply between being justified and being able to articulate one's justification; an agent can possess an infinitely extending chain without being able to state more than its first few links. Sixth, I maintain that the regress problem governs propositional justification, not knowledge itself, so knowledge can obtain even when the underlying justifying chain is not fully traversed or fully available to the agent.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each conflict? For every real tension, indicate the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what it costs me. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most problems at once.
