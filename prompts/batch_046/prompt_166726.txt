I'm trying to get my philosophical profile on aesthetic value straight for a discussion group, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present it. Here are the commitments I've ended up with. (1) I accept Hume's basic framework that judgments of beauty are expressions of a refined sentiment rather than discoveries of mind-independent properties like shape or mass; the feeling is not arbitrary, but it is still a feeling. (2) I think there is a genuine standard of taste that distinguishes correct from incorrect aesthetic judgments, grounded in the responses of observers with delicacy of perception, extensive comparative practice, freedom from prejudice, and sound mental faculties. (3) I hold that this standard makes aesthetic judgments objective in a minimal, intersubjective sense — they can be genuinely erroneous in a way that isn't reducible to "I happen to dislike this" — but not objective in the stronger, response-independent sense that beauty exists whether or not any minds exist. (4) I also think that thick aesthetic properties (for instance, being serene, being garish, being unified) depend for their instantiation on thinner perceptual properties plus culturally variable framing conditions, yet these framing conditions do not reduce the thick properties to pure projection or social convention. (5) Finally, I think the explanatory gap between physical descriptions of artworks and our aesthetic experience is real but not unique to aesthetics; it parallels the gap in moral experience, and this parallel supports treating both domains as irreducible but not therefore subjective.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only appear to be, and what the least costly revision would be if I want to keep the core Humean picture intact. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. For each genuine tension, specify whether the cheaper fix is to weaken my claim about minimal objectivity in (3), to complicate my account of thick properties and framing in (4), or to abandon the analogy with moral experience in (5). Also tell me what my package predicts for a case I haven't listed: can an artwork be genuinely beautiful if every observer meeting Hume's ideal conditions remains, even after full practice and comparison, entirely unmoved by it? If not, does that show that (2) collapses into a form of response-dependence I was trying to avoid in (3)?
