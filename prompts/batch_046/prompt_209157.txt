I am writing a novel in which a constitutional lawyer discovers that every statute in her country was originally drafted by a warlord's secretary and only later rewritten in the language of rights. Reading Pascal's fragments on justice and force—particularly the observation that because we cannot make what is just strong, we make what is strong just—I have become convinced that this is not mere political cynicism but a structural feature of legal narrative: force writes the first draft, and justice is the revision that makes the text readable to posterity. Please argue for this position: that Pascal's separation of justice and force should be interpreted as a theory of retrospective legal narration, in which the durability of a regime depends less on the justice of its origins than on its capacity to maintain a coherent story linking present authority to past violence. You need not cite scholars or provide a bibliography; work only from the conceptual resources of Pascal's thought and the logic of narrative form. At the end, state how confident you are in your overall claim and identify the single step in your argument about which you are least certain.
