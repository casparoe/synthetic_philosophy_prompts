Write a short story exploring weakness of will (akrasia) through the lens of hyperbolic discounting, but set in a near-future where the protagonist wears a non-invasive cognitive interface that renders their own intertemporal preference curves visible in real time—complete with the steep present-bias near the origin and the flatter long-range slope—without allowing them to alter the underlying dynamics. Center the narrative on one high-stakes decision in which the protagonist must choose between an immediate, trivial sensory reward and a substantially larger future benefit they have already endorsed as superior. Dramatize the specific phenomenology of watching the numerical discount curve steepen at the precise moment of choice, knowing the exact shape and magnitude of the future regret it predicts, yet finding that this knowledge exerts no corrective force on action. Avoid generic addiction or procrastination tropes; instead, treat the hyperbolic curve itself as an almost independent presence—something the protagonist can model, discuss, and temporarily constrain with external commitment devices, only to observe it reassert its original geometry the instant the constraint is tested. Include a scene in which the protagonist attempts to explain the failure to an interlocutor who discounts exponentially and therefore genuinely cannot understand why perfect information about one's own curve does not automatically align present choice with long-run preference. The story should ask, implicitly, whether akrasia remains intelligible as a failure of will when the agent possesses complete predictive knowledge of that failure in advance.
