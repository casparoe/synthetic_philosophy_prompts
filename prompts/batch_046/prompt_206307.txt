I'm writing up my philosophical profile on tragic art for a seminar and need a consistency audit before I present. Here are my commitments: (1) tragic enjoyment requires representational insulation—the suffering is directed at representations rather than real states, which makes negative affect aesthetically processable; (2) if that insulation fully collapsed and the audience took the suffering as genuinely occurring, enjoyment would be morally defective; (3) the best tragedies, which I call threshold works, deliberately weaken this insulation so the audience feels the suffering exceeds mere fiction and touches real human vulnerability; (4) aesthetic value is partly constituted by this weakening, not achieved despite it; (5) evolutionary explanations (emotional practice, social coordination) explain our general attraction to tragedy but cannot account for why we rank some works as profoundly meaningful and others as mere melodrama. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severely? For each real tension, specify the cheapest revision and what it costs. Then tell me which single change to my package would clean up the most problems without giving up my core claim that the greatest tragedies depend on weakening representational insulation.
