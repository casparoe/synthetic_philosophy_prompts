I work as a labor-policy auditor for a multinational firm subject to French 35-hour statutory limits, Japanese karoshi-liability norms, and U.S. Protestant-work-ethic organizational culture. I need a reusable decision procedure, not for productivity but for whether a scheduling proposal respects a right to leisure conceived as three non-substitutable temporal domains—vocational labor, recuperative rest, and autonomous leisure—rather than treating rest as leisure’s substitute. Construct an ordered five-node checklist: first, whether the proposal collapses autonomous leisure into labor or rest; second, whether it treats recuperative rest as substitutable for leisure; third, whether its justification invokes an internalized calling-ethic rather than an externally imposed duty; fourth, whether it generates karoshi-type structural coercion despite meeting formal hour caps; fifth, the precise point where the procedure must halt and yield to a values judgment. Specify for each node what answer stops the procedure with a rejection and what answer lets it pass, then apply all five nodes to two proposals—a compressed four-day week with mandatory on-call weekends, and an unpaid Saturday community-building shift—making sure to include the case where the procedure approves a result that clearly violates leisure rights. Present your response as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
