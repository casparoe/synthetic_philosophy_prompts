I'm taking a seminar on early modern metaphysics, and in our discussions of Berkeley's Principles and Dialogues my professor pressed us to distinguish the master argument proper from the various epistemic arguments about our inability to step outside our own ideas. I've become convinced that the master argument is best reconstructed not as a claim about the limits of human conception, but as a thesis about the conditions of meaningful property-attribution: to call something a material substance existing unperceived is to apply predicates whose content necessarily derives from perceptual or relational structures that already presuppose a perceiver. I think this reconstructed version actually gains support from contemporary representationalist theories of perception, which treat all object-property ascription as mediated by representational content, leaving no semantic room for the attribution of bare primary qualities to mind-independent matter. But I want to be honest about what could genuinely defeat it, not merely what sounds intuitively wrong.

What should actually move someone who holds this view? I'm particularly interested in whether there are successful cases of property-attribution in mathematics or physics—say, to unobservable entities or to structures defined purely by their formal relations—where no perceptual mediation is involved, and whether those cases show that my reconstruction mistakes an epistemic limitation for an ontological one. I also want to know whether quantum-mechanical descriptions of systems prior to measurement pose a distinctive challenge, or whether they can be accommodated by treating such descriptions as notional rather than ideational in Berkeley's own sense. Could you rank these potential defeaters from genuinely fatal to merely awkward adjustments, indicate which ones a committed defender of this reconstructed master argument would be forced to concede rather than dismiss, and assess whether the view has already been qualified into unfalsifiability—if so, whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a metaphysical position of this kind?
