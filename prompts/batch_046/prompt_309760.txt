I'm drafting some notes for an online writing group—mostly readers with backgrounds in analytic philosophy and rationalist forums—under the working title "Binding Without a Binder: A Representational Reading of Bradley's Regress."

The gist is this. Bradley's classic argument holds that if relations are genuinely real entities connecting terms, then a further relation is needed to connect that relation to its terms, generating an infinite regress. The standard replies—Russell's logical atomism and Frege's appeal to unsaturated functions—tend to treat the problem as dissolved by reclassifying relations as either external or logically incomplete.

My notes push a different framing. Rather than asking whether relations are "real," I ask what representational architecture could present a unified proposition without introducing a binding constituent that itself requires binding. I distinguish constituent-binding, where an explicit relational token is added to the representation (inviting the regress at the level of mental syntax), from frame-binding, where the representational medium itself carries structural roles—like grammatical frames assigning thematic roles without an explicit "assigner" symbol. The claim is that frame-binding relocates rather than resolves the regress: the frame is not a constituent, but explaining its operation explicitly risks falling back into constituent-binding.

Then I address an anticipated objection: this may just be a notational variant of Fregean unsaturatedness translated into cognitive architecture. I reply that the difference lies in the modal status of the frame—on my view, it is not an eternal logical form but a contingent feature of a particular representational system, which makes the unity of a proposition system-relative rather than absolute. The somewhat counterintuitive result is that two agents with different cognitive architectures could represent the same state of affairs with propositions that have different internal unity-conditions.

I'm particularly interested in whether system-relativity here is a feature or a bug.
