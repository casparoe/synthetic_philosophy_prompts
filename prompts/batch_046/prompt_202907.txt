Write a Shakespearean sonnet about inference to the best explanation. Use the volta at line 9 to stage the shift from the familiar, almost mechanical picture of abductive reasoning—surprising evidence, rival hypotheses weighed by empirical fit and simplicity—to the deeper methodological problem often called explanatory exclusion: the criteria by which we crown one explanation "best" (parsimony, absence of ad hoc adjustments, unifying power) are not independent measurements but part of the very inference, and they presuppose that the true cause lies within the set we have actually conceived. The octave should build the naive scorecard: data appear, explanations compete, the leaner account prevails. The sestet should turn to the "bad lot" worry—that our winner might be best only among a poor field of candidates, including ones never imagined—and to the suspicion that simplicity is a filter we impose rather than a feature the world guarantees. The closing couplet should crystallize the unresolved tension between discovery and disciplined preference, stating exactly what abduction risks being if the exclusion premise fails, but do not resolve it. Render the philosophical architecture with enough precision that a philosopher of science recognizes the problems of ad hocness, unconceived alternatives, and methodological circularity, yet avoid naming any particular philosopher, school, or text, and do not provide citations or reading lists. Write in strict iambic pentameter with an ABAB CDCD EFEF GG rhyme scheme.
