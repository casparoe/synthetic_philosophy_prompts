The target is legal positivism’s claim that law is a purely descriptive social fact fixed by a rule of recognition, especially H.L.A. Hart’s distinction between primary and secondary rules and Lon Fuller’s eight principles of law’s internal morality. Exaggerate the positivist stance to absurdity: present the rule of recognition as an admin-dashboard toggle, Fuller's desiderata (generality, promulgation, prospectivity, clarity, non-contradictory rules, possibility of compliance, constancy, and congruence) as optional cosmetic “skins” available for purchase, and Hart’s concession of a “minimum content of natural law” as a freemium retention feature justified solely by empirical sociology rather than moral necessity. Include a clause that permits in-app licensing of kidney-sale contracts, vote-trading agreements, and price-gouging exemptions, valid so long as the master rule has been updated to recognize them; another clause that resolves penumbral cases through a probabilistic AI trained only on past official behavior, with an explicit waiver of moral reasoning or interpretive integrity; and a “continuity dispute” clause stating that if a jurisdiction’s rule of recognition is overthrown, all prior obligations are void ab initio and users waive any grievance not derivable from the new dashboard settings. The satire should stay deadpan and readable to someone unfamiliar with the debate, but a specialist should recognize that it fairly captures both Hart’s descriptive project and Fuller’s counter-claim that law necessarily embodies an aspirational morality. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or page references; inject the niche framing of a subscription legal-system service, but keep the underlying philosophical references to widely known concepts.
