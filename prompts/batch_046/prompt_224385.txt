I am designing a seminar session for graduate students in the philosophy of artificial intelligence who have encountered the basic HCH literature but are not specialists in cognitive architecture. I want them to see that HCH and the factored-cognition framework are not merely competing engineering recipes but opposing normative idealizations of deliberation itself. Write a dialogue between two interlocutors—call them A and B—who are equally well-informed and neither is a strawman. A should defend HCH as the correct idealization: deliberation is essentially a recursive process in which an agent delegates evaluation to a more capable version of itself, anchored ultimately in human judgment, and this structure is normative rather than descriptive of actual brain processes. B should counter that factored cognition reveals HCH to be the wrong kind of abstraction altogether, because real deliberation is tractable only when a hard problem is decomposed into weakly-interacting subproblems solved by specialized modules, and that treating cognition as a homogeneous recursive loop ignores the architectural separation—say, between reward modeling and policy search—that makes deliberation feasible at all.

The dialogue must contain direct back-and-forth rather than parallel monologues: B should press A on how HCH avoids an infinite regress of evaluators without smuggling in a unified meta-evaluator, and A should press B on whether a factorized architecture can perform the kind of global consistency check that rational deliberation demands. Each should concede one specific, substantive point to the other before sharpening their remaining disagreement. Keep the exchange grounded in concrete steps—A should describe the exact moment in the loop where a human asks a question of its HCH copy, and B should describe the exact boundary where a factored system isolates one cognitive subtask from another—without asking for citations, bibliographies, or further reading, and without invoking theories or results so obscure that they could not be reconstructed from general knowledge of these two frameworks.
