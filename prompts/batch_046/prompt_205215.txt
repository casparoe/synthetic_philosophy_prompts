I'm preparing a seminar discussion on whether there exists a moral duty to preserve painful interpersonal memories, and I'd like to read Michel Gondry's film Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind through the opposing frameworks of Avishai Margalit's ethics of memory and David Rieff's case for strategic forgetting. The film's central device—the Lacuna procedure that allows Joel and Clementine to erase each other—seems to literalize the philosophical choice between remembrance and release. Please examine how the narrative treats Joel's subconscious resistance to erasure, particularly in scenes where he hides Clementine inside pre-relationship memories such as a childhood humiliation or attempts to preserve her in a collapsing beach house.

Does this resistance vindicate Margalit's claim that we owe loyalty to shared pasts, especially in what he calls thick relations, and that erasure amounts to a kind of ethical betrayal of the other? Or does the film ultimately support Rieff's warning that remembrance can become pathological, especially given that Joel and Clementine appear destined to repeat the same destructive patterns once the erasure fails and they meet again? I want the reading to be faithful in both directions: do not reduce the film to a philosophical allegory, and do not distort either philosopher to fit a particular scene.

To push beyond standard commentary, consider this more specific framing: the erasure is technically symmetric—each party deletes the other—yet the film is narrated almost entirely from Joel's interior perspective, with Clementine's experience of the procedure shown only in fragments. Does this asymmetry suggest that forgetting is never genuinely mutual, and that the ethical burden of remembrance always falls unevenly? Furthermore, the film's resolution—where the couple chooses to begin again despite knowing the likely outcome—seems to value the memory neither as an ethical obligation (Margalit) nor as an instrument of well-being (Rieff), but as something closer to an aesthetic or existential affirmation of shared finitude. Where does the film most clearly resist both philosophical frameworks, and what does it add to the debate about remembering and forgetting that argument alone cannot supply?
