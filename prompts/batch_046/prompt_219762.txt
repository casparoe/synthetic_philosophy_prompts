I teach an upper-level seminar on contemporary metaphysics and I am constructing the final exam. I want one essay question on metaphysical grounding and fundamentality that students cannot answer by reciting standard taxonomies—distinctions between full and partial grounding, conditions of well-foundedness, or the usual physicalist picture of a single bottom layer. The exam should ask students to evaluate whether an institutional fact—specifically, the binding authority of a constitutional court—can be simultaneously fully grounded in physical-social practices and in irreducible normative commitments without generating vicious overdetermination or violating the transitivity of grounding across domains. The question must compel them to decide whether cross-domain grounding is coherent, whether it undermines the uniqueness of fundamentality, and whether the notion of absolute fundamentality should be abandoned in favor of a layered pluralism.
