I've been thinking about the ethics of voting, particularly the tension between treating a vote as an expressive act — a declaration of one's values — and treating it as an instrumental act aimed at outcomes, including the familiar "lesser evil" justification. From my background in software engineering, this resembles a trade-off between preserving signal purity and accepting noisy input to prevent catastrophic system failure. But I think I'm stuck on a confusion: if I cast a ballot for a candidate I regard as seriously flawed solely to block a worse alternative, do I violate what philosophers call "clean hands" — corrupting the expressive integrity of my vote? And if there is a genuine moral duty to vote, must that duty be purely instrumental, or can it be expressive without requiring me to endorse every position of the candidate I support? I'm trying to determine whether strategic voting for the lesser evil is necessarily incompatible with an expressive account of voting, or whether the two can be reconciled without contradiction.
