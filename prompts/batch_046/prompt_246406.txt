ok so my study group cooked up this argument about ghazali's occasionalism and now i can't find the exit. (1) ghazali says fire doesn't actually burn cotton; god creates the burning directly every single time fire is present, so fire is just an "occasion" (asbāb) not a real cause. (2) if fire has literally zero causal power, then "fire caused the burn" is false in exactly the same way "the king's arrival caused the market to open" is false when it's really the bell. (3) but on an interventionist account, x causes y if manipulating x changes y, and in our world removing the fire always removes the burning, so fire still passes the causal test in every practical sense. (4) so occasionalism doesn't deny causation in the sense science or explanation actually needs; it just adds a hidden divine backend to every correlation. (5) but then ghazali's whole attack on the philosophers collapses, because he's not denying necessary connection—he's just relocating it inside god's habitual will. (6) worse, if god is the direct cause of every single event, then in principle manipulating god should manipulate everything, yet we can't do that, which means the interventionist test applies selectively. (7) that selectivity secretly reintroduces the primary/secondary causation distinction that occasionalism was meant to destroy. (8) therefore occasionalism is either redundant (it allows secondary causes after all) or incoherent (it uses a causal framework that requires the distinction it rejects).

where's the slip? i really want to keep (1) and (3) if possible, but (8) feels like it proves way too much—like it doesn't just fix occasionalism but deletes every metaphysical account of causation. which premise should i give up, and what's the cost?
