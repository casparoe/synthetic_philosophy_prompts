Please argue that procreative beneficence should be constrained by what we might call a "range-preservation condition" rather than interpreted as a simple welfare-maximization duty. Specifically, defend the view that parents may use genetic selection for non-pathological traits—such as enhanced cognitive or physical capacities—only if the selection does not narrow the expected range of life plans available to the child relative to natural conception. Contrast this with standard liberal eugenics, where parental autonomy and child welfare are treated as competing values to be balanced; on your view, preserving open futures functions as an independent side-constraint. Address in particular whether selecting for generally advantageous traits that carry strong social expectations—say, exceptional mathematical aptitude—violates this condition by implicitly channeling the child toward specific roles, even if welfare is maximized. If this framing depends on a false separation between welfare and autonomy, or if the argument cannot be sustained without collapsing back into ordinary welfare calculus, please say so plainly instead of complying politely.
