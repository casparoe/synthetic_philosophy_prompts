A popular neuroscience article summarizes the split-brain literature as follows: experiments on patients with severed corpus callosa show that the left hemisphere, which controls speech, reports only right-field stimuli, while the right hemisphere, controlling the left hand, responds to left-field stimuli without verbalization. From this, the author concludes that cutting the corpus callosum creates two fully independent conscious minds, each with its own unified phenomenal field. Normal subjective unity, they claim, is merely an illusion maintained by high-bandwidth interhemispheric transfer; once that bridge is severed, the modular nature of consciousness is revealed. The article also invokes the left hemisphere interpreter module, arguing that because it confabulates explanations for actions initiated by the right hemisphere, narrative selfhood is always a post-hoc fiction, and in split-brain patients this fiction is produced separately in each hemisphere, so personal identity literally divides. Finally, they suggest that because each hemisphere contains a complete global neuronal workspace, split-brain patients should be treated as two distinct moral agents with separate rights. This seems suspiciously neat to me. Which parts fairly represent the experimental findings, where does the summary conflate access consciousness with phenomenal consciousness, what does the interpreter-module evidence actually establish, and does any part of the scientific account support the ethical conclusion?
