I've come around to thinking that two-dimensional semantics is mostly a redescription of the necessary a posteriori problem rather than a real solution, especially for math. The primary/secondary intension split sounds neat, but I think for arithmetic terms like 'plus' the so-called primary intension is actually fixed by our implicit cognitive architechture — the actual computational routines we run — not by any pure conceptual content. So 2Dists are smuggling empirical psychology into semantics without noticing. That means the framework doesn't explain why math is necessary; it just buries the explanation in an unexamined box about how brains work.

What would actually count against this? Which parts of the 2D machinery survive if primary intensions are empirically loaded? Is there a prediction about how we correct arithmetical mistakes that only 2D can make, or does my view conceed that too easily? And please be honest: is my "computational architechture" move just changing the subject, or does it genuinely expose the framework?

Also tell me what would change your mind here — maybe a proof that primary intensions are genuinely non-empirical, or a case where the epistemic/metaphysical distinction does real work in math that contextualism can't replicate — and flag where your reasoning is shakiest.
