I defend a modified etiological account—call it the regulatory-etiological view. Rather than fixing a trait's proper function by direct selection acting on the trait itself, this view holds that function is determined by the evolutionary history of the regulatory network that currently maintains the trait's expression within the organism. Consequently, an exapted trait retains its original function only if its maintenance network was co-opted without being replaced by a newly selected regulatory architecture; and symbiotically acquired metabolic pathways (for example, plastid-derived routes operating inside a eukaryotic host cell) possess no autonomous biological function within the host—only a causal-role contribution to fitness—unless the host genome has been specifically selected to regulate them as endogenous components.
