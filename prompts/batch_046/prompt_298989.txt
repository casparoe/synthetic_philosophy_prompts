case 2: the printing press. caused centuries of religious violence and eventually secular states. verdict: also survived, but the adaptation took 200 years. so speed of adaptation can't be the whole story, or i'd have to call this a failure.

case 3: social media algorithms optimizing for engagement. they rewired political discourse faster than election law could adapt. we're not extinct, but i feel like we're in an unfinished failure state. verdict: not survived yet, because adaptation is still missing.

case 4: cheap at-home gene editing kits that let anyone synthesize a novel pathogen. verdict: we wouldn't survive this. not because regulation is impossible, but because one mistake is irreversible and global. this feels like a different category from case 1.

case 5: climate change driven by fossil-fuel technology. we have the tech to fix it, but the institutions are gridlocked. verdict: closer to case 3 than case 4 - failing, but not necessarily fatal, and possibly reversible if we eventually adapt.

case 6: von neumann's own example of self-reproducing automata. if they're earth-based, i say we don't survive. but if they're confined to lunar orbit or deep space, then maybe we do survive, because the error space is physically separated. verdict: survival depends on containment geography, which doesn't show up in any other case.
