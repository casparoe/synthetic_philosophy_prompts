I have been trying to reconcile Hilbert’s hotel with the finitist objection that actual infinities are incoherent. The hotel argument seems to show that, in an actually infinite collection, the part can equal the whole—since every guest can be moved to the next room, freeing space without anyone being removed. Rather than resolving this paradox, Cantor’s theory of transfinite cardinals appears to systematise these violations of the part-whole principle by assigning them different “sizes” of infinity. I therefore wonder whether finitism ought to be read not as a denial of Cantor’s mathematics, but as the claim that such mathematics describes only potential, never actual, totalities. To push this further, I have been considering what one might call the “occupancy-invariance thesis”: if an infinite spatial universe could be rearranged in an analogous way—say, by a uniform expansion that shifts every region—then its infinite extent would leave no observable trace distinguishable from a large but finite cosmos. Given this, is the dispute over actual infinity in cosmology really a substantive metaphysical question, or merely a difference in how we formalise the same physical structure? I should be grateful if you could identify the precise misstep in this line of thought, particularly in my interpretation of Cantor’s hierarchy as supporting rather than undermining finitism.
