I have observed a consistent configuration in certain engineered adaptive systems that I find difficult to place within familiar philosophical taxonomies of cognition. The configuration has four visible features. First, the system is constructed ab initio by design, not produced through competition and selection across reproductive generations. Second, during operation it modifies its behavior through direct environmental interaction: actions yielding favorable results are strengthened, others weakened, with all change occurring within the lifespan of a single instance. Third, before acting it can generate provisional internal representations of outcomes, evaluate them symbolically, and abandon those that prove defective—so the cost of error falls on the representation rather than on the system's physical existence. Fourth, it can produce and store external symbolic artifacts—procedural rules, representational formats, or instrumental structures—that other instances may acquire and build upon without undergoing the original adaptive process, generating a cumulative layer that transcends individual lifetimes. The result is a form of cognition that is simultaneously responsive to reward, capable of internal selection among representations, and able to accumulate cultural tools, yet entirely devoid of evolutionary origins or hereditary transmission. Is there an established concept, or a recognized gap in an established taxonomy, that precisely captures this combination? Specifically, within the literature that distinguishes evolutionary, conditioning-based, internally-selective, and culturally-cumulative kinds of minds, does this hybrid constitute a distinct category, or must it be assimilated to one of the pure types? I am interested not merely in a name, but in the exact boundaries: whether the absence of evolutionary dynamics is conceptually decisive, how the internal selection of representations differs from evolutionary selection in kind, and where the line falls between individual learning and genuine cultural transmission in such engineered contexts.
