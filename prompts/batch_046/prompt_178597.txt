Take the Norwegian boardroom quota law from 2003 - you know, the one that said public limited firms had to get to 40% women by 2008 or risk being dissolved - and run it through the idea of "signalling collapse under forced represention." I'm not asking for the usual "did it improve profits" take; I want to know what the law presupposed about where board authority actually comes from (is it expertise, is it social proof, is it just being there?), and whether the wave of firms going private to dodge the rule, plus the way women ended up clusterd in boards that were already seen as "soft" sectors, confirms that authority was always partly performative. Does the theory explain why some women on those boards reported feeling like they had to over-prove competence, or does that break the model? And what does the whole mess show about whether represention mandates can ever work when the underlying pipeline hasn't been restructured - does it validate the theory, or reveal that the theory was built for slower, voluntary shifts and falls apart under legislative speed?
