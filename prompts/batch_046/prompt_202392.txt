Write a deadpan parody of a peer-reviewed philosophy-of-science article, approximately 500 words in length, formatted with a title, abstract, and brief body sections, as though it appeared in a fictional journal titled The Quarterly Review of Deferred Predictions. The article should defend an invented methodological framework called "chronometric immunization," which proposes that a predictive theory gains scientific credibility in direct proportion to the duration and severity of its predictive failures, provided those failures occur within an ever-expanding "falsification latency window." Use this framework to satirize the genuine philosophical problem of whether long-run predictive theories—exemplified by Malthusian accounts of population collapse—can ever be conclusively falsified, or whether they are perpetually rescued by timeline extensions, auxiliary hypotheses about technological or institutional intervention, and quietly redefined boundary conditions. The satire must derive its humor from precise philosophical content rather than generic absurdity: invoke, without quoting verbatim, the tension between Popperian falsifiability and Lakatosian protection of a research program's hard core; invent a quantitative metric—such as the "Resilience-to-Refutation Coefficient," calculated by dividing the cumulative number of failed predictions by the years elapsed since the theory's initial formulation; and include an invented skeptical interlocutor, Dr. Elias Vorn, who warns that the framework renders every theory unfalsifiable, only to be dismissed in a footnote as suffering from "short-horizon bias." The voice must remain entirely straight-faced and internally consistent: the reader should recognize the irony solely because the reasoning is pushed to a logically extreme but coherent conclusion, not through any authorial winking, anachronism, or break in register. Do not include real bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific living scholars; invented institutions, metrics, and interlocutors are encouraged. The target is the methodological practice of immunizing long-run predictions against refutation, not any named individual.
