I am about to defend a position in a philosophy of cognitive science seminar. The thesis is that the tripartite architecture of frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages—associated with Hofstadter's GEB—fails to locate meaning stably in self-referential representational systems because the frame and outer message do not stand in a simple containment relation. Instead, I argue that there is an upward interpretive leakage—what I call frame-recoil—whereby the logical structure of the inner message exerts pressure on the frame's contextual intentions, so that meaning is neither in the frame, the outer message, nor the inner message alone, but is distributed across the unstable boundary between frame and outer message. I apply this specifically to hierarchical predictive-coding models, contending that precision-weighting does not merely frame lower-level predictions but is itself reinterpreted by them, leaving a semantic residue that destabilises the hierarchy.

The audience is precisely this: two cognitive architects who specialise in hierarchical predictive coding and regard precision-weighting as a complete solution to framing; one philosopher of language working in the Gricean tradition, who will treat my distinction as a redescription of speaker meaning versus sentence meaning; one AI researcher convinced that transformer attention architectures dissolve any strict level-distinction; and several doctoral students thoroughly familiar with Hofstadter's formal examples but doubtful that they extend beyond logic to neural computation.

Please provide a speaker-preparation sheet, not a defence of my view. Order the objections as they are likely to arise in the question period: begin with the predictive-coding specialists, then the Gricean philosopher, then the AI researcher, then the students. For each objection, phrase it exactly as that interlocutor would phrase it. For each, state what I should concede, what I must hold firm, and where my reply is weakest. Identify the single weakest point in my overall argument—the point I should actively prepare to defend rather than hope they overlook. Include, separately, the objection I am most hoping will not be raised—likely that frame-recoil is merely a category mistake between syntactic and semantic levels—and outline a concise response suitable for a brief exchange. Use British English throughout, and do not include any request for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
