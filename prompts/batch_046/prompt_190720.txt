I've been working through notes from a seminar on epistemic game theory—specifically how common knowledge of rationality interacts with higher-order uncertainty about one's own reasoning machinery—and I ended up in a long back-and-forth with two friends over coffee that I couldn't quite resolve. I'd like a point-by-point adjudication of the arguments as they actually unfolded.

(1) Maya argued that in any finite perfect-information game, if rationality is common knowledge and there is a unique backward-induction path, then private perturbations to payoff beliefs are harmless as long as every rationalizable strategy remains in the support. Even if I privately think you might miscalculate a payoff by a small epsilon, I still know you will eliminate dominated strategies, so the induction survives intact.

(2) Jordan replied that this misses what he calls "architectural noise": cases where agents have private, unobservable access to the error rate of their own cognitive machinery—say, I know my working memory is degraded, but I do not know whether yours is too. He proposed a modified muddy-children puzzle in which each child sees her own face with a private confidence interval, but also holds a private belief about how likely the parent's announcement was corrupted by transmission noise. Jordan claimed that because the noise model belongs to the epistemic architecture rather than the payoff matrix, common knowledge of rationality cannot anchor the induction: I cannot common-know that you share my noise floor, so I cannot be sure you will draw the same inference from the same silence.

(3) Maya countered that Jordan is merely smuggling a state-space expansion into the framing. If the reliability of the announcement is genuinely uncertain, the correct formalization is a Bayesian game with types corresponding to different noise levels; once that space is specified, rationality remains common knowledge over the expanded type space, and backward induction proceeds over beliefs about types. She said architectural noise is just private information about nature, not a failure of common knowledge.

(4) Jordan said Maya's move begs the question, because specifying the type space requires that agents common-know the distribution from which noise levels are drawn. But if the noise is about the reasoning process itself—meta-cognitive error—then an agent's belief about that distribution depends on her own current cognitive state, which she cannot fully disclose or verify in others. Thus the expanded type space cannot be common knowledge either; it is recursively unstable. He concluded that Maya's defense works only for first-order payoff uncertainty, not for second-order uncertainty about rationality's own execution.

Please adjudicate this move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Say specifically which party is right about which point, and identify anything important both are missing—perhaps about the distinction between common knowledge of rationality and common knowledge of the representational model being used to formalize rationality. Write the response in American English. Also, include a brief note on what argument or consideration would change your verdict—where your reasoning is most likely to fail and what evidence or reasoning step would cause you to revise your adjudication.
