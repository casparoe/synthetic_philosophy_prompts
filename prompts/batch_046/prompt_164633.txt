In discussions of anthropic reasoning, the disagreement between defenders of the self-sampling assumption and defenders of the self-indication assumption—particularly as tested by the so-called presumptuous-philosopher scenario—has persisted for decades without convergence. Both sides accept the same Bayesian formalism and agree on the observed data; where they diverge is on whether one's own existence should be treated as neutral background to be conditioned away, or as evidence that shifts priors over world-types or cosmological parameters. The standard objections—the doomsday argument, the sleeping-beauty problem, and the presumptuous-philosopher case—are well rehearsed, yet neither camp has revised its diagnosis of those cases. My working theory is that the stall is not about probability theory at all, but about whether anthropic reasoning is meant to be a normative rule for rational belief-updating or a metaphysical claim about which possible observers count as you, and that the two sides are therefore answering different questions under the single heading of how one should reason about one's own existence. Is that correct, or is there a hidden formal disagreement about reference-class construction or indexical conditioning that neither side has made explicit? I want a diagnosis supported by what each side actually says about the presumptuous-philosopher case and about reference-class boundaries: is the disagreement verbal (a dispute over what counts as evidence), substantive about standards of reasoning, a clash of bedrock intuitions about self-locating belief, or two distinct projects—one semantic, one normative—masquerading as one? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move the needle: a formal result establishing reference-class invariance, a conceptual distinction that cleanly separates the normative from the metaphysical, a change in which cases count as data, or should we expect no resolution because the disagreement traces back to pre-theoretic commitments about what it means for a particular observer to exist?
