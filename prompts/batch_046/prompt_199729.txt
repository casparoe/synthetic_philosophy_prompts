To make the problem precise, I am replacing the standard physical-invisibility ring with what I call the Ring of Epistemic Isolation. Wearing it, whatever you do leaves no trace in any other mind’s beliefs, and it also blocks your own future self from retaining any memory, narrative record, or cognitive integration of the act. There is therefore no external audience, no internal audience, and no possibility of self-reproach or consistency-based regret.

My opening move: Even under this ring, injustice appears self-defeating in a way that does not depend on being observed. Moral norms are not merely enforced social rules; they are conditions for the coherence of practical reasoning and for maintaining a unified practical identity across time. If you can violate them with absolute epistemic isolation, your actions cease to be attributable to a stable agent with consistent reasons—they become like random events occurring to a body. The ring therefore does not liberate a beneficiary; it dissolves the very “you” that could enjoy the fruits of injustice. So the challenge is not “why be moral if no one sees?” but “can there even be a subject who benefits from injustice if the conditions for agency are undermined?” Respond to this.
