In our philosophy of science seminar we discussed Popper's well-known claim that Darwinism functions as a metaphysical research programme rather than a strictly falsifiable scientific theory, and that he later partly retracted this after recognizing that evolutionary biology makes concrete risky predictions—especially concerning mutation-rate distributions and the statistical improbability of convergent evolutionary paths. I am trying to determine whether this revision requires us to build an explicit exemption into Popper's demarcation criterion for historical or evolutionary sciences, or whether the criterion can remain intact if we treat natural selection as the falsifiable mechanism operating inside a broader, unfalsifiable metaphysical framework of common descent. Which reading is closer to Popper's actual position, and does either option successfully preserve the sharp distinction he sought between science and pseudoscience?
