I'm developing an evaluative framework for autonomous intervention architectures—systems that either execute a resource deployment or maintain a programmed default of non-intervention in high-stakes environments—where the central normative question is whether the system's behavior should be assessed as an active doing or as an allowed omission. Rather than adopting an off-the-shelf moral taxonomy, I want to understand what philosophical criteria must be satisfied before any account of the doing/allowing distinction, or of killing versus letting die, can be considered adequate for this purpose.

Write the desiderata that a theory in this domain must meet: specify the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must classify correctly, the distinctions it must preserve, and the puzzles it must dissolve or accommodate—each paired with the consideration or concrete scenario that motivates it.
