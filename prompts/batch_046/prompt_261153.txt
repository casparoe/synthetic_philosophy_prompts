Write a short story in American English about a five-member arbitration council aboard the generation ship Mendelian that must decide whether to alter the vessel's genetic archive. The council uses a discursive procedure: each member votes independently on three premises—whether mutation risk exceeds the survival threshold, whether repair is possible without intervention, and whether alteration violates intergenerational fairness—with the collective decision on intervention derived from majority positions on the premises. Show how this premise-based aggregation produces a collective judgment that conflicts with the result of a direct majority vote on the intervention itself, and then have one member propose switching to conclusion-based voting, which reverses the outcome. Reveal that comparing these two methods against a third hybrid rule creates a cyclical preference among the aggregation procedures, and use the disagreement to examine whether collective rationality requires sacrificing either logical coherence or democratic consistency. End the story without resolving which rule the ship should adopt, keeping the focus on the interpersonal dynamics rather than on genetic science.
