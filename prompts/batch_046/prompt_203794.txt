ok so i've been trying to sort out where i actually stand on whether people can own ideas and i'm not totally sure my thoughts hang together. here's roughly my package: first, ideas feel different from physical stuff—if i tell you my idea i still have it, so treating them like property seems metaphysically weird, like owning air. second, but if someone spends ten years and a ton of money on a drug formula they should get paid back, and the amount should match the work they put in, not just whatever the market will bear. third, if two people come up with the same invention totally seperate, punishing the second one feels wrong, like punishing someone for discovering gravity twice; i think independent invention should be a full defense. fourth, even if we don't give people money for ideas, they still have a kind of moral right to be named as the originator—like, plagiarism is wrong even when there's no copyright. fifth, i think the line between an idea and its expression is real for books and songs but basically collapses for patents, since a patent is just owning the idea itself with different words. sixth, my main reason for supporting any of this is incentive—if people can't profit, they won't bother inventing—rather than thinking anyone naturally owns what's in their head. seventh, and this is maybe a weird extra thing, i think there's a difference between owning an idea-token and an idea-type, and copyright protects tokens while patents try to protect types, which is why patents feel more wrong.

do these actually fit? i'm especially wondering about the tension between saying we compensate people for labor (not just incentives) and saying the system is mainly about incentives—those feel like different reasons that point different directions. also, if independent invention is a defense, doesn't that gut the incentive? like, why invest if someone can just accidentally replicate it? and does my attribution right clash with my claim that ideas can't be owned—seems like it might but maybe it doesn't? also is the idea-type vs token thing consistent with saying patents are just idea-ownership, or am i double-counting? i don't need citations or a reading list, just an audit: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look bad, rank them by severity, and tell me the cheapest revision that fixes the worst one without breaking everything else. also please don't just say they're all fine—there's definatly at least one real contradiction in here.
