Me and a couple of mates from the rationalist forums got into it last week about evidential cooperation in large worlds — the Bostrom-style idea that if there's loads of copies of you floating about, your choice is evidence for what they do, so you should cooperate even with no causal link. I wasn't in the argument, just listening over beers, but they want me to referee and I honestly can't.

Alex started by saying that if you buy into a big multiverse, deciding to cooperate with suffering-reduction is evidence that your copies do the same, so you ought to do it regardless of direct causation. Jordan said okay, but only for copies with essentially identical utility functions — otherwise there's no correlation worth exploiting, and you're just doing generic altruism with extra steps.

Alex came back: decision-theoretically, the correlation runs through reasoning, not just values. If my reasoning process is close to agents who care about suffering plus liberty, then my choice is still evidence about them, so I should cooperate with a broader 'reasoning cluster' rather than a narrow value-clone. Jordan replied that this dilutes evidential cooperation into ordinary rule-utilitarian behaviour, and that if values diverge far enough the correlation breaks anyway because they'd defect for different reasons.

Then Alex threw in a twist about self-locating beliefs: learning I'm in a large world updates me on the prevalence of agents with my reasoning, regardless of their terminal values, so the evidential bridge doesn't need value-alignment, just shared epistemic structure. Jordan said that's hand-wavy — without a common prior linking the worlds, treating an indexical update as cross-world evidence just assumes what needs proving. He also said Alex never explained why my local action should track distant ones if there's no shared causal structure.

Neither of them would budge. Can you adjudicate point by point? I want a committed response — don't just lay out the menu, actually take sides where you can, say who's right about which move, and tell me what both are missing, especially on whether correlation needs value-alignment or just reasoning-similarity. Also please answer in American English, and accomodate the fact that I'm refering to all this from a British context.

There's also a case they kept dancing around but never resolved: what if your reasoning cluster includes agents whose values are mostly aligned but who weight them differently — say they care about suffering but also about autonomy, and they might defect if cooperation looks like paternalism? Does that break the correlation or just shift the cooperative equilibrium? I'd like your judgement on that too.
