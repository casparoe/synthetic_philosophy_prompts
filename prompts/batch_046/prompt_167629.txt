Her verdicts so far: she judges a life of intense pleasure coupled with constant underlying desire for more as poor; she judges a life of complete ascetic withdrawal with minimal pleasure but zero craving as excellent; she denies that artistic or intellectual achievement adds value when it was driven by ambition or anxiety; she endorses a drug-induced state of contentment without desires as excellent; yet she insists that an entity with no mental life whatsoever—like a rock—has no well-being at all, rather than perfect well-being. Now consider a hypothetical synthetic organism engineered to maintain homeostasis with no suffering and no craving, but also with no capacity for experience, preference-formation, or awareness. Would she say this organism achieves the tranquilist ideal, or that it falls outside evaluation altogether? Identify the precise rules that fit her track record—pure absence-matters, absence-plus-minimal-consciousness, or another variant—say what each predicts about this organism, give your best committed guess at which rule she is running with reasons, and name the further case that would best distinguish the leading candidates. Take a definite position rather than laying out the options neutrally.
