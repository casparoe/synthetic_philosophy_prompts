I've just retired from molecular biology and started sneaking into philosphy lectures at the local university—probably a mistake, but there it is. Last week the tutor mentioned Darwin's strange inversion, where selection builds eyes and wings with no comprehension of optics or flight, and Turing's parallel inversion, where a machine can play chess with no grasp of strategy. I tried weaving them into an argument for my study group and now it stinks of proof by exhaustion, but I can't spot the rot.

(1) Darwin showed that complex biological competence—eyes, immune systems—can emerge from mechanisms that understand nothing about what they build; selection is blind. (2) Turing showed that behavioural competence—passing tests, playing games—can emerge from formal symbol-manipulation that understands nothing about the task; computation is syntactic. (3) So if both evolutionary and computational processes generate full competence with zero understanding in the causal chain, then understanding is never causally necessary for producing competence. (4) Human comprehension—our grasp of reasons, meanings, concepts—is itself just another biological and neural-computational competence. (5) Therefore, if understanding is present in us, it must either be part of that competence or a seperate layer riding on top. (6) But if it's part of the competence, apply (3) again: the competence is produced without understanding, which means understanding wasn't actually part of it—only an appearance. (7) If it's a seperate layer, then by (3) it plays no causal role in generating the behaviour it accompanies; it's purely epiphenomenal. (8) So either human comprehension is an illusion, or it's causally inert. (9) Yet if it's an illusion or inert, the reasoning that led us here has no rational authority—there's no 'us' actually understanding the argument. (10) Hence the chain collapses unless we reject a premise.

I feel like something slips between (3) and (6)—maybe an equivocation about what 'part of' means—but I can't name it. I'd rather keep (1) and (2) intact, since they feel like solid science, and I'm happy to pay elsewhere. Which step is the weak link, why did it look fine, and what's the price of dropping it?
