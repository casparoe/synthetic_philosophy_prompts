Draft the joint statement that a committed Ubuntu relationalist and a liberal-capacity critic could both sign after an adversarial collaboration on whether relational conceptions of personhood constitute a genuine metaphysical alternative or risk collapsing into, or undermining, individualist moral equality.

Structure the output in two parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: what the Ubuntu view asserts about personhood being constituted by reciprocal recognition and communal belonging rather than prior to it; the formal distinction between an achievement-relation view and a baseline-capacity view; shared concerns about the moral status of profoundly isolated, severely disabled, or otherwise non-networked individuals; and what each side would treat as disconfirming evidence for its own position. Second, a disagreements table with the residual disputes phrased so each side accepts the wording of its own view: (A) whether being-a-person is relationally achieved or is a baseline capacity that relations merely develop; (B) whether Ubuntu can ground equal moral dignity without smuggling in individualist premises. For each crux, identify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it—such as obligations to hermits or to severely disabled persons outside dense communal networks—and record in advance what each side predicts the resolution should be. Keep the formulations conceptual and neutral; do not request bibliographies, citations, or verification of specific scholars, and avoid framing the dispute as merely verbal without showing exactly where the substantive disagreement lies.
