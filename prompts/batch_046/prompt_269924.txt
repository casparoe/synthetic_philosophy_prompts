Consider the conceptual project of specifying coherent extrapolated volition and related idealized-preference frameworks for artificial alignment. Rather than asking for a survey, I want a dependency-aware research agenda: which unresolved problems are upstream of others, and where should a small group with about two years and only formal-philosophical tools direct its limited effort if the aim is to make CEV operational for agents that may undergo substantial ontological or architectural change?

Frame your answer around three interrelated clusters. First, the endpoint problem: whether CEV should be modeled as a static reflective-equilibrium fixed point, or as an open-ended dynamic process in which the conditions of idealization themselves evolve—particularly when the agent being extrapolated can revise its own criteria for coherence or for what counts as a preference. Second, the subject-preservation problem: how to treat preferences that are essentially self-referential or indexical (for instance, preferences about being the kind of entity that holds preferences), given that full idealization might eliminate the very subject to whom the volition is originally attributed. Third, the aggregation-ontology problem: whether the move from individual volitions to a collective or universalized preference requires a prior commitment to a particular moral ontology—say, a person-affecting rather than an impersonal framework—or whether that ontological commitment should itself be included within the scope of extrapolation.

Please deliver a defended prioritization, not a literature review. For a research group without empirical human-subjects access, rank these clusters (and any others you judge essential) by which should be tackled first, which can be safely deprioritized because resolving them would not shift much downstream, and which are tractable with formal or conceptual tools versus those that require prior philosophical settlement. Be explicit about the criteria governing your ordering—such as upstream dependency, tractability with current methods, or neglect—and about the trade-offs between them. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.

Finally, state how confident you are in the ranking and reasoning you provide, and single out the one specific claim or dependency judgment about which you are least certain.
