Write a short story about a sculptor named Lian who works exclusively with a synthetic biomaterial that retains structural memory—call it mnemonic substrate—so that when reshaped too radically from its prior form, it fractures or reverts along old stress lines rather than passively accepting a new shape. Lian attempts to form a statue of a tree that has been stripped of all branches by a storm, but the substrate resists configurations that violate its accumulated continuity conditions. The narrative should explore material constitution through action rather than exposition: is the finished work a distinct object that dominates its material, or is the material—with its own persistence criteria—merely permitting a temporary arrangement? Include a decisive moment in which Lian must choose between forcing the substrate into the tree-form, thereby destroying its internal coherence, or yielding to its memory, producing a form that belongs to neither the tree nor the substrate alone but to their negotiated coexistence. Keep the philosophical tension embedded in the physical resistance of the material and in Lian's practical reasoning, not in explicit philosophical commentary.
