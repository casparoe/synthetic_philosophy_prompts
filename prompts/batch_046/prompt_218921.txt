I'm taking a seminar on epistemic paradoxes, and for a creative assignment I need a satirical piece that gets the formal logic right. Write a deadpan parody—around 600 words, formatted as an official university registrar's memo—announcing a "surprise accreditation review" that applies backward induction to its own scheduling. The memo should sincerely argue that since the review cannot fall on the final eligible Friday (because then it wouldn't be surprising), it also cannot fall on Thursday, Wednesday, Tuesday, or Monday, and therefore conclude that no review will occur—only for an inspector to arrive unexpectedly on Tuesday. Include a footnote from a logic professor pointing out the self-defeating announcement, and an administrator's rebuttal that misapplies the concept of common knowledge. The humor should come from the precise epistemology—conditionalization on public announcements, the knowledge condition for surprise, and the collapse of backward induction—rather than generic bureaucratic incompetence. It should be readable to someone who hasn't studied the paradox, but accurate enough that someone who has recognizes the structure immediately.
