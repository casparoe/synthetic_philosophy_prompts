Please explain the conceptual core of the Frege–Geach problem as it applies to non-cognitivist expressivism about normative language, but frame the difficulty specifically around embedded occurrences inside graded epistemic assessments—such as judgments about what credence one ought to hold conditional on the likelihood of harm exceeding a threshold—rather than inside ordinary indicative conditionals. Clarify how an expressivist can maintain that a term like “impermissible” retains a unified expressive function across both unembedded assertions and these nested probabilistic contexts, without either collapsing into cognitivism or fragmenting the term’s meaning into context-specific speech acts. Distinguish this embedding challenge from a general failure of compositionality, and explain why resolving it is particularly pressing if expressivism is to accommodate graded, evidence-sensitive normative reasoning rather than only binary moral verdicts.
