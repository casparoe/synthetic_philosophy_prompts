Design a diagnostic decision tree—exactly five binary branch nodes, root to leaves—that classifies philosophical positions on the knowing-how / knowing-that distinction. The root commitment is: "There is a substantive epistemic difference between being able to perform a skill and merely being able to state true propositions about it."

Structure each node as a concrete diagnostic question answerable without knowing which label it corresponds to. First branch: can knowing-how be fully analyzed in terms of propositional knowledge (knowing-that), or does it contain an irreducibly non-propositional component? Second branch (for the propositional-allowing side): must the relevant propositions include indexical or demonstrative elements—expressible only as "this is how I do it"—rather than being fully explicit and context-independent? Third branch: does possessing knowing-how require the actual present exercise of the ability, or is a counterfactual disposition to exercise it under suitable conditions sufficient? Fourth branch: can knowing-how be attributed to non-human animals or artificial systems that lack conceptual capacities, or does it require conceptual mastery? Fifth branch: does occasional failure or partial success in performing the skill undermine the attribution of knowing-how, or is knowing-how compatible with error?

Your tree must include these leaves, labeled descriptively if the path does not uniquely fix a canonical name:
A. Strong anti-intellectualism—no propositional component whatsoever; knowing-how is a multi-track disposition irreducible to mental content.
B. Context-sensitive intellectualism—reducible to knowing propositions expressed indexically or demonstratively, with practical modes of presentation.
C. Moderate intellectualism—propositional core necessary but not sufficient; requires a non-propositional ability condition.
D. Pure propositionalism—knowing-how is knowing-that, with no independent ability condition; occasional failure is irrelevant.
E. Hybrid cognitive-architectural view—knowing-how corresponds to procedural memory structures, knowing-that to declarative ones; functionally distinct but can co-occur.

For each leaf, state the exact combination of branch answers that produces it. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining whether they collapse into the same branch path or differ only on an unasked dimension. Identify every unoccupied combination of answers: for instance, whether pure propositionalism combined with an actual-exercise requirement is incoherent, and whether the hybrid view combined with strong anti-intellectualism is unstable or merely unexplored. Note specifically whether the tree can distinguish moderate intellectualism from the hybrid view, or whether that separation requires an additional question about the metaphysical status of procedural structures.

Write for an audience of philosophy graduate students familiar with the classic distinction but not with every subsequent taxonomy. Do not include citations, bibliographies, author-page references, or reading lists. Keep the tree to exactly five branch nodes; do not add auxiliary branches.
