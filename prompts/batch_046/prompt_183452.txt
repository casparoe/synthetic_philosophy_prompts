Write a deadpan satirical grant proposal—approximately 500 words, in the flat bureaucratic register of a federal funding application—submitted by an invented senior researcher at the Institute for Linear Historical Calibration. The proposal should advance a research program that quantifies every past society solely by its statistical distance from modern liberal-democratic norms, treating alternative social equilibria as developmental noise or calibration lag rather than as coherent endpoints in their own right. Divide the response into exactly five sections—Executive Summary, Intellectual Merit, Methodological Framework, Broader Impacts, and Risk Assessment—with no framing introduction or conclusion outside these five parts. The satire must remain sustained and internal: the standard objections to Whig history—presentism, survivor bias, the conflation of contingency with failure—should be legible only through the relentless logic of the proposal itself, never stated outright. After the five sections, append a brief separate paragraph identifying which historiographical premise the parody exaggerates most aggressively, and specify the argument or empirical consideration that would lead you to abandon this framing and treat cross-temporal institutional comparison as genuinely defensible rather than satirically absurd. Maintain a formal, polished tone throughout; this is for a dataset of conceptual prompts, not a casual joke.
