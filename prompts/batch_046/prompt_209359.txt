I retired from a physics lab a few years back and now I'm poking around philosphy of mind, mostly because I used to study visual percption. I've been reading about this color phi thing - flash red then green and people swear they saw orange move across, even though there was never an orange stimulus. There's this debate about whether your brain is like Orwell rewriting history after the fact, or like Stalin putting on a show trial where the report is just late but pretends it was always there. I think the Stalinesque version is right, basically. The brain doesn't go back and edit the tape; it just constructs the narrative with a fake timestamp. The Orwellian picture seems like it adds extra machinery for no reason. So what would actually count against this? Not just 'it sounds strange' - I mean real empirical or conceptual defeaters. Would we need to find neural evidence of actual retroactive editing of early sensory representations? Or is there an argument that the Stalinesque view collapses into the Orwellian one once you look closely? And has this position been qualified so much that it can't be wrong, or does it still stick its neck out? I want the stuff that should move me, sorted by how decisive it is, including whether defenders of Stalinesque would admit anything counts.
