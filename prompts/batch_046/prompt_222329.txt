Write the opening remarks for a workshop titled Distributed Authorship and Narrative Gravity. The audience is split among analytic philosophers familiar with Dennett's view of the self as a center of narrative gravity, literary scholars who study collective fiction-writing practices, and machine-learning researchers working on collaborative text-generation systems. Use that philosophical framework as the structural backbone of the address, not as decoration: explain how conceiving the self as a center of narrative gravity reshapes our understanding of authorship when a narrative is produced jointly by human writers and generative models, and identify precisely where the framework becomes strained—specifically, what happens to narrative gravity when it is not anchored in a single biological life. The speech must land for the philosophers without losing the scholars or engineers, and it should not claim that Dennett himself applied this idea to artificial intelligence. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. Keep the response to a maximum of 320 words.
