I'm working out what I actually think about personal survival across discontinuities, using my reactions to concrete scenarios rather than theoretical commitments. Here are my verdicts.

First, the standard destructive teletransporter: my brain is scanned cell by cell, the original body is destroyed, and an exact replica is assembled elsewhere with full psychological continuity. I survive; the replica is me in the only sense that matters, despite the physical break.

Second, the non-destructive variant: my brain is scanned without harm, I remain intact on Earth, and a perfect copy awakens elsewhere with identical psychology. Here the copy is not me; I remain the Earth-bound original, and the duplicate is at best a psychological twin.

Third, gradual synthetic replacement: over a decade, every neuron is replaced with a functionally identical synthetic unit, with consciousness uninterrupted. I survive continuously; there is no point at which a "new" person takes over.

Fourth, the "stutter" backups: every week I am scanned non-destructively, a server activates a perfect psychological copy for twenty-four hours, then deactivates it, while my biological brain continues uninterrupted. These brief copies are never me; they are like thinking photographs, not survivors.

Fifth, physical continuity with total psychological replacement: my body and brain are preserved intact, but targeted intervention erases all autobiographical memory, personality, and values, replacing them with an entirely different psychological profile. The original person is gone; bodily continuity does not preserve survival.

Sixth, fragmented branching: my psychology is divided between two reconstructions—one receives my memories and beliefs but not my emotional patterns or skills, the other receives the emotional and skill profile without autobiographical memory. Neither reconstruction is me; there is no single survivor.

Seventh, the delayed non-causal reconstruction: a recording of my brain state is stored for centuries, then used to build a psychologically identical being, but with no causal chain linking my original mental states to the new ones—merely informational identity. This being is not me; perfect psychological similarity without causal continuity is insufficient.

These judgments feel stable, but they sit in tension. The destructive scanner and the gradual replacement both support survival without physical continuity, yet the stutter backups and the non-destructive copy involve psychological continuity (with causal links) and I deny they are me—apparently because the original persists. Meanwhile, the non-causal reconstruction denies survival despite perfect psychology, suggesting that causal continuity is necessary. Yet in the fragmented case, causal continuity is present in both branches, and I still deny survival to either. So uniqueness, causal continuity, and psychological similarity all seem to play roles, but I cannot see how they combine without contradiction.

Is there a single principle—perhaps involving a specific kind of causal-psychological continuity plus a non-branching condition, or perhaps a graded account of "survival strength"—that delivers all seven verdicts? If not, which verdict should I abandon, and what does that abandonment cost? Also, what should the principle say about an unconsidered case: destructive teletransportation in which the reconstruction is delayed by a century, or in which the replica differs from me by exactly five percent in personality traits?
