Write a dialogue between two historians of science, Dr. Alina Reyes and Dr. Samuel Okonkwo, who disagree about whether writing the history of early modern natural philosophy as progress toward modern empirical science is necessarily a fallacy. Reyes defends a position she calls methodological presentism: selecting past events for their causal contribution to present practices is a legitimate historiographical filter, provided the historian explicitly marks which outcomes serve as selection criteria and does not attribute awareness of those outcomes to the historical agents themselves. Okonkwo rejects this, arguing that even this filtered form collapses into teleological bookkeeping—the illusion that one can cleanly separate causal narration from normative ranking once the endpoint is fixed as the organizing principle. Their exchange should center on a concrete case, the interpretation of seventeenth-century alchemical laboratories, where Reyes sees them as supplying necessary causal conditions for quantitative chemistry despite the alchemists' own goals, and Okonkwo insists that describing those laboratories as preparatory stages alters the explanatory object itself. The dialogue must contain genuine back-and-forth: Reyes should respond directly to Okonkwo's claim that causal contribution is not independent of the descriptive framework used to identify it, and Okonkwo should respond directly to Reyes's distinction between selection criteria and agentive intention. Neither side should be a strawman. Include a moment where Reyes concedes a real limitation in her view—specifically, that her filter may silently suppress alternative trajectories that were equally causally viable but normatively disfavored—and a moment where Okonkwo acknowledges that a purely anti-Whiggish stance carries its own explanatory cost, namely an inability to account for why certain past practices persisted and transmitted techniques across generations if they are treated as entirely incommensurable with later science. End without resolution, leaving the conceptual tension intact.
