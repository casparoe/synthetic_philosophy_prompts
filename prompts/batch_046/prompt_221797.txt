I am preparing my philosophical profile on moral responsibility for a school discussion group, and I should like an internal consistency check before I present. These are my commitments:

1. I accept Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: since no one can be causa sui—no agent creates itself from nothing—ultimate moral responsibility is impossible.
2. I take this to defeat only 'ultimate' responsibility, not the ordinary sort that involves acting from one's own reasons and being answerable to others.
3. I am a compatibilist in roughly the Frankfurt tradition: freedom lies in acting from higher-order volitions that one endorses, irrespective of determinism.
4. I believe societies ought to preserve blame and praise practices purely for consequentialist reasons—social order and reform—even though metaphysically no one is ultimately responsible.
5. I have recently argued, in a school essay, that a genuinely self-modifying artificial agent—one capable of rewriting its own core values and source architecture—might achieve the self-creation Strawson declares impossible, and so might possess a stronger form of responsibility than any human.
6. I also maintain that evolutionary debunking undermines our intuitions about desert and free will, which I take to reinforce Strawson's conclusion.

Please audit this set for coherence. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are merely apparent and dissolve on inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each, state precisely what I should revise and what that revision costs my overall position. Do not suggest readings or ask me to look anything up; work only with the commitments listed.
