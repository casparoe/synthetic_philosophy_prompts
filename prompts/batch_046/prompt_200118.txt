For an upper-level philosophy of psychiatry seminar, write a 50-minute essay exam question (30 marks, approximately 700 words) on psychiatric classification and Hacking's looping effects. The question must not be answerable by summarizing Hacking's account. Instead, embed a constructed hypothetical diagnostic category—"adaptive withdrawal syndrome" (AWS), defined in the prompt as sustained social retreat coupled with heightened sensory filtering among adolescents in high-stimulation urban environments—and ask students to compare its implementation in two institutional pathways: (1) a forensic-clinical pathway where an AWS diagnosis determines eligibility for court-mandated cognitive restructuring, and (2) a peer-led neurodiversity pathway where AWS is voluntarily self-adopted as an identity marker. Require students to identify the first-order looping mechanism operating in each pathway; predict whether a second-order "reverse loop"—in which active resistance to the label stabilizes the kind by defining its negative boundary—would emerge in one pathway but not the other; and evaluate whether this institutional variability undermines a modest realist view of psychiatric kinds. Provide (a) the exam question with explicit time, length, and marking instructions; (b) a model answer of about 600 words that specifies the conceptual moves an A answer must contain—including distinguishing first-order from second-order looping, linking institutional authority structures to loop direction, and articulating the tension between looping and modest realism—along with the specific errors that should cost marks; and (c) a marking scheme with point allocations that clearly separates an A from a B response. Write in American English. The response should state how confident it is in what it claims and single out the specific point about which it is least sure.
