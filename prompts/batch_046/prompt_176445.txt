Hey, I'm sitting on our city's charter review board and we're stuck on something that feels more like philosophy than zoning law. A citizens group is pushing the "Direct Mandate Protocol" — every registered voter gets a digital ballot on budget items after watching a standardized briefing video, no more council reps filtering things. But here's the twist that has me up at night: the system doesn't just count votes. It runs every response through a "preference normalization" step that weights your input based on demographic representativeness — if your group is already overrepresented in turnout, your vote gets scaled down; if you're underrepresented, it gets scaled up. The proponents say this corrects for bias and reveals the true "will of the people," not just the will of the people who actually show up.

Half the board thinks this is populism done right — stripping away elite intermediaries and using tech to hear the real popular voice. The other half thinks it's a shell game: you're not measuring what people want, you're manufacturing a synthetic mandate and slapping the word "popular" on it. They say if nobody actually cast the normalized vote, it can't be anyone's will.

We need to vote next month on whether to reccomend this to the city council. Could you draft a short memo — maybe 2 or 3 pages — that recommends approve, reject, or approve with conditions? I need it to take the normalization idea seriously instead of just calling it fake democracy, and I need it to honestly deal with the strongest point from the side that loses. It should read like something the board could actually act on, with a clear bottom line and a time frame for implementation if we go forward. No need for citations or a bibliography — just the reasoning laid out clean. Oh, and theres probably a typo or two in this message, but you get the idea.
