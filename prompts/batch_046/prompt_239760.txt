I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have been trying to work out for myself whether the idea of qualia—the private, subjective feel of experience—actually makes sense. I came across the notion of quining qualia, which I take to mean explaining the concept away rather than solving a problem about it, and I want to try applying that idea in my own way.

Here are my notes for a short essay. I begin from the claim that if a property is genuinely real, its presence or absence ought to be able to make some structural difference to the world, at least in principle. Qualia, however, are usually defined as intrinsic, non-relational, and causally inert: they are said to be exactly what they feel like, with no further structure. From this I draw a principle I call structural transparency—if a property is defined by having zero structural footprint, then attributing it to one thing but not to another becomes like attributing a secret colour to a number. To make the point concrete, I propose a mirror-inversion test: imagine two artificial systems with identical functional architectures, one running on silicon and one on synthetic biochemistry. If a defender of qualia insists that one system experiences red and the other does not, despite there being no detectable structural difference, then the attribution is not tracking any feature of the systems. It seems to me that this does not merely show we are ignorant of the qualia, but that the concept is being used inconsistently—like saying one identical twin possesses a hidden essence that the other lacks, where the essence is defined precisely by being hidden.

My question is whether this argument succeeds. Does the mirror-inversion test show that qualia are incoherent, or does it only show that we cannot know about them? And have I overlooked a reply that accepts structural transparency but denies that qualia are non-relational? Please comment on the accuracy of my reasoning, on whether the essay idea is interesting, and specifically on whether the principle of structural transparency is defensible.
