Consider a welfare-state design in which paid parental leave is divided into two non-transferable individual months for each parent plus a shared pool that either may take, yet public childcare subsidies are granted only when both parents work thirty or more hours per week and are withdrawn immediately if either drops below that threshold. What conception of care, gender, and economic citizenship does this pairing presuppose? Must it treat formal childcare as a labour-market instrument rather than a developmental good in its own right, or can a child-welfare theory accommodate the same rules? Reconstruct the implicit normative framework, identify whether the leave structure and the subsidy condition rest on consistent commitments or pull in different directions, and say what would have to change if the policy treated unpaid care work as socially productive labour rather than as a withdrawal from productivity.
