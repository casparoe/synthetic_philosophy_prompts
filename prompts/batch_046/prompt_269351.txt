I'm working through some philosophy of religion stuff and keep feeling like Molinism gets treated as a black box that just "solves" providence. Before I commit to anything, I want the actual spec sheet: what would a theory of divine middle knowledge have to deliver to count as adequate? Not just "God knows counterfactuals" -- I mean the full list of requirements with the case behind each.

So write the desiderata. Any adequate account needs to preserve real libertarian freedom, not just compatibilist wiggle-room, which means handling the freedom problem: if God knows via middle knowledge that I'd steal in situation S, and then actualizes S, in what sense do I still have the power to do otherwise? It needs to solve or at least dissolve the grounding objection: if counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are true prior to creation and not up to God's will, what makes them true without collapsing into determinism or brute fact? And it has to deal with the consistency problem -- if some feasible worlds contain unavoidable evil or universal salvation is impossible given the CCFs, then God doesn't have the range of options we usually think providence requires.

Then there's this loop thing I keep running into that nobody in my course mentions: multi-agent counterfactual dependancy. Say Agent A's free choice in world W depends on B being present, but B's presence in W depends on A's choice, and both are in God's middle knowledge before creation. The theory needs to explain how feasibility conditions are fixed without circularity breaking the selection of worlds. Also flag what's genuinely non-negotiable -- like comprehensive foreknowledge of future contingents -- versus camp preferences, like whether divine timelessness is required or just a nice-to-have.

Finally, score the main contenders briefly against the list: standard Plantinga-style Molinism, open theism, and an actualist/Ockhamist picture. Don't just name them; say which specific desideratum each fails on, and whether the multi-agent loop problem kills any of them outright.
