I'm putting together the take-home final for my grad seminar in phil of mind (mostly PhD students, a few MA’s). We spent the middle of the term on Dennett’s real patterns paper and the physical / design / intensional stance framework — yeah I know I keep misspelling that — but I’m tired of essays that just list the three stances and say people are best predicted intentionally. I want one essay question that forces students to actually use the framework on a weird edge case, not just recite it.

Here’s what I have in mind: ask them whether a system can genuinely exhibit real patterns that are only detectable from the intentional stance when, viewed from the design stance, it’s just running gradient descent with no explicit representations — think of an LLM or an adversarial vision model that behaves like it has beliefs or goals but is architecturally just optimizing a loss function. The crux should be about pattern ontology and stance-dependence: does Dennett’s claim that patterns are real only if they support successful predictions from some stance hold up when the design stance reveals nothing but weights and gradients? I don’t want students to need ML expertise or to cite specific papers; the philosophical tension should be do-able with just Dennett and some critical thinking.

The scheme should clearly separate A from B: B answers summarize Dennett and apply the stances mechanically but miss the deeper conflict between stance-dependence and pattern reality; A answers actually diagnose that tension — for instance, by asking whether the intentional stance is merely instrumental or genuinely tracks ontologically real patterns when the design stance undermines any representational interpretation. And please, no bibliography requests or citation lists — a student without web access should be able to answer this from the course readings alone.
