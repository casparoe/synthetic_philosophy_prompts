There's this pattern where cities and campuses push personal carbon footprint calculators—asking about your flights, beef consumption, car miles—but they don't ask about your investment portfolio or supply-chain emissions from your job. At the same time those same instutions run "every vote counts" drives for local elections where your single ballot is almost never decisive. What view of individual contribution to collective harm does that pairing presuppose? Do they think the inefficacy objection hits voting but not diet, or is there some hidden distinction—direct causation, visibility, control—that keeps it consistant? And if we actually applied the inefficacy worry evenly, what'd have to change in how they frame climate action versus civic duty?
