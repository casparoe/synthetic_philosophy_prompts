You sit on your university’s Joint Committee on Climate Governance and Endowment Ethics. The Board of Trustees has asked for a recommendation by the end of the quarter: should the institution (a) publicly endorse a state carbon-tax-and-dividend bill that prices emissions but exempts research-intensive manufacturing; (b) advocate instead for direct regulatory caps on institutional energy throughput; or (c) unilaterally adopt an internal “steady-state covenant” that caps the endowment’s absolute carbon footprint and ties investment performance to net energy-use reduction rather than to risk-adjusted financial growth, even if this conflicts with conventional fiduciary interpretation?

Draft the committee’s written opinion recommending one of these three courses, with a concrete phased timeline (e.g., over 12–24 months) and specific conditions for implementation. Your memo should engage seriously the strongest objection to your position—particularly the tension between treating atmospheric carbon capacity as an economically fungible externality correctable by price signals, and treating institutional growth itself as the problem that pricing fails to solve because of rebound effects and distributional asymmetry in dividend structures. Address whether fiduciary duty is compatible with an explicit degrowth mandate, and state clearly what the university should do if the state bill passes without the manufacturing exemption.
