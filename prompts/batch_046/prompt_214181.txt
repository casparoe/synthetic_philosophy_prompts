I've been messin around with superrationality stuff after a decison theory course — specifically the Platonia dilemma and that weird Luring Lottery setup where copies of you are supposed to coordinate without talking. I think I cooked up an argument that takes those ideas way past where they should go, and now I'm stuck.

Here's the chain:
(1) In Platonia, if two agents have literally identical reasoning and each knows it, then whatever choice one makes is proof about the other — so they should pick the superrational option, not the dominant-strategy one.
(2) The Luring Lottery is basically Platonia scaled up: a bunch of copies, and if enough of them enter, you win. So each copy should enter, treating its own choice as evidence about the others.
(3) Now here's the move I'm suspicious of: for any decision I make, there are always counterfactual or multiversal agents whose reasoning is structurally identical to mine facing structurally identical choices.
(4) So if (1) holds for Platonia agents, consistency says it should hold for these copies too — my choice here should be the one I'd want all my copies everywhere to make.
(5) That means every local decision collapses into a global optimization: in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with a stranger, I should cooperate because my copies elsewhere are cooperating with copies of him; in Newcomb's problem I should one-box; but also, if there's any scenario where universal cooperation is catastrophic, I'm still locked in.
(6) So rational choice stops being about local payoffs or causal structure altogether and becomes one universal policy profile. That feels like it proves too much — it wipes out the distinction between evidential and causal reasoning and makes decison theory apply globally whether there's any actual correlation mechanism.
(7) Therefore either (1) is false, or there's a hidden restriction I'm missing.

Where does the slip happen? Is it in (3) — that counterfactual copies don't count as Platonia-style correlates? Or in (4) — that logical correlation doesn't extend across unrelated decison contexts? Or is the argument actually valid, and the cost is just accepting that rationality really is globally uniform? I'd kinda like to keep (1) and (2) intact if possible, but I'm not sure what giving up (4) costs me. What's the honest diagnosis here?
