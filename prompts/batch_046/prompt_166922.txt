I am trying to sort out my position on typogenetic self-replication and its relation to Gödelian self-reference before presenting it in a seminar, and I want a rigorous consistency audit. Here are my commitments:

(1) DNA replication realises a typogenetic strange loop: the genome encodes the ribosomal and enzymatic apparatus that decodes and reproduces it, which is structurally isomorphic to Gödel’s construction of a self-referential sentence within a formal system.

(2) This isomorphism is substantive rather than merely poetic. It explains why transcription and replication errors are not external disturbances but structurally required degrees of freedom—without them, open-ended evolutionary adaptation would be impossible.

(3) Biological replication is not a formal system in the Gödelian sense. The genetic code has evolved, translation rules are mutable, and there is no fixed, recursively enumerable set of axioms; therefore the technical conditions for Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem are not satisfied by life.

(4) Accordingly, I hold that life exhibits Gödel-like self-reference while remaining entirely free of Gödelian incompleteness, and that this pairing is coherent because structural analogy does not entail logical instantiation.

(5) Any adequate account of replication must be mechanistic and information-theoretic, with no appeal to vitalism or non-physical interpretative agents; the loop operates solely through physical chemistry and information dynamics.

(6) At the same time, I am sceptical of purely computational or in-silico models of replication—such as digital genome simulations—because they presuppose a sharp separation between data and interpreter that DNA does not observe; in the cell, the code and the decoding machinery are chemically entangled.

Which of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, which only appear to do so, and what is the least costly revision to restore full consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what has to give, and indicate whether yielding on that point undermines (5) or (6) more seriously. Be precise about whether the difficulty lies in (1) overreaching, in (2) treating an analogy as an explanation, or in (4) attempting to retain the loop while discarding the logical machinery that makes it interesting.
