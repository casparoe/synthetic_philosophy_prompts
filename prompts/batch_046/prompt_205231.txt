Write a joke that plays on Kurt Gödel's rotating-universe cosmological model and the philosophical use he made of it. The punchline should hinge on two genuinely specific points: first, that the model's metric permits closed timelike curves, so that an observer could in principle travel into her own past; and second, that Gödel took this physical possibility as evidence for an idealist thesis—namely, that the objective passage or flow of time is not real, since a universe without a consistent global time-slicing cannot support an absolute "before" and "after." The joke should be intelligible to someone familiar with Gödel's argument that relativity, taken seriously, dissolves the intuitive A-theory of time, but it should not rely on invented jargon, obscure secondary sources, or requests for citations. Keep it brief, and make the philosophical point do the comedic work rather than generic physics humor.
