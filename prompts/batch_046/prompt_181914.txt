Since I began drafting the conceptual framework for a museum exhibition on technological diffusion in deep time, I have been working through explanatory norms with my advisor, an archaeologist who studies the historiography of grand narratives. Her judgments on specific claims have been steady enough to learn from, yet ambiguous enough that I cannot tell which principle she is actually running.

Her verdicts so far are these. On Harari’s claim in Sapiens that a single Cognitive Revolution—rooted in a genetic mutation enabling fictional language—was the decisive break that made all subsequent cultural complexity possible, she said it is a useful pedagogical fiction but fails as history because it treats culture as a passive downstream effect of biology, ignoring millennia of gradual, distributed experimentation. On Diamond’s argument in Guns, Germs, and Steel that Eurasia’s east-west axis and domesticable species deterministically produced technological and political dominance, she rejected it as geographic determinism that renders human choice epiphenomenal, though she granted that ecological constraints are real. On Graeber and Wengrow’s thesis in The Dawn of Everything that prehistoric societies actively switched between hierarchical and egalitarian modes, and that inevitable state formation is a modern myth, she endorsed it enthusiastically for restoring contingency and agency. On a recent monocausal explanation of the Rapa Nui collapse—that competitive statue-building alone drove deforestation and ruin—she insisted that ecological stress and social choice must be shown interacting, not that one explains away the other. And on a climate-determinist account of the Near Eastern Neolithic Revolution, she argued that foraging groups actively resisted cultivation for centuries despite favorable conditions, so any adequate account must show how material possibilities were taken up, resisted, or transformed by social practice.

These judgments fit at least two competing rules. One is an anti-determinism principle: she rejects any explanation that makes societies passive recipients of biological, geographic, or climatic forces, endorsing those that center agency, contingency, and social experimentation. The other is an integration principle: she demands that every explanation combine material constraints with social or ideational dynamics, rejecting not determinism per se but any account—materialist or idealist—that treats one domain as sufficient.

The new case concerns a hypothetical archaeological synthesis I will call the Balkan syncretism model, addressing the rapid, near-simultaneous adoption of bronze metallurgy across Central Europe around 2200 BCE. The model proposes that bronze objects served as boundary objects in inter-group ritual exchange, allowing previously hostile forager-farmer communities to form temporary alliances without shared kinship or language. It holds that ecological availability of copper and tin was a purely permissive background condition with no active constraining force; that economic utility and elite demand played essentially no role; and that diffusion followed ritual-exchange networks rather than trade efficiency or conquest. Crucially, it notes that in regions where local taboo prohibited the fusion of metals for cosmological reasons, bronze was present but deliberately rejected for over four centuries, then adopted almost instantly when a specific astronomical event was interpreted as lifting the prohibition.

What verdict should I expect? Would she endorse this model, reject it, or qualify her judgment? For each of the two candidate rules—anti-determinism and integration—state exactly what it predicts about the Balkan case, identify which of her earlier verdicts is the closest analog, and give your best guess at her actual response with the reasons for preferring that rule. If her track record genuinely underdetermines the outcome, specify what further case—preferably one involving a purely ideational explanation of technological change with minimal material interaction—would best reveal which principle she is running.
