Next month I'm giving a colloquium talk defending the idea that bureaucratic lock-in isn't just institutional inertia but can be modeled as precision-weighting gone wrong inside a predictive-processing framework: the institution treats prediction errors about procedural compliance as high-precision (must correct immediately) while treating errors about actual outcomes as low-precision noise (ignore). I'm using free-energy principle machinery purely as a formal analogy—no claim that bureacracies literally have brains or beliefs—but I do think the analogy explains why reform fails even when everyone sees the bad outcomes.
