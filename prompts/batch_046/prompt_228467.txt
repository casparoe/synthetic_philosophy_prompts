You serve as the senior review officer for a metropolitan infrastructure authority that evaluates proposed alterations to adaptive urban systems—smart floodgates, dynamic traffic networks, autonomous water-diversion protocols—under a new liability ordinance. Every month you review roughly ten proposals; each determination carries legal weight and must be signed off by a non-expert board within a 90-minute window, documented on a single page, with no access to external databases during the session. Write a reusable decision procedure that operationalizes the interventionist account of causation: a feature counts as a cause of harm only if, within an explicit structural model of the system, an intervention that disables or alters that feature changes the probability distribution of the outcome, with correlation, temporal order, and bare counterfactual dependence treated as preliminary but insufficient indicators. Present it as a word-flowchart: ordered questions, what answers are sufficient at each stage, explicit stop points returning NO CAUSE or CAUSE, and clearly flagged escalation nodes where the dispute is about model choice or values rather than fact. The procedure must handle spurious correlation through hidden common causes; direct mechanistic causation with identifiable mediators; and redundant-system overdetermination, in which disabling a feature activates an autonomous backup so the outcome distribution is invariant, even though the feature actually contributed in the realized world. Apply the procedure to three worked cases: (A) a proposed smart-levee algorithm that adjusts gate openings using real-time rainfall sensors, where critics claim downstream flooding will result; (B) a pedestrian-zone expansion statistically correlated with reduced asthma rates, where both are driven by a prior air-quality mandate; and (C) a dual-mode floodgate with an AI-driven predictive opening mode and an independent mechanical failsafe, where disabling the predictive mode leaves flooding risk unchanged because the failsafe activates—here the procedure should yield an answer that clears the predictive feature of liability despite an intuitive sense of shared blame, and you must mark exactly where human judgment must overrule the form. State precisely what the procedure cannot do—such as assign moral blame, resolve metaphysical disputes about actual causation outside the model, or substitute for democratic value judgments about acceptable risk. Do not invoke bibliographies, reading lists, named scholars, or specific texts.
