Since I recently acquired legal residency in a country where I was not born, while retaining citizenship in my country of origin, I have become personally preoccupied with the democratic boundary problem: who legitimately belongs to the demos that exercises collective authority? Write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—one defending a stakeholder-plus view that extends democratic membership to all those stably subject to a polity’s coercive power and to outsiders whose vital interests are systematically affected by its decisions, and one defending a bounded-association view that restricts full membership to citizens who share a reciprocal, constitutional commitment and mutual accountability within a specific order. The exchange should address three tensions in particular: the recursive problem that any decision about who belongs must be made by an already-constituted demos whose own legitimacy is thereby presupposed; the case of cross-border affected interests, such as communities harmed by upstream environmental decisions in which they hold no formal representation; and the normative distinction between being subject to law and being a co-author of law. Each speaker should respond directly to the other’s actual arguments rather than restate their own position. Do not cite specific scholars, texts, or historical cases; reason from first principles.
