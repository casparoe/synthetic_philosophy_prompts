I have drafted the outline of a short essay on a tension in Schopenhauer's aesthetics. The central claim is that Schopenhauer's elevation of music to the status of the highest art—on the ground that it is a direct objectification of the Will rather than a copy of an Idea—creates an unresolved conflict with his broader epistemology. According to the standard reading in The World as Will and Representation, all knowledge of the Will is mediated by the principle of sufficient reason and requires the subject-object form; yet Schopenhauer describes music as bypassing Ideas entirely and presenting the Will itself in a non-representational manner. I argue that this is not merely an exception but a structural anomaly: if music genuinely delivers unmediated access to the noumenal Will, then Schopenhauer's Kantian restriction of knowledge to phenomena is breached from within his own system, not just transcended in mystical experience.

The niche angle I want to develop is that Schopenhauer's occasional remarks in later essays on rhythm and melody suggest he was implicitly relying on a pre-representational, affective mode of cognition—something closer to bodily attunement than to intuitive knowledge. I propose that recognizing this hidden premise allows us to see music not as the crown of Schopenhauer's metaphysics but as its internal limit case: an art form that works precisely because it violates the conditions he sets for all other cognition. The essay would then ask whether this makes Schopenhauer's aesthetics more coherent or whether it collapses the distinction between representation and Will that underpins his entire philosophy.

I would appreciate comments on three specific points. First, is the alleged conflict genuinely present in Schopenhauer's texts, or does he have a consistent way of allowing music to access the Will without violating the subject-object structure—perhaps through a special kind of inner intuition? Second, is my injection of a pre-representational affective cognition a defensible reconstruction, or does it anachronistically import concepts from later phenomenology or embodied cognition that distort Schopenhauer's actual commitments? Third, if the tension is real, does resolving it in music's favor undermine his critique of Kant's unknowable thing-in-itself, given that Schopenhauer insists the Will is known only as phenomenon in self-consciousness?
