hey so i just started volunteering at a reentry center near me and its got me thinking hard about punishment and whether "just deserts" is even real or just useful fiction. ive been skimming dennett and caruso lately (the free will / punishment debate) and im drafting notes for a discussion group next week.

dennett thinks free will is a real evolved skill, so we can blame people without believing in some ghost-in-the-machine karma. caruso wants us to drop desert talk completely and handle crime like a public health quarantine — contain risk, dont moralize.

heres my half-baked twist: both miss the bookkeeping function. in small communities, saying someone "got what they deserved" isnt about cosmic justice, its a signal that the social contract is still symmetric. if we erase that language entirely, we might break coordination — people need to know rules are enforced fairly, not just efficiently. so im arguing for a "thin desert" view: keep the language but only as social equilibrium maintenance, not metaphysical moral worth. we still lock people up when needed, but the justification is stability, not balancing scales.

is this coherent? am i misreading dennett's point about evolved responsibility? and does caruso actually talk about social signaling, or am i inventing a gap? also, is there a name for treating desert-talk as functional bookkeeping rather than metaphysics? i dont care about citations, just want to know if the logic holds and where it falls apart.
