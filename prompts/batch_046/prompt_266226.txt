Been trying to wrap my head around the continuum hypothesis debate and honestly it feels stuck in mud since Cohens forcing result back in the 60s. You've got Woodin-style people arguing for a canonical inner model with a rich hierachy of cardinals between aleph-null and the continuum, which makes CH false, then the multiverse crowd saying asking for the size of the continuum is like asking for the true geometry, and then analysts who just want forcing axioms and to move on. But every new paper seems to rehearse the same moves — large cardinals, constructibility, determinacy arguments, except in analysts practice — and nobody ever shifts position. Is this a substantive disagreement about the actual size of the real numbers, or is it verbal, with continuum meaning different things? Or are the sides answering different questions — one about an objective universe of sets, one about which formal system is best for doing math? Maybe there's a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work that I'm not seeing? I want a diagnosis of the impasse, backed by what each camp actually says, plus what — if anything — would count as progress. Would another consistency result change minds, or is the real problem that we don't agree on what counts as evidence for set-theoretic truth? And if CH is genuinely indeterminant, does that mean the question was ill-posed all along, or just that our tools aren't sharp enough?
