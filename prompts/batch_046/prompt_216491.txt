Design a 75-minute classroom activity for a philosophy of science seminar of about 14 graduate students that makes them feel, from the inside, the gap between determinism and predictability—specifically through the idea that an observer embedded in a deterministic chaotic system is structurally prevented from acquiring the precise initial conditions needed for long-range forecasting, not merely because of practical limits but because the act of measurement and prediction becomes part of the dynamics being measured. The activity should be a runnable simulation or structured game with assigned roles, hidden deterministic rules, constrained information flow, and small symbolic stakes arranged so that participants run into predictive failure before anyone names determinism, chaos, or the observer problem. Include: the physical setup and materials needed; the interaction rules that enforce informational closure; a phase-by-phase timeline; the debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the distinction between ontological determinism, epistemic predictability, and embedded observation; an honest account of what will predictably go wrong on the first run, such as students treating hidden rules as probabilistic or attempting to circumvent information constraints; and a frank admission of which formal feature—specifically the exponential divergence of trajectories characterized by positive Lyapunov exponents—the activity can only analogize and the facilitator must supply directly. Do not include reading assignments, bibliographies, or citation requests.
