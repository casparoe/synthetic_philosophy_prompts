I'm giving the retirement toast for my old phil prof, Dr. Elena Vasquez, who taught Hegel's master-slave dialectic for like thirty years but always said people butcher it by treating it as an individual ego trip. Her version was about institutional recogntion — a career only becomes genuinely yours when the institution mirrors back a self-image you can actually live inside, and retirement is this strange rupture where the mirror just stops. Half the room are her grad students who'll catch every sloppy Phenomenology reference; the other half are her kids and partner's family who don't know Hegel from Heidegger. Write me a four-minute toast that uses that framework to say why she stayed so long, why leaving isn't just 'freedom,' and why calling her 'finally liberated' would actually insult the shape of her life. Keep it affectionate, not a lecture, and don't pretend retirement is pure escape. Also please don't quote Hegel directly — she always hated being turned into a citation.
