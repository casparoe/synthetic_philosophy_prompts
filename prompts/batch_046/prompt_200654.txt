Analyze the 2022 collapse of the TerraUSD algorithmic stablecoin and its associated lending protocol, Anchor, through the lens of Aristotle's distinction between oikonomia (household management oriented toward use) and chrematistike (wealth-getting pursued for its own sake), particularly his argument in the Politics that money was invented to facilitate exchange but that lending at interest—tokos—is the most unnatural form of acquisition because it treats coin, which is barren by nature, as if it could reproduce itself. Treat the automated mint-and-burn mechanism and the protocol's guaranteed yield not as conventional banking but as a purified instance of tokos detached from any underlying circulation of goods: identify which actors in the ecosystem correspond to the household manager directing resources toward a natural end, and which correspond to the chrematist engaged in unlimited accumulation; specify what Aristotle takes to be the proper telos of exchange that this arrangement violates; and then assess whether the collapse confirms his thesis—that separating monetary gain from the exchange of use-values produces inherent instability—or whether it instead reveals a limit to his framework, given that the 'use' being managed was precisely the synthetic generation of further exchange-value. Be explicit about whether algorithmic governance operating at global scale can still be assimilated to oikonomia, and whether the absence of a tangible commodity underlying the yield makes the analogy to barren coinage exact or instead shows that digital assets have altered the conditions under which these Aristotelian categories apply.
