hey, long time lurker here. been chewing on the art/artist separation thing after some threads on moral contamination. my study partner came up with this argument that seems to prove you can never really seperate aesthetic appreciation from the artist's moral status, but if you buy it you end up having to boycott basically every old painting because who knows what some 17th century patron was up to. feels like a reductio but i can't spot the sleight of hand.

(1) Dependency: if work W has aesthetic property P, then P depends causally on the mental states and choices of W's makers.

(2) Transparency: to appreciate P you need some grasp of the causal story that produced it - not full biography, but you have to understand what choices shaped the work.

(3) Contamination: if any maker in that chain had severely immoral mental states (say, abusing people to get a specific performance), those states are part of the causal explanation of P.

(4) So grasping the causal story of P requires grasping those immoral mental states.

(5) Accommodation: but grasping an immoral mental state as part of your appreciation means you're accomodating it - letting it structure your aesthetic experience rather than bracketing it.

(6) So if there's contamination in the chain, appreciating P requires accommodating immorality.

(7) Therefore you aren't separating the work from the artist; you're letting the artist's moral status shape the appreciation.

(8) Extension: apply this not just to the named artist but to the whole production chain - editors, producers, grips, patrons.

(9) Epistemic limit: we rarely have full moral knowledge of everyone in the chain, and historical works definately involved hidden exploitation (slavery funding art, etc.).

(10) So we can never be sure the chain is clean, meaning separation is always epistemically blocked and we should never fully appreciate any work without moral reservation.

this seems like it proves too much - i definately still appreciate old films without worrying about every crew member's personal life. but each step feels kinda defensible? tell me which numbered step to reject and why it looked fine. especially keep (1) and (2) intact if you can since they feel like basic philosophy of art. also, what's the cost of rejecting it - do you have to deny that art appreciation is causal at all, or can you reject it more surgically? and what do the standard "autonomist" vs "moralist" positions in aesthetics have to pay if they reject that step?
