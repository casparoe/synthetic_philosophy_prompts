I'm a journalist covering philosophy of mind and value theory, and I'm drafting a long-form feature on whether hedonism can survive the heterogeneity objection. Before interviewing scholars, I want to stress-test a provisional framework I've developed in conversation with a philosopher who studies affective phenomenology. Here is the package of commitments I've adopted—some conventional, some idiosyncratic.

First, I take the heterogeneity problem to be decisive against any view that posits a shared intrinsic phenomenal quality across pleasures: the warmth of a bath, the satisfaction of a proof, and the thrill of a risky decision share no common qualitative feel. Second, what unifies them is a structural-relational feature I call an "affective frame"—a mode of present-moment awareness that marks its object as "to-be-continued" rather than "to-be-avoided." Third, this frame is sui generis: it is not reducible to belief or desire, though I remain agnostic about whether it is best understood as a mental state or a purely functional disposition. Fourth, the frame can attach to otherwise aversive stimuli, which explains masochistic pleasure: the pain retains its aversive sensory character, but the frame directs continuation, yielding pleasure. Fifth, despite its unifying role, I deny that the frame itself carries any "what-it-is-like" quality or qualitative residue; it exerts only a directional force. Sixth, I am a value hedonist: pleasure is the sole intrinsic good, and its goodness derives precisely from carrying this frame.

Please audit this set for consistency. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension—particularly any conflict between the sui generis claim and the denial of qualitative residue, and between the masochism explanation and the absence of felt quality—versus which conflicts only appear serious and dissolve on inspection. Rank the tensions by severity, specify the cheapest revision for each, and say what that revision costs, especially whether it forces me to abandon hedonism, the frame model, or the anti-phenomenal claim. Give me a committed verdict: is the package salvageable with minimal surgery, and if not, which single commitment should be dropped first? Answer in American English.
