In my philosophy-of-decision-theory seminar, debate over the Pasadena game—the Nover-Hájek variant of the St. Petersburg construction in which the expectation is not merely infinite but genuinely undefined, with standard dominance reasoning failing to assign any coherent expected value—has been deadlocked for over a year. One faction treats this as a direct reductio of expected-utility theory and demands revised axioms for cases of undefined expectation; another faction, following the ill-posedness line, insists that the game describes no genuine decision situation because rational agents cannot face payoff structures that violate the conditions needed for a well-defined expectation; and a smaller group proposes non-Archimedean utility representations, arguing that the undefinedness is an artifact of modeling payoffs on the real line rather than a failure of rational choice itself. We all share the same formal setup, agree that no standard expectation exists, and can reproduce each other’s proofs, yet no position has moved. My working hypothesis is that the stall is not a substantive disagreement about decision rules but an implicit, unargued clash over whether rational choice requires structural completeness—that every admissible option must carry a numerically comparable value across all outcomes—or whether rationality can operate with locally ordered but globally incommensurable preferences. Is that diagnosis correct, or is there instead a hidden formal disagreement about the scope of dominance principles when expectations diverge, or are the sides actually answering different questions—one normative, one about formal representation, one about descriptive applicability—under the single heading “the Pasadena paradox”? I want a committed diagnosis with reasons: is this verbal, substantive, bedrock, or a case of misaligned questions? And given your diagnosis, what would actually constitute progress—a conceptual distinction between ontic undefinedness (no fact of the matter about the value of playing) and epistemic undefinedness (our inability to compute it), a proof that non-Archimedean fixes collapse into incoherence, a new axiom restricting admissible payoff structures, or an argument that the problem is malformed and should be dissolved?
