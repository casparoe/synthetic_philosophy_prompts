Hey, I'm trying to get my head around Schrödinger's Vedanta thing — the idea that there's only one consciousness and individual minds are just facets of it. I understand he was influenced by the Upanishads and liked the idea of atman being identical to brahman, but I'm stuck on a conceptual issue that doesn't get talked about much.

If consciousness is literally numericaly one, not just qualitatively similar across people, then brain damage should in principle affect the whole pool, right? But empirically it only seems to knock out one person's stream. Schrödinger's usual answer seems to be the radio analogy: the brain is a reciever, not a generator, so damaging the reciever just stops that particular tuning without affecting the broadcast. But here's a framing I haven't seen worked out: what if we replace the receiver model with a 'localized occlusion filter'? Instead of tuning in, each brain selectively blocks out parts of the universal consciousness field. Damage then doesn't destroy anything globally; it just changes the occlusion pattern. The problem is, this immediately raises a question about privacy: why don't damaged filters start leaking other people's experiences? You'd need some hidden mechanism of 'private occulsion' that keeps the blocked parts hidden even when the filter is messed up. So my actual question is: can Schrödinger's monism survive without smuggling in a pluralist mechanism that does all the real work? Or does the need for private occlusion mean his view collapses into funcitonalism with one big background pool?

Can you explain this conceptual knot? I'm particularly interested in whether the filter/receiver distinction actually solves anything or just relabels the hard part. Also sorry for the messy framing — there's probably a logic slip in there, but don't fix my grammar, just dig into the concept.
