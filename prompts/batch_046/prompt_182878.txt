Please describe the most important implications of maintaining a strict distinction between knowing how and knowing that—specifically when supplemented by a further division between embodied procedural knowledge, manifested only in situated performance and resistant to extraction as explicit belief, and reconstructible propositional knowledge, which can be articulated and verified independently of action—for the design of assessment protocols for artificial systems that replicate expert performance in domains where human practitioners deny being able to articulate their reasoning, such as expert radiological diagnosis. Address whether achieving equivalent practical performance can ever establish that a system possesses the same epistemic kind of knowledge as the human expert, or whether an unavoidable asymmetry forces us to treat machine expertise as a categorically distinct phenomenon. Your answer should cover the implications for institutional liability, for the structure of training and validation regimes, and for the appropriate scope of explainability requirements, and should identify the points where these implications become indeterminate without further normative commitments.
