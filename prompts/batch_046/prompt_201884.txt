Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the Uniform Hedonic Tone camp (UHT) and the Heterogeneous Family-Resemblance camp (HFR) on the heterogeneity problem for qualitative hedonism. UHT holds that every pleasure—whether somatic, intellectual, aesthetic, or social—contains one and the same intrinsic phenomenal quality, a unitary hedonic tone whose cross-modal differences are fully explained by variations in intensity, duration, and the sensory or cognitive content with which the tone is fused; HFR holds that pleasures share no single intrinsic phenomenal quality, that cross-modal comparison reveals genuinely distinct affective textures, and that the unity of pleasure is secured only extrinsically—by functional roles, intentional relations, or patterns of pursuit—while the appearance of a common feel is an artifact of abstraction.

The deliverable should contain three parts.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims, including: (a) the precise formulation of the problem—why the warmth-pleasure of sunlight, the pleasure of resolving a proof, and the pleasure of a harmonic resolution appear phenomenologically dissimilar in ways that threaten qualitative hedonism; (b) the joint rejection of purely quantitative solutions, specifically that differences in intensity and duration alone dissolve the objection; (c) agreement on the relevant empirical constraints, particularly that reward-circuitry shows partial convergence across pleasure-types and that this convergence is interpretively ambiguous with respect to phenomenal identity; (d) agreement that the dispute is substantive rather than purely verbal, since both camps acknowledge that there is a real question about whether phenomenal structure is uniform or modality-specific; and (e) agreement that neither camp is committed to denying that people can rank pleasures or that pleasure is generally sought for its own sake.

Second, a disagreements table with one row per crux. The cruxes should include at least: (i) whether introspective comparison across pleasure-types can reliably isolate a common hedonic tone or whether such comparison only reveals family-resemblance relations; (ii) whether neural and functional convergence across modalities supports phenomenal identity or merely analogical functional roles; (iii) whether pleasantness is decomposable into modality-specific affective textures plus a separable valence dimension, or whether valence itself is the uniform tone. For each crux, provide UHT’s formulation phrased so UHT would sign it, and HFR’s formulation phrased so HFR would sign it.

Third, for each crux, specify one concrete conceptual case, phenomenological observation, or argument—do not invent empirical studies or depend on obscure primary texts—that both camps agree is genuinely probative, and record each side’s explicit prediction about what that case demonstrates. Then conclude with a brief section stating which residual disagreements, if any, both camps concede are terminological (for example, disputes over whether “pleasantness” denotes a natural kind or a functional kind) and identifying the exact point of phenomenal-structure divergence that remains unsettled.

The prompt should be answerable without web access: do not request bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific editions or page numbers. You may inject technical distinctions—such as that between hedonic valence and hedonic texture, or between phenomenal fusion and phenomenal addition—to keep the framing specific and non-generic, but do not depend on niche theories, unpublished experiments, or data that a well-trained language model would not have properly encountered. Write in formal, polished prose.
