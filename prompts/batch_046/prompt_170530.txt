The doxastic-transparency side should hold that first-person belief is essentially transparent: sincerely believing p entails being disposed to avow p, so holding p together with the belief that one does not hold p is not merely unwise but doxastically incoherent — a contradiction in the very architecture of belief. The pragmatic-norm side should hold that belief lacks this constitutive transparency condition; compartmentalized or hierarchically separated belief is genuinely possible, and the absurdity is confined to the speech act — specifically, a violation of the assertion norm that one should not present p as warranted for an audience while signaling that one does not take it to be warranted for oneself.
