I hold that the analytic-synthetic distinction can be rehabilitated against Quine's arguments in Two Dogmas of Empiricism by treating it as a graded feature of belief networks rather than a categorical boundary: a statement is analytic for a scheme when abandoning it would force revisions so extensive that the scheme's identity conditions are lost, not because it is conventionally true but because it is structurally central. What would genuinely count against this view? I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they would be, from fatal to merely awkward: empirical or formal results showing that no non-arbitrary metric of centrality exists; historical episodes in science where apparently core commitments were abandoned without scheme collapse; and arguments that the proposed scheme-identity condition is either too vague to do work or can always be adjusted post hoc to protect the distinction. I also want an honest assessment of whether this rehabilitation risks unfalsifiability through such adjustments, and whether that insulation is a theoretical weakness or an acceptable feature of a framework-level claim.
