Write an essay that takes up the boundary problem in democratic theory—who belongs to the demos—under the specific conditions of a transboundary river-basin authority that exercises binding regulatory control over water allocation, pollution limits, and infrastructure development across sovereign borders. In weighing the all-subjected principle, the all-affected-interests principle, and a stakeholder-derived co-determination criterion adapted from corporate governance theory, examine the competing membership claims of riparian-state citizens, indigenous nations with treaty-based territorial rights, long-term non-citizen agricultural workers, and future generations who inherit the hydrological consequences. Reach and justify a definite conclusion specifying which of these groups should possess voting membership, and explain why your preferred boundary criterion should override its rivals in this particular institutional context.
