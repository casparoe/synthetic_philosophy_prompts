I have been trying to understand, in a non-technical way, whether my existence is essentially tied to the exact physical sperm and egg that created me, or only to the genetic information those cells carried. To check my own intuition, I have been describing hypothetical situations to my friend Lena, who has never studied philosophy but gives remarkably steady answers. Her judgments so far are these.

If I had been born from the same two parents but from a different sperm and egg—essentially one of my actual siblings—she says without hesitation that this would not be me.

If I had come from the exact same sperm and egg cells, but they had been implanted in a different woman’s womb, she says that is still me.

If those same two cells had been cryopreserved and then used to conceive me twenty years later than they actually were, she still says that is me.

Now I want to present her with a case I only recently thought of, involving something I encountered in passing about laboratory techniques: scientists can take an adult skin cell, revert it to a pluripotent stem state, and then derive synthetic gametes that carry precisely the genome of that adult. Suppose researchers take one of my own adult skin cells, generate stem cells from it, and then grow a sperm and an egg that carry my exact DNA sequence, though these synthetic cells never existed inside either of my parents. Those lab-grown gametes are fertilized in vitro and develop into a child.

I have not asked Lena about this scenario yet. Based on her earlier pattern, do you think she would say that child is me? More generally, what are the two or three consistent principles that could explain her first three answers—perhaps one focused on the physical continuity of the original cells, another focused only on preserving the genetic sequence, and perhaps a third about the natural reproductive context? Which principle do you think she is actually following, and why? Finally, if her answer to the synthetic-gamete case does not fully resolve the matter between those principles, what single further hypothetical—maybe involving a small, precise genetic alteration introduced into the original gametes before fertilization—would best reveal whether she cares about the physical cells themselves or only about the information they contain?
