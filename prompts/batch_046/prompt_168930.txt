Illusionism about phenomenal consciousness claims that phenomenal properties are not genuinely instantiated but are systematically misrepresented by introspective mechanisms. Rank the following five philosophical positions or objections by defensibility, from 1 (most defensible) to 5 (least defensible): (A) uniform illusionism applies to both sensory and affective phenomenology, treating all introspective reports as misrepresentational; (B) restricted illusionism applies only to sensory phenomenology, leaving affective feelings genuinely instantiated; (C) illusionism is coherent only when combined with direct realism about perception, since representationalist accounts of experience undermine the misrepresentation story; (D) illusionism necessarily collapses into eliminativism about consciousness, because without phenomenal properties there is no substrate left to misrepresent; (E) illusionism is correct, but introspection delivers accurate meta-representational judgments about non-phenomenal cognitive states, leaving no explanatory gap. Present the result as a numbered list with exactly five entries in rank order, each entry one or two sentences that name the position and state the central reason for its rank. Include no introduction or conclusion.
