I organize a philosophy club at my high school—about fifteen students, meeting for ninety minutes. I want a structured role-play that makes us feel the force of Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor: the claim that people will freely trade freedom for security and happiness, and that the burden of choice is itself the source of misery. Design a two-round simulation with rules, assigned roles, a timeline, and a payoff scheme that uses only social stakes—no real money—so that in round one the group chooses a hidden rule-maker who promises to eliminate hard decisions, and in round two they discover the rules were engineered without their full knowledge and must vote on whether to retain them. Include a debrief that maps each round onto the Inquisitor's argument, notes honestly which part of Dostoevsky's theological framing—namely that free will is a divine gift whose weight becomes unbearable—this activity cannot reproduce, and predicts what will go wrong the first time I run it.
