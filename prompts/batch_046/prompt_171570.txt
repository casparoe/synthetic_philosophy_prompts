Since my father was recently evaluated for a corpus callosotomy to treat intractable epilepsy, I have been trying to understand what split-brain cases actually reveal about whether consciousness is necessarily unified. I am less interested in the popular-science summary than in a disciplined conceptual analysis.

Take the classic split-brain experiments conducted in the 1960s by Roger Sperry and Michael Gazzaniga — in which patients with a severed corpus callosum were presented with visual stimuli restricted to one visual field, leading to situations where the left hemisphere could verbally name an object that the right hemisphere had guided the left hand to select, and in which the left hemisphere often confabulated explanations for actions it had not actually initiated. Analyze this episode through two lenses: first, the philosophical unity thesis, which claims that a single conscious subject cannot simultaneously hold genuinely contradictory beliefs or intentions; second, the concept of institutional decoupling, drawn from organizational sociology, according to which a unified system can split into autonomous subsystems that maintain local functioning after the coordinating mechanism is removed, with one subsystem continuing to issue authoritative reports about the whole.

In your analysis, be precise about the mapping: who or what counts as the agent or subject in each hemisphere, what the available options and evidence look like under each framework, and whether the left-hemisphere interpreter module should be understood as preserving unity, manufacturing a fiction of unity, or serving as the remaining bureaucratic center of a decoupled system. Then turn the analysis back on the theories: does the existence of apparently independent streams of processing refute the unity thesis, or does the thesis survive by redefining what counts as a contradiction? Does institutional decoupling illuminate the split-brain case, or does it misapply a macro-social concept to subpersonal processes? Finally, tell me what this episode suggests about whether phenomenal unity is an intrinsic feature of consciousness or a post-hoc narrative achievement, and identify the points where both frameworks are silent rather than stretching them to cover every detail. I do not need a bibliography or citation list; I want an honest account of where the mapping succeeds and where it breaks.
