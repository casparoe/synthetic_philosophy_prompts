1. i think lewis-style counterfactual dependence gets type-level causation right, but only if we restrict it with a 'stability parameter' that holds background conditions fixed across a specific modal range.
2. i think interventionist logic (woodward/pearl) is the right tool for token-level overdetermination, where neither cause is counterfactualy necessary but both register in the do-calculus.

4. there's a normative/pragmatic layer: which counterfactuals count and which variables are 'intervenable' depends partly on our explanatory goals and what we hold agents responsible for.
5. that pragmatic layer is purely epistemic - it affects how we discover causes, not what causation metaphysically is.
