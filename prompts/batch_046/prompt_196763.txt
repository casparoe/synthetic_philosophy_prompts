I am taking an upper-level seminar on Wittgenstein’s later philosophy, and we have been working through the language-game passages and the family-resemblance discussion in Philosophical Investigations §§65–67. Our instructor recently introduced Waismann’s notion of open texture as a way of thinking about concepts that lack sharp boundaries, and I have become stuck on what feels like a tension between that idea and Wittgenstein’s internalist account of grammar.

Here is my difficulty. Family resemblance is standardly taken to show that the concept language game possesses no essential feature shared by every instance—only a network of overlapping similarities. If that is correct, then the concept itself seems open-textured: there ought to be borderline cases that we cannot determinately classify as belonging or not belonging to the family. Yet Wittgenstein also insists that, within any particular language game, grammatical rules establish a rigid distinction between correct and incorrect use; meaning is fixed by public norms, not by family similarities among individual utterances. So my question is whether these two commitments can actually coexist. If the concept language game lacks an essence and therefore admits of genuinely indeterminate boundary cases, does that not entail that the rules internal to any given game are themselves open-textured? In other words, for any purported grammatical rule, might there not be a borderline application where it is genuinely unclear whether the rule governs the move, which would dissolve the correct/incorrect distinction from within rather than preserving it?

More specifically, I worry that I am illegitimately letting the inter-game structure of family resemblance bleed into the intra-game structure of grammatical norms. But I also wonder whether there is a deeper problem: if open-textured categorization applies to the very idea of a language game, then perhaps Wittgenstein cannot consistently claim that individual games have non-negotiable norms without smuggling in the kind of essentialist boundary he elsewhere rejects. Is my error precisely in assuming that family resemblance operates inside the game rather than only across the family of games, or does the tension point to a real instability in Wittgenstein’s picture?
