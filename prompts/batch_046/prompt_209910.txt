Consider the following exam question and a student's answer. The student is being evaluated on their grasp of Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures, and on their ability to apply that taxonomy to a contemporary artificial system.

Question: Dennett distinguishes four kinds of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—each defined by a distinct evolutionary or cognitive mechanism. Define each category precisely, emphasizing the specific feature that distinguishes it from the others. Then apply the taxonomy to a contemporary large language model trained via self-supervised prediction across a broad text corpus and subsequently refined with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). Which category, or combination, best captures its architecture? In particular, address whether generating and comparing token sequences internally before emitting an output counts as the kind of internal pre-selection Dennett describes for one of these kinds.

Student's answer:
Darwinian creatures are those whose behavioral repertoire is fixed entirely by natural selection; they exhibit no learning within an individual lifetime. Skinnerian creatures add individual learning through operant conditioning: they explore a space of possible behaviors and are shaped by reinforcement and punishment, but they do not maintain internal representations of future outcomes. Popperian creatures are defined by their reliance on culturally transmitted symbolic tools—language, writing, mathematics—which they internalize and which restructure their cognitive architecture; they are essentially minds augmented by culture. Gregorian creatures, in contrast, possess an internal representational environment in which they can generate and evaluate potential actions before performing them—Dennett borrows Karl Popper's idea that they allow hypotheses to perish in place of the organism itself—thereby avoiding dangerous physical trial-and-error.

Turning to the language model: it is not Darwinian, since its parameters are updated during training. Its RLHF phase clearly involves Skinnerian reward-based shaping. Its pretraining on human-generated text means it absorbs the products of culturally evolved language, which aligns it with the Popperian profile of internalizing symbolic tools. At the same time, during inference it generates and compares token sequences internally before selecting one to emit, which fits the Gregorian criterion of internal pre-selection. Although it lacks biological survival stakes, its capacity for internal evaluation makes it best understood as Gregorian in its core architecture, with Skinnerian features in its fine-tuning and Popperian features in its linguistic substrate.
