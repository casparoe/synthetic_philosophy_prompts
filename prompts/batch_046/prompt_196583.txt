In a philosophy-of-religion seminar, a speaker summarized Molinism with the one-liner: “God already knows every free choice you’d make in every circumstance, without causing any of them.” He used it to argue that Molinism gives God exhaustive providential control while leaving libertarian freedom intact, and therefore that open-theist denials of middle knowledge are mistaken. I think the sentence is doing too much work at once. Can you lay out the precise theses it might be expressing—distinguishing, for instance, whether “every circumstance” ranges over all logically possible worlds or only over “feasible worlds” compatible with God’s creative decree; whether “knowing” requires justified true belief or merely accurate tracking; whether the knowledge is logically prior to creation, epistemically prior, or only counterfactually independent; and whether “without causing” excludes strong determination, weak determination, counterfactual dependence, or merely direct efficient causation? For each reading, say whether it is a near-platitude, a defensible Molinist commitment, or a false conflation, and indicate which combinations actually support the speaker’s anti-open-theist conclusion and which instead collapse Molinism into determinism or trivialize divine foreknowledge.
