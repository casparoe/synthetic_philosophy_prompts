I am writing an essay for my philosophy of logic seminar, and I would like you to help me think through the assignment before I draft it. Our course has moved beyond the familiar framing of logical omniscience—where an idealized agent is said to believe every logical consequence of her beliefs—to a more specific dispute about resource-bounded belief revision. In this framing, an actual agent updates her doxastic state on the basis of only a computable subset of the logical consequences of her prior commitments, not because she is irrational, but because her cognitive architecture imposes processing constraints. One camp holds that this boundedness is merely a practical limitation that an adequate normative logic of belief should correct for: logical omniscience remains the regulative ideal, and any formal system that builds in failure of closure is describing cognitive deficiency rather than rationality itself. The opposing camp argues that logical omniscience is a descriptive error rooted in an unrealistic model of belief: if belief is fundamentally a resource-limited cognitive state, then an adequate logic of belief ought to incorporate non-closure into its formal structure, treating bounded revision as constitutive rather than deviant. Write an essay that weighs these two positions carefully. Do not conclude with a non-committal balance—defend a specific verdict about whether logical omniscience should be retained as a normative ideal, eliminated as a descriptive mistake, or reconceived in some other way in light of bounded revision. Your conclusion should be justified by direct engagement with the strongest objection that could be raised against your view.
