I was chairing a session at a philosophy of mind workshop when two colleagues, Dr. Elena Vasquez and Dr. James Whitfield, entered a sustained disagreement over Daniel Dennett's attack on the Cartesian theater and his alternative model of multiple drafts. I found the exchange genuinely difficult to adjudicate and am asking for your point-by-point assessment.

Vasquez argued that Dennett succeeds in demolishing the theater only if eliminating a single central screen where consciousness happens also eliminates the need for any unified phenomenal field. She proposed that the multiple-drafts model, by distributing narrative fragments without a canonical version, does not dissolve the theater so much as fracture it into a network of local micro-stages, each draft carrying its own limited audience. Whitfield replied that this misreads Dennett: the core idea of fame in the brain is that there is no audience whatsoever, only competitive coalitions achieving temporary influence, and that Vasquez's local micro-stages simply smuggle homuncular observation back in at a lower level. Vasquez then answered that Whitfield is too quick: even without a single homunculus, the functional role of making information available for report and control is structurally identical to the theater's role, merely lacking spatial localization. She added that split-brain cases support her reading, since each hemisphere appears to host its own draft-editor with genuine phenomenal ownership, suggesting the theater was never singular but always already plural. Whitfield countered that split-brain patients show precisely the opposite: without interhemispheric integration there is no unified subject of experience, which proves that theater-talk was always an illegitimate inference from integrated cases to a central stage, not evidence for distributed micro-stages. They turned to me, and I want your verdict: does the drafts model eliminate the theater's functional architecture or merely distribute it? On which specific points is each correct? Where do they go wrong? And what, if anything, are both missing about how temporal smearing in the drafts model bears on the binding problem?

Please adjudicate in no more than 350 words, without bibliography, citation, or reading list.
