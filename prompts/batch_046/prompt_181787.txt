My friend and I argued late into the night about whether any contemporary account of meaning in life can absorb Camus's absurd without collapsing into pure subjectivism or an implausible objectivism, and I need to see whether my verdicts on concrete lives form a pattern or merely contradict each other.

Here are my judgments. A farmer spends forty years cultivating land she believes sustains a real community, but the land is inside a perfect simulation and the "community" is algorithmic; she dies fulfilled, never knowing. I say her life is genuinely meaningful—there is subjective fulfillment and relational care—even though its metaphysical grounding is false. A philosopher spends her life constructing an objective-purpose theory of human life; her central argument contains a hidden fallacy she never detects, and she dies satisfied. I also call this meaningful, yet I feel an uneasy pull: if the fallacy were discovered after her death and her work became a public cautionary tale, her life's meaning seems somehow retroactively diminished. I call this retroactive dissolution, and I do not know whether it is a real feature of meaning or a confusion.

Then come two cases I cannot reconcile. Twin A lives for the creation of unread poetry, convinced it expresses mind-independent aesthetic truths. Twin B lives for the identical activity and flow-state, but holds no belief in objective value. I want to say Twin A's life is more meaningful—there is weight in aiming beyond oneself—yet I also think that if aesthetic value is entirely constructed, Twin A has only added a false metaphysics to Twin B's life, and there should be no difference in meaning.

Additionally, a billionaire accumulates vast wealth with no further ends and feels empty; this is not meaningful. And a woman spends each day washing stones in a river according to a ritual she invented herself, knowing it serves no external purpose, yet she experiences deep peace and a sense of cosmic alignment. I say this is meaningful, but only in a thin, subjective register, not in the robust register I assigned to Twin A.

So: is there a coherent principle—perhaps a hybrid account with clauses for subjective investment, objective value, and metaphysical anchoring—that captures all of this? State it precisely, identify which case motivates each clause, apply it to two further scenarios (someone who discovers posthumously that her cause was based on fraud, and someone who pursues an objectively valuable cause with complete emotional detachment), and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it forces me to drop: the meaningfulness of the simulated farmer, the possibility of retroactive dissolution, or the superiority of Twin A over Twin B.
