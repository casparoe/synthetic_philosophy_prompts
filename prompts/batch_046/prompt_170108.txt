In the UK, an MP who breaks the three-line whip can be deselected by their local party, while recall rules only apply to criminal convictions, not disloyalty—so party discipline is really the only practical sanction. Yet we still talk as if that MP represents every voter in the constituancy, not just members. What conception of representation and moral agency does that arrangement presuppose? Does it treat party loyalty as collective responsibility that trumps individual judgement, and is that consistent with Simone Weil's argument that parties annihilate personal conscience? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say wether the practice strictly needs it or is philosophically underdetermined, and tell me what a parliamentary system that took Weil's critique seriously would have to look like.
