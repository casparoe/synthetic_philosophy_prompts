I've been trying to figure out why the deflationism versus correspondance debate about truth feels so stallled. On one side you've got people saying truth is basically just disquotation, there's no heavy metaphysical property hanging around, and the predicate 'true' only exists so we can make generalizations we couldn't make otherwise. On the other side people insist that true beliefs correspond to reality in a substantive way that actually needs explaining, and that deflationism can't tell us why some beliefs succeed and others fail because it refuses to treat truth as a real feature of the world. My working theory is that the disagreement isn't really about ontology at all but about what counts as an explanation in semantics - the correspondence people want a causal or representational story about how beliefs hook onto the world, while deflationists think semantic ascent already does all the explanatory work and any further 'property of truth' is just redundant metaphysics. But maybe I'm wrong and there's actually a hidden formal issue about whether disquotational schemas can be consistently axiomatized without circularity, or whether correspondence requires committing to an ontology of facts that deflationists can avoid. I want an honest diagnosis with reasons, not a survey: are they talking past each other about different things - one about language function, one about metaphysical grounding? Or is it a genuine substantive clash that just happens to be really hard?
