I buy a kind of patchwork language-of-thought view: not one universal mentalese but a bunch of modality-specific representational formats that get stiched together in working memory, so compositionality only shows up at the integration stage. I think this saves Fodor's productivity argument but it kills the classic systematicity claim in its original form, since there's no single shared syntax behind every concept combo. What'd actually count against it? Rank the defeaters for me—fatal to awkward—including whether neural decoding finds pure amodal symbols, whether stitching can yield real systematicity without shared syntax, and whether I've qualified this into unfalsafiability. Say which ones a defender of this patchwork view would concede, and whether being well-insulated is actually a problem for a view like this.
