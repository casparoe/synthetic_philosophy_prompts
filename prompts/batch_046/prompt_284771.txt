Provide your credence that Epicurus’s core argument—death is not bad for the one who dies because harm requires a present subject and death eliminates that subject—successfully undermines the deprivation account of death’s badness, under the conditions that personal identity does not survive bodily death and that there is no post-mortem experiential state. Decompose your reasoning into four components: (a) the probability that the “no-subject” condition is a necessary constraint on all genuine harm, not merely on experienced suffering; (b) the probability that deprivation goods, such as the loss of future projects or pleasures, can be coherently attributed to a bearer who no longer exists without violating that constraint; (c) the probability that the temporal asymmetry between pre-vital and post-mortem non-existence actually weakens the deprivationist reply rather than being neutralized by it; and (d) the probability that a counterfactual or modal account of harm—wherein the dead are harmed in virtue of what would have occurred in nearby possible worlds—can supply a concrete bearer of harm despite the absence of an actual experiencing subject. Assign a rough numerical value to each component, indicate how they combine into an overall figure, state which component carries the greatest uncertainty, and specify the particular philosophical distinction or argument that would most likely shift your overall credence upward or downward.
