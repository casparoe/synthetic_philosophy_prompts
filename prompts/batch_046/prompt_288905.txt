I have been noticing a particular institutional move that does not sit comfortably within the standard taxonomy of speech acts. Consider two concrete cases. First, a committee chair declares, “This session is adjourned for lack of quorum,” at a moment when attendance is genuinely ambiguous—say, two members have stepped out but have not formally withdrawn. The declaration does not merely report a pre-existing procedural fact; it establishes the authoritative interpretation of the attendance record that makes the quorum failure official, and in doing so it retroactively determines whether the votes taken minutes earlier stand. Second, an umpire calls a baseball game on account of darkness, but the relevant rule permits this only when visibility falls below a specified threshold that was genuinely borderline at the time. By issuing the call, the umpire does not simply apply an independent criterion; he constitutes the borderline case as having satisfied the criterion, and that constitution then validates the call itself.

In both instances the performative succeeds—the meeting ends, the game is over—yet the utterance operates on its own felicity conditions rather than on an independently settled state of affairs. This is not exactly a misfire, since there is no failure of convention or authority; the speaker is authorized and the convention is satisfied precisely through the utterance. Nor is it an ordinary constitutive rule, because the rule being invoked governs the very conditions of the act that invokes it. Is there an established concept—perhaps in work on institutional authority, declarative power, or procedural closure—that captures this kind of self-certifying or retroactively constitutive performative? If the literature offers only an approximate fit, I would like you to identify the nearest neighboring concepts, explain precisely how the phenomenon I have described differs from each, and indicate which fit is best. If no adequate term exists, please propose one, together with a definition and clear boundary cases that distinguish it from misfire, from abuse, and from standard constitutive declaration.
