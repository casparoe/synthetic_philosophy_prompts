Arendt's phrase the banality of evil describes the observation that Adolf Eichmann was neither a sadist nor an ideological fanatic, but an ordinary bureaucrat motivated by career advancement and a reliance on stock phrases. Consequently, Eichmann's evil was banal because it stemmed not from monstrous intent but from a psychological deficit in empathic imagination coupled with uncritical obedience to institutional hierarchy.
