I am a retired experimental physicist who, since leaving the laboratory three years ago, have taken up philosophy with the same obsessive attention I once reserved for error analysis. I have been invited to deliver the opening remarks at a workshop I co-organized, titled Norms and Naturalism, attended by working scientists—many of whom regard ethics as either subjective noise or a subject for theologians—and by philosophers who will catch any careless gloss on Quine. I want the remarks, approximately five minutes in length, to establish the ground rules for our discussions by doing real conceptual work: I want to present Quine's view that moral values are neither reducible to empirical observations nor dismissible as mere expressions of feeling, but rather occupy a place in our conceptual scheme analogous to theoretical entities—indispensable for coherence, yet situated nearer the periphery of the web of belief than physical law or logic, and therefore more susceptible to revision in light of experience, though never derivable from experience alone. To make this intelligible to my former colleagues without betraying the philosophers, I want to frame the idea through an analogy I have developed in retirement: moral values operate like renormalization constants in theoretical physics—they are not directly observable in any single measurement, they cannot be calculated from first principles in isolation, yet they are necessary adjustments that allow the broader framework of prediction and cooperation to remain consistent. The speech must therefore accomplish three things: reassure the scientists that we are not asking them to detect goodness with an instrument, warn the philosophers that I reject both naive moral realism and crude emotivism, and set a tone of disciplined humility about what naturalism can and cannot claim. It should be formal, polished, and entirely free of bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the argument must stand on its own conceptual legs, as a model without access to external texts could construct it from general philosophical and scientific literacy alone. Please write the remarks.
