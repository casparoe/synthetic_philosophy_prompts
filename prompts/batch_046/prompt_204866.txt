hey — im drafting a piece for a science-and-ideas column on whether things really persist through time, and i want to sanity-check my own view before i go on record. so heres the package: (1) im an endurantist — my laptop tomorrow is literally this same laptop, not a different time-slice of a four-D worm. (2) i think diachronic identity tracks functional and causal continuity, not spatiotemporal path or material overlap — so the ship of theseus stays the same ship if its role is preserved. (3) in brain-split or fission cases i think its genuinely indeterminate which branch is me; theres no hidden fact of the matter. (4) i reject temporal parts because they feel ontologically extravagant — "yesterdays me" shouldnt be a separate being. (5) i think change is intrinsic: my laptop genuinely acquires a crack, its not just a relation to different times. (6) im also convinced that in gradual part-replacement — like neurons swapped for silicon chips one by one — theres no sharp cutoff where i suddenly become someone else; survival is continuous all the way.

do these actually hang together? my gut says (6) is in trouble with (1), since endurantism seems to need a sharp yes/no about whether im wholly present at each stage, but (6) denies any sharp stage. and (2) might be feeding that problem, because functional continuity is gradable and vague, which doesnt sit well with the all-or-nothing presence (1) demands. then (3) leans into vagueness too — but endurantism cant have two of me in different rooms at once, so does (3) actually commit me to dropping (1)? on the flip side, (4) and (5) look like they clash — without parts, how can one thing have contradictory properties? — but maybe thats a fake tension that goes away once you see how temporary instantiation works? can you audit this for me: rank the real tensions by severity, say which ones only look bad, identify the weakest commitment, and sketch the least costly revision? dont need a survey of the literature, just trace what my own views commit me to.
