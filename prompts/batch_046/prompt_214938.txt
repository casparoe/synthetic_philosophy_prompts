I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a researcher defending a refined version of iterated amplification and distillation: she claims that decomposing complex reasoning into evaluable sub-tasks can preserve both alignment and competence, but only if the distillation target is not behavioral mimicry of the amplified model. Instead, she requires what she terms "counterfactual stability"—the distilled model must reproduce the amplified model's outputs not only on observed inputs but across a specified neighborhood of counterfactual perturbations to the reasoning chain. Write a sequence of questions that makes her reliance on this criterion progressively harder to defend. Begin by asking how she distinguishes genuine alignment preservation from evaluator overfitting that merely mimics stability on tested perturbations. Anticipate her reply—that the evaluator is simpler and can be independently verified—and then ask whether that simplicity solves misalignment or merely relocates it one level up in the recursive evaluation loop. Next, press whether counterfactual stability requires access to reasoning trajectories the distilled model never actually traverses, and if so, whether the competence being preserved belongs to the model or only to the evaluator's reconstruction of it. End with a direct question: what specific observable failure, either during training or at deployment, would convince her that decomposition under this modified distillation criterion still fails to preserve alignment, competence, or both?
