Hey, I need a consistency check on my consciousness views before I present them in my grad seminar next week. I've been going back and forth on access versus phenomenal consciousness and where global workspace theory actually lands, and I think I've stitched together a position that feels coherent in parts but probably collapses somewhere in the middle.

So here's my profile. I think Block's distinction between phenomenal consciousness and access consciousness is real and not just semantic - they're genuinely different kinds. I also buy the overflow-style arguments that phenomenal content can be richer than what gets accessed or reported at any given moment. I'm basically a global workspace theorist about access: access just is information being broadcast globally in something like the Baars-Dehaene architecture. But I also think there's this intermediate state I'm calling the pre-broadcast phenomenal buffer - sensory information sitting right at the gate of the workspace, not yet globally broadcast, but already carrying minimal phenomenal character, like there's something it is like to be in that buffer state. And I think this buffer is literally part of the workspace architecture, it's the input module that feeds broadcasting. So in a weird way I think GWT does cover phenomenal consciousness if we include these buffers. At the same time I think phenomenal consciousness is not functionally or computationally definable - there's a real explanatory gap that no broadcasting story can close. I also reject zombie conceivability arguments, though I think conceivability is usually a decent guide to possibility in other domains. So my commitments are: (1) phenomenal and access are genuinely distinct kinds; (2) phenomenal can overflow access capacity; (3) access equals global broadcasting; (4) there's a pre-broadcast phenomenal buffer inside the workspace gate; (5) phenomenal consciousness isn't functionally definable and GWT can't close the gap; (6) because the buffer is part of GWT, the theory actually accounts for phenomenal consciousness too; (7) zombie arguments fail despite conceivability being generally okay.

Can you audit this? I need to know which pairs are actually in tension, which ones just look bad but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity with the least costly fix for each. Also tell me what single revision would clean up the most mess if I had to pick one.
