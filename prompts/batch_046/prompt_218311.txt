Construct a concrete case of collective omission — drawn from institutional, environmental, or technological contexts — in which no individual failure to act makes a counterfactual difference to a harmful outcome, yet the group’s aggregate inaction is intuitively causally responsible. Design the case so that it isolates a precise conceptual tension: is the collective causal efficacy due to a non-linear threshold, to symmetric overdetermination among potential interveners, or to a distributed-agency structure that resists decomposition into individual omissions? Show why the scenario does not reduce to a simple summation of individually causally inert omissions, and indicate which theory of omission-causation — counterfactual dependence, interventionist, or normative — can accommodate the collective dimension without presupposing a contested account of group agency.
