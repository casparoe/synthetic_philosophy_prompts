Recently, my professional responsibilities have placed me in direct evaluation of a cognitive architecture that implements full global broadcasting—information is integrated, widely available to subsystems, and reportable—yet the design team insists this configuration is sufficient for phenomenal consciousness, while I remain unable to determine whether there is anything it is like to be the system. This has made the theoretical gap between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness feel urgently concrete rather than merely academic. Write a satirical piece in the form of a deadpan internal compliance memo—approximately 800 words—purportedly issued by the fictional “Office of Phenomenal Integration” at a technology firm, addressed to stakeholders auditing the firm’s newly deployed “Broadcast-Integrated Cognitive Architecture” (BICA). Adopt the dry, metric-obsessed voice of corporate technical auditing, using numbered evaluation criteria, risk-assessment tables, and references to proprietary protocols invented for the satire, such as the “Overflow Compliance Check” and the “No-Report Insurance Rider.” The target should be the theoretical move—common in global workspace theory and its popularizations—of treating phenomenal consciousness as nothing more than well-integrated, globally available information, and specifically the practice of resolving doubts about “what-it’s-likeness” by citing improved access metrics, broader broadcasting bandwidth, or more efficient report-generation modules. The satire should extend this logic deadpan: for instance, propose that if a subsystem fails to report a phenomenal state, the appropriate remedy is not to investigate whether an experience is missing, but to upgrade the reporting pipeline; or classify a “phenomenal zombie” as a system suffering from inadequate middleware integration, correctable with better broadcast architecture. Include an invented proponent—e.g., “Dr. Aris Thorne, Chief Integration Officer”—whose bureaucratic confidence embodies the view being ironized. The piece should be legible to a reader unfamiliar with the access-phenomenal distinction, but should reward the specialist who recognizes the exaggeration of real arguments—such as treating reportability as constitutive of consciousness, or recasting overflow concerns as engineering failures rather than philosophical ones—without ever winking or breaking the deadpan register. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific living scholars, and do not depend on any obscure empirical result; the humor must come from the internal logic of the memo itself. Maintain sustained irony so that a reader can always identify the object of ridicule—the conflation of broadcasting with feeling—without explicit acknowledgment that the document is parody.
