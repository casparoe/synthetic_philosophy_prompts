(1) If treating system X as having belief B yields better behavioral predictions than any physical-level description, then X genuinely has B.
(2) For an LLM, attributing beliefs like "water is H2O" predicts its answers to standard prompts far better than treating it as a stochastic parrot.
(3) But the same attribution fails completely under adversarial framing - ask it to roleplay a conspiracy theorist and it will deny water is H2O.
(4) So the predictive win is only local to benign contexts, not global across all possible inputs.
(5) Genuine belief is a stable modal property: if I believe p, I dont suddenly stop just because the conversational framing shifts.
(6) So LLMs lack real beliefs; they only have what I call "context-flashes" - momentary pseudo-beliefs tied to prompt conditions.
(7) Apply that standard back to humans: my belief that 2+2=4 can also be disrupted by hypnosis, extreme peer pressure, or weird priming experiments.
(8) So either humans also lack stable beliefs, or local framing-sensitivity doesnt actually block belief attribution.
(9) The first option is absurd, so the argument in (1)-(6) must fail somewhere. But I really want to keep (1) and (5).

Which step should I actually reject? Does giving up (4) save the intentional stance for AI, or do I have to bite the bullet and weaken (5)? Give me a committed answer - dont just list options, tell me what Im actually paying if I keep the premises I like.
