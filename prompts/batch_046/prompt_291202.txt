The thing is, I had this wierd experience two weeks ago during conscious sedation for a dental procedure: I was awake, heard everything, could respond, but the discomfort seemed to sit outside me, like I was reading a report about someone else's body rather than feeling it from the inside. Since then I've been stuck on illusionism — not the denial that consciousness exists, but the claim that phenomenal properties are kind of representational artifacts, mistakes our introspective machinery makes when it models its own states — and now I'm worried I'm the wrong person to open a conference about 'what it's like.'

Could you write me about four to five minutes of remarks? They need to welcome both camps without pretending there's consensus on whether phenomenal consciousness is real in the robust sense, and they shouldn't dismiss the clinicians' practical work on patient awareness. I'd like the sedation story to do structural work: use it to separate what we all agree on — that there are conscious processes, attention, reportability — from what's genuinely contested, namely whether there's an irreducible 'feel' over and above those processes. Please write in British English. Also, please separate what's settled in the field from what's genuinely contested, and don't present illusionism as the mainstream view, because it's definately not. If you take any liberties with the philosophical details or lean on a contested reading, flag that in a brief note afterwards.
