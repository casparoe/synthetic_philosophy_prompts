Consider a case of normative arbitrage between two agents whose value architectures are structurally incommensurable: Agent A operates within a lexical deontological framework in which certain rights-based constraints are genuinely non-tradable, while Agent B employs a continuous consequentialist calculus that treats all commitments as substitutable along a single metric of welfare. They reach a compromise in which A relaxes a non-lexical procedural safeguard—say, a requirement of full transparency in collective decision-making—in exchange for B accepting a hard side-constraint on an otherwise welfare-maximizing policy. Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises specifically when one tries to characterize this exchange as a Pareto improvement in value satisfaction. In particular, address whether the relaxation and the side-constraint can be jointly evaluated within any shared evaluative space without either collapsing A's lexical ordering into B's scalar framework, or rendering B's acceptance of the constraint irrational by B's own internal lights, and whether the very notion of "gain from trade" presupposes a commensurability that the compromise itself undermines.
