hey so im trying to write up my position for a seminar on historical collapse and im worried im talking out of both sides of my mouth. heres where i stand:

1. i think "collapse" is mostly a narrative frame we slap on after the fact—societies dont really "fail", they adapt, fragment, and reorganize. the bronze age and classic maya cases are perfect examples: political restructuring, not societal death.

2. but i also think easter island was genuinely different. it was ecological overshoot leading to massive population loss and real cultural discontinuity, so calling it a collapse isnt just framing—something actually broke.

3. im pretty anti-historicist—there are no universal laws of societal decline, popper was right about that. but i do think localized ecological carrying-capacity breaches can predict rapid welfare drops in specific contexts.

4. and i want to avoid normative language like "failure" or "decline" altogether, since that implies a teleology societies dont have.

do these hang together? specifically, is my easter island exception just special pleading against (1)? and does (3) smuggle in the kind of predictive law im rejecting in (1) and (2), or is local mechanism different enough? also, does (4) undercut (2)—if im avoiding "failure" talk, why am i ok with "collapse" for easter island but not for the maya? rank the tensions by how bad they are and tell me what the cheapest fix is for each. i dont want to just drop one randomly—i want to know which revision costs me the least of what i actually care about.
