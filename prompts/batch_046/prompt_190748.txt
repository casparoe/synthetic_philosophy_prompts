Please explain the discursive dilemma in collective judgment aggregation, focusing on a constructed case in which three members assess premises P, Q and R with unequal epistemic weights: A’s views on P and Q carry twice the weight of the others, B’s on Q and R carry standard weight, and C’s on P and R carry half weight. First, show how a weighted premise-based rule and a simple conclusion-based majority can yield contradictory collective verdicts despite each member being internally consistent. Second, identify the exact asymmetry that the weighting introduces, which does not arise under uniform majority voting. Third, discuss whether adopting a hierarchical tie-breaker—deferring to the member with the greatest aggregate weight whenever the two methods conflict—genuinely resolves the dilemma or merely disguises a different aggregation failure. Write in British English.
