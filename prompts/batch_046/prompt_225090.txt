I'm taking this casual philosophy of economics course on the side and suddenly I'm seeing my own life in it, which is annoying. Me and some friends started this repair cafe in our basement about eight months ago — people bring broken stuff like toasters or bike chains and whoever knows how fixes it, no money changes hands. We just had this chalkbord where you'd write your name and what you owed, so it stayed kinda casual.

Now one of the regulars wants to digitize everything into "fix-credits" you earn for repairing and spend when you need help. The part that bugs me is he wants them tradeable for actual cash with outsiders, and he proposed that if you hoard enough credits they should earn "interest" — like, bonus slots on the repair queue if you hold over fifty. He keeps saying it's just making the system more efficient, since the best repairers get rewarded and newcomers can buy in. But I feel like once credits can accumulate and be sold, we've stopped being a cafe and started being a bank with tools. I was arguing with him over beers and he said I'm just romantacizing poverty. Is there a real philosophical boundary between exchange that meets needs and exchange that feeds on itself? Or is the chalkbord just inefficient nostalgia?
