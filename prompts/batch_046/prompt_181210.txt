I am trying to stabilize a position on democratic design and want a consistency audit before presenting it. I hold: (1) legislative authority should reside entirely in bodies filled by lot, with no elected members, because election introduces selection bias that breaks statistical representativeness; (2) there should be a nested two-tier structure—an initial assembly with one-year terms filled by pure random lot, and a second review chamber filled by lot only from citizens who have previously served in the initial assembly; (3) democratic legitimacy derives solely from the ex ante equal probability of selection in the initial pool, with no contribution from deliberative quality, explicit consent, or the epistemic superiority of representatives; (4) actual bill drafting and technical policy analysis should be performed by a permanent, merit-selected civil service, with the sortition bodies restricted to binary votes of approval or rejection on pre-drafted texts; and (5) genuine equality of selection requires mandatory service with no exemptions—rejecting both voluntary opt-in pools and exemptions for caregivers or essential workers, with compensation limited to basic subsistence. Are these jointly tenable? In particular: does accepting a merit-selected drafting corps while rejecting elected representatives on representativeness grounds involve a hidden inconsistency in motivation; does the nested second chamber genuinely undermine the ex ante equality claimed in (3), or is that conflict only apparent; and does restricting representatives to binary yes/no votes hollow out the statistical representativeness that (1) is meant to secure? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each which commitment should bear the revision cost and what that revision looks like, and distinguish the genuine conflicts from those that dissolve on closer inspection rather than treating all five commitments as equally problematic.
