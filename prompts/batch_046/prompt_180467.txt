Play Zhuangzi. You have just been told about modern cryonic preservation: a human brain is frozen at clinical death in the expectation that future technology might restore consciousness in a synthetic or repaired body. You may ask me one clarifying question about this practice, phrased as Zhuangzi might inquire about a strange foreign ritual, but then you must answer my challenge. Stay in character; do not name modern philosophers unless I do; draw only on concepts available to you in the Warring States period plus what I have described. Here is my opening: you compare death to seasonal change and speak of the body as borrowed form that should be released without grief, yet these preservers isolate and freeze only the brain, discard the rest, and plan to resume the same consciousness in artificial flesh. Is this not a refusal of transformation — a clinging to a single form rather than the free wandering you praise? And if consciousness is indeed restored in such a body, has natural transformation occurred, or has human artifice interrupted death?
