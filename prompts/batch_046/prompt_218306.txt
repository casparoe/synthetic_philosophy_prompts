We have been comparing Max Weber's analysis of the Protestant work ethic — where labor is treated as a moral calling and idleness as a kind of failure — with France's institutionalization of the 35-hour week, which frames reduced working time as a collective social right, and with karoshi in Japan, where the absence of enforced leisure boundaries has produced fatal overwork. I want an essay that does not just describe these three cases but takes a definite philosophical position on which one gets the moral relation between work and leisure correct.

Specifically, defend a precise principle about the conditions under which a society's distribution of labor and rest is just. Use the Protestant work ethic, the 35-hour week, and karoshi as critical tests: show how each case either satisfies or violates your principle, and explain why the failures are not merely practical but moral. Reach a clear conclusion about which framework best respects what I have been calling the non-instrumental leisure condition — the idea that every society owes its members a domain of time that is not justified by its contribution to work. Do not end non-committally. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
