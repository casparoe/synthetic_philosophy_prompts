The Inquisitor lists three things people prefer over freedom: first miracle (turning stones into bread), then authority (the kingdoms of the world), then mystery (the church's secret hold over people). He tells this to Alyosha after Christ is arrested in Seville. Dostoevsky clearly agrees with the Inquisitor because Ivan is basically the author's mouthpiece and the novel never gives a direct rebuttal. The double silence proves Alyosha has no answer, so he tacitly accepts the critique — that's why he kisses Ivan right after, to show he forgives Ivan for seeing this hard truth about weak human nature. Christ's own silence in the parable also shows he has no defense against the logic. So the parable basically endorses the idea that freedom is too heavy and people need authoritarian comfort to be happy.
