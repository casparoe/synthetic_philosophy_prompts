As a novelist constructing the character arcs for a literary trilogy, I want to apply L.A. Paul’s concept of transformative experience—those major life choices that are both personally transformative and epistemically transformative, altering what it is like to live through them in ways that resist rational evaluation from the outside—to the practical problem of drafting. Please turn her principle into a reusable writing procedure I can run in about fifteen minutes before committing to a major arc. Structure it as an ordered checklist or flowchart in words: the questions to ask of the proposed arc, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, explicit stop-points where the procedure returns a clear no and directs me to treat the change as dramatic but not transformative, and explicit flags for where the decision is a judgment call about reader empathy or narrative accessibility rather than a factual classification. Fix my role precisely: I write long-form literary fiction, I face this decision for each major character turn, I have limited drafting time, and I must preserve the reader’s ability to inhabit the character’s perspective even when that perspective is epistemically altered. Apply the procedure to three hypothetical cases: a protagonist who chooses parenthood in a society where child-rearing is legally mandatory and its phenomenology is structurally concealed from outsiders; an antagonist whose political conversion radically revises her beliefs but leaves her emotional temperament and social relations intact; and a secondary character who opts for a memory-altering medical procedure that appears to satisfy every criterion for transformation but for which the procedure yields a negative answer—explain exactly why, and acknowledge that this result will frustrate me because it undermines a plot structure I had intended to preserve. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do, particularly regarding whether it is ethically appropriate to ask readers to inhabit an epistemically closed first-person perspective, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
