Here is a package of commitments I hold regarding justice, authority, and coercive power, broadly in line with Pascal's pairing of justice and force. (1) Justice has no natural or transcendent foundation; it is entirely conventional and ultimately rests on arbitrary custom. (2) Justice without coercive backing is powerless, while coercive power without justice is mere tyranny. (3) A legal system is legitimate only if its force is directed in service of justice rather than naked domination. (4) Because justice is purely conventional, any stable legal order that effectively prevents chaos is de facto just, regardless of its particular content. (5) Nevertheless, citizens have a moral duty to resist manifestly unjust laws, even when such resistance threatens social stability. Do these hang together? In particular, does commitment (4) collapse the normative distinction I try to preserve in (3), and does commitment (5) presuppose an objective standard of injustice that (1) denies? Is there an apparent tension between (2) and (5) that dissolves once we distinguish sustaining order from endorsing its justice? If the package is genuinely inconsistent, rank the tensions by severity, specify which commitment has to give, and say what that revision costs the rest of the view.
