I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of mind working on personal identity, and I've developed a package of views about Parfit-style teletransportation that turns on a distinction I think the standard literature neglects—call it the narrative-integration threshold: a psychological successor counts as a survivor only if it can embed the predecessor's autobiographical narrative without contradiction or fragmentation, regardless of the causal mechanism involved. Here is where I stand: (1) In standard teletransportation I survive because psychological continuity with appropriate causation is sufficient, and bodily continuity is irrelevant. (2) In full branching—my psychology duplicated with equal fidelity into two bodies—I should be rationally indifferent between death and branching, which shows that strict identity is not what matters; yet I want to say I survive in both branches in a plural but non-contradictory sense. (3) Survival is graded rather than all-or-nothing: in a partial-copy case, a successor retaining 85% of my psychological traits and full narrative coherence is more me than one retaining only 40%, who is closer to a different person. (4) I reject fusion—two streams merging into one—because neither predecessor's narrative can be fully integrated into the resulting single psychology without contradiction, so neither survives, despite the presence of psychological continuity relations. (5) For practical rationality, however, I must treat my future successors as uniquely me: I plan, assign probabilities, and bear responsibility as if there is one determinate survivor, even in branching cases.

Do these commitments hang together? I am especially concerned about the tension between (2) and (3)—plural, non-identitarian survival versus graded survival—and between (3) and (5), since graded plural survival seems to undermine the uniqueness required for rational planning. I also want to know whether (4) is genuinely supported by the narrative-integration idea or whether it smuggles in an arbitrary asymmetry between division and merger that my other commitments reject. Please audit the set: rank the tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give (including, if necessary, the narrative-integration threshold itself), flag any apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection, and indicate what the package looks like after the cheapest restoration of consistency—particularly if that requires abandoning a view I seem attached to.
