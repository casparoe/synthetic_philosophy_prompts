You serve as the senior philosophical ethics advisor to the Phenomenology and Consciousness Research Board at the Meridian Institute for Cognitive Studies. A multidisciplinary team has submitted Protocol 2024-D7, entitled The Dream Archive: Retrospective First-Person Reports as Ground-Truth for Phenomenal Consciousness Models. The proposal asks participants to record detailed narrative reports immediately upon waking, treating these accounts as direct experiential data about phenomenal content during sleep. The Board is divided. One faction, drawing on Malcolm's sceptical argument that there are no adequate behavioural or neurological criteria for attributing experience to a sleeping subject, contends that dream reports are not first-person data but post-hoc confabulations constructed at the moment of arousal; consequently, using them as ground-truth for consciousness modelling misrepresents both the phenomenon and the consent process. The opposing faction, informed by Dennett's cassette theory and his multiple-drafts account, maintains that even if reports are reconstructed upon waking—whether through replay of a stored cognitive trace or assembly within working memory—they still track a genuine underlying event, and therefore remain valid phenomenological data provided the protocol explicitly frames them as retrospective rather than claiming direct access to sleeping consciousness.

Please draft the Board's formal decision memo, addressed to the Principal Investigator and the Institute Director, recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions. The memo must present a clear actionable recommendation—including any required amendments to consent documentation or analytical protocols—within a concrete timeframe, such as a resubmission deadline or phased approval schedule. It should engage rigorously with the strongest Malcolm-style objection (that without criteria for experience during sleep we risk treating narrative fiction as empirical data) and with the strongest Dennett-style response (that retrospective reconstruction does not entail the non-existence of the reconstructed event). Think carefully, analyse the epistemic stakes for consciousness science step by step, and ensure your reasoning is precise enough that the research team can reply with substantive revisions rather than general reassurances. Write in polished British English, adopt a formal institutional voice, and do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or external reading lists.
