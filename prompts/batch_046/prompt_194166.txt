I keep thinking about that backwards induction thing in the centipede game — you know, where if both people are perfectly rational they should grab the pot on move one, but in real life people pass it back and forth like ten times? I was arguing with my friends about it and we all had different instincts, but we don't know the academic labels and honestly I don't care about them. What I'd really like is a sorting quiz or decision tree — maybe six or eight questions — that puts you into a camp based on concrete answers, not on whether you say you're "behavioral" or whatever.

The tree should split on stuff that actually matters: do you think the rational "grab immediately" result is correct in principle but just breaks for real people? Or do you think it's wrong in principle because treating the whole chain as one big decision is the mistake, and each move should be evaluated on its own? There's also that view where people aren't being irrational, they're just playing a social signaling game — passing is a message, not a move — and then there's the view that the paradox only hits if you have perfect common knowledge, which collapses the second you allow even a hair of doubt. I want each leaf named (even if it's unnamed) with one sentence on its weirdest commitment — for example, one camp has to say an AI that stops on move one is technically correct but socially broken. And please leave a couple of branches empty: combinations of answers that don't line up with any real position, with a quick note on whether the gap is because the view is incoherent or just unexplored. Keep the cases concrete — like "if there were only two moves, does your intuition flip?" or "if you could secretly see the other player's plan, should that change anything?" — and definatly no bibliographies or citation lists, since I couldn't check them without google anyway.
