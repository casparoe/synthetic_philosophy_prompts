Compare Ned Block's distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness with Bernard Baars's global workspace theory and its contemporary elaboration by Stanislas Dehaene. To make the comparison concrete, consider a hypothetical variant of global workspace theory that adds an "integration gate": global broadcasting across cognitive modules is necessary but not sufficient for phenomenal consciousness, which also requires recursive self-monitoring of the broadcast content. On this variant, a representation could be fully access-conscious—available for report, reasoning, and voluntary action—while lacking phenomenal character if it fails to trigger the gate. First, does this variant preserve Block's claim that phenomenal consciousness can exist without access consciousness, as in his super-blindsight scenario, or does the recursive self-monitoring requirement collapse phenomenal consciousness back into a form of access? Second, how does the modified theory treat the reverse dissociation—phenomenal consciousness with minimal access, as in brief, unattended peripheral experiences—better or worse than Block's original framework? Third, is the integration gate an ad hoc patch that saves global workspace theory from Block's critique, or does it identify a mechanistic distinction that Block's framework already implicitly requires? Conclude by assessing whether the modified global workspace theory offers a more unified account than Block's dualistic distinction, or whether it merely relabels the same separation under mechanistic vocabulary.
