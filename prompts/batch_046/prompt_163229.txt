I'm a retired physicist who only got into philosphy in the last few years, mainly through an online course that didn't quite scratch the itch, so now I'm running a little discussion group for other retired scientists who want to argue about time. I realise I've stitched together commitments that might not all cohere, but I can't see exactly where the crack is, so I want a consistency check before I present them.

Here is where I stand. I accept the Rietdijk-Putnam argument that special relativity kills any global "now": if determinism holds, then every event is equally real and the block universe follows. But I reject determinism — I think Bell's inequalities and the Kochen-Specker result show quantum mechanics is genuinely indeterministic, so that determinist premise is false. Even so, I still want to be an eternalist, because Lorentz invariance alone seems to rule out any privileged hypersurface of simultaneity, presentism included. At the same time, I can't accept that consciousness is just an illusion of flow; I think there's a physically real specious present of a few seconds tied to an observer's decoherance history. So globally I'm a block-universe person, but locally I want a real, flowing present picked out by decoherence — I call it "decoherent local presentism". I also think moral responsibility requires an ontologically open future, not just ignorance about what's coming, and I want moral facts to be objective, not frame-dependent. So my bundle is roughly: (a) eternalism is correct globally via Lorentz symmetry; (b) determinism is false for quantum reasons; (c) consciousness involves a real local flow fixed by decoherance; (d) moral agency requires an open local future; (e) moral objectivity holds across all frames.

My worry is that some of these pull in opposite directions. Does rejecting determinism actually damage the Rietdijk-Putnam conclusion, or is that tension only apparent because Lorentz symmetry does all the work? And if my open future is frame-relative, doesn't that make moral responsibility frame-relative too, which clashes with (e)? Or is there a hidden problem between holding a global block and insisting that local flow is physically real rather than merely perspectival? I'd like the tensions ranked by severity — which pair is genuinely incompatible, which just looks bad, and what's the least costly revision? I don't want a reading list or references I can't check; just treat it as an audit of my own position, tell me what I'd have to give up, and what it costs me.
