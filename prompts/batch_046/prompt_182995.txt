I'm trying to sort out my stance on procreation for a discussion group, and I want a consistency check before I open my mouth. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I buy Benatar's asymmetry pretty straight — there's a strong reason not to create suffering lives, but no matching reason to create happy ones, even really flourishing ones.

(2) I also think the non-identity problem kills most person-affecting objections to procreation: if the kid wouldn't exist otherwise with that life, there's no specific victim you've harmed.

(3) So I think Benatar's argument actually survives on impersonal grounds — it's not about harming someone, it's about the absence of benefit not being a loss.

(4) But then I also think that if you have solid reason to expect a child will genuinely flourish — rich autonomy, good relationships, not just "worth living" — there's at least a weak reason in favor, especially if parenting is part of your own conception of a good life.

(5) And I think adoption is always the better call if you want to parent, since it answers an existing need instead of manufacturing a new potental person.

My sense is that (1) and (4) are in real tension — Benatar says happiness doesn't generate reasons, but (4) seems to make it do exactly that, conditional on flourishing. Meanwhile (2) and (4) might also clash: if non-identity undermines specific-person evaluations of harm, doesn't it also undermine my appeal to "this specific child will flourish"? At the same time, (2) and (3) feel like they should conflict but maybe they don't? And I'm not sure how (5) hangs with (3): if there's no victim-based reason against procreation, is adoption actually "better" or just a different path? Could you rank these tensions by severity, say which ones are only apparent, and tell me the least costly single revision? Also please seperate what's settled in the literature from what's genuinely contested here — I don't want you presenting one side as if everyone's agreed.
