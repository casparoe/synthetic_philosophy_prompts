Rather than a lecture comparing the two, I want a runnable in-class simulation or structured role-play that makes participants feel the conceptual pressure point where the modern, psychologized version collapses into treating emotional management as a form of outcome-control. To keep the exercise from feeling like a standard classroom debate, please build it around a collective, technologically mediated scenario: the group must jointly maintain a shared digital archive that is continuously altered by an opaque recommendation algorithm and by anonymous external inputs, with each student assigned a specific role—such as curator, moderator, algorithm auditor, or external contributor—complete with rules and a non-monetary payoff scheme (e.g., reputation tokens or collective access rights). After running the simulation, I need a debrief that maps specific moments onto the ancient distinction between what is eph' hēmin and what is not, predicts what will go wrong on the first run—likely students conflating emotional regulation with causal influence over the archive—and is frank about which feature of ancient Stoicism, probably its rigorous monism about the good, the social dynamics of the game cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply. Please provide a full design: a timeline, role descriptions, decision rules, the incentive structure, the point at which participants should experience the conceptual collapse, and the structured debrief.
