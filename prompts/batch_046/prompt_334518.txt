I've been trying to formalize Émilie du Châtelet's account of happiness from her essay on the subject, and I think the underlying structure is an epistemic hedonism: well-being equals the net balance of rationally secured beliefs over illusory passions, weighted by the intensity of intellectual engagement. This looks like a hidden normative commitment that the calculus can't justify. So my question is: is this quantitative framing actually in her text, or have I projected a modern decision-theoretic grid onto a view that is really about the qualitative transformation of the self through disciplined study? And if the latter, does her account still allow us to compare lives across people, or does it collapse happiness into an incommensurable, first-person achievement? Give me a committed answer—no fence-sitting—about whether her view is reducible to an epistemic calculus and what that implies for comparing human flourishing.
