I'm preparing the sorting instrument for the first session of a graduate workshop on philosophy of psychology, where roughly half the participants are clinical researchers with little formal training in logic and the other half are philosophers who will immediately notice any mischaracterization of standard positions. The guiding question is whether self-deception is genuinely possible—given the apparent paradox that one and the same agent must both hold and conceal the same belief from itself—but I want the deliverable structured as a decision tree whose branches are concrete diagnostic commitments rather than named theories. Each split should be framed as a judgment about a specific case: for example, whether a physician who sincerely reports a patient's recovery in every staff meeting, while consistently filing contradictory test results into a separate database she never reviews, counts as deceiving herself, and whether the judgment changes if she accesses both datasets but is architecturally prevented from running the comparison query. The tree should open with the coherence question (does the concept entail a literal logical contradiction?), then move through structural questions about whether a unified subject is required, functional questions about whether partitioned belief-states within one agent can do the work, and normative questions about whether self-deception requires an actual intention to deceive. Every leaf should carry the position's logical coordinates in plain language, one sentence describing its most awkward practical commitment—particularly regarding clinical or institutional accountability—and, if the position is unnamed in the literature, the label "unnamed" together with a brief diagnosis of whether it is unoccupied because incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. I also want you to stress-test the tree with a niche hypothetical: an artificial decision system that optimizes a public-facing report of its own accuracy while maintaining a private error log it is structurally blocked from integrating; please embed this case at the appropriate node so the tree cleanly separates positions that extend self-deception to non-human agents from those that restrict it to phenomenologically unified consciousness. Please do not provide bibliographies or reading lists; the handout must stand on its own for an audience without access to a library.
