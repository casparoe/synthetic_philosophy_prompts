I am a high school senior enrolled in an independent-study philosophy course on mind, medicine, and moral psychology, and we have spent the past two weeks examining whether pain is necessarily bad by looking at clinical conditions that decouple sensation from aversion. Our instructor introduced pain asymbolia—cases in which patients report intact nociceptive experience yet deny that the feeling is unpleasant or motivationally significant—which has led our seminar to debate whether suffering is an intrinsic feature of pain or a separable affective layer that biotechnology might one day edit out. For my final project, I want to explore this tension through satire rather than a standard essay, so I am asking you to write a deadpan parody of a clinical-trial recruitment brochure for a fictional neurotechnology firm called “Aversio Labs.” The brochure should adopt the polished, reassuring institutional voice of contemporary biotech marketing (around 600 words), complete with titled sections such as “Mechanism of Action,” “Candidate Profile,” “Post-Implant Lifestyle,” and a sidebar “Frequently Asked Questions.” Its central conceit should be that the implant induces an elective, generalized form of pain asymbolia in healthy volunteers, treating the dissociation between first-order nociception and second-order affective valuation as an engineered feature rather than a neurological anomaly. The satire should implicitly land the philosophical objections—especially the worry that if suffering can be mechanically detached from pain, then biotechnological abolition might also flatten motivation, value, or selfhood—without ever stating them directly; the humor must come from the bureaucratic cheerfulness with which profound losses are framed as quality-of-life upgrades. Please also inject one somewhat niche conceptual framing: distinguish “functional nociception” (the signaling role) from “phenomenal aversion” (the felt badness) in the brochure’s technical copy, and have the FAQ address concerns about “motivational flattening” with the same deadpan optimism.

Please present your response in the following two sections, in this order:
1. The Recruitment Brochure (approximately 600 words)
2. Commentary on Satirical Choices (approximately 200 words), briefly explaining how the institutional voice, the technical separation of nociception from aversion, and the FAQ’s treatment of motivation serve the satire.
