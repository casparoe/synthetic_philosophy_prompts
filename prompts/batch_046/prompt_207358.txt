ok here's my rough philosophical profile on Aristotle and money, I need to know if it hangs together or if I'm just being incoherent. Commitments:

(1) Money is purely conventional — nomisma exists by nomos, not physis. The exhange-value of a shoe tracks real human needs but the coin itself is artificial.

(2) Chrematistike is genuinely unnatural not because greed is bad in some vague moral sense, but because it has no built-in ceiling; the oikos and politis need finite wealth, so unlimited accumulation violates the natural end.

(3) I think the argument that interest is the most hated gain is actually strong: coin is barren, it doesn't reproduce like crops or livestock, so charging tokos is like demanding rent on a field that bears no fruit. It's extraction without production.

(4) At the same time, I think if money is purely conventional, then its "barrenness" is also conventional. Modern banking or staking rewards just make the convention more complex; there's no deep metaphysical barrier to "fertile" money.

(5) So I apply Aristotle's critique to modern yield-farming and algorithmic lending pools, saying they're chrematistike run wild, but I don't want to ban interest outright — I want political caps on accumulation, not a metaphysical prohibition.

(6) The bridge is human nature: our needs are finite, so the natural limit on wealth is real even though the medium is artificial.

My worry is that (3) and (4) are in direct conflict — if sterility is just convention, then (3)'s normative bite disappears. Also, does (5) sit ok with (2), or am I smuggling in a modern liberal tolerance for markets that Aristotle wouldn't recognize? And is there a fake tension between (1) and (6) that goes away once you distinguish medium from end?

Please audit these: rank the real tensions by severity, say which commitment is the weakest link, and tell me the least costly fix. Don't just flag everything — tell me which pairs only look bad and which ones are genuinely pulling apart.
