Following my mother’s death, the religious language of my childhood has become personally urgent again, and Wittgenstein’s remarks on belief—once abstract—now feel unavoidable. I want a joint statement drafted as if between two camps in philosophy of religion who have long argued past each other: the “Framework Quietists,” who read Wittgenstein as holding that religious belief is a non-cognitive form of life whose grammar makes empirical refutation conceptually impossible, and the “Internal Realists,” who read him as allowing religious beliefs to carry substantive cognitive content that is answerable to reality through criteria internal to the practice but not reducible to it.

Please produce the document an adversarial collaboration between them should yield. First, a numbered section of claims both can sign about Wittgenstein’s position—specifically that he rejects treating religious belief as a scientific hypothesis, that belief is inseparable from practice and form of life, and that religious pictures function differently from empirical predictions—phrased so that neither camp takes them as conceding its central thesis. Second, a disagreements table giving each camp’s precise formulation of (i) whether religious belief requires what we might term “counterfactual resilience” (the commitment that no empirical discovery could overturn it) and (ii) whether a form of life can be rationally assessed from outside its own grammar; each formulation should be worded so the camp in question would accept it as its own view. Third, identify the cruxes—particularly (a) whether the grammar of religious assent permits internal contradiction, and (b) whether Wittgenstein’s talk of “depth” in religious pictures implies a standard of evaluation independent of mere internal coherence. Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete case both agree bears on it—such as how a believer should respond to an archaeological discovery that seems to contradict a founding narrative—together with each camp’s predicted interpretation of Wittgenstein’s likely response, recorded in advance.

Keep the wording neutral and non-vacuous, avoid bibliographies or citations, and inject conceptual specificity—especially around the distinction between the grammar of assent and the grammar of commitment—so that the result is not a generic summary but a genuinely discriminating map of the disagreement.
