I sit on the interdisciplinary review board for our university's Center for Behavioral and Decision Sciences, and we are evaluating a proposed two-phase protocol titled "Sequential Coherence Auditing and Preference Reconstruction." In Phase 1, participants allocate real but modest charitable donations across lottery bundles structured to reproduce the independence-violation pattern associated with the Allais problem. In Phase 2, any participant whose revealed preferences violate the independence axiom is automatically enrolled in a "normative calibration" module: the investigators construct a personalized Dutch Book argument drawn directly from that participant's own Phase 1 choices, demonstrate that the preference pattern is vulnerable to a sure-loss contract, and require the participant either to revise the preferences or to remain in a documented "non-coherent" category. The investigators further intend to share anonymized non-coherent profiles with an external policy partner for targeted behavioral-intervention campaigns, arguing that coherence arguments do not merely describe consistency conditions but establish categorical practical obligations—so that failure to maximize expected utility is not simply structurally inconsistent but rationally defective in a way that licenses downstream paternalistic correction.

The board is divided. One faction maintains that Dutch Book and coherence arguments possess genuine normative force: if an agent's preferences are money-pumpable, there is an all-things-considered reason to revise them, and documenting persistent non-coherence for applied policy purposes is ethically permissible science. The opposing faction contends that coherence conditions are at best structural constraints on rational preference ordering, not substantive directives about what one ought to choose; treating them as grounds for enrolling subjects—without explicit prior consent—in a normative-correction regime with external reporting consequences collapses the distinction between rational consistency and moral correctness, and violates respect for persons.
