My verdicts on gender clustering in egalitarian countries and I want the principle behind them, if there is one. In Sweden and Norway, occupational gender sorting is stronger than in less egalitarian societies; I read that as people expressing thier real preferances once coercion is lifted. The Soviet Union pushed women into medicine and engineering through planning, and the pattern collapsed after 1991, which to me shows state structure can manufacture clustering that looks like nature. There's this weird Iceland occurence where pay equality is near-perfect but job sorting is extreme—feels like Sweden, but I can't explain why it's different. Then there's the hypothetical case of a society with zero bias where clustering tracks exactly with prenatal hormone variance; I'd call that nature, but I also feel justice should correct for it, which makes no sense if nature is automatically benign. So is clustering ever unjust, or is it only unjust when caused by discrimination? And if the Nordic pattern is free choice, why do I still feel uneasy about it? Give me the principle that saves the most verdicts, what it says about cases I haven't mentioned, and which one of my takes I should just drop.
