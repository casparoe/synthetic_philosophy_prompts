I am a retired condensed-matter physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has begun working seriously through philosophy of science and metaphysics, particularly the literature on time and relativity. Next Thursday I am presenting to an interdisciplinary philosophy-of-physics reading group—mostly analytic metaphysicians and philosophers of science with some physics literacy, a few presentists who regard special relativity as a threat to be neutralized, two committed eternalists who treat the Rietdijk-Putnam argument as decisive, and several physicists in the audience who think talk of the reality of distant events is conceptually empty. My thesis is that the standard Rietdijk-Putnam argument for the block universe succeeds only by smuggling in what I call a global-frame assumption: it treats hyperplanes of simultaneity as carrying metaphysical weight for co-reality, even though the local dynamics of fields and causal influence propagate along null cones, not across those hyperplanes. From this I defend a position I have been calling localized eternalism—the view that all events within a given causal structure are equally real in the sense of being fixed by local determination relations, but that there is no globally unified all-at-once ontology licensed by physics alone, because the extrapolation from local determinacy to universal co-reality commits a category error about the scale at which physical determination operates. I have sketched the argument using only standard special-relativistic concepts—light cones, determination relations, and the conventionality of simultaneity—without invoking any technical quantum-gravity formalism, so the audience can engage it on conceptual grounds.

Please draft my Q&A preparation sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually face from this specific mix of listeners, ordered by how damaging they are likely to be, each phrased in the vocabulary an analytic metaphysician or a skeptical physicist would actually use. For each objection, provide a concise reply I can deliver in about two minutes, a note on where that reply is weakest, and an indication of what I should concede up front to preserve credibility with this room. Include the one question I am most hoping nobody raises—probably the one that targets the boundary between my localized eternalism and a merely disguised presentism. Do not include bibliographic recommendations, reading lists, or citation requests; I need arguments, not assignments. I was discussing this framing with a friend over dinner, and she warned me that physicists in the audience will ask whether my distinction between null-cone propagation and hyperplane co-reality is doing any work that conventionalism about simultaneity does not already do, so please treat that family of objections with particular care.
