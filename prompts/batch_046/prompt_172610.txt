Rather than giving the standard presentation, focus on a specific tension: the argument typically assumes that if a physical cause P is sufficient for effect E, then any mental cause M that supervenes on P is causally redundant. Explain why non-reductive physicalists often accept (ii) but reject (i), and why critics think accepting (ii) collapses the view into a form of reductionism or eliminates the distinctive causal work of the mental. Conclude by assessing whether framing mental causation as realised causation genuinely answers the exclusion worry, or whether it simply renames the problem.
