Defenders of David Lewis's modal realism frequently analogize concrete possible worlds to the pocket universes generated in eternal-inflation cosmology: both are causally isolated domains governed by consistent internal laws, and the argument runs that if physicists take a multiverse seriously, philosophers should not treat modal pluralism as ontologically extravagant. Audit this analogy in detail: lay out the correspondence table—what plays the role of a world, an accessibility relation, a counterpart, and the indexical property of being actual—then identify where the mapping breaks down, especially regarding spatiotemporal separation versus Lewis's lack of inter-world connections, the individuation of duplicate structures across cosmological regions, and whether physical multiverses possess anything like the counterfactual semantics required for modal talk. Does the analogy genuinely license the conclusion that modal realism is scientifically respectable, or is it carrying that conclusion through hidden disanalogies about ontology versus empirical physics? If the comparison collapses, suggest a better analogy for conveying Lewis's view to an audience that takes cosmology seriously but lacks philosophical training.
