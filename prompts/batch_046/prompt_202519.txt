Write an essay on the interpretive controversy surrounding Buddhist anatta and its bearing on personal identity. The central dispute concerns whether anatta is best read as metaphysical reductionism (the self is nothing over and above the five skandhas), hard eliminativism (the self is a pervasive illusion with no conventional validity), or deflationary conventionalism (the self is a pragmatically indispensable designation—prajnapti—without ultimate ontological weight). Examine how the two-truths framework (samvriti-satya versus paramartha-satya) shapes each reading, and consider whether treating the person as merely conventional can preserve the coherence of moral agency and karmic continuity without smuggling a hidden substrate back in. Weigh the principal objections: reductionists charge conventionalists with collapsing into nihilism about the subject of liberation; conventionalists reply that reductionists illicitly reify the aggregates into a pseudo-substance. Do not remain neutral—defend one interpretation as the most philosophically stable. Your conclusion should specify whether anatta is ultimately a claim about ontology, a therapeutic prescription to dissolve attachment, or a semantic thesis about first-person discourse, and justify why the other two readings fail to account for both the soteriological function of the doctrine and the need for conventional moral responsibility.
