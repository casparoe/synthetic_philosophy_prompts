ok so for our zhuangzi reading group i've been trying to pin down my view on death and whether it actually holds together. here's where i've landed:

1. when zhuangzi drums on the tub after his wife dies, he's not being callous—he's showing that grief is always based on a mistake about what death is, namely treating a natural transformation (hua) as an absolute loss.

2. he clearly denies there's any persisting self or soul; it's all just qi re-aranging, so there's literally no "me" that gets destroyed.

3. but then he also talks about the dead possibly experiencing being a horse's liver or an insect's leg, which sounds like he thinks some kind of subjectivity carries over into the transformed state.

4. he rejects confucian mourning rituals not because feeling is wrong, but because they lock people into a social behaviour that prevents the actual afffective shift he thinks is appropriate.

5. so overall i'd say his position is closer to an afffective reorientation—learning to feel differently—rather than stoic suppression of emotion.

do these hang together? i feel like 2 and 3 are pulling in different directions, and maybe 1 is too strong given 4? but i'm not sure which ones are genuinely in tension and which just look like it. can you go through the pairs, rank the tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest fix would be? i don't want to just ditch one randomly—i want to know which revision cleans up the most without losing the core idea.
