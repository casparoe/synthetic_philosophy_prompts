I've been trying to work out whether the Twin Earth thought experiment actually shows that the meanings of our words aren't in our heads, and I've landed on a compromise that feels unstable. I think Putnam's argument works cleanly for natural kind terms like "water" — if Twin Earth has XYZ in its lakes, then Oscar's word "water" refers to XYZ rather than H2O, despite Oscar and I being molecule-for-molecule identical internally. So far, so standard. But I think the argument breaks down, or needs serious qualification, when applied to social kinds — things like "marriage," "money," or "president." For these, the environment that fixes extension isn't just a causal chain back to some physical sample; it includes institutional structures partly constituted by collective intentions and shared representations. So if Oscar lives in a society with different marriage institutions, his term "marriage" might refer differently, but that difference is mediated through internal mental states (norms, intentions) rather than bypassing the head entirely. Natural kind terms seem fully externally anchored, but social kind terms are only partially so, with an irreducible internalist component that Twin Earth doesn't capture. That gives me a hybrid view: semantic externalism holds for nature, but not uniformly for the social world, and extending Putnam's reasoning across both domains is a category mistake. This also predicts that we should find asymmetries in how people correct errors about water versus errors about social roles — corrections about the former point outward to chemistry, corrections about the latter point partly inward to shared understanding.

What I want is an honest map of this position's exposure. What would actually count against it — not just disagreement, but which observations, arguments, or conceptual results would undermine the hybrid distinction? Could someone show that social kinds are causally anchored to physical tokens in exactly the same way as natural kinds, causing my distinction to collapse? Is there a rigorous version of the Twin Earth case applied specifically to a social kind that still forces full externalism, with no internalist residue? I'd also like to know whether defenders of full-blown externalism would themselves concede that social kinds pose a special problem, or whether they'd dismiss the internalist component as theoretically irrelevant. And if my view survives only by using "meaning" differently for natural and social terms, is that a fatal theoretical cost or merely a price worth paying? Please rank the potential defeaters: which ones would genuinely destroy the view, which are merely awkward adjustments, and whether there are any results in social ontology or philosophy of language that my hybrid account uniquely predicts but that turn out false. Finally, has this kind of qualified externalism been insulated against criticism so thoroughly that it risks being unfalsifiable — and if so, is that insulation itself a weakness for a view of this kind?
