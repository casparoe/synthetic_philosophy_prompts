I have recently come to hold the view — in the spirit of what Chase and Sanborn call "quining" qualia — that the posit of intrinsic, non-relational phenomenal properties is not merely unexplained but conceptually incoherent. My conviction turns on a structural constraint: for anything to count as a genuine property within our ontology, it must either be decomposable into parts or relations, or it must stand in causal or logical connections with other entities. Qualia, as traditionally characterised, are defined precisely by failing both conditions: they are allegedly simple, non-structural, and causally inert with respect to the physical world. This strikes me not as an unsolved mystery but as a reductio; we are asked to admit a category of entities that violates the minimal conditions for being entities at all. I am currently drafting a dissertation chapter defending this eliminativist line, and before I commit fully I want an honest audit of where the argument is exposed.

What, then, would genuinely count against this incoherence claim? I am not looking for the familiar restatements of the knowledge argument or inverted-spectrum intuitions, which I take to be compatible with interpreting phenomenal talk as a misleading description of functional or structural facts. Instead, I want to know what specific observations, formal results, or conceptual innovations would have to obtain for the charge of incoherence to collapse. For instance, if a rigorous theory of consciousness demonstrated that phenomenal properties are in fact fully decomposable into a globally integrated but locally analysable relational geometry — say, a non-classical information structure that correlates with first-person reports without being reducible to them — would that rescue qualia as coherent relational entities, or would it merely show that what we call "qualia" are never the intrinsic properties we imagined? Conversely, is there a live philosophical analogy — perhaps to self-relating properties of a subject, or to certain abstract objects — that permits genuinely simple properties to be epistemically accessible without being causally embedded in the physical order? I also want a candid assessment of whether the incoherence view has been insulated into unfalsifiability: if every phenomenological report is interpreted either as tracking an underlying structural state or as falling into conceptual confusion, does the view risk being true by definitional fiat, and is that a philosophical virtue or a vice? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which ones defenders of the incoherence view should themselves concede as genuinely damaging rather than dismissible, and offer an honest verdict on whether the position is well insulated or genuinely exposed. Write your answer in British English, avoid any bibliography or reading list, and conclude with a final line beginning exactly "Verdict:" followed by a one-sentence summary of your assessment.
