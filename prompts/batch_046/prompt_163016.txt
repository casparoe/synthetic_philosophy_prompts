I work as a senior ethics reviewer for a regional health-technology board that meets monthly to approve or reject population-level interventions—vaccination programmes, mental-health outreach, rehabilitation funding, and chronic-disease management—where benefits fall unevenly across socioeconomic groups. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in prioritarianism but explicitly designed to avoid the leveling-down objection, that my team (one junior analyst and myself) can run in about 90 minutes per proposal before I sign off. The procedure should be presented as a step-by-step flowchart in words: the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the procedure returns a clear reject or approve, and where it must halt and hand the decision to a values panel because the question is normative rather than factual. It must include a quick leveling-down check that distinguishes reducing welfare for the better-off without benefiting anyone from legitimate redistribution, and it should use a simplified, non-mathematical weighting rule—no complex calculus—so that someone without philosophical training can apply it consistently. Please include a small set of concrete test conditions (three or four sentences) that a reviewer can apply to classify a proposal's welfare distribution, with one worked example of each classification. Then apply the full procedure to three proposals: (1) funding a high-cost gene therapy that extends healthy life by ten years for affluent urban patients but is inaccessible to rural poor populations; (2) cutting a successful, high-quality physiotherapy service for moderately well-off stroke survivors to free resources for a marginal, uncertain gain for severely disabled patients in deprived areas—this is the case where I expect the procedure to return an answer I will dislike; and (3) a universal vaccination programme against a painful but non-fatal disease that benefits all groups but delivers the greatest absolute welfare gain to the already well-off because of baseline health differences. For each application, show the step where judgment is unavoidable and state explicitly what the procedure cannot settle—such as the precise exchange rate between a unit of welfare at the bottom and a unit at the middle, or whether non-welfare values like autonomy should override the prioritarian weighting. Write the answer in British English.
