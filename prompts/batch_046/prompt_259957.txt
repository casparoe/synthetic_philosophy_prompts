For a final exam in an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar (90 minutes, 25 marks), draft one essay question that requires students to apply Dennett's framework of real patterns and the three stances—physical, design, and intentional—to a novel constructed case rather than summarizing the view from memory. Use this scenario: the "Thermoswarm," a decentralized network of temperature sensors with no central processor in which each local node adjusts its sampling rate based on noisy local inputs, yielding globally stable thermal-gradient maps; engineers routinely describe the nodes as "representing" gradient vectors and "performing error correction," yet the dynamics are fully captured by coupled nonlinear differential equations. The question should direct students to (i) specify the distinct explanatory predictions or commitments of the physical, design, and intentional stances with respect to the swarm; (ii) argue whether the representational and error-correcting descriptions identify real patterns in Dennett's sense—patterns that support counterfactual predictions and are not merely heuristic overlays—or whether they collapse into instrumental convenience; and (iii) evaluate whether adopting the intentional stance toward individual nodes is ever justified, with explicit attention to how the presumption of rationality interacts with the design-level and physical-level accounts. Provide a model answer that executes all three moves with conceptual precision, and supply a marking scheme organized around four must-hit points with assigned weights (totaling 25), each accompanied by a one-line description of the typical error that should cost marks. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
