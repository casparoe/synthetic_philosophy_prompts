You serve as senior editor for a public digital encyclopedia of ancient and medieval history, personally signing off on submissions that blend scholarship with revisionist claims. You face about ten such submissions monthly, must render each decision within thirty minutes without calling in outside specialists, and must defend your call in plain language to a non-specialist oversight board. Your board is especially concerned with three recurring genres: narratives that read Plato's Atlantis as encoded Bronze Age history rather than allegory; claims that advanced artifacts such as the Antikythera mechanism require extraterrestrial transmission; and statistical new chronologies—in the manner of Fomenko—that collapse conventional timelines by rejecting radiocarbon and textual-critical methods.

Construct a reusable decision procedure—an ordered checklist with explicit stopping points—that translates the philosophy-of-science demarcation problem into an editorial workflow. For each step, specify what answer is sufficient, where the procedure issues a hard rejection, where it mandates publication with a mandatory disclaimer, and where it must defer to human judgment because the dispute concerns values or interpretation rather than shared fact. Build in an explicit distinction between submissions that suffer only evidential underdetermination (they play by shared rules but the data are ambiguous) and those that invoke methodological incommensurability (they reject the evidentiary rules themselves). The procedure must run solely on reasoning visible in the submission; do not request citations, bibliographies, or expert reading lists.

Then apply the procedure to three submissions: (A) an article arguing that Plato's Atlantis encodes a real Minoan collapse through symbolic geography and should therefore be treated as allegorical history; (B) an article using statistical pattern-matching across royal chronicle texts to merge the Roman Empire with early medieval Russia, explicitly treating physical dating methods as circular; (C) an article attributing the Antikythera mechanism's gear ratios to non-human knowledge, without engaging Hellenistic astronomical tradition. Include one case where the procedure produces a result your board will dislike. Finally, identify the procedure's blind spots and the submission types most likely to cause misfire.

Open with a one-sentence bottom line stating the procedure's core rule. Keep the entire response to a maximum of 650 words.
