Please take on the role of a senior philosopher of ethics with expertise in comparative moral philosophy and theories of desert. Before you commit to an answer, spend a moment brainstorming: list the core conceptual nodes—diachronic agency, transpersonal moral accounting, secularization without collapse into consequentialism, moral luck, and institutional design—that any defensible research program in this area must navigate.

Here is the framing. Treat karma not primarily as a religious cosmology but as a diachronic, agent-transcendent theory of moral desert: the claim that the moral quality of an agent’s actions generates outcomes for that agent, or for a causally linked psychological successor, across time, largely independent of institutional enforcement. Given that framing, which open questions or lines of philosophical work at the intersection of karma and modern theories of moral desert matter most for the field over the next several years? I am asking for a defended prioritization—say, for a research group with about three years and a background in normative ethics rather than metaphysics or physics—not a survey.

Specifically, I want you to do the following. First, identify three or four genuinely unresolved conceptual problems. For instance: whether diachronic desert requires strict personal identity over time or can survive on a thinner causal-psychological chain; whether karma’s apparent determinism can be reconciled with modern commitments to moral autonomy and agency; whether secularized versions of karma can avoid collapsing into pure consequentialism or standard retributivism; and whether transpersonal moral accounting—where desert is pooled across generations or social roles—can be made consistent with liberal individualism. Fourth, flag at least one apparent tension—perhaps between karma’s long temporal reach and modern intuitions about proportionality—that dissolves on closer inspection, so that I do not mistake a superficial contradiction for a real research obstacle.

Please avoid bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or appeals to texts I should consult. I want reasoning about where limited intellectual effort should go, not a catalog of sources. Think carefully, trace the dependency relations between these problems precisely, and defend your ordering with the criteria you have named.
