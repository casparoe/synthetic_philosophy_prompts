As a software engineer without formal philosophical training, I recently worked through Hofstadter's presentation of the MU-puzzle and his idea of JOOTSing—jumping out of a formal system to reason about it rather than within it—because the analogy to program verification felt immediate. In a discussion group with some philosophy-reading friends, a debate about whether JOOTSing is genuinely possible has stalled completely. One side argues that every apparent escape is just nesting: finding that MU is unreachable requires discovering an invariant (the number of I's is never divisible by three), which is comparable to writing a loop invariant in code verification, and that invariant lives inside a larger formal system with richer rules—just as a compiler targets a meta-language rather than transcending computation. The other side insists that recognizing that invariant involves an irreducible shift in standpoint: a human grasps why the derivation is impossible, whereas an algorithm confined to the MIU rules can only generate strings forever without ever knowing its search is futile, and this is not merely syntax but a real cognitive transcendence.

We have circled the same examples—the four transformation rules, Gödel numbering, the record-player analogy—for months without movement. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it verbal, with one side using "system" to mean syntactic rules and the other using it to mean cognitive architecture? Is it substantive but hidden, perhaps turning on whether "understanding" requires subjective experience or can be fully captured by formal meta-theory? Or is it a bedrock clash of intuitions about human exceptionalism that no further argument can resolve? Please diagnose it using what each side actually claims, not by surveying interpretations. I am particularly interested in whether modern practice in formal verification—where dependently typed proof assistants employ reflective tactics and meta-programming to reason about their own kernels—clarifies the distinction or collapses it, showing that every escape hatch is just another layer of formalism. Given your diagnosis, what conceptual distinction—perhaps between object-level derivation and tactic-level reflection, or between interpretation and compilation—would count as genuine progress, or does the persistence of the stalemate suggest the question is ill-posed?
