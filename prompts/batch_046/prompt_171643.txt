My friends Marisol and Jake got into it over coffe about whether Kelly betting logic applies to beliefs, not just money. (1) Marisol said if geometric rationality is real, then updating your credence should work like a Kelly bet: you allocate confidence proportional to the log-likelihood of the evidence, so over time your accuracy grows geometically instead of linearly. She said standard Bayes is basically just Kelly in disguise, since multiplying priors by likelihoods is the same as maximizing expected log-posterior. (2) Jake said that's mixing up instruments and epistemics — beliefs don't have a bankroll, and "maximizing geometric accuracy" isn't a real norm unless you already assume some weird utility function over being right. He also said if you take Kelly literally for credences, you'd have to put nonzero weight on every wild conspiracy just to hedge, which is absurd. (3) Marisol replied that you don't hedge across all hypotheses, just within your current model space, and that Bayes already does the multiplicative update, so Jake's "category error" is just resistance to seeing the math. (4) Jake came back that the math being similar doesn't mean the justification transfers — in finance you care about growth because ruin is permanent, but in belief formation being slightly wrong about one thing doesn't wipe you out, so the ruin-avoidance argument that makes Kelly attractive doesn't apply.
