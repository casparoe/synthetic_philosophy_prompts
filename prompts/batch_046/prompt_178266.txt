A friend tried to walk me into this and I can't find the exit. (1) The standard sorites tolerance principle: if n grains of sand make a heap, so do n-1. (2) Apply the same logic to collective belief — if 10,000 people in a group knowing X makes "the group knows X" true, then by tolerance 9,999 should do it too. (3) Keep going down to a single person, so "the group knows X" is true when only one individual does. (4) That's clearly wrong; collective knowledge isn't just summed belief. (5) So the tolerance principle is false in general, not just for heaps. (6) But if we reject it only for social cases and keep it for sand, we're being arbitary; if we reject it everywhere we lose the usual diagnosis of vagueness. He claims this shows the paradox realy infects social epistemology and that vagueness can't be quarantined to physical predicates. I want to keep that collective knowledge is genuinely different from summed individual belief, but I feel like something slipped between (2) and (3). Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
