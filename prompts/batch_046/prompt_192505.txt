Rudolf Carnap died in 1970, long before the emergence of real-time algorithmic governance. Suppose a contemporary city government proposes to abolish its traditional annual budget process and replace it with a continuously operating digital framework: citizens register preferences through a standardized interface, an open-source optimization model reallocates municipal resources in real time, and every normative weighting in the system is displayed as an explicit, revisable parameter rather than hidden in bureaucratic language. Given Carnap's well-known commitments to conceptual engineering, the separation of empirical questions from value choices made transparent within a chosen linguistic framework, and his sympathy for non-coercive rational planning, how would he likely evaluate such a scheme? Would he regard it as a legitimate political application of logical reconstruction—making value commitments explicit and revisable—or as an illegitimate conflation of technical efficiency with democratic legitimacy?
