Design a thought experiment that separates causal inefficacy from deliberative inefficacy in an individual contribution to collective climate harm. The case must meet these constraints: the agent’s action is deterministically decisive for a specific, concrete harm—not a statistical expectation—if and only if exactly k other agents act identically, where k is small, fixed, and known to the agent; yet the agent lacks any mechanism to identify those k others or coordinate with them. Use an everyday consumer decision rather than exotic technology, and restrict the agent’s reasoning to information available without special investigation. Once the scenario is presented, show: (1) precisely why the standard causal-inefficacy objection collapses; (2) which form of inefficacy—deliberative, epistemic, or structural—survives and on what grounds it does not reduce to causal redundancy; (3) the single structural feature whose removal turns the case back into the familiar probabilistic climate-action problem; and (4) the line of attack available to someone who holds that individual action remains morally required despite the remaining objection. Keep the narrative to one paragraph, avoid perfect predictors, demons, and statistical aggregation across populations, and do not include citations, references, or bibliographic requests.
