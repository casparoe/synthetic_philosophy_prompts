Please draft the formal decision memo I will circulate before our International Partnerships and Academic Freedom Committee meets in March. I chair the committee, and we must recommend whether to maintain, suspend, or conditionally renew our faculty-exchange agreement with a major public university in Hungary, with a concrete implementation timeline. The context is well known: since 2010 the governing party has rewritten the fundamental law, packed the constitutional court, captured media and NGO regulation, and compelled one internationally accredited university to relocate; our partner remains formally accredited, yet several faculty have been dismissed after criticizing government policy, and ministerial approval now governs curriculum in two disciplines. The board is divided. One side holds that "democratic backsliding" is a selectively applied partisan label, notes that competitive elections continue, and warns that severing ties punishes scholars for state actions they oppose. The other side holds that continuing normal relations legitimizes authoritarian consolidation and betrays our institutional commitment to academic freedom. Your memo should recommend one course—maintain, suspend, or conditional renewal with explicit benchmarks—and address the strongest consideration on the losing side with intellectual honesty rather than caricature. Do not treat backsliding as self-evident. Instead, engage the philosophical architecture: what counts as backsliding (procedural erosion, normative de-legitimation, or loss of democratic identity over time), who possesses authoritative standing to declare it (international indices, domestic courts, affected scholars, or this committee), and whether invoking the concept here collapses a contested normative baseline into a geopolitical slogan. Write so that non-philosophers on the board can follow the reasoning, but make the conceptual stakes explicit.
