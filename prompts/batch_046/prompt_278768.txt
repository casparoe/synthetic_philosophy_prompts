I am writing a short essay for my metaphysics seminar on artifact ontology, and I found a summary in an unsigned handout—apparently drawn from a popular philosophy blog—of the classic statue-and-clay problem. The summary presents the constitution-without-identity position roughly as follows: the statue and the lump of clay are two numerically distinct objects that occupy exactly the same region of space and share every microscopic part during their overlap, yet they are not identical because they have different persistence conditions and different modal profiles—the clay can survive being melted and reshaped, while the statue cannot. So far, so standard. But then the summary claims that this modal-difference strategy is self-defeating when applied diachronically: if the statue loses a finger on Monday and is restored on Tuesday, then the Monday-statue and the Tuesday-statue share almost all parts but differ in one, and if modal or part-based differences are enough to generate distinct objects, we should conclude there are two statues, not one statue that persisted. The author of the summary takes this to show that constitution theorists, if consistent, must treat every artifact as a four-dimensional temporal worm composed of statue-stages, which contradicts their usual commitment to three-dimensional endurance. The summary concludes that the only consistent way to keep the statue-clay distinction is to abandon modal logic altogether and accept that identity is purely qualitative, which the author dismisses as implausible.

Before I build an argument around this, I want to know whether the summary is giving me a fair target. Which parts correctly report the constitution view's use of modal or historical properties to block identity? Where does it conflate the cross-categorial relation between an artifact and its matter with the intra-categorial persistence conditions of a single object over time? And is the leap to perdurantism or to abandoning modal logic a genuine consequence, or does it ignore standard replies—perhaps involving dominant sortals, temporary intrinsics, or the idea that persistence conditions are fixed by kind-membership rather than by part-comparison across times? I do not need citations or reading lists; I am asking for a conceptual audit: which objections are live and which are straw men built on a hidden equivocation?
