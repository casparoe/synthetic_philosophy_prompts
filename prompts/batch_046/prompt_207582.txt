I'm giving the opening remarks at the retirement occassion for Lena Voss — thirty years in the phil dept, and about half the room are philosophers who argued with her for decades about tranquilism, specifically this subtractive teaching idea she had: the only point of education is to remove false cravings and status-anxiety, not to build knowledge or virtue. The other half are family and staff who just know she was "into negative ethics" and don't want a lecture. I need about four minutes for maybe fourty people. The speech needs to do real structural work with her view — it should actually constrain what I can say about her legacy, about comfort, about what we owe her — not just decorate the talk. Please don't fall back on saying she lives on in our students or watches over us; she'd hate that false comfort and I want to honor it without leaving the non-philosophers cold. And no bibliography or reading list at the end, just the speech itself. The tone should be warm but not sentimental.
