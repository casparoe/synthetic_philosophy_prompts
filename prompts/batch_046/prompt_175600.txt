I'm making a handout for a small philosophy-of-AI blog about whether deep nets actually have representations or just weights that act like them. Write a taxonomy tree that sorts positions by concrete commitments, not by the labels people like. First branch: does representation require an internal consumer mechanism that uses the state for guidance? Second: must that consumer be causally decoupled from the producer like a language-of-thought symbol, or can it be fully distributed? Third: is content fixed by counterfactual invariance under surgical intervetion, or just by correlation with the training distribution? At each leaf name the position if it has one (strong mechanistic realism, distributed functionalism, anti-representational mechanism-only, instrumentalist interpretationism) or mark it unnamed, then give its most awkward commitment in one sentence. Flag any pair of named leaves your tree can't separate, and point out one answer-combination that looks incoherent versus merely unexplored. Keep it casual — this is for a reading group, not a journal — and don't ask for citations or a bibliography.
