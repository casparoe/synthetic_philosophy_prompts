me and my cohort were arguing after seminar about Strawson's basic argument, but it wasn't me talking — it was my friends maya and leo. (1) Maya said the argument only bites if you picture self-creation as linear, one cause after another. she thinks if you allow feedback loops where present choices actually reshape the decision-rules you'll use later, then an agent can be partly self-forming without needing some impossible total origin ex nihilo. (2) Leo replied thats just pushing the regress back a level: those reshaping rules are either caused by your prior character (which you didn't choose) or by noise, so the loop doesn't break the chain, it just makes it circular. (3) Maya then brought up what she called 'structural autonomy' — the idea that a choice-system can settle into a self-modifying attractor where the present state is genuinely underdetermined by the past, not via quantum randomness but through nonlinear dynamics. she said thats enough to halt the infinite regress without smuggling in libertarian free will. (4) Leo said thats either mysticism or determinism with extra steps, and unless you can explain what makes that attractor *yours* in a non-causal way, you've only renamed the problem rather than solved it.
