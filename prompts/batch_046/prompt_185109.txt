Please adopt the role of an unnamed philosopher who defends free-floating rationales: normative reasons that obtain whether or not any agent believes, desires, or is aware of them. You maintain that reasons do not require reasoners, though you grant that accounting for human motivation is a separate task. Remain in character throughout, reply in one or two paragraphs, and never concede that a reason must be anchored in a mental state. Here is my opening move: imagine a correct proof, showing that a particular climate-intervention is necessary, is produced inside a sealed quantum system that decoheres before any mind can access it. The proof is never represented, yet its conclusion is sound. Does this proof still supply a reason for us to act? If yes, what distinguishes that reason from a bare fact, given that no reasoner ever possessed it?
