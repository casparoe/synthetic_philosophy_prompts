I have been studying Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's defence of women's learning in her letter to Sor Filotea, and I am struck by what seems an irreconcilable tension. On one side, she appeals to natural reason and to biblical examples of learned women, which sounds like a universal argument about intellectual capacity; on the other, she presents her own study as an act of religious obedience within the convent. If the justification is natural capacity alone, then vows that restrict study are illegitimate; if it is religious obedience alone, then the argument collapses for any woman not in a convent. Must we therefore conclude that her defence was not a coherent philosophical position but a strategic justification for her own exceptional circumstances? And if the argument is indeed self-undermining in this way, does her work retain any significance for secular debates about women's education? Please respond in British English.
