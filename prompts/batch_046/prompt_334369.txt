Write an essay on whether the bootstrapping problem actually kills reliabilist accounts of basic knowledge in the so-called easy-knowledge cases—where you seem to learn your eyes work by using your eyes. Weigh the circularity worry raised in that literature against the standard reply that no vicious circle occures, but don't sit on the fence: defend a real verdict. Then consider this twist: a child learns to trust mirror-images for recognizing her own face not by cross-checking with touch, but by noticing the "other kid" copies her exactly. Does this mirror-case avoid bootstrapping, or just relocate it? Make sure your conclusion handles both the standard debate and this mirror scenario, and don't bother with a bibliography—just argue.
