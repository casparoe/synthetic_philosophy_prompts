Write the full list of desiderata that any adequate theory resolving Sen's liberal paradox must satisfy, treating adequacy as conceptual defensibility rather than mere formal consistency. Begin with the triad at the heart of the paradox: minimal liberalism (each individual has decisive authority over at least one pair of alternatives in a personal domain, illustrated by the standard two-agent, two-state case in which both agents strictly prefer that the other choose the option associated with the prurient material yet each claims sovereignty over his own choice), the Pareto principle (if every individual strictly prefers state x to state y, then society must rank x above y), and unrestricted domain (the rule must yield a social ordering for every logically possible profile of individual preferences). For each, state the precise requirement, the motivating case or theorem, and whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp—especially note whether unrestricted domain is genuinely essential or only a methodological convenience inherited from Arrow's framework.

Then specify the four less-obvious but still mandatory requirements. First, a criterion for domain assignment: the theory must supply a principled, non-circular account of which choice-pairs count as personal and therefore fall under liberal protection, one that does not collapse into a comprehensive conception of the good or allow post hoc gerrymandering of domains to avoid conflict; explain why this is a desideratum and not merely an implementation detail. Second, consistency with Arrow's impossibility theorem: the theory must clarify whether it escapes Arrow's result by restricting the domain of admissible preferences, weakening the liberal condition, or relaxing Pareto efficiency, and it must state what normative cost each escape route exacts. Third, interdomain rights aggregation: if an agent holds protected liberal domains in both, say, dietary choice and expressive conduct, the theory must determine whether rights in one domain can be weighted, traded, or overridden by collective welfare considerations in another without violating minimal liberalism—this is the analog of inter-domain preference aggregation, and you should flag whether any answer to it reintroduces the paradox at a higher level. Fourth, counterfactual preference governance: address the analog of counterfactual mugging in social choice—if an agent's revealed preference within her protected domain conflicts with the preference she would have held had the domain boundaries been assigned differently, which preference governs the social outcome, and does allowing counterfactual override destroy the very idea of a protected sphere?

Next, identify any subset of these desiderata that you believe is jointly unsatisfiable given known impossibility results, and say which single desideratum, if dropped, does the most damage to the intuitive appeal of minimal liberalism.

Finally, briefly score the three main contending strategies against this list: (a) Sen's own move of restricting minimal liberalism to self-regarding domains defined by a substantive criterion; (b) weakening minimal liberalism to a weighted or probabilistic form that allows collective preference to break ties within protected domains; and (c) abandoning Pareto in favor of a lexicographic rule that gives liberal rights absolute priority over collective welfare. For each, indicate which desiderata it satisfies and which it fails, with particular attention to the interdomain and counterfactual requirements.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on historical exegesis; do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
