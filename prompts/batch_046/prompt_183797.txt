As a retired physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy in a systematic way, I am asking you to take a definite, committed position—giving explicit reasons rather than surveying both sides—on the comparative strength of the two main cosmological arguments. Specifically, argue that the kalam version is fundamentally weaker than the contingency version, but only on the condition that the latter is reconstructed away from object-oriented explanations and toward what I shall call state-space synchronic grounding. Defend the thesis that the kalam argument erroneously applies a principle of efficient causation—appropriate to intra-cosmic events possessing well-defined temporal parts—to the boundary condition of space-time itself, where the notion of a prior cause becomes category-mistaken, much as asking what lies north of the North Pole misconstrues the geometry of the pole. By contrast, argue that a properly formulated contingency argument should ask not what caused the universe to begin in time, but why the present total state-space of physical law—including its vacuum structure and decoherence conditions—obtains rather than a null state. In developing this view, commit firmly: do not retreat into agnosticism or present both arguments as equally viable. Instead, explain precisely why treating cosmic origin as an event within time conflicts with a four-dimensionalist and quantum-field-theoretic understanding in which temporal parts are not ontologically prior to the manifold they inhabit, and show that this failure is metaphysical rather than merely scientific. Your response should be decisive and self-critical: state your conclusion with confidence, identify exactly where your reasoning depends on contested premises—such as the coherence of synchronic grounding across modal spaces or the interpretation of vacuum fluctuations—but do not allow those dependencies to dissolve into neutrality about the comparative assessment.
