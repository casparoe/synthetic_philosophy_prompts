Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration would produce between two opposing camps on the lottery paradox and the preface paradox. Camp One — call them the Unified Aggregationists — holds that both paradoxes reveal a single error: applying a uniform closure principle across belief-domains that require different aggregation norms, and they defend a domain-relative belief-policy in which empirical conjunction and self-referential humility are governed by separate coherence constraints. Camp Two — call them the Divergent Diagnosticians — holds that the paradoxes have structurally different sources (probabilistic dilution over large sets versus self-reference under belief revision) and that any unified treatment obscures the distinct fixes required: a statistical-correction rule for large-set empirical credence in the lottery case, and a humility-operator restricted to reflective self-assessment in the preface case.

The deliverable should include: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, covering shared formal descriptions of both paradoxes and shared rejections of at least one inadequate common resolution (for instance, a bare rejection of the Lockean thesis that offers no replacement machinery); (2) a disagreements table with two columns, each phrased so its own camp accepts the wording, pinpointing exactly where the domain-relative policy and the divergent fixes disagree on closure conditions, on the explanatory role of self-reference, and on the synthetic scientist-case (a researcher who assigns 0.99 credence to each of one hundred independent empirical hypotheses while also assigning high credence to the claim that at least one is false, based on her track record of error) — specifically, whether this case demands one mechanism or two; (3) a cruxes section listing at least three precise sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp, with each side recording in advance what finding or argument it would treat as disconfirming; and (4) a brief concluding assessment of whether any part of the disagreement is verbal rather than substantive.

The statement should be precise enough that a philosopher familiar with the literature recognizes the positions rather than seeing generic placeholders. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
