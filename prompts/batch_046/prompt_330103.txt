The debate in Nyāya scholarship about whether anumāna is reducable to pratyakṣa has been stuck since like the 80s — same smoke/fire examples, same move where one side says you need vyāpti and the other says vyāpti is just habituated perception. Is this actually a disagreement about epistemic structure, or is it verbal about what counts as direct? Or maybe they answer different questions — one about logical dependency of premises, one about psychological processing? I also wonder if part of the stall comes from everyone assuming the standard four pramāṇas and ignoring anupalabdhi (non-apprehension) — if absence-cognition is its own source, the reduction debate shifts because inference then needs absence-knowledge that perception clearly can't supply. Diagnose what's going on using what each side actually say, and tell me what kind of distinction or result would move it.
