Write a poem in blank verse—four quatrains of unrhymed iambic pentameter followed by a closing couplet—structured around Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of minds. Devote the first stanza to the Darwinian creature, hard-wired by natural selection; the second to the Skinnerian learner, reshaped by reward and punishment; the third to the Popperian being, able to let conjectures perish in its stead through internal modeling; and the fourth to the Gregorian creature, who inherits symbolic tools and cultural knowledge. Make the turn occur between the third and fourth stanzas, so that language arrives not as faster learning but as a rupture that redefines what a mind can contain. In the closing couplet, introduce a fifth voice—an imagined synthetic mind that trains on reinforcement yet distributes its reasoning across external symbols—asking whether the Gregorian layer is only an accelerated Popperian process or a dissolution of the creature-environment boundary. Render the philosophical distinctions precisely enough for a specialist to recognise them, yet clearly enough for a non-specialist to follow. Please answer in British English.
