Read Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind through the ethics of remembering and forgetting -- Margalit's idea that shared memory binds communities, and Rieff's argument that sometimes forgetting is a kind of mercy or even a duty to oneself. But I want you to push past the usual "is erasure good or bad" framing. There's this niche idea I keep coming back to: mnemonic asymmetry. The idea is that when you erase a memory of harm you did to someone, thats a different moral move than erasing a memory of harm done to you. Joel and Clementine both erase each other, symetrically, through the same tech. Does the film treat those two directions as morally equivalent? Point me to specific scenes -- the discovery of the erased tapes, Joel's attempt to hide Clementine in other memories, the final decision to start over despite knowing it will hurt. And tell me: does the film actually understand that forgetting might be obligatory in one direction and forbidden in the other? Or does it collapse that distinction because its a romance, not a moral philosophy? I'm writing an essay for a film-philosophy seminar and I need the reading to take both the movie's emotional logic and Margalit/Rieff seriously without reducing either to the other.
