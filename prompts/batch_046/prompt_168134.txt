A professor in an introductory neuroscience course summarized the split-brain findings like this: After surgical section of the corpus callosum, the cerebral hemispheres are largely disconnected except for subcortical pathways, and experiments by Sperry and Gazzaniga demonstrate that each hemisphere can independently acquire information, form preferences, and guide behavior. The left hemisphere, which controls language, contains an interpreter module that spontaneously constructs a unified narrative explaining the patient's actions—even when those actions were initiated by the right hemisphere without the left's knowledge. Because the right hemisphere lacks speech but can match images or solve spatial puzzles that the left cannot access, there is, she argued, a second stream of consciousness that is present but silenced. The patient therefore reports a single self only because the left hemisphere's interpreter suppresses awareness of the right hemisphere's independent mental life. From this she concluded that split-brain patients contain two conscious subjects in one body, and that philosophical accounts requiring a unified ego—whether Kant's transcendental unity of apperception or contemporary global-workspace theories—are empirically refuted.

Is this summary fair? Please distinguish what is accurate about the empirical literature, what is a plausible philosophical reading that has been overstated, and what misrepresents either the neuroscience or the philosophical theories named. Specifically: does the right hemisphere's nonverbal performance demonstrate a second conscious subject? Does the interpreter module suppress awareness of the other hemisphere, or does it do something else? And does the empirical evidence actually undermine unified-ego theories of consciousness, or is that a non sequitur?
