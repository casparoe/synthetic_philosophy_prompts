From an effective-altruist, longtermist perspective that takes suffering reduction as a primary objective but is wary of moral-paralysis via infinite dilution, please rank the following four frameworks for pan-sentience and ethical weight in fundamental physics into tiers (S, A, B, C, or D) according to how seriously a rationalist should take their direct moral implications—specifically, whether each offers a principled cutoff that avoids distributing infinitesimal suffering across every quantum interaction, or whether it collapses into an aggregation error: (1) constitutive panpsychism that assigns minimal phenomenal properties to fundamental particles and addresses the combination problem through relational or informational integration; (2) integrated information theory applied only to quantum-coherent substrates, suggesting that suffering-relevant consciousness might exist at mesoscopic scales far below neurons but well above individual quanta; (3) a hypothetical "decoherence-threshold" view—treat this as a speculative boundary condition rather than an established theory—in which moral patienthood activates only when quantum coherence spans interactions among roughly Avogadro-number-scale particle ensembles, thereby localizing potential suffering to macroscopic physical processes; and (4) physicalist eliminativism that denies intrinsic phenomenal properties to matter altogether, assigning zero direct moral weight to non-biological systems. For each tier, justify in one or two sentences why it lands there, with explicit attention to whether the view permits tractable ethical obligations or instead dissolves moral urgency into an undifferentiated background of microscopic experience.
