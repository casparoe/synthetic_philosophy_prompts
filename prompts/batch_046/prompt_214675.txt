I'm on the grant review board for our university's Center for Comparative History. We recieved this big proposal titled "The Needham Contrast Revisited: Mechanization in Britain vs. the Lower Yangtze, 1700-1850." The PI frames the whole study around the classic "why Britain and not China?" question. Half the board says that's the right comparative frame—without it you just have two disconnected national histories. The other half says the contrast is philosophically broken: Britain and China weren't running the same race, "mechanization" meant totally different things in each institutional context, and asking why one "won" smuggles in a teleology that makes the answer circular.

There's also a specific methodological snag. The PI plans a chapter comparing coal geograpy in Newcastle to coal access in Shanxi, treating both as "resource endowments" that either blocked or enabled innovation. But one reviewer noted that in Qing bureaucratic practice "access" wasn't a market variable but an allocation problem, so treating them as the same causal factor is comparing apples to... well, not apples.

We have to decide by next Friday: fund, reject, or approve with conditons. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a clear timeline—especially if we go conditional, say what revisions are due and when. It needs to take the strongest objection seriouslly, not just dismiss it as presentisim. I personally lean toward saving the contrast by reframing it as tracking local difference-makers rather than a global race, but I'm not sure if that fixes the proposal or just changes the subject entirely. And please don't give me a reading list—just the recommendation and the reasoning.
