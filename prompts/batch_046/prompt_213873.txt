I'm drafting the core commitments for a philosophy-of-language workshop and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is my position on Quine and the analytic–synthetic divide:

(a) Quine's argument in Two Dogmas of Empiricism succeeds: attempts to ground analyticity in synonymy, or synonymy in analyticity, are circular, and there is no sharp, philosophically deep boundary separating meaning-dependent truths from fact-dependent ones.

(b) Despite (a), scientific practice routinely treats framework-defining stipulations—coordinate conventions, mathematical formalisms, logical rules—as structurally different from empirical hypotheses, and dismissing this difference as merely pragmatic or psychological fails to explain why some revisions are routine adjustments while others are framework-collapsing.

(c) Confirmational holism is correct: individual statements face the tribunal of experience not in isolation but as part of an interconnected web of belief.

(d) I therefore defend what I call protected scaffolding: certain logical, mathematical, and methodological commitments are treated as non-revisable in practice, not because they are analytic, but because abandoning them would undermine the very conditions that make empirical testing and rational revision intelligible.

(e) To capture this without reinstating the binary, I propose replacing analyticity with a graded metric of epistemic entrenchment that measures, for any belief, the total predictive and explanatory coherence that would be lost across the web if that belief were revised.

(f) Yet this replacement seems unstable: if entrenchment can be ranked in a principled, non-arbitrary way, then high-entrenchment beliefs just are the analytic ones and low-entrenchment ones the synthetic ones, so I have reconstructed the distinction under a different name rather than eliminated it.

Please audit this set for genuine internal tension. Which pairs actually conflict, which only appear to conflict upon closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision to restore coherence? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally problematic. In doing so, clearly separate what is broadly settled in the literature—such as the failure of reductionist definitions of analyticity—from what remains genuinely contested, including whether holism licenses global skepticism about logic, whether pragmatic protection of central beliefs is compatible with Quine's naturalism, and whether entrenchment-based reconstructions genuinely avoid the analytic–synthetic binary. Do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
