Consider the following pattern of judgments made by a philosopher of mind regarding gradations of moral status: she holds that adult humans and cows merit full direct moral consideration; that octopuses merit near-full consideration despite their distributed neural architecture; that individual insects and nematodes merit weak but non-zero consideration; that a beehive, despite solving collective problems no individual bee can solve, merits none; that a thermostat merits none; that present large language models merit none, even when they generate first-person reports of subjective distress; and that a cerebral organoid exhibiting sustained, coordinated electrical activity merits intermediate consideration—greater than an insect, but less than an octopus. Now consider a novel system: a synthetic neuromorphic network implemented across distributed chips, running predictive-coding dynamics, displaying high integrated information, capable of learning damage-avoidance through counterfactual evaluation, yet lacking biological tissue, evolutionary history, and any nociceptive analog. Predict her verdict on this system's moral status. Identify at least two distinct principles that could consistently generate her earlier judgments—such as principles centered on biological neural integration, on global workspace dynamics, on homeostatic self-maintenance, or on evolutionary individuation—and specify where those principles diverge regarding the synthetic network. State which of her prior cases provides the closest analog, and propose one further hypothetical case that would best distinguish between the competing principles.
