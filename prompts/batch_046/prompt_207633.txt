Question: Dr. Elena Vasquez, a marine ecologist, concludes that Bay Alpha is recovering based on a spectrometer reading of 18.3 ppm chlorophyll. She justifies this belief by citing the instrument’s official calibration certificate, which she has personally verified. Unknown to her, the certificate is a forgery; the spectrometer is generally miscalibrated. By coincidence, however, the device is functioning correctly today, and the true chlorophyll level is indeed 18.3 ppm. Is Vasquez’s belief knowledge? Analyze using the JTB framework, determine whether this is a Gettier case, explain what Nozick’s tracking conditions and Sosa’s safety condition each deliver, and evaluate whether distributing epistemic credit to the lab technician who executed the scan changes the verdict.

Student Answer: Vasquez clearly holds a true belief that is well-justified by standard scientific practice: she verified documentation and relied on a calibrated instrument. Nevertheless, this is a Gettier case. Her belief depends on the forged certificate—a false lemma—so despite being true, it falls short of knowledge. Gettier’s own solution was that knowledge requires justification free of false premises; since Vasquez’s reasoning includes the forged document, she lacks knowledge. Applying Nozick, we use his tracking condition, which demands that the belief be safe: in all nearby worlds where Vasquez uses this instrument, her belief about chlorophyll remains true. Because the instrument is generally miscalibrated, there are nearby worlds where she believes 18.3 ppm but the reading is wrong, so tracking fails. Sosa, by contrast, offers a sensitivity condition: if the chlorophyll level were not 18.3 ppm, Vasquez would not believe it was. Given the instrument’s unreliability, that counterfactual is not satisfied either, so both conditions agree she lacks knowledge. Finally, distributing epistemic credit to the technician who ran the scan changes the analysis. The technician knows the instrument’s state and performed the scan correctly, so the team as a collective possesses knowledge even when Vasquez individually does not. Thus the Gettier problem is solved at the group level, though Vasquez personally remains in ignorance.
