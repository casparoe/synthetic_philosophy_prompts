I am taking a graduate seminar on social choice and welfare economics, and over the past month I have been keeping careful notes on our professor’s rulings about whether proposed social-preference profiles qualify as valid applications of Harsanyi’s aggregation theorem. The track record so far runs as follows. First, a profile with three agents who all satisfy the von Neumann–Morgenstern axioms, combined with a social ordering that respects both Pareto preference and Pareto indifference as well as independence, was approved with weights 0.2, 0.3, and 0.5. Second, a profile in which one agent has lexicographic preferences—so continuity fails—while the other two are standard vNM and the social ordering respects independence and Pareto conditions only among that standard pair was approved, but strictly on the condition that the lexicographic agent receives weight zero and is treated as lying outside the aggregation. Third, a profile with four vNM agents but proposed weights that included a negative coefficient and summed to more than one was rejected outright. Fourth, a profile in which every agent is vNM but the planner restricts the domain of lotteries to those that exclude a specific catastrophic outcome, with independence and both Pareto conditions holding inside that restricted domain, was approved with the explicit caveat that the representation is local and cannot be globally extended without further argument. Fifth, a profile in which one agent’s utility is subjected to an arbitrary positive affine transformation, with the proposed weights rescaled proportionally, was approved on the ground that such scaling is absorbed into the weights.

The new case we face this week is a climate-risk aggregation problem that introduces a feature none of the precedents have touched. There are three individual stakeholders with standard vNM preferences over policy lotteries mapping to temperature and economic outcomes, plus a fourth party represented by a “future-generation proxy” whose preferences are vNM for outcomes up to a 3°C warming threshold but then exhibit a sharp discontinuity—violating continuity and hence the vNM axioms—once that boundary is crossed. The social planner’s ordering respects independence and both Pareto conditions for every lottery whose support lies entirely below 2.5°C; however, for lotteries that straddle the 3°C boundary, the planner violates independence in a specific directional way, since the presence of the catastrophic tail shifts the ranking of the non-catastrophic outcomes. The proposal assigns equal weights of 0.25 to all four parties, including the proxy, and asserts that the overall social preference is a Harsanyi-weighted sum. I want to know what our professor should decide if she aims to stay consistent with her five earlier rulings: which of those precedents is the closest analog—the exclusion of the lexicographic agent, the local-domain approval, or perhaps the scaling case? Please identify the candidate rules that jointly fit the track record, say what each predicts about this climate-risk case, give your best-guess verdict with your reasons for preferring it, and be explicit about what principle the body would be adopting if it went one way rather than the other. It seems to me that the precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer: one reading supports a strict global rule, according to which any localized failure of vNM or independence anywhere in the outcome space disqualifies the aggregation entirely, which would require either rejecting the proposal or excluding the proxy and restricting the domain to the safe region; another reading supports a piecewise or threshold-sensitive rule, according to which the theorem applies locally wherever the axioms hold, catastrophic-region violations are quarantined, and weights may be adjusted downward for agents whose preferences fail near the boundary, which might permit approval with modified weights—say, roughly 0.33 for each standard stakeholder and near-zero for the proxy. If that underdetermination is real, please state the principle each choice embodies. Finally, name one further case—perhaps involving a proxy whose discontinuity occurs inside the safe region, or a planner who violates independence only for non-catastrophic lotteries—that would best reveal which of these two rules she is actually running.
