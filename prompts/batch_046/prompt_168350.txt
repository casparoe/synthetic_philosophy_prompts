Design a reusable selection and classroom procedure for a British secondary-school humanities department that wishes to use biography for moral education without falling into hagiography. Ground it in Plutarch’s practice in the Parallel Lives—pairing figures to show virtue and vice as intertwined rather than presenting saints—but inject the ‘shadow-exemplary’ rule: a biography is pedagogically valuable only if it preserves at least one structural failure inseparable from the virtue being taught. The procedure must include: a screening checklist with concrete textual indicators to distinguish shadow-exemplary biography from sanitised hagiography; a three-step teaching protocol (identify the agent’s practical reasoning, locate the virtue-vice nexus, require students to revise their initial moral judgement in light of that inseparability); an exclusion rule for texts whose distortions are structural rather than incidental; and two worked applications—one acceptable (for example, a life of Marcus Aurelius that retains his complicity in persecution) and one the procedure must reject despite historical prestige. Specify every point where a teacher must exercise judgement rather than apply a rule, state clearly what the procedure cannot settle, and reach a brief conclusion on whether this Plutarchan approach can genuinely teach virtue without either idealising its subjects or reducing them to mere cautionary tales. Keep it usable by a non-specialist teacher running a term-long module.
