I'm drafting a piece on metaphysical symmetry and have constructed an argument about Black's classic two-sphere universe that spirals into what feels like an absurd conclusion; I need your diagnosis of where, if anywhere, it collapses and what repairing it costs. The argument: (1) Leibniz's principle holds—if two objects share every property, they are identical. (2) In an otherwise empty, perfectly symmetric universe containing only sphere S1 and sphere S2—identical radius, mass, and composition, separated by two miles of empty space—each sphere bears exactly the same relational properties, since separation is symmetric and there is no external reference frame. (3) So by (1) and (2), S1 and S2 are numerically identical. (4) But a single material object cannot occupy two disconnected regions without violating topological continuity. (5) To avoid contradiction we must either reject (1) or posit a non-qualitative individuator—a primitive thisness, or haecceity—for each sphere. (6) Rejecting hidden essences as metaphysically mysterious, we keep (1) false: qualitative identity never guarantees numerical identity. (7) But then no two objects anywhere, including elementary particles, can be genuinely indistinguishable; quantum mechanics is wrong to treat fermions or bosons as lacking individual essences, and symmetric wavefunctions must be approximations. (8) Yet quantum statistics are experimentally robust, so (7) is unacceptable. (9) Therefore we must accept primitive thisness for spheres. (10) But if spheres carry hidden thisnesses, so does every macroscopic object—including human bodies. (11) Hence the counterfactual "I could have been born in 1900" becomes incoherent: the 1900-person shares my qualitative profile but lacks my primitive thisness, so is not me, yet without shared thisness there is no ground for calling them anyone else either, since qualitative duplicates are impossible. (12) So resolving Black's thought experiment consistently destroys both quantum mechanics and ordinary modal reasoning about personal identity. Where does the chain break? I especially want to know whether the flaw is an equivocation between qualitative and quantitative properties, a scope error in applying Leibniz's law, or a modal slide from physical to metaphysical possibility.
