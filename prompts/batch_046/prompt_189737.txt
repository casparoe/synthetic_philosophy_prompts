After retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have spent the last few years working through philosophy of science and philosophy of mind, though I find that my instincts remain those of someone trained to look for mechanism rather than metaphor. One problem that has begun to occupy me is the causal status of symbolic representations within physical systems—specifically, whether a symbol can genuinely direct the behavior of the material that instantiates it, or whether it is always the underlying physics that does the work while the symbol merely describes it. To sharpen the issue, a colleague proposed two hypothetical constructs: the careenium, a substrate in which informational patterns are embedded but are not themselves physically forceful, and simmballs, symbolic tokens within that substrate that appear to steer its dynamics from a higher level of organization.

The core conceptual question is whether, in such a system, the simmballs actually push the careenium substrate around—exerting downward causal influence—or whether every transition attributed to a symbol can be fully decomposed into local physical interactions within the substrate, leaving the symbol as an epiphenomenal gloss. I would like a precise, in-the-weeds explanation of what conditions would need to hold for symbol-level causation to be real rather than apparent, whether downward causation offers a coherent resolution without violating the closure of physics, and what this implies for the careenium specifically. Please treat this as a direct conceptual analysis of mechanism and emergence rather than a literature review; I am not asking for citations or a survey of existing theories, but for a working-out of whether symbols can be genuine causal agents in their own right.
