My study partner cooked up this Boltzmann-brain argument and now I'm paranoid it's not just cosmology that dies but literally everything.

(1) In an eternal infinite universe, thermal fluctuations eventually produce conscious brains - Boltzmann brains - with every possible mental state.

(2) So for any belief I have right now, whether it's that 2+2=4 or that natural selection happens, there are infinitely many Boltzmann brains that randomly have that exact belief, and only a tiny number of evolved brains that actually reasoned to it.

(3) By self-locating reasoning, I should assign near-certainty to being one of the random ones.

(4) If I'm a Boltzmann brain, my reasoning is just noise, so I shouldn't trust step (3) - that's the familiar self-undermining loop in cosmology.

(5) But here's where my friend extends it: the counting in (2) doesn't depend on the cosmological premises being true. Even if I reject the eternal-universe model, within any model where Boltzmann brains are possible at all, the referance class for "beings with belief B" is still dominated by random fluctuations.

(6) So for every belief B I hold, the self-locating argument tells me it's probably noise. Not just my belief about cosmology, but every belief.

(7) That means either I reject self-locating reasoning globally, or I end up in total skepticism.

(8) My friend says (8) is correct and we should ditch indexical reasoning entirely. But that feels like throwing the baby out with the bathwater - I want to be able to ask "am I a Boltzmann brain?" without that question destroying my ability to think about biology or math.

Where does this slip? Is there a hidden scope error between (4) and (6), or is (5) wrong about the referance class being model-independent? And if I do have to bite a bullet, is it less costly to reject self-location in general or to accept that my reasoning is unreliable?
