ok so I'm organizing thoughts for a seminar on interpreting the french revolution and want a consistency check before I present. heres roughly where I've landed:

(1) the revolution was driven mainly by class struggle — the bourgoisie breaking aristocratic privilege — not just an ideological mix-up.
(2) revolutions devour their children; once you commit to total rupture with the old order its structurally inevitable.
(3) but contingency is huge — bad harvest in 88, crown fumbling finaces, and suddenly the estates-general collapses into something no one planned.
(4) the rights-talk and citizenship ideology was mostly post-hoc rationalization for material interests.
(5) the terror was a defensive reaction to invasion and rebellion, not baked into revolutionary ideas themselves.
(6) I also buy this idea I call "institutional phantom pain" — revolutionary assemblies kept copying monarchy-style hierachical decision structures but stripped of the rituals that made them legitimate, which explains the hyper-violence.

do these hang together? (2) and (3) look like they clash — if the crisis was basically an accident of weather and finance, how can child-devouring be structurally necessary? and (5) vs (6) feels tense: was the terror defensive or was it caused by this phantom institutional copying? also does (4) sit okay with (6) since phantom pain is about institutions, not class?

go through the pairs, rank tensions by severity, flag which ones only look bad, and tell me the single cheapest revision that cleans up the worst conflict.
