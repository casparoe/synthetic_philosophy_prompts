In debates about the Fischer controversy and whether it makes sense to hold a nation responsible for the First World War, I keep encountering the line “the powers sleepwalked into catastrophe” used to dismiss talk of German war guilt. I want to bring a precisified menu back to the argument. Lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be expressing—for example, that no cabinet intended a general European war; that causal responsibility is so distributed that singling out one state is conceptually confused; that moral culpability requires intentionality that was absent across the board; that the Versailles war-guilt clause was therefore unjust; or a stronger thesis that collective agents cannot bear responsibility in the first place. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive historical claim, a moral principle, or a category error; indicate which ones actually undermine the project of holding Germany (or any nation) responsible and which merely change the subject; and tell me which reading people invoking the slogan in this context are probably relying on, and whether that reading can do its work without requiring me to deny Fischer’s documentary evidence about deliberate German risk-taking. Present the readings as a list I can use in discussion.
