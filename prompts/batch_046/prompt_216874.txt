Write a dialogue between a proponent of Ryle's distinction—who holds that knowing-how is irreducible to propositional knowledge and essentially involves embodied, norm-responsive practice—and a representationalist who argues that knowing-how is a form of knowing-that with appropriate causal and counterfactual conditions. Center the exchange on two cases: an AI surgical system with perfect procedural representations and high-fidelity simulation capabilities but no history of operating on living tissue; and a master surgeon who, following a localized neurological injury, retains flawless manual execution of complex sutures yet has lost all explicit anatomical and procedural memory and cannot justify her movements. Have each interlocutor defend her view by analyzing both cases, with genuine back-and-forth: the representationalist should explain why representation plus counterfactual control is sufficient for knowing-how, and the Rylean should explain why embodied attunement and practical norm-sensitivity are necessary. Each should directly answer the other's charge—whether of over-intellectualizing skill or of reducing practical mastery to information processing.
