I am a high school student enrolled in an advanced course on comparative political memory, and I have become absorbed in a stalemate I keep encountering in discussions of how different societies address traumatic pasts. When scholars compare Germany's Vergangenheitsbewältigung with memory politics in Japan, Turkey, and the American South, they seem to agree on the basic empirical record—what monuments stand, which textbooks were revised, which state apologies were delivered, and which civil society movements pushed for recognition—yet they have reached no evaluative convergence in decades. One side treats Japan and Turkey as incomplete iterations of the German model, arguing that without sustained, state-led educational and judicial restructuring, collective moral repair remains superficial; the other side contends that applying the German framework to East Asian or American Southern contexts commits a category error, because Vergangenheitsbewältigung emerged from a specific post-totalitarian civic contract and institutional architecture that was never present in those societies.

What kind of disagreement is this, precisely? I wonder whether it is a hidden normative dispute about whether justice for historical wrongs requires institutionalization by a sovereign state, or whether it is a deeper conceptual disagreement about whether working through the past can be detached from the particular Westphalian conditions that produced it in postwar Germany. More specifically, I have begun to think that both camps may be implicitly arguing over what I am tentatively calling mnemonic sovereignty—the question of which authority legitimately decides which past injuries become subjects of public redress—without ever making that premise explicit or examining whether the four cases share enough institutional commonality for the concept to apply uniformly. Given this framing, is there a conceptual distinction, a comparative empirical move, or a change in the framing question that could actually advance the debate? Or is the impasse structural, arising because the four societies are being pressed into a single evaluative schema that none of them fully inhabits?
