I'm writing out my take on the absent-minded driver problem for a small discussion group, and I want a consistency check before I open my mouth. Here's what I actually think, not polished for publication:

1. When the driver misses the store turn because he's zoning out on the familiar route, that's still an intentional omission — not just a reflex or mechanical slip. There's a real action here.
2. Intentional action needs guidance control, but I think it can be purely dispositional: you don't need to be monitoring every move right now, just able to redirect if something comes up.
3. At the exact moment he drives past, he has no occurent awareness of the turn decision and isn't actively tracking the reason to stop.
4. So he lacks occurent guidance control over that specific choice, but he still retains the dispositional version.
5. He's fully moral responsability for missing it — he should have set a reminder or kept attention available.
6. But I also think moral responsibility requires a fair-opportunity condition, and that needs at least some minimal occurent awareness that a decision point is approaching.
7. I draw a hard line between autopilot (the high-level goal is still active, just not monitored) and habit (no current goal representation, just stimulus-response).
8. The predicitive processing / active inference story about driving explains autopilot well — the brain suppresses prediction errors to maintain the route prior.
9. But that same story seems to collapse autopilot into habit, since both just involve high-precision priors suppressing error signals, with no clear room for an active-but-unmonitored goal.
