You are a professional philosopher specializing in philosophical logic and the metaphysics of contradiction. Before you begin, take a moment to brainstorm the structural taxonomy of stalled philosophical disputes: distinguish verbal disagreements, substantive disagreements about facts, disagreements about standards of evidence or data selection, hidden empirical or formal questions, and cases where interlocutors answer different questions under a single heading.

The debate over dialetheism—the view that some contradictions are literally true—has run for decades with little apparent movement. Dialetheists point to semantic paradoxes such as the Liar, to set-theoretic paradoxes arising from naive comprehension, and to apparently contradictory descriptions in quantum physics, arguing that these are not merely representational failures but true inconsistencies in the world; they defend paraconsistent logics that reject explosion and allow non-trivial reasoning from contradiction. Critics concede that contradictions appear in our theories and language, but maintain they are resolved by distinguishing object-language from metalanguage, by contextual restriction, by incomplete description, or by treating them as artifacts of modeling rather than features of reality. Both sides have refined their positions—dialetheists have developed formal paraconsistent systems; critics have sharpened distinctions between apparent and genuine contradiction—yet the core stalemate persists.

Diagnose why this debate has stalled. Is it a substantive ontological disagreement about whether reality contains true contradictions, a verbal disagreement about what counts as a 'real' contradiction versus a representational artifact, or are the two sides actually addressing different questions—one logical (which formal system best captures reasoning), one metaphysical (what the world is like), one semantic (how language relates to reality)—under the single label 'dialetheism'? In particular, consider the meta-level closure challenge: a dialetheist who accepts contradictions in the object domain must still describe that domain in a metalanguage, and if contradictions are permitted everywhere the theory risks trivializing itself, whereas if they are restricted to the object level critics charge the restriction is ad hoc. Does this hidden formal question—whether paraconsistent semantics can achieve non-trivial closure without universal explosion—do the explanatory work behind the impasse?

Using what each side actually says about paradox, contradiction, and the scope of logical principles, determine whether the disagreement concerns facts, standards of evidence, which cases count as data, or starting intuitions that neither side can independently justify. Is the question ill-posed, or merely hard? Finally, specify what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a formal result about semantic closure for paraconsistent systems, a conceptual distinction that dissolves the appearance of contradiction, an empirical finding, a change of question, or an argument that no progress should be expected and why.
