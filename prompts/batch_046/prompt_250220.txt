I work as a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I have been trying to work through lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: the view that severe suffering is lexically worse than any quantity of pleasure, while below some intensity threshold pleasure and pain are incommensurable and cannot be rationally traded off against each other. I built the following argument and it seems to show the view collapses into either arbitrariness or standard commensurable negative utilitarianism, but I cannot locate the error.

(1) If pleasure and pain are genuinely incommensurable below the lexical threshold, then there is no rational basis for weighing one sub-threshold pain against one sub-threshold pleasure.

(2) But specifying where the lexical threshold lies requires comparing a pain near the boundary with a large quantity of pleasure near the boundary; otherwise the cutoff is unmotivated.

(3) That comparison is exactly the kind of cross-category weighing that the incommensurability claim in (1) rules out.

(4) So the view cannot non-arbitrarily fix its own threshold without violating its core commitment.

(5) If, to avoid this, the view permits such comparisons to locate the boundary, then pleasure and pain are commensurable after all, and the lexical claim is just an extreme point on a shared scale rather than a true lexical discontinuity.

(6) Therefore either the view lacks a determinable core boundary, or it abandons incommensurability and collapses into ordinary negative utilitarianism.

(7) A moral theory that either cannot specify its defining threshold or collapses into a different theory is defective.

(8) Hence lexical threshold negative utilitarianism is defective.

Which step should I reject? I would prefer to keep (1) and (4), since incommensurability blocking rational weighing feels central, and the self-reference problem about locating the boundary feels genuine. If I must abandon one, I want to know precisely what I am paying: does giving it up force me to accept full commensurability, or is there a middle position that preserves the lexical intuition about severe suffering without the paradox?
