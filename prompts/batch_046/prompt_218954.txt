Me and my mate were arguing about illusionism in consciousness studies, but not the usual stuff about whether it denies experience outright. I said if phenomenal consciousness is an illusion, then the illusion itself must have some phenomenal character—otherwise it wouldnt feel like anything to be misled, right? He said thats just confusing the vehicle with the content: the illusion is in what we represent, not in the representing itself. Its like thinking a movie projector needs to be made of images.

Then I brought up this thing—call it the "medium problem". In normal illusions like the Müller-Lyer lines, theres a representational mistake about length, but the lines themselves dont have a mysterious "phenomenal length" that needs explaining. But with consciousness, the mistake seems to be about the very medium of experience—like were misrepresenting the nature of the vehicle itself, not just whats in it. So illusionism owes us an account of "medium error" that doesnt just collapse into saying the brain misrepresents its own physical state, because then were back to explaining why that misrepresentation feels like something.

He came back with: thats exactly the illusionists point—the "feeling like something" is part of the misrepresented content, not a feature of the vehicle. The brain represents itself as having phenomenal properties, and that representation is false, but the representing is just neural firing. First-person authority is just another feature of the content: we have a built-in bias to take our representations at face value, not because theyre transparent, but because evolution made us poor epistemic agents about our own cognition.
