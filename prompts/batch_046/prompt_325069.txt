I'm trying to work out my own view on the ethics of space settlement, and I want an audit of whether the package hangs together rather than a lecture. Here is where I've landed: (1) Planetary protection obligations should cover not only biological life but any system exhibiting functional ecological autonomy—homeostasis, evolutionary dynamics, or analogues—regardless of chemical substrate, because the moral reason is the disruption of self-organizing processes, not carbon chauvinism. (2) The initial wave of settlement should be led by private actors rather than state monopolies, partly to avoid replicating the bureaucratic extractivism of terrestrial colonial administration and partly because competitive innovation is needed for viable habitats. (3) At the same time, all settled territory and extracted resources must be governed as a non-proprietary commons—no private land titles, no exclusive mineral rights—to prevent replicating terrestrial colonial patterns. (4) There is a positive moral duty to establish self-sustaining off-world settlements in order to reduce existential risk, which means settlement should not be postponed indefinitely for perfect knowledge. (5) But we should also observe a precautionary survey protocol: we should not begin large-scale ecological transformation unless we have conducted a sustained, multi-decade assessment that rules out the presence of the kind of autonomous systems mentioned in (1) to a reasonable standard of confidence.

The tensions are not obvious to me. Is (2) genuinely incompatible with (3), or can private leadership operate inside a commons framework without contradiction? Does (4)'s urgency undermine (5)'s precaution, or can they be reconciled by distinguishing minimal-footprint presence from transformative settlement? And does (1)'s broad protection criterion make (5) practically impossible—since functional autonomy might be ambiguous or deeply hidden—thereby rendering (4) moot? Order the tensions by severity, specify for each whether it is real or only apparent, identify which commitment has to give in each case, and say what the cheapest repair is and what it costs. I don't know which one I should be willing to drop.
