The prompt was: "Strawson in 'Freedom and Resentment' distinguishes reactive attitudes from the objective attitude and claims determinism doesnt threaten our practices of holding people responsible. Explain the reactive/objective distinction, and evaluate whether Strawson thinks the objective attitude is a live global option if determinism turns out true."

The student answer (handwritten, messy):

"Reactive attitudes like resentment and gratitude are interpersonal feelings that depend on seeing people as members of a moral community. The objective attitude is what we take toward people we cant hold responsible, like young kids or the insane - we see them as things to be managed rather than blamed. Strawson says determinism might seem to push us into the objective attitude for everyone, since every action is caused. But he thinks this is both impossible and undesirable because reactive attitudes are hardwired emotional responses that dont depend on beliefs about free will. So moral responsibility survives determinism because its based on social practices, not metaphysics. The objective attitude is basically a scientific view of humans as causal mechanisms, and we could never apply it to normal adults without losing all social bonds."
