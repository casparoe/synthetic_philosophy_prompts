Analyze the Memorial Medical Center crisis during Hurricane Katrina in 2005 through the doing-vs-allowing distinction, but I want you to do it with a weird framing. Here are the salient facts: the hospital didnt evacuate critically ill patients on Sunday before landfall; by Tuesday the power was out, conditions were extreme, and some doctors administered high doses of morphine and midazolam to patients who then died. Now, instead of isolating the Tuesday injections as 'killings' and the Sunday non-evacuation as 'letting die,' treat the whole 72-hour span as one decision-tree where Sunday is an omission-node and Tuesday is an action-node that only exists because the omission structured the available options. Call this a 'nested omission' — an omission built into the conditions of a later act. So map precisely: at each node who is the agent, what are the actual options, and does the distinction survive when the doctors themselves created the branch that led to Tuesday? Then reverse the analysis: what does this case show about the theory? Does institutional collapse just make every action carry its prior omissions inside it, or does it actually collapse the killing/letting-die line? Be honest about where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak. No need for citations; just work with the concepts.
