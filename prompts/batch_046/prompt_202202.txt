Right, I'm putting together a semniar on corruption and whether Western norms are universal, and I've noticed a particular argumentative slide that keeps cropping up in the literature and in classroom discussion. A scholar will defend a hard universalist position — say, that any exchange of favours for official action is corrupt, end of story — and when someone points out that Western institutions are full of functional equivalents (corporate hospitality, revolving doors, campaign finance that looks an awful lot like gift-giving) they don't abandon universalism. Instead they shift ground and say those aren't corruption because they don't subvert the *purpose* of the institution, whereas the practice being criticised abroad does. But then, if you press them on a Western case that genuinely does subvert institutional purpose — say, regulatory capture by industry — they slide back to the formal-rule criterion and call it corrupt precisely because it violates the rules. So you've got two criteria, procedural violation versus functional subversion, being used like a ratchet: procedural for familiar contexts, substantive for unfamiliar ones, neither held consistently.

I've seen this discussed near legal centralism and maybe institutional dualism, but neither quite captures the specific two-criterion swap I'm after. Is there an established term for this exact pattern — where a normative framework deploys a formal standard domestically and a substantive standard elsewhere, toggling between them to preserve the conclusion that 'our' practices are clean? If there isn't one, could someone coin a term that carves it off properly from plain hypocrisy, from motte-and-bailey, and from just applying context-sensitive judgement? I won't be satisfied with a bare label; I want to know where the edges are. Does it still count if the toggle is unconscious? Does it require that both criteria be available within the same culture, or is it enough that one culture gets the procedural defence and another gets the substantive attack? I'd like something precise enough to use in the semniar without just sounding like I'm calling people dishonest.
