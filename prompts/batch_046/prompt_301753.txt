Suppose Cicero were brought back to advise on aging in the 21st century. In De Senectute he defended old age against charges of misery by appealing to nature's order, continued public service, and the acceptance of mortality as release rather than evil. How would he likely respond to three modern conditions he never faced: first, biogerontological interventions such as senolytics and partial cellular reprogramming that could extend healthy lifespan by decades, dissolving the boundary between old age and prolonged middle age; second, algorithmic hiring and management systems that use age-correlated data to structurally exclude older workers, creating a form of dismissal he fought in republican Rome; and third, welfare-state discourse that frames the elderly as a fiscal "silver tsunami" and as unpaid caregivers rather than as counselors?

For each, predict whether he would adapt his defense of old age or reject the development as violating nature's order. Would he view radical life extension as corrupting the dignity of acceptance, or as extending the window for wisdom? Would he condemn algorithmic exclusion on Stoic and republican grounds of justice, and would his ideal of elder statesmanship survive an economy that treats aging primarily as dependency? Be specific about which commitments—natural law, active citizenship, or the release from bodily burdens—drive each prediction, and identify which modern condition most threatens to overturn his central claim that old age is best lived by embracing its limits.
