I have been working through the philosophy of science with colleagues who are mostly practicing physicists, and I have come to defend a reconstructed version of Popperian falsificationism as the solution to the demarcation problem. The view is not that science is defined by logical falsifiability in isolation, but by what might be called structured vulnerability: the methodological practice of arranging theories so that their empirical content systematically exceeds the elasticity of their protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses, where that elasticity can in principle be assessed rather than merely intuited. On this account, astrology and psychoanalysis fail not because they generate no predictions, but because their practitioners absorb anomalies by adjusting peripheral assumptions without altering core commitments, and this pattern distinguishes them from research programs that genuinely risk their central claims. I take this to rescue Popper from the standard Duhem-Quine objection and from Lakatos's charge that falsificationism reduces to naive methodological decisionism.

What I want is an honest map of this position's exposure. What would have to be true for reconstructed falsificationism to be wrong? Which formal results, historical case studies, or conceptual arguments would show that structured vulnerability either collapses into a rival framework—such as Lakatosian research programs or Bayesian confirmation theory—or fails to draw any non-circular boundary? I am particularly interested in whether all successful sciences actually display low protective-belt elasticity, whether the metric can be applied without presupposing the very demarcation it seeks, and whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability. Please distinguish the defeaters that a committed defender should concede as genuinely damaging from those that can be dismissed as misreadings of the normative, rather than descriptive, character of the criterion. Rank these vulnerabilities from fatal to merely awkward, and say whether the insulation of the view is itself a defect. Give me a committed response: take a definite view on whether this reconstructed falsificationism is well-protected or actually vulnerable, and defend that judgment with reasons rather than presenting the options neutrally.
