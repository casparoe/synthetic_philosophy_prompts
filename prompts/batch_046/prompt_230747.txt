I'm drafting notes for an essay on the Rietdijk-Putnam argument and want to check whether the physics and the philosophy actually connect the way I think they do. The standard reconstruction runs like this: special relativity makes simultaneity frame-relative, so there is no invariant "now" slice; if presentism claims that only present entities exist, then existence becomes frame-relative in a way that looks incoherent; therefore presentism should be abandoned for eternalism, or saved only by accepting a Lorentzian privileged frame. I want to push on a response that gets less attention. Treat "now" as an indexical, on the model of "here." Just as "this spatial location is metaphysically special" is false but "I am here" expresses self-locating information, "this temporal location is special" might be false while "it is now" expresses temporal self-location. This saves presentism without postulating a hidden frame.

But I think this indexical rescue creates a downstream problem that is rarely discussed. Counterfactual reasoning—"if event E had not occurred, outcome O would have followed"—seems to require that the temporal relation between the present moment of deliberation and E be intersubjectively stable. In special relativity, however, two observers in different inertial frames can disagree on whether E is in the past, present, or future relative to that moment of deliberation. So if "now" is purely indexical, then whether E is available to be counterfactually varied depends on the observer's frame in a way that fragments rational evaluation. The cost of saving presentism is not a privileged frame, but a loss of frame-independent counterfactual structure.

My notes for the essay are:

1. The frame-dependence of simultaneity undermines any global present slice, which is the core of the Rietdijk-Putnam challenge to presentism.

2. Eternalism resolves this cleanly; Lorentzian presentism resolves it by paying the price of an undetectable privileged frame.

3. Indexical presentism avoids both costs by analogizing "now" to "here," but it must then explain why temporal self-location does not generate the same kind of fragmentation in practical reasoning.

4. The fragmentation appears specifically in counterfactual deliberation: if agent A considers a counterfactual about E, and agent B assigns E a different temporal relation to A's present due to relative motion, then the truth conditions for A's counterfactual depend on B's coordinate assignment in a way that undermines shared evaluation.

5. Therefore the argument might be reconstructed not as "special relativity proves eternalism," but as "presentism, even in its indexical form, cannot supply the intersubjective counterfactual architecture that rational decision-making presupposes."

I would especially like comments on whether point 4 is physically and philosophically sound or whether I am committing a category mistake by importing decision-theoretic requirements into metaphysics. I also want to know if this connection between indexical presentism and counterfactual fragmentation is already established in the literature, since I do not want to present a rediscovery as original.

Please say how confident you are in each of these assessments, and single out the point you are least sure of.
