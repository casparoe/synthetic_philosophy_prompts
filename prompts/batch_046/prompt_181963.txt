I've ben in this little philosophy of math reading group and we've gotten totally stuck on Thomson's lamp—the supertask where you toggle a switch at intervalls of 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 and so on, then ask if it's on after two minutes. One side says it's not paradoxical at all—there's just no final toggle, so asking for the state is like asking what's north of the North Pole. The other side says determinismm demands an answer: every previous state is fixed by a rule, so if there's no contradiction in the rule itself, there should be a definite fact at the limit, maybe defined by some kind of omega-completion.

We've argued about physical realizability, but that feels like a red herring. My hunch is the stall comes from a hidden disagreement about whether state-properties need a "last cause" to be well-defined at a limit ordinal, or whether "being on" is even a property that can stabilize over a tail-set without a final member. Is that right? Are we actually fighting about formal semantics of infinite sequences, or is it a verbal dispute about what "explanation" means? And if it's substantive, is there any conceptual distinction—maybe about cofinality or how we assign values at transfinite steps—that could actually move one side?
