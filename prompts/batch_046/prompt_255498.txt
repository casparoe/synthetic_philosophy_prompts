I've been thinking about how we can meaningfully talk about things existing in parts of reality we can never observe—not merely distant objects, but regions where information is structurally blocked, such as behind an event horizon or in causally disconnected patches of spacetime. Please defend this position in detail. Argue that if the boundary behaves differently depending on whether one region or another lies behind it—exhibiting distinct causal signatures, thermal profiles, or geometric effects—then that structural difference is sufficient to justify counting them as distinct existents, even in the complete absence of internal descriptions. Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then develop the reasoning without any introductory summary or repetition of the prompt.
