I am a retired physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has turned to philosophy of mind and comparative religion. I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with an unnamed scholar who defends Erwin Schrödinger's Vedanta-inspired thesis that consciousness is fundamentally singular—that the apparent multiplicity of minds is an illusion analogous to the discreteness of particles within a continuous wavefunction. I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking him to restate this monism. Specifically, I want at least one question pressing how Schrödinger's quantum-mechanical ontology, particularly his treatment of superposition and entanglement, is intended to map onto the Vedantic relation between Brahman and Atman rather than serving as a loose scientific metaphor; the follow-up should anticipate his likely appeal to non-local correlation and ask whether that genuinely resolves the problem of individuation or merely relocates it. I also want at least one question on what I shall call the mnemonic-compartmentalisation problem: if awareness is genuinely one, why do autobiographical memories appear rigorously bounded to particular bodies, and how should personal identity be reconstructed under this view? The follow-up should address his probable reply that individual minds are like localised nodes in a single distributed field. Finally, include at least one question on the ethical asymmetry that seems to follow: if harming another is strictly self-harm, does altruism become conceptually redundant, and can self-sacrifice retain coherent meaning? Each of these should be paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give. Please draft the questions in British English, and afterwards include a brief note stating how confident you are in the philosophical reconstruction of Schrödinger's position that underlies them, singling out the specific interpretive claim about which you are least certain. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
