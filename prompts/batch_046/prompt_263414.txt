I am designing a graduate seminar on Bradley's regress and the problem of propositional unity, and I need to calibrate how different resolutions fare when judged by a single criterion: whether they preserve the reality of relational structure without either generating an infinite regress or treating relationality as an unexplained primitive. Please rank the following five strategies on a scale of one to ten, with ten being the most philosophically adequate, and present your evaluation as continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a formal letter: first, Russell's doctrine of external relations coupled with logical form as the residual bearer of unity; second, Bradley's own dismissal of relations as unreal appearances subsumed within an undifferentiated Absolute; third, the contemporary structuralist claim that relations are internal to the structure and therefore require no further binding element; fourth, the adverbial strategy that reconstrues relational properties as modifications of the relata considered jointly rather than separately; and fifth, a position I shall call distributive co-presentation, according to which the unity of a proposition is secured not by any single connector but by each constituent being presented in an intentional mode that is already directed toward every other constituent within the same mental or propositional act. In your continuous discussion, address whether distributive co-presentation genuinely escapes Bradley's regress or merely relocates the binding work across the terms, and explain how your scores reflect the tension between avoiding infinite regress and maintaining an intelligible account of propositional unity.
