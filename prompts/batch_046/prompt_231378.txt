I'm helping design a conceptual framework for an international bioethics review board that must assign regulatory status to synthetic chimeric organisms—specifically engineered microbes whose genomes are distributed across multiple wild-type lineages and that reproduce mainly through horizontal gene transfer and artificial insertion rather than vertical inheritance. The board needs to know whether "species" is even the right unit of moral and legal classification for these entities, and I want a map of the philosophical terrain rather than a biological manual. Please construct a branching taxonomy—structured as a decision tree—of the major positions on the species problem, but frame every split as a concrete judgment the board must make. Begin with whether reproductive isolation remains a coherent criterion for organisms that lack standard parent-offspring lineages; then branch by whether phylogenetic clustering should override functional phenotype in assigning membership, whether horizontal gene transfer renders species boundaries permanently porous or only locally fuzzy, whether synthetic organisms inherit the status of their closest wild-type ancestor, should be treated as sui generis entities, or should be denied species status altogether, and whether the entire taxonomic project smuggles in an essentialist ontology that post-Darwinian biology has rejected. At each leaf, name the position if it has an established label, mark any unoccupied combination and briefly diagnose why it might be empty, and state in one sentence the most awkward commitment that view carries in this synthetic-biology context—for instance, what it implies about the regulatory protection of a bacterium carrying thirty percent of its genome from a different phylum. The branches should separate positions by how they answer these concrete cases, not by which label a philosopher prefers. Finally, include an explicit note on where your reasoning is most vulnerable: what feature of synthetic or chimeric organisms is most likely to break this taxonomy, and what specific argument or finding in microbial taxonomy would cause you to restructure the tree or abandon one of its branches?
