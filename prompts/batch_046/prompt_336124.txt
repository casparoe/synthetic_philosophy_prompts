I am a student in a senior-level moral philosophy course working on a handout for our unit on moral particularism versus generalism. Rather than organizing the debate around the standard question “Are there moral principles?”, I want a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a decision tree with explicitly labeled branches—that sorts positions by their account of moral salience, analogical transfer, and default weight. Please design the tree with these specifications:

- Root split: Use this case—an agent breaks a serious promise to prevent grave harm. In version A the agent feels reluctant, regretful reluctance; in version B the agent feels cold, strategic indifference. Ask whether the emotional posture alters the moral status of the promise-breaking, or whether the justification is fixed solely by the harm-prevention feature regardless of posture. Label the branch by the claim that separates the two paths.

- Second-level split (for both sides of the root): Ask whether moral knowledge is analogically transferable across domains. Label this branch by the commitment about cross-contextual reasoning.

- Third-level split: Ask whether a particularist framework can still accommodate “default reasons”—presumptive weights (e.g., promise-keeping carries initial weight) that apply unless actively defeated—without collapsing back into generalism. This should split remaining branches.
