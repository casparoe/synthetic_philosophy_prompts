Write a short research proposal (approximately two pages, suitable for a dissertation prospectus or a one-semester fellowship application) on a question at the intersection of social-choice theory and the normative evaluation of democratic procedures. The specific problem: the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem demonstrates that any non-dictatorial voting rule over three or more outcomes, with universal domain and unrestricted preference profiles, is vulnerable to strategic manipulation. Standard interpretations treat this as a constraint on institutional design: if we want strategy-proofness, we must accept dictatorship or severely restrict the domain. Your proposal should investigate a less-explored boundary condition—whether the impossibility result holds, weakens, or transforms when voters hold second-order, mechanism-sensitive preferences, that is, preferences not only over outcomes but over the voting rules themselves and over the informational conditions under which strategic reasoning is permitted. Frame the precise question: does the introduction of endogenous mechanism preferences (where agents can rank-order different voting rules according to how those rules constrain future strategic behavior) create a new class of strategy-proof mechanisms, or does it collapse the distinction between strategic and sincere voting? Explain why existing literature on strategy-proofness leaves this open—specifically, that it treats the mechanism as exogenously fixed and the preference domain as first-order only. Describe your proposed method: a conceptual analysis that distinguishes mechanism-preference from outcome-preference, possibly supported by a formal model using a two-level preference structure, and state why this approach is preferable to purely empirical surveys of voter behavior or to purely axiomatic extensions that keep mechanisms fixed. Specify what a result would look like—whether a proof of extended impossibility, a characterization of the restricted domains where second-order strategy-proofness is achievable, or a demonstration that mechanism-preference makes the concept of manipulation incoherent—and identify what would count as failure (e.g., the distinction between first-order and second-order preferences turns out to be formally inexpressible without importing tools from modal logic that exceed the project's scope). Give an honest assessment of scope and timeline risks, particularly the danger that formalizing two-level preferences requires resources beyond a single semester. Do not include a bibliography, literature review by citation, or requests for reading lists; refer to positions and arguments by name where necessary, noting that citations will be added separately. Write the answer in American English, keep the tone formal and polished, and present the deliverable as a plan for work not yet done rather than a defense of an existing thesis.
