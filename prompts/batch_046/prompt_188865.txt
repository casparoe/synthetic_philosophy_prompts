write a short story about a tired archivist named Mira who works in a bureau that files away possible worlds that didn't make the cut. her job is to apply the actual Leibnizian selection criteria — maximum variety governed by the simplest laws — not the Sunday-school version where God just picks the happiest one. she stumbles on a file for a world with almost no suffering, rich in beauty, and she realizes it was rejected precisely because it was *too* harmonious; the lack of conflict meant less variety, and therefore a worse score on the metric. she starts forging entries to push it through. the story should show, through her paperwork and one argument with a senior clerk, why the best world might need its worst parts structurally, not just as a trade-off. don't let anyone lecture with quotes from books — let the idea sit in the filing system itself. write it casual, like you're pitching it to a friend who runs a philosophy blog, and feel free to misspell "recieve" or drop a comma somewhere.
