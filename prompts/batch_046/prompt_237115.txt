the exam q was: Teleosemantics holds that mental representations get their content from proper functions fixed by selection history. Imagine a synthetic neural circuit built in a lab that duplicates a frog's predator-detection system exactly, except it was assembled last week with zero evolutionary or learning history. Does pure selectionist teleosemantics deny that this system represents predators? And does this kind of case force teleosemantics to supplement selection history with current funcitonal role or designer intentions? Defend your answer with reasons.

heres the student answer (paraphrased from handwritten, about 350 words):

"Pure teleosemantics says the synthetic circuit lacks representational content altogether. Proper function requires being selected for, and since this thing has no evolutionary or ontogenetic history there is no selection event to ground content. This is basically Davidson's swampman problem applied to representation rather than belief. The only fix is derived proper function: the designers selected the parts for that role, so the system borrows content from their intentions. But once you allow that, teleosemantics collapses into intentional-stance functionalism, because all that matters is the arranged causal role, not actual selection in nature.

One might reply that learning-based selection could save it, since conditioning history can ground content on some accounts. But this system never learned anything either, so that path is closed. So teleosemantics faces a dilemma: either artificial systems can never represent, or it must add present functional role to selection. The second horn is more plausible, but it destroys the normative account of misrepresentation. If content depends partly on current causal role, then a malfunction is just a physical deviation, not a failure to do what the representation is supposed to do. So the case forces teleosemantics to become just another causal theory, losing its distinctive advantage over other naturalistic views."
