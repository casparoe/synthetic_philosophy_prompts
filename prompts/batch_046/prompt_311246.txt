ok so i'm trying to get my just-war view straight before a seminar and want to check if i'm talking out of both sides of my mouth. here's roughly where i've landed:

1. i buy Walzer's moral equality thesis — just and unjust soldiers have the same rights and restrictions in combat, full stop. the justice of the cause doesnt change what you can do to the guy in the trench.
2. but i also think soldiers fighting for an objectively unjust cause are doing something gravely wrong, and they should face some kind of post-war accountability, not necessarily criminal but not nothing either.
3. intentional killing of civilians is always forbidden, no matter the cause or military necessity.
4. there's an asymmetry in proportionality: a just belligerent can accept higher collateral damage if it genuinely shortens the war, but an unjust one cant claim that same license.
5. i reject the subjective-belief defense — if you're wrong about justice you dont get a pass, but your combat rights stay intact.
6. drone warfare breaks the old symmetry because the operator faces near-zero risk, so i think equality needs reinterpreting: remote pilots shouldnt have the same lethal permissions as front-line soldiers facing comparable danger.
7. all of this should be guided by case-specific moral intution rather than rigid rules.

do these hang together? i'm especially worried that (1) and (4) clash, and that (6) might actually undermine (1) more than it helps. also not sure if (2) saves (1) or makes it worse, and whether (5) messes with (6). can you audit this for me: rank the real tensions by severity, flag the pair that only looks like conflict but dissolves, say what the cheapest revision is in each case, and tell me what i'd have to give up if i want the cleanest package overall. no need for citations, just trace the logic.
