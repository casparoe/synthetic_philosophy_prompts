For a philosophy reading group of about ten people meeting for two hours, design a structured deliberation game that makes participants feel why moral particularism might be unavoidable rather than merely sloppy. Inject a specific mechanism I'm calling salience drift: each round, participants receive a case card describing an action with three explicit moral features—intentional harm, broken promise, and an unexpected good side-effect—plus a hidden context token that doesn't appear morally relevant on its face, such as whether the decision was made at night or mediated by software. The rules should require the group to assign each feature a consistent weight and reach a collective verdict, but the scoring system must be arranged so that adding the same feature in a different token-context inverts its contribution: the broken promise counts against the action in token A but supports it in token B, with no further explicit rule to explain the flip, only a shift in the overall gestalt. Do not tell participants about salience drift beforehand; they should discover it when their own weighted sums repeatedly fail to match the group's intuitive judgments. Include specific roles—e.g., a generalist advocate who must defend consistent cross-case weighting, and a particularist recorder who logs every inversion without explaining it—a timeline with at least three rounds, small stakes or point payoffs for predictive accuracy, and a debrief that maps each inversion onto holistic reasons. Be explicit about what feature of particularism this can reproduce—likely that reasons are not atomistic and can be enabled or defeated by apparently irrelevant companions—and which feature it cannot reproduce, probably the stronger metaphysical claim that no true moral principles exist at all, and state exactly how the facilitator must supply that gap verbally. Also include an honest warning about which round will predictably cause the group to abandon the weighting rules, and how to handle the resulting frustration without letting the session collapse into mere confusion.
