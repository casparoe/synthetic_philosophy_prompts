As a journalist covering science and ideas, I need a reusable editorial procedure to assess whether government practices touching on religion violate state neutrality, because I evaluate proposals and policies in England, Germany, France, and the United States without legal training and under deadline pressure. The underlying principle is secular neutrality, but I want it translated into an ordered decision flow that my team can actually run: a sequence of specific questions about fiscal extraction, symbolic presence, and institutional delegation, with defined stopping rules that yield a clear permissible, clear violation, or hand-to-judgment outcome. The framework must include two non-obvious checks: an institutional asymmetry test that compares the administrative and financial exit costs borne by religious citizens versus secular citizens, and a reverse-establishment screen that asks whether stripping away all religious reference would itself impose a secular worldview coercively. Specify what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, identify the hard cases where simplification will misfire, and mark precisely where value judgment rather than fact must take over. Then run the procedure on Germany's state-collected church tax—where formal exit from the church requires bureaucratic registration and tax liability ends only after state processing—and on France's prohibition of conspicuous religious symbols in public schools, where secular dress faces no comparable restriction. Ensure that at least one application produces a result that runs against standard liberal intuition, and explain why the procedure generates that conflict. Lead with the bottom-line summary of the decision flow in the first sentence, then present the reasoning and applications, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
