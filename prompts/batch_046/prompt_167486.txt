Construct a realistic, single-paragraph workplace or clinical scenario—no lotteries, no fake barns, no science fiction, no perfect predictors—in which two agents face the same non-obvious decision. Agent K holds a belief that is true, justified by a reliable method, and counts as knowledge on standard reliabilist criteria. Agent B holds an identically true belief formed through an unreliable heuristic that B themselves regards as dubious; B therefore lacks knowledge, but retains awareness of the belief's shaky basis. Engineer the situation so that conditions shift subtly: K's justification entrenchs the belief and blocks updating, producing a worse outcome, while B's absence of justification makes them receptive to counter-evidence and produces a better outcome. Then verify the design: state each agent's belief, the method that generated it, the relevant nearby worlds that test safety and sensitivity, and precisely why K knows and B does not. For each framework, state whether it ranks K's state above B's in this specific scenario, and justify the verdict with reference to the scenario's mechanics. Next, isolate the feature that reverses the usual value ordering—the non-stationarity of the environment combined with high entrenchment cost—explain what happens if that feature is removed (e.g., a stable environment), and describe how a defender of the stability account would attack the scenario.
