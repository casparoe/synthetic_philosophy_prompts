okay so i'm helping organize a small memorial next month for my great-grandmothers people — they lost their language and schools were banned, but there was never mass killing. i've been reading lemkin and now my study group walked me into this argument i can't exit.

1. Lemkin's original concept included cultural genocide — destroying a groups language, religion, or culture.
2. If we take that seriously, then assimilation policies like forced language bans count as genocide.
3. But the UN convention deliberately narrowed the defintion to physical or biological destruction.
4. So either we honor lemkin and call these policies genocide, which triggers legal obligations designed for mass murder, or we keep the narrow legal term and say cultural erasure isn't genocide.
5. Either way, naming something 'genocide' does political work that distorts the moral harm — it either over-activates legal machinery or under-acknowledges real suffering.
6. Therefore the category manufactures a false moral binary instead of describing harm.
7. But activists clearly use the term successfully without this distortion, so (6) must be wrong.

i really want to keep (1) and the idea that naming does political work, but i feel like something slips between (4) and (5) and i can't name it. which step do i reject, and what does rejecting it cost for families like mine who want recognition without being forced into a legal frame built for extermination?
