Design an eight-leaf decision tree that sorts positions on whether global history can be told without an implicit geographic or cultural center. Use concrete commitments rather than labels: begin with the case of a historian who writes the thirteenth-century Indian Ocean world using Chinese, Arabic, and Swahili sources while deliberately excluding available European archives—does this achieve “everywhere at once,” or merely replace one center with others? Then branch through three further distinctions: (i) whether a single chronological framework can be applied across regions without distortion; (ii) whether incommensurable causal concepts—kinship obligation, market contract, divine command—can share one explanatory narrative without being translated into a dominant vocabulary; (iii) whether abandoning a privileged observer requires giving up synthesis entirely or only rotating the vantage point. Each leaf should be named first by its answer pattern, then matched to an actual historiographical stance (for instance, synchronic polycentrism, methodological cosmopolitanism, ontological pluralism, or what might be called archival rotationism). Identify any answer combinations that yield empty leaves, and briefly diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored. The instrument must be answerable by someone unfamiliar with these terms, must accommodate all four named stances, and must not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
