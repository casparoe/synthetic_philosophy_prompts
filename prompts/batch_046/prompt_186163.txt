Critics of greedy reductionism in philosophy of mind often deploy the clock analogy—explain the timepiece by its gears and springs, with no downward-causal skyhook—to argue that mental content and biological function should be decomposed into neural mechanisms in the same bottom-up fashion. Opponents counter that this misses the organizational pattern, comparing it to explaining a democratic election solely by counting atoms in the ballot box: the parts only play their roles within an institutional structure that is not reducible to their physical properties. I want an audit of both analogies: map precisely what plays the role of gears, springs, and display in the mental case, and what corresponds to atoms, voters, and rules in the reductionist target. Identify where the mappings collapse—particularly around normativity, misrepresentation, and whether functional roles can be individuated without presupposing the very content being explained. Then say whether the "cranes-only" conclusion actually follows, or whether it covertly relies on a skyhook about selection history or functional equivalence that a consistent naturalist must reject, and suggest a less misleading analogy if there is one.
