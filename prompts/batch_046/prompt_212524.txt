Do not rely on loose metaphor; translate the logical form of each theory's account of causal efficacy, dependency relations, and the evaluation of alternative scenarios into the corresponding engineering notions of force distribution, alternative load-bearing configurations, and engineered redundancy.

In particular, address the niche case of symmetrical overdetermination in which two redundant pathways are not merely backup supports but co-constitutive of the structural outcome, and examine how this maps onto the interventionist distinction between conditioning on a variable and performing a do-intervention—especially in cyclic systems where feedback loops violate the acyclicity presupposition of standard graphical causal models. Then address the counterfactual evaluation of nearest worlds in terms of law-preserving similarity: does this have any rigorous counterpart in structural design scenarios where material properties and code specifications are held constant?

Conclude by assessing whether this residue indicates a genuine deficiency in the structural vocabulary, which lacks modal and normative resources, or whether it reveals that certain disputes in the philosophical literature—such as disagreements over the metaphysical grounding of causal laws—are less substantive than they appear once stripped of their technical apparatus.

Please write precisely and formally. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts or page numbers; I am interested solely in the conceptual architecture of the translation and its failures.
