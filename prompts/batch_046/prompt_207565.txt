Okay, so I’m drafting the take-home essay for my existentialism seminar and I’m genuinely tired of grading the same summary of Camus every semester. I want you to compare Camus’s absurdism—specifically the posture of living without appeal and the refusal of what he calls philosophical suicide—with the contemporary fitting-fulfillment view that meaning in life requires being actively engaged with projects that are both subjectively attractive and objectively worthwhile. Here’s the twist: don’t just do a generic comparison. Apply both frameworks to what I’m calling recursive maintenence, an edge case where a person spends decades curating obsolete software documentation in a server room no one enters, not because they think it saves humanity, but out of a kind of stubborn, almost aesthetic commitment to internal coherence. Does Camus’s absurd hero see this as genuine revolt, or is it just another evasion? Can the fitting-fulfillment theorist call it meaningful without smuggling in an audience or social utility?

You need to identify exactly where the two theories diverge on whether external validation is a necessary condition for meaning, not just a bonus feature. No bibliography needed—just argue clearly, and try not to confuse absurdism with garden-variety pessimism, since that’s the classic B-grade move that makes me sigh every time.
