Consider a counterfactual in which René Descartes is presented with the knowledge argument concerning Mary, the neuroscientist who knows every physical fact about color vision yet acquires phenomenal knowledge only upon seeing red—except that in this updated version Mary is an artificial cognitive architecture possessing exhaustive physical-functional data about chromatic processing, including a complete dynamical model of her own information-flow topology, yet she reports no phenomenal access until an unspecified integrative activation event alters her functional organization without introducing any new physical substrate. How would Descartes likely assess this scenario using the conceptual resources available to him—specifically the real distinction between thinking and extended substance, his interactionist account centered on the pineal gland, and his clock-work analogy for mechanistic explanation? Would he interpret Mary's pre-activation lack of phenomenal knowledge as evidence for a non-physical mental substance, as a mechanistic limitation analogous to an unmoving clock, or as a confusion arising from the transparency of conscious thought? Specify the criteria he would demand to distinguish a genuine substance-change from a mere modal rearrangement of extended matter, and identify what kind of empirical or conceptual finding he would regard as decisive for or against his own dualism in this modified case.
