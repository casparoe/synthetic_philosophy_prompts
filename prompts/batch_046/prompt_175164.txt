Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between hierarchical integrationists and modal pluralists could both sign, focused specifically on which varieties of free will are worth wanting and whether they constitute a single capacity or a family of distinct ones.

The integrationist camp holds that the free will worth wanting is a unified capacity: a second-order endorsement of first-order desires that exhibits counterfactual resilience, meaning the endorsed volition would survive moderate perturbations to the agent's circumstances, values, or emotional state, and that this integration is both necessary and sufficient for the kind of authorship that grounds moral responsibility. The pluralist camp denies that any single capacity captures all we value; they defend a disjunctive account comprising three separable capacities—deliberative openness (the ability to suspend judgment and genuinely reconsider), expressive authenticity (acting from values with which one is not alienated), and accountability readiness (being susceptible to reactive attitudes in ways that depend on social recognition)—and they maintain that none of these requires either hierarchical endorsement or counterfactual resilience, that they can come apart in actual human psychology, and that treating them as a unified package has led philosophers to conflate genuinely distinct normative achievements.

Write the collaboration document with the following structure:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. Include claims about the inadequacy of both libertarian "unmoved mover" caricatures and hard determinist eliminativism; about the importance of reflective self-evaluation to mature agency; about the fact that moral responsibility practices do not obviously collapse if metaphysical libertarianism is false; and about the empirical reality that human agents often experience internal conflict between endorsed and alienated motivations.

2. A disagreements table with columns for the claim, the integrationist formulation in wording they would accept, and the pluralist formulation in wording they would accept. Cover at least: whether free will is a unitary capacity or a cluster; whether counterfactual resilience is necessary for any valuable form of agency; whether deliberative openness can occur without hierarchical endorsement of the desires that result; whether expressive authenticity requires any modal ability to do otherwise in a robust sense; and whether accountability readiness is conceptually prior to authorship or derives from it.

3. The cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. Specify at least three: (a) whether a single integrated capacity can ground all the normative practices we associate with freedom and responsibility, or whether different practices require different capacities; (b) whether counterfactual resilience is empirically distinguishable from post-hoc rationalization, and whether there are real cases of "alienated but stable" desire in which an endorsed volition survives perturbation yet remains estranged; and (c) whether the three plural capacities can genuinely dissociate in human psychology—e.g., through conditions in which an agent retains deliberative openness and accountability readiness while lacking expressive authenticity.

4. For each crux, identify a concrete case, thought experiment, or empirical pattern that both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. For crux (b), both sides might agree that clinical and phenomenological reports of "alienated but stable" long-term commitments—where an agent reports that a central desire would survive life changes but feels fundamentally not "theirs"—are relevant; the integrationist should predict that closer examination will reveal either hidden instability or a failure of true second-order endorsement, while the pluralist should predict that resilience and authenticity are genuinely independent dimensions. For crux (c), both sides might agree that studies or well-described cases of agents who are highly responsive to moral reasoning (deliberative openness and accountability readiness) but act consistently from values they disavow (lack of expressive authenticity) bear on the dispute; record what each side expects such cases to show.

5. Conclude by stating whether any portion of the disagreement is merely verbal—if, for instance, the integrationist's "counterfactual resilience" and the pluralist's "deliberative openness" turn out to describe the same psychological mechanism under different descriptions, identify exactly which part and explain why the residual disagreement is substantive rather than terminological.

Format the agreed claims as a numbered list, the disagreements as a table, and the cruxes with predictions in clearly labeled sections. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works; phrase all positions in your own terms and rely only on concepts and distinctions that can be evaluated without access to external literature.
