Your state-space is partitioned into four mutually exclusive quadrants—Red Headquarters, Red Forward, Blue Headquarters, Blue Forward—mirroring the Judy Benjamin partition, with an initial uniform prior. Write an ordered decision procedure—tests in sequence, pass/fail conditions, explicit nodes marked OFFICER DISCRETION, and a flowchart in words—that translates the philosophy of updating on conditionals into practice. The procedure must distinguish three report types: indicative conditionals that constrain joint probabilities; material-filter reports that eliminate quadrants outright; and counterfactual dependency injections that stabilize one quadrant without redistributing credence elsewhere. Ground each step in the semantic distinction that justifies it.

Use the canonical Judy Benjamin case—uniform prior over the four quadrants; message received that if Blue then Headquarters—as your first worked application, showing both the symmetry-preserving revision and the symmetry-breaking revision, and flagging which result conflicts with intuitive risk assessment. Then apply the procedure to: (b) a logistics case where the antecedent already carries near-zero credence; and (c) an ambiguous report that could be read as either an indicative update or a counterfactual injection, requiring hand-off to discretion. For every case, list the revised quadrant credences.

Finally, specify exactly where philosophical disagreement over indicative versus material semantics becomes an unresolvable operational risk, what the procedure cannot settle, and the required sign-off format for those nodes. Include one case where the procedure produces a recommendation you will find counterintuitive but must still document.
