I am taking a graduate seminar in comparative political ethics, and I have adopted a modified universalist view of corruption that I want to stress-test before writing my final paper. The standard Western legalistic framework defines corruption primarily as the abuse of public office for private gain, but I think this is both too narrow—because it misses structural distortions that do not involve explicit rule-breaking—and too culturally specific, since it tends to treat practices like clientelism and ritual gift-giving as deviations rather than as locally legitimate forms of reciprocity. My own position is that corruption should be defined not by reference to formal rules but by reference to what I call role-constitutive obligations: the duties that are internal to a particular social or institutional role regardless of local convention. A benefit or exchange is corrupt, on my view, only when it generates a hidden obligation that is incompatible with the proper performance of that role; if the exchange merely reinforces social solidarity without distorting role-performance, it is not corruption, even if it violates Western transparency norms. This allows me to say that some gift-giving is genuinely corrupt—when it creates a concealed conflict of interest—while other forms are not, without collapsing into cultural relativism. But I am worried that my distinction between a hidden, role-distorting obligation and social reinforcement is too malleable, and that I might always be able to redescribe a case to save the theory. So my question is: what would genuinely count against this position? I want you to identify the kinds of empirical cases, conceptual objections, and cross-cultural arguments that should move me, ranking them from decisive to merely awkward. In particular, is there a formal or structural feature that my role-obligation framework must have but cannot provide? And if my distinction turns out to be unfalsifiable in practice, is that a fatal problem for a normative theory of this kind, or only a methodological limitation that I should accept?
