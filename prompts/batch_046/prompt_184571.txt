I am writing an essay for my philosophy elective on a topic my teacher describes as the problem of competence without comprehension, and I have noticed a specific argumentative pattern that I do not know how to classify. When people discuss Darwinian natural selection—how blind variation and selective retention produce structures like eyes or immune systems without any foresight about optics or pathology—or Turing’s demonstration that purely syntactic mechanical procedures can solve mathematical problems without grasping what the symbols mean, they often run into the same kind of resistance. Rather than accepting that a process can genuinely succeed while genuinely lacking any internal understanding, interlocutors seem to fall into an oscillation: either they insist that there must be a hidden designer, a guiding intelligence, or some implicit mental representation directing the outcome, or they dismiss the achievement altogether as mere accident and deny that it counts as a real solution. What interests me is not the evolutionary or computational mechanism itself, but this particular reasoning failure—the refusal to remain in the middle ground where competence is present but comprehension is absent.

I am familiar with concepts like anthropomorphism and Dennett’s intentional stance, but those seem to concern the attribution of minds to entities that lack them, whereas the pattern I am describing involves something closer to a binary trap: the assumption that if there is no understanding inside the system, then its success cannot be genuine, coupled with the simultaneous refusal to accept that success without understanding is possible. Does this specific failure mode—this inability to tolerate competence that is not paired with comprehension—have an established name in philosophy of mind, philosophy of biology, or philosophy of science? If there is no precise term, how should I distinguish it from ordinary skepticism about mechanistic explanations, and what boundary conditions would define it? For instance, how do I tell the difference between a legitimate demand for explanatory detail and this particular oscillation between hidden-mind theories and dismissive appeals to luck? I would like to know whether I am rediscovering an old error, and if so, where its edges lie.
