I’m leading a ninety-minute session for a rationalist-adjacent reading group—members fluent in Bayesian epistemology and falsificationism, but with no prior exposure to Rorty. The goal is to make his antirepresentationalism and neopragmatism intelligible without reducing them to either skepticism or social constructivism. I need a teaching plan, not the lecture itself. First, the dependency chain: what prior distinction (for instance, vocabulary versus belief, or causation versus justification) must be secured before the rejection of representation-as-correspondence makes sense rather than sounds like a denial of objective reality? Second, the carrying example: I want one concrete case drawn from scientific practice or prediction-market dynamics—not the mirror-of-nature trope—that demonstrates how a vocabulary can be instrumentally superior without being metaphysically closer to the real. Third, the misconception sequence: I anticipate they’ll slide into “truth is just consensus” and then into “all frameworks are equally warranted.” At which points in the arc should I intercept each, and with what distinction? Fourth, the understanding check: one question I can ask at the end that separates someone who has grasped the pragmatist move from someone who merely thinks Rorty is rejecting objective reality. Finally, what should I explicitly bracket—for instance, Davidsonian triangulation or the politics of liberal ironism—and how should I signal that omission without undermining authority?
