Think of it as asking whether the culture runs like a Bayesian update mechanism with revisable priors, or like a loyalty test with fixed ones.

I'd like to apply this to four cases I take to be structurally different: post-war Germany's Vergangenheitsbewältigung; Japan's contested WWII memory ecology (textbook authorization, Yasukuni politics, comfort-women historiography); Turkey's state-enforced narrative closure around the Armenian genocide; and the American South's recent shift from Lost Cause institutionalization toward monument removal and curricular revision.

Please rank these four from highest to lowest mnemonic reflexivity, using explicit tiers (e.g., Tier 1 through Tier 4).
