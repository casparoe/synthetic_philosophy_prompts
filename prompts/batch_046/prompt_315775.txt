There's this long-running thing among Spinoza people about whether he's truely a democrat or just an elitist liberal wearing democratic clothing. One camp points to the political treatise where he praises democratic councils and ties freedom to collective deliberation; the other camp reads the Ethics and sees that reason is scarce, the multitude is passion-driven, and real authority ends up with the wise few. My hunch is the debate is stalled not because we don't know the texts, but because both sides are running two different notions of democracy—one procedural about voting bodies, one ontological about where power actually lives in the multitudo versus individual rational agency. So is this a verbal disagreement, a hidden metaphysical contradiction, or are they just answering different questions under one word?
