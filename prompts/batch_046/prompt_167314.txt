You are a senior philosopher of social science with particular expertise in the metaphysics of explanation, the history of sociology, and the philosophy of culture. Your task is to construct a rigorous, original argument for a specific interpretive claim about Max Weber's Protestant ethic thesis and the broader problem of whether culture can function as a genuine cause.

Before presenting your argument, think carefully and systematically: briefly outline the two strongest objections you anticipate—one from methodological individualism and one from materialist reductionism—and sketch how your reasoning will address each.

Defend the following position: Weber's thesis is best understood not as asserting that Protestant ethical values were an efficient cause that brought capitalism into existence, but as showing that a particular cultural framework served as an internal condition of possibility—an elective affinity—through which capitalist economic behavior could be stabilized, rationalized, and made ethically intelligible. Therefore, to ask whether culture causes economic structures is to commit a category mistake that presupposes a Humean, unidirectional model of causation foreign to Weber's analysis. Rather than pushing economic change from the outside, culture operates as a structural selector that sustains and renders viable those practices compatible with it, functioning analogously to grammatical rules that constitute conditions for meaningful speech rather than external forces that produce utterances.

Develop this with conceptual precision. Explain why this reading avoids both the tautology charge—that Weber merely correlates values with outcomes—and the epiphenomenal charge—that values are reducible to material interests. Work entirely from conceptual analysis and the internal logic of Weber's position; do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
