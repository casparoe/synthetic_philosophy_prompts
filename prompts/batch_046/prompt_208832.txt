I'm writing about ICU triage rules and keep hitting this philosophical snag. Most protocols — think CRS-R and those global-workspace-derived checklists — treat a patient as "not there" if there's no reportable broadcasting, even when fMRI picks up what looks like locked-in phenomenal experience. Meanwhile the same system will preserve life for decades over a single blink-response, which is pure access consciousness. So what's the hidden theory? Are they implicitely buying Ned Block's split and deciding only access counts for moral status? Or are they just being pragmatic? And here's the weird part: some European ethics boards now apply an "access-first" rule that explicitely discounts any "phenomenal redundancy" — experience that never enters the global workspace — when deciding on withdrawal of care. Does that practice actually need global workspace theory to be true, or can a control-based view of personhood justify it? Or is it just inconsistently smugling in metaphysics under cover of tractability? I want to know what commitments are actually required, and whether the practice is philosophically underdetermined.
