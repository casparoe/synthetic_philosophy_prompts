Consider a legal system whose sole written constitution contains Clause A, stating that Clause A itself may be amended only by unanimous consent of all citizens, and Clause B, stating that any constitutional provision not invoked within fifty years is automatically repealed unless re-ratified by a simple majority. Give me your credence that this system can avoid a self-amendment paradox and an infinite regress of meta-rules without appealing to an external sovereign, decomposed into: (1) the probability that the interaction between Clause A and Clause B produces a logical contradiction rather than a resolvable practical deadlock; (2) the probability that treating the constitution as a dynamic social practice rather than a static rule-set dissolves the tension; and (3) whatever other factor you think belongs. Provide a number with one line of justification for each term, combine them into an overall credence, identify which term dominates your uncertainty, and specify what kind of jurisprudential argument or concrete constitutional-history example would shift your overall number the most.
