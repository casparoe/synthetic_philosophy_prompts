I am drafting a scene in my novel in which an essayist investigating the architecture of subjective time conducts a private interview with a philosopher who has advanced a heterodox account of the color-phi phenomenon. She maintains that perceptual consciousness involves a Stalinesque revision mechanism at the subpersonal level—rewriting the mnemonic record of a prior state without altering the original phenomenal content—while simultaneously generating an Orwellian phenomenology at the personal level, so that the subject experiences a retroactively unified present that never corresponded to any single neural state. To keep the dialogue conceptually rigorous rather than expository, please draft eight to ten interview questions that press her on the mechanics of this asymmetry. At least one question should target empirical work on backward masking and temporal integration windows; another should ask how her view distinguishes genuine temporal revision from mere post-hoc confabulation; a third should confront her with the standard objection that if the revision is subpersonal and introspectively inaccessible, it collapses into an untestable dual-process story. For each of these, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer—specifically, her appeal to orphaned present moments, brief phenomenal fragments that survive revision without being bound into the narrative stream of consciousness. The questions should avoid generic biographical prompts and must not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; they should assume the interviewer has engaged with her published arguments but wishes to expose the tension between her commitment to first-person authority and her denial that the present moment is ever fully present to itself.
