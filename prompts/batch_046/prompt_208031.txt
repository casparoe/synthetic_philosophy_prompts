I have recently begun advising a secular university chaplaincy on questions of theological ethics, and next Thursday I defend a restricted Molinist account before our philosophy of religion seminar. I hold that divine middle knowledge should be limited by a structural-stability condition: God knows only those counterfactuals of creaturely freedom that remain invariant across worlds with continuous causal histories near the actual world. This, I argue, answers the revived grounding objection without surrendering to open theism or collapsing into determinism. The audience comprises analytic metaphysicians who are sceptical of modal realism and theistic explanations, a theologian firmly committed to open theism, and one decision theorist who regards counterfactual semantics as largely ornamental. I need a prep sheet, not a defence: list the objections I will actually face, ordered by likely damage, phrased exactly as this audience would put them (grounding, explanatory redundancy, ad hoc restriction), each with a concise reply suitable for two minutes, an explicit note on where that reply is weakest, what I ought to concede before they ask it, and the single question I am hoping nobody raises. Please also state clearly, in your reply, what argument or finding would change your mind about this restricted Molinism—whether a decisive failure of the stability condition, a revived grounding argument, or something else—and identify the point in your reasoning most exposed to failure.
