Design a 75-minute seminar activity for 12 advanced philosophy or rationality students that forces them to feel the divergence between self-sampling and self-indication updating—specifically the “presumptuous philosopher” pull—but relocated away from cosmology into a nested, local reference-class problem. Give them a structured deliberation with real small stakes (points that convert to a shared snack budget, winner-takes-all split). The hidden setup: each pair receives a sealed “world profile” describing either a small-family world (few actual children, few counterfactual possible siblings) or a large-family world (many actual children, vast numbers of counterfactual possible siblings), with the true profile selected by hidden die roll. Through three staged indexical revelations—“you exist,” “you are the third-born,” “you live in a high-fertility normative environment”—each pair must publicly update their credence in the large-world profile. Crucially, assign half the pairs an SSA rule (update over actual observers only) and half an SIA rule (update over all observers in the reference class, including counterfactual ones). The rules and payoff scheme should be arranged so that the SIA pairs are pushed toward near-certainty in the large-world profile after the very first revelation, and they should feel that epistemic arrogance in real time—arguing, resisting, or doubling down—before anyone names the distinction. Include: the exact card/envelope and point-allocation mechanics; a minute-by-minute timeline (no lecture up front, terms revealed only in debrief); the debrief that maps each revelation onto the formal update step, explains where the “presumptuous” feeling comes from, and shows what resisting each step costs; an honest prediction of what will predictably go wrong the first run (participants will confuse indexical updating with causal/frequency reasoning and will try to “check” the envelope); and a frank note on which formal feature the activity cannot reproduce—normalization over infinite or ill-defined reference classes—so the facilitator must supply that limitation verbally at the end. Specify exactly what students should leave seeing: that SIA generates extreme, apparently arrogant conclusions whenever reference-class size correlates with the hypothesis, and that blocking it requires either restricting the reference class (SSA) or paying a specific theoretical price. Do not ask for citations or a bibliography.
