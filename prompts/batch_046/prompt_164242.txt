Below is a take-home exam question from an advanced philosophy of logic course, followed by one student’s response. The course has recently introduced a distinction between source normativity (the authority of a norm derives from its origin or the practices that institute it) and content normativity (the authority derives solely from intrinsic features of what the norm governs), and students were asked to apply it to logical entailment.

Question: Reconstruct the Euthyphro dilemma as it applies specifically to the normative force of logical entailment. Does an inference become valid because rational agents recognize it as truth-preserving (the recognition horn), or do agents recognize it as valid because it is independently so (the independence horn)? In your answer, deploy the source/content normativity distinction precisely, and be explicit about whether the dependence at issue in each horn is constitutive, merely causal, or neither.

Student answer:
Reconstructing the Euthyphro structure for logic, the recognition horn holds that an inference is valid in virtue of being recognized by rational agents as preserving truth. This makes logical authority source-normative: the norms derive their binding force from the inferential practices that institute them. If those practices shifted, the validities could shift with them, so logic lacks mind-independent objectivity.

The independence horn holds that inferences are valid in virtue of structural relations among propositions that hold regardless of whether anyone notices them. Here authority is content-normative: norms bind us because of intrinsic features of propositional form, not because of any origin in practice. Our recognition is therefore only causally related to these structures—we discover them rather than constitute them. Because causal independence guarantees that practices do not determine validity, the independence horn secures full objectivity. The only residual worry is motivational: why should causally independent structures constrain our reasoning? But content normativity resolves this, since grasping intrinsic structural authority just is itself a rational motivation.

Thus the dilemma dissolves once we see that the independence horn pairs metaphysical independence with content-based authority, leaving logic both objective and normatively compelling.
