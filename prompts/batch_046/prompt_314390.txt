Please describe the most important implications of Hofstadter’s conception of the self-referential symbol I—understood as a strange loop that denotes the total pattern of its substrate rather than any particular token—for whether an artificial system could possess a genuine first-person perspective. Focus specifically on the case where the system’s self-model is implemented not as a globally integrated feedback loop but as a discontinuous, modular subroutine that can be suspended or swapped without altering other cognitive processes. Take a definite, reasoned position on whether substrate-level continuity or pattern-level continuity is the non-negotiable condition for maintaining an I across such interruptions, and defend that position against the objection that biological brains are themselves modular and periodically disrupted by sleep or anesthesia. Do not survey competing views neutrally; argue for the view you take to be correct.
