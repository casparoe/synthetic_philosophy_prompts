I am writing a feature for a science and ideas magazine on the popular overextension of Gödel's incompleteness theorems into domains outside mathematics. Before filing, I need an accuracy audit of a summary given to me by a senior science editor. He argued that Gödel's first theorem proves that in any consistent formal system rich enough for arithmetic, there exist true statements that the system cannot prove; therefore any scientific theory expressed in mathematical form will necessarily contain true physical predictions that lie beyond its reach. He added that the second theorem shows such a system can never demonstrate its own consistency, which several theoretical physicists take to mean that a complete theory of everything is logically impossible, not merely technically out of reach. He then applied the same logic to artificial intelligence, claiming that any consistent ethical framework formalised in algorithmic rules will be unable to verify its own consistency and must therefore harbour hidden biases. Finally, he concluded that human mathematicians must possess non-computational insight, since we can recognise Gödelian truths that formal machines necessarily miss.

Is this a fair representation of what the theorems establish and where they are legitimately applied? Please work through it claim by claim: distinguish what is accurate about Gödel's results for formal arithmetic, identify where the leap to physical predictions or to algorithmic fairness involves an unstated premise about formalisation, assess whether the claim about physicists reflects an actual consensus or an isolated interpretation, and evaluate whether the conclusion about human cognition presents the Lucas–Penrose argument as a direct logical consequence rather than as a contested philosophical extension. I am particularly concerned that the move from unprovable arithmetic sentences to unprovable scientific predictions conflates different senses of "formal system", and that the AI-ethics extension is an original analogy rather than an established result.
