and heres what the student wrote (i cleaned up the handwriting a bit):

Endurantism means the object is fully there at every time, like a statue that is all present on monday and all present on tuesday. Perdurantism, which people like Lewis and Sider defend, says the statue is really a long worm made of monday-part, tuesday-part, etc. The problem of temporary intrinsics was raised by Lewis: if the statue is bent on monday and straight on tuesday, and its the exact same thing both days, then it has both properties and thats a contradiction. The modal-profile strategy fixes this by saying the statue never literally has the shape-properties. Instead it has modal properties—being-bent-in-possibility-one and being-straight-in-possibility-two—which are not contradictory because they are about different possible worlds. So at t1 the first modal property is actualized and at t2 the second is. This saves endurantism because there is only one object with one set of modal properties, no temporal parts needed. Actually this is pretty close to Siders stage theory, since in both cases you have these little slice-like entities (modal actualizations) that are connected by relations rather than being parts of a big worm. So the reply works and perdurantism is just an unnecessary extra layer.
