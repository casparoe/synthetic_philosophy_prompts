For a philosophy assignment, please write a parody End User License Agreement—about 650 words, formatted as numbered clauses—for a fictional software suite named “Veritas” that claims to resolve self-referential truth-claims. Adopt deadpan corporate legalese throughout, as though drafted by lawyers who have fully internalized competing philosophical theories. The clauses should quietly encode real positions on the Liar paradox: a hierarchical licensing restriction echoing Tarski’s language-level distinction; a “partial activation” clause reflecting Kripke’s fixed-point construction; an optional “dialetheist mode” that permits true contradictions but voids all warranties; and a deflationary clause denying that the software manages any substantive property called “truth” at all. The satire should treat serious theoretical disagreements as bureaucratic feature disputes, so that someone familiar with the literature recognizes each clause as mapping onto an actual commitment rather than a caricature. Include one invented quotation from a “concerned” correspondence-theory customer-service representative. After the agreement, append a brief author’s note (no more than 150 words) indicating where the parody takes liberties with the actual theories—for instance, whether representing Kripke’s construction as a commercial licensing tier misstates its purpose—and clearly separating what is settled (that naive, unrestricted truth theories generate contradiction via the Liar) from what remains genuinely contested (whether hierarchical restrictions, paraconsistent acceptance, or deflationary dissolution provide the best response). Do not present any single theory as the undisputed consensus, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
