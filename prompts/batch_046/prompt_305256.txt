As a novelist who has spent the last decade writing about characters suspended between ambition and renunciation, I am preparing a philosophical memoir and want a consistency audit before I commit these views to print. Here is my cluster: (1) I hold a tranquilist account of well-being—the only intrinsic goods are the absence of suffering and the cessation of craving; positive pleasures, achievements, and aesthetic triumphs possess value solely as temporary interruptions of negative states. (2) I believe that narrative fiction is structurally dependent on what I call irreducible teleological tension: protagonists must possess desires that generate further desires upon satisfaction, and a fully tranquil character becomes narratively inert, not a subject but a landscape. (3) Morally, I think social institutions ought to be arranged to minimize aggregate craving and suffering, including through limits on competitive economic and artistic production that generate widespread anxiety. (4) I maintain that consciousness is not essentially constituted by craving; a mind in complete tranquility retains full phenomenal awareness, lacking only the temporal projection of future lack. (5) I also hold that the craving fiction induces in its readers is vicarious craving—phenomenologically vivid but metaphysically second-order, lacking the self-referential urgency that makes real-world desire harmful—which permits me, as an author, to cultivate intense emotional turbulence in my work without moral contradiction. (6) Finally, I think that if global tranquility were achieved, the conditions for meaningful art would vanish, but this is not a moral loss because art possesses only instrumental value.

Please examine these for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision in each case? I am particularly uncertain whether the notion of vicarious craving in (5) actually saves (3) and (2) from undermining each other, and whether (4) introduces an unexpected problem for (2). Rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what commitment must be modified or abandoned, and tell me whether a single revision exists that resolves the most conflicts without destroying the core tranquilist view in (1).
