I sit on the acquisitions board for our university's museum of mind and perception. We've been offered a permanent immersive installtion called 'The Qualia Exchange' — sensory-deprivation suits that claim to let visitors 'experience inverted spectra' via cross-modal feedback loops. The artist's statement cites Dennett's old quining-qualia arguement and a newer Chase-Sanborn line that phenomenal properties are incoherent posits, but then says the piece proves you can still 'run the machinery' artistically. Half our board wants to take it: they say even if qualia are incoherant, the installation makes that debate visceral for visitors. The other half wants to reject it on principle — they say a university museum shouldn't host work premised on a philosophical mistake, and that visitors will leave thinking inverted spectra are real possibilities. We vote Thursday. Draft the decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with an actionable recommendation and a concrete time frame if we need changes. Deal seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side: does incoherence actually undermine the art, or can art operate with incoherent concepts? Also mention whether there's reputational risk if philosophers later say we endorsed pseudoscience. Write it like a document we can circulate, not personal advice.
