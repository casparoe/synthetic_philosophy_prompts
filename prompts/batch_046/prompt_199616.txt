I'm supervising a postgraduate seminar on philosophical logic and scientific practice, and I want you to turn the Kripkean rule-following considerations — specifically the sceptical challenge that there is no private fact of the matter about which rule one is following, and the communal-response idea that correctness conditions depend on shared agreement within a practice — into a reusable adjudication protocol for a journal referee who must decide whether authors of an empirical study have genuinely followed their pre-registered analysis plan or have instead offered a post-hoc rationalisation that privately reinterprets the rule. The protocol should be a step-by-step procedure (no more than six stages) that a non-specialist referee can apply within an hour, specifying: the documentary checks to run first; the distinction between justifications that appeal to shared disciplinary norms versus private intuitions about what the rule "really meant"; the point at which further evidence cannot resolve the sceptical gap; and the explicit judgement call the referee must sign off on. Apply it to three hypothetical submissions: (i) a minor deviation justified by an unanticipated data-collection error that the authors document transparently; (ii) a substantial analytical change defended solely by the lead author's claim that the original rule would have permitted this interpretation in the present context; and (iii) a case where the relevant disciplinary community is itself split on how to read the pre-registration guideline. For each, state what the protocol delivers and whether the referee should accept it confidently or treat it as genuinely indeterminate. Conclude by saying precisely what the protocol can never establish — for instance, a private mental fact about the authors' intentions — and how that limitation ought to be reported.
