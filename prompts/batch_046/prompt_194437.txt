I was arguing with my roommate last night about whether a smart speaker could ever count as 'a real person' if the software got advanced enough, and we kept going in circles. Every time I said it could do something people do — understand questions, learn your preferences, even tell a joke — he found some other feature to say it was missing. First it was no physical body, then no feelings, then when I asked how he knew that he said well it wasn't born so it doesn't have the right kind of awareness. It felt like he was just moving the fence every time I caught up to it, and the category of 'real people' was getting emptier and emptier, almost like he was protecting it by stripping away properties one by one.

That reminded me of something I half-remembered from a podcast about old Chinese philosophers, maybe the School of Names, where someone argued a white horse isn't a horse because white is a color and horse is a shape. Is there an actual name for this kind of move, where you keep peeling off one property after another to keep something outside a category? I'm not asking about essentialism in general — I know people talk about that — but this specific pattern where each defeated boundary spawns a new, more hidden one. Does it connect to that white horse dialogue, or is it a seperate failure mode? And if there's no term for exactly this, what should I call it?
