Here are my gut verdicts on whether something counts as a genuine moral dilemma — I mean a situation where you've got two obligations that both survive defeat and you can't meet both, so there's real moral residue left over no matter what you do. Case one: surgeon with one heart, two patients, picks arbitrarily. Not a dilemma — just scarcity, she hasn't actually wronged anyone. Case two: soldier gets ambiguous drone footage, strikes, civilians die. That IS one — duty to obey and duty to protect both stay binding, and he should feel agent-regret either way. Case three: whistleblower breaks an NDA to expose dangerous contamination. Not a dilemma — the contractual promise just doesn't generate a moral weight that can conflict with stopping serious harm. Case four: widow promised dying husband never to remarry, years later there's a partner who'd genuinely help her kids, but she still feels the vow binds her. I think that's a real dilemma — both commitments are alive and neither clearly defeats the other. Case five: ship captain caused the flooding by her own navigation error, can only save one compartment of passengers. Also a real one — her fault doesn't dissolve the dual obligation to rescue, so she's genuinely torn. Is there a single principle that captures all five, or do I have to overturn one of my calls?
