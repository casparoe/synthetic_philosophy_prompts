I'm currently taking an upper-division seminar on Borges and the philosophy of time, and we've been working through Borges's 1947 essay "A New Refutation of Time" alongside older texts that shaped his thinking. In that essay, Borges denies temporal succession altogether, arguing that the present is infinitely divisible and therefore lacks any real duration, which he takes to dissolve the problem of personal identity across moments—if there is no genuine passage, there is no need to explain how one remains the same self from one instant to the next. Our instructor then assigned Book XI of Augustine's Confessions, specifically the well-known section where Augustine analyzes time as a kind of distention of the soul: past and future do not exist as independent objects, but only through the present consciousness that remembers what has passed and anticipates what is to come. He insists that measuring any interval of time requires a present attention that holds together what has already ceased to be and what has not yet arrived, so that time is real only insofar as the soul actively stretches itself across these non-existent dimensions.

I'm struggling to understand exactly how these two accounts relate, and in particular whether they are compatible at the point where Borges's denial of succession meets Augustine's psychology of temporal measurement. Augustine seems to preserve the reality of time by grounding it in the soul's present activity, whereas Borges seems to deny that there could be any present activity with sufficient duration to perform this work, since he treats every present moment as an abstraction without extension. The specific difficulty I'm stuck on concerns personal identity: Borges's argument dissolves the need for narrative continuity across time, yet Augustine appears to depend on a continuous self that stretches from past to future precisely in order to carry out the measurement of time. So is Augustine's account secretly committed to the kind of temporal continuity that Borges rejects? Or could Borges's view be read as a radicalization of Augustine's insight—that time exists only in consciousness—taken so far that consciousness itself collapses into an eternal present with no distention at all?

Could you help me understand Augustine's passage in light of this tension? In particular, I'd like to know whether Borges's claim that time is ultimately illusory is compatible with Augustine's claim that the soul's present attention makes time measurable, and if they conflict, which element of Augustine's analysis—the distention of the soul, the reliance on present attention, or the implicit commitment to a continuous measuring self—is the point of fracture. Please explain in American English, and I'd also appreciate your thoughts on how this tension applies to a case I haven't fully worked out: what would Borges likely say about Augustine's prayer in the same book, where he asks God to grant him knowledge of the true nature of the present moment, if Borges believes that very notion of a present with knowable nature is itself the root of the illusion?
