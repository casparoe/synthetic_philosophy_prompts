Student answer:
Hofstadter thinks concepts live in a multidimensional space where each axis is a knob—size, speed, emotional tone, etc. Creativity isn't magic; it's adjusting those knobs like on a synthesizer. Now, conceptual-engineering folks talk about meta-knobs: they don't change the direct properties of a concept but change how the other knobs interact. I believe Hofstadter mentions these in his later writing on analogical reasoning, saying they let you restructure the space from within. So take Einstein's spacetime: it's not adding a brand-new dimension, it's turning the "geometry of space" knob way past Newton's range, probably mediated by a meta-knob that relaxes Euclidean constraints.

That means radical innovation is still just interpolation, only now it's second-order. There's no real "outside" to the space; restructuring is another form of knob-twiddling. So the model actually covers everything, including what looks like discontinuous leaps.
