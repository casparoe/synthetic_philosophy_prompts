Please argue that Hannah Arendt's separation of factual truth from rational or philosophical truth, together with her claim that factual reality is sustained by plural testimony and institutional record rather than by coercive proof, generates an internal tension for democratic politics: if the preservation of fact depends primarily on the health of shared epistemic practices rather than on punishing individual deceivers, then treating political lying mainly as a crime to be sanctioned may actually erode the very conditions of witness and record-keeping that make factual truth resilient. In developing this argument, explain how an Arendtian politics of action and appearance might prioritize the maintenance of public archives and deliberative spaces over punitive accountability, and identify the strongest objection—that without sanctions, strategic deception will colonize those same spaces.
