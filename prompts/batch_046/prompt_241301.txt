For a graduate seminar on skepticism and selfhood I am drafting a brief philosophical profile, and I want an audit before I present it. Here is my current package. First, I read Zhuangzi's butterfly passage as advancing a transformation thesis rather than Cartesian dream skepticism: the self is not a fixed substance but a dynamic pattern (hua) that can genuinely become something else—Zhuang Zhou can become the butterfly—without leaving any underlying substrate or core self behind. Second, I hold a process-realist ontology: there are no enduring substances, only relatively stable configurations of activity and experience, so being Zhuang Zhou and being a butterfly name temporary patterns rather than essences. Third, I think personal identity requires narrative continuity—the coherence of an autobiographical thread linking memory, intention, and self-attribution—rather than phenomenal continuity; without that thread there is no fact about which future experiences belong to me. Fourth, I take the dream to show that narrative continuity is broken (the butterfly lacks Zhuang Zhou's memories and projects) while phenomenal continuity is seamlessly preserved, which demonstrates that phenomenal flow is insufficient for identity and supports the claim that transformation is a real change of subject. Fifth, I reject the disjunctive account of waking and dreaming: there is no intrinsic phenomenal marker distinguishing them, since both involve representational content, affective valence, and first-person presence; the difference lies only in causal embedding and practical authority. Sixth, I want to maintain that global dream skepticism is self-undermining, because the skeptical argument itself depends on conceptual resources acquired in causally embedded, action-guiding contexts—though this undermines only global doubt, not local transformation claims.

The set feels mostly coherent, but I am unsure whether I am smuggling in incompatible motivations. There seems to be a serious pull between the transformation thesis and the narrative-continuity criterion: if identity requires narrative continuity and that continuity is broken in the dream, then Zhuang Zhou does not survive as the butterfly, yet the transformation thesis seems to require that he genuinely becomes it. There is also an apparent tension between rejecting any intrinsic waking-dreaming distinction and insisting on a practical asymmetry—if there is no internal marker, it is unclear how causal embedding alone can ground the authority of waking beliefs without collapsing back into a metaphysical disjunction. Finally, I worry that my process ontology undermines the very idea of a narrative bearer: if there are no enduring substances, what exactly sustains the autobiographical thread? I suspect one of these dissolves on inspection, but I cannot tell which. Please audit these commitments, rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each pair whether the conflict is genuine or only apparent, specify the cheapest revision and its cost, and then say which single change would clean up the most.
