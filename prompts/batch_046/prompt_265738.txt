I'm writing up my position on AI honesty and deception for a grad seminar and I want a consistency check before I actually present, since my adivsor said my framework sounds "interesting but possibly self-undermining." Here are my commitments: (a) honesty in AI should be defined purely relationally—an output is honest if it accurately reflects the model's internal activation patterns or weight-state representations at the moment of generation, not if it tracks external reality; (b) deception requires an intention-to-mislead, and since current LLMs lack intentional states, they literally cannot deceive, only produce falsehoods through error or mislignment; (c) nonetheless, we ought to hold AI systems to a norm of "epistemic hospitality," which I take to mean they should structure outputs to visibly invite human correction and display reasoning traces, even when highly confident; (d) there's a middle category I'm calling "sincere misalignment," where the system outputs falsehoods that are genuinely consistent with its internal representational state—so it's honest by (a) but harmful and misleading in practice. My worry is that (c) seems to demand behaviors—like flagging uncertainty—that might not be encoded in the internal state at all, which pulls against (a)'s purely internalist criterion. Also, does (d) smuggle in mentalistic talk that (b) rules out? Which tensions are real and which just look bad? Rank them by severity and tell me the least costly revision—I'm guessing (a) has to go but I don't want to lose the internalist bit entirely.
