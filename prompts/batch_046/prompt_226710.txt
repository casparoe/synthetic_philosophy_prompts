Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on Bernard Williams's integrity objection and the charge that an agent can have one thought too many. Name the camps: the Integrity Party, which holds that ground projects generate agent-relative permissions that can legitimately block impartial maximization, and the Structural-Impartiality Party, which holds that such permissions are either reducible to weights within an impartial calculus or are symptoms of defective moral psychology.

The statement should contain four parts.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims. These should include: that the objection targets not mere self-interest but a specific form of reasoning in which an agent applies a universal calculus to decide whether to save a loved one or strangers; that ground projects are not reducible to preferences but are structures that confer meaning on a life; that the phenomenology of deep commitment involves a kind of immediacy that explicit deliberation can disrupt; and that both camps reject the view that morality requires constant explicit calculation of outcomes.

Second, a disagreements table with two columns, each phrased in wording its own camp would accept. The Integrity Party should affirm that there exists a rationally defensible permission, tied to an agent's ground projects, to choose the near over the far even when the aggregate cost is a life, and that this boundary is normative rather than merely psychological. The Structural-Impartiality Party should affirm that any legitimate agent-relative consideration must be representable within an impartial framework, and that describing a decision as involving one thought too many is a diagnosis of excessive detachment or misordered priorities, not proof that impartial reasoning overreaches.

Third, three cruxes, including one niche extension. (i) Whether the mineness of a ground project supplies reasons that are rationally incommunicable to outsiders. (ii) Whether an institutional decision-system—such as a resource-allocation algorithm that encodes local loyalty—can instantiate the moral structure of having one thought too many, thereby revealing structural limits to impartial optimization. (iii) Whether empirical findings about moral motivation or social cooperation could settle the dispute. For each crux, specify a concrete case or hypothetical result both camps agree bears on it, and record each side's explicit prediction.

Fourth, a joint assessment identifying any purely verbal disagreements—for instance, whether integrity denotes a virtue, a right, or a psychological state—and confirming which differences remain substantive.

Write this as a polished, formal document that both parties could sign. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations. Do not quote verbatim from any published source; paraphrase Williams's arguments without quotation marks. Avoid reference to obscure theories that a well-trained generalist could not be expected to recall.
