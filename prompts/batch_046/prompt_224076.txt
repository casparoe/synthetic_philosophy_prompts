In my advanced ethics seminar, a fellow student recently tried to explain what she called "lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism" to a group of us who were more familiar with standard act-utilitarian frameworks. She presented it as a view that does not merely prioritize suffering reduction over pleasure maximization, but treats pleasure and pain as phenomenologically incommensurable: suffering possesses what she described as a disintegrative character that breaks down experiential coherence, whereas pleasure is only integrative and therefore lacks comparable moral weight. From this, she argued, defenders derive a lexical barrier—no aggregate of pleasure, regardless of magnitude, can ever justify, offset, or outweigh even a minimal quantum of suffering—while also rejecting any cardinal welfare calculus that would treat them as points on a shared scale. She added that because the theory denies a common metric altogether, it is structurally immune to the classic sacrificial counterexample in which one person is harmed for the greater good of many, since that objection presupposes the very commensurability the view rejects. I found the account compelling but worry she may have conflated a normative priority claim with a stronger metaphysical claim about measurement, and I am unsure whether the immunity she asserts really follows.

Could you audit this summary for fairness? Please separate what is accurate to the lexical-threshold position, what is a plausible simplification or a conflation with related asymmetry theses, and what is a misrepresentation—particularly regarding whether the view genuinely denies all quantitative comparison between pleasure and pain or only denies that pleasure can cross a moral threshold to compensate for suffering. I also want to know whether her conclusion about structural immunity to sacrificial objections survives once the summary is corrected: does the denial of commensurability, if it is indeed part of the view, actually block the standard counterexample, or does the objection re-emerge in a different form?
