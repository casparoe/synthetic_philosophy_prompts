I am developing a conceptual framework that I call reflexive boundary ascription for the democratic boundary problem. Rather than treating membership in the demos as fixed by territory, citizenship, or the all-affected principle, the framework proposes a recursive procedure: the current demos establishes the epistemic conditions for membership, but those conditions must themselves be revisable by anyone who satisfies them. This generates a bootstrapping problem that standard accounts—stakeholder, territorial, or co-legislative—do not directly address.

Assuming this framing is coherent, what are the principal open conceptual questions that must be resolved before it could guide institutional design? I am trying to allocate about twelve months of research time, so I need a defended prioritisation rather than a survey. Specifically: does the reflexivity necessarily entail a vicious circularity in membership determination, or can it be stabilised by a provisional dominance relation among competing boundary criteria? How should we weigh epistemic competence against affected interests when the two come apart under recursive revision? And which of these problems is genuinely upstream—meaning that its resolution would restructure the others—while which are largely taxonomic?

Please rank the questions, explain the dependency relations between them, and indicate what kind of conceptual or empirical result over the next year would cause you to reorder them. I am not looking for a bibliography; I want to know which lines of work deserve the limited effort and why.
