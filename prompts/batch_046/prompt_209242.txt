I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with an unnamed metaphysician who defends restricted constitutionalism about artifacts—specifically, the claim that a statue and the clay that constitutes it are not identical, and that the difference is not merely shape but a property she calls "diachronic artistic integrity": a continuous creative-historical trajectory linking the object to its original making. On her view, the relation is asymmetrical—the clay can survive being reshaped into a statue, but if the statue is melted down and the same clay is later remolded into an indistinguishable form, the result is only a replica, because the integrity-chain has been broken. She insists this integrity condition is metaphysically primitive, not reducible to physical continuity, intentional states of the artist, or social practices of attribution. I'm looking for eight to ten interview questions that press well beyond restating this position. For each question, supply a follow-up keyed to her most likely reply—particularly the move that integrity is primitive. Frame the entire sequence with a definite critical stance: the questions should argue, by progressive pressure, that her primitive-integrity postulate either collapses into familiar origin essentialism or cannot explain why restored antiques are treated differently from perfect replicas, rather than neutrally laying out theoretical options. Take a committed position in the framing and in the follow-ups; do not ask for reading lists, citations, or bibliographies.
