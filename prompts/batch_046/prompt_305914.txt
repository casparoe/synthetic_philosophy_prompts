Here's an argument my freind cooked up and now I'm stuck in it. (1) Moral reasons have to be generalizable: if I have reason to help X because of property P, then anyone with P should be helped by anyone who can. (2) "Being my compatriot" is indexical — it depends on who I am — so it can't be a generalizable reason. (3) Therefore I have no special reason to help compatriots over strangers. (4) But "being my sister" is also indexical, so by the same move family obligations disappear. (5) My freind says we can block this by saying family ties are "counterfactualy robust" — they survive across worlds where I have different citizenship — while compatriot ties don't. (6) So family gets a pass on generalizability but nationality doesn't. (7) But democratic obligations like paying taxes or obeying law depend on being "this citizen" in "this state," and those survive counterfactual shifts in family structure but not in citizenship. (8) So if counterfactual robustness saves family, it should save democratic obligations too, which means compatriot claims are back in. (9) Yet my freind wants to keep (3) and reject (8). I think something slipped between (5) and (8) — maybe robustness isn't the right filter — but I can't name the slip. Which step do you reject, and what does giving it up cost? I'd like to keep (4) from being false if I can, since I do think family is special.
