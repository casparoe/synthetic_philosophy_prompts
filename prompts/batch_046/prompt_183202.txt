I'm preparing a single seventy-five-minute seminar for advanced undergraduates in political philosophy on "lost causes" as a structural category of national myth—specifically how military defeat is converted into durable moral-political identity. Rather than treating the Confederacy, Masada, the Battle of Kosovo, and Dunkirk as four parallel anecdotes, I want to build the session around one conceptual distinction: defeat retrospectively framed as voluntary sacrifice for collective purity versus defeat framed as strategic endurance that enabled future survival. I need a teaching plan, not a script. Take a definite position: identify the clearest exemplar of the purity-sacrifice framing and argue for whether Kosovo belongs on the sacrifice side or the endurance side, with brief reasons.
