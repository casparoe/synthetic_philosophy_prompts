I was appointed three weeks ago to serve on our municipality's Civic Memory and Public Symbolism Board, and I have been asked to draft the board's formal recommendation—due by next Friday—on whether to retain the Columbus monument and its associated civic lecture series in the central plaza. The question became personally pressing after I attended a public hearing where indigenous community representatives described the monument's continued presence as an active institutional harm, while several advisory historians invoked the 'man of his time' defence, arguing that retrospective condemnation applies norms Columbus could not have accessed. Rather than framing our choice as a simple binary between removal and preservation, I want the memo to turn on a conceptual distinction I have been working through: 'synchronic moral accounting,' which evaluates an agent strictly by the normative possibilities available within their own historical horizon, versus 'diachronic institutional endorsement,' which asks whether maintaining a public symbol today implicitly authorizes that figure's legacy as part of our present civic identity. The recommendation should propose a concrete course of action—whether retention with substantive transformation, relocation with contextual framing, or another structured option—together with a clear implementation timeline, and it must treat the strongest version of the 'man of his time' argument not as a crude excuse but as a serious claim about the non-transferability of moral blame across incommensurable moral economies.

At the same time, the memo must examine whether that defence survives once we separate the evaluation of a historical agent from the symbolic work a monument performs in the present, and it should address whether applying synchronic accounting to Columbus while demanding diachronic accountability for our own institutional choices introduces an asymmetry we can justify. Please present this as a formal board document with an unambiguous bottom line that the committee could vote on, including an honest account of the weightiest consideration favoring the option you reject. Finally, please state how confident you are in the reasoning that supports your recommendation, and explicitly identify the specific point—whether in the synchronic/diachronic distinction itself or in its application to this case—where your analysis is least certain and why.
