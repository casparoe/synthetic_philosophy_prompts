I'm designing a single ninety-minute seminar for first-year comparative-philosophy students who have just finished a unit on Aristotelian substance and accident but have not yet encountered pre-Qin Chinese logic. My aim is not to present Gongsun Long's white-horse exchange as a quaint sophism, but to use it to open the problem of how compound names relate to the objects they denote—a central concern of the School of Names (Mingjia).
