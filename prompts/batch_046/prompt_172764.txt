I am an undergraduate student in an advanced seminar on epistemic value, and I am helping to prepare the Q&A for a departmental colloquium with a visiting speaker, Dr. Elena Voss, who defends a specific answer to Plato's Meno problem that I want to make sure we probe correctly. Her central claim is that knowledge is not more valuable than true belief in virtue of greater stability, of credit for intellectual achievement, or of any intrinsic property of the mental state itself; rather, its surplus value is purely relational and derives from what she calls the transfer condition. On her view, knowledge alone can be transmitted from one agent to another without forcing the recipient to independently reproduce the original justification, whereas true belief always requires the recipient to fall back on first-order verification. She takes this to explain why knowledge carries a distinctive social-epistemic value — it supports coordination, delegation, and testimony — even though, in solitary practical reasoning, true belief guides action just as well.

Please draft eight to ten formal interview questions that do not ask her to restate the view in general terms. I want at least one question that presses her directly on the Platonic premise that true belief and knowledge guide action with equal success: does her transfer condition actually solve the original Meno problem, or does it shift the topic from individual practical efficacy to collective coordination? I want at least one question that anticipates her likely response to the reliabilist swamping problem — she will probably argue that reliability is swamped as a value-maker unless it is also reflectively accessible — followed immediately by a sharp follow-up asking whether that reply collapses her position into a form of internalism about justification. I want one question about testimonial knowledge acquired without understanding: if a non-expert accepts a mathematician's testimony about a complex proof she cannot follow, does she possess knowledge in Voss's sense, or only true belief? And if only true belief, does that entail that experts cannot genuinely transmit knowledge to laypersons? I want one question comparing her view to the value of understanding: understanding also seems transmissible in testimony, so why privilege knowledge specifically?

Finally, I want one question built around a hypothetical scenario we discussed in our seminar, which I will call credal erosion. In this case, an agent receives a well-justified belief through reliable testimony, retains it for years through habit and social reinforcement, but gradually loses all access to the original justification. On Voss's account, does this belief degrade from knowledge into mere true belief, or can it retain knowledge-status in virtue of some structural feature of its transmission history? For each of the questions above, please include a brief, keyed follow-up that targets the answer she is most likely to give. The tone should suit a philosophy colloquium: precise, adversarial but respectful, and focused on the actual structure of her argument rather than on biographical or generic questions.
