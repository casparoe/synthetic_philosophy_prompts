Consider the track record of a philosopher of physics, Prof. L, on Einstein’s hole argument. In Case 1—a vacuum region enclosed by a Cauchy surface, with only the metric present inside—she judges that general relativity fails to determine the metric inside: applying a smooth diffeomorphism that fixes the boundary yields a genuinely distinct physical possibility, so determinism is undermined. In Case 2—the same geometry, but with a single point-particle worldline running through the hole—she reverses herself: the worldline anchors the points, so the two diffeomorphically related descriptions represent the same world and determinism holds. In Case 3—the hole filled with a homogeneous perfect fluid of uniform density and pressure—she returns to the first verdict: the fluid is featureless, so point-permutations inside still generate physically distinct states. In Case 4—two point-particle worldlines separated by a fixed proper distance inside the hole—she again rules that determinism is restored; the pair supplies enough relational structure to eliminate the ambiguity.

The new case, Case 5, is this: inside the hole there is only a single, smooth, closed filament of uniform tension—like a cosmic string—arranged in a figure-eight knot. There are no endpoints, no localized energy concentrations, no other matter, and vacuum everywhere else. The knot type is preserved by any smooth deformation, but the specific embedding (position, orientation, and smooth shape within that knot class) can vary continuously inside the region.

Predict Prof. L’s verdict on whether determinism is undermined in Case 5, identify which of Cases 1–4 is the closest analog, and articulate the two most plausible principles that jointly explain her first four rulings but diverge here. For each principle, say what verdict it yields for Case 5. Then specify the further case that would best distinguish the principles—ideally one that isolates whether only point-worldlines can restore determinism, or one in which the two rules predict opposite outcomes for the same reason. Keep your response to about 300 words; open with a one-sentence bottom line stating your predicted verdict, the closest analog, and the principle you take her to be running.
