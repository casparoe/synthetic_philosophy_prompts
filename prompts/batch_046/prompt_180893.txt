Following a prolonged and somewhat heated argument with a friend last evening about whether an artist's moral failings can diminish the value of the work itself, I have been trying to determine whether his summary of a view he called contextual separation is fair. He described the position as holding that a work's aesthetic merits should be evaluated independently of its creator's biography, but only on the condition that the creator's moral flaws have not been internalized within the work's formal structure — for instance, through compositional or narrative choices that systematically dehumanize a subject. When the flaw remains external to the form, aesthetic admiration is said to remain intact; when it is embedded in the form, the aesthetic value collapses into the ethical defect. He then claimed that this framework is essentially Roland Barthes's argument about the irrelevance of authorial intention to interpretation, translated from the domain of meaning into the domain of ethics, with the result that a work is never morally owned by its maker in any relevant sense. From this he drew the practical conclusion that, since immoral artists typically formalize their vices within their art, we ought in practice to reject most works created by morally compromised individuals. I have a strong suspicion that he has conflated interpretive autonomy with ethical evaluation, that he overstates how frequently moral defects actually permeate formal structure, and that his practical conclusion does not follow from the theoretical premises, yet I am unsure exactly where the misrepresentations lie. Is his characterization of contextual separation accurate as a philosophical position, or does it smuggle in an overstatement? Is it legitimate to associate this ethical separation with Barthes's argument about interpretation? And does his conclusion — that we should broadly reject art by immoral artists — actually derive from the view he describes, or does it depend on an empirical claim about formalization that the position itself does not entail? Please separate what is a faithful reconstruction, what is a standard but misleading simplification, and what is simply wrong, and indicate whether the practical conclusion survives once the summary is corrected.
