Construct a realistic, non-science-fictional thought experiment that isolates whether compatriots have special moral claims beyond cosmopolitan distributive justice, engineered so that every morally relevant variable except citizenship is inverted against the usual nationalist expectation: one life-saving treatment can be given to either a lifelong citizen who has never contributed to state institutions, or to a non-citizen who has consistently paid taxes to those same institutions through transnational employment, with both facing identical need and neither having personal ties to decision-makers. Present your answer as exactly five numbered items, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. Item 1 should describe the concrete situation in everyday terms without naming nationalism, cosmopolitanism, or the underlying philosophical problem. Item 2 should state the allocation decision at stake, specify the conditions of reciprocity, membership, and equal need, and state the opposing verdicts a membership-based nationalist and a strict cosmopolitan would reach. Item 3 should identify the precise feature that isolates citizenship from contribution, explain why removing it collapses the distinction between the two theories, and note what the scenario is designed to show. Item 4 should explain how a reciprocity-based defender of compatriot priority would attack the case—particularly by challenging the tax-payment equivalence—and why that attack either succeeds or fails within the stipulated conditions. Item 5 should conclude with what, if anything, remains defensible about giving weight to compatriot status once contribution-based reciprocity has been disarmed in this setting.
