Hey — I'm a retired field biologist (spent thirty years counting wading birds in mud) who got bored and started reading philosophy books. I now sit on our university's Instutional Research Integrity and Recognition Panel, which sounds grander than it is. We've got a case that's driving me crazy and I need a real memo, not generic life advice.

There's a junior ecologist — call her Dr. Chen — who found a hidden sampling bias in a big shared dataset our whole dept relies on. Instead of just emailing a warning, she rebuilt the corrected version herself, released it anonymously, and delayed her own tenure-track paper by eight months to finish it. Some panel members want to award her the "Beyond Duty" medal, defined for acts that are "morally good but not morally required." Others say correcting bad science is literally part of the job, so it's obligatory, not supererogatory — they worry giving the medal cheapens basic integrity.

Please act as a professional philosopher of science with expertise in institutional ethics. Think carefully and systematically. Before drafting, do a quick brainstorm of the conceptual problem, especially the space between role-based obligations and agent-neutral moral demands — I sometimes think of this as "professional supererogation," though I'm not sure that holds up. Don't end on "it depends" — I need an actual bottom line: approve, reject, or approve with conditions. And please, no citation lists or reading lists; I can't look them up and I want reasoning I can follow offline.

Please write the memo with these sections, in this order, with rough lengths:

Executive bottom line: 2–3 sentences stating the reccomendation.
The conceptual knot: about 150 words on why applying supererogation to scientific roles is tricky.
Case for awarding the medal: about 200 words.
The strongest honest objection: about 150 words, treating the "it's just her job" argument seriously.
Verdict and reasoning: about 250 words defending your call, including whether "professional supererogation" is a coherent category here.
If we approve — conditions: about 100 words on practical safeguards (e.g., how we frame the citation so it doesn't look like we're excusing minimal compliance elsewhere).

Keep it formal enough that the board can act on it, but don't hide behind jargon. Thanks!
