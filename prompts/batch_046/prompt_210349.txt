I serve on the Membership and Governance Committee of the Kijani Watershed Cooperative, a bioregional institution that combines ecological stewardship with participatory research governance. Our charter currently defines members as discrete individuals who hold autonomous rights, bear individuated liability, and vote separately. A collective of twelve researchers and local stewards—who have cohabited the upper basin for fifteen years, share all resources without private ownership, and make decisions through a consensus process that deliberately refuses to attribute positions to named individuals—has applied for admission not as twelve separate persons but as a single relational unit. They ground their request in an Ubuntu-informed conception of personhood: they hold that individual identity is constituted through reciprocal care and communal belonging rather than possessed by a pre-social substance, and they argue that forcing them to register as separate voters and liability-bearers would fracture the very relational structure that makes their stewardship effective. Our legal counsel objects that collective membership without individuated consent and accountability creates unassignable liability, undermines democratic transparency, and conflicts with state nonprofit law. The committee must decide by next Thursday whether to amend our charter to recognize “relational personhood” as a basis for collective membership, to maintain the individualist framework and require separate applications, or to approve a hybrid arrangement with specific conditions.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action with an implementation timeline if appropriate. The reasoning should treat the applicants’ Ubuntu-based argument seriously—specifically, the claim that individuating them destroys their functional and moral identity—while giving an honest account of the strongest liberal-individualist counter-consideration, particularly regarding liability, informed consent, and democratic accountability. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. The opinion should be self-contained and should avoid reducing the dispute to a generic “East versus West” framing; instead, address the specific institutional stakes of recognizing relational personhood within our membership rules.
