Draft the joint statement that a frequentist reformist camp and a Bayesian structuralist camp should have produced after a sustained adversarial collaboration on the replication crisis in psychology and biomedicine.

The frequentist camp holds that null-hypothesis significance testing and p-values remain sound inferential tools whose recent failures are explained by institutional pathologies—underpowered designs, undisclosed researcher degrees of freedom, publication bias, and p-hacking—rather than by any internal logical flaw; they maintain that rigorous pre-registration, adequately powered samples, multiverse analyses, and stricter error-rate control can restore frequentist practice without abandoning its framework.

The Bayesian camp holds that the crisis exposes a structural limitation in error-statistical reasoning itself: binary reject-or-retain decisions discard continuous evidential information, p-values conflate sample size with effect magnitude, and frequentist procedures provide no coherent mechanism for updating beliefs across studies or quantifying support for null hypotheses; they advocate replacing NHST with explicit hierarchical Bayesian modeling, transparent prior specification, and continuous belief revision.

The document must contain the following, in this order:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims—precise statements both camps can sign. Include shared empirical observations about the scope of replication failures in social and biomedical sciences; shared formal descriptions of what p-values and Bayes factors actually compute; agreement on the existence and taxonomy of researcher degrees of freedom; and any shared normative commitments, such as the principle that scientific inference should be corrigible and that hidden analytic flexibility undermines it.

2. A disagreements table. For each camp, formulate its position in wording that camp itself would accept. Separate genuine disagreements from verbal ones; if any portion of the dispute turns out to concern only the definition of "evidence," "probability," or "objectivity" without differing in predictive content, identify that portion explicitly and explain why it is verbal rather than substantive.

3. The cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would cause at least one camp to update its view substantially. One crux must address the reflexivity problem in human-subjects research: whether specifying a statistical model (whether a null sampling distribution or a hierarchical prior) is itself an intervention that can alter the data-generating process, thereby undermining the assumption of a stable, study-independent sampling distribution. A second crux must address whether pre-registered frequentist multiverse analyses genuinely eliminate analytic flexibility or merely relocate that flexibility into the design of the multiverse space and the choice of summary statistics.

4. For each crux, specify a concrete hypothetical study, empirical pattern, or argumentative result that both camps agree would bear on it, and record each camp's explicit prediction in advance. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for further reading.

Write in a formal, precise register appropriate for a long-form rationalist forum publication, treating both camps as intellectually serious and separating disagreements about words from disagreements about the world.
