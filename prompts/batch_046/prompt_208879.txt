I'm helping organize the final session of a reading group on Fear and Trembling, and we've reached an impasse that I think only form can express rather than solve: Johannes de Silentio's insistence that Abraham's silence is not strategic reticence but an ontological breakdown—once he occupies the sphere of the absolute, the language of the ethical universal, the shared we that judges fathers, no longer applies to him, not because he is hiding a secret but because there is literally no common category in which to speak it. We want a poem for the closing, but it must do philosophical work structurally rather than merely illustrate the story. Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains that must converge in the final quatrain—so that the form itself performs the teleological suspension of the ethical.

The first refrain should be spoken in the voice of the universal ethical order, something along the lines that a father owes his child to the world and to justice, and it should return with slight grammatical mutations that show the universal trying to reclaim its authority. The second refrain should come from Abraham's singular position, something like that he owes the world nothing except this singular call, also shifting slightly with each repetition but never softening into explanation. Between these two voices, and never answered within the poem, must appear a line spoken by Isaac asking after the missing lamb for the offering; this line should interrupt both refrains at structurally predictable moments, so that the repetition is always broken by the gap that makes translation impossible. By the closing couplet, the two refrains must stand side by side with Isaac's unanswered question placed between them, enacting the permanent structural separation rather than any synthesis.

The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with the four problemata can identify which voice belongs to the knight of faith and which to the tragic hero—Agamemnon can still speak to the fleet, Abraham cannot speak to anyone—and should recognize why the interruption prevents any higher mediation. Do not resolve the tension, do not moralize about intervention or obedience, and do not include explanatory notes, citations, or bibliographic references; let the rhyme and repetition carry the argument alone.
