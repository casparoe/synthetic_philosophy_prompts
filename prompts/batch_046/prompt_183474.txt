Write a short story about a municipality that has institutionalized the Deweyan conception of democracy as continuous social inquiry: every policy is treated as a provisional, falsifiable hypothesis about cooperative living, citizens serve on rotating "experience juries" that gather qualitative data on lived outcomes, and no ordinance is permanent because all are subject to revision through reconstructed experience. Center the narrative on a crisis in which the jury discovers that their democratically refined welfare program—optimized over decades through participatory feedback—systematically excludes a minority group whose suffering remains statistically invisible within the community's own metrics of "successful" experience. Have the protagonists confront whether democratic inquiry, having perfected its internal method of correction, can recognize a normative blind spot that its experimental framework has structurally rendered unobservable, or whether reform requires an external moral claim that the Deweyan model explicitly denies. Explore whether the community can reconstruct its own method of inquiry without abandoning the core commitment that democracy is not the rule of the majority but an experimental intelligence testing itself in action.
