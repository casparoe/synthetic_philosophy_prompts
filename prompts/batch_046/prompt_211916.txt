I was having a drink with two coursemates after a seminar on historiography and they got into a right arguemnt about whether the Middle Ages, the Renaissance and the Scientific Revolution are actual things or just later inventions. Maya said they're basically all narratives cooked up afterwards — the Middle Ages was invented by Renaissance humanists who wanted to feel superior, the Renaissance itself is mostly a 19th-century fantasy of individualism, and the Scientific Revolution was a label slapped on by 20th-century philosophers of science to make their discipline look inevitable. She thinks calling them real periods is like treating genres in fiction as natural kinds.

Leo said that was too quick. Even if the names are retroactive, there are real discontinuities in how knowledge was made and checked. He talked about the shift from monastic scriptoria to university faculties, and then to royal societies with peer review — not just ideas changing, but the actual protocols for verifying claims. Then he introduced this idea he called protocol density: measuring a society by how many standardised procedures exist for testing and transmitting knowledge, regardless of the content. He said if you map protocol density across Europe from 1300 to 1700 you get clusters that roughly match the old periods, but they're local and uneven, not universal. He called this polytemporal layering — different regions hit high protocol density at different times, so there's no single global timeline. Maya shot back that he's just smuggling the old periods back in through metrics, and that protocol density was probably higher in Song China or parts of the Islamic world during the same centuries, so his framework is still Eurocentric even if he pretends it's neutral. They went back and forth a bit more — Leo saying material practice has to ground our categories, Maya saying any clustering is an artifact of where historians choosen to look — and then they turned to me and asked me to referee.

I honestly couldn't decide. Say who is more right overall, and flag anything important both of them are missing.
