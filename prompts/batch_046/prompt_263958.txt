I'm trying to identify a specific kind of situation I've noticed in both physical crowds and online groups. In one instance, strangers on a crowded train platform suddenly rearrange themselves—some stepping back, others directing flow with gestures, a few holding doors—so that a bottleneck clears without anyone having decided to organize it. Every participant can honestly say, "I was just adjusting where I stood," and there was no leader, no prior agreement, and no moment when anyone thought "we are doing this together." Yet the outcome is clearly not random individual movement; the adjustments are interdependent, corrective, and oriented toward a shared result.

In another instance, thousands of independent users on an open forum, with no organizational charter, gradually develop an implicit division of labor—some verifying claims, others synthesizing answers, others flagging errors—so that the space produces reliable information. Each user insists they were only pursuing private goals that happened to align. There is no charter, no vote, and no mental representation of the group as acting.

The question is whether this specific configuration—functionally coordinated collective behavior that lacks both a prior shared plan and any retrospective sense that "we intended this"—has an established philosophical name. I know there are discussions of group minds, shared intentions, and collective action, but this seems different: it is too structured to be mere coincidence or summed individual behavior, yet too thin mentally to count as genuine joint intention. Is there a precise term for this kind of "structural jointness without mental jointness," and if not, can you define it by specifying its necessary conditions, its boundary cases, and what separates it from both accidental convergence and true collective agency? I am particularly interested in how it relates to ideas like spontaneous order or emergent coordination, without assuming those are the correct labels.
