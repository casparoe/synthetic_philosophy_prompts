Please describe the most important implications of adopting the most restrictive, defensible interpretation of Descartes's cogito—namely, that it proves only the existence of a present instance of thinking, without establishing personal identity across time, a persisting mental substance, a soul, or the reliability of memory—for my attempt to reconstruct my practical and intellectual life after a recent prolonged cognitive disruption that severely undermined my sense of temporal continuity. Specifically, I want to understand how this epistemic limitation should shape my attitude toward my pre-illness beliefs, my long-term obligations to others, and my capacity to make rational commitments that bind a future self, given that on this interpretation the present thinker has no philosophically certified continuity with any subsequent thinker who must live with the consequences. Does the restriction of the cogito to present-tense existence render long-range practical reasoning irrational, or can it be sustained without tacitly importing the very metaphysical assumptions—particularly about personal identity—that the argument was designed to suspend?
