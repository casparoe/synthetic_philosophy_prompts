The core claim is that the moral permissibility of retaining, altering, or removing a statue or street name depends not on the static moral biography of the figure, but on whether the commemorative object currently operates within what I term a regime of semiotic capture: that is, whether the community's present use has become decoupled from its original honorific intent and now functions chiefly as a site of contested historical narration rather than active endorsement. I derive from this that removal is justified only when the object remains inside an active endorsement regime, whereas retention-with-recontextualisation becomes obligatory once semiotic capture has occurred, since removal then destroys a functioning site of public memory-work rather than eliminating an unjust endorsement.
