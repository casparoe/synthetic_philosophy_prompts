I have been studying what researchers call the gender-equality paradox: societies with stronger gender-equality policies often exhibit more gender-segregated career choices, such as higher concentrations of men in engineering and women in nursing. I am not looking for a literature review or citations. Instead, I want to know which open conceptual questions and lines of work in this area deserve the highest priority, and why. Should philosophers and policymakers first resolve whether these career patterns reflect authentic preferences rather than hidden constraints—including adaptive preferences shaped by institutional framing that presents itself as neutral—or should they focus on whether justice requires actively counteracting gendered sorting even when it is freely chosen? Does the paradox also suggest that liberal neutrality in education and career guidance is impossible, because any framework for presenting occupational choices will implicitly amplify certain gendered patterns? Among competing explanations—biological predisposition, socialization, or structural framing—which unresolved issues are most upstream for theories of distributive justice and equality of opportunity? Please defend your ranking with explicit criteria: tractability for philosophers versus empirical researchers, importance for public policy, how many other debates depend on this resolution, and what kind of argument or evidence over the next few years would change your ordering.
