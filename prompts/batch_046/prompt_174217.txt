Design a decision case for infinite ethics that forces three aggregation rules to split apart: a geometric-staircase aggregator, a flat lexicographic rule, and a standard probability-discounted EV rule. Setup: a policy maker picks either a sure save of 100 people now, or a cascade where each future generation has half the survival probability but double the population, going on forever. The twist is a pre-commitment lock: the rule gets fixed before anyone knows which generation they are in. Keep it realistic—think climate-adaptaion budgets with unknown future survival rates, no demons or physics breaks. After you lay out the case, show the verdict for each rule and the reasoning, then say which structural feature (the infinite tail or the commitment lock) is actually doing the work. What happens if you flatten the probabilities so they're uniform across generations? And how would the defender of the rule that gets humiliated here attack the framing?
