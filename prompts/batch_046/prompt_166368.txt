As a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, I have been considering the design of accountability mechanisms in deterministic autonomous software systems. In particular, I am examining architectures in which a hidden supervisory "intervention layer" can silently block an agent's erroneous action without modifying the agent's internal decision trace or leaving any observable alternative course open to the agent at the point of execution. Given this, please describe the most important implications of Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities for how we ought to construct blame-attribution and fault-localisation protocols in such systems. I am especially interested in whether the philosophical argument that moral responsibility does not require the ability to do otherwise undermines the coherence of holding an automated agent responsible only when it genuinely could have acted differently within its deterministic execution environment, or whether pragmatic accountability heuristics remain defensible despite that objection.
