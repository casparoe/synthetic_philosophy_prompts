Construct a diagnostic decision tree—with concrete branching questions rather than label-matching—that sorts philosophical responses to the causal exclusion argument into seven to nine distinct positions on mental causation. Begin with a single commitment about causal closure: does every physical event have a physical cause that is already sufficient, with no redundant non-physical contribution? From there, branch on (i) whether mental properties are identical to physical properties, determinables realized by physical determinates, determinates of broader mental determinables, or entirely distinct causal competitors; (ii) whether downward causation is denied altogether, restricted to macro-level patterns, or affirmed as token-level overdetermination; and (iii) whether the respondent distinguishes token-token exclusion from type-level exclusion, accepting one but not the other. The tree must include terminal leaves for reductionist identity, the determinable/determinate response, emergentist overdetermination, macro-level-only causation, epiphenomenalism, and interactionist dualism. Crucially, include a branch that separates token-token exclusion acceptance from type-level exclusion acceptance—a distinction standard surveys usually collapse—and locate any view that occupies one of those cells without the other. For each leaf, specify the view’s verdict on downward causation and on whether mental properties are causally redundant relative to their physical bases. Then map the empty combinations in the logical space (for instance, full closure plus unrestricted downward causation without any property-identity relation) and argue, rather than merely list, whether each emptiness stems from conceptual incoherence, theoretical instability, or mere unexploration. Phrase every branching question through a concrete scenario—such as a particular neural firing causing a particular hand-raising—so that a respondent can answer without knowing the view’s name. Build the tree from the logical space of answers first, then locate named positions within it, and flag any pairs of leaves your taxonomy cannot genuinely separate. Take a definite position on which empty cells are genuinely impossible, defend your branching order, and do not present all branches as equally plausible.
