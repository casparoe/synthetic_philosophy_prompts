I'm putting together a workshop on democratic theory for about 18 people — mostly grad students plus a couple sharp undergrads — and I need a 90-minute activity that makes them actually feel the boundary problem, not just read about it. I want them to hit the circularity live: they have to decide who gets to sit on a council before they can vote on a shared resource, but the decision about who decides has to be made by... whoever they already accept as legitimate. So they run into the problem of defining the demos democratically without already having a demos.

To ground it, I'm using a fictional case: the Morvan Basin, a transboundary watershed shared by a sovereign state, a semi-autonomous region, climate migrants in temporary camps (8 years, pay taxes, no vote), and a downstream foreign community that gets the pollution but has no seat. I want 5-6 assigned roles with hidden incentive cards — some want the dam built, some want it blocked, some want universal inclusion regardless of outcome — so participants feel the strategic pull of drawing the boundary to win the policy fight. The rules should force them to agree on voting eligibility for the council before they can deliberate on the dam, and they only get 20 minutes for that meta-decision.

Then the debrief should map each phase onto parts of the idea — the circularity, the all-affected vs all-subjected tension, maybe a "future generations" guardian role — and be honest about what the game can't reproduce, like the historical violence through which actual demos are formed. I also want a heads-up on predictable failures: one faction monopolizing air-time, or everyone ignoring the hidden-payoff layer and treating it as pure deliberation. How should I stage it, with materials and a timeline? I really want the rules to do the work, not the facilitator explaining after.
