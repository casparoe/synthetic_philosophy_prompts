I have just come out of a long, exhausting argument with a close friend about Schopenhauer’s metaphysics of music—specifically his claim in Book III of The World as Will and Representation that music is not, like painting or poetry, a copy of the Platonic Ideas, but rather a direct objectification of the blind Will itself. My friend kept pressing that if music truly bypasses conceptual mediation, Schopenhauer still owes an account of why specific harmonic progressions feel like narrative resolutions—essentially demanding that the bass (the inorganic ground), melody (the history of human striving), and harmony (the graded objectifications of the Will) perform explanatory labor that looks suspiciously representational. Write a villanelle that stages this disagreement structurally: let one refrain articulate Schopenhauer’s thesis that music makes the Will audible without passing through Ideas, and let the other refrain voice my friend’s counter that every auditory experience is mediated by interpretation and conceptual form. Over the nineteen lines, weave in Schopenhauer’s specific analogy—bass as inorganic nature, melody as the will’s life-history, harmony as the scale of Ideas—so that the form’s unavoidable returns and final coupling of the refrains enact whether direct expression and mediated listening can actually coincide, or whether they are doomed to keep passing each other without resolution. The poem should be serious and polished rather than parodic, accurate enough that a reader familiar with Schopenhauer recognizes the precise structural parallel between musical parts and the grades of the Will’s objectification, and it should work as verse even for someone who does not already know the theory.
