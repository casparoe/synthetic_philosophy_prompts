After recently discovering that my parents' IVF treatment in the early 1990s produced several embryos—only one of which was implanted and became me—I have found myself genuinely unsure whether origin essentialism applies to cases like mine, or whether the very possibility of embryo selection undermines it. Rather than surveying interpretations, I want the specification first: what must any adequate theory of whether a person could have originated from a different sperm and egg actually be able to deliver?

Please write the full desiderata list. For each requirement, state the phenomenon or distinction it must cover—such as monozygotic twins, chimerism, donor gametes, adoption, or the modal status of genetic origin versus causal-historical origin—together with the reason it counts as a constraint, and flag whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference. Be explicit about consistency conditions with embryology and with modal logic: for instance, if genetic essentialism requires that no other zygote could have developed into this same person, does that conflict with the fact that multiple embryos with different genomes were available for implantation in the very same causal history? If some subset of the desiderata proves jointly unsatisfiable—perhaps because preserving strong essentialism is incompatible with the modal flexibility that IVF selection seems to demonstrate—map that tension precisely; that is the part I most want to see. Finally, very briefly, indicate how a Kripke-style origin-essentialist account, a fully anti-essentialist account, and any intermediate hybrid view stand against the list. I am not asking for citations or a bibliography; I am asking for the conceptual architecture of adequacy itself.
