I serve as the senior procedural referee for a constitutional court’s referendum-admission board, reviewing proposed direct-democracy ballot measures within a forty-eight-hour window before a three-judge panel with no philosophical background signs off. I need a reusable decision procedure—ordered linguistic tests, flag points, and an explicit flowchart in words—to determine when a proposed referendum improperly invokes “the will of the people” in ways that violate our procedural rules by committing what I will call the authorization double-bind: illegitimately shifting between treating popular will as an empirical aggregation of preferences and treating it as a pre-political, homogeneous moral command that supersedes constitutional safeguards. The procedure should operationalize three modes of popular-appeal language—representative aggregation (citing measurable preference), constituent homogeneity (positing a singular, non-negotiable popular identity), and anticipatory substitution (claiming to represent the people’s “true” will despite contrary empirical indicators)—with concrete phrase-level tests staff can run without theory training, precise points where judgment is unavoidable and must be flagged for the panel, and an honest statement of what the procedure cannot settle. Apply it to three hypothetical measures: (a) a tax-reform proposal that uses explicit polling data but frames the result as an irresistible moral command; (b) an anti-corruption measure I personally favor that relies on anticipatory-substitution rhetoric (“the silent majority demands justice”) and should be flagged despite my sympathy; and (c) a local zoning override whose ballot language is ambiguously implicit. Say what argument, counterexample, or institutional failure would change your assessment—where the three-mode distinction collapses or the filter systematically misfires—and state the reasoning most likely to fail. Lead with your bottom-line verdict on whether such a procedural filter is workable at all, then present the procedure, the worked cases, and its hard limits. No preamble, no restatement of the question, begin immediately with the bottom line, and include no requests for citations or reading lists.
