Here are the steps she laid out:
(1) Virtue ethics needs stable, cross-situational character traits that cause moral behavior.
(2) Situationist data (Milgram, the dime study, etc.) show behavior swings wildly with tiny contextual tweaks, with near-zero trait-behavior correlation.
(3) So stable moral character traits don't exist.
(4) Hence virtue ethics is false.

(5) The scientists who discovered these effects must themselves possess stable epistemic traits — rationality, bias-correction, methodological care — to reach valid conclusions.
(6) If (3) denies all stable traits, then scientists lack these epistemic traits too, reducing there findings to situational noise.
(7) So accepting (3) undermines the scientific reasoning that supports (3).
(8) Therefore the situationist critique is self-defeating, and we should reject (3) after all.

She wants to keep (1)-(4) intact and just fix the later steps, but I smell an equivocation between moral-character traits and cognitive-epistemic traits, or maybe a modal slide from 'no perfect consistency' to 'no traits whatsoever'.
