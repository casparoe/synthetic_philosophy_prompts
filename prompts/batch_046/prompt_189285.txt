I serve on the Inter-University Committee on Adaptive Learning Systems, which governs a shared AI career-advising platform used by students across our three partner institutions: a technical polytechnic, a secular liberal-arts college, and a university with an explicit religious mission. Our charter requires that the system remain liberally neutral among competing conceptions of the good life, and currently the platform operates in a procedural-neutral mode: it ranks career pathways solely by coherence with each student’s self-reported interests and prior course selections, without weighting income potential, social prestige, or spiritual vocation. A coalition of students and faculty from the polytechnic has filed a formal grievance arguing that this very neutrality is not neutral at all, because by refusing to weight economic security and technical mastery as legitimate goods, the algorithm implicitly privileges the liberal-arts conception of intellectual flourishing over other comprehensive doctrines; they propose replacing the neutral framework with an explicit democratic aggregation mechanism in which enrolled students vote quarterly on the relative weights assigned to different value dimensions. Meanwhile, representatives from the religious university contend that any aggregation mechanism that treats spiritual calling as one preference among others fails to respect the incommensurability of vocation, and they ask either for an opt-out module or for the system to be redesigned around a threshold model rather than an optimization model. The committee must decide by the end of the semester whether to approve the aggregation proposal, defend the current procedural-neutral design, or mandate the hybrid threshold approach, with full implementation required before the next academic year.

Please draft the committee’s decision memo recommending one of these three courses of action, addressing the polytechnic’s charge that neutrality masks a substantive value commitment, engaging seriously with the religious university’s incommensurability objection, and specifying concrete conditions or a timeline if your recommendation is conditional; present the memo as exactly five numbered points, each consisting of one to two sentences, with no introductory or concluding paragraphs outside these five.
