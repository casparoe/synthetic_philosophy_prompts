As a retired experimental physicist now studying philosophy, I am preparing an upper-level undergraduate exam on Hellenistic ethics. I need a single essay question—thirty minutes, twenty-five marks—on Stoicism's modern revival and the dichotomy of control, designed so that memorizing popular self-help summaries is insufficient.

The question should ask students to compare Epictetus's framing in the Enchiridion—where what belongs to prohairesis (judgment, impulse, desire, and aversion) defines the scope of what is genuinely in our power—with the way contemporary Stoic-inspired cognitive therapy often collapses this into a generic distinction between controllable and uncontrollable outcomes, frequently treating emotional states themselves as external results rather than as judgments internal to moral character. Students should be required to determine whether this modern collapse is a legitimate clinical adaptation or a conceptual distortion that undermines the Stoic therapeutic claim.

Please provide: (1) the exam question itself; (2) a model answer that makes three specific moves—correctly delimiting the ancient scope of what is in our power, explaining why classifying emotional responses as externals misplaces them within the Stoic framework, and offering an evaluated judgment on whether the distortion matters for practical aims; and (3) a marking scheme that awards marks for conceptual precision rather than labeling, penalizes answers that treat the dichotomy as purely psychological rather than ethical or characterological, identifies the common error of listing health, wealth, and reputation as examples without explaining their status as externals, and specifies point allocations totaling twenty-five marks.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
