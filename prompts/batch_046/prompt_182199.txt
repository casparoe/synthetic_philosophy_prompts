I've been tinkering with a hybrid view that stiches together Camus-style absurdism with recent work on what philosophers of emotion call refractory states—basically emotional conditions that resist rational resolution and stay sticky. The idea is that life's meaning isn't missing in the absurd condition, it's present specifically as an unreconciled tension between our coherence-demand and cosmic silence. Resolve it through faith or nihilistic surrender and you don't fix the problem—you destroy the specific value that only lives in the gap. I'm not fully sold but I want stress-testing.

What would actually count against this? I'm especially worried about three things: empirical psych showing sustained dissonance of this type always collapses into resolution or clinical despair; a philosophical argument that value-bearing states need some integration rather than pure tension; and maybe a result from value theory showing that negative coherance can't do the normative work I'm asking it to do. Rank these and any others you see—fatal vs awkward. Which ones would a committed defender concede? Is this view genuinely exposed or has it been insulated by qualifications? And what exactly would the world have to look like—psychologicaly, metaphysically, normatively—for the core claim to fail?
