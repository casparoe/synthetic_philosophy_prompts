Next Tuesday I present to a philosophy-of-science workshop a modified accuracy-first epistemology. The core claim is that global accuracy maximization should be lexically constrained by a calibration-first condition: an agent's confidence curve must demonstrate reliability across deciles—measured via the calibration component of a decomposed Brier score—before any world-indexed accuracy aggregation is applied, and I reject the symmetric treatment of worlds that standard epistemic utility theory assumes. The audience comprises six formal epistemologists working in Bayesian and decision-theoretic frameworks and one applied statistician whose research concerns meteorological forecasting and proper scoring rules. None are interested in moral or existential philosophy; they care about rational belief, scoring-rule justification, and whether epistemic norms should be world-sensitive.

Prepare my Q&A defense sheet. Identify the seven or eight objections I will actually receive, ordered by combined likelihood and damage. Include the single objection I am hoping no one raises—the one that exposes a structural tension in the calibration-first constraint for which I do not yet have a satisfactory resolution. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need strategic preparation, not archival retrieval.
