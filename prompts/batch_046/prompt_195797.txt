I'm working on a novel where my main character — a surgeon's granddaughter who writes fiction and hates public speeking — gets roped into giving the dedication at the reopening of her grandfather's old free clinic, now named after him. He was one of those rare people who actually read Rand cover to cover and lived like it: he treated anyone who walked in, but he was vicious about the word altruism, always saying that helping people was a trade, not a sacrifice, and that the idea of 'moral duty to suffer for strangers' was exactly what ruined medicine. The crowd will be split between patients who think he's a saint and philosophy grad students who know the actual texts, and I need her speech to land for both without turning into a lecture or a eulogy. The idea needs to do real work: she can't praise his generosity in the usual self-sacrificial language, but she also can't make his patients sound like props for his ego. Can you write the speech? Keep it around four minutes, write it in her voice (she's a novelist, so she's precise about language but not acedemic), and please don't just quote Atlas Shrugged at people — he'd roll in his grave. Also there's a typo on the brass plaque that says 'devoted to self-interest' when it should probably say something about reason or something, and she notices it halfway through and has to improvise around it. I want the philosophical tension to shape every paragraph, not just decorate it.
