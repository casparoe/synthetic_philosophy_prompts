I'm giving a talk next thursday at our philosophy of mind seminar defending a somewhat weird take on panpsychism. The idea is that the combination problem only bites if you think macro-subjects are literally built out of micro-subjects, but if phenomenal unity is actually a modal constraint on distributed experiential states — not an aggregate but a condition on the possibility space of the parts — then there's no combination mechanism needed at all. The audience is mostly physicalists who think consciousness is either emergent from complex information integration or just global workspace dynamics, plus one cognitive scientist who works on IIT but thinks panpsychism is mysticism in a lab coat, and a grad student who just read Chalmers and is convinced combination is a knockdown. They care about empiracal adequacy and not sounding like they're endorsing little minds. I sketched the argument like this: physical systems can be decomposed without losing the physical description, but phenomenal unity resists clean decompostion — cut a brain and you don't split the experience in two neat halves — so unity can't be compositional; it must be a constraint relation across parts.
