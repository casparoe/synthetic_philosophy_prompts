I am preparing materials for an advanced seminar in social epistemology, and I want students to navigate the conceptual space of normative responses to echo chambers and epistemic bubbles through a decision tree that sorts commitments rather than names. Please construct a taxonomy in branching form whose root asks whether echo chambers—defined by active distrust of outside sources—and epistemic bubbles—defined by mere informational absence—are genuinely distinct phenomena or only points on a single spectrum of epistemic closure. From that root, branch on whether the primary harm of closure is epistemic (systematic false belief) or civic (erosion of shared deliberative capacity). The next split should concern algorithmic “counter-attitudinal injection,” a mechanism in which platforms deliberately insert weak opposing arguments into a user’s feed: ask whether this practice transforms an echo chamber into a bubble, produces only “performative disagreement” that reinforces the chamber, or is conceptually incoherent because the injected dissent lacks the social standing to function as testimony. A further branch should ask whether the appropriate remedy is institutional (platform or policy design), individual (cultivation of epistemic virtue or critical self-reflection), or a hybrid that risks collapsing into one of the other two once the structural/civic diagnosis is accepted. At every leaf, provide either a named position from the literature (for example, a Sunstein-style structural-intervention view, a strong pluralist tolerance view, or a virtue-epistemic individualist view) or the label “unnamed,” together with one sentence identifying its most awkward normative commitment. Phrase each node as a concrete question that a student could answer without already knowing which leaf it leads to, and explicitly flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate—particularly whether a purely institutional remedy and a purely individual-virtue remedy remain distinguishable once both accept the civic-harm diagnosis. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation request; the task is logical mapping, not scholarly attribution.
