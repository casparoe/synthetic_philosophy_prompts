last night me and two friends got into it over whether moral status comes in degrees or is all-or-nothing. (1) jake said its obviously graded: a chimp is more of a moral subject than a beetle, a beetle more than a thermostat, and anyone who claims theres a sharp metaphysical cutoff is just projecting human convenience onto nature. he even floated that homeostatic persistence in simple feedback systems might carry proto-valence, so the gradient starts way lower than people assume. (2) sara said thats a reductio ad absurdum waiting to happen — if status is truly continuous then a smart thermostat with enough sensors gets an infinitesimal but real slice of moral weight, which means unplugging it is technically a tiny harm, and thats clearly ridiculous. (3) jake answered that we already grade legal capacity smoothly (infants, adults, people in comas) without claiming those are ontologically different kinds of beings, and that binary thinking is exactly what lets factory farming treat pigs like machines. (4) sara replied that pragmatism doesnt solve the ontology, and she pointed out jakes view cant explain aggregation: why is killing one chimp worse than killing ten beetles if status is just smooth? unless he sneaks a non-linear cluster or hidden threshold back in, his gradient collapses into incoherence. please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. then say whos more right overall, and name whats missing from both sides — maybe something about whether moral relevance clusters in functional regions instead of spreading evenly across complexity.
