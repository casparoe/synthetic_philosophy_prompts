Below are notes for an essay I am drafting. I argue that von Neumann's concern with surviving technology is best read not as a warning about nuclear escalation—the usual interpretation—but as an epistemic problem: technologies that replicate and vary faster than institutional verification mechanisms can correct errors will eventually outpace the very frameworks that judge them dangerous. I draw a line from his 1950s work on self-reproducing automata and error-correcting codes to his less-discussed remarks on the accelerating divergence between technological capability and social comprehension, suggesting that survival depends less on restraining specific technologies than on building adaptive verification architectures. I then ask whether this framing anachronistically projects modern resilience engineering onto von Neumann, and whether it underplays his actual pessimism about institutional adaptation.
