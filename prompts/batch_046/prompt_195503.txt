I'm preparing the metaphysical section of a seminar defense on persistence and personal identity, and I want a consistency audit of my commitments before I present. Here is the package I intend to defend:

(1) I am an endurantist about concrete particulars: an object that exists at t₁ and t₂ is literally the same entity, wholly present at each of those times; there are no temporal parts.

(2) I solve the problem of temporary intrinsics by adverbialism—an enduring thing is bent-at-t₁ and straight-at-t₂ in the same non-contradictory way a spatially extended thing is bent-here and straight-there—without indexing properties to times or invoking stages.

(3) I accept a psychological-continuity account of diachronic personal identity, and I take fission (branching) cases to be metaphysically possible: one person can become two, and I do not regard this as entailing a logical contradiction.

(4) I hold that identity is never vague or indeterminate: for any enduring objects x and y, at any time, either x is strictly identical to y or not.

(5) I deny that an object's material-compositional properties are essential—complete replacement of parts is compatible with survival, given appropriate causal-functional continuity. At the same time, I hold that origin conditions are essential: the particular matter from which an object initially arose is a necessary feature of that object.

(6) I reject counterpart-theoretic accounts of trans-world identity for enduring objects: if an object exists in another possible world, it is literally that same enduring entity, not a counterpart.

Please audit this set. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and rank the real conflicts by severity. For each tension, specify exactly what must be revised—whether endurantism, adverbialism, psychological continuity with fission, determinacy of identity, the essentiality of origins, or the rejection of counterparts—and state the cheapest fix and what it costs. Be precise about how (3) and (4) interact with (1), about whether (5) is internally contradictory or only conflicts with other commitments, and about the pressure that (6) puts on (5). Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems at once, and what I would have to concede to make it.
