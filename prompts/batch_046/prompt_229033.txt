I've been building this side project where I use a variational autoencoder to map abstract concepts to continuous vectors so I can interpollate between them - like blending melancoly and triumph to generate hybrid images. I got into this after reading Douglas Hofstadter's idea that creativity is basically knob-twiddling inside conceptual spaces, where you adjust parameters until you hit something interesting. I was explaining to my roommate that this proves AI and humans are doing the excat same thing, since both are just navigating high-dimensional spaces and tweaking values.

But then I started wondering: if everything is just sliding knobs inside a pre-existing space, where do entirely new dimensions come from? Like, when jazz musicians invented atonality, they weren't just turning up the dissonance knob - they seemed to change what the knobs even meant. So is Hofstadter's model actually incompatible with true innovation, or am I just confusing the map with the teritory here? I feel like there's a hole in my reasoning but I can't pin it down.
