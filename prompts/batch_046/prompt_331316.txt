Consider the following claim: If one operationalizes coherent extrapolation not through an open-ended reflective equilibrium, but through a constrained optimization in which the extrapolated preference ordering is required to be invariant under the relabeling of temporal slices separated by any interval of subjective experience less than a threshold τ—where τ is analogized to the characteristic decay constant of episodic memory consolidation—then the resulting volition will be unique up to isomorphism and will not depend on the initial anchoring preferences of the agent performing the extrapolation.

Please provide your own credence, expressed as a precise number between 0 and 1, that this claim is true. More importantly, decompose the reasoning into four distinct sub-questions:

(a) The probability that coherent extrapolation can be given a well-defined formal structure at all, independent of this particular symmetry constraint.

(b) The probability that imposing a temporal-invariance symmetry of the kind described actually eliminates initial-condition dependence, rather than merely encoding it in the choice of τ.

(c) The probability that uniqueness up to isomorphism follows from this symmetry alone, without requiring additional normative assumptions about intertemporal aggregation or discounting.

(d) The probability that τ can be assigned a non-arbitrary, principled value within the framework, rather than functioning as a free parameter.

For each sub-question, assign a rough probability with one line of justification, then combine them—whether multiplicatively or through another weighting you judge appropriate—to reach your overall figure. Finally, identify which sub-question represents the weakest link in your derivation, and specify what kind of argument or empirical finding—concerning, for example, the dynamics of long-term preference change, the formal properties of aggregation rules under symmetry constraints, or the neuropsychology of memory consolidation—would most substantially shift your credence.
