Write a 90-minute final-exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy-of-mind seminar on functionalism and multiple realizability. Center the question on the following constructed scenario: a research team constructs a planet-scale “distributed cognitive lattice” composed of simple silicon nodes arranged so that the network preserves the exact causal-graph topology of a human brain—every node-to-node causal dependency is mapped one-to-one—yet none of the nodes instantiate biochemical processes, continuous electrochemical gradients, or token-identical physical states; instead, the system operates on discrete clock cycles with no temporal dynamics matching neural firing. Ask students to evaluate whether this lattice realizes mental states, and require them to distinguish and apply three positions: (1) machine-state or formal-transition functionalism that identifies mental states with abstract state-transition rules; (2) analytic or causal-role functionalism that identifies them with relational causal structures regardless of physical substrate; and (3) psychofunctionalism that ties mental-state ascription to evolutionary-selected biological functions and their proper causal histories. The question must force students to confront the tension between organizational invariance and substrate independence—specifically, they must explain why preserving causal-graph isomorphism might be sufficient for some functionalists yet insufficient for others, and they must identify one precise conceptual cost of accepting the lattice as a mind, such as the explanatory irrelevance of physical implementation or the risk of trivializing mental-state ascription, without merely summarizing well-known macro-physical objections.

Deliver, in American English:
- The exam question itself, clearly worded for a 90-minute essay of approximately 800 to 1,000 words.
- A model answer of about 700 words.
- A marking scheme out of 20 points built around five must-hit conceptual moves. For each move, specify the point value, the specific analytical content required, and a one-line note on the typical student failure that costs those marks. The scheme must separate an excellent answer—which must identify exactly where the lattice case diverges from standard macro-physical realization examples and why that divergence matters for at least one of the three functionalist positions—from a merely competent answer that only recites the standard narrative that mental states are multiply realizable.

Keep the tone formal and polished. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not depend on precise publication dates, page numbers, or verbatim quotations from specific texts; if you mention familiar thought experiments, refer to them by common name and paraphrase their structure without quotation marks. Ensure the prompt is answerable by a model without web access.
