Consider the specific claim that a regulatory institution's failure to update emissions standards can be a partial cause—not merely a background condition—of a particular subsequent climate-related infrastructure failure, on the condition that counterfactual intervention on the omission was technically feasible and the institution held a defined duty to act. Give me your credence in this claim, decomposed into four sub-questions: (i) whether negative events can enter causal relations on a counterfactual-interventionist account without collapsing into mere absence; (ii) whether institutional duty-roles supply the normative filter needed to distinguish genuine omission-causation from universal non-intervention; (iii) whether current climate-attribution methods can isolate the marginal contribution of the regulatory omission from the background emissions trajectory; and (iv) whether accepting causation here forces an implausibly expansive ontology of omissions elsewhere. For each sub-question provide a rough probability with one line of justification, combine them into an overall credence, and identify which term is the weakest link and what kind of empirical finding or conceptual argument would shift it most.
