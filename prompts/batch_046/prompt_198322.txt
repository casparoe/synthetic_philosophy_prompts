I'm a high school student working on an independent research project about how predictive theories survive long runs of failed forecasts. I've noticed a recurring argumentative pattern that I haven't been able to name, and I want to know whether philosophy of science already recognizes it as a distinct concept or whether it needs a new one.

The pattern works like this. A theory makes a concrete, time-bound prediction—say, that a critical resource will be depleted within roughly fifty years because of an underlying growth mechanism. The prediction fails: the resource remains available, largely because of technological substitutes and policy adjustments that the original theory did not specify. Rather than revising or abandoning the core mechanism, defenders claim it is still operating but is being "temporarily masked" by these contingent conditions. They do not add a precise new law; they shift to a flexible temporal framing that says the mechanism simply runs on a longer clock. Decades later, an unrelated crisis—perhaps a regional shortage driven by geopolitical conflict rather than by the original mechanism—occurs. At that point, supporters cite the crisis as vindication, arguing the theory was "right but early" and that the masking conditions have finally lifted. Every intermediate failure is then explained away as part of the suppression period, while any future disruption can be counted as confirmation.

I've seen this structure invoked around Malthusian predictions, but it seems to appear in other domains too. What distinguishes it, in my view, is not a standard auxiliary hypothesis—there is no new coefficient or boundary condition being added—but a movable temporal excuse that turns present falsification into delayed confirmation without making the theory's timing precise or risky.

Is there an established term for this exact move, one that captures the combination of latency framing, retrospective credit after unrelated crises, and the refusal to specify in advance when the masking conditions will end? How exactly does it differ from an ordinary ad hoc rescue, from a Lakatosian degenerating research programme, and from the general idea that a theory can be correct but premature? If no precise term exists, take a definite position: argue whether this pattern deserves its own concept, propose a rigorous definition with clear boundary conditions that separate it from ordinary delayed confirmation and from unfalsifiable theories, and defend why your proposed carving is the right one rather than merely listing alternatives.
