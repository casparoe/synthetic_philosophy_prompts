As a journalist covering science and ideas, I also serve on the ethics advisory panel of the Meridian Institute for Bio-Digital Systems. We must issue guidance on a protocol that turns directly on whether moral status admits of degrees rather than being an all-or-nothing property. Researchers have developed distributed nociceptive lattices—networks of cultured neuronal tissue integrated with thermodynamic sensors that generate adaptive, harm-avoiding responses to damaging thermal gradients. These systems display functional architectures analogous to peripheral nociception in invertebrates, yet lack any demonstrable unified subjective perspective or centralized phenomenal integration. The institute’s existing framework grants welfare protections solely to entities that cross a binary sentience threshold. A new proposal requests that we extend a limited, intermediate tier of protection—restricted to harm-minimization and non-destructive testing protocols—to these lattices, arguing that they occupy a gradated position on a spectrum running from reflexive thermostats through insects to fully conscious mammals. The panel is divided between those who see gradation as conceptually coherent and those who hold that moral status requires an indivisible capacity for subjective experience.

Please draft the formal opinion memo I will circulate ahead of Thursday’s session. It should recommend one concrete course of action—approval of the tiered extension with specific conditions and a six-month review timeline, rejection with reaffirmation of the binary threshold, or a provisional pilot governed by a sunset clause—together with reasoning that takes the strongest opposing argument seriously rather than dismissing it. The memo should not request bibliographies or external citations; work instead from the conceptual logic of gradated moral status, the boundary conditions implied by treating a thermostat as lacking any moral claim, and the analogical weight of insect sentience.
