I defend a qualified version of Gödel's argument that objective temporal passage is unreal, drawn specifically from his rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations. The claim is not that the cosmos actually rotates — modern cosmological bounds on global vorticity are conceded — but that the formal permissibility of closed timelike curves within a valid metric demonstrates that relations of before and after are not intrinsic to spacetime structure. I further maintain that quantum chronology-protection mechanisms, even if they prevent physical realization of such curves, leave the formal consistency untouched and thus do not rescue objective passage; and that entropic or causal-grounded accounts of time's arrow fail to address the geometric point because they presuppose the very temporal ordering the argument puts in question. Given this package — modal geometric argument, irrelevance of empirical rotation, survival despite chronology protection, and dismissal of entropic rescue — what would genuinely count against it? I want the defeaters sorted by decisiveness: formal results that make closed timelike curves impossible in principle rather than merely unobserved; arguments that modal consistency in a metric cannot license metaphysical conclusions about time; and whether a committed defender must concede that retreating from empirical cosmology to pure formal consistency has insulated the view into unfalsifiability. Is that insulation a virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind?
