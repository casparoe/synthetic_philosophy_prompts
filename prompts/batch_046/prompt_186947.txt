I've adopted an extension of van Fraassen's Reflection principle into what I'd call reasoning dynamics: if I can anticipate that a specific argument will be severely weakened by evidence I haven't encountered yet, I should preemptively reduce its current weight in my belief structure by a factor tracking that expected revision. This applies Reflection not to static credences but to the lifecycle of arguments themselves. What would genuinely count against this? I want the defeaters sorted by severity: formal results about coherence or self-reference, intuitive cases where anticipation creates self-fulfilling failure, and distinctions that might collapse the view into either ordinary Bayesian updating or an unfalsifiable epistemic humility. Which of these would a defender have to concede, and which can be absorbed by tightening scope conditions? Finally, does this extension risk anything empirically or conceptually, or has it been qualified into tautology — and if it's insulated, is that a problem for a normative principle of this kind?
