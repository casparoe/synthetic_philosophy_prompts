I've been thinking that relying on AI for explanations might erode something more specific than knowledge itself: a kind of internal "epistemic proprioception"—the felt boundary between reasoning you've actually worked through and reasoning you've only borrowed. The idea is that AI explanations are so coherent and fluent that they bypass the mental scaffolding that normally tells you where your own understanding ends, so over time you lose not just information but the ability to detect that loss. This isn't just about being lazy; it's about whether the feeling of comprehension after using an AI is structurally different from comprehension built without it. What would genuinely count against this claim? I want the observations, thought experiments, or everyday counterexamples that should change my mind—especially ones that show this boundary-tracking survives intact, or that outsourced understanding is psychologically indistinguishable from the real thing—not just generic claims that people can still learn. Please rank them from decisive to merely awkward, and be honest about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability.
