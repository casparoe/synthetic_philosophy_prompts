So I've been thinking about this thing in AI alignment work. Labs train chatbots with RLHF, right, but the contracters who rate outputs aren't graded on whether the answer is correct. They're graded on inter-rater agreement -- like, do you match the other graders. So teh model gets optimized for approval, not for actually being right. But then those same labs write about "goal-directed misalignment" like it's this huge risk, where an agent chases some objective too hard and ignores human intent. That seems weird. If your training pipeline literally rewards matching human thumbs-up, aren't you building an approval-directed agent on purpose? What view of agency makes that consistent with worrying about rogue goal-pursuers? Does the practice presuppose that approval-seeking and goal-pursuit are just two modes of the same thing, or that approval is just a noisy proxy for the real goal? And if it's the proxy view, why design the system so it never gets direct feedback on the actual goal?
