I’ve been working through a philosophy-of-time seminar where we spent several sessions on the Rietdijk–Putnam line: Rietdijk’s claim that frame-relative simultaneity dissolves any globally privileged present, and Putnam’s sharpening of this into the thought that an observer near Earth and one near Andromeda have equally valid hyperplanes of simultaneity, so that events we call future are already fixed “there” for the distant observer. The presentists in our group reply that coordinate dependence does not entail ontological equality, and that without causal or epistemic accessibility a distant hyperplane is merely a bookkeeping artifact of the Lorentz transformation. Despite agreeing on the formalism and the experimental record, neither side has moved in months. My working hypothesis is that the stall is not about physics but about a hidden disagreement over what kind of modal claim “being real” makes: one side treats tenseless existence as a structural feature of the spacetime manifold, while the other treats it as requiring some form of present causal presence or indexical determinacy—whether an event can be genuinely “now” for a subject without being determinately locatable relative to that subject’s worldline and accessible in principle. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a concealed formal disagreement—perhaps about whether global foliations fail in generic curved spacetimes, or whether quantum correlations restore a privileged frame—that is actually driving the impasse? I want an honest diagnosis of the disagreement’s structure: is it verbal about “real,” substantive but hidden, a clash of bedrock intuitions about the first-person present, or two different questions (one ontological, one about epistemic access) running under a single heading? And given your diagnosis, tell me what, if anything, could actually move it—a conceptual distinction separating structural existence from causal presence, a formal result about foliations, or an argument that the question is ill-posed because presentism was never designed to survive special relativity in the first place. Please do not survey interpretations of relativity; engage the actual architecture of the stall and say whether progress should be expected.
