I'm working out my own view of life by testing principles against concrete judgments. Here are my verdicts. A tardigrade in cryptobiosis, metabolically suspended but structurally intact and revivable, is alive. A virus replicating inside a host is not alive, despite its evolutionary dynamics. A synthetic minimal cell built bottom-up with an engineered genome of only a few hundred genes, which maintains its own boundary, metabolizes, and divides, but was designed with no evolutionary history and lacks open-ended adaptive capacity, is alive. A real-time computer simulation running a complete metabolic model with energy accounting and feedback loops is not alive. A prion propagating its misfolded conformation through tissue is not alive. An isolated mitochondrion kept in culture, preserving membrane potential and dividing briefly, is alive. A crystal growing in a supersaturated solution is not. A self-sustaining fire spreading through fuel is not, despite apparent growth and reproduction-like behavior. Is there one account—centered on autonomous metabolism, organizational closure, evolutionary potential, or something else—that delivers all eight verdicts? If not, which verdict is the outlier and should be overturned, and why? Also state what your best principle predicts for a case I have not listed: a hypothetical replicator that substitutes arsenic for phosphorus in its backbone, divides slowly, and has never belonged to an evolutionary lineage on Earth.
