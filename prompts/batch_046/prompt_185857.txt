People explaining Hofstadter's idea that 'I' is a strange loop often compare it to holding a mic near a speaker: the symbol 'I' is emitted by the sytem, picked back up, and fed into the loop that made it, amplifing some traits and drowning others. They use this to claim selfhood isn't an internal object but just the pattern in the feedback. Audit the analogy: map the mic, speaker, amp, and squeal onto actual brain parts or processes. Where does it break — is there really one speaker, or do language, memory, and action-planning subsytems each use 'I' with a different referent? Does it smuggle in an outside listener that Hofstadter's loop doesn't need? Can it actually support 'no core self', or does it just redescribe one? And if we tweak it to a broken loop — mic only sometimes connected — does that map onto dissociation, or just onto ordinary shifts in self-story?
