I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have been reading about Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism—the view that fire does not truly burn cotton, but rather God produces the burning on each occasion that fire is present, with created things serving only as constant conjunctions rather than real causes. I would like you to write a dialogue between two interlocutors who both understand this position well: one who defends occasionalism and holds that God alone is the true agent behind every event, and one who defends the reality of secondary causation, maintaining that created substances possess genuine causal powers that operate within the order God established. Rather than debating in the abstract, they should discuss a specific, modern scenario: a surgeon who relies on a precision robotic arm that executes movements exactly when a command signal is sent, yet where the physical motion could be described either as the arm's own mechanical efficacy or as God acting directly on the occasion of the signal. They should examine whether this distinction makes any difference to scientific explanation, to the surgeon's moral responsibility if the arm harms the patient, and to whether we are ever justified in saying the arm caused the injury rather than God caused it on the occasion of the arm's position. Both characters should be equally sharp—neither a straw man—and they must respond to each other's actual arguments, not talk past one another. Please write this in polished British English, without asking for references, further reading, or bibliographies.
