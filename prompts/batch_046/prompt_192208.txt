I'm designing a module for an advanced philosophy of time seminar, and my students have become somewhat stuck in the familiar dialectic between the A-theory and the B-theory, repeatedly citing the same canonical thought experiments. To push them toward a more imaginative engagement with presentism and eternalism, I want a short story set in the Ontological Repository, an institutional archive where curators do not merely preserve physical artifacts but also catalog the temporal ontology in which each item is said to exist. The protagonist is Dr. Elara Voss, a senior archivist who specializes in presentist objects—items that are documented as existing only when directly observed and that leave no trace in storage once attention is withdrawn. During a routine inventory, she discovers a single brass astrolabe engraved with four-dimensional spacetime coordinates; unlike every other presentist artifact in her care, this object remains in its case regardless of whether anyone is present, and its inscriptions seem to describe past and future positions as if they were equally real locations rather than memories or predictions. The narrative should follow her attempt to determine whether the astrolabe is genuine evidence for eternalism—the view that past, present, and future entities all exist—whether it is instead a presentist illusion sustained by collective attention, or whether it reveals a flaw in the Repository's classification system itself.

Please write the story in a polished literary style, allowing yourself to invent institutional procedures, metaphysical jargon, and speculative mechanisms as needed for dramatic coherence. At the end, append a brief analytical note that distinguishes what is philosophically settled—such as the core distinction that presentism denies existence to non-present entities while eternalism affirms it—from what remains genuinely contested, for instance whether presentism can be rendered consistent with the findings of special relativity or whether eternalism can account for the phenomenological asymmetry of temporal experience. In that note, also flag any points where the story takes liberties with these positions, relies on fictional premises, or leans on a contested interpretation, and avoid presenting any one metaphysical view as the consensus among specialists.
