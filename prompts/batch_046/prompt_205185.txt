I need about four minutes, and I want Quine's indeterminacy idea to actually structure the talk, not just decorate it. The niche framing I'm after is this: treat the interpreter in an asylum hearing as doing radical translation, not word-for-word conversion. They're deciding whether the applicant's term — which might cover both being hunted by police and being hunted by relatives — maps onto our legal category 'persecution,' and Quine's point is that there's no hidden fact of the matter beyond the behavioral and contextual evidence right in front of them. That should constrain me: I can't claim there's a perfect dictionary we're failing to consult, but I also can't say it's pure power or arbitrary, because then the interpreter's careful judgment doesn't matter. The speech should navigate that tension and end with something practical — maybe about training people to work inside that gap rather than pretend it away.
