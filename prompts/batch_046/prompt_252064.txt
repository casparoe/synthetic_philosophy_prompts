I'm a peer-review coordinator for a small interdisiplinary journal and I'm tired of submissions that claim empirical status but hide behind what I call a "double-hedge" -- an explicit thesis plus a fallback clause that reinterprets any counterevidence as actually supporting the view. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Popper's falsifcation idea but stripped down for non-philosophers, that I can run in about fifteen minutes per paper and sign off on without calling a philosopher. Give me ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, hard "stop and flag" points, and clear markers where I'm making a value judgment rather than applying a rule. Add a small test-set of claim-types I can apply in under two minutes. Then apply it to three submissions: one in neuroscience saying consciousness collapses quantum states with the fallback that null results only mean the observer wasn't conscious enough; one standard social-psych replication study; and one health claim saying the effect exists but is only measurable with their proprietery instrument. Include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer I'll hate. Keep the whole thing under 450 words, and tell me exactly what the procedure can't catch.
