I'm trying to map out what I actually think about desert in meritocracy, going off gut reactions to cases. Here they are, in no particular order:

1. A kid with a photographic memory studies hard and gets into med school — she deserves the spot.
2. A kid with average memory studies twice as hard, still gets in through sheer grit — also deserves it, maybe more?
3. A guy inherits a biotech firm, runs it competantly, but never built it — he deserves the salary but not the *status* that comes with owning it?
4. Two researchers apply for the same grant. One had private tutors since age 5 (rich parents), the other taught herself in a library. Both produce identical papers. I feel the second deserves the win more, but if we actually award her for that reason, aren't we punishing the first for her upbringing?
5. A factory worker busts his back for twenty years, then the plant automates and he's out of work. The result-only system says he failed to produce, so he gets nothing. That feels wrong — he doesn't deserve poverty.
6. But effort-only also feels wrong: if I try really hard to be a surgeon but I'm naturally clumsy, I shouldn't recieve the scalpel just for trying.

Then there's the case I keep flipping on: a brilliant painter produces one masterpiece purely by accident (she spilled paint and it looked amazing) vs a mediocre painter who labored for years and made something okay. The first gets fame — she doesn't *deserve* it, but should we take the painting away? Not sure.

Is there one principle that gets all these? Maybe it's about control, or contribution, or some mix? Can you give me the best principle (or a couple candidates) that captures my verdicts, and also tell me which ones I have to drop if I want something simple instead of a messy patchwork?
