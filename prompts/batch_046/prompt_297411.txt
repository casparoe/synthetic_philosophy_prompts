I am defending a revised taxonomy of cognitive architectures at a joint philosophy of mind and comparative cognition seminar. My thesis is that Dennett’s four-way classification of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—should be refined by splitting the Gregorian category into representational-tool users and procedural-tool users. I argue that the standard taxonomy conflates two distinct mechanisms: the social transmission of explicit mental representations that an agent can manipulate as instruments for reasoning, and the socially scaffolded acquisition of behavioral and cognitive routines that never become explicit, manipulable instruments for the agent. This conflation, I claim, misclassifies both distributed collective behaviors in non-human animals that lack representational mediation and the architecture of artificial systems that acquire linguistic competence through statistical pattern matching without representing linguistic structures as tools. The audience has three constituencies: philosophers of mind who treat the four-fold framework as a canonical naturalistic taxonomy and will resist post hoc subdivisions; comparative psychologists who will demand empirical criteria that distinguish the two proposed Gregorian sub-types in observable animal behavior; and philosophers of AI who will press whether the distinction applies coherently to systems that lack evolutionary history or explicit representational architectures. Please prepare my Q&A defense sheet. List the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased in the vocabulary and framing each constituency uses. For each objection, indicate whether I should concede, hold firm, or qualify my position; provide a concise reply suitable for delivery in two to three minutes; and explicitly flag the weakest point in that reply. Order them by combined likelihood and potential damage to the thesis. Include the objection I am hoping nobody asks—the one that targets the original motivation for splitting Gregorian minds rather than merely challenging the split’s coherence, empirical applicability, or extension to artificial systems—and draft both the objection as a hostile interlocutor would phrase it and a reply that does not simply restate the distinction. I will have forty-five minutes, with the final fifteen reserved for discussion.
