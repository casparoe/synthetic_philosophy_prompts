As a doctoral candidate in jurisprudence, I am developing a conceptual framework for my dissertation that tests the boundary between legal positivism and natural law through a constructed hypothetical. Write an essay that takes up the following controversy and reaches a defended conclusion.

Consider a hypothetical jurisdiction, Lexicon, whose constitutional order contains exactly two provisions. First, a meta-rule of interpretation states that whenever a statute is ambiguous, judges must resolve the ambiguity by appealing to the prevailing moral common sense of the community, not to legislative history or textual canons alone. Second, a supremacy clause requires that no official action—including judicial interpretation—may violate any of Fuller's eight procedural principles of legality: generality, promulgation, non-retroactivity, clarity, non-contradiction, possibility of compliance, relative constancy over time, and congruence between declared rules and official conduct.

The controversy is this: Does Lexicon's system vindicate H.L.A. Hart's claim that law and morality are conceptually separable, with moral criteria entering law only through contingent social conventions captured by a rule of recognition? Or does it instead vindicate Lon Fuller's claim that law necessarily embodies an internal morality and that extreme violations of procedural legality cause rules to fail as law altogether?

In your essay, weigh both interpretations. Specifically, defend a verdict on whether treating moral common sense as an interpretive criterion collapses Hart's separation thesis into Fuller's natural law conditions, or whether Hart can accommodate Lexicon by arguing that the moral criterion is itself validated by a conventional rule of recognition rather than by necessary moral truth. Your conclusion should be substantive—do not end on an agnostic note—though a nuanced intermediate position, for example that Lexicon reveals a false dichotomy, is permissible if rigorously justified. Do not provide a bibliography or citation list; reason directly from the conceptual machinery of both positions. I need this for conceptual clarity, not for source gathering.
