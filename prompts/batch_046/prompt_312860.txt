I want you to restate the core dispute—particularly the Walzerian defence that uniformed soldiers on opposing sides share an equal permission to kill one another regardless of the justice of their cause, and the revisionist challenge that only those who pose an unjust threat forfeit their protection—entirely within my vocabulary. Treat combatant status as a role assigned through a costly separating signal (the uniform functioning as an observable marker in a uniform-signaling ESS model), treat the permission or prohibition on killing as an allocation rule in an asymmetric repeated game, and treat notions such as innocence, threat, or moral desert as properties of a player’s strategy profile and causal contribution rather than of any intrinsic status. Map the distinction between jus ad bellum and jus in bello onto constraints in mechanism design, and show me precisely where each philosophical claim lands in this framework.
