From what I understand, the catuskoti lays out four exhaustive ontological registers for any phenomenon: it exists, it doesn't exist, it both exists and doesn't, and it neither exists nor doesn't. Then Madhyamaka emptiness comes in as this kind of meta-register, a fifth-layer substrate that holds all four in suspension without collapsing them into contradiction. My professor mentioned that this sounds more like a modal realist picture with four possible worlds stacked on top of each other, but I don't see why that's wrong—if emptiness is just the absence of svabhāva, doesn't that mean the four registers are all equally real but lacking intrinsic nature?

So here's where I'm stuck. If we take this five-layer model seriously—where emptiness functions as a kind of mereological glue binding the four corners—wouldn't it actually solve the problem of parts and wholes that modern metaphysics struggles with? Instead, emptiness as the fifth layer explains how they can be dependantly originated without being reducible to parts or reducible away into a whole. Is this why Nāgārjuna rejects all four positions individually but keeps the framework intact as a kind of hierarchical ontology?
