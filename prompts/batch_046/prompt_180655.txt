I'm working on a story about chaos and determinism and a physicist friend walked me through this argument. It feels like it proves determinism collapses in any chaotic domain, which can't be right. (1) If determinism holds, then fixing the exact initial state plus the laws fixes exactly one future trajectory. (2) In chaotic systems — weather, brains, whatever — Lyapunov exponents mean any epsilon error in measurement blows up exponentaly, so observers never actually have the exact initial state. (3) There's the shadowing lemmma: near any approximate trajectory there's a true deterministic orbit that stays close. (4) But the lemma only guarantees existence, not uniqueness; inside our error ball there are infinitly many true initial conditions, hence infinitly many real trajectories that all shadow our data equally well. (5) So determinism combined with chaos means our evidence is compatible with countless actual futures. (6) But determinism was supposed to mean there's only one actual future given the real initial conditions, not just given our ignorance. (7) Therefore in chaotic domains determinism is empirically empty — it can't distinguish itself from a branching multiverse — and applying it to brains makes determinism about human action incoherent. Where's the slip? I'd prefer to keep determinism if possible, but I need to know which step I'm paying for and what the cost is.
