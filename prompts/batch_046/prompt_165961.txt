I have just spent an evening arguing with a close friend about dialetheism and paraconsistent logic, and we have reached the kind of impasse that feels less like disagreement and more like two people talking past one another. We both accept the formal results: we agree that in relevant paraconsistent systems the principle of explosion is blocked, that the liar paradox and boundary cases of change can be formalised without triviality, and that dialetheists claim some contradictions are genuinely true rather than merely tolerated. My friend insists that accepting a true contradiction is not a metaphysical discovery but a decision about how to regiment negation — she suggests that dialetheists treat negation as contrary opposition, allowing both a statement and its denial to hold at a boundary, whereas classical logicians treat it as exhaustive exclusion, and that the dispute is therefore about the semantics of logical notation rather than about reality. I countered that this mischaracterises the dialetheist's position: they are not merely redefining negation but arguing that certain semantic paradoxes reveal that reality itself can be inconsistent, so the choice of formal system tracks an ontological feature, not a linguistic convention. Yet after an hour we had not moved. Is this stalemate a hidden verbal dispute about whether negation functions as contrary opposition or exhaustive exclusion, masked by shared formal vocabulary? Or is there a substantive metaphysical disagreement about whether contradiction can be instantiated, with both sides using the same logical machinery to answer different questions — one about correct reasoning, the other about the structure of the world? Could it even be that the question is ill-posed because it conflates semantic ascent, in choosing a logic, with ontological commitment, in claiming the world is inconsistent? I want a diagnosis that engages with what each side actually says about the relationship between formal paraconsistency and ontological inconsistency, not a survey of interpretations. And given your diagnosis, is there anything — a formal result distinguishing contrary opposition from exclusion, a conceptual distinction about boundary cases, or a reframing that separates logic-choice from world-description — that could genuinely advance the debate, or should we expect permanent deadlock?
