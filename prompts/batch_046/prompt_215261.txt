Write a deadpan, satirical government FAQ—structured as exactly five numbered sections, with no introduction or conclusion, in American English—for a fictional nation called Aleatoria that has abolished elections in favor of the Recursive Delegation Lot (RDL). In this system, every adult is technically entered into the selection pool, but an opaque “deliberative temperament audit” designed by the previous session’s delegates filters the pool before any draw; those chosen serve one seven-day legislative term, after which each delegate must randomly designate one non-delegate citizen to inherit the seat, producing an unbroken chain that official brochures describe as “pure randomness with accumulated wisdom.” Keep the voice consistently that of a cheerful, unwavering bureaucrat: the humor and critique should emerge purely through deadpan exposition, so that a reader notices—without any explicit statement—the circularity of delegates authoring the audit criteria that select future delegates, the absence of meaningful accountability in a seven-day term, and the quiet demographic sorting hidden inside the language of “temperament.”

Your satire should land the standard objections to sortition—who controls the screening mechanism, whether competence at the audit translates to competence at governing, and what happens to representation when delegation chains drift away from the original random sample—entirely through the bureaucrat’s own explanations. Invent one tangible institutional ritual to ground the absurdity, such as a national holiday called Chain of Chance during which citizens exchange small wooden tokens representing delegated authority, and include at least one invented procedural detail—perhaps a “continuity clause” that treats a broken chain as a temporary vacancy filled by the audit committee—that sounds plausibly administrative yet quietly undermines the system’s democratic pretenses. Aim for roughly 100 words per section, so the whole piece reads like an authentic government brochure that has slowly, politely gone wrong.

Present each section as a numbered item (1 through 5), with no introductory paragraph and no concluding remarks; the first item should open immediately with the FAQ’s welcome notice, and the fifth should end on the practical tourist question about attending a session without accidentally being drafted into the recursive chain. Do not include citations, reading lists, or references to real-world scholars; all terminology should either be common knowledge in democratic theory or invented specifically for Aleatoria’s RDL.
