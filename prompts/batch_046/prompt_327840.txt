A hypothetical International Naming Tribunal has ruled as follows on whether to apply the term "genocide" in its full legal-moral sense (beyond "atrocity" or "crimes against humanity"). Ruling 1: a campaign of direct mass execution of an ethnic group—genocide. Ruling 2: a state program of forced assimilation, language suppression, and physical destruction of cultural archives, with no killings—genocide. Ruling 3: targeted assassination of a group's knowledge-keepers combined with burning of repositories holding oral and written history—genocide. Ruling 4: direct state-engineered famine in a specific territory with documented specific intent to destroy the group—genocide. The Tribunal now faces Case 5: a state directs proprietary AI content-moderation and recommendation architectures to systematically suppress a minority language and erase digital historical archives; internal documents show explicit intent to dissolve the group's collective identity; no physical violence or direct detention occurs, but linguistic continuity collapses within a generation through mediated algorithmic exclusion alone. If the Tribunal wants to remain consistent with its own track record, how should it rule? Identify the closest analog among Rulings 1–4, lay out the competing principles that would justify either extending or withholding the genocide label, state what principle the Tribunal would be adopting with each choice if its precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, and specify the further case that would best distinguish those principles. Also say what argument or consideration would change your recommendation, and note where your reasoning is most vulnerable. Write the response in American English.
