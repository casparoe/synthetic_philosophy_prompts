I've been working through the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe and tried to extend it one step further to see what ontological commitments it actually carries. The result feels like it proves far too much, but I cannot locate the precise leak. Here is the chain as I have it:

(1) Special relativity establishes that simultaneity is frame-relative for spacelike-separated events: for any two such events E and F, there exists an inertial frame in which they occur at the same coordinate time, and others in which they do not.

(2) If presentism is true, there is a metaphysically privileged present slice containing exactly those events that are genuinely "now."

(3) Because simultaneity is frame-relative, there is no frame-independent fact about which spacelike-separated events belong to that privileged slice; different frames assign incompatible memberships.

(4) To avoid making presentness an arbitrary, frame-indexed property with no metaphysical weight, the presentist must either deny the principle of relativity or deny that spacelike-separated events can differ in presentness.

(5) Denying the principle of relativity is physically untenable; denying that they can differ in presentness collapses presentism into eternalism, since all events assignable to "now" in some legitimate frame must be included in the real.

(6) So eternalism follows: all events in spacetime exist with equal ontological status, tenselessly.

(7) Now consider what I will call transitive co-presence. If event A is simultaneous with B in frame F₁, and B is simultaneous with C in frame F₂, and frame equivalence gives each assignment equal metaphysical standing, then A stands in the same ontological relation to B as B does to C.

(8) By transitivity, A and C are co-present, despite being arbitrarily distant in space and time.

(9) Iterating this chaining across the manifold, every event is co-present with every other.

(10) But if every event is co-present with every other, then temporal separation is not merely tenseless but ontologically void: there survives no structural distinction between "before" and "after," and spacetime collapses into an undifferentiated totality, contradicting the very geometry that generated the argument.

I want to preserve (6), and I am reluctant to reject frame equivalence, though I am open to abandoning (7) if that is where the error hides. Where exactly does this go wrong? Is there an equivocation between frame-relative simultaneity and metaphysical co-presence, a scope or quantifier shift in how "present" is applied across frames, or does eternalism genuinely commit us to (9) and we must instead reject (5) or (6)? What does each option cost?
