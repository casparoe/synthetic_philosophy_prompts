I hold a strong Gricean view of speaker meaning and conversational implicature: speaker meaning is exhaustively determined by the speaker's M-intention — the intention to produce a belief in the hearer by means of the hearer's recognition of that very intention — and every genuine implicature is calculable from the Cooperative Principle together with the maxims of Quantity, Quality, Relation, and Manner, leaving no residue that requires lexical convention, semantic rule, or non-rational inference. Even apparently non-cooperative speech — irony, strategic ambiguity, adversarial utterance — is treated as a locally suspended maxim whose suspension is itself rationally intended and calculably recoverable. What would count against this view? I want more than the standard objections about conventional implicature or lexicalized inferences. Specifically: which empirical or formal results should move me? For example, are there documented cases in which hearers recover an intended meaning that cannot be reconstructed through any plausible first-order or second-order application of the maxims, even allowing that the speaker intends the hearer to recognize the maxim-flouting? What about formal pragmatic frameworks — information-theoretic, game-theoretic, or Bayesian — that generate predictions about implicature recovery systematically diverging from human judgments in ways that cannot be dismissed as performance error? And are there commitments this view makes about the persistence of speaker-meaning across collective or institutional contexts — for instance, about whether meaning can survive the absence of any individual M-intention — that turn out to be unacceptable? Rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. For each, indicate whether a committed Gricean should concede it as damaging, or whether it can be absorbed by a qualification of the original thesis — and if absorbed, whether the qualification risks rendering the view unfalsifiable. I want an honest assessment of how exposed the position actually is, including whether its insulation is a theoretical virtue or a methodological vice.
