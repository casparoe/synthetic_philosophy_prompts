For a workshop on meta-epistemology I am drafting my position on higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia, and I would like a consistency audit of the package before I present it. Here are the commitments I currently hold:

(1) Epistemic akrasia is rationally impermissible in every form: if I take my belief that P to be inadequately supported by my total evidence, then rationality requires me not to believe P.

(2) Higher-order defeat is level-sensitive: evidence that my reasoning is unreliable in a domain D undermines my first-order beliefs in D, but it does not undermine the higher-order belief about my unreliability, even when that belief was produced by the reasoning now in question.

(3) In peer disagreement, conciliation is required for empirical and predictive claims, but not for normative or conceptual ones; moreover, my judgment that a particular interlocutor is an epistemic peer is itself a normative assessment and therefore immune to conciliationist revision.

(4) Self-locating beliefs about my own current cognitive state—e.g., that I am presently fatigued—are not supported evidentially in the same manner as world-directed beliefs, which means they may be retained with full confidence even when I possess higher-order reason to doubt my capacity for accurate self-assessment.

(5) Reflective equilibrium is the correct method for resolving conflicts among epistemic norms, and it applies uniformly: if my object-level and meta-level commitments clash, I should revise the less central one regardless of whether the conflict is first-order or second-order.

Please examine these five theses. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? For each real tension, specify its severity, identify which commitment must be modified or abandoned, and say what that revision costs. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Finally, tell me what single revision—if I am forced to make only one—would restore the greatest coherence to the set, and whether that revision leaves the remainder of the package intact.
