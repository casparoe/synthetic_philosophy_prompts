Next week I defend a thesis chapter that applies Benacerraf's dilemma to computer-verified mathematics. My claim is that when a mathematician knows a theorem through a formal proof assistant, the causal chain runs only through software and hardware, never touching the abstract objects the theorem concerns, yet we do not demote that knowledge to second-class status. I take this to show that the causal theory of knowledge fails for mathematics not because mathematics is metaphysically special, but because all instrument-mediated knowledge breaks the direct causal link—so Benacerraf's first horn is blunted, but only if we accept mediated knowledge as genuine across domains.
