(1) For an individual act to be wrong on harm-based grounds, it must make a causal difference to the harm. (3) So harm-based morality doesn't condemn those individual acts. (4) Apply the same principle to collective goods: no single speaker's practice determines language survival, no single citizen's compliance determines institutional integrity, and no single researcher's effort determines scientific progress. (5) Therefore individuals never have harm-based obligations to maintain collective goods, which means free-riding, neglect, or withdrawal are never wrong in that register. (6) But that can't be right: deliberately undermining a shared institution you benefit from feels wrong in a way that needs explaining. (7) So (1) is false—wrongness doesn't require causal efficacy.
