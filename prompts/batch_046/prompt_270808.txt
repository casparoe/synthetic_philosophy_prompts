I am a doctoral student in history and philosophy of science advising a forecasting-audit office that evaluates predictive theories with horizons of fifty to two hundred years, particularly in resource and demographic dynamics. The office needs a reusable decision procedure—one that analysts without philosophical training can apply repeatedly—to classify a theory whose quantitative predictions have been contradicted by observed outcomes. Drawing on the conceptual problem that arises with Malthus-style predictions—whether a theory can be correct in its dynamics yet contradicted because it is premature or because its boundary conditions changed—the procedure must distinguish three outcomes: abandonment as genuinely falsified; retention through a legitimate rescue that preserves core dynamics while adjusting auxiliary parameters or boundary conditions; and retention on the ground that the predicted outcome is delayed rather than avoided.

Please construct this as an ordered sequence of questions or tests, specifying for each what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure terminates with rejection, where it terminates with retention, and where it must defer to a human panel because the issue is methodological convention or values rather than fact. The procedure must handle hard cases: quantitative failure coupled with qualitative success; predictions saved by technological interventions not included in the original model; and predictions whose time horizon exceeds available observation windows. It should also flag conditions under which it will misfire—specifically, where the line between structural rescue and ad hoc immunization collapses.

Then apply the procedure to three cases: (a) the classic Malthusian claim that food production grows arithmetically while population grows geometrically, contradicted by sustained per-capita food growth across two centuries; (b) a hypothetical 1970s forecast that global phosphorus reserves would be depleted by 2050, contradicted by improved extraction and recycling technologies; and (c) a demographic model predicting rapid regional population collapse that instead observed gradual decline and aging. Include at least one case in which the procedure recommends retaining the theory despite strong apparent falsification—an answer the office is likely to resist—and state explicitly what the procedure cannot adjudicate, such as whether retaining a contradicted theory is epistemically sound or merely politically expedient. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then provide the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
