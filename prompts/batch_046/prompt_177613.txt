I was trying to work through that color-phi illusion on my own—the one where two dots flash in different positions and colors, yet you swear you saw a single dot travel and shift color right in the middle, even though nothing was ever physically there. I started wondering what that means for when an experience actually takes place, and I ended up with an argument that feels like it destroys either our present-moment awareness or the very distinction I was trying to use. Here it is, step by step.

(1) In the color-phi case, the brain clearly needs both flashes registered before it can construct the story of motion and the intermediate color change, so the conscious experience of that middle moment must be assembled only after the second flash arrives. (2) If we call this a Stalinesque revision, then the experience itself is fabricated and inserted back into the timeline; you never genuinely lived through that middle instant in real time, you only acquired a constructed representation of it afterward. (3) But every conscious experience involves neural processing delays, so by the same reasoning every experience is assembled after its triggering event and then assigned to an earlier "now." (4) Therefore, if color phi is Stalinesque, then all consciousness is Stalinesque: we are never actually experiencing the present, only retroactively built representations mislassigned to false times. (5) That makes the judgment "I am conscious right now" systematically false, since the "now" of judgment is always a reconstructed past state. (6) The only escape is to say color phi is Orwellian instead: the middle experience genuinely occurred in real time, and only our later report or memory of it is mistaken. (7) But that requires the brain to have generated an experience of green before the green stimulus ever arrived, which is backward causation. (8) So either all conscious experience is a self-undermining retroactive fiction, or consciousness routinely reaches backward in time. (9) Therefore the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction is useless for explaining the timing of experience, and color phi reveals nothing special—just an exaggerated version of how all awareness works.

This feels like it proves far too much, but I cannot see the exit. I want to keep both the idea that there is a real difference between fabricating an experience and misremembering one, and the intuition that I am genuinely experiencing something in the present rather than replaying a delayed reconstruction. If both of those stay, which step must I reject, and what does giving it up cost me? Is the slide hidden in treating all processing delay as Stalinesque revision, or is there something wrong with assuming that real-time experience requires simultaneous physical stimulation?
