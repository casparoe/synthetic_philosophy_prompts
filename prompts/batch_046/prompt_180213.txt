Write a pantoum of at least six four-line stanzas about deflationary theories of truth, using the form’s mandatory repetition—where the second and fourth lines of each quatrain become the first and third lines of the next—to enact the deflationary claim that adding a truth-predicate to a claim (“it is true that p”) introduces no substantive metaphysical content beyond the original claim (“p”). Within this framework, maintain a distinction between two versions of the view: a “disquotational” version, in which repetition is mechanically identical and serves only logical ascent and endorsement, and a “prosentential” version, in which repetition still lacks metaphysical weight yet carries minimal expressive or normative force. Structure the stanzas so that they alternate between these versions: in disquotational stanzas, the recycled lines should recur verbatim, enacting pure redundancy; in prosentential stanzas, the same lines should return with a barely perceptible shift in punctuation, register, or connotation, enacting the idea that something—however thin—is still being done. By the closing stanza, in which lines from the opening quatrain should reappear according to standard pantoum closure, the poem should leave explicit but unresolved whether the accumulated repetitions have constructed any metaphysical architecture or have merely circled back to their origin. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of language can identify which stanzas represent which version of deflationism and can recognize the structural argument, while remaining coherent and moving for a reader who has never encountered the debate. Do not include citations, named authorities, or reading suggestions.
