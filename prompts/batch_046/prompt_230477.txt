I have recently been involved in caring for a family member whose dementia is progressing, which has made me unusually attentive to discussions of whether artificial systems could possess something analogous to self-awareness. In these conversations I repeatedly encounter the same argumentative pattern. A speaker notes that Gödel showed how a formal system can contain a formula that encodes its own syntactic structure and asserts its own unprovability. They then observe that writers such as Hofstadter have drawn analogies between this kind of self-reference and the emergence of conscious minds from formal substrate. From there, the speaker extends the point: if a system—say, a large language model—can represent its own representations or track its own outputs, then it inherits the Gödelian situation, necessarily possessing true beliefs about itself that it cannot verify, with these structural blind spots constituting the core of self-awareness.

What strikes me is not the analogy itself but a specific slide within it. The argument treats syntactic self-reference, in which a formula bears a Gödel number encoding its own formal structure, as if it were already semantic self-modeling, in which a system employs a representation of itself to guide behavior. It then treats the formal limitation on derivability—the presence of true but unprovable sentences within a fixed axiomatic framework—as if it automatically constrained the epistemic completeness or reliability of that self-model. The speaker does not distinguish cases in which extensive self-representation occurs without any corresponding formal undecidability, nor cases in which formal undecidability arises in the absence of any meaningful introspective model.

Is there an established term for this particular analogical failure—the conflation of syntactic self-reference with introspective self-modeling, coupled with the unwarranted transfer of Gödelian incompleteness results to claims about necessary limits on self-knowledge? If the philosophy of mind or logic literature offers a precise diagnosis of where this analogy breaks down, I would value knowing its name. If there is no such term, I would appreciate a careful definition that marks exactly when a system's self-representation genuinely invites Gödelian analysis and when it does not, distinguishing this error from both legitimate structural analogy and from ordinary category mistakes.
