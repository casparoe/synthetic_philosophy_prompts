i've been trying to formalize the frame problem for a seminar and now i'm stuck in an argument that seems to prove rational planning is literally impossible. here's the chain:

(1) if you're rational, then after you act you only update beliefs that actually changed.
(2) to do that you need frame axioms listing everything that stayed the same — the sky's color, the president, your shoe size.
(3) for any real-world action the non-changes outnumber the changes by orders of magnitude.
(4) so rational belief-update requires representational resources that scale exponetially with your belief base.
(5) no embodied system has that kind of capacity.
(6) but people plan, act, and update beliefs constantly without melting down.
(7) so humans can't be running explicit frame axioms.
(8) without explicit axioms there's no principled boundary between 'relevant' and 'remote' effects, so every button press might have teleported your coffee cup to mars for all you know.
(9) that means you'd have to reconsider every belief after every action, which is just the frame problem in reverse.
(10) therefore rational planning needs infinite capacity, which is impossible, so rational planning doesn't exist.

it feels like (8) is doing something sneaky by equating 'no explicit axiom' with 'no constraint at all,' but i can't name the move. which step do i reject, and what does it cost me? i'd like to keep (1) and (6) if i can, but i want to know what i'm paying.
