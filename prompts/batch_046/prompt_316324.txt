Write an essay on whether prioritarianism genuinely escapes the leveling-down objection, or whether the objection just mutates and comes back through the wieghting function. Now imagine a budget cut that hits the misclassified "better-off" group, doesn't actually reach anyone genuinely worse off, but makes the measured gap shrink and triggers a reallocation away from them. Is that leveling-down in the morally relevant sense? Or does the epistemic noise break the analogy? Weigh both sides, but don't sit on the fence—definately defend a clear verdict on whether prioritarianism survives here or needs a different kind of defense, and say what that implies for how weighted allocation rules should actually be designed. Keep it philosophical, not policy-wonkish, but don't shy away from the mechanics.
