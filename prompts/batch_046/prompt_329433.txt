Write a dialogue between a Confucian role ethicist and a cosmopolitan liberal debating whether a wealthy state owes stronger climate-adaptation obligations to its own citizens than to poor nations harmed by climate change. The Confucian should argue from differentiated care scaled to institutional roles—the state as a relational position with primary obligations to those within its sphere, without licensing indifference to outsiders—while the liberal should challenge whether this analogy survives once causal responsibility crosses borders, and whether it avoids collapsing into arbitrary partiality. Each speaker must respond directly to the other's actual points; neither should be a strawman. After the dialogue, include a brief committed note stating which argument you find stronger, identifying the specific internal tension that weakens the opposing view, and giving your reasons rather than presenting both sides neutrally. Keep the prompt focused, write in formal American English, and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
