For my junior-year philosophy seminar, I need to compare two responses to the combination problem in panpsychism—constitutive panpsychism, which treats macro-consciousness as literally composed of micro-level experiences, and cosmopsychism, which derives individual minds from a single, undifferentiated cosmic consciousness. Rather than repeating the standard catalog of objections, I want to focus on a more specific tension: introspective accessibility. If my own consciousness is either built out of or carved out of innumerable micro-subjects, are those micro-subjects in principle available to my introspection? Or does each view require a kind of phenomenal opacity that makes the micro-level permanently hidden from the macro-level subject? To ground the comparison, imagine a hypothetical neurotechnology that could isolate and report the experiential state of a single neuron—would that report describe something recognizably part of my own experience, or something irreducibly alien? Please argue which view handles the tension between compositional transparency and subjective unity more coherently, and do not provide a bibliography or citation list; I am looking for conceptual analysis that I can adapt for my own essay.
