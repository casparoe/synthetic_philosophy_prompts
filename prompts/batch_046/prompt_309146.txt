My roommates got into it over Grice and speaker meaning last night. (1) One said Grice's real view is that speaker meaning is just M-intention all the way down: you mean that p by uttering X only if you intend your listener to recogize you intend them to think p, and you intend that recognition to actually cause them to think p. She said that's why "it's cold in here" can literally mean "close the window" — the speaker meaning just is that request, not an implicature calculated separately. (2) The other said she's mixing up Grice's 1957 Meaning paper with the Logic and Conversation stuff. In that later work, implicature is computed from the cooperative principle and maxims, not from direct mental-state recognition. So "some" meaning "not all" doesn't require anyone to read the speaker's mind; it's just a default inference from Quantity. (3) She came back saying even in Logic and Conversation the cooperative principle only works because listeners recognize that speakers intend to be cooperative. The M-intention structure is hidden in the machinery. Without it you can't tell implicature from accidental correlation — like if I always sneeze when I lie, that's not implicature. (4) He said that's overintellectualizing. Conventionalized implicatures like "some" -> "not all" work even when listeners don't think about intentions at all; they just apply the rule. The speaker's private mental state is irrelevant once the pattern is established. (5) Then she threw in a twist: what if two friends have a private code where "some" actually means "all" in their specific conversations? Then the conventional rule is suspended, and you have to ask what the speaker specifically intended in this context. He said okay, that's the exception that proves the rule — in normal cases it's conventional, and only in weird private codes do you need M-intentions. She said no, that shows M-intentions are always doing the work, even when they align with convention.

Please adjudicate move by move — who's right, what's wrong, and whether both are missing something about how Grice actually relates speaker meaning to implicature.
