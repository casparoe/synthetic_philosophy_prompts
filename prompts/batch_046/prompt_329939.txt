I'm working through Frege's political diary, composed in 1924 and published posthumously, and I'm stuck on how to read a specific entry in relation to his philosophy of logic. The entry, written in the spring of 1924, criticizes parliamentary democracy as a system that disperses authority among subjective opinions rather than directing the people toward an objective national destiny; it praises the idea of a single leader who can transcend individual preference, and it expresses concern that Jewish political participation introduces an alien element that prevents the German people from achieving a unified, objective cultural form.

On the surface this is just ugly politics, but I'm trying to understand whether there's a hidden philosophical architecture behind it. Frege's logic depends on a sharp separation between objective thoughts (Gedanken), which are mind-independent and shareable, and subjective ideas (Vorstellungen), which are psychological and private. His anti-psychologism treats logical laws as governing an objective realm, not as generalizations about human thinking.

The passage seems to apply a structurally analogous distinction to politics: democratic deliberation is treated like psychologism—an aggregation of private, subjective opinions—while authoritarian leadership is treated like logical objectivity, a direct access to an objective national essence that individuals must submit to rather than construct. Is this a consistent extension of his philosophical framework, or is it a category mistake that reveals the limits of importing logical objectivity into political value?

More specifically, I'm wondering whether the diary reveals an implicit platonism about culture—treating the German people as an objective, non-psychological entity analogous to the number two, and treating democratic disagreement as a kind of logical contradiction that must be eliminated rather than accommodated. If that's the right reading, does it mean his philosophy contains resources for authoritarian politics that are usually ignored because commentators focus only on the logic? Or does the diary instead show that he failed to apply his own anti-psychologism consistently, allowing private racial prejudice (Vorstellung) to masquerade as objective national thought (Gedanke)?

I'd like help understanding the passage in these terms—particularly whether there's any evidence in Frege's published philosophical work that he intended his logical distinctions to carry normative or political weight, or whether the diary should be read as an entirely separate, psychologically driven document that tells us nothing about the structure of his logic. I'm less interested in biographical moral judgment than in whether the philosophical machinery he built for mathematics and logic has hidden political analogues that become visible only in these private entries. If the analogical reading holds, which specific features of his logical realism—mind-independence, shareability, normative force—map cleanly onto the political claims in the entry, and which ones break down?
