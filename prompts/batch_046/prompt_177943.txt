I sit on the governance committee of a decentralized research collective. Individual scholars retain their own funding and institutional affiliations, yet they share a common digital repository, a collective peer-review protocol, and an agreed-upon research agenda. A funding agency has recently asked whether we should apply as a single organizational applicant or as a consortium of independent applicants. The members are divided: some argue that shared infrastructure and coordinated output make us one body, while others insist that legal and financial independence makes us many. I am tasked with drafting a recommendation, but I find myself unable to settle the prior factual question—does this collective genuinely compose one research entity, or is it only a loose aggregation? The boundaries are genuinely indeterminate: participation is continuous rather than binary, resources flow informally, and there is no charter defining membership conditions. If the parts and their relations do not uniquely determine whether a whole exists, what should guide our decision? Should we treat the vagueness as evidence that there is no correct ontological answer, or should we adopt a practical criterion that resolves the ambiguity for institutional purposes?
