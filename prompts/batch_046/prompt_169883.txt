hey, I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether everyday conventions are moral obligations, especially when you're visiting somewhere. Here are my gut calls, and they're kind of scattered.

If I'm in the US and I don't tip at least 15-20% for okay service, that feels genuinely wrong, not just rude. But if I'm in Japan and I leave a tip for great service, that also feels wrong - like I'm treating staff like charity cases. So tipping norms seem to bind both directions?

Queueing: using my backpack to save a spot in a London coffee line feels like cheating, clearly wrong. But if I'm in Rome and people kind of swarm the bus door without a real line, me insisting on strict order and scolding people feels wrong too - like I'm importing rules that don't fit the context.

Jaywalking in NYC with zero traffic feels harmless, barely a rule-breaking. But in Berlin, crossing against the light right in front of a school where kids watch - even with no cars - I keep going back and forth. Sometimes it feels wrong because I'm modeling law-breaking; other times it feels like nobody actually cares.

Here's the pair that really bugs me: if I visit a market in Marakech and pay the first price for a rug because haggling makes me uncomfortable, I feel foolish but not immoral. But if I'm back home and try to haggle at a grocery store, that feels disrespectful, like I'm violating a real social contract. So not-following a local convention is okay in one place but wrong in another, and I can't see the line that seperates them.

There's also this weird "ghost" norm in some Nordic countries where you don't sit right next to a stranger on an empty bus. If I do it as a tourist, am I being rude? Is that morality or just etiquette?

What principle, if any, captures all this? Maybe something about coordination harm versus dignity harm? And which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? Please also say what it predicts for a case I haven't listed - say, should a tourist in Singapore avoid chewing gum in public just because it's banned, and is it wrong to talk loudly in a cafe in Japan but okay in Italy? I need an honest statement of which verdict doesn't fit and what the resulting account says about visitors in general.
