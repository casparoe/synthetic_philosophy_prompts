I recently joined the ethics review panel for a state-level wellbeing infrastructure board, and I am now responsible for evaluating proposals that trade discomfort or suffering for gains in quality of life—urban noise-abatement installations, licensed recreational analgesic programs, voluntary cold-exposure health campaigns, and similar interventions. Our board has adopted a specific normative framework: a lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism that treats severe suffering as lexically dominant over any quantity of pleasure, paired with a strict incommensurability thesis holding that below that lexical threshold, pain and pleasure do not share a common metric and therefore cannot be summed, traded, or offset in a single calculus. Instead, evaluation below the threshold is supposed to run on two independent tracks—first minimize aggregate suffering using a cardinal severity scale, then determine whether the proposal clears a separate positive-welfare floor—without allowing pleasure gains to compensate for residual pains.

I need a reusable decision procedure that my team can complete in about thirty minutes per proposal, with the steps arranged in strict order, clear stopping rules that yield automatic rejection or conditional approval, explicit criteria for what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, and marked points where the framework forces a value judgment that cannot be disguised as arithmetic. The procedure must accommodate heterogeneous populations, in which some individuals bear the costs and others receive the benefits, and it should include a small set of diagnostic tests for cases where empirical data is missing or only qualitative. Please apply it to three proposals: a city-wide sound-abatement program that eliminates chronic noise stress for eighty percent of residents but causes temporary tinnitus in three percent; a licensed event program using a synthetic analgesic compound that produces intense short-term euphoria but carries a fifteen percent risk of lasting anhedonia; and a voluntary public-health campaign encouraging regular cold-water immersion that causes acute physical discomfort but is claimed to reduce long-term anxiety. Include at least one case where the procedure delivers a verdict I am likely to reject—I believe the second proposal should be permitted—and explain precisely which step generates the conflict. Finally, flag the boundary conditions where the incommensurability assumption collapses and a human reviewer must decide whether cross-track comparison is permissible.
