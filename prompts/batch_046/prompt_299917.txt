I am a PhD candidate in moral philosophy running a 90-minute workshop for fifteen MA-level students on whether maximal moral goodness is genuinely desirable. Rather than asking students to summarize familiar arguments, I want them to run into the problem structurally. My guiding interest is in a premise that often silently drives the intuition that moral sainthood is unattractive: the assumption that moral goods and personal projects are fungible within a single “life-plan budget.” I want the group to discover, through interaction, that if personal projects instead possess what I will call narrative incommensurability with moral goods—meaning they are constitutive of a life rather than consumable items within it—then the optimization model for moral improvement collapses and no non-arbitrary stopping point remains.

Please design a runnable classroom simulation or constrained role-play for this group and time frame, with explicit rules, roles, and small stakes, so that participants initially pursue collective moral maximization and then experience how that maximization erodes the very conditions that made their choices narratively meaningful. Specify a clear timeline within the 90 minutes, the mechanics by which moral “output” is scored, and the structural constraint that forces trade-offs. The rules—not the facilitator’s lecture—should generate the moment of breakdown.

Include a debrief that maps the key experiential moments onto Wolf’s distinction between the moral saint and the loving saint, and onto the fungibility assumption versus the narrative-incommensurability claim. Be frank about which feature of this idea—particularly the metaphysical claim about narrative constitution—the mechanics can only approximate and the facilitator must supply verbally. Also predict what will characteristically go wrong (for instance, participants conflating a structural constraint with a mere personal preference) and suggest a brief intervention to correct it, and note explicitly which aspect of the idea the activity is constitutionally unable to reproduce. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; the activity should work for students who have not read the source essay.
