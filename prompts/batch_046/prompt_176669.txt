hey, i'm building a sorting tree for a grad seminar on democratic backsliding — not the usual left/right label game, but what actually counts as the damage. i want students to land in one of about six conceptual frames by answering concrete institutional questions, not by picking a name they already like.

branch one: is the core problem vertical (elections and voting rights), horizontal (courts, agencies, civil service), or epistemic (the state's capacity to gather and act on information — census manipulation, statistical capture, not just media bias)?

branch two: do you require a discrete breakpoint event, or does gradual erosion count?

branch three: who gets epistemic authority to call it — domestic opposition, international observers, or the affected groups themselves?

the leaves should cover competitive authoritarianism (Levitsky/Way-style), liberal constitutional decay, participatory erosion, and at least one unnamed combo that sounds incoherent but might be occupied. for each leaf write one sentence on its most awkward commitment — like having to deny Hungary ever backslid since elections still technically happen, or treating US state-level voter suppression as a seperate category entirely. use Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the US as the concrete reference points.

phrase every node as a claim a student could answer without knowing where it leads — e.g., "if court-packing happened but elections stayed competitive, is that backsliding?" flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate, especially Poland-style judicial capture vs Hungary-style media capture. keep it to maybe 6 leaves max, audience is grad students who know the basic country references but shouldn't need to cite anyone. typo's are fine.
