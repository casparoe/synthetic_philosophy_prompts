A study-group handout I read summarizes the dogmatism paradox as follows. Once a rational agent knows that P, she can apply epistemic closure to derive that any future evidence against P must be misleading; she is therefore justified in permanently dismissing counterevidence, which makes downward revision irrational. So dogmatism is not a cognitive vice but a consistency requirement for anyone who knows anything. The handout adds that this “closure-based dogmatism” conflicts with Bayesian updating, because treating P as having prior probability 1 forces all counterevidence to have likelihood 0, violating Bayes’ rule. It then claims that recent regulative epistemology resolves the tension by introducing a “meta-evidential filter”: before an agent may invoke closure to ignore counterevidence, she must possess independent second-order knowledge that the source is unreliable; in ordinary inquiry this condition fails, so fallibilism is restored and dogmatism is blocked.
