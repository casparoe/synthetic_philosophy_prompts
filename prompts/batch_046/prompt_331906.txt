Write an essay on whether sustained epistemic dependence on large language models necessarily erodes human understanding, or whether such dependence can be reconceived as a benign extension of cognition akin to writing or computation. Your essay should take seriously the view that understanding is not reducible to true belief or predictive accuracy, but requires what philosophers of science sometimes call "structural transparency"—a grasp of the explanatory and inferential dependencies that link evidence to conclusion. Weigh the objection that outsourcing reasoning to AI collapses the user's own explanatory map, rendering beliefs brittle and unowned, against the reply that cognitive tools have always mediated understanding without destroying it. To move beyond a generic pro-and-con format, introduce and defend your own conceptual distinction between "instrumental delegation," in which the user retains the capacity to reconstruct the reasoning path if needed, and "hermeneutic outsourcing," in which the reasoning path is not merely hidden but structurally unavailable because the system optimizes for coherent outputs rather than traceable justifications. Reach a definite verdict: does hermeneutic outsourcing constitute a categorically different threat to understanding, and if so, why? Ground your conclusion in at least one concrete domain—such as scientific peer review, legal interpretation, or mathematical proof verification—where the distinction between these two modes of dependence could be empirically or normatively tested, and explain what conditions would have to hold for delegation to remain instrumentally safe rather than hermeneutically corrosive.
