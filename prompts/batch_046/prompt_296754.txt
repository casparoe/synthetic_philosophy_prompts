Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a defender of French republican laïcité—who maintains that state neutrality requires the absence of conspicuous religious symbols in public institutions and that citizenship precedes group identity—and a defender of Anglo-American multicultural recognition—who maintains that state neutrality requires equal accommodation of group-differentiated practices and that citizenship is enriched by public recognition of minority identities. The statement should contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, including shared descriptions of the formal structure of laïcité and multicultural policy, the empirical fact that both regimes generate tension with minority religious practices, and the shared normative commitment to peaceful coexistence and gender equality; (2) a disagreements table stating each side’s position in wording that side would accept, distinguishing empirical disagreements (e.g., whether removing visible religious symbols in schools improves social cohesion) from normative disagreements (e.g., whether equality requires sameness of treatment or proportionate recognition of difference); (3) at least two cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving one in either direction would cause the opposing side to revise a central claim—specifically, whether “visibility neutrality” can be coherently maintained without “practice neutrality,” and whether an integration contract that channels religious expression into private life remains genuinely voluntary when structural incentives penalize public expression; (4) for each crux, one concrete case or argument both sides agree is relevant, with each side’s predicted evaluation recorded in advance. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and phrase every disagreement so that neither party is asked to endorse a straw-man version of their view.
