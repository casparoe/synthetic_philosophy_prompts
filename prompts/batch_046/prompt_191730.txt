Write the full specification of desiderata that any adequate philosophical theory of opera's artistic and ethical significance must satisfy, taking Bernard Williams's scattered but distinctive remarks — notably his engagement with Janáček's opera on immortality and his broader resistance to reducing human particularity to moral system — as the guiding benchmark rather than as doctrine to be summarized. The list should cover: (1) the phenomena it must explain, including the coexistence of extreme emotional directness and narrative artifice, and the peculiar status of the singing body as both physically present to the audience and aesthetically transparent to the fictional world; (2) the cases it must get right, such as being moved by a character's particular situation without extracting a universalizable moral lesson, and accommodating the experience of vocal labor without collapsing the fiction into mere performance display; (3) the distinctions it must preserve, especially between having ethical significance and delivering moral instruction, between aesthetic appreciation and theatrical illusion, and between the work's narrative content and its embodied musical realization; (4) any results from aesthetics or philosophy of mind it must remain consistent with, such as the claim that emotional response to performed art is not fully dissociable from bodily awareness of the performer; (5) which of these are non-negotiable and which reflect the preferences of particular camps (formalists, moralists, phenomenologists). Crucially, inject and address a niche conceptual problem: call it the "vocal presence paradox" — the fact that the singer's laboring, breathing body is irreducibly present yet must be suppressed for the fictional character to hold — and specify whether resolving it requires a special ontology of performed art, a modified theory of theatrical convention, or the abandonment of one of the other desiderata. Then identify which single desideratum does the most to exclude the most popular accounts of opera's value, and flag any subset of requirements you take to be jointly unsatisfiable given Williams's commitments to irreducible particularity and anti-reductionism. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; brief scoring of formalist, moralistic, and phenomenological accounts against the list is permitted but should not dominate.
