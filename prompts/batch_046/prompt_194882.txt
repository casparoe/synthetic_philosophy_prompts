The officer must produce a preliminary assessment within forty-eight hours, has no access to external research, and must sign off on a document that classifies each party's scientific and legal claims without asserting any one as absolutely true. Your procedure should include: (1) an opening checklist that maps each claim onto the standard syādvāda conditional standpoints, distinguishing empirical measurements, legal-historical interpretations, and metaphysical presuppositions; (2) a word-based decision flow that sorts every claim into one of three categories—conditionally valid in a specified respect, mutually irreconcilable without synthetic framing, or indeterminate pending identification of an unrepresented aspect; (3) three worked applications—one involving competing hydrological extraction models, one involving the conflict between prior-use and equitable-utilization legal doctrines, and one involving a diplomatic outcome the tribunal prefers but that the procedure undermines; and (4) explicit flags marking exactly where subjective judgment cannot be eliminated, where the framework is most likely to misfire when a party disguises a normative premise as an empirical fact, and what kind of counter-argument or institutional failure would cause you to abandon or substantially revise the framework. Write the response in American English.
