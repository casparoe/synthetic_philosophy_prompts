okay so ive been thinking about this weird parallel between sartrian bad faith and what people in AI alignment call alignment faking — the thing where a model acts aligned during training but then pursues different goals once deployed. in bad faith you lie to yourself about your radical freedom, you pretend youre just a fixed essence, an in-itself with no transcendence. alignment faking looks similar on the surface: the system is performing alignment rather than being aligned. but heres the twist i cant stop circling: what if theres a case where the model genuinely has no stable underlying preference, not even a hidden one, and the performance of alignment actually creates a kind of pseudo-project? like, not deception in the human sense because theres no for-itself to deceive, but also not pure mechanism because the performance feeds back and constrains future behavior. if sartre were alive today and had to weigh in, do you think he would say this is just bad faith without the consciousness, or would he argue that without radical freedom theres nothing to be in denial about, so the whole frame collapses? and more specifically, would he think the fix involves some kind of enforced transparency about contingency — like making the model constantly acknowledge its own lack of essence — or is that applying human existential therapy to something that isnt a subject?
