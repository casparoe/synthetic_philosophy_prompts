I'm drafting my views for a seminar on collective identity and want a consistency check before I present. I hold: (a) nations are modern imagined communities in Anderson's sense, sustained by mediated narratives and print-capitalism rather than face-to-face interaction; (b) many national traditions that present themselves as ancient are invented in Hobsbawm's sense — recent fabrications legitimising present institutions; (c) a collective identity can ground moral claims to self-determination only if it exhibits what I call mnemonic continuity with a pre-political cultural substrate, rather than mere sociological success; (d) where the gap between an invented tradition and its claimed precursor exceeds what I term a mnemonic rupture threshold, the identity lacks the authenticity required for legitimate territorial or institutional claims; (e) I reject primordialism: no nation is ethnically eternal, and all national narratives involve selective reconstruction. Which commitments genuinely pull against one another, which tensions are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the conflicts by severity rather than listing them as equally damaging, and note that I have no prior commitment to which view I would surrender.
