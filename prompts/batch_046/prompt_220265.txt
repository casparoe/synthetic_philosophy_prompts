I'm on the review board for a regional archaeology authority and every month we recieve excavation reports claiming they've reconstructed specific beliefs or social institutions just from pots, pits, and wall foundations. I need a hard-nosed evaluation procedre I can actually run in about thirty minutes — a flowchart in words — for deciding whether a report's institutional claim is supported by the material or if it's just storytelling with dirt.

The principle I want turned into practice is that inferring an institution (not just repeated behavior) from objects requires passing three filters. First, behavioral specificity: the material trace has to be specific enough that it isn't equifinal with a bunch of mundane explanations. Second, negative-space evidence: there needs to be a prohibitive pattern — an absence, constraint, or asymmetry — that only makes sense if a rule is operating, not just habit or convenience. Third, relational analogy: the bridge from modern comparison to ancient case has to map the social logic onto the material logic, not just look formally similar on the surface. If any filter fails, the claim should be downgraded to behavior-only or rejected.

Write me the step-by-step procedure: the questions in order, what counts as passing each filter, where it stops with a hard no, and where it has to hand off to a person because the call is about values or interpretation risk rather than evidence. Give me a quick pattern-check for each filter — specific things to scan for in the report text. Then apply it to three cases: (1) a report arguing circular pit arrangements prove communal land-ownership norms; (2) a report arguing the total absence of metal tools in burial contexts proves a ritual taboo on iron; and (3) a well-funded report the board politically wants to accept, arguing standardized brick sizes across three distant sites prove a centralized religious authority directing labor. Include one case where the procedre gives an answer the board won't like. End by saying exactly what the procedure can't do — especially the line between reconstructing behavior and reconstructing belief — and flag any steps that are judgment calls dressed up as rules.
