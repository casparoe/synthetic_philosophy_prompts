I cover philosophy of language and mind for a science and ideas publication, and over the past decade I have watched the same debate resurface at every major conference: what exactly is wrong with Moore-paradoxical assertions—statements in which someone says, for instance, that it is raining while simultaneously denying belief in that very fact? The literature has long divided into three broad camps. One treats the paradox as a pragmatic contradiction in the very act of assertion, drawing on familiar ideas about the transparency of first-person belief reports. Another interprets it as an epistemic failure, a breach of the norms that govern rational self-knowledge. A third holds that such statements are strange but not impossible, merely describing an uncommon dissociation between what one says and what one takes oneself to believe. Despite extensive exchange, each camp accepts the same intuitive judgments about the absurdity of these utterances and the same standard thought experiments, yet the disagreement has not advanced in any measurable direction. My own hypothesis is that this is not a substantive dispute about belief or assertion at all, but a case of conceptual slippage masked by shared vocabulary: the phrase I believe is doing radically different work for each camp—sometimes naming a transparent mental state, sometimes tracking an introspective report, and sometimes serving as a behavioral disposition—while everyone assumes they are arguing about a single, unified phenomenon. Is this diagnosis correct? Or is there a hidden formal or empirical question—perhaps about the logic of self-reference or about the architecture of self-monitoring—that neither side has made explicit? I am asking for a diagnosis of the disagreement's structure, not a survey of the interpretations: tell me whether this is a verbal dispute, a clash of bedrock intuitions about mental transparency, or an ill-posed question that needs reframing. And given your diagnosis, what—if anything—would actually constitute progress: a conceptual distinction that separates the phenomena, a formal result about indexical belief, or a change in how we model first-person authority?
