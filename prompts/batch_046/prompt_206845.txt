student answer:

buridan's ass shows that when reason can't tell options apart, action stops. the donkey starves because there's literally no reason to go left instead of right. in modern terms, if utility is exactly equal, the choice function is undefined, so perfect indifference does paralyze the agent — they can't move at all.

some people say flip a coin, but that just moves the arbitrariness around. you still have no reason to pick heads over tails, so it's the same problem. actually, this is related to the principle of sufficient reason, which buridan used to argue that when there's no difference the will has to stay suspended.

there's also an existantial angle. sartre's concept of radical freedom applies here specifically to tie-breaking: we randomize our choice to prove we're not determined by reason. so sartre's solution is basically the coin flip, but for freedom instead of utility.

in my view, rationality actually demands inaction in these cases. if you act without any reason, you're being irrational — you're violating the basic rule that rational choice needs a reason. so buridan's donkey is being perfectly rational by starving; any movement would be an irrational leap.
