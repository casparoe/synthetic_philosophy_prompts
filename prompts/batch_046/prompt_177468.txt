I recently joined a mutual-aid collective and watched a decision collapse because every member believed they had privately agreed, yet no one felt bound by what “we” had done—so the gap between individual intention and collective commitment is now personally urgent. I am preparing a colloquium interview with an unnamed defender of a modified plural-subject account of joint action: she accepts Gilbert-style joint commitment but insists it generates irreducible plural obligations that cannot be decomposed into individual duty-sets, and she denies that shared mental states are necessary for joint action. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press the actual mechanics of her view rather than asking her to restate it. Include at least one on how plural commitment differs from a merely convergent set of individual intentions, one on whether a single member can unilaterally exit without dissolving the plural subject, and one on how her account handles cases in which participants hold incompatible interpretations of what “we” are doing. For each question, include a brief follow-up keyed to her most likely reply—for example, that exit requires collective release, or that the commitment itself resolves interpretive conflict rather than private belief alignment. Keep the whole response under 350 words; begin with one sentence stating the sharpest internal tension you see in her modified position, then present the questions with their follow-ups in a clear numbered format.
