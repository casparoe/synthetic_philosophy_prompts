In our seminar on percetual psychology we've been circling the cognitive-penetration debate for like a year and nobody's budged. The usual setup: do beliefs or expectations literally alter what you see, or do they just bias your report after the fact? Both sides point to the same expectation-bias expiriments and say "see, my interpretation fits." Lately I wonder if the real stall isn't about penetration at all but about a hidden third option nobody names -- that cognition might restructure the temporal sampling of sensory input (what gets attended to when) without changing the static perceptual representation or the post-perceptual judgment. So is the debate actually three-way disguised as two-way? Is it verbal over "perceptual system," or are they asking different questions about phenomology vs information flow? And given that diagnosis, what'd count as progress -- a new taxonomy, an empirical marker that distinguishes sampling effects from content effects, or is the question just ill-posed?
