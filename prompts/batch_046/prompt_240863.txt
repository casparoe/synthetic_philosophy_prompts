Two people in my metaphysics reading group got stuck arguing about the special composition question and I cant tell whos actually winning. Friend A started by saying universalism is obviously false — if every scattered collection counts as an object, then theres an object made of my coffee cup and the Andromeda galaxy, which is just rediculous. Friend B said nihilism is just as bad, because then tables and chairs dont exist, only simples arranged table-wise or chair-wise, and thats not a metaphysically serious option since science constantly refers to macro-objects. Friend A came back with a restricted view: parts compose a whole only when they enter into a relation of functional interdependence, like organs in a body or gears in a clock — the whole does something the parts cant do alone. Friend B said "functional" is too anthropocentric; a glacier is one object even though its not functioning for anything, and a random pile of sand can have emergent properties without being a real whole. Then Friend A proposed this tweak: instead of function, use structural dependency, meaning you cant remove or replace a part without destroying the global geometry of the system in a way that cant be decomposed into local changes. Friend B hit back with a swarm of autonomous drones that maintain a perfect sphere formation — each drone can be swapped out constantly, so no part is essential, but the sphere-shape is globally stable and depends on every drone being in the right relation to every other. Friend A said thats not an object, thats just a dynamic process, like a wave in water. Friend B said if a wave can have properties like speed and momentum, and the drone-sphere can have properties like center of mass and rotational inertia, then the distinction between "object" and "process" is doing all the work without earning it. They kept going in circles.

Can you adjudicate this move by move? I want to know whos right at each step — the universalism objection, the nihilism reply, the functional-interdependence criterion, the anthropocentric objection, the structural-dependency fix, and the drone-swarm counterexample. Also tell me what both of them are missing — like, is there a hidden assumption about identity over time, or about whether composition needs to be transitive? Just go through it point by point and say where each lands.
