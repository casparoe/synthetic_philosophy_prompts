I will defend in next week's philosophy-of-physics seminar a relationalist interpretation of Newton's bucket: I accept that the curvature of the water demonstrates absolute rotational motion, but I claim this inertia arises from a direct dynamical coupling between local bodies and the global topology of matter distribution—without invoking an independent substantival spacetime. The audience is mostly substantivalists who regard the bucket as a definitive refutation of relationism, plus historians of science attentive to whether Newton aimed his argument at Cartesian vortices or at a more robust relationalist position. Give me a prep sheet for the Q&A: present the objections I will actually receive as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, ordered from the most damaging downward, phrased in the vocabulary those listeners would use, with each item stating what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest point in my response—no introduction or conclusion.
