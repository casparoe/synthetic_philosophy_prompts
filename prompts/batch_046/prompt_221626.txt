Yet I recently commissioned an independent audit of my own reporting history, and it documented a recurring pattern—I tend to identify precisely this kind of methodological flaw in studies whose policy conclusions conflict with my prior views. Two methodologists I regard as epistemic peers have now reviewed my replication and said it bears the signature of that documented bias. Nevertheless, the regression output still looks erroneous to me, and I need to decide whether to file the critique. If my direct technical assessment points one way, but the strongest available evidence about my reliability as an assessor points the other way, what does rational belief actually require? Should I defer to the meta-evidence about my own judgment, or is there a principled reason to privilege my first-hand reading of the data?
