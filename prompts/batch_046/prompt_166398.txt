Consider the following argument, which seems to rehabilitate bootstrapping rather than expose it as vicious, and I cannot see where the circularity actually hides.

(1) A subject S can come to know that a particular perceptual belief B is true — say, that there is a red cube before her — without first possessing independent, non-perceptual proof that her visual faculty F is generally reliable, provided no defeaters are present.

(2) Once S knows B, and B was formed by F in environment E, S has at least one confirmed data point: F delivered truth in E.

(3) By repeating this process across similar environments E₁...Eₙ, S can accumulate further confirmed data points, all observed via F but recorded in memory and reasoned about through general inductive inference rather than through F itself.

(4) From these data points S can inductively form the belief that F is reliable in environment-class C; this inference is not viciously circular, because its premises are particular known truths and its mechanism is induction, not the very faculty being evaluated.

(5) Once S knows F is reliable in C, she can deploy F within C to test for skeptical hypotheses — for instance, checking cross-modal coherence to rule out being a brain in a vat fed consistent false inputs.

(6) Thus S can acquire anti-skeptical knowledge using only F plus ordinary induction, never having validated F by some independent faculty.

(7) But this is precisely the "easy knowledge" structure that the bootstrapping objection treats as a reductio: if one can validate a faculty by using it and then defeat skepticism with it, skeptical challenges are answered too cheaply.

(8) So either the circularity in (4) has been misdescribed — because every premise about F's success still traces back to F — or (1) is false and perceptual knowledge requires prior independent warrant, which pushes us toward radical skepticism.

(9) I would strongly prefer to keep (1) and reject the framing in (7), but I want to know the exact cost: does accepting (1) through (6) commit me to allowing every self-validating faculty to bootstrap itself into legitimacy, or is there a principled restriction that blocks the vicious cases while preserving this one? If you think the argument is valid and one premise must go, say which one is least costly to abandon and what that abandonment buys us.
