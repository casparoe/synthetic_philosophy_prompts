You advise municipalities and tribal councils on restorative-justice protocol design. A philosophical literature distinguishes guilt—action-focused and repair-oriented—from shame—identity-focused and socially isolating—while disputing whether institutional design can deliberately shift a culture’s dominant moral emotion or only accommodate existing biases. Your task is to translate that theoretical debate into a reusable decision procedure your office can run whenever a community requests a new accountability mechanism.

Draft the procedure as an ordered checklist with explicit stop conditions. For each step specify: the empirical or normative question being answered; the minimal evidence required to proceed; and the precise point at which the procedure must halt and refer the decision to community representatives because it is a values judgment rather than a fact. The procedure must incorporate the following distinctions without requiring users to master them: guilt’s specificity to acts versus shame’s implication of global self-worth; the risk of toxic shame versus constructive guilt; the way a community’s linguistic and ritual resources bias emotional availability; and the contested claim that emotional regimes can be engineered rather than merely inherited. Be explicit about which steps assess conditions and which prescribe outcomes.

Apply the procedure to two contrasting requests: (a) a technology firm designing an internal whistleblower-accountability protocol that aims to preserve employee professional identity and avoid public humiliation; and (b) a tribal council reforming a traditional conflict-resolution circle that historically relied on collective shame but now seeks to reduce stigma-linked distress among younger members. For each case, execute the steps in order, identify where the procedure produces a recommendation the requester will likely resist, and flag exactly where the question of whether a culture can “choose” its moral emotion becomes an irreducible practical deadlock that the procedure cannot settle. Conclude by stating the hard limits of the framework—what it can standardize and what it must always leave to judgment.
