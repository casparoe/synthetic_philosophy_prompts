I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind preparing a colloquium Q&A with a visiting scholar who defends a distributed extension of the narrative-gravity account of selfhood: she holds that narrative centers need not be confined to individual brains but can be shared across social groups or embedded in technological artifacts, sustained by external scaffolding rather than internal psychology alone. I need eight to ten interview questions that do not simply ask her to restate this view. At minimum, include one question pressing how first-person authority survives if the center is genuinely distributed; one on whether her functional criteria for narrative gravity risk attributing literal selves to corporations or to large-scale language models; and one on how she distinguishes her position from both eliminativism about the self and from standard social-identity theories. For each of these three, supply a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—especially if she appeals to functional continuity or to the role of external narrative structures. The remaining questions should probe under-explored implications: for example, what becomes of moral responsibility when part of one's narrative gravity is constituted by artifacts that no one fully controls, and whether this view forces a revision to ordinary practices of autobiographical memory and self-attribution.
