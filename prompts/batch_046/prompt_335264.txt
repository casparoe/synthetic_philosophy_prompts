I've come around to the hinge-epistomology reading of Wittgenstein's On Certainty — the idea that certainties like the earth having existed long before I was born aren't beliefs we hold but more like the axis a langauge-game turns on. They're not known or justified; they're conditions that make doubt and justification possible in the first place. I think this dissolves radical skepticism, but I want an honest stress-test.

What would actually count against it? Not just generic "you're wrong," but specific defeaters. Do cases of scientific revolution show hinges being revised like ordinary beliefs? Do psychology results show people treat them as defeasible? There's also this distributed-hinge worry I keep coming back to: if different communities hold different framework conditions, does that undermine the claim they're non-propositional conditions of sense?
