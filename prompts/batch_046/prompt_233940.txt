I sit on the National Health Priority-Setting Panel. By 14 March we must recommend whether to fund a pilot programme providing continuous neuroadaptive hedonic calibration to twelve patients with congenital anhedonia. The intervention reportedly produces sustained subjective well-being scores several orders of magnitude above population norms, yet each recipient requires a dedicated multidisciplinary team that could otherwise deliver standard psychiatric care to approximately two hundred patients with moderate depressive disorders. The applicants’ ethical appendix defends the proposal on aggregate-welfare grounds: the total utility generated per specialist-hour far exceeds that of conventional distribution. A panel minority objects that this reasoning permits extreme subjective states to act as distributive black holes, effectively reproducing the utility-monster dilemma in clinical resource allocation, and warns that approval would establish a precedent for funding according to peak intensity rather than breadth of benefit. Draft the panel’s formal written opinion for circulation before Thursday’s meeting. It must recommend a concrete course of action — approve, reject, or approve with conditions and a specified review timeframe — with reasoning that the programme directors and patient representatives could engage with directly. The opinion should acknowledge and fairly represent the strongest consideration on the losing side, without dismissing it. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the argument should stand on its own.
