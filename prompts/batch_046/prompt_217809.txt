I hold that the hole argument, reconstructed through modern gauge-theoretic and fiber-bundle formulations of general relativity rather than the traditional bare-manifold presentation, undermines not only manifold substantivalism but also a more resilient relationalist fallback: the claim that spacetime points recover derivative identity through stable coincidence with actual material fields. On the bundle view, both the metric and matter fields are sections of appropriate bundles, and the full symmetry group includes automorphisms that can reassign field values across point-pairings — or leave pairings invariant in physically indistinguishable ways — so that there is no fact of the matter, not even relational, about “this particular point bearing this particular field configuration” outside the equivalence class. The argument therefore forces a stricter structuralism: not just that points lack primitive thisness, but that the point-field pairing structure itself is the only ontology, with no recoverable individual terms. Take a definite position on whether this stronger extension holds. I want the decisive defeaters separated cleanly from the merely awkward ones: formal results in differential geometry or gauge theory that would block the bundle-extension of the argument; physical scenarios in which point-field coincidence seems to carry explanatory weight that survives equivalence-classing; metaphysical objections that this pairing-structuralism secretly smuggles point-identity back in through the relational structure; and whether defenders of the standard, weaker anti-substantivalist reading should concede this stronger claim as a legitimate consequence or reject it as an overreach that risks insulating itself from counterevidence. Rank them by how fatal each would be, say which ones a committed defender of the stronger view must face honestly rather than dismiss, and deliver your best argued verdict — not a survey of options, but a judgment about whether the extension is sound, where exactly it is most exposed, and whether that exposure is a problem for a view of this kind.
