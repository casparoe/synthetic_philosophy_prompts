Below are notes for an essay I am drafting on what I call the careenium problem. A careenium is a hypothetical substrate—imagine a continuously deformable medium—populated by discrete nodes I term simmballs, each encoding a symbolic state and altering its state according to local rules that reference other simmballs. The central question is whether the symbolic relations among simmballs can exert downward causal pressure on the careenium substrate, or whether every apparent case of symbol-driven change is fully reducible to substrate-level dynamics that merely implement those symbols.

The notes lay out three moves. First, the substrate-first claim: since simmballs are themselves configurations of the careenium, any change in a simmball is just a local deformation of the substrate; talk of symbols pushing is at best a useful fiction. Second, the organizational-reply claim: once simmballs form dense, recursive networks, the system acquires error-correcting dynamics best described at the symbolic level—when a symbolic contradiction arises, substrate energy is redirected to restore coherence, suggesting that symbolic structure constrains substrate flow rather than merely riding it. Third, a middle path: downward causation might be real but non-mysterious if the careenium has multi-level dynamics, where symbolic-level descriptions capture stable attractor structures to which substrate-level dynamics are sensitive.

I am not asking for bibliographies or references to the literature on downward causation or computation. Rather, I want comments on whether the distinction between substrate-level and symbolic-level causation is drawn sharply enough, or whether it collapses once the dynamics are fully specified. Does the error-correction example genuinely support downward causation, or can it be redescribed entirely in terms of local physical thresholds? Is the analogy to computation illuminating here, or does importing computer-science language obscure the fact that silicon computation is engineered, whereas the careenium is meant to be a natural substrate? Please focus especially on whether the three moves are genuinely distinct, and whether there is a hidden dualism in treating simmballs as both substrate-configurations and autonomous symbolic agents.
