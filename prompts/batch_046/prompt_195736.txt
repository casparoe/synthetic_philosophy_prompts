I'm in a seminar on Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling and I'm preparing a short presentation on the teleological suspension of the ethical. I want to close with a villanelle that enacts the idea structurally rather than merely illustrating it. Use two refrains: one expressing the universal ethical obligation of father to child, the other expressing Abraham's particular faith that provision comes from outside the universal law. Arrange the nineteen lines so the refrains cycle through the stages of ascent—preparation, binding, the raised knife, the angel's interruption—so that their relation shifts across stanzas: first as direct opposition, then as uneasy coincidence, finally as a paradox that sounds together without synthesis. In the closing couplet both refrains must appear side by side, not resolved, so the form itself demonstrates that the ethical is teleologically displaced rather than abolished. Keep it rigorous enough that a reader familiar with the pseudonymous authorship and with the distinction between the tragic hero and the knight of faith can identify which refrain belongs to which sphere, and can see why their co-presence in the final lines is the philosophical point rather than a contradiction.
