I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends a revisionist Strawsonian account of moral responsibility: she holds that reactive attitudes are not merely natural emotional responses, as Strawson describes them, but are partly constituted by normative expectations that remain open to rational revision—call this the “revisionist participant stance.” I want six to eight interview questions that do not ask her to restate Strawson’s classic essay but instead press the internal tensions of her hybrid view. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. At least one question should challenge whether allowing rational revision of resentment undermines Strawson’s claim that the participant stance is practically non-optional; at least one should ask how her view treats cases in which a reactive attitude remains psychologically appropriate even after the relevant normative expectation has been explicitly repudiated by the community; and at least one should probe whether her revisionism ultimately collapses into a form of moral realism about the conditions for responsibility. Keep the questions sharp, specific to her position, and free of generic biography or bibliography requests.
