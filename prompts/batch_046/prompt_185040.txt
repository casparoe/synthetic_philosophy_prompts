I've been working through some ideas for my diss chapter on existantial value theory, specifically trying to stitch together Camus-style absurdism with more recent fittingness accounts like Wolf's but with a twist I'm calling reflexive absurd commitment. The basic claim is that meaning doesn't come from achieving some objectively valuable end, nor from mere subjective fulfillment, but from actively sustaining engagement with a project while maintaining a kind of second-order awarenes that the project is ultimately groundles — what I'm calling refractive futility. The idea is that the tension itself generates a distinctive kind of value, not despite the futility but through it, kind of like how irony works but in an existantial register. I'm trying to defend this against both the standard objectivist charge that it's just resigned nihilism in disguise and the subjectivist charge that it collapses into mere preference satisfaction once you drop the objective-value requirement.

But I'm worried I'm just insulating the view. So I want to know: what would actually count against reflexive absurd commitment? I don't want the usual laundry list of objections to Camus or Wolf — I want the defeaters that are specific to this hybrid. For instance, does the view predict that people in flow states or with diminished self-reflective capacity have less meaningful lives? Would empirical results from moral psychology about how people actually form commitments undercut the claim that second-order awareness is necessary rather than destructive? And if the view can always retreat to saying 'well, the awareness just needs to be latent,' has it become unfalsifiable — and is that a problem for a normative account of meaning? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each say whether a defender of this view should concede it as damaging or can reasonably dismiss it. Also, are there formal or conceptual results — maybe about self-reference, or about the coherence of valuing something you regard as groundless — that would collapse the position into either pure objectivism or pure subjectivism?

I'm writing this up for a workshop next week so I'd like the answer to be pretty granular, not just broad strokes — especially about which intuitions or possible cases (say, someone who genuinely forgets their futility for long stretches, or someone who never achieves the reflexive distance at all) the view is genuinely exposed to, and which ones it can absorb without damage.
