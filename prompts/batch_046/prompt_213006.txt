I've been running discussion sections for a philosophy course on ethics and I keep running into the same occurence with my students. We'll work through variations on a moral dilemma—say, a driverless car that might harm one person to save five, but now there's a 60% chance the harm happens either way, or the agent is a committee rather than an individual—and some students can immediately tell me whether thier intuition shifts and why the case feels different, but if I ask them to state the principle behind the shift they just shrug and say "it feels wronger somehow". Meanwhile other students can map every version onto a precise rule—utilitarian calculus, deontological prohibition, virtue-based concern—and they never miss a formal distinction, but when the scenario gets tweaked in a way that doesn't match the rule's conditions they apply it mechanically and miss the intuitive adjustment that the first group caught straight away.

This isn't just about being smart or not, because the same split shows up across domains. The student who navigates the moral cases intuitively is often the one who can read social behaviour perfectly without being able to list the norms she's following, while the rule-mapping student can recite every etiquette code but is constantly putting her foot in it in actual conversation. So I'm wondering whether there's an established concept for this specific kind of divergence—where practical fluency in navigating cases runs separately from, and sometimes inversely to, the ability to articulate the governing principles. I know there's a broad distinction between knowing how and knowing that, but this feels more specific: it's not just that one person has a skill and another has information, it's that within the same domain—moral reasoning, social interaction—the two capacities seem to come apart in opposite directions for different people. Does this configuration have a proper name, and how exactly does it relate to that broader distinction? If it doesn't have its own term, could you define it with clear boundary conditions—say, whether it applies only when the practical skill is unconscious, or also when it's fully deliberate but just inarticulate?
