I've been rewatching Inception and thinking about Escher's Drawing Hands and that concept of an inviolate level — the base reality that doesn't get drawn by anything above it. There's this idea about recursive grounding failure, where every layer in a hierarchy owes its coherence to the layer above, yet there's supposed to be a bottom that owes nothing. In the movie Cobb's totem is meant to detect whether he's in that bottom level, but the ending refuses to confirm it.

Read the film through the lens of tangled hierachies and the inviolate level. Where does the narrative set up an apparent base — waking on the plane, maybe? — and where does it undermine that? I'm especially interested in limbo: it's described as unconstructed dream space, yet Cobb and Mal built a city there. Does that mean limbo is the inviolate level that got colonized by a higher loop, or does it show there never was one? And does Cobb's final indiference to whether the top falls imply that if there's no inviolate level, the ethics of staying in a projection versus returning to reality just collapses?

Don't reduce this to Plato's cave; I want the actual strange-loop mechanics. What's the film getting right about hierarchical reality that standard philosophy papers miss? And be honest about where the movie resists the reading — maybe the emotional arc needs Cobb to treat one level as special even if the ontology denies it.
