I am drafting a proposal for a four-month philosophy fellowship (deliverable: one 5,000-word research paper) focused on Molinism and middle knowledge. The core idea I want developed is somewhat unorthodox: rather than analyzing counterfactuals of creaturely freedom as purely individual properties, I want to investigate whether middle knowledge can accommodate irreducibly joint counterfactuals—counterfactuals about what two or more agents would freely do together under conditions that do not reduce to the conjunction of individual counterfactuals. The motivating suspicion is that if such joint counterfactuals are primitive, then the standard charge that Molinism renders God's selection of a feasible world arbitrary might be weakened, because the range of worlds available for actualization would be constrained not only by individual free choices but by collective free structures that God does not unilaterally determine. Write the proposal with the following elements: a sharply formulated research question that distinguishes the joint-counterfactual thesis from both standard individualist Molinism and from deterministic group-choice models; an explanation of why existing treatments of middle knowledge—treating freedom as an atomistic capacity—leave this question genuinely open rather than settled by extension; a clear methodological statement (conceptual analysis, formal modeling, or analogical reasoning from social-choice theory) with a justification for that choice over theological exegesis or empirical psychology; a description of what success and failure would look like, including which considerations (e.g., the coherence of collective agency without a group mind, the risk of hidden determinism in joint conditions) would adjudicate the outcome; an honest scope assessment identifying which part of the project is manageable in four months and where the boundaries must be drawn to avoid overreach; and a concise argument for why this is a philosophical rather than theological or sociological project worthy of funding. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Finally, include a brief meta-commentary stating how confident you are in the viability of this framing and explicitly flagging the point in the proposal where your confidence is lowest.
