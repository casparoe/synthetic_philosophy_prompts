Construct a Gettier-style thought experiment that satisfies the following joint specifications. First, subject S must form a belief that P is true, where P is a specific empirical claim about an observable feature of a physical object (for example, the exact hue of a flower or the weight of a container), and S’s belief must be justified to a high degree by internalist standards—grounded in direct observation and valid inference—while the reasoning chain contains no false premises and no false intermediate beliefs. Second, the truth of P must depend on an environmental factor unknown to S and absent from her justification: specifically, the scenario should exhibit what we can call a correlated misalignment, in which a measuring or identifying instrument is systematically miscalibrated with respect to one parameter, but an independent, contingent regularity in the local environment systematically counteracts that miscalibration, so that correct outputs occur only within that localized context. Third, engineer the case so that an externalist safety condition of the kind associated with Sosa is satisfied—there should be no nearby possible world in which S forms the belief via the same method and P is false—while a sensitivity condition of the kind associated with Nozick is violated—there should be a nearby possible world in which P is false and S still believes P via the same method. Fourth, present the scenario in a single paragraph, using only realistic conditions with no exotic physics, no demons, no perfect predictors, and stakes that an ordinary person could face.

After presenting the scenario, provide a step-by-step demonstration: show precisely why the safety account classifies the belief as knowledge, show precisely why the sensitivity account withholds it, and identify the exact structural feature—the localized environmental compensation for the instrument’s miscalibration—that drives the divergence. Then answer three further questions. (a) If that compensatory environmental feature is removed, does the case collapse into a standard Gettier problem that relies on a false premise, or does it collapse into unproblematic knowledge? (b) How would a defender of the sensitivity account attack the case, particularly regarding the modal proximity of worlds in which the compensation fails? (c) Given that the reasoning chain contains no false premises, does this weaken or strengthen the intuitive judgment that S lacks knowledge, and what does that imply for the role of reasoning luck versus environmental luck in generating Gettier intuitions?
