I've come round to thinking generational labels — Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z — aren't real social kinds, just temporal slicing artifacts. For a cohort to be a kind, I think it needs diachronic causal homeostasis: shared mechanisms that keep group properties stable across contexts, not just arbitrary birth-year cuts plus marketing. Generational cohorts fail this; membership is fixed by birth, but experience is mediated by class, geography, tech access, so there's no homeostatic core. Even institutional kindhood seems out of reach. What would count against this? I want real defeaters, not the usual "but they share TikTok" reply. Maybe empirical results from longtudinal studies where cohort effects consistently dominate period and age effects? Or an ontological argument that fixed membership plus collective recognition is sufficient? Rank them fatal to awkward, say which I'd have to concede if I'm honest, and check whether demanding homeostasis just qualifies my view into unfalsifiability — and whether that's a problem. Also, if race and gender turn out to be real kinds despite lacking homeostasis, does that sink my argument by analogy? Write it casual, like we're arguing over coffee, not grading papers.
