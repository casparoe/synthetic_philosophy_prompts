Consider a refined version of Pascal's wager that attempts to answer the many-gods objection by constraining the decision matrix to only those theistic hypotheses whose salvation conditions are pairwise mutually exclusive, and that explicitly includes a finite cognitive-cost term for maintaining belief in the absence of evidence. I want your credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that this refined wager still provides a rational justification for an agent with negligible prior evidence to deliberately adopt belief in one specific classical-theistic framework rather than remain agnostic.

Please decompose your reasoning into four sub-questions, each with a rough probability and one line of justification:

(a) That expected-utility reasoning is even the right normative framework for doxastic choice, not merely for action.

(b) That the mutual-exclusivity restriction genuinely collapses the many-gods problem to a unique optimal choice, rather than leaving a residual symmetry among equally exclusive alternatives.

(c) That the included cognitive-cost term is small enough in magnitude—relative to the postulated payoff differential—that it does not reverse the sign of the expected value.

(d) That an agent can successfully adopt a belief by direct decision despite standard epistemic-involuntarist objections, at least in the limited form required for the wager to be action-guiding.

State your independence or dependence assumptions, combine the terms to reach an overall credence, and then say which single term your final number is most sensitive to. What kind of conceptual argument—not an empirical study—would move that term by at least 0.15?

Conclude with your final rounded credence in the form \answer{X.X}.
