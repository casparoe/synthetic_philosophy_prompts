I'm advising a small philosophy research group with three years of funding and no neuroscience lab. They are focusing on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—the long-standing challenge that pleasures seem to lack any shared phenomenal quality that could ground a unified theory of well-being. They want to know which open conceptual questions in this area are actually worth the time, especially given growing interest in formalizing reward and well-being metrics for artificial decision systems. Which two or three lines of inquiry should they prioritize? Defend your ordering: for each, explain why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers working with conceptual and phenomenological tools alone, and what kind of result over the next few years would cause you to revise the ranking. In particular, address whether hedonism can be rescued by a pluralist taxonomy of pleasures rather than a single unified account, and whether that distinction matters for attempts to operationalize pleasure in non-biological agents.
