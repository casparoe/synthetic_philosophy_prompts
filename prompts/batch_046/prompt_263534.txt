I'm taking an advanced aesthetics seminar, and after a long discussion with classmates about whether we should still teach Wagner or screen Polanski, I realized that nobody in the room was working with the same specification of what a theory relating moral defect to aesthetic value is even supposed to deliver. Before I evaluate autonomism, immoralism, or contextualism, I want the criteria list first.

Write the desiderata: what must any adequate theory of how an artwork's moral flaws relate to its aesthetic merit actually deliver? For each requirement, state the case behind it. It must cover standard individual authorship (a painter whose personal cruelty is well documented), collective production (a feature film in which only one of many collaborators acted badly), works where the moral danger is in the reception rather than the production (propaganda that becomes harmful only under specific interpretive conditions), and works in which the artist's vice is thematically inseparable from the achievement (a novel about psychological cruelty whose precision seems drawn from the author's own disposition). For each, say whether it is genuinely load-bearing or merely a disciplinary prejudice of moral philosophers or formalists. Then flag any pair of desiderata that looks jointly unsatisfiable: in particular, is it consistent to hold both that moral flaws never diminish aesthetic value and that aesthetic value can depend on moral insight, given that the latter seems to require that moral failure can sometimes diminish value? I do not want a bibliography, a reading list, or a scored comparison of existing views; I want the specification sheet, with an honest judgment about which single requirement eliminates the most popular theories. Finally, apply the framework you have articulated to one case I have not listed: an image generated by a system trained partly on non-consensual imagery, where the final visual form bears no trace of that origin and the immediate creator is an anonymous user. Does your list of criteria deliver a verdict, or does the case expose a gap?
