I've been covering the resurgence of Stoic practice in tech and productivity culture, and a reductio keeps bothering me. Consider this chain. (1) The classical dichotomy of control places only judgments, impulses, and volitions within our power; health, reputation, wealth, and—crucially—the engineered architecture of attention lie outside it. (2) Behavioral research on digital interfaces treats sustained attention as largely shaped by externally designed variable-reward schedules that operate beneath the level of rational assent. (3) If attention is therefore not within our power, Stoic reason demands that we regard its fragmentation or capture with complete indifference. (4) That licenses the conclusion that a practitioner should be equally tranquil compulsively scrolling or engaging in deep, deliberate work, since neither outcome is ultimately up to them. (5) Yet that conclusion collapses the distinction between self-cultivation and passive consumption that modern Stoicism is meant to defend. (6) So either the dichotomy fails in the digital age, or its modern defenders must reject the cognitive science. Where is the weakest link—does (2) overstate the externality of attention, does (3) conflate indifference with non-attachment, or is the error elsewhere? I'd prefer to preserve (1), but I want to know exactly what it costs.
