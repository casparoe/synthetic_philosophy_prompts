In a seminar on symbolic-substrate interaction we have been working with the careenium—the putative physical-cognitive substrate governed by non-symbolic dynamics—and the simmballs, discrete symbolic structures that some accounts allow to feed back into that substrate. A fellow student keeps repeating the line, "simmballs never push the careenium; they only ride it," most recently to dismiss my claim that a high-level symbolic inference could alter low-level careenial dynamics. I suspect the slogan conceals several distinct theses. Could you lay them out as a menu, from the trivial definitional reading to the radical epiphenomenalist one, indicating for each whether it is defensible, whether it actually supports ruling out causal feedback from symbols to substrate, and which version people who invoke it in debate usually mean? I want to bring the list back to the seminar to see which reading, if any, blocks my argument.
