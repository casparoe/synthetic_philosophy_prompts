I am preparing a diagnostic handout for an advanced metaphysics seminar on the modal question: could you have come from a different sperm and egg? I want a branching taxonomy—not a list of named philosophers—whose nodes are concrete commitments and whose leaves map the logical space of answers.

Root commitment: There exists at least one modal constraint on one's biological origin.

From the root, branch through three binary distinctions, each phrased as a claim a student can evaluate without knowing philosophical labels:

A. The Gamete-Token Constraint: Must the origin involve these exact sperm and egg tokens, or is identity of nuclear genetic profile sufficient (permitting a different gamete pair that reproduces the same genome)?

B. The Gestational-Environment Constraint: Even with a fixed genome, must development occur in the same uterine and early epigenetic environment, or could an identical genetic origin yield the same person in a different gestational context?

C. The Parental-Causal Constraint: If neither token gametes nor token environment are essential, must the causal chain still pass through these same parents in this reproductive event, or is even parental identity dispensable?

This yields eight leaves (the 2×2×2 combinations). For each leaf:
- State whether the combination is occupied in the literature or by intuitive defenders. If it has a standard name, provide it; if it is unnamed or genuinely unexplored, mark it 'unnamed' or 'unoccupied.'
- Give its most awkward commitment in exactly one sentence.
- If the combination is incoherent or biologically impossible, diagnose why briefly rather than leaving it blank.

Then, flag any pairs of leaves that these three distinctions fail to separate—cases where two answer-patterns collapse into the same metaphysical view despite different branches.

The audience is graduate students familiar with modal logic and basic reproductive biology, but the tree should be navigable by someone who has never read the origin-essentialism literature. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific page numbers. Do not invoke recent technical formalisms that depend on niche literature; construct the taxonomy from first-principles reasoning about identity, origin, and modal constraint.
