ok so im trying to figure out my actual view on whether the Holocaust is comparable to other things, and whether Bauman's modernity argument holds water. here are my gut verdicts.

comparing it to the Herero genocide (around 1904) feels totally valid—rail, camps, racial census, modern burocracy turned to killing. comparing it to the Armenian genocide feels wrong though, even though that was also state-led mass killing of an ethnic group; my gut says it was more traditional pogrom and expulsion, missing that specific modern-industrial-burocratic fusion.

i basically buy Bauman's idea that modern rationality and division of labor made the Holocaust possible, but then Rwanda messes with me. on one hand it was low-tech, intimate, machetes and neighbors—so modern machinery cant be necessary. on the other hand they used identity cards and radio lists, so maybe modern burocratic distancing was there too. i keep flipping on Rwanda, thats my stuck case.

on uniqueness: i think its garbage to say its unique just because the death toll was huge. but i do think theres a defensible uniqueness claim if we mean "the only case where a modern state mobilized industrial infrastructure specifically to exterminate every member of a group as an administrative end in itself." i also think comparing the Holocaust to Hiroshima or Dresden is okay if were talking about modern moral distancing—killing people you dont see via technology—but not okay as moral equivalence, since one was intentional genocide and the other strategic bombing.

then theres the Holodomor, which i cant settle. it had modern census and grain requisition, burocratic state planning, but the explicit goal wasnt direct shooting or gassing with extermination as the end. so is it inside the Bauman frame or outside?

is there one principle that captures all this? maybe something like "valid comparisons require both modern burocratic mediation and an explicit intent of total annihilation as an administrative goal"? thatd save Herero yes, Armenian no, Rwanda maybe, Hiroshima partial, Holodomor partial? but i feel like im just forcing it. or should i drop one verdict—probably my Armenian one? please tell me if theres a cleaner account, which verdicts it has to sacrifice, and what it says about a case i havent mentioned: the Belgian Congo under Leopold. that had modern extraction burocracy and massive death, but the intent was labor extraction not total annihilation. where does it land?
