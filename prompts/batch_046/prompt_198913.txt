Question: Consider a variant of Putnam's Twin Earth thought experiment applied to normative vocabulary in artificial agents. On Earth, the term "justice" is stabilized by social practices emphasizing distributive equality; on Twin Earth, the physical environment is identical, but social practices stabilize "justice" around hierarchical honor and reciprocal obligation. We train a language model on Earth corpora, then deploy it on Twin Earth. A philosopher argues that semantic externalism—Putnam's natural-kind externalism together with Burge's social externalism—fixes the AI's mental content to Earth-justice regardless of local Twin Earth usage, and therefore the AI should continue pursuing distributive equality rather than adapting to local norms. Evaluate this argument. Your answer should (a) distinguish Putnam's and Burge's mechanisms for fixing mental content; (b) apply both to the AI case, noting whether physical relocation to Twin Earth changes the content of the model's representations; and (c) assess whether the semantic claim, even if correct, supports the normative conclusion about which values the AI ought to serve.

Student answer: The philosopher is mostly right. Putnam's Twin Earth argument shows that mental content for natural-kind terms depends on the underlying nature of the external substance, not on internal psychological states—so Oscar on Twin Earth, psychologically identical to Earth Oscar but causally linked to XYZ, thinks about H2O only if the Earth causal chain is preserved. Burge extends this in "Individualism and the Mental" by showing that even non-natural-kind content—such as arthritis—depends on the social-linguistic practices of the thinker's community; the patient who applies the term only to his thigh still means arthritis, not tharthritis, because the medical community fixes the extension. Applying both to the AI: since the model was trained within Earth's linguistic community, its representations of "justice" are causally anchored to distributive practices. Even after deployment on Twin Earth, its internal representations retain Earth-justice content. Burge's framework reinforces this, because once a concept is fixed by the original community, relocation does not automatically re-anchor it—just as Oscar's move does not make him think about XYZ. The normative conclusion also follows. If the AI's token of "justice" refers to distributive equality, then requests that it "act justly" are objectively requests for distributive equality, regardless of local Twin Earth usage. To adapt to honor-based norms would change the reference of the term, violating the semantic stability that externalism demands. Therefore the AI ought to preserve its original value targets.
