Having spent my career in experimental physics, I have come to associate intellectual progress with predictive refinement, reproducible convergence, and the gradual replacement of competing quantitative frameworks by a shared model that resolves prior anomalies. Since retiring, I have begun studying philosophy in earnest, and I find myself genuinely perplexed by a discrepancy I cannot dismiss: debates over consciousness, moral obligation, free will, and personal identity persist across centuries without producing anything resembling the consensus or cumulative precision I took for granted in science. Some philosophers insist that philosophy does progress, but only in a distinctive register—perhaps by dissolving pseudo-problems, sharpening conceptual distinctions, or restructuring the very space in which questions are asked—while others treat the endurance of fundamental disagreement as evidence of stagnation rather than depth.

Write an essay on whether philosophy makes progress. Do not reduce the issue to whether philosophy produces empirical predictions; instead, weigh three specific conceptions of philosophical achievement: (a) the accumulation of correct answers to perennial questions, (b) the refinement of conceptual distinctions that restructure how problems are formulated without necessarily resolving them, and (c) the dissolution of questions once they are shown to rest on linguistic confusion or false presuppositions. Reach and justify a definite verdict: is there a form of philosophical progress that ought to satisfy someone trained in the natural sciences, or is the comparison with science fundamentally misguided and therefore uninformative? In defending your position, apply your framework to at least one sustained philosophical debate—such as personal identity, the nature of causation, or the problem of free will—showing whether the apparent persistence of disagreement masks a genuine transformation in the problem space. Conclude by stating what, if anything, philosophy can legitimately claim to have achieved, and whether that achievement should count as progress.
