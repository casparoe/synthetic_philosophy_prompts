I'm a high school senior writing up my epistemological commitments for our school's philosophy symposium, and I want a consistency audit before I present them. Here is my current package:

(1) Foundationalism about perceptual knowledge: direct sensory experience justifies basic beliefs about color and shape without inference, and these beliefs need no further support to be rational.

(2) Coherentism about ethics: moral judgments are justified entirely by mutual coherence within a system of normative and descriptive beliefs, with no basic moral facts that ground them.

(3) Infinitism about modal knowledge—claims about possibility and necessity: every modal judgment requires a further modal reason, the chain never terminates or repeats, yet we avoid vicious circularity because justification only requires the availability of further reasons, not actual possession of an infinite chain.

(4) I hold that there is exactly one correct answer to the regress problem, and it applies uniformly across all domains; justification is a single normative relation that does not change its nature with the subject matter.

(5) I accept that if belief B requires justification J, and J requires further justification, then B is unjustified unless the chain stops, loops harmlessly, or is infinitely available; this means any viable theory must block the vicious regress in one of these three ways.

(6) I think reflective equilibrium is the proper method for testing ethical belief systems, but I also think it is the wrong method for testing perceptual basic beliefs, which should be evaluated by reliability rather than coherence with other beliefs.

(7) I think an artificial system could have justified beliefs if it implements the right structural relation—foundational for perceptual inputs, coherentist for value alignment, infinitist for counterfactual reasoning—provided the system can respond to demands for justification.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. Rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each what has to give and at what cost. In particular, check whether my domain-specific triad in (1)-(3) can survive my domain-neutral commitment in (4), and whether my distinction in (3) between availability and actual possession is doing stable work or collapses into one of the other positions. Also examine whether (6) and (7) introduce conflicts I haven't noticed—especially whether allowing AI justification requires abandoning a conscious-subject condition I implicitly rely on elsewhere, and whether evaluating basic beliefs by reliability secretly smuggles in coherentist assumptions. Be specific: for each tension, name the commitments involved, explain the incompatibility precisely, and say what the cheapest revision would be.
