I cover philosophy of science and mathematics for a general-interest publication, and I am preparing a feature on whether debates about actual infinity track a real divergence in our understanding of completed processes or remain purely notational. In a seminar I attended recently, an instructor used Hilbert’s hotel to illustrate Cantor’s pairing argument, but I kept wondering whether one could engineer a concrete, human-scale institutional protocol—say, a municipal archive’s perpetual re-cataloguing procedure or an open-ended hostel booking algorithm—that reproduces the structural logic of accommodating one more entry after every entry already has an assigned place, without invoking exotic physics, cosmological scales, or any empirical prediction that differs between the two sides. I want a scenario built to precise specifications: a finitist should be able to deny consistently that any “completion” of the rearrangement ever occurs, treating the protocol as describing only an indefinite potential extension, while an actualist about infinity should accept that the shift is genuinely finished for all entries simultaneously; both should agree on every observable outcome after any finite number of steps, so the disagreement is isolated to the ontology of the process itself. The protocol must stay describable to an intelligent non-specialist, avoid demons or perfect predictors, and remain under two paragraphs. Once you have laid it out, demonstrate that it satisfies these constraints: state the claim each position makes about the protocol’s completion status, identify the nearby alternative descriptions in which the verdict flips, and pinpoint the exact structural feature—actual simultaneous completion versus open-ended potentiality—that drives the divergence. Then explain what happens if that feature is removed: does the case collapse back into a standard potential-infinity argument, or does some residue remain? Include an honest note on how a finitist defender might attack the scenario by arguing that you have smuggled actual infinity into the description of the rules rather than deriving it from them, and please separate what is mathematically settled about Cantor-style one-to-one correspondence from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers of mathematics about whether such correspondences can be instantiated in completed concrete procedures.
