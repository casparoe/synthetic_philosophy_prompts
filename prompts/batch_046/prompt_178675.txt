I'm giving the toast at my aunt's retirement dinner next saturday. She was a hospice nurse for 35 yrs, and about half the room are old coworkers who studied Marx in nursing school and actually know that exploitaion and alientation are different things, while the other half are family who just think 'exploited' means low pay. I want the toast to do real structural work with that distinction — ask whether she was exploited in Marx's specific surplus-extraction sense even though she was never alienated from her patients, and in fact loved being with them. There's this thing she always said, that capitalism makes you feel grateful for being permitted to suffer alongside strangers, and I want that idea in there but I dont want the toast to sound like a lecture. Keep it warm, a little messy, dont tie it up with a bow because she hated neat moral endings. Maybe mention she kept some old labor pamphlet in her locker but only used it to prop up a wobbly table. Oh, and if I misspelled alientation in my notes please dont correct it — I want to stay close to how she actually talked.
