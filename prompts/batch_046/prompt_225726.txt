Write a deadpan parody of a Which?-style consumer comparison review, approximately 600 words, evaluating two rival navigation-subscription services: "TrueBelief Basic" and "KnowHow Premium". Both promise to guide subscribers to the correct destination—use the classical example of reaching Larissa—but TrueBelief operates on ungrounded correct guesses that happen to align with the route, whereas KnowHow requires verified map-data, justified route-selection, and an audit trail. The satire should target Plato's Meno problem—specifically the puzzle of why knowledge is prized above true belief when both seem to guide action with equal success—together with the standard philosophical responses: the "tethering" account (knowledge is tied to the fact), the reliability or virtue-epistemic account (knowledge involves competent belief-formation), and the "epistemic credit" view (knowing earns you praise for reaching the goal). The piece must remain internally consistent: every joke should come from treating these philosophical distinctions as literal service features, such as a "justification rollover" clause, a "tethering warranty" with small print excluding lucky guesses, and an "epistemic credit" loyalty scheme. Include a sidebar comparing customer-service complaints: TrueBelief users report sudden "belief evaporation" when the route changes, while KnowHow users complain about the "method verification" paperwork. Do not state the philosophical objections explicitly; let them emerge through the comparison metrics, the star ratings, and the reviewer's deadpan bewilderment at why anyone pays the Premium price when the Basic tier reaches Larissa just as often. The voice should be that of a meticulous British product reviewer who treats epistemology as engineering specifications. Avoid asking for citations, named philosophers beyond Plato, or reading lists; the humour should depend on understanding the conceptual structure, not on recognising specific texts. Use British spelling and punctuation throughout.
