Assume the role of senior policy advisor to the Regional Heritage Commission. By next Thursday the Commission must vote on whether to grant protected ceremonial status to the "Covenant of Rivers" festival, first staged in 1927 and now defended by local groups as a centuries-old expression of regional identity. Internal historians have documented that the event was assembled from unrelated folk practices and a selectively edited 18th-century treaty, yet it now operates as the primary ritual through which residents coordinate mutual obligations and recognize shared membership. Draft a formal decision memo recommending one concrete course—full protection paired with an interpretive disclosure condition, provisional protection pending a structured community consultation, or denial of protected status—along with a specific implementation timeline. Before writing, briefly brainstorm the philosophical tension between genealogical accuracy and performative solidarity: can an institution legitimately safeguard a synthetic practice if that practice now sustains genuine communal bonds? In the memo, present reasoning the commissioners can defend publicly, and include a substantive, honest engagement with the strongest argument on the opposing side (for example, that state endorsement of a fabricated origin risks epistemic harm to citizens, or conversely that stripping protection destroys living social cooperation). Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further sources, and avoid framing the issue as merely legal. Write in an actionable, formal register suitable for circulation.
