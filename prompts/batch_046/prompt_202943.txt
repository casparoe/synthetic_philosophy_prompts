Toynbee's challenge-and-response model feels mostly linear-progressive even though he talks about civilizations falling, since he thinks a society can break the pattern through creative response.

here are my concrete verdicts on made-up cases, and i want to know if there's one principel behind them or if some just have to go:

3. a political movement starts democratic, turns oligarchic, gets overthrown by populists who establish democracy again but this time with universal suffrage and women's rights: cyclical but with a ratchet - something accumulates.

6. a Spengler-style culture that just keeps producing more sophisticated art and science forever without ever decaying or dying: i say that's not Spenglerian at all, it's linear progress in disguise.

the case i'm really stuck on: a civilization that goes through regular 200-year cycles of centralization and fragmentation, but every centralization period is administratively more complex and technologically more advanced than the last one. is that cyclical history with linear progress layered on top, or is the cycle itself just an illusion caused by the linear tech curve?
