I'm preparing a graduate seminar on the moral status of artificial and distributed computational systems, and I want to move beyond the familiar functionalism-versus-biological-naturalism framing that students tend to memorize rather than inhabit. For this purpose, I need a diagnostic taxonomy—built from the logical space of commitments rather than from a list of named positions—that sorts views on which computations matter morally. The taxonomy should turn on three binary distinctions I take to be genuinely separable: first, whether moral patienthood requires causal integration with a living biological substrate or can be satisfied by purely informational or functional dynamics; second, whether recursive self-modeling is necessary, or whether non-reflective information integration suffices; third, whether the computation must instantiate valenced or affective representational content, or whether neutral structural dynamics alone can ground moral consideration.

Please construct a decision tree or flowchart that takes a student through concrete answers to these three questions, with each branch node phrased as a substantive claim about computation and moral attribution—never as a self-label like "are you an integrationist?"—so that the instrument classifies by commitments rather than by self-identification. For each of the eight logical combinations, indicate whether it corresponds to an existing view (broadly characterized, without requiring bibliographic citation), whether it represents a coherent but apparently unoccupied position, or whether the combination is unstable or conceptually incoherent, with a brief diagnosis in each case. Also flag any pairs of positions that these three axes alone cannot cleanly separate—perhaps because both allow substrate-independent, self-modeling, affective computation but differ on temporal persistence or spatial distribution—and specify what further distinction, such as the presence of error-correction loops or a requirement of continuous causal history, would be needed to distinguish them.
