I am preparing a taxonomy for a philosophy-of-biology graduate seminar on the units-of-selection debate. Rather than sorting positions by the labels their defenders prefer, I want a decision tree built from three successive diagnostic questions that isolate the structural commitments separating gene-level, individual-level, group-level, and developmental-systems views.

The root split should be framed as a question: does selection require discrete, heritable replicators with copy-fidelity (the replicator criterion), or is statistical covariance between a trait and reproductive success at a given organisational level sufficient regardless of inheritance mechanism (the statistical criterion)?

The second-level split should ask whether selection at a level above the individual organism is causally efficacious in its own right, or whether it is always reducible to an aggregation of lower-level dynamics.

The third-level split should ask whether horizontally transmitted, non-genetic symbiont communities can form part of the selective environment in a non-reducible way—feeding back onto the unit under selection—or whether all such niche-constructed feedback collapses back onto selection on genetic or individual substrates.

Please map the resulting logical space onto named positions where they exist (for example, strict gene-selectionism of the Dawkins variety; multi-level selection in the Sober–Wilson framework; evolutionary developmental systems theory that denies discrete units; and standard individual adaptationism). Identify any combination of answers that appears logically available but is actually unoccupied—particularly any view that combines the statistical criterion with denial of higher-level causal efficacy while still treating symbiont-mediated environments as altering the unit rather than merely its context—and offer a brief diagnosis of whether that vacancy stems from incoherence, theoretical unattractiveness, or historical neglect.

The tree should also flag any pair of named leaves that share enough commitments that your branching questions cannot genuinely separate them, explaining precisely where the collapse occurs.

To test the taxonomy, apply it to the following engineered scenario: a eusocial insect colony in which caste determination depends jointly on a host nuclear polymorphism and an obligate, vertically transmitted gut microbiome that exhibits heritable strain-level variation influencing host fertility. For each named leaf, state which entity or process the view designates as the unit of selection in this case, and justify the designation by reference to the branch answers.

Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation of specific texts. Write your answer in formal British English.
