Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Alina and Bren, on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. Alina should defend the argument in its strongest form: special relativity eliminates absolute simultaneity, and together with the principle that if an event is real for one observer it is real for all, this forces the conclusion that every spacetime event is equally real, leaving no ontological distinction between past, present, and future. Bren should resist, but not by rejecting physics. Instead, he should introduce a distinction between the absence of a globally privileged hyperplane and the absence of any form of temporal becoming. Specifically, he should propose a “worldline-indexed presentism” according to which becoming is local to each worldline and requires no universal now-slice, then press Alina on whether her inference from frame-dependence to eternalism smuggles in an unstated metaphysical premise—namely, that objects are three-dimensional entities that endure through time rather than four-dimensional entities that perdure. Have Alina respond by arguing that without a shared present, causal interaction and the coherence of temporal experience fragment across frames in a way that worldline-local becoming cannot repair, and that perdurantism does not avoid the problem but merely renames it, since the future temporal parts of an object are still assigned full reality. Have Bren reply that this is precisely where the argument overreaches: the Rietdijk–Putnam premises establish only that simultaneity is frame-relative, not that temporal parts exist tenselessly, and that a presentist can consistently reject both a global present and the ontology of perdurance without contradiction. The exchange should be genuinely dialectical—each speaker must address the other’s actual point, neither should be a strawman, and both should display equal philosophical competence. Keep the dialogue focused and approximately 450–600 words. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts beyond the argument itself.
