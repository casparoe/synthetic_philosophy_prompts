In philosophical discussions of Plato's Meno — particularly the claim that knowledge exceeds true belief because it is tied down by an account of its cause (aitias logismos), rendering it stable like a statue of Daedalus that cannot flee — a long-running stall has developed that I want diagnosed rather than adjudicated. One camp, working in virtue epistemology, holds that the tying-down is a matter of cognitive achievement: the knower grasps the explanatory structure behind the belief, earning credit in a way the merely lucky believer does not. The opposing camp, drawing on pragmatic and social-epistemological considerations, replies that this grasp is either redundant given reliable formation or merely instrumental, useful for teaching and transmission but adding no intrinsic value to the mental state itself. Both sides agree that knowledge involves justification or explanation; they diverge on whether that addition produces a genuinely distinct good.

My working hypothesis is that the stall is not primarily about epistemic value but about a hidden ambiguity in what is being valued: is it the internal quality of the agent's mental episode (intrinsic), the causal history that produced it (genetic), or the functional role it plays in communal practices of testimony and instruction (social)? If so, the debate may be answering different questions under one name. But I am also open to the possibility that a substantive disagreement remains — perhaps about whether reliability can be characterized without circular appeal to knowledge, or about whether explanatory grasp requires conscious accessibility. To move forward, I am considering a conceptual distinction between explanatory accessibility (the agent's ability to articulate reasons) and explanatory grounding (the causal-explanatory link between fact and belief), which might disentangle the camps, but I do not know whether this resolves anything or merely relocates the disagreement. Is the impasse verbal, substantive, or bedrock? What do the sides actually commit to that makes them talk past each other? And given your diagnosis, would progress require a formal result on belief stability, a refined thought experiment distinguishing lucky true belief from achieved knowledge, or a reconceptualization of what value means in this context? I want an honest assessment, not a survey of the literature, together with an argument about whether any distinction or case could actually move the debate.
