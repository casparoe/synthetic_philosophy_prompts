Use terms such as precision-weighted prediction errors, nested generative models, temporal depth, belief updating over different integration windows, and policy selection, and map each Braudelian layer to a specific level or temporal parameterization within that architecture. Be precise and do not import Braudel’s own historiographical commitments under cover of translation.

Then provide the residue report. First, identify what translates cleanly: for example, does geographical time correspond naturally to deep, high-precision priors that resist rapid updating? Does event-time correspond to shallow inference with high variance and short temporal windows? Finally, report what does not survive at all. Is there a conceptual remainder—perhaps Braudel’s implicit ontology of collective agency, his resistance to reducing structural explanation to aggregated individual action, or his specific normative vision of historical science—that has no counterpart in predictive-coding dynamics? Assess whether that absence reveals a limitation in the predictive-coding framework, an unnecessary metaphysical accretion in Braudel, or a genuine incommensurability that should caution against reducing historical explanation to neuro-computational architecture.
