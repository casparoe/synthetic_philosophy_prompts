I am drafting a short essay for an advanced metaphysics workshop that revisits Black’s symmetric-universe thought experiment, in which two qualitatively identical spheres are suspended in an otherwise empty space. Rather than rehashing the familiar dialectic over whether spatial relations rescue Leibniz’s identity of indiscernibles, or whether weak discernibility is enough to preserve it, I want to pursue a less common line. My claim is that if we take the scenario seriously as a metaphysical model rather than a mere reductio, it reveals not just two indiscernible objects but a structural underdetermination in the space they inhabit: because there is no external frame of reference, the directional relation “sphere A is to the left of sphere B” lacks a determinate orientation that is independent of an arbitrary labeling choice. This suggests that the failure of the principle of identity of indiscernibles might extend upward from individual objects to the determinable relational structure of space itself, forcing us to ask whether the principle should be reformulated as a constraint on structural determinacy rather than on object-identity alone. I then consider whether this move genuinely shifts the metaphysical burden, or whether it collapses back into the standard response that relations are sufficient for discernibility once we allow them to be irreflexive and asymmetric. I am especially concerned about the middle section of the argument, where I move from epistemic inaccessibility—no observer inside the universe could tell which sphere is which—to metaphysical indeterminacy about orientation itself, and I worry I may be conflating the two. Could you comment on whether this extension of Black’s scenario to structural orientation is coherent, on whether I am mischaracterizing the original thought experiment by treating spatial direction as underdetermined rather than merely relative, and on whether this framing makes the essay philosophically interesting for specialists or merely convoluted? I would also welcome any suggestions for clarifying the distinction between relational discernibility and structural determinacy, particularly if there are hard cases where the two come apart that I should address explicitly.
