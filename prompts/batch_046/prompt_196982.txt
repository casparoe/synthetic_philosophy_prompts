Two mates of mine were arguing about Descartes' cogito after a lecture — acutally, they were in the pub and it got a bit heated. Maya said the cogito only proves there's thinking happening right now; Descartes sneaks in a whole substantial self just by using "I", but that's like saying "it is raining" proves there's an "it" doing the raining. You only get "there is thinking occurring here-now", nothing more. Tom said that's wrong because the "I" isn't like "it" in weather talk — every time you think, you can't help being aware it's you doing it. The indexical "I" is transparent, you don't observe yourself to use it, so you've genuinely got a self, just not via observation.

Maya came back with this idea about "thin" versus "thick" self-models: even if you grant the "I", you only get a thin, momentary self-model — not the thick Cartesian substance that persists through time. The cogito doesn't bridge one moment of awareness to the next; you're just stitching together snapshots. Tom said that's unfair — Descartes wasn't using the cogito alone to prove persistence, he uses God for that later. The cogito's job is just to show some thinking thing exists, and "I" correctly picks it out. If you reject the "I" completely, you can't even formulate the doubt properly.

Then Maya said if you allow "I" only as an indexical pointer, then an artificial system with a continuous self-referential loop could say "I think" without any inner life or substance. So the cogito proves too little — it can't tell a real mind from a well-designed echo chamber. Tom replied that she's mixing up conditions for knowing something with conditions for being something. Just because a machine could fake the words doesn't mean the original first-person certainty is hollow. You're demanding external verification for an internal fact, which misses the point entirely.
