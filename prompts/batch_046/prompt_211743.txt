Second, a separate "social-cost tariff" is added, scaling with the actual harm inflicted, even when that harm was unforeseeable or resulted from pure chance. Does that refusal reveal that the criminal tariff is not a neutral consequentialist add-on but an implicit admission that punishment, unlike tort, partly expresses social condemnation of harmful results?
