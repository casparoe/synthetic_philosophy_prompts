I just finished a long, frustrating argument with a friend about the international response to the violence in Darfur during the early 2000s. She insisted that the refusal to apply the genocide label—despite Colin Powell's 2004 determination that genocide had occurred—was straightforward strategic avoidance: states and the UN did not want to trigger the enforcement obligations of the 1948 Genocide Convention. I pushed back, arguing that something conceptually deeper was happening. The Convention adopted a narrowed version of Raphael Lemkin's original proposal, stripping away cultural and political destruction to focus narrowly on physical and biological destruction with specific intent. I think this narrowing does not merely limit legal obligations; it systematically shapes which mass atrocities are even visible to international law. I want you to take the Darfur episode—specifically the 2003–2005 violence, the U.S. determination, the UN's reluctance, and the 2005 Security Council referral to the International Criminal Court—and analyze it through the lens of Lemkin's broader original conception versus the Convention's legal category. Map the episode onto the theory: in what ways did the violence fit or fail to fit Lemkin's full framework, including attacks on group culture, language, and identity, rather than just the Convention's intent-focused definition? How does the politics of naming—who applies the label and what obligations it triggers—look different if we use Lemkin's framework rather than the 1948 text? Where does the theory miss the dynamics of the episode, for instance, the strategic role of advocacy groups, the fact that even without the genocide label intervention was still blocked, or the ICC's subsequent involvement and how its legal framing interacted with the naming debate? And finally, does the case show that naming politics is mainly about legal avoidance, or does it reveal that the conceptual framework itself—what counts as genocide—determines which atrocities the international system can recognize in the first place?
