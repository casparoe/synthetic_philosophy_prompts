hey — i'm putting together a decision-tree quiz for my grad seminar on torture ethics, specifically the ticking-bomb cluster but not the usual 'pro vs con' garbage. i want to sort students by what they actually say about concrete cases, not by which theorist they like.

the tree should have about 6-8 nodes and branch on three fault lines:

1. pure ticking bomb: near-certainty there's a bomb, you have the captor, torture works in time. do you authorize?
2. bureacratic framing: same scenario but there's a legal protocol, paper trail, agent just following orders. does the instutional wrapper change your answer?
3. corrupted-agent case: it saves thousands but the agent is destroyed — severe PTSD, can't live a normal moral life. is that psychological ruin a seperate moral cost, or just part of the price?

the leaves i need:
- absolute prohibition
- threshold deontology (yes only if direct agent + high epistemic bar, but institutionalization kills it)
- instutional consequentialism (bureaucracy is required, not corrupting)
- agent-neutral consequentialism (count agent suffering in calculus, still do it)
- agent-relative virtue ethics (agent's corruption is decisive: never do what ruins your character, regardless of numbers)

plus one branch on the 'enhanced interrogation' gap — sleep deprivation and sensory overload aren't torture legally but are morally. i want the tree to seperate people who think that legal/moral distinction tracks something real from those who think it's pure politics.

the scoring key should map answer patterns onto these leaves, flag unoccupied combos (e.g. yes to 1, no to 2, and treating 3 as irrelevant — incoherent, ugly, or unexplored?), and flag positions the tree can't cleanly seperate. audience is grad students who've read the standard stuff, but they answer concrete yes/no/depends — no 'are you a deontologist?' garbage.

build it like i'm emailing a colleague.
