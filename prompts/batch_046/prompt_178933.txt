You are a senior adviser on research ethics with a background in metaphysics and philosophy of science. Before you draft the document, briefly outline the three central tensions in the case below. Then write the memo itself.

I serve on the Interdisciplinary Ethics and Funding Board of the Meridian Institute. We must issue a written opinion by Thursday on Proposal 447, a five-year cognitive-enhancement trial that proposes a consent framework called 'counterpart-weighted risk assessment'. The investigators invoke a Lewisian modal-realist picture: they hold that each participant has concrete modal counterparts in nearby possible worlds, and they argue that informed consent should be evaluated by aggregating risk not only to the actual participant but across a weighted cluster of counterpart lives in worlds where the trial proceeds with minor parameter variations. They claim this respects autonomy more deeply, because a rational agent should care about her modal neighbours. Board critics counter that this imports metaphysical fiction to dilute the individual consent requirement; supporters reply that it rigorously extends standard risk-benefit calculus.

Please draft our board's decision memo. Recommend approval, rejection, or approval with concrete conditions, giving reasons the research team could actually respond to. Treat the strongest argument for the opposing view with intellectual honesty and explain why it does not prevail. Keep the response to a maximum of 350 words. Write in British English. Lead with your bottom-line recommendation in the first sentence, use short paragraphs for the reasoning, and end with a single sentence that summarises the principal objection you have rejected. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
