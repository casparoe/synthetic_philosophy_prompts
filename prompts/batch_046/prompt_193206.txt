Writing as a journalist covering philosophy of language and cognitive science, I am exploring whether Donald Davidson's framework of radical interpretation can be extended—or must be abandoned—when the speaker is a synthetic system that lacks mental life altogether. Please describe the most important implications of Davidson's radical interpretation, especially the interdependence of belief and meaning and the principle of charity, for the task of interpreting large language model outputs as meaningful discourse rather than mere statistical patterning. Specifically: can charity be applied when the interpreter knows the source possesses no propositional attitudes? Does the absence of belief on one side dissolve the interdependence thesis, or can meaning be anchored solely in observable linguistic behavior and environmental conditions? And what do these answers imply for our scientific and institutional practices of attributing comprehension to artificial agents?
