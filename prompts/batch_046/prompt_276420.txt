Write a poem of three six-line stanzas, spoken in Abraham’s first-person voice on the road to Moriah. In each stanza the opening line should repeat the same syntactic frame but change its addressee: first Sarah, then Isaac, then the servant left with the donkey—tracking the three silences that structure the narrative in Fear and Trembling. Maintain an ABABCC rhyme scheme through stanzas one and two; in stanza three, break the rhyme precisely at the B-position, leaving it unrhymed exactly where the universal ethical bond—the love that ought to return father to son—would have sounded. Do not explain or justify the suspension; enact it. The speaker should address the absolute relation without translating his act back into the ethical universal. By the closing couplet, someone familiar with Kierkegaard should be able to tell whether the posture is that of the knight of infinite resignation or the knight of faith—let the broken rhyme and the final address do the distinguishing work. Keep it precise: no modern commentary, no sermon on obedience, just the voice at the moment the universal has been suspended.
