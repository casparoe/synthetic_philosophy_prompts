I'm a PhD student in philosophy working at the intersection of metaethics and philosophy of biology. For an interdisciplinary workshop handout, I want a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on evolutionary debunking arguments—specifically, the claim that evolutionary explanations of our moral judgments undermine their justification. The tree should be built from logical branches, not from a list of named camps.

Start with the root question: do evolutionary origins of moral belief constitute an epistemic defeater at all? From there, branch on whether the defeater applies only to moral realism or extends to non-cognitivist and constructivist positions as well. Next, branch on whether a third-factor response—claiming that evolutionary selection tracks cooperation-promoting traits that also align with independent moral truths—is available for the specific domain in question. Then include a branch that distinguishes thin moral concepts (good, wrong) from thick ones (cruel, kind, courageous): does evolutionary anchoring in observable social dynamics shield thick concepts from debunking while leaving thin ones vulnerable? Finally, branch on whether successful debunking requires that our moral beliefs be systematically miscalibrated relative to evolutionary fitness, or merely that they lack any independent calibration mechanism.

Each leaf should be named (e.g., evolutionary debunker in the Street tradition, third-factor realist in the Enoch tradition, unnamed) and should carry one sentence stating its most awkward commitment—particularly regarding what it must say about the reliability of untutored first-order moral judgment or about the accessibility of moral truth without evolutionary scaffolding. Identify at least one branch combination that is currently unoccupied in the literature and briefly diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The tree should include concrete diagnostic questions at each node—phrased so a reader can answer them without already knowing which interpretation or philosopher they map onto—rather than asking readers to self-identify with a label.

Please present the taxonomy in American English. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Avoid references to highly specialized technical literature that could not be reconstructed without external search.
