As a doctoral student working at the intersection of philosophy of biology and formal systems theory, I am developing a comparative analysis of self-replicative architectures. Please compare the DNA–Gödel isomorphism—that is, the analogy between DNA replication and Gödel's arithmetisation of syntax yielding self-referential statements—with John von Neumann's universal constructor model. Specifically, examine whether the analogy holds once we acknowledge that DNA replication depends on an interpretive apparatus (polymerases, ribosomes, and the translational machinery) that is not fully encoded within the genome being copied. To sharpen the comparison, consider retrotransposons and other mobile genetic elements in which the copying machinery is itself partly embedded within the sequence being replicated, generating a nested reflexivity that resembles a tangled hierarchy rather than a strict object-language/meta-language separation. Does this embeddedness strengthen the Gödelian parallel by internalising the decoder, or does it undermine the isomorphism by dissolving the boundary between formal system and interpretation? I would appreciate a rigorous but accessible discussion, written in British English.
