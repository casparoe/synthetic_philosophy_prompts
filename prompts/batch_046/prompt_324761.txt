You're advising an interdisciplinary working group — a philosopher of mind, a scholar of Indo-Tibetan Buddhist philosophy, and a phenomenologically oriented cognitive scientist — with three years of funding and no obligation to produce empirical papers alone. Their shared question is whether the Buddhist doctrine of anatta (no-self) should be treated as a metaphysical denial of diachronic and synchronic unity conditions, or as a phenomenological-constructivist thesis about the malleability and constructedness of self-experience in advanced contemplative practice. They need to decide which of three research directions to prioritize.

First, a philosophical-textual line: clarifying whether Abhidharmic reductionism and Madhyamaka's two-truths framework commit anatta to a specific ontology of persons — for instance, a mereological bundle without substrate — or instead suspend all ontological commitment about the self, leaving the doctrine as a soteriological tool rather than a metaphysical thesis. This line asks whether anatta can be cleanly separated from Western bundle theories or whether it presupposes a distinctively Buddhist metaphysics of dependent origination that resists direct translation.

Second, an empirical-phenomenological line: investigating whether long-term practitioners of specific meditation techniques report a stable dissolution of phenomenal self-ownership — not merely a conceptual denial — and whether neurophenomenological data can distinguish a genuine absence of self-referential processing from a reinterpretive framing of it. The stakes are whether such findings would adjudicate between metaphysical and phenomenological readings of anatta, or whether they remain compatible with both.

Third, a normative-reconstructive line: examining whether accepting anatta on either reading undermines standard foundations for moral responsibility, narrative identity, or rights-bearing agency, and whether those foundations can be reconstructed using resources internal to Buddhist ethics — such as karma and compassion without an enduring agent — rather than imported from Western liberal frameworks.

Rank these three directions for the group's effort, defend the ordering with explicit criteria (tractability given the team's composition, potential to resolve ambiguity in the source texts, downstream consequences for debates in personal identity and moral philosophy), and specify which single result — if achieved — would most alter your ranking. I want a defended agenda, not a survey of the literature: explain what makes one line upstream of the others, where the trade-offs between philosophical precision and empirical access lie, and whether one direction risks being unfalsifiable or merely exegetical.
