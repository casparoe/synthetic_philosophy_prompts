I've been stuck in the prioritarianism literture lately and the leveling-down objection keeps circling round and round. You know the drill: Parfit-style priority to the worse off, then the reply that prioritarianism is weirdly compatable with dragging everyone down to the same miserable baseline, then the prioritarians saying they never endorsed equality as such, then the egalitarians saying that's exactly the problem. Feels like nobody's budged since the nineties, and I'm wondering what kind of disagreement this actually is. My working theory is that it's not really about distributive justice but hidden in the maths — specifically whether welfare can sit on a single cardinal scale across lives, and whether 'better off' even means the same thing ex ante versus ex post. But maybe it's just bedrock intuitions about fairness with some formal notation painted on top. Can you diagnose the stall properly? Is there a distinction — maybe around threshold effects in well-being, or about how we model benefits — that could actually move it, or should we just accept this one's stuck for good?
