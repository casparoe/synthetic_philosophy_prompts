Construct a thought experiment in which a scientist derives a non-obvious, empirically testable prediction about an unobservable physical process using only mental manipulation of an ordinary laboratory apparatus—without performing any actual measurement—engineered so that two epistemological accounts of scientific thought experiments reach opposite verdicts on whether genuine knowledge is gained. One account holds that such experiments yield knowledge solely by exposing internal logical inconsistencies in rival theories; the other holds that they work by exercising a mental model that tracks real modal or causal structure in nature. Constraints: the scenario must be realistic, confined to one paragraph, avoid Einstein’s elevator, Galileo’s falling bodies, Schrödinger’s cat, or any perfect-predictor device, and concern a prediction that could in principle be checked experimentally afterward. After presenting the scenario, walk through what each account says and why they diverge, identify the specific structural feature of the case that forces the split, and explain how a defender of the “logical consistency” account would attack the scenario as smuggling in empirical premises. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
