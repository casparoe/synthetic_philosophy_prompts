I've come around to a pretty narrow reading of Descartes's cogito: in the actual Meditations text, not the Discourse summary, the claim that he exists whenever he is thinking only licenses that there's currently an occurence of thinking, not that there's a persisting substantial self whose essence is thought. I think this thinner reading saves Descartes from the standard objection that we should say thinking is happening rather than attributing it to an I, and from circularity worries about clear and distinct ideas, but I'm not sure what actually kills it.
