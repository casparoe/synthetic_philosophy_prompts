I've been working through some problems in philosophy of mind for a graduate seminar on consciousness and causation, and I keep returning to the classic paradox that threatens epiphenomenalism: if phenomenal properties lack causal efficacy, then judgments about those properties are not caused by the properties themselves, which seems to rob those judgments of their justification and leave the epiphenomenalist with no stable ground for believing in consciousness at all. The standard replies either accept a limited skepticism about phenomenal knowledge or attempt to preserve causal relevance through overdetermination or property dualism. I want you to defend epiphenomenalism along a different, less traveled route. Argue that phenomenal judgments should be reconceived not as representational states that require causal interaction with their objects for reliability, but as what we might call "indexical calibration mechanisms"—functional roles within a physical system that register internal structural coherence conditions without representing phenomenal properties as causal agents. Specifically, maintain that once we reject the representationalist premise that phenomenal concepts must be anchored in causal contact (the premise that drives the paradox), the epiphenomenalist can claim that phenomenal judgments are trustworthy not because consciousness causes them, but because they are structurally isomorphic to the physical processes that do cause them, and that this isomorphism is maintained by evolutionary and cognitive selection for systemic stability rather than for veridical tracking of non-physical causes. You should address the objection that this reduces phenomenal judgment to a kind of grammatical fiction or eliminable heuristic, and explain why the indexical-calibration model avoids self-undermining without smuggling causal efficacy back into the phenomenal realm. Please develop the argument conceptually without offering a bibliography, reading list, or author-specific citations; treat the framework I've sketched as the starting point and build the defense from there.
