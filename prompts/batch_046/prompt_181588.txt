I am consulting for a small digital-preservation archive that has begun acquiring synthetic artifacts—AI-generated texts, images, and audio recordings—for its permanent collection, and the board wants a reusable intentional-status classification protocol rather than an ad hoc judgment for each acquisition. The protocol must assign every artifact one of three designations—original intentionality (borne by a conscious biological agent), derived intentionality (borne by an artifact in virtue of design, interpretation, or functional role), or null (no intentional content whatsoever)—and it must be operationalized from the philosophical disagreement between Dennett, whose functionalist framework allows artifacts to carry derived intentionality through the intentional stance and is sometimes characterized in terms of a two-bitser model of borrowed aboutness, and Searle, who holds that only biological brains possess original intentionality and that any derived case depends entirely on that prior biological source, leaving artifacts themselves semantically empty except as observer projections. I need this translated into a step-by-step checklist that an auditor without philosophical training can complete in roughly twenty minutes per artifact, with explicit decision nodes, clearly marked judgment calls that cannot be eliminated by further procedure, and instructions for recording ambiguity when origins are mixed—such as a human-edited model output, or an AI trained on intentional texts that produces novel syntactic combinations.

The deliverable should include worked applications to three specific cases: a fully automated language-model text with no human editing of the output; a synthetic portrait generated from a photographer’s highly detailed prompt specifying emotional tone and compositional intent; and a hybrid audio file in which an AI voice synthesizer reads a script written by a deceased author whose estate approved the synthesis but the author never heard or endorsed the result. One of these cases—most likely the hybrid audio—should be one where the procedure forces a classification that the acquisition committee will find uncomfortable, for instance that the artifact carries no intentional content of its own despite the estate’s authorization and the script’s original authorship, so that I can see exactly where the framework demands an answer that conflicts with institutional intuition. The checklist should state what it can and cannot do: in particular, it should acknowledge that it does not resolve whether derived aboutness in synthetic images is genuinely borrowed from the prompt-designer or merely projected by the viewer, and it should avoid any request for bibliographies, literature reviews, or citation lists, since I will supply those separately.
