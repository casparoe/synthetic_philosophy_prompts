I've been reading the old rationalist threads on phenomenal concepts since long before they got archived, and I'm tired of the standard framing where Mary either "learns a fact" or "doesn't." Instead, I want to audit an analogy that tech-minded physicalists often lean on: Mary's situation is like a programmer who has read the complete source code of a graphics-rendering pipeline—every shader, every matrix transform, every photon-emission equation—but has never actually compiled and executed it on hardware that produces visible light. The analogy is used to defend physicalism: running the code doesn't reveal a hidden non-computational fact; it just activates processes the programmer already knew about, so Mary's first seeing red activates neural processes she already knew about, with no extra ontology required.

Audit this analogy rigorously. First, lay out an explicit correspondence table: what maps to Mary's black-and-white education, to the physical facts she masters, to the moment of leaving the room, to the experience of red, to the brain states involved, and to the "knower" in each case. Then identify the disanalogies that matter for the physicalist conclusion: does the analogy smuggle in the idea that phenomenal experience is causally inert, like a rendered image that doesn't feed back into the GPU? Does it treat Mary's brain as lacking a "runtime environment" rather than lacking information, and if so, does that import a kind of substrate-dependent dualism? Does comparing physical knowledge to "source code" implicitly treat phenomenal properties as functional or relational roles, thereby begging the question against those who think they are intrinsic? Separate decorative mismatches (e.g., programmers are conscious, GPUs aren't) from structural ones that actually bear on Jackson's argument.

Next, consider whether repairing the analogy saves physicalism or breaks it. What happens if we replace the programmer with a perfect simulator that can run the rendering pipeline in silico without emitting photons—does Mary's analog then "see red" in simulation, and does that mean phenomenal experience is just high-fidelity modeling? Or if we insist that execution requires actual retinal stimulation, do we end up conceding that there is a non-physical "display layer" that code alone cannot reach?

Keep the analysis precise enough that someone who knows the knowledge argument can follow without searching for citations. Do not ask for bibliographies or reading lists.
