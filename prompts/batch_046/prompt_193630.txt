I defend a therapeutic-scaffolding reading of Nāgārjuna's catuskoti: the four corners are not jointly negated to establish śūnyatā as a metaphysical thesis about how things are, but are deployed sequentially to exhaust conceptual reification, with the fourth corner serving as a soteriological release-point into non-conceptual prajñā rather than as a logical operator. On this view, emptiness is not an ontological property but the absence of svabhāva-mistake, and the tetralemma functions like a ladder that is climbed and then discarded — not unlike the structure Wittgenstein describes. What would genuinely count against this reading? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, sorted by decisiveness: philological passages that treat at least one denial (e.g., of intrinsic existence) as literally true; formal or logical results showing the fourfold structure collapses without a substantive metaphysical commitment; cross-traditional comparisons that break the ladder analogy in damaging ways; and whatever defenders of the therapeutic view themselves concede as damaging. Please distinguish decisive defeaters from merely awkward ones, assess whether the position risks being insulated by ad hoc qualifications, and say whether such insulation is a problem for a soteriological interpretation. Also, tell me what would change your mind about this map of defeaters, and point to where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
