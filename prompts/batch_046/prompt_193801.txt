I used to study fish populations for thirty years, and now I'm taking philosophy courses in retirement because my wife said I needed a hobby that doesn't involve boats. There's this other student, Marcus, who talks about evidential cooperation in large worlds — the idea that if there are copies of you out there, you should act like you're coordinating even when you can't talk. He's given me his takes on several made-up cases, and I'm trying to guess his hidden rule, but I think I've hit a wall.

So far he says yes, cooperate, when there are perfect copies spread across causally seperate universes. He also says yes for two scientists on distant planets who evolved identical brains by pure chance, as long as they care about the same things. But he said no when two people just happen to read the same decision-theory blog and then make matching choices — he called that "environmental contamination," not real correlation. He said yes for me being one of millions of simulations on different servers, but no when two AI systems run the exact same downloaded code; he said that's just one program copied twice, not two minds.

Next seminar he's presenting his "staggered-entropy bridge" case, which he invented: two agents sit in totally isolated bubbles with different origins, different code, no shared training. But both use a deterministic procedure that reads local quantum vacuum fluctuations as a random seed. Because the vacuum state is globally correlated across the bubbles, they get identical inputs and make perfectly matching choices, without knowing about each other, and their values align. Will Marcus say cooperate? I'm guessing he might, but I can't tell if he's running a "shared mental origin" rule, a "no shared external cause" rule, or a "functional correlation is enough" rule. Which of his earlier verdicts best support each interpretation? And what further case — maybe one where reasoning is anti-correlated but world-structure forces alignment anyway — would actually settle which rule he's using? I don't need the "correct" philosophical answer; I just want to know if Marcus is consistent with himself, and which of his past cases is the closest analog to this bridge thing.
