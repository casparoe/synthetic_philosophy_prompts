As a novelist long preoccupied with the moral weight of complicity, I pose this question: imagine a voter who believes both candidates embody serious wrongs, yet holds that abstention is not neutrality but a silent delegation of power to the greater evil. Write an essay on whether she is morally bound to vote for the lesser evil, or whether that act transforms her from a reluctant shield into an expressive accomplice to the very harm she sought only to diminish. Weigh the instrumental logic of damage limitation against the symbolic corruption of democratic consent, and reach a definite verdict—should she cast the ballot or withhold it?—grounding your conclusion in a precise account of moral residue and participatory guilt.
