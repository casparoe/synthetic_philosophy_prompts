I sit on our city's Commission on Public Memory and we have to vote Thursday on a proposal for a downtown memorial plaza. The designers want a "negative heritage" layout that puts our local Lost Cause mythology side-by-side with Germany's Vergangenheitsbewältigung, Japanese peace museum debates, and Turkish state denialism — all in one site. The core philosophical tension is whether this comparative framing actually builds what some call "mnemonic solidarity" or whether it just dilutes specific moral accountability to local Black communities into an abstract global pattern. Half the commission wants to approve it as a bold intervention; the other half thinks it's a conceptual cop-out that avoids direct reckoning. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting: reccomend approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, include a concrete time frame or next step, and take seriously the strongest argument from the side you're arguing against. Write it in American English, and don't include any bibliography or citation list — just reasoning the body can actually act on.
