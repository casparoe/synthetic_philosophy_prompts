I've been having a row with my sister about whether our mum made an unethical choice back in the mid-nineties. At the time she was living near an old industrial site outside Leeds — she actually did some volunteer soil testing for the local council, so she definately knew about the contamination — and she went ahead and had me in '95 rather than waiting until '98, when she and dad were planning to move to Cornwall for his new job. I was born with this chronic respiratory condition that's pretty clearly linked to the prenatal exposure. Now mum always says I can't be angry with her, because if she'd waited I'd literally be a different person — different egg, different sperm, probably born in Cornwall with healthier lungs but also a totally different personality and life path — so there's no specific 'me' that was harmed by the decision. I've been reading a bit about whether you can wrong someone just by bringing them into existence, and I understand the logic, but it still feels like she picked the path that she knew would produce a damaged child over the path that would produce a healthy one, and that should count for something.

Does her defence actually hold water, or is she hiding behind a metaphysical technicality? And does it matter that dad wanted to wait but she overruled him because she was worried about losing her seniority at work — so there's this extra layer of agency and disagreement that complicates the picture? I'm trying to work out whether my gut feeling that she was reckless is just sentimental, or whether there's a real moral gap in her reasoning that the philosophy doesn't cover.
