Consider the following argument, which applies Jaynes's presumption of independence to Bell's locality condition and then extends the result to all statistical reasoning.

(1) Formalize the presumption of independence (PoI): if a model specifies no coupling mechanism between U and V, their joint distribution factorizes; spatial separation with no post-preparation interaction is standardly taken as just such an absence.

(2) Bell's locality factorization is PoI made conditional on a complete hidden state: P(A,B | X,Y,λ) = P(A | X,λ) P(B | Y,λ).

(3) For quantum-entangled pairs, the statistical predictions violate the Bell inequalities implied by (2).

(4) So for these pairs, either PoI fails despite spatial separation and absence of interaction, or λ is not locally complete—there is a hidden coupling that transcends space.

(5) In ordinary science—epidemiology, cosmology, econometrics—we invoke independence for distant measurements precisely on the grounds of spatial separation and lack of interaction.

(6) But if those grounds are insufficient in quantum mechanics, they are insufficient everywhere; Bell's derivation relies only on probabilistic factorization and counterfactual settings, not on scale or on Planck's constant.

(7) One might try to confine the failure to quantum domains. Yet there is no principled boundary in the argument itself: any hidden-variable model for any system, macroscopic or not, is subject to the same factorization demand if it claims local completeness.

(8) Therefore, either we accept that every spatially separated pair of events is linked by a non-local hidden mechanism (rendering inductive modeling impossible), or we abandon PoI globally (rendering statistical inference groundless).

The conclusion—that maintaining Bell's result together with ordinary statistical practice requires either universal hidden correlations or the rejection of independence reasoning—seems absurd. I want to preserve both the validity of Bell's theorem and the legitimacy of using independence assumptions outside quantum physics. Which step should I reject? In particular, is the flaw an equivocation between epistemic modeling independence and ontic physical factorization, a scope shift about what λ must encode for prepared versus unprepared pairs, or an illicit generalization from entangled-state correlations to arbitrary macroscopic separation? And what does the correct rejection commit me to about whether locality is a physical constraint or merely a default modeling heuristic?
