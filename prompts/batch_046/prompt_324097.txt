Here are my gut calls on welfare conditionality and I can't square them. If a claiment misses a workfare chek-in because the bus broke down and they have no phone credit, cutting benefits for a month feels like punishing poverty itself — wrong. If they miss it hungover, cutting them feels... maybe still wrong? Or at least not clearly right? But if they refuse a job that'd leave them worse off after childcare costs, I think they should keep support; if they refuse the same job just because they hate early mornings, I think sanctions are okay. Meanwhile working cash-in-hand while claiming is clearly wrong and should mean loss of benefits, but forgetting to declare a small family gift and getting fully disqualified feels like bureaucratic cruelty, not justice. I also think forcing people into unpaid "work experience" stacking shelves is degrading, but I'm unsure whether that means they can opt out without penalty, or whether the penalty itself is the problem. So is there a principle — maybe separating desert-tracking conditions from behavioral-correction ones, or tracking whether the breach is within the claimant's control versus a structural failure — that gets all these? Or do I have to drop one? And what does your best principle say about the case I keep flipping on: refusing to apply for jobs outside a 30-minute commute because you're part-time caring for an elderly parent, when the jobcentre says that's not a valid exemption? Also predict what it says about someone who turns down a training course not because it's useless but because it conflicts with a religious observance they never mentioned before.
