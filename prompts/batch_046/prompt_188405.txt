My philosophy tutor has assessed several classic thought experiments, judging whether each functions as a legitimate intuition pump or misleads through framing, level-confusion, conventional ambiguity, or unexamined modal intuition. Her verdicts are consistent but not unambiguous. She regards Mary’s room—the scientist who knows every physical fact about colour vision yet has never experienced red—as legitimate, because it isolates a conceptual gap between complete physical description and phenomenal acquaintance without smuggling in empirical assumptions. She regards Davidson’s swampman—an exact physical duplicate of a person that arises by pure chance in a swamp—as legitimate, because it cleanly tests whether intentionality requires a specific causal history. By contrast, she dismisses the zombie argument as misleading, on the grounds that conceivability is a fallible guide to possibility and the scenario depends on an intuitive modal leap rather than on an isolable conceptual variable. She similarly rejects the switch-versus-push framing of the trolley problem, arguing that the divergence in judgement tracks psychological proximity and direct agency rather than any deep moral principle. Finally, she treats the ship of Theseus as misleading, holding that the puzzle dissolves once we recognise that identity conditions for artefacts are conventionally fixed rather than metaphysically profound.

Next term she will consider the fading-qualia scenario: a subject’s biological neurons are replaced one by one over a decade with functionally identical silicon chips, with the subject reporting uninterrupted conscious experience throughout, until no neural tissue remains. The pump asks whether consciousness has gradually faded, vanished suddenly at the final replacement, or persisted unchanged, and whether this tells us anything about the substrate-dependence of mind.

Predict her verdict—will she treat this as a legitimate pump or as misleading? Identify the two most plausible principles that could jointly account for her earlier pattern, say which best fits her track record and why, name the closest analogues among her previous verdicts for each principle, and specify one further case that would distinguish the rules. Be precise about what hangs on each choice: in particular, whether the gradual nature of the replacement introduces a framing artefact or instead provides a clean stress-test for reductionism.
