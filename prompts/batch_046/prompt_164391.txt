Next month I defend my thesis at an interdisciplinary workshop on AI alignment and philosophy of cognition. My core claim is that the HCH (Human-Computer-Human) scheme, understood as an idealization of deliberation through factored cognition, implicitly treats complex judgment as decomposable into independently verifiable sub-processes, and that this decomposition eliminates the holistic, context-sensitive integration that makes human oversight genuinely epistemic rather than merely procedural—so that HCH scales a simulacrum of judgment inheriting the biases of its factoring protocol without retaining the corrective capacity of unified expert evaluation. The audience will include: two alignment researchers who built parts of the factored-cognition pipeline and regard decomposition as an unproblematic engineering convenience; three philosophers of mind skeptical of modularity but largely unfamiliar with the technical architecture of HCH; and a cognitive scientist who studies expert judgment and believes both sides neglect the role of tacit knowledge in evaluation. I need a speaker’s briefing, not a general essay: the objections they will actually raise, in roughly the order they will come, phrased in the vocabulary each faction uses; for each, whether it has real force against my specific claim about holistic integration, what I should concede up front, and where my reply is weakest. Please also identify the one question—likely about whether non-factored oversight is feasible at scale—that I am hoping nobody asks, together with a concise reply that avoids hand-waving, and flag the weakest point in my own position that I should prepare to defend rather than hope they miss.
