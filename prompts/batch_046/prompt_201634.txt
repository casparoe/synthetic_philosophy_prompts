To remedy this, she introduces a second-order tracking requirement: for deductive closure to hold, the subject must not only track the premise p but also track that her method tracks p, so that in nearby worlds where the conclusion q is false despite p being true, she can detect the misapplication of inference. For each main question, sketch the reply she is most likely to offer, then provide a targeted follow-up designed to block her standard escape routes—especially appeals to pragmatic encroachment, attempts to restrict tracking to empirical beliefs only, or claims that closure is merely a heuristic norm rather than a knowledge-generating mechanism.
