My study group has this argument about Darwin's views on morality and religion that seems to prove way too much. (1) Darwin argued that moral sentiments are just evolved social instincts in herd animals, not tracking objective moral truths. (2) He also claimed religious belief comes from our causal-reasoning machinery overfiring - seeing agents behind random events. (3) But if morality and religion are both adaptive misfirings of the same cognitive equipement, then the scientific reasoning that produced evolutionary theory is also just an adaptive misfiring, since it uses that exact same reasoning apparatus. (4) So Darwin's account implies that natural selection itself is just another fiction produced by unreliable cognitive machinery. (5) Therefore evolutionary naturalism undermines every belief it produces, including itself. This can't be right, but I can't see the weak link. Which premise do I drop? I'd really like to keep Darwin's actual explanation of morality intact if I can, but I don't know what that costs.
