I serve on my university's Senate Subcommittee on Associational Recognition. We must decide, by the end of this month, whether to renew institutional recognition—including funding and facility access—for the campus chapter of a national political party that applied under our revised Charter for Civic Engagement. The charter, adopted last year, introduces what we are calling a "deliberative autonomy standard": any recognized association must guarantee that its members may articulate, in university-sponsored forums, positions that diverge from the organization's official line without facing internal sanction or loss of membership status. The party chapter has refused to adopt this clause, arguing that party discipline is essential to democratic representation and that the standard violates freedom of association.

My subcommittee needs a concrete recommendation—grant recognition with conditions, deny it, or propose a phased compliance timeline—backed by reasoning that takes seriously Simone Weil's critique of political parties as organizations that substitute collective passion and organizational loyalty for individual moral judgment and attention to justice. In particular, the memo should engage the chapter's argument about democratic efficacy rather than dismissing it, and it should explain whether Weilian concerns about the corruption of individual conscience apply differently to a campus affiliate than to a national party apparatus. The recommendation must include a specific time frame for implementation or appeal. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting.
