I need a reusable procedue — like an ordered checklist with stop points — based on this rough idea: Dutch directness protects propositional transparency, Japanese indirectness protects social face, and white lies are wrong when they create a "shadow claim" (my term for a literally true sentence arranged so the listener infers a false commitment about facts or intentions) but okay as "buffer clauses" that are pure social grease and change nothing accountable.

For each memo I have about five mintues, no legal background, and both unit heads have to sign off. The procedure should ask questions in sequence, say what counts as enough to pass, flag where it stops with "rewrite required", and clearly mark judgment calls where norms clash. Also tell me what this procedure absolutely can't settle — like whether intentions are public property in the first place — and where it will misfire if one side treats every indirectness as deception. Keep it practical, not academic, and flag any step that requires human judgment rather than a rule.
