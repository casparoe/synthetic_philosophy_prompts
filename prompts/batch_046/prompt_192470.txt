okay so i've been thinking about this deadlock in the epistemic closure literature and it's driving me a little nuts. Nozick's tracking conditions clearly violate closure — if you track that you have hands, you don't track that you're not a BIV, even though the entailment holds. The anti-closure camp says this is a feature: it dissolves skepticism without denying entailment. The closure-defenders say that's a pyrrhic victory, because if knowledge can't be transmited across known entailments then it can't license practical reasoning — you can't derive "this bridge is safe" from structural integrity if the transmission breaks. Both sides have been saying this since the 80s. My hunch is that they're not disagreeing about the formal conditions but about whether tracking and closure answer to the same normative standard — tracking cares about belief-formation, closure cares about inferential license, and nobody has shown they have to align. But maybe i'm wrong and there's a hidden formal disagreement about how tracking behaves across modal spaces. What's the actual structure of this impasse? Verbal, normative, or substantive? And given your diagnosis, is there a distinction or formal result that could break it, or are we stuck with competing intuitions about skepticism?
