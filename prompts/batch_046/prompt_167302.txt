I am trying to determine whether the following set of normative commitments is jointly tenable, and if not, where the least costly revision lies.

First, I endorse the separateness-of-persons objection to utilitarian aggregation: I deny that a society can be treated as a single subject of experience whose total welfare is the sum of individual welfare levels, and I hold that this blocks trade-offs in which one person bears severe harm for the sake of large aggregate gains distributed among others.

Second, I believe that in evaluating long-range climate and demographic policies, we should compare outcomes by computing expected aggregate welfare across all individuals who will exist under each policy, precisely because the non-identity problem prevents us from framing the choice as harming particular future people.

Third, I accept that agent-relative reasons are irreducible: a parent has a stronger moral reason to rescue their own child than to rescue an equally endangered stranger's child, and this asymmetry is not captured by giving the parent's preference a heavier weight in an impersonal calculus.

Fourth, I support prioritarian social choice: distributions should be ranked by a weighted sum in which benefits to the worse-off count for more, but the evaluation still requires aggregating across individuals.

Fifth, I reject the classic one-to-save-five organ-harvesting scenario, but my ground for rejection is substitutability rather than rights: it is impermissible to treat one person's catastrophic loss as fully offset by five others' catastrophic gains.

At first glance, commitments one and five seem to reject interpersonal fungibility, while commitments two and four appear to rely on it. But I am unsure whether this is a genuine contradiction or whether there is a principled distinction—perhaps between direct agency and structural evaluation, or between ex post and ex ante perspectives—that can preserve all five. I also wonder whether commitment four's prioritarian weighting genuinely avoids the aggregation problem or merely modifies its coefficients. I want an audit: which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and if consistency requires a revision, which commitment is the cheapest to modify or abandon.
