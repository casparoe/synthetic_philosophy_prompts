For a graduate seminar on philosophical conceptions of embodiment I have been attempting to formalize my interpretive stance on Nietzsche's scattered but insistent writings about diet, digestion, and physiological health, and I need an audit of whether my commitments actually hang together. Here is the profile: I take the gastric metaphors that recur in Twilight of the Idols and in Ecce Homo to be performing substantive philosophical labor rather than functioning as decorative rhetoric; I hold that Nietzschean health denotes not homeostatic equilibrium but a volatile, self-overcoming capacity that thrives on internal conflict and can therefore require what looks like physiological instability; I interpret his apparently contradictory dietary prescriptions—praise of meat and strong nourishment in some contexts, advocacy of lighter, vegetable-based regimes in others—as unified by a contextualist principle according to which nutrition should be calibrated to the specific demands of the will to power in a given life-form; I maintain that his critique of modern German cuisine and his rejection of ascetic dietary denial share one underlying logic, namely the prohibition against any regimen that suppresses the body's productive tensions; and I wish to maintain that this entire physiological framework can be endorsed without committing to the elitist cultural conclusions Nietzsche sometimes draws about which types of human being are entitled to such robust health. Please examine this set for joint tenability. Identify which pairs pull against each other and rank the tensions by severity; distinguish genuine conflicts from merely apparent ones; specify, for each real tension, what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost; and advise me which single commitment I should surrender if I want the cleanest restoration of consistency.
