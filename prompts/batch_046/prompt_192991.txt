Write a branching taxonomy — a decision tree that sorts conceptual positions — on whether and how to apply the category of genocide to mass atrocities that sit at or beyond the boundaries of the 1948 Convention definition. Build the tree from logical distinctions rather than from a roster of named scholars, so that a reader can answer each node without already knowing which leaf it produces. Start with the root question: is the defining feature specific intent to destroy a protected group in whole or in part (the Convention's Article II formulation), foreseeable group destruction as an unintended but certain side-effect of state policy, or the performative speech act of naming and recognizing the event as genocide by a powerful state or international body? From there, branch through four further splits: (i) whether the protected group must exist as a social entity prior to and independently of the perpetrator's categorization, or whether the perpetrator's labeling can partly constitute the group that is destroyed; (ii) whether cultural, linguistic, or economic destruction without direct mass killing falls within the category (the broader original Lemkin conception) or remains outside it (the narrow legal conception); (iii) whether political recognition — who names the atrocity, in what forum, and for what strategic purpose — is constitutive of its status as genocide or merely descriptive and retrospective; and (iv) whether moral gravity is binary (the event either is or is not genocide) or scalar (it can possess genocidal character to varying degrees). Each leaf should represent a unique combination of answers, labeled with a recognizable position name if one exists or marked 'unnamed,' and should carry one sentence describing its most awkward or counterintuitive commitment. Place four concrete reference cases at appropriate nodes or leaves: the Herero and Namaqua killings (1904–1908), the Bangladesh killings of 1971, the Holodomor (1932–1933), and a hypothetical scenario in which a state systematically eliminates a minority's language, legal personhood, and educational institutions without direct mass killing. For any logical combination that remains unoccupied, briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, politically unstable, or merely unexplored. The deliverable should be answerable through conceptual analysis alone; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
