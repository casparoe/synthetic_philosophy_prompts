I'd like the discussion to center on reading the film through two linked frameworks: procreative beneficence — the view that prospective parents have a moral reason to select, via embryo screening or genetic engineering, the child with the best expected life — and liberal eugenics, which insists that any such selection must be parent-driven and non-coercive rather than state-mandated. The film seems to treat Vincent, born without genetic selection in the old-fashioned manner, as the very same person who would have existed had his parents used the available technology. That invites the non-identity problem: if selecting a different embryo produces a numerically different child, can we coherently say Vincent was harmed by not being selected, or that his parents wronged him?

Read Gattaca against both ideas. Where does Vincent's struggle — his heart condition, his borrowed identity, the constant genetic surveillance — dramatize the case for liberal parental choice? Where does it instead expose a confusion between preventing harm and selecting for advantage?
