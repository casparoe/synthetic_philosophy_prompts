hey, in my philosophy of mind reading group we've been going in circeles for like six months on the Dennett vs Searle intentionality debate, but we're stuck on this specific version of it. Dennett's two-bitser tracks quarters vs half-dollars because its design history ties internal states to coin-types — that's derived intenionality, right? Searle says sure, but that's not real aboutness; the chip doesn't understand anything, the designer does. So far so standard. But then half our group says "derived" is just a euphemism for "fake," and since the chip's tracking is causally robust, there has to be some original intentionality in the system itself, or else we'd have to deny it to animals too. The other half says Searle isn't denying animals, he's just saying syntax never becomes semantics without a biological substrate, and that Dennett is smuggling in original intentionality through evolutionary history without arguing for it. We all agree on the mechanics of the two-bitser, we agree understanding isn't literally in the chip, but we can't agree on whether the disagreement is metaphysical — about what kinds of things can bear original intentionality — or just terminological, with "original" doing normative work. I keep wondering if we're actually answering different questions: one about the causal origin of aboutness, one about whether derived states have explanatory autonomy. Is this a hidden factual question about whether evolutionary history is necessary? Or bedrock intuitions about naturalness? Given the stall, what kind of disagreement is it actually, and is there a formal distinction or a change of question that could move us, or should we just accept it's ill-posed?
