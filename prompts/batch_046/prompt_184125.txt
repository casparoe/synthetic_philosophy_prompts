I am a high school student preparing for an advanced philosophy examination, and I would like you to construct a rigorous essay question together with a model answer and a marking scheme. The topic should be Bernard Williams's integrity objection to utilitarianism, centered on the idea that certain moral calculations involve one thought too many. The question must not be answerable by merely summarizing Williams's argument or repeating the phrase as a label; instead, it should require the respondent to explain why the objection targets the structure of utilitarian moral reasoning rather than only its conclusions, and to evaluate whether a rule-utilitarian or two-level account can absorb the objection without abandoning impartial aggregation. Specifically, the respondent should be forced to clarify whether Williams's concern is about the agent's direct relationship to her own projects or merely about emotional cost and psychological damage. Please provide a model answer of approximately 650 to 750 words and a marking scheme that distinguishes an excellent answer from a good one by naming the specific analytical moves each must contain—particularly the recognition that integrity is not reducible to preference-satisfaction or welfare maximization. In both the model answer and the marking scheme, clearly separate what is settled in the literature—namely, that Williams rejects the separation of moral reasoning from the agent's ground projects—from what remains genuinely contested, such as whether the objection succeeds against all forms of indirect utilitarianism and whether integrity can be defended without circularity. Do not present contested interpretations, for instance the claim that Williams is simply advocating for partiality over impartiality, as if they were the consensus among philosophers who work on this topic.
