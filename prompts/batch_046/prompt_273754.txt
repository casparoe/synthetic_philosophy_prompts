Write an essay examining whether tranquilism—the thesis that moral value resides solely in the absence of craving and suffering, with the presence of pleasure or satisfied desire being morally neutral—can sustain a coherent account of procreative ethics. You should address the following specific tension: on a tranquilist view, bringing a being into existence introduces the risk of suffering but does not confer any positive moral benefit if that being lives a craving-free, contented life, since contentment is valued only as the absence of distress rather than as a good in itself. Consider whether this commits the tranquilist to a form of antinatalism, or whether there is a principled distinction between the duty to relieve existing suffering and the permissibility of creating beings who are likely to remain in a state of neutral, craving-free equilibrium. Evaluate the objection that tranquilism implicitly relies on a hidden positive axiology—namely, that the state of satisfied non-craving is preferable to non-existence—without which the view cannot explain why relieving suffering matters more than preventing existence altogether. Reach and defend a definite conclusion about whether tranquilism must reject procreation outright, permit it under strict conditions, or revise its core commitments. Please provide your response in American English.
