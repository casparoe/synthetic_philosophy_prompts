I'm moderating a podcast Q&A with a contemporary philosopher defending a revived Goodman-style account of induction: the grue paradox is dissolved not by discovering hidden natural structures but by treating projectibility as a matter of predicate entrenchment, where "green" is entrenched and "grue" is not, with entrenchment defined descriptively as the accumulated history of successful projections within a linguistic practice rather than prescriptively or metaphysically. She routinely deflects the circularity charge by saying entrenchment catalogs induction's habits rather than justifying them, and she dismisses Bayesian reconstructions as smuggling in an unearned simplicity prior. Write me a sequence of interview questions that makes that deflection progressively harder to deploy, ending with one about what evidence could undermine the view. Press her on how a purely descriptive catalog of past projections licenses future ones without collapsing into a record of lucky streaks; on whether the paradox re-emerges in probabilistic form if gerrymandered predicates can be assigned identical evidential weight; on what rational basis remains for extending entrenched predicates into domains—such as exoplanet mineralogy—where our projection history is absent; and on whether radically different cross-cultural entrenchment patterns, or a predictive system that succeeds using grue-like partitions, would force her to concede that induction tracks something beyond linguistic habit.
