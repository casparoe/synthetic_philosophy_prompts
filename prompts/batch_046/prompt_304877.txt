As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy of mind and artificial intelligence, I keep returning to an analogy with measurement disturbance: in quantum mechanics the apparatus is part of the system measured, and in embedded agency the reasoning process is part of the world it reasons about. I want to press this analogy past metaphor, but I need help calibrating whether it reveals a genuine structural limitation.

Consider the following precise claim: for any agent physically embedded in the environment upon which it acts—including, specifically, artificial systems built with present transformer-based or reinforcement-learning architectures—any attempt to construct a genuinely predictive, as opposed to performative or self-fulfilling, model of its own decision procedure, for the explicit purpose of correcting biases in that procedure, necessarily faces an exhaustive trilemma. Either (1) the model remains causally coupled to the decision process in a way that precludes independent evaluation of its predictive accuracy; or (2) the agent achieves decoupling only through a physical or logical separation that renders the correction inapplicable to the generating process itself; or (3) there exists a stable intermediate regime that avoids both pitfalls, but this regime requires an architectural feature—such as a genuinely indexical forecasting layer with causal insulation—not present in current methods.

Please give me your overall credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that this trilemma is correct and exhaustive for systems built with present methods, with the reasoning decomposed. Specifically, assign a rough probability to component (1): that causal coupling genuinely undermines independent accuracy-evaluation for embedded self-models, rather than being a manageable engineering constraint. Assign a rough probability to component (2): that decoupling necessarily defeats integrated self-correction, rather than allowing a useful feedback loop through indirect channels. Assign a rough probability to the negation of component (3): that is, the probability that no stable intermediate regime exists within current or near-current architectures. Then explain how these three values combine—whether conjunctively, multiplicatively, or by some other logic—to yield your overall number. Tell me which component your final credence is most sensitive to. Finally, describe the specific theoretical argument or empirical result—perhaps involving a particular architecture or a formal result about self-reference—that would most move that sensitive component, and in which direction.
