These verdicts seem jointly consistent with more than one principle, so I want to use them to predict his response to a deliberately constructed contemporary case. The case is a philosopher of mind who lives in a climate-controlled apartment, consumes a fully synthetic, AI-calibrated nutrient regimen engineered to eliminate digestive variability, suppresses any remaining discomfort with medication, replaces outdoor walking with indoor neurostimulation, and reports sustained cognitive brilliance—defending the arrangement on the grounds that philosophy aims at pure thought and therefore the body should be engineered entirely in its service, with natural conditions replaced by optimized ones.

Please answer in the following sections, with approximate lengths: "Summary of the Track Record" (one paragraph); "Candidate Governing Rules" (two paragraphs identifying at least two distinct principles—such as instrumental, naturalistic, or anti-ascetic—that accommodate all six earlier verdicts); "Prediction and Reasoning" (two paragraphs giving your best guess of Nietzsche’s verdict on the synthetic-regimen philosopher, naming the closest analog in the track record, stating what principle he would be adopting if he approved and what principle if he condemned, and being honest about where the precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome); and "The Decisive Further Case" (one paragraph describing a hypothetical variation that would distinguish the rules you have identified). Do not provide a bibliography or reading list, do not ask me to verify sources, and avoid verbatim quotations from Nietzsche’s texts; paraphrase freely. I am particularly interested in whether the underlying rule is that diet serves cognition, that it must harmonize with natural environment and bodily nature, or that any treatment of the body as a mere instrument for the mind is a form of life-denying decadence—since all three fit his hatred of beer and his praise of Italian simplicity, yet they pull in different directions here.
