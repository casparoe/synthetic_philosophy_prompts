Design a 90-minute classroom activity for an upper-level metaphysics seminar of about 12 students that makes participants feel the conceptual pressure of finks and masks on analyses of dispositions. Rather than relying on the standard physical examples, frame the exercise around institutional normative dispositions: construct a simulated Ethics Review Board in which each student is secretly assigned an underlying dispositional profile—specifically, being disposed to approve risky research proposals versus being disposed to reject them. Give two hidden students “fink” cards that automatically reverse their manifest vote when the triggering conditions are met, and give two others “mask” cards that suppress their manifest vote regardless of conditions. Over three staged proposal rounds, the board must first observe preliminary behavior, then predict each member’s underlying disposition for the final round, and finally vote collectively on resource allocation based on those predictions. Use a simple token-based payoff scheme that rewards accurate identification of underlying dispositions rather than correct prediction of observed votes, so that observable consistency can actively mislead. Include explicit rules, materials (cards, proposal briefs, prediction sheets), roles for the facilitator and for the four hidden mechanism holders, and a precise timeline dividing observation, prediction, revelation, and structured debrief. In the debrief, map the preliminary prediction failures onto the failure of the simple conditional analysis of dispositions; use the actual card distributions from the run to distinguish fink-induced reversals from mask-induced suppressions; explain what will predictably go wrong the first time the activity is run—for instance, students conflating behavioral consistency across rounds with possession of the underlying disposition, or mechanism holders experiencing social discomfort when revealed—and be explicit about which metaphysical feature, such as the modal stability of the disposition across nearby counterfactual conditions, this social simulation cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator. Write the response in American English, maintain a formal and polished tone, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Refer to specific philosophers or arguments only by paraphrase without quotation marks.
