You are constructing a reusable audit protocol for a multidisciplinary journal whose submission guidelines now require authors to label every substantive claim with one of four confidence tiers: near-certain, highly probable, plausible, or speculative. The board needs a procedure—runnable in fifteen minutes by an editor without philosophical training—that applies the consistency constraints underlying the lottery paradox and the preface paradox. Do not treat named philosophical positions, such as contextualism or threshold adjustment, as black-box solutions; instead, derive each step directly from the logical tension between rational belief in individual claims and rational belief about the collective set.

Design the procedure as an ordered sequence of decision nodes. For each node, specify: the precise diagnostic question about the manuscript's claim-set; the logical test, drawn explicitly from either the lottery problem (the accumulation of individually probable errors) or the preface problem (the coexistence of full belief in each member with belief that at least one member fails); the mandatory output (revision request, warning flag, or hand-off to editorial judgment because the issue is one of values rather than logic); and the exact point at which judgment cannot be eliminated. Be honest about where the procedure will misfire—particularly where scientific norms of epistemic humility conflict with logical consistency.

Then apply the protocol to three submissions. First, a paper advancing forty distinct empirical predictions, each tagged highly probable, with no meta-level qualification. Second, a theoretical paper tagging its central framework near-certain while explicitly noting that several auxiliary assumptions may be false—thereby activating both paradoxes simultaneously. Third, a review article tagging every cited study's conclusion as highly probable but including a preface-style disclaimer that some of these results will not replicate. For each, report the protocol's output, highlight the case where the result conflicts with intuitive editorial practice and forces an unwelcome revision, and identify what remains unsettled after the procedure ends.

Deliver a practical checklist or flowchart-in-words, not an abstract essay, and flag the limitations that no procedural fix can resolve.
