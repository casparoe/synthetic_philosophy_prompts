I was arguing with a colleague over coffee whether the etiological and causal-role accounts of biological function ever genuinely diverge in practice, or whether philosophers just pick convenient examples—hearts, kidneys, eyes—where the two stories align. I think they can split violently, but only if you leave the comfort of naturally evolved organs and look at mechanisms where evolutionary history is fragmented or missing altogether. So I need you to design a single, runnable classroom activity for my graduate philosophy-of-biology seminar: fourteen students, one ninety-minute session, no prerequisites beyond an introductory course. The goal is to make the divergence visceral before anyone names it.

Frame the case study around a synthetic-evolved chimeric subsystem—an organism containing both naturally evolved tissue and a synthetic graft that has never undergone natural selection, yet is fully metabolically and regulatorily integrated. Divide the class into a deliberative “Repair Board” with assigned mandates: half must defend verdicts using only selection-history reasoning, half using only current system-contribution reasoning. Give them decision cards describing malfunctions in both the evolved and grafted components, impose a peer-evaluation payoff scheme (not money) that rewards consistency with one’s assigned account, and force a binding vote on whether the graft’s activity counts as a genuine function worthy of preservation or replacement. The rules must be arranged so students hit the contradiction themselves—where causal integration is complete but evolutionary origin is absent—before the debrief maps anything onto philosophy.

Deliver the full design: a minute-by-minute timeline, role assignments, card contents or decision criteria, the payoff structure, and a debrief that maps specific game moments onto the distinction between selection-based proper function and causal-role contribution. Predict honestly what will go wrong the first time—likely students smuggling design-intention into evolutionary history, or treating “function” as purely normative without seeing the metaphysical split—and be frank about which feature, probably the normative force of dysfunction or the appeal to evolutionary “properness,” this simulation cannot reproduce and you will have to supply verbally. Make a committed choice: pick one activity structure and defend why its mechanics necessarily generate the tension, rather than surveying options. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the design should run without any external lookup.
