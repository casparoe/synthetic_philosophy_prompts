I sit on the clinical-ethics board at my hospital and we're deadlocked over a policy for ClinAssist, the LLM that drafts preliminary radiology reports. The thing sometimes outputs first-person language like "I'm worried about the shadow in the upper lobe," and half the board wants us to formally prohibit clinicians from adopting the intentional stance toward it — they argue it's a category mistake that creates liability if a doc treats machine output like a colleague's opinion. The other half says banning it is useless because people will read it intentionaly in the heat of a shift anyway, so we should instead set conditions under which that stance is predictive and non-redundant with the design stance. There's also this philosophy consultant who keeps citing Dennett's real patterns idea: she claims that if intentional-stance predictions about ClinAssist's errors are stable and can't be matched by the physical or design stance — especially since we don't have real-time access to weights — then the patterns are genuinely real for governance purposes, not just heuristic fiction. I need a memo for Thursday recommending one concrete course — approve restricted use, ban outright, or run a 90-day pilot — with clear evaluation criterias and a fair treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side. Please don't include a bibliography or "see Dennett 1991"; we have the texts, I just need reasoning the board can actually vote on.
