I have never studied philosophy in a university, but for several years I have thought on my own about a problem I later learned is called the regress of justification: if I need a reason for every belief I hold, then either I eventually reach beliefs that need no further reason, or my reasons circle back on themselves, or the chain never ends. I understand that philosophers call these positions foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism. Recently I have begun to wonder whether the same structure applies not just to beliefs about the world, but to practical commitments—such as deciding to honor an agreement, to maintain a long-term collaboration, or to uphold a shared plan when individual incentives diverge—where the “reasons” are not merely propositions but mutual expectations and interdependent obligations. Given this framing, I want a defended agenda rather than a survey. Among the open questions in the regress debate—whether genuinely basic beliefs exist and can be recognized without circularity; whether circular coherence can avoid arbitrariness without smuggling in foundations; whether an infinite chain of justification is rationally sustainable for finite minds; and whether practical commitments have a different logical structure that breaks the analogy altogether—which matter most for someone without institutional support who simply wants a stable, defensible framework for both judgment and action? Please rank them by importance, tractability for a non-specialist using reasoning alone, and how many other questions depend on their resolution. For each, explain the trade-offs: why advancing one might come at the cost of another. Specify also what evidence or argument over the next few years would cause you to reorder them—for instance, if it turned out that joint obligations generate a non-circular stopping point, or that human cognition naturally terminates reasoning at a specific depth. Finally, which questions or sub-debates should be deprioritized because settling them would change little downstream for practical reasoning?
