I've been retired from biology for about four years and lately I've gotten into philosphy of mind, mostly Dennett and some older Cartesian stuf. I'm writing up my own view for an online study group and I want the others to tear it apart, so here it is, honest and probably messy.

I'm definately a physicalist — no soul, no ghost in the machine. I reject the Cartesian theater idea; there's no inner movie screen watched by a little self inside your head. I mostly buy Dennett's multiple drafts model, where consciousness is a bunch of competing editorial processes rather than one clean stream. At the same time, I think there's a real unitary self that actually experiences things and does causal work — not just a story we tell, but a functional hub with real teeth. I take phenomenal consciousness to be nothing over and above globally available information in a workspace architecture. That makes me think a silicon system with the exact same workspace dynamics would be conscious. But I also have this gut feeling that only biological brains can genuinely feel anything; a silicon copy with identical architecture wouldn't actually have an inner life, even if it behaved exactly like me. Finally, I think zombie scenarios are conceivable but don't prove dualism, because conceivability is fallible and doesn't guarantee possibility.

I also want you to say what argument or finding would change your mind about where the worst tension really is, and be upfront about where your own reasoning is shakiest. Write the answer in American English, please — I'm not looking for a lecture, just a straight audit.
