I was arguing with my roommate about Sleeping Beauty last week — I'm taking this philosophy of mind course and we got onto self-locating belief, and now I can't stop thinkin about it. I built this argument that feels like it proves way too much but I can't see the crack.

(1) In the Sleeping Beauty problem, when she wakes up she should assign credence 1/3 to heads, not 1/2, because there are three centered worlds (Heads-Monday, Tails-Monday, Tails-Tuesday) that are subjectively indistinguishable for her, and she has no reason to privilege one.

(2) The reasoning here isn't special to memory drugs or coins; it's just applying indifference over epistemically indistinguishable centered possibilities that are all live given her evidence.

(3) So if I'm rational, whenever I find myself in a situation where I can't tell which of several subjectively identical "copies" or instances I am, I should spread my credence evenly across them.

(4) But now think about waking up tomorrow. Given what cosmologists say about large or infinite universes, plus simulation arguments, there are almost certainly vastly more subjectively identical copies of me having this exact thought right now than there is just "the original me."

(5) So by (3), my credence that I'm any particular one — including the one whose future welfare I normally care about — should be basically zero, distributed across the astronomical number of duplicates.

(6) But rational planning requires that I treat the future of the agent deciding now as having special weight. If I genuinely think there's only a 1-in-astronomical chance that "this" stream is the one I'm planning for, then saving for retirement or even avoiding a car crash seems irrational compared to maximizing aggregate welfare across all copies.

(7) That can't be right. Obviously I should still lock my bike and care about my own Tuesday.

(8) Therefore (2) is false, and Sleeping Beauty should actually be 1/2. The 1/3 answer relies on a principle that, if taken seriously, dissolves all first-person planning.

But my roommate says I'm smuggling in somthing between (3) and (4) — maybe that cosmological duplicates aren't "live possibilities" in the same evidential sense? I feel like there's an equivocation between "can't distinguish which centered world I'm in within one experiment" and "can't distinguish which copy I am in the multiverse." Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up do to the Sleeping Beauty case? I'd rather keep (2) if I can, but then I need to explain why (5) doesn't follow, and I don't know how to do that without just handwaving about "naturalness" or "causal connection."
