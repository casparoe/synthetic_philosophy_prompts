I'm a high school junior writing an opening piece for my philosophy club's semester-end session on moral conflict. I need an original poem — specifically a villanelle, nineteen lines with refrains A1 and A2 following the standard AB pattern and converging in the final couplet — that takes seriously the question of whether genuine moral dilemmas are possible. Before you write, please take on the role of both a professional moral philosopher and an experienced poet, and please begin with a brief brainstorm: how can a repeating poetic form mirror a conceptual structure in which two obligations keep returning?

Here is the philosophical content I want rendered with precision. One position holds that if two actions truly conflict, one must be less obligatory, so apparent dilemmas dissolve upon analysis. The opposing position holds that an agent can face two incompatible, fully binding moral requirements with no rational resolution. I want the poem to go further and incorporate the idea that even when one horn is justifiably chosen, the unchosen obligation does not disappear but leaves a moral remainder — a lingering claim or agent-regret that must be acknowledged rather than canceled. This should not resolve into either a sermon about decision-making or a shrug about tragedy; the tension should remain open at the end.

Please make the form do the philosophical work. Let refrain A1 articulate the first binding demand, and let refrain A2 articulate the second. The repeating structure should show that each obligation returns regardless of the other. In the final couplet, place A1 and A2 side by side without declaring a winner, so the form itself demonstrates why the two positions keep passing each other. The language should be specific enough that someone familiar with the debate recognizes the concept of an unresolved remainder, and strong enough that someone unfamiliar still feels the weight of both claims.

Please think carefully about accuracy: avoid reducing either obligation to mere emotion or preference, and do not invoke a hidden principle that secretly resolves the conflict. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or named authors in quotation marks. Write in American English and keep the tone formal and polished.
