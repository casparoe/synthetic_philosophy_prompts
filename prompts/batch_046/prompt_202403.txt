The debate over Benatar's asymmetry — that pain's presence is bad and its absence good even for no actual subject, while pleasure's presence is good but its absence is not bad unless there is an actual subject to be deprived — has cycled through the same four moves for years: defenders invoke the actual-versus-potential interest distinction, critics press the symmetry objection and person-affecting constraints, and neither camp shifts. I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not another defense or refutation. Is the disagreement substantive — about whether de re harm or deprivation can apply across modal contexts — or is it verbal, with 'bad for' operating in different registers, one comparative-axiological and one reason-giving? Are antinatalists and their critics answering different questions — one about all-things-considered procreative obligations, the other about axiological rankings of worlds — under one heading? Could a hidden formal issue be doing the work, such as whether welfare comparison requires a shared subject across possible worlds, or whether 'potential interest' is a coherent modal category? Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a distinction that separates the questions, an argument that the dispute rests on bedrock intuitions about value, or a formal result about cross-world identity conditions?
