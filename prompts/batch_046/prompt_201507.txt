I've been trying to work out my own position on a practical problem that keeps coming up in our neighborhood cooperative: we maintain a shared garden, and a few members are openly saying they want to dismantle the communal plots and fence them off for private use. Some neighbors argue we must tolerate these members because tolerance is our founding rule; others say that tolerating people who want to destroy the cooperative is self-defeating. I recognize this as a version of the paradox of tolerance, but I am not looking for a survey of philosophers or a history of the idea. Instead, please write a decision tree that starts with the concrete question, "Should the cooperative exclude members who reject shared ownership?" and then branches through the commitments that actually divide answers: whether tolerance applies to beliefs and speech only or also to actions that undermine the group; whether destructive intent can be recognized with enough confidence before harm is done; whether expelling someone for opposing the group's purpose counts as intolerance or as legitimate self-preservation; and whether the cooperative's survival is a necessary precondition for practicing tolerance at all. Each leaf should carry a plain-language name for the stance and one sentence describing its most awkward or costly commitment. Where two stances cannot be separated by the tree, flag them explicitly. Please begin with the tree itself—root node and branches shown clearly—followed by a brief explanation of why each branching question was chosen and what logical space it opens up. Do not open with a summary of my situation, a restatement of the question, or citations to authors and texts.
