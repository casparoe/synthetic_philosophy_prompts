next thursday i'm presenting at our ethics and policy colloquium and i need a prep sheet for the q&a. my thesis is a kind of inversion of the usual inefficacy story — i call it redundancy inversion. the standard line says your vote matters more than your burger because voting is collective decision-making while meat is just linear consumption. i'm arguing the opposite: in monopolistic supply chains like industrial meat, individual withdrawal is low-redundancy because procurement contracts are lumpy and discontinuous, so refusing can actually shift a supply threshhold. but in first-past-the-post voting with strategic blocs, your vote is genuinely redundant and the inefficacy objection sticks. so meat reduction carries more individual normative weight than voting, contrary to the usual EA framing.
