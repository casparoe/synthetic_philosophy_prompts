hobbes in his geometric writings went after euclid's definitions and tried to square the circle—he thought geometry had to be built from motion and clear causal reasoning, not abstact axioms. imagine he could read about two things that came long after his death: first, lobachevsky and riemann's non-euclidean geometries where parallel lines behave weird; second, the flyspeck formal verification of hales's proof for the kepler conjecture, where a computer checks every step of a 3d sphere packing argument. do you think he'd have accepted hyperbolic geometry as legitimate geometric knowledge, or rejected it because it violates the natural intution of space he assumed? and would he have trusted a computer-checked proof more than human survey, given his obsession with eliminating hidden errors in reasonning?
