I'm trying to figure out if there's a established term for a pattern I keep running into in climate ethics arguments, especially when people talk about what we owe people who haven't been born yet and whose very existence depends on what we do now.

Case one: A speaker says we have to cut emissions now because people in 2150 will suffer from storms and heat. The standard reply comes back—those particular people in 2150 wouldn't exist if we'd actually cut emissions, since different jobs, different cities, different marriages happen, so we can't say we harmed them specifically. I know that's near the non-identity problem, but that's not the pattern I'm asking about.

Case two: Someone proposes going much further—say, deliberately slowing economic growth so drastically that the social conditions producing the next few generations are totally different. Now critics say that's wrong because it would 'erase' millions of people who, in the high-growth world, would have lived good lives. So here, the fact that those people depend on our choices is used against changing course, not to excuse harm. It's like the logical mirror of case one.

Case three: Then there's the move where someone says, okay, forget specific people—just compare the two worlds. One world has more suffering, one has less, pick the one with less. But immediately someone else asks whether we're also comparing the number of people, because maybe the high-emission world has way more people, and some of them have lives that are barely worth living. So now the argument is about whether creating those barely-worth-living lives is worse than not creating them at all, which is a different question from 'did we harm anyone.'

What I'm noticing is that in real debates these three moves get mashed together. Someone starts with case one (we harm them), gets hit with the identity reply, switches to case three (world comparison), and then gets hit with case two (you're erasing people) or with the 'creating suffering lives' objection. They treat all of this as 'the problem of obligations to future generations,' but the three moves seem to have opposite structures—one says we can't blame you because they wouldn't exist, one says we must blame you for preventing them from existing, and one says existence doesn't matter, just compare worlds.

Is there a name for this specific cluster, or for the slide between these frames? If not, could you name the slide and say exactly what distinguishes it from just being confused about the non-identity problem? I'd also like you to say how confident you are in what you claim here, and specifically point out the part you're least sure about—probably whether there's actually literature on this framing-slide or if I'm just describing ordinary confusion in a new way. Also, does the same cluster show up in other long-term issues like nuclear waste, or is it specific to climate because the scale affects identity?
