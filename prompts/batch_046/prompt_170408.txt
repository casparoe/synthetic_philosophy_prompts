Question: Molinists say God has middle knowledge of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom—propositions about what free agents would do in circumstances that never actualy occur. The grounding objection asks where the truthmakers for these counterfactuals come from. Now consider a different worrie: call it the modal-fixation problem. If God uses middle knowledge to actualize world W, he knows not only that S would choose A in W, but that S bears the modal property of being-an-agent-who-chooses-A-in-circumstance-C across every world he surveys. Does selecting W thereby fix Ss modal profile everywhere, turning a contingently free chooser into something with a necessarily-characterized disposition? Is this just grounding in disguise, or a separate problem about modal properties? And can the hard/soft fact distinction about Gods past knowledge do any work here?

Student answer:
The grounding objection is that without actual events theres no ontological basis for counterfactuals. The modal-fixation problem sounds different but its really the same thing—if theres no ground for the counterfactual, theres no ground for the modal property either, so its just grounding applied to modal language. As for hard and soft facts, the distinction shows Gods prior beliefs are soft, so they dont causally determine Ss choice in W; that solves the freedom problem but not grounding, since grounding is metaphysical not causal. So modal-fixation collapses into grounding, and hard/soft facts dont help because the issue was never about causation in the first place.
