Construct a thought experiment designed to pressure the qualitative version of hedonism—the view that all pleasures share a single uniform phenomenal quality—by isolating heterogeneity in both intentional structure and temporal dynamics. Your scenario must be realistic, confined to a single paragraph, and free of science-fiction or neuroscientific stipulations. Engineer two experiences for the same subject: (i) an attitudinal, intellectually mediated pleasure taken in recognizing the consistency of a specific non-classical logical system (for instance, grasping why a particular intuitionistic proof avoids the law of excluded middle in a way that resolves a prior confusion), which unfolds over several minutes with variable intensity and depends on understanding a complex relational structure; and (ii) a non-attitudinal, purely sensory pleasure of tactile warmth spreading across the skin after stepping out of cold water, which is immediate, uniform, and lacks any representational content. The case must be arranged so that, upon careful introspection, the subject reports no common felt tone, pro-attitude, or qualitative similarity between the two states—only that each is desired-to-continue and judged intrinsically good—while it remains genuinely plausible that both are pleasures rather than, say, mere satisfactions or reliefs.

After presenting the scenario, provide a step-by-step analysis that demonstrates it meets each specification: (a) state the judgment each theory delivers—qualitative hedonism, quantitative hedonism (which treats pleasure as a scalar magnitude without insisting on uniform quality), and preference-satisfaction hedonism; (b) explain why qualitative hedonism is forced either to deny that one experience is pleasure or to postulate an ad hoc shared phenomenal feature that introspection fails to reveal; (c) show how quantitative hedonism accommodates both but only at the cost of being unable to distinguish intense pleasure from intense non-pleasure (e.g., severe pain or overwhelming anxiety) on qualitative grounds; and (d) note how preference-satisfaction hedonism avoids the problem entirely but changes the subject. Identify precisely which feature of the case does the work—specifically, the conjunction of divergent intentional structures with the absence of introspective qualitative overlap—and explain what happens if that feature is removed (e.g., if both experiences share the same representational object or the same temporal profile). Finally, present the most plausible defense available to the qualitative hedonist—likely an appeal to a second-order or thin phenomenal quality that is present but unnoticed—and explain why that defense either begs the question against the heterogeneity objection or renders the qualitative claim empirically unfalsifiable.
