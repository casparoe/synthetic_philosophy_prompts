As a software engineer without any formal philosophical training, I have been attempting to articulate where I stand on the conceptual status of infinity, partly because it keeps arising in discussions about algorithmic complexity, dynamic memory allocation, and the theoretical limits of computation. I find myself holding the following set of commitments, and I should like an audit of whether they hang together or whether I am inadvertently helping myself to incompatible premises.

First, I treat the notion of an array or data structure that can grow without a predetermined bound—through dynamic allocation or lazy evaluation—as a perfectly legitimate abstraction, which suggests that I am comfortable with actual infinity as a working concept in formal systems, not merely as a potential or asymptotic limit. Second, I accept Cantor's argument that there is no surjective mapping from the natural numbers onto the real numbers, and I take this to establish that there are genuinely distinct cardinalities of infinity, not just one undifferentiated notion of "the infinite." Third, I maintain that every physically instantiated computation runs on hardware with finite memory and energy, so that in any real-world execution we are always dealing with potential infinity: we have not yet reached the bound, but the bound is there in principle. Fourth, I am inclined towards a finitist stance in the philosophy of mathematics: I am sceptical that mathematical objects which cannot, even in principle, be constructed or verified within a finite—though unbounded—procedure should be granted full ontological status. Fifth, I find Hilbert's hotel intuitively coherent: the idea that an actually infinite hotel can accommodate a new guest by shifting every existing occupant to the next room strikes me as a consistent feature of actual infinity, not a reductio.

Given these five positions, I should be grateful if you could examine them for tension. Which pairs are genuinely in conflict, which only appear to be, and how severe is each tension? For each genuine conflict, please indicate what the least costly revision would be and what it would commit me to giving up. Finally, please tell me whether there is a single revision—say, abandoning finitism, or restricting Cantor's result to formal rather than ontological interpretation—that would clean up the majority of inconsistencies, or whether the package is more fundamentally fractured than that. I am particularly concerned that my acceptance of Cantor's hierarchy of infinities may sit uneasily with my finitist leanings, and that my reliance on unbounded dynamic allocation in programming may be smuggling in an ontology I officially reject.
