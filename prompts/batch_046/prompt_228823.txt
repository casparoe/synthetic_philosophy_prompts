I am serving as a grader for an upper-level comparative philosophy exam that asked students to analyze whether Ubuntu’s relational conception of personhood can consistently maintain individual moral responsibility, or whether it necessarily collapses into a form of collective determinism. The specific prompt required students to address whether recognizing that personhood is constituted through interdependence implies that wrongdoing is always a communal failure rather than an individual one, and whether Ubuntu can accommodate the intuition that an individual can be wronged in her own right, rather than merely as a node within a damaged social network. Below is one student’s complete essay response.

Ubuntu’s central insight—often summarized by the phrase that a person is a person through other people—is that human personhood is relationally constituted rather than ontologically prior to community. This does not reduce the individual to a mere cell in a social organism; rather, it holds that the capacities for reason, moral agency, and selfhood emerge through reciprocal recognition and shared practices. Against liberal individualism, which treats autonomy as a pre-social possession, Ubuntu argues that independence is itself a form of interdependence. Thus the self is not erased but enriched by its embeddedness in communal life. Nevertheless, this relational ontology has significant implications for moral responsibility. Because the self is constituted through its relations, an agent’s wrongful choices are never purely self-authored; they are shaped by the communal conditions that formed the agent’s character and available options. Consequently, attributing blame solely to the individual misses the relational failures that made the wrong possible. This does not eliminate accountability, but it transforms it: the appropriate response is not punitive isolation of the offender but collective restoration of the damaged relationships. The student also observed that Ubuntu cannot recognize harm to an individual independent of harm to the community, since to injure one person is necessarily to tear at the web of relations that constitutes everyone involved. Therefore, while Ubuntu preserves individuality as a relational achievement, it ultimately subordinates personal moral accountability to the health of the communal whole.

Please evaluate this answer with a letter or numerical grade and provide a point-by-point justification. Assess where the student correctly identifies Ubuntu’s departure from ontological individualism, where she mischaracterizes the relationship between relational constitution and personal authorship, and whether her conclusion that Ubuntu ultimately subordinates individual accountability to communal health actually follows from the premises she establishes. Do not indicate your own independent philosophical position; deliver only the grading assessment, identifying both the argument’s visible strengths and its specific, non-obvious errors—particularly any conflations between relational influence and causal determinism, or between mediated harm and the denial of private injury.
