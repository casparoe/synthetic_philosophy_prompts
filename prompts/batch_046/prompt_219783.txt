I want you to restate the core logic entirely in the vocabulary of treaty reinsurance and risk pools: primary carriers, retrocession, uncorrelated risks across jurisdictions, capital reserves, and the way a pool survives only if every member pays in even when no auditor can verify. Take the key pieces—why an agent chooses to cooperate, the temptation to defect when no one will know, the substitution of shared reasoning for a shared contract, and the "timeless" quality of the commitment—and translate each one. For each, say what maps cleanly onto insurance practice, what must be distorted to fit (for example, the total absence of legal enforcement or a central claims adjuster), and what has no counterpart at all in how pools actually work. Then give me the residue report: are the untranslated parts just limits of the reinsurance analogy, or do they reveal that acausal trade depends on something—like logical counterfactual causation—that has no analogue in real markets? Keep the translation honest; don't smuggle decision-theoretic assumptions into the insurance terms and call them translations.
