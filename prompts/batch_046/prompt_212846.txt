As chair of the university's Committee on Just Allocation and Voluntary Exchange, I must circulate a formal opinion before Thursday's vote on a proposed pilot program. The proposal would allow distinguished faculty to offer fee-based advanced seminars outside the standard curriculum, with students paying supplemental tuition directly to the instructor under an 80/20 revenue split. Our department's most prominent scholar—functionally the Wilt Chamberlain case in our institutional setting—seeks to launch such a seminar on modal metaphysics and entitlement theory, charging a substantial per-seat fee. Opponents argue that this creates an unjust distributive pattern, erodes equal access to advanced instruction, and violates the university's commitment to educational equity. Defenders invoke Nozick's entitlement theory: if baseline faculty compensation and student-aid allocations are just, and enrollment is entirely voluntary, then the resulting inequality is just regardless of pattern, provided the Lockean proviso is satisfied. The committee must recommend a concrete course of action—approval, rejection, or conditional approval with explicit structural safeguards—by Friday. Please draft the memo I will circulate. Take a definite position; do not merely survey the arguments. Your reasoning should engage seriously with the entitlement-theoretic defense, address whether the proviso is met in this educational context, and include an honest, substantive account of the strongest objection to your recommendation. Write in a polished, formal style suitable for an institutional board, and do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading; the analysis should be self-standing for a reader without web access.
