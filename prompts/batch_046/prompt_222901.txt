As a retired physicist recently turned to philosophy of science, I understand Jeffrey conditionalization technically—redistributing credence over a fixed, exhaustive partition when evidence only constrains weights rather than pinpointing outcomes. But I am stuck on a conceptual tension that standard expositions gloss over. The procedure requires that the categories dividing the possibility space—say, the taxonomy of observable phenomena—are settled before the update occurs. Yet in actual scientific practice those very categories are often theory-laden and are themselves revised in light of the evidence being incorporated. Does this mean Jeffrey conditioning implicitly assumes that our observational ontology is stable and independent of the theories being tested? Or can one coherently treat the partition itself as open to revision without collapsing the update into circular reasoning? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then lay out the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating my question.
