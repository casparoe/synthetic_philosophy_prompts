Write a short story set in two causally disconnected regions of a multiverse. In each region, a governing decision procedure—functionally identical but physically isolated—must choose whether to divert scarce energy from local survival into a computation that benefits both regions only if the other makes the same choice. With no possible signal between them, cooperation can only be evidential: each procedure treats its own reasoning as statistical evidence about what its twin will do. Center the narrative on the crisis that arises when one procedure misclassifies its own reference class of observers, temporarily including non-cooperating variants in its self-locating belief and thereby breaking the evidential correlation. Show how correcting that reference-class error—without any external message or discovery—restores the rational basis for mutual aid, and let the outcome turn entirely on the internal logic of the decision framework rather than on causal interaction.
