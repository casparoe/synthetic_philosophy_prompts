I'm working on material constitution and keep running into a variant of the statue problem that involves absences rather than shapes. Take a brass key with a hole for the keyring. The hole is clearly part of what makes it a key -- if you fill it with brass, it's not a key anymore, it's just a stick. But the hole isn't made of brass; it's made of nothing. So if the key just is the brass aranged key-wise, then part of the key (the hole) isn't part of the brass. Meanwhile if you melt the brass, the key is destroyed but the brass survives. So now we have an object that contains a part that isn't material, and also has persistance conditions different from its matter. People treat this as obvious in ordinary talk ("this key has a hole in it"), but when I try to formalize it I end up saying there's one thing with both material and immaterial parts, plus a seperate matter-track. Is there an established term for this specific knot -- where an object's form includes negative space that isn't part of its material, and yet we deny there are two things? Or does it just collapse back into the standard object-and-matter puzzle? If there's a sharper diagnosis, I'd like to hear it, especially one that distinguishes the negative-part issue from the usual coincidence problem.
