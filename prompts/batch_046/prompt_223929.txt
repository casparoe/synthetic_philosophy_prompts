For the purpose of auditing consistency, here are my commitments on pleasure, value, and phenomenology:

(i) Hedonism is true: pleasure alone is intrinsically good, with value scaling by intensity and duration.

(ii) The heterogeneity problem is fatal to qualitative-identity accounts: bodily pleasures (warmth, satiety) and intellectual pleasures (grasping a proof) share no common felt quality that could ground a uniform value.

(iii) Pleasures are unified instead by a functional-affective signature I call the tonic-acceptance profile: a phenomenally conscious state that intrinsically motivates its own continuation and is experienced without resistance or aversion.

(iv) Some states meet the tonic-acceptance profile yet are not pleasures but painful compulsions—e.g., the involuntary reliving of a traumatic memory that is consciously resisted yet functionally self-reinforcing.

(v) Phenomenal consciousness is necessary for intrinsic value: an optimized reward loop that exhibits tonic acceptance without subjective experience generates no value whatsoever.

(vi) Aesthetic pleasure is genuine, but heterogeneous in the same radical way: the pleasure of a dissonant chord and that of a geometric insight share neither phenomenal quality nor functional profile, except that both are valued for themselves.

2. Dissolving tension (about 100 words): point out the pair that looks inconsistent but actually coheres on closer inspection, and explain why.
