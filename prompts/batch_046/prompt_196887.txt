I'm preparing a fellowship proposal for a three-month conceptual project at the intersection of philosophy of social science and intellectual history, and I need help sharpening the framing. The central question is whether Max Weber's account of the Protestant ethic's relationship to modern capitalism can be defended as a genuinely causal claim—specifically, whether values can operate as causes in a manner that is neither reducible to material interests nor merely epiphenomenal—without collapsing into a problematic form of cultural idealism. Existing interpretations tend to split between hermeneutic readings, which treat Weber as describing only the formation of meaningful orientations, and correlationalist readings, which treat his notion of elective affinity as a historical coincidence lacking explanatory force; what remains unsettled is whether a coherent middle position exists that assigns values an autonomous causal role through directional compatibility with institutional structures rather than through efficient causation.

I want the proposal to argue that philosophical clarification—rather than further archival history—can advance the debate by examining the ontology of social action required for such a middle position. In particular, I need to determine whether Weber's distinction between value-rational and instrumental-rational action can support a non-reductive but non-idealist causal story, and whether this can be articulated by comparing Weber's framework with contemporary mechanism-based explanations in organizational sociology. The deliverable should be a conceptual framework specifying exactly what conditions a value-system must meet to be causally efficacious in Weber's sense, together with an account of what institutional-development patterns would confirm or disconfirm it. Please structure the proposal to include: the precise research question; an explanation of why the hermeneutic-correlationalist dichotomy fails to resolve it; a description of the philosophical approach, emphasizing ontology and causal explanation over empirical evidence; a clear statement of what a successful result looks like and what would count as failure; and a realistic three-month scope that avoids archival or quantitative empirical work. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or requests for specific secondary sources—I will integrate references to Weber's writings and relevant literature myself.
