so i've been thinking about this weird pattern in how american law treats buying and selling. like, you can legally sell your blood plasma twice a week for cash, sell your hair, or sign up for a risky clinical trial and get paid to take drugs that might damage you. but if you try to sell a kidney, that's a federal crime with prison time. at the same time, after a hurricane it's illegal to charge fifty bucks for bottled water, but drug companies can charge a hundred grand for cancer meds that cost pennies to produce, and nobody calls that price gouging.

it feels like there's some hidden rule about what can have a price tag and what can't, but the logic seems inconsistent alot. does this whole setup presuppose that certain body parts are sacred or tied to human dignity in a way plasma isn't? or is it really about protecting poor people from being exploited? and if exploitation is the issue, why is selling hair okay but selling a kidney isn't? also, does the price gouging law presuppose that need creates a moral claim that overrides the market, or is it just politics?

what i want to know is: can you reconstruct the implicit theory of value or commodification that makes both the kidney ban and the emergency price rules make sense together? is there actually one coherent view behind both, or are they quietly relying on different and maybe incompatible ideas? and if we replaced whatever hidden commitment is in there with either a pure market view -- if both sides agree and it's informed, it's fine -- or with a strict view that you can never sell body parts or charge extra in disasters, what parts of the current rules would have to change?
