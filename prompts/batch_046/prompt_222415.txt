I'm moderating a student-paper Q&A next week with a philosopher who argues shame is the adaptible moral emotion and that a culture can genuinely choose it over guilt — but only if it restructures its moral attention economy away from private rule-tracking and toward public status-calibration. She also distinguishes indexical shame from propositional guilt. I need about 6-7 interview questions that go past restating the view. At least one should press whether guilt is biologically hardwired in ways that make cultural displacement impossible, with a follow-up keyed to her likely answer. Another should tackle the mental-health objection — that shame drives depression and social withdrawal — and include a follow-up for her standard reply. I also want one that probes whether the moral attention economy framing is descriptive or normative, especially in large anonymous societies where status signals are weak. Finally, ask whether the indexical/propositional split is a real conceptual boundary or just slippage that lets her smuggle in normative conclusions. Keep it casual, no biography questions, and dont ask for citations.
