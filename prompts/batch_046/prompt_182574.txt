Consider Thomas Reid’s account of testimony and the social transmission of knowledge through common-sense principles. If Reid were confronted with the modern practice of relying on large language models to generate explanations that users routinely accept without being able to reconstruct, verify, or fully parse them, how do you think he would assess the epistemic standing of such beliefs? Would he treat machine-produced outputs as continuous with human testimony, or would he argue that the absence of a knowing, accountable speaker undermines the very chain of justification that makes second-hand knowledge legitimate on his view?
