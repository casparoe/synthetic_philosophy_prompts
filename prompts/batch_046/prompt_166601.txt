I am working with a particular construal of the tri-level message architecture associated with Gödel, Escher, Bach: semantic content is not housed in any single stratum but is constituted by a recursive dynamic in which the inner message operates not as mere implicit commentary but as an executable instruction capable of rewriting the frame conditions that govern interpretation of the outer message, while the frame message supplies syntactic scaffolding without semantic autonomy. On this view, a system that processes frame and outer messages without this self-modifying loop—say, a fluent language model that lacks any mechanism for altering its own interpretive scaffolding—fails to possess meaning not by degree but categorically, irrespective of behavioral competence. I want an honest map of the position's exposure. Which empirical or conceptual findings would decisively undermine the claim that such frame-rewriting via inner message is necessary for meaning? Could semantic content survive the elimination or freezing of the loop, whether through external social framing, distributed interpretation, or pure syntactic coherence? Among these, which defeaters would defenders of the loop thesis themselves concede as damaging, and which would they reject as misdescribing the loop's operation? Are there formal results—perhaps in computability theory or the philosophy of language—showing that meaning can be fully grounded at a single level without recursive restructuring? Rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and assess whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if the loop is always posited as hidden or emergent whenever absent, is that insulation a theoretical virtue or a vice? In answering, separate what is relatively settled about multi-level message structures in cognitive science from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers of mind and language, and do not present either the loop thesis or its rivals as having achieved consensus.
