Write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors about what Descartes's cogito actually establishes. One should defend the reading that it proves the existence of a thinking substance that persists through time; the other should defend the reading that it proves only the present occurrence of a thought, with no entitlement to a continuous owner of that thought. Structure their exchange so that each responds directly to the other's last point rather than talking past one another. As a software engineer without formal philosophy background, I am particularly interested in whether the cogito can distinguish between a transient computation (a process running) and the system that executes it—so have the characters examine whether Descartes's first-person certainty licenses an inference from "there is thinking" to "there is a thinker," or whether that move smuggles in an unproven ontology. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific secondary literature.
