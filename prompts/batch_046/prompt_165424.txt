I’m the senior ethics chaplain on the review board for a 12-hospital catholic network, and every week staff send us little case files asking whether a specific communication counts as lying under the Augustinian framework our code cites. I need a reusable decision proceedure — ordered questions, stop points, hand-offs — that my board can actually run in 15 minutes without anyone having a PhD in patristics. Ground it in Augustine’s real distinctions: false signification versus just keeping quiet, the intent to deceive, the harm done to the speaker’s own soul, and whether the falsehood is in words or in some mediated form. For each step say what counts as a sufficient answer, where the proceedure stops with a clear yes/no, and where it has to punt to human judgment because the issue is values, not facts. Then apply it to three cases: (1) a nurse tells a panicking family the doctor is coming right away knowing he won’t be there for hours; (2) a staffer files an accurate whistleblower report using a pseudonym that hides their identity; (3) a palliative team approves an AI chatbot message that tells a dying patient you will recover because the message is comforting and staff reviewed it. Make sure at least one case comes out with an answer the board probably won’t like. Also, please seperate what is settled among people who actually study Augustine from what’s genuinely contested — don’t present a disputed reading as the consensus. No bibliographies needed; just the procedure, the cases, and the settlement/contest map.
