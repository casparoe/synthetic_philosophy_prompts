I was arguing with a friend about Hofstadter's MU-puzzle and whether JOOTSing — jumping out of the system — is something a rational agent could ever actually do, not just talk about. My take is pretty strong: I think the MU-puzzle reveals a structural ceiling for any formalized reasoning system. Once you're inside a framework — whether it's formal logic, Bayesian updating, or even a trained neural architecture — any attempt to evaluate that framework's consistency or limits has to use the framework's own tools. That means the 'jump' is either circular or depends on a stronger meta-system you can't fully acccess from within. So when people claim they've stepped outside their prior assumptions, they've really just moved to a bigger box they haven't acknowledged. Even altering your logic rules, like switching to paraconsistant reasoning, feels more like redecorating the inside than leaving the building.
