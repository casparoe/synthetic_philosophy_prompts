The curriculum aims to institutionalize exceptional moral responsiveness to patient suffering as a training objective rather than leaving it to individual aspiration. Do we thereby inherit the objections about psychological unsustainability and one-dimensional character? Set out the most significant implications for how such a program should be structured, but be explicit about where the analogy between individual moral psychology and institutional pedagogy collapses. If my framing presupposes—without argument—that the conception of the good life underlying this critique applies directly to professional identity formation, or if translating the view into curricular terms requires a distortion you cannot justify, correct me plainly rather than forcing the implications.
