I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun to study philosophy in earnest, and I am presently working through F.H. Bradley's Appearance and Reality (1893). I should be grateful for assistance in understanding a particular passage and its relation to modern discussions.

Bradley argues, in the latter part of the work, that truth is not a simple correspondence between an isolated idea and an independent reality, because reality itself is an indivisible, coherent whole. Any particular judgement, he suggests, captures only a fragment or aspect of this whole. Consequently, truth is not an all-or-nothing property but admits of degrees: an idea is true to the extent that it can be integrated into the wider system of experience without contradiction, and false to the extent that it conflicts with other established aspects and requires revision. The ultimate standard, however, is not mere internal consistency but coherence with the nature of absolute reality itself, which Bradley identifies with a fully integrated system of experience.

What I find puzzling is how this account relates to contemporary deflationary theories of truth. The deflationist, as I understand the position, denies that truth is a substantive metaphysical property and treats the truth-predicate as a logical device for disquotation and generalisation. Bradley, by contrast, treats truth as a graded, structural feature of ideas in relation to an absolute reality. Yet there is also a striking resemblance: Bradley rejects the view that truth consists in a simple copying relation between idea and fact, which sounds rather like the deflationist's rejection of correspondence.

My specific question is whether Bradley's coherence account is genuinely an inflationary rival to deflationism, or whether it can be read as an early, metaphysically laden anticipation of the deflationist move—namely, that truth-talk does not describe a substantial relation but rather expresses the integration of a claim into a system. I am particularly struck by an analogy drawn from my former field. In physics, we distinguish fundamental laws, which are universal, from effective or phenomenological laws, which hold approximately within a limited domain and are integrated into broader theories only as limiting cases. Could Bradley's graded notion of truth be interpreted as analogous to these effective theories—locally coherent, useful within their scope, but not corresponding to the final structure of reality? If so, does this make Bradley closer to a pragmatic or even deflationary position than is usually acknowledged, despite his commitment to absolute reality?

I should particularly value an analysis of whether the deflationist must reject Bradley's graded notion altogether, or whether a deflationary framework can accommodate degrees of truth by treating them as measures of epistemic integration rather than as metaphysical grades of correspondence. I am not seeking a bibliography or reading list, but rather a conceptual clarification that might help me navigate the passage without imposing modern categories anachronistically.
