I've been thinking about Dennett's four kinds of minds thing - Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian - and I've come to think the hierarchy is stricter than people usually say. Not just that each one builds on the last, but that Gregorian cognition involves what I'd call second-order selection: the tools (language, math, whatever) don't just store Popperian hypotheses, they actually reshape the fitness landscape for which hypotheses get to die in your stead. So a Gregorian creature isn't a Popperian creature with better RAM; it's running a different control architectue altogether. That'd mean there's a real discontinuity between human minds and everything else, not just a smooth gradient.

What would actually count against that? I'm not asking for the usual 'animals are smarter than we think' examples - I want the stuff that'd hit the architecture claim directly. Could you show me cases where the selection-environment modification collapses back into first-order? Or arguments that language-tools are just external scaffolding, not part of the cognitive architecture? And be honest about whether this view is just insulated by definitional tricks - like, have I defined Gregorian minds so that nothing could ever be one except humans? I'd also like to know which of the four kinds you'd think are actually the shaky ones in this stack, if any.
