I am a graduate student in philosophy of history and heritage studies. For a course project, I must design a reusable decision procedure—not a one-time judgment—for officers at a community heritage board who repeatedly face conflicts between local collective memory and documentary historical records when drafting public monument texts. The board operates under a one-hour review constraint and has no philosophy training. Ground the procedure in the conceptual distinction between collective memory as a living social practice that sustains group identity and history as a critical, evidence-based corrective, but translate it into an operational checklist: ordered criteria that distinguish factual, causal, and moral-evaluative claims; explicit tests for material counter-evidence and for harm to the community’s capacity for self-interpretation; decision nodes that yield a recommendation (privilege memory, privilege archive, or split representation); and clear “judgment unavoidable” flags where the officer must vote. Provide three worked applications—a toponymic dispute, a disagreement over whether a 1962 labor action involved violence, and a commemorative framing of a historical figure as protector versus oppressor, with the last being the case where the procedure directs the officer to side with the documentary record despite anticipated community opposition. State precisely what the procedure cannot settle, especially conflicts that are purely symbolic rather than evidential, and flag the points where an editor’s or board member’s discretionary call is irreducible.
