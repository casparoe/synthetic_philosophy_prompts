I am preparing a consistency audit for my high school philosophy seminar on weakness of will and intertemporal choice. Here are five commitments I presently hold. First, I take akrasia to be genuinely possible: an agent can fully recognize that one course is better and nonetheless choose the worse, and this is not reducible to mere misjudgment, hidden information, or ignorance of the good. Second, I accept hyperbolic discounting as the correct descriptive model—future rewards are devalued according to a hyperbolic rather than an exponential curve—which explains the preference reversals characteristic of weakness of will. Third, I maintain that because hyperbolic curves are mathematically continuous, there is no metaphysically divided self in these moments; there is only a single preference function evaluated at different temporal distances. Fourth, I nevertheless think that agents engage in intertemporal bargaining through precommitment devices, and that this practice only makes strategic sense if one treats successive temporal stages of oneself as counterparties with genuinely divergent interests rather than as one continuous will. Fifth, I reject the ego-depletion model of willpower and hold instead that failures of self-control are best explained as failures of attentional framing rather than as the exhaustion of a depletable mental resource.

Please audit these commitments for joint tenability. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which only appear incompatible upon first inspection? Specifically, does commitment three undermine commitment four, and does the conjunction of two and five collapse commitment one into a mere framing effect? Rank the tensions by severity, identify the apparent tension that dissolves once the dependency structure is made explicit, and specify the single least costly revision that would restore coherence—whether that requires abandoning the divided-self language implicit in four, qualifying the explanatory reach of hyperbolic discounting in two, or reconceiving akrasia in one as a failure of implementation rather than of will itself.
