Write a short story that treats the dark forest hypothesis as a conceptual framework rather than as a backdrop for interstellar warfare. The protagonist should be a solitary, long-lived observer—perhaps a self-modifying probe or a distributed cognitive system—whose architecture lacks discrete beliefs and desires in any folk-psychological sense; instead, it updates global predictive weights across a unified model of its environment. When it detects a single ambiguous signal from a distant system, it must decide whether to maintain absolute radio silence, emit a calibrated reply, or actively suppress its own detectable emissions. Render its deliberation intelligible without attributing to it fear, curiosity, or strategic cunning. Embed the philosophical question of whether the core logic of the dark forest—resource scarcity, technological asymmetry, and the impossibility of verifying benign intent—retains any force for an agent that does not possess propositional attitudes about other minds. Keep the narrative minimal and the conceptual structure explicit; the interest should lie in what strategic silence looks like when stripped of the mentalistic vocabulary usually used to motivate it.
