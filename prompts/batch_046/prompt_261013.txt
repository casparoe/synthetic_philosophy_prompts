I'm preparing a research proposal for my philosophy-of-mind seminar (about 5,000 words, one semester). The Chase-Sanborn charge that qualia are incoherent derives its force, I suspect, from an unstated analogy between introspection and perceptual observation: qualia are treated as objects that must be either fully private and incommunicable or fully public and non-qualitative. My proposal asks whether this observation-model is obligatory, or whether modeling introspective awareness as graded, activity-based self-monitoring—without a sharp subject-object divide—lets the realist retain a consistent, non-mysterious notion of qualia. Standard responses either deny one of the assigned properties or concede to eliminativism; neither examines whether the contradiction is an artifact of the introspection model itself. The project would reconstruct the formal properties Chase and Sanborn treat as jointly incompatible, relax the observation-analogy, and test whether the remaining characterization can still do the explanatory work qualia are meant to do without collapsing into representationalism. A successful result would be a defensible, deflationary account of qualia that survives the incoherence charge; failure would be showing that any relaxation either retains the contradiction or eliminates qualitative character entirely. Please frame this with a precise statement of the question, an explanation of why the existing defenses leave it open, the specific method (conceptual reconstruction and comparison with non-observational models of self-awareness), what the deliverable should look like, an honest assessment of tractability within one semester without empirical work, and clear conditions for failure. No bibliography or citations needed—I will supply references.
