I sit on my university museum's Acquisition and Integrity Committee, and we must circulate a formal decision memo before Thursday's meeting. The case concerns "Riverbed," a ceramic installation donated in 1998 and catalogued as a permanent-collection work. The artist designed it to be rebuilt periodically with local river clay, and after her death in 2015 the museum has reconstructed it three times using chemically identical but source-different unfired clay. A recent conservation grant required embedding a distributed network of humidity sensors into the matrix; in several rebuilt sections these sensors are now structurally load-bearing, and parts of the clay have been fired to stabilize them, producing a hybrid body of fired and unfired material with integrated monitoring function. The donor's heirs argue that the object is now materially and functionally a different kind—part artwork, part apparatus—and demand either deaccession or formal reclassification as a posthumous reconstruction, which would invalidate aspects of the original tax-deductible gift agreement. The director insists the work remains the original bequest, citing the artist's own practice of rebuilding. Our committee needs a written opinion that recommends one concrete course—retain with amended provenance, reclassify, or deaccession with conditions—includes a clear implementation time frame, and engages the heirs' strongest argument seriously rather than dismissing it. Please write the memo in American English. The reasoning should identify the crux of the material-constitution problem here: whether an object can survive not only the replacement of all its parts but also the acquisition of new essential functional properties that alter its material kind, applied specifically to the fired/unfired, sensor-embedded clay body rather than through generic textbook examples. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not end on a non-committal note—deliver a bottom line with reasons the losing side can recognize as having been steel-manned.
