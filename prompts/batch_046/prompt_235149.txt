I'm curating a small neighborhood gallery opening and must choose a centerpiece by Friday. One submission is an enormous, technically flawless oil painting of a solitary luxury banquet—every reflection of silverware rendered with photographic precision. The artist admits he chose the subject purely for its difficulty and feels no particular emotion about isolation or wealth. The other is a rough, almost childlike collage made by a recent immigrant depicting strangers sharing food in a crowded kitchen across language barriers; it breaks every conventional rule of perspective and color harmony, yet visitors consistently report feeling unexpectedly connected to people they do not know. A donor will fund the show only if the masterful painting is featured, arguing that without objective craft standards, "art" collapses into whatever makes people feel good. A community organizer insists the collage is the only piece that matters because it actually changes how viewers treat one another afterward. I have to decide which work defines the exhibition and whether rejecting the other is a mistake. Is there a principled way to choose that isn't just personal taste or political pressure?
