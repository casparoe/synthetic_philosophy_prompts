Since I began curating an exhibit on how societies classify their origin stories, I have been caught in a consistency problem that seems to swallow far more than I intend. Here is the argument:

(1) A legend becomes history only when material or documentary evidence independent of the narrative tradition itself—evidence that predates the tradition and was not produced to support it—confirms the core events.
(2) The Trojan War meets this standard: Hittite diplomatic references and stratigraphic layers at Hisarlik exist independently of Homeric epic.
(3) The Exodus does not: archaeological surveys across the Sinai and Late Bronze Age Canaan reveal no population movement on the scale described, and the earliest extensive account is the text that celebrates it.
(4) King Arthur falls in between—late Roman withdrawal records exist, but nothing ties them to a specific martial leader of that name—so the Arthurian core remains text-dependent.
(5) By (1), Troy is history, Arthur is legend, and the Exodus is legend.
(6) Apply (1) rigorously, however, and most ancient events we treat as historical—specific battles, the reigns of minor dynasts, provincial administrative acts—rest on single textual traditions with little independent material corroboration.
(7) Therefore (1) does not merely sort Arthur and Exodus away from history; it threatens to reclassify the bulk of pre-modern political history as legend.
(8) I am reluctant to abandon (1), because without it the boundary between history and legend collapses into mere cultural preference.
(9) I could reject (6) by arguing that bureaucratic texts count as independent in a way epic texts do not, but that feels like special pleading.
(10) Or I could reject (2) and treat even Troy as legend, but that costs me the intuition that archaeological convergence genuinely distinguishes some narratives.

Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If I must keep (1) and (8), am I forced into the revisionism of (6)-(7), or is there a hidden equivocation—perhaps between corroborating a core event and corroborating every detail—somewhere between (1) and (6)?
