You are acting as an expert political philosopher and professional seminar adviser with particular expertise in global justice, liberal nationalism, and the ethics of membership. I am a final-year undergraduate student on a philosophy and politics course, and I need rigorous, structured preparation for a high-stakes seminar defence next week. My thesis: I defend a moderate position I call institutional co-authorship nationalism. The core claim is that compatriots bear special associative obligations—specifically, directed duties of democratic accountability and non-abandonment—that arise from shared authorship of coercive state institutions. Crucially, I deny that these obligations justify giving compatriots distributive priority in global resource allocation or welfare aggregation. I distinguish sharply between accountability obligations (non-transferable, directed duties to fellow citizens) and distributive weights (the moral importance assigned to interests in impartial aggregation). I argue that liberal nationalists conflate these two categories, and that cosmopolitan critics wrongly assume that rejecting distributive priority entails rejecting all special obligations. I also introduce a secondary framing—differentiated associative obligations—to capture the idea that the strength of special duties varies with the density of institutional interaction, without thereby licensing global inequality.

The audience: My seminar comprises twelve advanced students—approximately half with strong cosmopolitan commitments (several have engaged closely with arguments for global distributive equality and open borders) and half sympathetic to associative-duty accounts of nationalism or liberal patriotism. There is also a visiting lecturer in international relations who habitually translates normative philosophical claims into empirical claims about state interest, stability, and institutional feasibility. The group values conceptual precision above rhetorical flair, is deeply sceptical of middle-ground positions that appear to split differences without principled structural grounds, and is trained to detect hidden premises about legitimacy and coercion. They will not be satisfied with intuitive appeals; they want clarity on exactly where the boundary lies and why it does not collapse.

Please prepare me for the Q&A by producing a structured prep sheet with the following features, written in British English:

4. A brief opening concession I should deliver at the start of the session to disarm scepticism and demonstrate intellectual honesty, without undermining my core thesis.
