I have been finalizing the take-home exam for my upper-level seminar on metaphysics and philosophical logic, which spent three weeks on the problem of propositional unity and Bradley's classic regress argument. After discussing assessment design with a colleague, I want an essay question that forces students past rote summaries of the standard dialectic—Bradley's worry that relating relations generate an infinite regress, Russell's reply with external relations, and recent attempts to ground predication in tropes or mental files. To that end, please build the question around a specific, somewhat constructed scenario: call it "second-order inherence monism," the view that the unity of any proposition is secured not by a relation between its constituent properties, but by a single second-order property that inheres simultaneously in every first-order property that figures in the proposition, so that the parts are unified by shared inherence rather than by any relating factor. The question should ask students to determine whether this view genuinely escapes Bradley's regress, whether the instantiation of that second-order property itself reintroduces a hidden relating relation, and whether the view can preserve the distinction between predicative judgments and genuinely relational judgments without smuggling relations back in. Please provide the exam question itself, a model answer of approximately 900 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A answer—which identifies the hidden regress in the inherence relation, compares the proposal with Bradley's own absolute, and shows why the predicative/relational distinction collapses—from a B answer that accurately describes Bradley and Russell but treats second-order inherence monism as an unproblematic solution, and from a C answer that confuses the regress with Russell's theory of relations. The exam is worth 30 marks, students have 48 hours, and the word limit is 1,200 words. Do not include any request for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the prompt and answer should be answerable entirely from conceptual reasoning and the course material. Please keep the wording formal and precise.
