You advise an intergenerational climate-trust board that votes on proposed “binding irreversibility clauses” — constitutional-level commitments that make it prohibitively costly for future trustees to deviate from carbon budgets or preservation mandates, even when deviation would be locally rational. You need a reusable decision procedure grounded in the logic of rational irrationality (the hitchhiker structure: it can be rational now to adopt a policy that will make future deviation irrational, in order to secure present cooperation or credibility). The board has legal and economic expertise but no philosophy background; every clause needs a yes / no / hand-to-values recommendation with documented reasoning, and the board, not you, signs off on value judgments.

Write the procedure: list the questions in the exact order they must be answered, what counts as sufficient evidence for each, where a “no” stops the process with “reject binding,” where it stops with “hand to board values,” and where it returns “approve binding.” The procedure must explicitly test: (i) whether future deviation is genuinely rational in the local circumstances, not merely convenient; (ii) whether the present binding is credible to outside parties; (iii) whether radical unforeseen change could make being bound catastrophic; and (iv) whether a less restrictive mechanism achieves the same credibility. Be explicit about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure.

Apply it to three proposals:
1. A fifty-year lock-box reinvesting 90% of returns in climate adaptation, with penalties for diversion exceeding any local gain.
2. A binding arbitration clause preventing future trustees from releasing funds for emergency fossil-fuel transition during an energy crisis.
3. An automatic data-destruction protocol: if temperature thresholds are crossed, proprietary geoengineering research is deleted to prevent misuse, even if using it might save lives.

In one of these, the procedure should yield an answer the board will find unwelcome. End by stating precisely what the procedure cannot determine — especially about predicting future states of rationality and measuring credibility ex ante — and mark those limits as structural, not remediable with more data.
