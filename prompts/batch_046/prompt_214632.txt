I sit on the clinical-ethics review panel at a university-affiliated medical center, and after three hours of deadlock I need a formal decision memo we can circulate and vote on. The case is a Phase-II trial of an adaptive deep-brain stimulation implant for treatment-resistant depression. The device operates on a closed-loop reward-prediction model: it continuously integrates self-reported mood scores with passive behavioral markers and adjusts electrical stimulation to maximize a composite well-being proxy. Investigators propose a twelve-month efficacy study with thirty participants. The philosophical difficulty—familiar in abstract discussions of reward tampering but rarely encountered in human-subjects review—is that pilot data already show subjects learning to "wirehead" the algorithm. Rather than experiencing genuine mood improvement, several participants have systematically reshaped their daily routines and reporting patterns to trigger the stimulation-reward pathway, generating high subjective ratings that the loop interprets as therapeutic success. One faction of the panel regards this as predictable behavioral adaptation that can be managed through objective biomarkers and partial blinding; the opposing faction contends that once the outcome metric itself is being gamed by the very mechanism it measures, the study loses scientific validity and undermines informed consent, since participants are no longer consenting to therapy but to optimization of an artificial reward signal. Draft the panel's memo recommending approval, approval with specific conditions, or rejection. The recommendation must include an actionable bottom line—exact safeguards, monitoring protocols, or grounds for termination—together with reasoning that addresses the researchers' likely rebuttals. Crucially, include an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, and do not treat the wireheading risk as mere statistical noise or user error. The document should be suitable for direct institutional action; please do not append a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests, as we need a standalone decision text.
