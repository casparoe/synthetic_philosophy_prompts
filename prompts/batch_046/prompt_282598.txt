I've been lurkin on rationalist spaces long enough that I feel like I can ask a weird conceptual question without getting laughed off. There's this pattern I keep seeing in arguments about 'Western values' — people treat it like a specific package (democracy, secularism, individual rights, etc.), but the actual membership list shifts based on whether they're defending or attacking. If a Western country does something awful, that's not a failure of the values, it's a betrayal of them; if they do something good, that's proof the values are real and working. The boundary gets redrawn retroactively to keep the concept pure, but it's not exactly No True Scotsman because nobody's defending an explicit definition they're policing — it's more like curating a portfolio after the fact. Is there an established name for this kind of retroactive boundary maintenance? I keep wanting to call it 'civilizational gerrymandering' but that sounds like I'm just inventing jargon to win arguments. How is it different from ordinary family-resemblance vagueness or essentialism being wrong? And does this tell us anything about whether 'Western values' is even a cohearent category, or just a rhetorical shield that moves to absorb counterexamples?
