I’m giving a workshop paper next thursday defending a weird hybrid on the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem. Most people run through Williams and Smith and call it a day, but I’m arguing the deadlock only looks fatal because we’re using one flat notion of "reason." If you split explanatory reasons from framework-adoption reasons using something like the structural-realist ontic/epistemic split — but applied to moral belief, not science — then pragmatic motivations for holding a moral view turn out to be wrong-kind for explaining the belief’s content but perfectly legitimate for justifying adoption. So internalists and externalists are both tracking different parts of the same structure, and the motivation problem dissolves without either side winning.

Audience: mostly metaethicists who think internalism is roughly right but are nervous about motivation; two philosophers of science who know structural realism cold and will think I’m committing a category error; one epistemologist who works on pragmatic encroachment and will accuse me of smuggling evidential norms into normative ethics. For each: what I should concede upfront, the reply I need ready, and the weak point in that reply. Include the question I’m praying nobody asks — probably whether this just relocates Benacerraf-style worries into moral psychology. And please seperate what’s actually settled in the literature from what’s genuinely contested; don’t present my hybrid as consensus.
