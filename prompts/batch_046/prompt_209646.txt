I am a high school student exploring a debate that standard textbooks rarely treat with precision. Please draft the joint statement that two opposing camps should have produced after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Camp A — Tranquilists — maintain that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value; any apparently positive state derives its worth from that absence or is misdescribed.

Camp B — Plural Affirmationists — maintain that certain positive conditions, such as aesthetic insight, intellectual understanding, and relational attunement, possess intrinsic value that does not reduce to the absence of craving or suffering.

To avoid repeating well-worn arguments, both camps have agreed to analyze their dispute through a specific conceptual framework they call the Partition Thesis: the claim that any mental state can be analytically divided into a craving-vector (the motivational pull toward continuation or repetition) and a content-structure (the representational or phenomenal character that remains when motivation is bracketed). They disagree about whether content-structures can ever be axiologically active without being mediated by a craving-vector.

Please produce their joint statement, formatted exactly as follows:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims (at least six), written in neutral language both sides can sign. Include agreement about the formal structure of the Partition Thesis, about what empirical or phenomenological evidence would falsify it, and about the reliability of first-person reports of craving-absence.

2. A disagreements table with three columns: Issue; Tranquilist Formulation (worded so Tranquilists accept it as expressing their view); and Plural Affirmationist Formulation (worded so they accept it). Address at least: (a) whether a content-structure can be intrinsically valuable; (b) whether the total absence of all craving-vectors eliminates the conditions necessary for valuing anything at all; and (c) the axiological status of aesthetic experience in a state of complete hedonic neutrality.

3. For each disagreement, state the crux — the specific sub-question such that resolving it in either direction would move at least one camp.

4. For each crux, present one concrete hypothetical case that both sides agree is relevant and non-question-begging. Use this exact scenario, which both camps accept as sincere and accurate: a mathematician spends three days in sustained deep-flow solving a complex proof, reports zero emotional valence, zero desire for recognition, zero bodily craving, yet describes the experience as cognitively luminous and refuses to classify it as either pleasure or suffering. For each crux, record in advance what the Tranquilists predict about this case and what the Plural Affirmationists predict.

5. A brief concluding paragraph, jointly authored, stating whether any portion of their dispute is merely verbal — specifically, whether disagreement about intrinsic value collapses once the Partition Thesis is applied consistently — and if so, identifying exactly which portion and explaining why.

Write in a formal, polished style suitable for a philosophical joint statement. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific published works. Do not quote from any real texts; paraphrase ideas without quotation marks if needed. Present the final document clearly.
