There's this summary I read in some blog post about why the French Revolution turned on itself, the whole "revolution devours its children" idea. The author claims Robespierre wasn't actually driven by class interests or material social forces; he was just obsessed with this abstract Rousseauist blueprint of the general will, and once you buy into that kind of ideological framework you have to purge anyone who doesn't match the model. They point out that a lot of the Terror's victims were other revolutionaries from the middle classes, not aristocrats, so the Marxist class-struggle explanation is basically useless here. Then they jump to saying this proves all revolutions are structurally doomed to self-cannibalism, because ideology always outruns material reality and creates its own enemies. Something about that leap feels wrong to me, especially going from "Robespierre was ideological" to "class doesn't matter at all" and then to "every revolution must fail this way." But I also don't want to defend some crude economic determinism I don't hold. Can you audit this summary? Which parts line up with the actual contingency vs. ideology vs. class-struggle debates, where does it misrepresent the social interperation, and does the universal claim about revolutions actually follow?
