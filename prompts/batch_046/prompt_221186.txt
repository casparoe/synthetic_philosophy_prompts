I'm a retired biochemist — thirty years in enzyme kinetics, now I spend my mornings with philosphy of science — and I've cobbled together a view about explanation that I think owes a lot to Dennett but also to my own uneccessary worries about reductionism. Basically I think every genuine explanation is a crane: it builds up step by step from simpler parts, with no skyhooks that invoke some top-down cause with no bottom-up pedigree. Greedy reductionism — trying to explain cognition or evolutionary development straight from quantum mechanics — is clearly wrong, but that failure doesn't license skyhooks; it just means we need nested cranes operating across intermediate levels. So my working position is that all real explanation is multi-level and crane-based, and that whenever we think we've found irreducible downward causation in biology or cognition, closer inspection will always reveal hidden scaffolding that was assembled gradually. But lately I've been wondering about what I call the scaffolding reversal problem: in some evolutionary developmental systems, the lower-level genetic mechanisms seem to exist primarily because they preserve a specific morphological form, not the other way round. If that's right, it looks like the higher-level pattern is doing causal work in maintaining the lower-level machinery, which smells like a skyhook unless you can show it's still just a crane with feedback loops. So what I really want is an honest map of what would actually count against my crane-only view. Would demonstrating a genuine scaffolding reversal in a well-studied biological system be decisive, or could defenders just redescribe it as stabilising selection? Would a formal model of downward causation that survives without collapsing into epiphenomenalism damage the view, or merely make it awkward? And do proponents of this position concede any particular observation or argument that would genuinely hurt it, or have they insulated themselves by defining every counterexample away as hidden scaffolding? I want to know exactly where the view is exposed — what it genuinely risks, and whether those risks are live rather than merely logical.
