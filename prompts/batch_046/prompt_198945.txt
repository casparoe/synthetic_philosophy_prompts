I am a novelist who has served for three years on the clinical ethics board of the Meridian State Psychiatric Hospital. The administration proposes expanding a pilot program—the Stoic Resilience Curriculum—to all long-term wards. The curriculum teaches Epictetus’s dichotomy of control, instructing patients to distinguish rigorously between what lies within their power and what does not, with the stated aim of reducing pharmaceutical dependency and fostering autonomy. Patient advocates and several attending psychiatrists counter that in a coercive institutional setting—where patients have minimal control over medication schedules, visitation rights, or discharge timelines—the framework functions less as empowerment and more as an ideology of acquiescence: it teaches the vulnerable to accommodate conditions that ought to be contested rather than changed.

Please draft the board’s formal decision memo for circulation before our meeting on the 18th. Recommend a concrete course of action—full approval, rejection, or approval subject to specific conditions—together with an explicit timeline for implementation or review. The reasoning should treat the advocates’ concern seriously, engaging the possibility that the modern revival of Stoicism risks legitimizing structural neglect when applied inside an institution that already restricts liberty. At the same time, address the administration’s evidence that the pilot reduced acute agitation episodes without increasing adverse events. Write in American English, maintain a formal institutional tone, and conclude with a clear bottom line that members on the losing side could still recognize as fair to their position.
