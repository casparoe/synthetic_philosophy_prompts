I am drafting my position on embedded agency and moral responsibility for a philosophy of mind workshop, and I want a consistency audit before presenting. Here is the package: (1) Agency is not a property of isolated internal representational architectures but emerges from sustained feedback loops between an organism and its environment; (2) This relational, broadly enactivist picture is fully compatible with robust moral responsibility, because practices of holding one another accountable are themselves nodes within the loop rather than external sanctions applied to a pre-given, bounded subject; (3) If an agent’s preference-formation is gradually reshaped by an algorithmic environment—say, a recommendation architecture that slowly restructures what the agent finds valuable—the agent remains responsible for actions that flow from those reshaped preferences, provided the agent endorsed the outputs of that loop at some point; (4) What undermines responsibility is not causal influence per se but a discontinuity with the agent’s prior normative commitments, so manipulation only excuses when it severs the thread of self-governance rather than extending it; (5) Because agency is embedded, there is no principled boundary at the skin: cognitive and normative processes extend into tools, institutions, and social structures; (6) Consequently, we can bear responsibility for outcomes occurring outside our bodies when those outcomes flow from genuinely extended cognitive processes that remain continuous with our normative commitments.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and what the least costly revision would be in each case. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. In particular, I worry that (3) and (4) seem to presuppose a bounded self whose prior commitments can be identified and compared against later states, while (1), (2), and (5) deny that any such boundary exists. Is that contradiction real, or can the loop-model accommodate a functional notion of self-governance without reifying the boundary? And does (6) overextend responsibility in a way that (2) cannot defend, or does the loop-structure naturally constrain it? Be specific about which single commitment would have to go if the set does not hang together, and what I would lose by dropping it.
