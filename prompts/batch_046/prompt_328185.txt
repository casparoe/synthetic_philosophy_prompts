I am a high school student preparing interview questions for my school's philosophy podcast. Our guest is a metaphysician who defends a specific revisionary view: she holds that apparent cases of masked or finkish dispositions are never genuine blockages, but rather instances of what she calls "dispositional redirection," in which a property is channeled into a different manifestation form rather than being suppressed. She uses this to reject the conditional analysis of dispositions and to deny that there are any unmanifested dispositions that lack an actual pathway. I need six to eight rigorous interview questions that avoid asking her to restate the view. Please include at least one question pressing how her redirection account differs from the standard antidote case for fragility; one asking what her framework predicts for compound objects in which one component is finked and another is not; and one probing whether her view can accommodate dispositions that appear to have no manifestation pathway whatsoever. For each of these three, provide a follow-up keyed to her likely reply—especially her tendency to claim that any apparent failure of manifestation is simply a misidentified redirection pathway. The questions should be respectful, precise, and accessible to an audience of advanced high school students studying metaphysics.
