Hey - working on a column about whether teleosemantics can handle thinking about math. I think I have a reductio but can't find the hole.

(1) On the naturalist view, mental content is fixed by the seleciton history of the mechanism that produces it.
(2) Now take offline reasoning: I'm stuck in traffic and derive that 17 is prime. The reasoning module was selected for planning and threat-detection, not number theory.
(3) So the belief '17 is prime' has no direct selection history; it's just spillover.
(4) If content needs direct selection history, then this belief either has no content or borrows the module's survival content ('avoid danger').
(5) But that'd make every spillover belief mean the same survival thing, which is nuts - I don't think '17 is prime' means watch out.
(6) So either teleosemantics can't cover abstract offline thought, or we drop the direct-selection rule.
(7) But if mechanisms just confer content on all outputs, misrepresentation disappears: every output does exactly what the module was picked for, even false beliefs.
(8) So the view either fails for math-like cognition or eliminates error entirely.

My gut says (4) is the weak link - maybe there's derived proper function? - but I'm not sure what giving it up costs. Which step should I reject, and what's the price? Also, is there a standard middle ground I'm missing, or is this a real tension?
