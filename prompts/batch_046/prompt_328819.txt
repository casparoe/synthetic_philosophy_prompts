I'm preparing a consistency audit of my commitments in Confucian role ethics for a seminar presentation and want to verify that the package hangs together before I present it. Here is roughly where I have landed:

(a) Moral obligations are constituted by social roles—parent, child, ruler, subject, friend—rather than derived from universal properties such as rational agency or sentience; there is no transcendent standpoint that generates duties independently of relational context.

(b) The virtues appropriate to each role are plural and incommensurable. There is no lexical ordering and no single master virtue, not even ren, that can mechanically resolve conflicts between, for instance, filial piety and the demands of administrative justice.

(c) Reflective equilibrium is the correct normative method, but it operates intra-role rather than globally: we refine our grasp of exemplary parenthood or rulership by balancing particular judgments and general principles within that specific relational domain, without requiring trans-role coherence.

(d) The framework survives evolutionary debunking of particular norms—say, specific rituals of filial piety—because role ethics concerns the formal structure of relational obligation, not the contingent evolutionary origin or historical content of any given practice (li).

(e) In hard cases of irreducible role conflict—e.g., a magistrate who is also a filial child—the correct resolution is guided by the principle that the role with the broader institutional scope of responsibility takes precedence; the obligation belonging to the narrower role is not erased but persists as a moral remainder.

Do these commitments form a coherent set? I want a targeted audit rather than a general lecture:

- Identify any genuine tensions between pairs, and distinguish them from conflicts that only appear serious but dissolve on closer inspection. In particular, is there an implicit ordering principle embedded in (e) that (b)'s denial of lexical ordering and (a)'s rejection of a transcendent standpoint should jointly rule out?
- Does (d)'s claimed immunity to evolutionary debunking sit uneasily with (c)'s reliance on considered judgments that evolutionary psychology might undermine?
- Rank the tensions by severity, and specify for each what would have to be revised—whether weakening (e)'s scope principle, restricting (c) to purely internal coherence, or modifying (b)'s incommensurability claim.
- Finally, indicate whether the package is salvageable with minimal cost, and what that least costly revision actually costs conceptually.

Please do not assume I am already disposed to abandon any particular thesis; trace the commitments precisely and show what each one commits me to.
