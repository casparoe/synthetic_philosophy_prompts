A philosophy forum post I read tries to separate Grice's notions of speaker meaning and conversational implicature into two independent modules. The poster summarizes Grice like this: "Speaker meaning is always fixed by the speaker's primary communicative intention—specifically, an intention to induce a belief in the hearer via recognition of that very intention—while conversational implicature is computed automatically by the hearer through the Cooperative Principle and its maxims, with no further reference to the speaker's particular intentions. What is said supplies only the conventional linguistic content; speaker meaning adds the intended belief; and implicature supplies the cooperative surplus derived from maxim-flouting. In irony, the speaker's intended meaning (the opposite of the literal content) belongs to speaker meaning, while the contradiction with reality belongs to implicature via a flouted Quality maxim. So Grice's framework is modular: speaker-meaning recovery and implicature calculation run on parallel tracks." This sounds tidy, but I worry it misrepresents Grice's unified account of meaning and rational cooperation. Is this a fair summary? Specifically, does Grice treat implicature derivation as intention-independent in this way, or is the recovery of speaker intentions inseparable from recognizing which maxims are being exploited? And is irony correctly parsed as a split between speaker meaning and implicature, or does Grice's own treatment place it squarely within maxim-based implicature?
