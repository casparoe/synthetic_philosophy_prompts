My mother was recently diagnosed with progressive frontotemporal dementia, which has forced me to revisit von Neumann's comparison between neural and digital computation not as an intellectual curiosity but as a way to understand why her decline feels like erosion rather than system failure. I work in neuromorphic hardware design, so I think in terms of memristive crossbars, spike-timing-dependent plasticity, stochastic resonance, graceful degradation under adversarial bit-flip injection, and distributed fault-tolerant clusters—vocabulary I use daily and in which I am fluent.

Restate von Neumann's core position about the brain—his claims that neural computation is a hybrid analog-digital logic, that reliability is achieved through statistical redundancy rather than explicit error-correcting codes, that individual components are highly unreliable yet the system is robust, and that this architecture raises the possibility of mechanical self-replication—entirely within the conceptual machinery of modern neuromorphic engineering and fault-tolerant distributed computing that I have described. Do not smuggle in biological metaphors or psychological language unless they genuinely have counterparts in that hardware vocabulary; the point is an honest translation, not a comforting analogy.

Then provide the residue report, claim by claim. For the hybrid-computation thesis, the statistical-reliability thesis, the unreliable-component thesis, and the self-replication thesis, say which translate cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which have no expression at all in neuromorphic terms. In particular, address whether the phenomenological fact that my mother's cognitive errors feel like gradual dissolution—what clinicians call the loss of narrative continuity—can be captured by graceful-degradation models, or whether forcing that experience into engineering vocabulary is a category mistake.

Finally, evaluate the residue. Is what fails to translate a deficiency of the neuromorphic framework—does it lack the resources to talk about subjective continuity, ethical personhood, or the normative weight of cognitive decline? Or does the failure show that von Neumann's original comparison was already over-abstracted, treating the brain as a logic machine precisely by stripping away dimensions that no engineering vocabulary should be expected to carry? I want your assessment of whether I am using the wrong framework to understand her condition, or whether the framework is doing exactly what it ought to do by revealing what it cannot say.
