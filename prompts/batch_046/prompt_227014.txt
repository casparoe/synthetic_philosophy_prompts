I'm helping scope a three-year, interdisciplinary project—mostly philosophers and institutional designers, not a large empirical lab—aimed at clarifying how social categories should be structured in policy and technology. The domain is the social construction of kinds, specifically race, gender, and money, but I'm not looking for a literature survey. Instead, I want a defended prioritization: which currently open conceptual questions or lines of work in this area matter most for actual institutional design, which are overrated or don't change downstream practice, and in what order should a small team tackle them?

Please defend the ordering with explicit criteria—upstream dependency, tractability without massive data collection, and the magnitude of practical change if the answer comes out one way versus another. I especially want you to engage with the distinction between looping effects (where classifying people under a kind changes the kind's properties) and homeostatic property clustering (where the kind is stabilized by causal mechanisms largely independent of classification), and ask whether this distinction does genuinely different work across race, gender, and money, or whether it collapses into a single continuum in practice. For instance, does treating monetary value as a socially constructed kind with looping effects commit us to different design principles than treating it as a homeostatically clustered functional kind?

Finally, identify which implications of your answers actually carry weight for policy—say, census architecture, gender-recognition protocols, or the legitimacy conditions of fiat currency—and which are philosophically interesting but institutionally inert. I want an agenda, not a reading list: tell me where limited years and attention should go, what evidence or argument over the next few years would cause you to reorder the priorities, and what feature of the problem your agenda cannot address and must be bracketed.
