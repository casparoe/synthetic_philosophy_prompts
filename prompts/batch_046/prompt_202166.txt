The summary claims that Nozick proves well-being can never be reduced to mental states, because people refuse a simulated life even when it guarantees perfect happiness and fully satisfied preferences. It presents Nozick as arguing that being genuinely connected to an actual reality is itself an intrinsic component of well-being, which places him squarely within the objective-list tradition alongside thinkers such as Derek Parfit, and it concludes that hedonism, desire-fulfilment theory, and all preference-satisfaction accounts are therefore equally refuted—since each allows that a perfectly virtual life could be maximally good for the person living it. I feel this may conflate Nozick's specific anti-hedonist point with a broader objective-list thesis, and perhaps exaggerates the scope of the argument, but I lack the philosophical training to say exactly where the summary slips. Could you audit it for me: which parts are faithful to Nozick's actual position, which are subtle misrepresentations, and does its sweeping conclusion survive once those errors are corrected?
