I am a high school student currently enrolled in an introductory course on political philosophy, and over the past week my instructor has assigned excerpts from the Marquis de Condorcet's 1785 Essay on the Application of Analysis to the Probability of Majority Decisions. I have been attempting to work through the section in which Condorcet imagines a collective body choosing between two outcomes, only one of which is correct, where each member votes independently with some probability p of being right. He demonstrates that if p exceeds one-half, then enlarging the body raises the probability that the majority decision is correct, approaching certainty in the limit, whereas if p falls below one-half the optimal jury consists of a single voter. I follow the arithmetic well enough, but I am having trouble interpreting what Condorcet intends the independence condition to represent.

Specifically, in our class discussion a fellow student raised an objection—possibly drawn from a modern commentary rather than the original text—that in actual assemblies voters are not independent: they deliberate, share evidence, and can fall prey to common errors or shared misconceptions. This led me to wonder whether Condorcet's model implicitly assumes a kind of isolated, silent voting that would never occur in practice, or whether he is simply constructing an abstract formal system whose premises must be protected from empirical contamination. I have also encountered the idea, though I am unsure whether it originates with Condorcet or with later interpreters, that if errors are positively correlated—say, through an epistemic cascade—the asymptotic convergence to certainty collapses, and a large jury can perform worse than a single expert even when p is substantially above one-half. Is this correlation critique already present in Condorcet's 1785 reasoning, or is it an anachronistic addition? I would greatly value help in understanding whether the independence clause in the passage functions as a normative prescription about how assemblies ought to be structured, or merely as a mathematical boundary condition, and whether there is any distinction in the text itself between the formal derivation and its practical application that I have overlooked.
