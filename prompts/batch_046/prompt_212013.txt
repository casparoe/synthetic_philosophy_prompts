I have been observing a recurring dialectical pattern in the recent literature on epistemic logic and the problem of logical omniscience, and I wonder whether it has received a specific label. The pattern runs as follows. A theorist begins by denying that rational belief is closed under arbitrary logical entailment, proposing instead a restricted closure principle—say, closure only under entailments derivable within a bounded number of explicit inferential steps from the agent's occurrent beliefs. Critics then construct a case in which the agent knows p, p transparently entails q in a single step, yet the bounded-step principle permits her not to know q. Rather than abandoning the restriction, the theorist introduces an auxiliary condition: closure holds when the entailment is "salient" or "cognitively cheap" relative to the agent's architecture. But salience is then characterized in a way that tracks precisely the cases critics have flagged, so that each new exception restores closure exactly where its absence seemed counterintuitive. The result is not a single, determinately falsifiable restriction but an accumulating patchwork of individually reasonable exemptions that collectively insulate the core principle from refutation while rendering the overall theory progressively less predictive.

Is there an established term for this particular dynamic—the incremental accretion of locally justified, case-driven exemptions around a resource-sensitive closure principle, where each exemption appeals to cognitive cost or salience but together they reproduce the very omniscience the restriction was designed to block? If so, how does it differ structurally from standard immunization strategies in philosophy of science, and from the familiar charge of ad hocness? If there is no specific name, what is the most precise characterization of its logical structure, where exactly do its boundaries lie—does it apply only to closure principles, or to any resource-bounded epistemic norm defended through successive refinement—and what would be the appropriate coinage? I will not be satisfied with a bare label: I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges cut.
