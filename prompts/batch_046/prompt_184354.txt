For a metaphysics workshop I am drafting my position on material constitution and want a consistency audit before presenting. Here is my current profile:

(1) There is only one material object present: "statue" and "lump of clay" are not names of distinct entities but different conceptual descriptions applied to the same thing.
(2) That single entity survives crushing and scattering if we apply the concept clay, yet is destroyed under the concept statue; persistence conditions are kind-relative.
(3) I reject relative identity—identity is absolute and not sortal-dependent.
(4) I am an endurantist: objects are wholly present at each moment they exist, not constituted by temporal parts.
(5) I accept organizational continuity for artifacts: gradual replacement of constituent matter preserves identity, but instantaneous replacement of all matter at once does not, because form requires spatiotemporal continuity in the organizing process.

Please assess which pairs genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and what the least costly revision would be. Rank tensions by severity rather than treating them as equal, specify for each serious conflict exactly which commitment must be modified or abandoned, and identify the single change that would resolve the most problems at once. I have no prior preference for which thesis to drop, and I want a point-by-point evaluation rather than a general impression.
