Having recently observed that my late mother's characteristic patterns of behaviour and moral judgement seem to have been distributed between myself and my sibling—so that each of us preserves different fragments, with neither alone constituting a clear continuation—I have become preoccupied with Douglas Hofstadter's suggestion that a self is essentially a replicable cognitive pattern that need not be confined to a single brain. If John Locke, whose account of personal identity rested upon the continuity of consciousness and memory, were confronted today with the possibility of such a bifurcated 'I' spread across two people, how do you think he would have responded? Would he have modified his forensic conception of personhood to allow for a divided yet genuinely persisting self, or would he have insisted that this kind of distribution destroys the very unity that makes personal identity meaningful?
