Write a dialogue between an eliminative materialist and a realist about propositional attitudes—make them both sharp, no strawmen. Have them argue over a chess grandmaster who reports she 'believes the knight is pinned' and 'wants to castle kingside' but can't explain why. The eliminativist should claim this is just neural pattern-matching misldescribed in folk terms; the realist should say the explantion only works if we actually attribute the belief and desire. Then have them wrestle with why her first-person report feels authortative if eliminativism is right—does it carry weight or is it just noise? Don't resolve it; end with each saying where their view goes silent. Keep it casual, like a late-night seminar argument with bad coffee.
