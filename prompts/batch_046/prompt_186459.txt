As a software engineer without formal philosophical training, I have been trying to understand distributive justice through analogies to resource-allocation algorithms. I recently encountered a summary of Robert Nozick's entitlement theory and the Wilt Chamberlain argument that runs roughly as follows: Nozick claims that any distribution is just provided it arose from a prior just state through entirely voluntary exchanges, so if every person begins with an equal share and then freely transfers small amounts to watch Wilt Chamberlain play basketball, the resulting extreme inequality is automatically just, no matter how large the disparity becomes. The summary asserts that this demonstrates all patterned principles—including Rawls's difference principle—are incoherent, since preserving any pattern requires continuous coercive interference with voluntary transactions. It further concludes that Nozick has shown taxation for redistribution is necessarily theft, and therefore libertarianism is the only logically consistent theory of justice. Is this characterization fair? Please distinguish the accurate core of the historical entitlement principle and the Chamberlain scenario from the overstatements, clarify whether Nozick's argument applies regardless of how the initial distribution was acquired, and evaluate whether the claim that all redistribution is theft follows from the argument or is an independent normative addition.
