I have recently taken up an advisory position with a local authority planning committee, where I am required to weigh heritage preservation, carbon-reduction obligations, and affordable housing targets against one another in redevelopment cases. The philosophical literature on hard choices and incommensurable values—particularly Ruth Chang’s account of options being on a par and Joseph Raz’s treatment of incommensurability as non-comparability—seems designed for dyadic comparisons, yet my practical dilemmas involve three or more values that exhibit cyclical dominance: heritage outweighs carbon reduction in one framing, carbon reduction outweighs housing in another, and housing outweighs heritage in a third. I am applying for a nine-month research fellowship to produce a 10,000-word philosophical paper together with a decision-framework appendix. Please turn this into a formal proposal that states the precise research question—whether multi-value cyclical incommensurability is genuinely distinct from dyadic incommensurability, and whether a procedural resolution appealing to democratic legitimacy or deliberative fairness can manage it without collapsing the values into a single metric. The proposal should explain why existing accounts of incommensurability and hard choice leave this triadic structure unaddressed; set out the conceptual method, which will analyse institutional case studies rather than collect empirical fieldwork, and justify why this approach is preferable to formal modelling or pure normative ethics; specify what a successful result would look like, such as a taxonomy of cyclical structures and a defensible procedural criterion, and what would count as failure, for example demonstrating that all apparent cycles reduce to hidden pairwise comparisons; and provide an honest assessment of whether the project is tractable within nine months. Write in British English, do not request citations or a bibliography, and present the deliverable as suitable for a philosophy-of-public-policy funding panel.
