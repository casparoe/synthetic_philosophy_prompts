I've been trying to understand whether patterns like jealousy, market crashes, or computer errors are actually real, or only convenient ways of talking. I keep returning to the idea that anything can be described in three completely different registers: the physical register (particles and forces), the design register (what something is built for or how it functions), and the intentional register (what someone believes, wants, or feels). I tried to formalize when a pattern counts as real across these three, and now my reasoning seems to prove that either social science studies fictions, or every human being contains two fully separate layers of reality existing side by side. Here is the chain.

(1) If a pattern is genuinely real, then it must remain present no matter which of the three descriptive registers we adopt; reality should not depend on our vocabulary.
(2) Consider the pattern "Sarah resents her colleague's promotion because she believes she deserved it." This is visible only in the intentional register. In the design register it becomes an evolutionary competitive response; in the physical register it becomes neurotransmitter fluctuations.
(3) The same happens with "this software has a bug." In the design register it is an error; in the physical register it is precisely correct electron flow.
(4) So patterns like resentment and bugs disappear when we switch registers.
(5) By (1), if they disappear, they were never genuinely real—only register-locked shadows.
(6) But if bugs are not real, then repairing one is like repairing a shadow, yet the computer behaves differently afterward.
(7) Therefore (1) must be false: a pattern can be real even if it is register-locked, visible only within one framework.
(8) Now apply (7) consistently. The intentional register reveals real patterns (beliefs, desires, resentments), and the physical register reveals real patterns (neurons, atoms).
(9) Because these patterns are real in different registers, they all exist together in the same person or object.
(10) So every human action is simultaneously a fully real physical pattern and a fully real intentional pattern, with neither reducing the other.
(11) That means every person carries two complete, non-overlapping descriptions of reality inside them—like a car being both a metal object and a separate "car-ness" pattern that exists alongside it.
(12) This duplication seems absurd. But if we deny (7), then psychology and engineering study fictions, which also seems absurd.
(13) So either we accept massive ontological duplication, or we admit that register-locked patterns are unreal and abandon large parts of science and common sense.

Where does this argument break? I want to keep (6)—that fixing bugs really changes something—and I want to preserve the idea that the three registers are genuinely different, not just translations of one another. I am willing to give up (1), but I want to know exactly what I am paying if I do: does it commit me to saying reality is divided into separate layers, or can I hold that the patterns are real without duplicating the world? If the flaw is elsewhere—perhaps in how I move from (7) to (9)—I want to know which step slips and what rejecting it costs me.
