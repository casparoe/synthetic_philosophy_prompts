hey, i'm trying to figure out what i actually think about karma as a theory of moral desert — not the instagram version, the philosophical one where suffering and flourishing track moral quality across lives or actions in some proportional way. my gut reactions to cases are messy and i want to know if there's a principle behind them or if i just have to drop one.

case one: someone lives with obvious virtue but dies in horrible pain with no hidden vice to explain it. i think the pain is undeserved, full stop — and that actually makes me skeptical of the theory, becasue if desert can be hidden behind an unknown past life it stops being morally meaningful.

case two: a fraudster lives rich and dies peacefully. i think that's undeserved comfort, but i also think that if he never suffers at all, he hasn't "escaped" desert — the desert is still there, just unmanifest. but then in case three, a small-time thief spends twenty years in crushing guilt and self-punishment, then gets hit by a bus. i feel like the guilt already paid the debt, so the bus feels undeserved. but that conflicts with case two, where unmanifest desert still counts.

case four: a corporation poisons a river; fifty years later, the executives' grandkids suffer health problems. i think there's collective desert at work, but it's strange to assign it to people who didn't choose anything.

case five: someone does good purely to build up "karmic credit" and then gets rewarded. i think the reward isn't deserved because the motive is corrupt. but then in case six, someone saves a child purely out of reflex, with zero moral reasoning, and later inherits money. i think they DO deserve it, which pulls against case five's purity requirement.

so is there a coherent account? maybe something like "desert requires both proportionality and motivational transparency, but collective desert works differently"? or do i have to give up on hidden causes? also, what about retroactive annulment — if the fraudster in case two sincerely repents and makes amends, does that erase the unmanifest desert, or just convert it?

what principle captures all this, and which of my verdicts should i overturn? also predict for me: if someone does harm but is genuinely ignorant that it's wrong, is there desert at all?
