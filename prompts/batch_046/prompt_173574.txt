I sit on our university's interdisciplinary research ethics board. There's this proposal from the cognitive architecture lab — they want to build what they call a careenium (basically a synthetic substrate where symbolic tokens, or simmballs, can be rearranged by an external parser) to test whether the symbolic level actually pushes the physical substrate around, or if it's all just substrate all the way down. The PI claims if the simmballs change their own careenium environment based on representational rules, that proves downward causation and has big implications for how we treat AI minds or even human cognition. The board is split: half think it's a neat philosophy-of-mind experiment, half think it's pseudoscience that risks misallocating funding and maybe creates weird moral-status confusion if people start treating the substrate like it has beliefs. Our policy says we can approve with conditions but doesn't specify which conditions apply to symbolic-causation studies. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before next tuesdays vote: recommend either approval with specific safeguards, rejection with a path for resubmission, or a deferral with an external philosophy review — pick one and defend it. You need to take seriously the PI's claim about downward causation and also the skeptics worry that were conflating functional description with causal mechanism. Make it readable for scientists who don't do metaphysics and include a concrete timeline for whatever you recommend.
