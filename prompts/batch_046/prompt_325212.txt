As a retired experimental physicist who has lately turned to philosophy of history, I find myself troubled by the boundary between legend and history, particularly in the cases of the Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus. I should like you to construct a formal taxonomy—structured as a decision tree—of the positions one might hold on when, if ever, a legend becomes history. The root node should divide stances according to whether they posit a sharp epistemic threshold (for instance, contemporaneous documentary attestation) or instead allow a graded spectrum of historicity. The subsequent branches should turn on the status of archaeological correlation: is it treated as necessary, sufficient, or merely corroborative? At each leaf, identify the position—named if it is defended in the literature, otherwise 'unnamed'—and state in one sentence its most awkward commitment with respect to at least one of the three reference cases. For example, indicate whether a view must deny any historical kernel to Arthur despite early medieval references, or must treat the Exodus narrative as essentially unhistorical despite its institutional centrality. Every branch must be labelled by the substantive claim that separates it from its siblings, not by an author's name. Please present the tree clearly, and write your response in British English.
