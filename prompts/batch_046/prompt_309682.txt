I am looking for an established term—if one exists—for a pattern that resembles self-deception but differs from the familiar divided-mind or motivated-ignorance accounts in a specific structural way. The pattern is not a post-hoc defense of a particular belief or action. Instead, it is a standing interpretive framework that preemptively sorts incoming evidence about one's own motives into categories that preserve a desired self-image, without ever generating an internal conflict that needs resolution. For instance, consider someone who regards herself as motivated solely by justice, yet consistently finds that her objections align with her social standing. Rather than suppressing the correlation, she develops a taxonomy in which status-sensitive objections and justice-sensitive objections are defined so that any given objection can be placed in the latter category by default; the original taxonomy itself is protected from scrutiny because questioning it is interpreted as evidence of bad faith. The same structure appears institutionally: an organization treats all internal criticism as procedural non-compliance, not by rebutting specific claims but by having a permanent classification rule that assigns dissent to an illegitimate type. In neither case is there a hidden belief being concealed from a conscious self; rather, the architecture of interpretation is arranged so that the threatening interpretation is not merely false but categorically unavailable. Is there a recognized concept for this kind of preemptive, architectural filtering—something distinct from rationalization, from self-deception in the standard sense, and from what is sometimes called motivated ignorance? If not, I would value a precise definition that distinguishes it from those neighbors, together with an account of whether it still qualifies as self-deception or represents a different failure of self-knowledge altogether.
