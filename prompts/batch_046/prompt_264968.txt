ok so here's a thing that bugs me about public arts funding: grant boards say they want art that 'builds community' and 'reaches ordinary people,' but then they score applicatons on things like prior gallery representation, reviews in art-world publications, and whether the project involves 'stakeholder consultation' with institutional partners. Meanwhile if someone proposes a simple, emotionally direct painting about grief that anyone could understand but that has no curatorial framing, it gets rejected for lacking 'critical engagement.' What view of what art is for does that scoring system actually presuppose? Is it consistent with Tolstoy's claim that the only real test is whether a work infects people with feeling across class lines? Or does the practice secretly presuppose that art's job is to maintain professional art-world structures, with the 'community' language just being window dressing? And if we actually ran grants on an infection criterion — judging by whether non-expert audiences report shared emotional response — what parts of the current funding apparatus would have to be dismantled?
