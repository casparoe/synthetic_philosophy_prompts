The other, adopting a steadfast stance, maintains his original credence because he believes he has correctly identified a subtle calibration error in the shared dataset that his peer missed. The seminar splits evenly. Some students say the steadfast modeler is rationally permitted to hold firm because disagreement is not symmetric when one party has access to a private reason; others reply that private reasons destroy the very conditions for peerhood and that conciliation should apply regardless. We have mapped every standard argument—the symmetry considerations, the weighting strategies, and the bootstrapping objections—and yet nobody moves.

My working hypothesis is that the stall is not about the formal rules of belief revision but about a hidden disagreement over what epistemic equality requires: does it demand identical total evidence, or only identical cognitive virtue?
