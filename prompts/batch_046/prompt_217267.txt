Design a minimalist counterfactual model-world for the purpose of testing whether the contrastive historical question "why Britain and not China?"—the core of the Great Divergence and Needham debates—is well posed when all macro-variables except one are held constant. The world should contain two agrarian societies, Albion and Cathay, that are identical in climate, arable land, proximity to coal deposits, population density, formal property-rights institutions, and access to global trade networks. The single engineered difference is an institutional feature I will call a "knowledge-circulation filter": in Albion it is absent, so artisanal techniques can be modified, recorded in non-state archives, and reinvested as capital without guild or bureaucratic authorization; in Cathay it is present, allowing the same techniques to be practiced but blocking their cumulative modification and capital reinvestment. Present this scenario in one paragraph, with no anachronistic technology, no appeal to exotic physics, and no citation of specific historians or works by name.

Then apply three analytical stances to the scenario:
(i) The isolated-necessary-cause stance, which holds that a well-posed "why A and not B?" requires identifying a factor present in A and absent in B that was individually necessary for the outcome.
(ii) The comparative-causal-weight stance, which holds that the question is well posed only when multiple co-present factors can be weighed against one another, and that holding all others constant either clarifies or artificially collapses this comparison.
(iii) The path-dependence-and-nonlinearity stance, which holds that contrastive framing is misleading when small initial differences compound stochastically, so that asking "why A and not B" assumes a determinism that the dynamics do not support.

For each stance, state the precise verdict it delivers about whether "why Albion and not Cathay?" is well posed in your model-world, and show the reasoning that generates that verdict from the scenario's structure rather than from external historical data. Next, identify the specific structural feature—namely, the combination of total macro-identity with the filter's probabilistic, non-linear operation—that separates the three verdicts. Explain what happens to the argument if that feature is removed, for example if the filter operates deterministically or if one macro-variable is allowed to vary. Finally, include a brief, honest note on how a defender of the stance that comes out worst in your design would attack the model-world internally—by challenging its logic, not by importing real-world counterexamples.
