Write a dialogue between two equally informed philosophers, call them A and B, debating whether Daniel Dennett's warning that artificial intelligence can produce counterfeit people is consistent with his broader philosophy of mind.

The dialogue should focus on a specific tension: Dennett's heterophenomenological method treats human beliefs, intentions, and consciousness as interpretive constructs warranted by predictive success rather than direct access to inner essences. Yet his counterfeit-people framing seems to depend on a hidden distinction between genuine persons and artificial mimics that appeals to causal pedigree, evolutionary embedding, or biological origin. One interlocutor (A) should defend Dennett by arguing that the counterfeit worry is not metaphysical essentialism but a practical concern about predictive breakdown: the intentional stance works for humans because of deep evolutionary and social embedding, and applying it to AI is decorative rather than genuinely explanatory. The other interlocutor (B) should press the charge that Dennett is smuggling essentialism back in, and should introduce the further point that if counterfeit status depends on lacking the right causal history, then many humans with atypical neural development, severe brain-computer interfaces, or radically altered cognitive architectures would also count as counterfeit — a reductio that Dennett must block without abandoning his anti-essentialist commitments.

The dialogue must contain real back-and-forth: each speaker should respond directly to the specific argument just made, not deliver parallel speeches. Neither side should be a strawman. The disagreement should remain substantive and unresolved by the end.

Please organize the response in the following sections, in order, with approximate lengths:
1. Setup and framing (about 150 words): introduce the interlocutors and the precise conceptual tension.
2. Opening positions (about 350 words): A and B each present their view, with B introducing the reductio involving atypical humans and brain-computer interfaces.
3. First exchange (about 400 words): direct rebuttals on whether evolutionary embedding functions as a predictive criterion or a disguised metaphysical one.
4. Second exchange (about 400 words): probing whether the intentional stance can be applied consistently to AI without generating the counterfeit category at all.
5. Closing without resolution (about 200 words): each summarizes the core point they believe the other has failed to answer.

Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. If you reference Dennett's specific claims, paraphrase them; do not use verbatim quotations unless you have verified the exact wording from a source.
