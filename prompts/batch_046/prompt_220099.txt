I've been thinking about this idea that consciousness is basically a user illusion - like the icons on your laptop aren't literally the programs, just a useful picture your brain runs so you can navigate without seeing all the code. So my position is that all this rich inner feeling I'm having right now isn't direct access to reality but more like a dashboard or interface my brain builds mainly for behavioral control. I mentioned it to my friend last week and he said "so you think you're not real?" and I was like no no, the illusion is still happening, it's just not what it seems. Definately not saying I'm a zombie or anything.

But I want to actually stress-test this view properly. What would count against it? I'm not asking for the basic "but I feel pain" objection - I get that an interface can include pain-simulations. I'm more interested in the harder defeaters: what about phenomenal overflow, where people seem to experience way more detail than any streamlined dashboard would actually need for navigation? Or direct cortical stimulation producing pure qualitative states with basically zero behavioral utility - wouldn't that be like a screen glitch that doesn't serve the user at all? And with split-brain patients, if both hemispheres run seperate awareness streams that sometimes overlap in weird ways, does that fit a single-interface model or does it break it?

Also, what does this view predict that rivals - like direct realism about consciousness - don't predict? And has it been qualified into unfalsifiability, where every weird neuroscience result just gets folded in as "another feature of the dashboard"? I want these sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which ones defenders of the illusion view would themselves concede are real problems versus which they'd brush off as misunderstandings. If it turns out the view is totally insulated and risks nothing, is that actually a problem for a theory of this kind? Don't need book lists or citations - I just want to understand where my idea is genuinely exposed.
