Question: Explain Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model of creativity—how mental spaces have adjustable parameters that generate variations on a theme—and connect it to his view that analogy is the core of cogniton. Then apply it: a writer takes the old 'stranger comes to town' plot and keeps everything identical except shifting the emotional regsiter from dread to longing. Would Hofstadter call that genuine creativity or just a mechanical tweak?

Student answer:
'Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model says every concept is like a control board with dials—scale, emotional color, moral weight, temporal speed. Creativity is turning these dials to make variations on an underlying theme. It's tied to analogy because analogies map structures between domains, and knob-twiddling lets you explore the neighborhood around a concept. So if you flip the dread knob to longing in the stranger story, that's real creativity, not mechanical, because it forces a new analogical bridge between the stranger and the town. Actually, Hofstadter thinks there are no deep themes, just clusters of knob settings that feel stable, so 'classic narrative' is an illusion. He worked this out with Melanie Mitchell in Copycat, and he says you need at least three knobs shifting simultaneously for true analogical cogniton. So technically one dial shouldn't count, but here the emotional shift is big enough that it does.'
