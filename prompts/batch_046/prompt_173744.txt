me and my study group have been arguing about public reason and religious arguments in politics for like a year and we're going in cirlces. the basic setup is familiar -- Rawls says citizens need reasons everyone can access, critics say that forces religious people to split themselves in too. but here's where we stall: everyone in the group agrees that secular liberal frameworks embed metaphysical commitments about personhood and time that are just as 'comprehensive' as religious ones. the disagreement is whether that symmetry actually undermines the public-reason requirement or just shows that all politics is comprehensive and we should stop pretending otherwise. the liberals in our group say the secular commitments are 'political not metaphysical' but they never say what makes them political except that they're shared, which feels circular. the critics say if accessibility just means 'shared by the dominant group' then public reason is just majoritarianism in disguise.

my working theory is that this isn't about religion or democracy at all but about what kind of thing a 'reason' is -- like, is it a proposition you can evaluate from outside your worldview, or is it always embedded in a framework that has to be inhabited first? and maybe both sides are answering different questions: one asking 'what justifies coercion?' and the other asking 'what lets us live together without pretending to be neutral?' but i'm not sure if that's a real diagnosis or just me being frustrated.

what i want is an honest diagnosis: is this verbal, about hidden empirical assumptions about what citizens can understand, or about bedrock intuitions on democratic legitimacy? and please separate what's actually settled in this literature from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic -- don't present a contested reading of Rawls or Habermas as the consensus. also, what would actually move this? a conceptual distinction, a change of question, or is it just hard and we should expect it to stay stuck?
