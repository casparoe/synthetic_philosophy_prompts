The fourth quatrain should loop syntactically and imagistically back to the first quatrain, but with the formal machinery now applied to the mental case rather than the mathematical one. The poem's final line should be the opening line with exactly one word substituted, so that the poem enacts the loop structurally; the turn between quatrains two and three should mark the shift from formal proof to philosophical speculation, and the rhythm should remain consistent so that the return feels like a loop rather than a new argument.

Please do not include citations, page references, a bibliography, or a reading list. The poem should stand alone for a reader familiar with Gödel's incompleteness theorem and the basic strange-loop analogy, but it should be precise enough that someone who understands the distinction between syntax and semantics recognizes the analogy is being handled with technical care rather than as decoration.
