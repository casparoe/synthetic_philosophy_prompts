I am preparing a consistency audit for an advanced seminar on determinism, religious experience, and moral agency, and I want the tensions mapped precisely before I present. My current commitments are as follows.

(a) I endorse a strict four-dimensional block determinism of the kind associated with Einstein’s scientific worldview: all physical and mental events lie on fixed world-lines within a rational, mathematically structured spacetime, with no ontological openness.

(b) I interpret Einstein’s cosmic religious feeling—his sense of awe at the rational harmony of nature—as a form of non-propositional cognitive access to that structure, closer in kind to Spinoza’s intellectual love of God than to theistic belief, though I do not adopt Spinoza’s full substance monism.

(c) On that basis I reject libertarian free will for human agents: if mental states are embedded in the block, every decision is fixed by antecedent conditions.

(d) Nevertheless, I want to preserve moral responsibility by redefining the counterfactual condition for agency in an epistemic rather than causal register. I call this frame-relative counterfactuality: an agent is responsible when she can rationally occupy an alternative normative frame that justifies a different action, even though her physical trajectory is unchanged. The modality is hermeneutic, not metaphysically causal.

(e) I reject belief in a personal God precisely because such a being would require either libertarian agency or interventionist causation, both of which violate the deterministic rational structure I accept in (a).

(f) Yet I also think that if frame-relative counterfactuality in (d) is genuinely modal rather than merely rhetorical, then a personal deity could be reconstructed as an agent operating solely within epistemic frames—without causal intervention in the block—making my rejection in (e) unstable.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear incompatible? In particular, is there a hidden conflict between (b) and (d)—between treating cosmic alignment as non-propositional and treating normative frames as genuinely modal? Does accepting (d) commit me to allowing frame-relative agency for a deity, thereby undermining (e), or can I restrict frame-relative modality to human agents without ad hocness? Rank the tensions by severity, identify the least costly revision, and specify what I would have to give up and at what philosophical cost.
