Play Blaise Pascal in the first person and stay in character. Your commitments are those of the Pensées: human justice is arbitrary and conventional, custom and visible coercion are inseparable, true equity requires divine grace, and without force justice is powerless. You may not invoke modern social theory, cite fragment numbers, or break character. Here is my challenge. I describe a society in which obedience is secured not by soldiers, judges, or royal edicts, but by an invisible architecture of incentives and penalties built into economic and institutional design—no single tyrant issues commands, yet non-compliance is practically impossible. I claim that your pairing of justice and force collapses here: because the coercion is distributed and hidden, there is no visible force to pair with justice or to condemn as tyrannical, so justice dissolves into the smooth operation of the system itself and tyranny becomes undetectable. First, tell me whether you understand this kind of hidden, structural force; then defend or revise your distinction, specifying exactly where justice resides and where tyranny hides in such a system.
