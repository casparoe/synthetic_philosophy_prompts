I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether the agricultural revolution was a mistake and where inequality really comes from, going by my gut on specific cases. Comparing immediate-return foragers like the Hadza — maybe 20 hours of work a week, decent health, basically no chiefs — with early Neolithic farmers at Catalhoyuk who had worse teeth, more joint problems, and signs of social ranking: in that comparison agriculture looks like a clear mistake and probably seeded hierachy. But then Göbekli Tepe had massive communal ritual labor and storage before anyone was actually farming, which makes me think the arrow might run the other way — maybe social complexity pulled people into farming rather than farming pushing them into hierarchy. Then there's Jomon Japan: complex pottery, storage pits, semi-sedentism for millenia without agriculture, and burial goods stay pretty flat, so storage alone doesn't automatically create chiefs. The Amazon terra preta societies farmed manioc intensively at large scale but kept relatively flat structures until European contact disrupted them, so farming isn't sufficient for inequality either. But then look at the Pacific Northwest salmon fishers — not farmers at all, but they had slavery, hereditary nobility, and crushing potlatch labor obligations tied to stored surplus. That makes me think the real culprit isn't agriculture specifically but the shift to storable, defensible surplus, whether salmon, grain, or manioc. Yet I still feel like the Neolithic transition was uniquely bad in a way Northwest complexity wasn't, maybe because agriculture locks people into a single resource they can't walk away from. I'm going back and forth on this: if we could rewind and keep everyone as immediate-return foragers but still give them pottery and storage, would we avoid both the labor trap and the hierarchy trap? Is there a principle that gets all of these verdicts, or close, and what does it say about that rewind case? And if some verdict has to be dropped to keep the principle clean, which one should go?
