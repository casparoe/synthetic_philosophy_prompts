In our phil of east asian texts reading group we've been deadlocked for like a year on Zhuangzi's butterfly dream. One camp reads it as straight-up dream skepticism — the waking state is indistinguishable from the dream state, so our beliefs about the world are undermined. The other camp says that's missing the point entirely: it's not about epistemic justification but about the dissolution of a fixed self, since Zhuangzi literally transforms into the butterfly and back, so there's no stable subject that could claim knowledge in either state. They keep talking past eachother, using the same passage to prove opposite things. Is this actually a verbal disagreement about whether the word skepticism applies, or are they running two different arguments — one about phenomenal indistinguishability, one about narrative or mereological identity — that just happen to sit in the same story? And given that diagnosis, could any new distinction or hypothetical case (say, Zhuangzi waking up as a swarm with shared consciousness) actually move the needle, or does the stall mean the text is deliberately doing both and the debate is just ill-posed?
