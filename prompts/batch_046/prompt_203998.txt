As a software engineer writing technical specifications for an asset-tracking platform, I need a reusable decision procedure that applies the problem of material constitution—the statue and the clay, the ship of Theseus, and related puzzles—to determine when an engineered object retains its identity after repair, replacement, or virtualization. The system must assign the same audit and warranty IDs to genuinely continuous entities and generate new IDs when continuity breaks, but the specs currently lack any principled rule for this.

Please construct a step-by-step procedure I can run during spec review. Order the questions as follows: first, identify the dominant sortal—what kind of thing the artifact essentially is (a server, a vehicle, a running service); second, classify which parts or substrates are essential to that sortal versus replaceable; third, test for continuity of material substrate, functional design, and temporal overlap; fourth, determine whether replacement was simultaneous or gradual and whether any persistent core remains. At each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to continue, where the procedure mandates a "new object" determination, and where it must halt and hand the decision to a human because the issue is a business value or legal convention rather than a metaphysical fact.

Keep only the distinctions that survive simplification for an engineer—functional role, design specification, and part taxonomy—and drop those with no tracking consequence. Be explicit about misfires: for instance, with artifacts whose essential kind is ambiguous, or with entities that lack any persistent physical part. Then apply the procedure to three cases: (1) a physical server whose chassis and motherboard survive but all drives and memory are replaced; (2) a vehicle rebuilt around its original VIN and control software after its body is destroyed; and (3) a continuously running microservice whose underlying compute nodes are replaced every few minutes, leaving no enduring substrate. In at least one case, the procedure should yield an answer that conflicts with standard engineering intuition—state this plainly, and say whether that signals a flaw in the procedure or a genuine metaphysical tension that specs must accommodate. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need something operable without consulting texts.
