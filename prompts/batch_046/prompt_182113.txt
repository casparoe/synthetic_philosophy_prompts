I am drafting a fellowship proposal for a six-month project in the philosophy of mathematics. The domain is Benacerraf's dilemma, but I want to avoid the standard platonism-versus-nominalism framing that dominates introductory treatments. Instead, I want to pose the following precise question: does Benacerraf's epistemological challenge re-emerge for structuralist accounts of mathematics once we recognize that our actual access to mathematical structures is always mediated by formal systems, automated proof-checkers, or algorithmic representations rather than by direct cognitive contact? Call this the problem of mediated structuralism. The proposal should: (1) state the question with enough precision that a philosopher of science outside the subfield can see why it is not merely a replay of the original dilemma; (2) explain why existing structuralist responses—particularly those appealing to implicit definition, pattern recognition, or the dispensability of causal contact—fail to settle whether mediated access preserves genuine mathematical knowledge; (3) argue for a conceptual-philosophical method rather than an empirical or computer-science method, specifying why analyzing the epistemology of mediation requires philosophical clarification rather than testing actual algorithms; (4) describe what a successful result would look like—perhaps a taxonomy of mediation-types that either preserve or undermine structuralist knowledge-claims—and specify what would count as failure, such as the analysis collapsing into generic skepticism about all instrument-mediated science; (5) outline a realistic six-month scope, distinguishing work achievable in months one through three from the final deliverable, which should be a workshop-ready paper or journal-length draft; and (6) include a brief justification for why a funding body should support philosophical analysis of this specific mediation problem rather than empirical research on automated theorem proving. Do not request a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I will supply references myself. Write in formal, polished proposal language.
