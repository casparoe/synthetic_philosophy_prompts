I was chatting with a grad student after our metaphysics seminar and she summed up the Humean vs anti-Humean debate about laws like this: Humeans think laws are just the axioms of the best system that compresses all actual regularities, so there's no real modal force—laws don't govern, they just summarize what happens. Anti-Humeans, she said, hold that laws are real relations of necessitation existing independantly of description, like metaphysical glue holding things together. She added that Humeans therefore can't seperate laws from accidental generalizations except via simplicity and strength, while anti-Humeans must swallow mysterious necessary connections that violate Hume's fork. Then she concluded the whole thing is basically a stalemate between parsimony and metaphysical extravagence, since neither side can answer the other without giving up its core motivation. I feel like she's flattening the anti-Humean camp—maybe conflating Armstrong-style universals with dispositional essentialism?—and I'm not sure her claim that Humeans lack all modal resources is right either, given counterfactual versions of the best-system view. Could you audit this summary for me: which parts are fair, which are subtle misrepresentations or overstatements, and does her stalemate conclusion actually survive once we correct the errors?
