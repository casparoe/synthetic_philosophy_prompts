I am a sixth-form student preparing materials for an extended essay on whether practical stakes should alter what we take ourselves to know. Would you please construct a sorting quiz in the form of a decision tree that classifies philosophical positions on pragmatic encroachment and epistemic contextualism? The tree should begin with the question of whether the truth-conditions for "S knows that p" vary with the subject's practical situation, then branch according to whether any such variation is semantic (contextualism) or pragmatic (encroachment), and whether invariant knowledge can nevertheless coexist with stakes-sensitive requirements for action. Each terminal node should name a position — for instance, strict invariantism, epistemic contextualism, interest-relative invariantism, or pure pragmatic encroachment — and summarise in one sentence its most awkward or counter-intuitive commitment. To make the instrument genuinely diagnostic rather than a list of labels, please include a branch built around the following scenario I have devised: a forensic accountant notices a minor discrepancy in a client's ledger that is almost certainly a clerical error, but if it is fraud, exposing it will bankrupt the client and destroy her livelihood; the accountant must advise the board whether she "knows" the books are sound. The tree should separate positions that treat this as a deficit in knowledge from those that treat it merely as a deficit in warrant for action, and it should flag any combination of answers that currently has no named defender, with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Please present the result in British English, with clear branching logic, and do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation of sources.
