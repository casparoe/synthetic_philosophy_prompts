Construct a philosophical argument for the claim that a particular human person could have originated from a different sperm and egg than the ones that actually produced them, but do not base your case on the familiar modal intuition that their parents might have met at a different time or that conception might have occurred on a different day. Instead, build the argument around the thesis that diachronic personal identity is constituted by the continuity of a self-modeling predictive architecture—an integrated system of generative models that maintain a first-person perspective across time—rather than by the persistence of a specific biological substance or genetic sequence. Show how this architectural continuity is metaphysically compatible with a counterfactual origin in which a genetically distinct zygote develops functionally isomorphic architecture through convergent developmental pathways, and therefore why fixing the physical substrate at conception does not entail origin essentialism for persons. Then address the objection that any physical realization of that architecture necessarily inherits the causal-historical chain of its specific substrate, and explain why that chain, while fixing the token realization, does not fix the essential conditions of the person-type instantiated. Present the reasoning in American English, with explicit premises and conclusions, and avoid appeals to named authorities, specific texts, or bibliographic references.
