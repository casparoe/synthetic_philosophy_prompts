hey, been thinking about natural kinds lately — not the usual "is species real" debate but a specific pattern i keep running into. scientists split the world with some feature, then find loads of things that don't fit, and instead of dropping the split they just pile on exception clauses. "this is a metal except at nanoscale," "this counts as a predator except when it's symbiotic," that kinda thing. each clause is reasonable alone, but together they turn the category into a patchwork. what's weird is that people defend keeping it not by saying nature is genuinely fuzzy here, but by pointing to all the instruments, regulations, and papers already built around the word — like if you dropped "wetland" or "planet" you'd break too many other systems. i see this with "gene" too in some molecular contexts, where the definition gets stretched to cover weird cases not because biology demands it but because funding structures and databases depend on the term.

so is there an actual name for this failure mode, where a kind survives not because it carves nature at it's joints but because it's locked into a network of practical dependancies? i know about family resemblance, prototype theory, and Kuhn's paradigm stuff, but those feel too general — i'm asking about the specific case where the fuzziness is manufactured by institutional inertia rather than discovered in the world. if there's no established term, what should we call it, and how exactly do we distinguish it from a genuinely gradated natural kind or just ordinary polysemy? i don't want just a label; i want to know where the edges are and what it's definitely not.
