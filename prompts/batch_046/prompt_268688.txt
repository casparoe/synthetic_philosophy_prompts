Write a dialogue between two political theorists—one defending, one attacking whether long-run prosperity and public order can justify authoritarian governence, with China and Singapore as background examples. Don't just rehearse democracy-vs-autocracy talking points. Instead, build the argument around a made-up wrinkle I call the 'prosperity trap': imagine a regime delivers 8% growth for two decades, then slows to 2%, and citizens stop measuring themselves against their parents' poverty and start comparing themselves to democratic neighbors with lower growth but higher civil liberties. The defender should claim the boom years created a 'legitimacy reservoir' that justifies tightening control during stagnation. The critic should reply that this reservoir was never unconditional—it was indexed to continued outpreformance—and that pointing to democratic disorder (housing crises, polarization) doesn't justify authoritarian exception, it just lowers the bar for everyone. Make them actually engage: the defender concedes democratic disorder exists but says it proves democracies can't manage stagnation; the critic admits stagnation is brutal but asks why the regime gets to swap its justification from prosperity to order precisely when prosperity fails. Keep the tone casual, like a late-night argument over beers, not a seminar presentation. No citations, no reading lists—just the conceptual moves.
