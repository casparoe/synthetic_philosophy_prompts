Please argue that illusionism about phenomenal consciousness is epistemically self-defeating in a specific way that its defenders rarely address. The standard illusionist diagnosis takes our introspective reports of intrinsic, qualitative, non-functional properties as data requiring explanation—treating them as systematic errors. But if phenomenal properties are genuinely illusory, then the meta-cognitive judgments identifying them as illusions must themselves be products of that same unreliable introspective machinery. This generates a regress: either we trust introspection enough to establish the illusion, which should also license taking phenomenal reports at face value, or we distrust it globally, which removes the evidential basis for illusionism altogether. To block this, the illusionist needs a principled reason why second-order judgments about illusion escape the error theory applied to first-order phenomenal reports, without invoking phenomenal transparency in a question-begging manner.
