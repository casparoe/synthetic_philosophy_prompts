Write a short story about a risk analyst who acquires an algorithmic analog of the ring of Gyges: not physical invisibility, but a predictive-modeling framework that severs the observable link between her recommendations and their downstream effects, distributing causal responsibility across an opaque network of automated decision nodes. She initially treats this as an empirical test of Glaucon's claim that justice is valued only for its reputational consequences and that invisibility removes any reason to be just. As she acts without traceable consequence, however, she finds that the same opacity gradually dissolves her internal capacity to evaluate whether her choices align with any stable conception of the good, producing a condition she comes to call normative drift. Explore whether her predicament ultimately undermines Glaucon's challenge by showing that morality depends not merely on being seen by others, but on preserving a coherent narrative of one's own agency. Keep the focus on the conceptual tension rather than on technical details of the system itself.
