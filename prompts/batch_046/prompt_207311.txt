I was discussing with my friends over pizza about those AI safety reports where labs run long behavioral consistency checks on a model before release, like asking it the same ethical dilemmas hidden in different contexts to see if it's faking values. One friend said this treats the AI like a job applicant taking a personality test. But I was thinking about that idea I read somewhere, maybe Dennett, about counterfeit people — like if something acts exactly human but there's nothing going on inside. So here's what's confusing me: these audits check both external answers and sometimes internal weights or attention patterns, but they never do this for other consistant systems, like my grandpa's old chess program that always plays defensivly, or a book of philosophy that answers consistently if you ask it questions by searching the index.

What theory of personhood or consciousness does this practice actually need? Does it assume there's a hidden inner thing that behavior only tracks, which is what Dennett might deny? Or could a pure functionalist also justify these audits? And why do books and chess programs get left out — is the practice secretly assuming consciousness needs a specific kind of physical substrate, or is it just about tractability? I'm not in philosophy formally, just trying to understand if these labs are accidentally committed to some view they don't realize, and whether the practice is actually consistent with several different theories or only works under one.
