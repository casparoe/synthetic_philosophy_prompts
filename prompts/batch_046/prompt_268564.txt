Over time, however, she comes to see that the very relational framework she already values (her commitment to collaborative inquiry, for instance) makes acceptance of the disclosure norm rationally unavoidable, not because she acquires a new desire, but because rejecting it would undermine the coherence of commitments she already holds. Some philosophers describe this kind of shift as "structural embedding".

Write a dialogue between a committed Williamsian internalist and a defender of external reasons (in a broadly Parfitian or McDowellian vein) debating whether this "structural embedding" case genuinely collapses the internal/external distinction, reveals that internalism secretly depends on social ontology, or can be accommodated by internalism without conceding ground. Both interlocutors should be equally sharp; each should respond directly to the other’s actual points rather than talking past one another. The dialogue should explore whether the internalist can maintain that the reason remains "internal" once the embedding is acknowledged, and whether the externalist can avoid the charge of smuggling in a new motivational element.
