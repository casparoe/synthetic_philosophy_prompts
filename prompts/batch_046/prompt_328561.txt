Write a two-page proposal for a one-year dissertation fellowship in philosophy, framed for a committee familiar with Nietzsche scholarship but not with narrative-identity theory. The central question is this: Is the doctrine of eternal recurrence best understood as a cosmological claim, an ethical imperative, or a diachronic criterion for personal identity—and if the third reading is viable, does it dissolve the apparent contradiction between Nietzsche’s denial of a substantial self and the demanding nature of the recurrence “test”? State the problem precisely: the literature largely treats the cosmological and ethical interpretations as exhaustive, with occasional appeals to the “thought experiment” framing, yet all three tend to presuppose a pre-given subject who either does or does not recur. I want to argue that this presupposition is exactly what Nietzsche’s critique of substance undermines, and that recurrence should instead be read as constitutive of diachronic identity: a future life counts as “mine” not because it belongs to a persisting ego, but because it stands in a specific relation of affirmable repetition to my present practical orientation. The proposal should explain why the standard binary leaves this open, outline a method combining close conceptual analysis of relevant passages in the Nachlass—particularly where recurrence is linked to the will to power and to the rejection of substance metaphysics—with a targeted engagement with contemporary narrative-identity theories, specify what success would look like (for instance, showing that the identity-interpretation resolves textual tensions that defeat both cosmological and purely ethical readings), identify a clear failure condition (e.g., if the textual evidence forces recurrence back into an ethical register that still requires a substantial agent), and provide an honest scope assessment for a twelve-month project, including which months would be devoted to textual work versus conceptual reconstruction. Do not include a bibliography, literature review, or citation list; reference positions and arguments by name only, and note where citations will be added later. The tone should be formal, the framing should make clear why this is not a standard “Nietzsche and identity” project, and the risk assessment should be realistic rather than promotional.
