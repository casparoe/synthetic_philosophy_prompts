My roommate Jordan and his coworker Casey got into it at lunch over whether switching beef for chicken actually lowers total suffering if you include wild animals killed in crop farming. (1) Jordan said the standard advice ignores feed crops — chickens live on corn and soy, so cultivating that kills field mice and insects via pesticides and machinery, while beef cattle mostly graze grassland that wouldn't be farmed for crops anyway. So per calorie beef might cause less wild death. (2) Casey said that's comparing different kinds of harm — factory chickens are brought into existance specifically to suffer, you kill dozens per cow-equivalent, and their suffering is prolonged and intense, while wild crop deaths are mostly quick and involve animals with less capacity for suffering. Direct farm suffering should dominate. (3) Jordan then said something weirder: if beef demand falls, pasture can revert to wild habitat, boosting wild populations and thus wild suffering, while cutting chicken just shrinks crop acreage. So reducing beef could paradoxically increase suffering by growing wild ecosystems. (4) Casey replied that Jordan was being inconsistent — using wild suffering to defend beef but then treating habitat reversion as harmful — and that the rebound idea is mostly speculative, since old pasture usually becomes development or degraded land anyway. Also, Casey said, Jordan never addresses the moral difference between causing suffering by creating farmed animals and accidentally harming wild ones. They asked me to referee and I realy couldn't. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each point from clearly mistaken to mostly right, say who's right about what, and note what's missing from both sides.
