Write a dialogue between Riley and Jordan—both grad students in history who took way too many philosophy seminars—arguing about whether ranking historical atrocities is possible or just obscene. Riley thinks we can compare them if we stop treating "evil" as one big blob and instead break it into dimensions: raw scale, how bureacratic the normalization was (forms, schedules, office buildings involved), and whether the perpetrators cooked up an explicit ideological justification for dehumanization. Jordan thinks this is just putting suffering into a spreadsheet and argues every atrocity has its own historical singularity—its own texture, its own afterlife in survivors' communities, its own relationship to silence and memory—that makes cross-case comparison conceptually broken, not just rude. Make them actually fight it out: Riley should defend why bureaucratic normalization counts as a moral dimension and not just sociology, and Jordan should say whether "historical singularity" is a real claim about events or just fancy talk for discomfort. Have them clash over whether refusing to rank actually protects victims or just lets governments avoid hard choices about prevention priorities. Keep it casual, like their talking in a campus cafe, and include a moment where they talk past each other then catch it. Let a typo or two slip in—maybe Jordan writes "their" when they mean "there". Don't ask for sources or reading lists; just have them argue from the concepts.
