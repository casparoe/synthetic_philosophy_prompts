Below are notes for an essay arguing that global workspace theory (GWT) cannot accommodate phenomenal consciousness without reducing it to access consciousness, because its defenders conflate two separate processing breakdowns. I distinguish broadcast failure, in which a representation never reaches the global workspace, from integration failure, in which a representation reaches the workspace but fails to bind with the self-model that makes reportability possible. I propose that phenomenal consciousness tracks resistance to integration failure rather than successful global broadcast, and that this permits a modified view—what I call a federated workspace model—in which local recurrent loops within partially overlapping workspaces achieve a minimal integration threshold without requiring a single global stage. This framing, I suggest, can explain overflow phenomena without positing globally broadcast but unreported content. I ask whether the distinction between broadcast and integration failure is conceptually coherent, whether the federated model genuinely differs from standard local-recurrence accounts, whether it avoids the usual objections to higher-order theories, and whether there is an obvious empirical or conceptual counterexample I have overlooked. Please comment specifically on the accuracy of the argument, whether the federated framing is interesting or merely redundant, and where the reasoning is weakest.
