I recently learned through genetic testing that I was conceived via in vitro fertilization, and that the embryo actually implanted was not the one my parents intended but a donated embryo with entirely different genetic parents—though I was gestated, born, and raised by the couple I have always called my parents. This discovery has made the metaphysical question of origin essentialism urgently personal. Write an essay addressing whether my identity is essentially tied to my actual genetic origin: specifically, could I have come from a different sperm and egg and still been the very same person I am now? Weigh the intuitive argument that biological genesis is essential to who I am—such that being formed from different gametes would have produced a different individual—against the competing view that personal identity tracks psychological continuity, relational history, or the narrative coherence of a life rather than the particular fusion of cells that initiated it. Analyze the concrete scenario of an unintended embryo transfer: if the embryo that developed into me carried the genes of strangers rather than my intended genetic parents, does that entail that I am literally not the person I would have been had the correct embryo been implanted, or does it merely show that I have different biological ancestors while remaining one continuous individual? Reach and defend a definite conclusion—either a qualified, restricted form of origin essentialism that allows genetic contingency without destroying identity, or a rejection of genetic essentialism in favor of a non-biological criterion—and justify it by explaining what genuinely constitutes being this particular person, given that the biological starting point now appears to have been contingent.
