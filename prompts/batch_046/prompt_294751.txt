ok so i've been trying to work out what i actually think about family and role obligations, going by gut feeling on cases, trying to see if confucian role ethics actually fits my instincts. here's where i land:

- driving across town to help my dad mend his fence after he was rude to me at christmas: fine, thats what a child does.
- telling my sister her new haircut is decent when its actually awful, because shes already had a rubbish day: fine.
- covering for my old mentor at work by fudging a report that'd get clients in trouble, just because he trained me like a father: not fine.
- keeping quiet to the police that my brother scratched a car and drove off, because hes family and it was an accident: feels fine? but then i look at the mentor one and they seem the same.
- telling my dying aunt that my estranged cousin sent her love, when he hasnt: not fine, even though itd comfort her.
- telling my friend their business idea is brilliant when i think itll flop: not fine.
- writing on a form that ive lived here five years when its only four, to get the council flat: not fine, but somehow less wrong than the mentor cover-up?

theres also this one i keep flipping on: telling my young kid that grandads cancer is just 'old man tiredness' so she dont panic. part of me says thats exactly what a parent should do — shield the vulnerable — but another part says its the same kind of lie as the aunt one, and that should be not fine.

my half-baked idea is that the rule is about 'protecting the bond, not the person' — you lie or cover up when it stops harm coming into the relationship from outside, but not when it lets someone dodge the consequences of failing inside their role. so shielding my kid is okay (outside harm: fear), but covering my brother is not okay (he failed in his role as a responsible driver), and covering my mentor is wrong because he failed in his duty of care to clients. does that hold up? is there a sharper principle that gets all these, or close? what does it say about the grandad lie? and am i being inconsistent on the brother vs mentor pair — do i have to drop one of those verdicts?

also i keep wondering if theres something about 'vertical' vs 'horizontal' roles — parents to children, children to parents — that explains why the sister haircut is okay but the friend business plan isnt. but i cant quite nail it.

so: whats the principle, which verdicts survive, and where does my tentative one break?
