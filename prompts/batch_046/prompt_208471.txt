I was arguing with a friend about whether giving an artificial system genuine feeling would change what kind of thing it is, and he answered with the old School of Names line that "a white horse is not a horse," insisting it proves that adding any property dissolves category membership. I lack the training to tell if he is misusing an ancient logical puzzle or hitting on something real. Could you separate the precise theses that single sentence might express—about extension versus intension, about whether properties are parts of things, about how names latch onto the world, about logical form, and about whether color and kind belong to different ontological registers—rating each as trivial, defensible, or plainly false? Then take a definite stand, with reasons rather than a balanced menu, on which interpretation is philosophically serious and whether any of them actually supports his claim that altering one feature breaks kind-identity.
