Say Kant was alive today and had to deal with factory farming, lab-grown meat, and geneticly editing livestock so they lack nociception. He'd still hold that animals arent ends-in-themselves and that cruelty is wrong only because it corrupts human moral character—indirect duty, not direct. So: if no animal actually suffers, does his indirect-duty argument collapse? Or does he think treating beings that could suffer as pure resources is still corrupting even if they dont feel it? And where exactly does he draw the line—would he allow engineered insensitivity for food but not for sport? Predict his actual reasoning, dont just summarize the old texts.
