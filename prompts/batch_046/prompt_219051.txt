I keep noticing this pattern after reading some of Moore's ethics stuff about friendship and art being good just in themselves, not for anything they lead to. Someone claims friendship or beautiful art is valuable intrinsicly, not instrumentally. Then you point to a messy case: two people who are genuinely close but only because they help each other lie and cover tracks, or a painting thats absolutly stunning but made specificaly to glorify something horrific. Instead of saying "yeah maybe intrinsic value can sit inside bad contexts," the defender says "well thats not true friendship, its just mutual corruption" or "thats not real beauty, its just surface manipulation." So the claim survives, but the category gets smaller every time you test it. Its kind of like No True Scotsman except applied to goods rather than groups, and not quite circular reasoning since each redefinition sounds plausible on its own. Does this move have an established name in value theory? And if not, can you name it and say exactly where the line is between legitimate sharpening of a concept and just protecting the claim? I dont want a bare label - I want to know what makes it distinct and where its edges are.
