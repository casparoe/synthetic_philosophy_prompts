Write a short story about Mira and Jonah, a couple in 2047 who can tweak their embryo's affective baseline. They have to choose between having a child who'd live with a constant, faint existential itch — not unbearable, but definitely not flourishing — or staying childless. Their counsellor frames it using the procreation asymmetry: creating the itchy kid is wrong because you'd be causing harm, but not creating a happy kid is fine because there's no specific person you're depriving. Mira finds this weird because she feels like she's failing someone either way, and Jonah point's out that if they do have the itchy kid, she can't complain she was harmed since she wouldn't exist otherwise. I want the story to sit with that tension — the non-identity problem bumping up against the asymmetry — without resolving it neatly. Make it feel lived-in, not like a philosophy essay in disguise. Keep it human, no sci-fi gimmicks beyond the embryo tech, and maybe let one of them mix up 'impersonal' with 'interpersonal' in a way that shows they're not philosophers, just people trying to think straight.
