I'm writing this for my political philosophy course. I live in a cooperative house governed by majority vote with pooled finances for shared infrastructure. A quarter of the residents—the east wing—want to split off and form their own cooperative. They don't invoke any distinct culture or history; instead they say that being permanently outvoted on maintenance priorities and house rules means they are governed without real consent, even though they signed the charter when they moved in. The rest of us reply that letting a subgroup leave whenever it loses a vote would destroy collective decision-making, and that splitting the building's shared heating and mortgage obligations would unfairly burden those who remain. I'm genuinely unsure: does persistent democratic minority status by itself create a right to exit, or does that turn ordinary political disappointment into a license to dissolve institutions? And does the original agreement to join bind them even if they now experience the arrangement as a form of domination?
