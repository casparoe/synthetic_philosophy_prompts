Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Mara and Eli, about Nietzsche's diet and health stuff. Mara thinks his rules — avoiding heavy German food and beer, preferring small Italian-style meals, constantly tweaking air and digestion — aren't just coping with migraines but form what she calls a "gastric genealogy," using digestion to trace whether a culture has moral vitality or creeping resentment. Eli says that's romanticizing a chronically ill man's practical adjustments, and that modern gut-brain science actually undermines his vitalism instead of confirming it. Make them actually talk back: Mara should invoke his idea that teh body holds more reason than our best wisdom, Eli should counter that reducing values to stomach chemistry is exactly the mechanistic explanation Nietzsche himself hated. Keep both sharp, no strawmen, and leave it unresolved.
