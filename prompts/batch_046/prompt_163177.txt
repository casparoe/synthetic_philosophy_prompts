Write a short story that dramatizes the conflict between collective memory and documented history, centered on a small maritime community whose founding ritual celebrates an ancestral “first sighting” of a comet that archival astronomical logs show occurred decades before their settlement. Invent and deploy two conceptual frames within the narrative: “mnemonic sovereignty,” meaning the community’s authoritative right to maintain the past that sustains its present moral and social bonds, and “epistemic custodianship,” meaning the duty of record-keepers to preserve documentary truth irrespective of its social disruption. Through at least two characters—one defending the ritual memory and one defending the archives—explore whether either principle should yield when they conflict, without resolving the tension into a clear moral victory. After the story, include a brief note identifying the points where the narrative takes liberties with established philosophical accounts of collective memory (such as the idea that social groups reconstruct the past to serve present needs) and indicating which interpretations are contested; in that same note, state your confidence level for each claim and explicitly single out the claim about which you are least certain. Present the story first, with no introductory framing, no commentary before it, and no restatement of the prompt; begin immediately with the narrative.
