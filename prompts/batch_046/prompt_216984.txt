I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher defending a reconstructed Cartesian account of animal minds. She argues that Descartes's bête-machine thesis is usually misread as denying animals all affective life, when in fact he distinguishes mechanical sentiment—non-conscious bodily affect—from the conscious passion available only to res cogitans. On her reading, the real Cartesian criterion is not mere behavioral flexibility but a specific kind of compositional, universal reason revealed through language use; recent comparative-cognition work on corvids and cetaceans therefore misses the point, because it measures problem-solving adaptability rather than the open-ended rational universality Descartes took to require an immaterial soul. She also maintains that attributing mechanistic sensation to animals does not smuggle consciousness back in, since sensation for Descartes is a purely corporeal mode of the pineal gland's interaction with the body, lacking the reflexive awareness that defines human feeling.

Write me eight to ten interview questions that press well beyond a restatement of the automaton view. I want at least one question on how her reconstructed distinction between mechanistic sentiment and conscious passion avoids circularly defining consciousness in terms of the very rational capacities it is meant to explain; at least one on whether using linguistic-compositional behavior as evidence for an immaterial soul risks circularity, given that the soul is then invoked to explain that very behavior; and at least one on what her view says about animals that appear to exhibit compositional or referential communication—include for each a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. End with a question asking what specific empirical finding about animal cognition or neurobiology could genuinely threaten her position rather than being absorbed as merely another layer of mechanistic complexity.
