Here are my gut reacions on a few cases involving vague belief, and I want to see if there's a single principle that holds them together. First, if I'm about 60-70 percent sure it'll rain, I grab an umbrella but I wouldn't take even odds on a bet about it. Second, if a clinical trial reports efficacy somewhere between thirty and eighty percent, I think it's rational to withhold both approval and outright rejection, yet I still feel like I have a definite attitude rather than none at all. Third, when one epidemiologist gives point probabilities and another gives wide intervals, I instinctively trust the interval guy more, but I also think he ought to be able to recommend a policy. Fourth, when new data shrinks my interval, it feels like I'm learning something about the world, not just updating my mental state. Can one account of imprecise credences save all four intuitions, or do I have to throw one overboard? And whatever principle you land on, tell me what it says about a case I haven't mentioned: what if my interval is symmetric but I still have a gut feeling that the lower end is more plausible?
