From the perspective of a journalist covering science and ideas: Compare Weber’s claim that ascetic Protestant values held an elective affinity with early capitalist economic behavior—treating culture as an independent motivational force—with the analytical distinction in philosophy of social science between causal-mechanistic explanations and constitutive explanations. Assess whether Weber’s thesis demonstrates that culture can literally cause economic transformation, or whether it only establishes that capitalist practices were constituted by particular meanings that rendered them intelligible. Clearly separate what is settled, such as Weber’s explicit rejection of monocausal economic determinism, from what is genuinely contested among specialists in social explanation—particularly whether values can operate as autonomous causal variables rather than as post-hoc rationalizations. Reach a reasoned verdict on whether culture can be a cause in a robust, non-metaphorical sense, and avoid presenting any contested interpretive position as the consensus view.
