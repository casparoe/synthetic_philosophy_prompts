Fitch starts from the knowability principle, which says that for any true proposition there is some possible world in which a rational agent knows it. He then adds a closure condition—if you know a conjunction, you know each conjunct. From these two premises he derives that every true proposition is actually known right now, which is absurd since we clearly do not know everything.
