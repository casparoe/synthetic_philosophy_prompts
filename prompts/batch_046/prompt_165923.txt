Consider the argument—call it the Arithmetic Transmission Argument—that dialetheism about the Liar paradox cannot be rationally localized to semantics. The reasoning runs as follows: if the Liar sentence "This sentence is false" is genuinely both true and false, then by constructing a Gödel-style self-referential arithmetical sentence that encodes this very contradiction, the contradiction is transmitted into Peano Arithmetic. Consequently, an arithmetic statement corresponding to the Liar is both true and false, yielding something like 0 = 1 being true and false together. A dialetheist who accepts the semantic contradiction but rejects the arithmetic one therefore owes a principled, non-ad-hoc boundary; absent such a boundary, accepting dialetheism in the semantic domain rationally commits one to accepting true contradictions in arithmetic.

Give me your credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that this argument succeeds in undermining dialetheism—specifically, that no adequate localization principle exists and the transmission to arithmetic is unavoidable. Decompose your reasoning into four sub-questions and assign each a rough probability:

(i) That a Gödel-style embedding can genuinely preserve literal contradiction in arithmetic rather than merely represent it meta-linguistically.

(ii) That a dialetheist lacks a principled, non-ad-hoc boundary that permits semantic contradictions while blocking arithmetic ones.

(iii) That an arithmetic contradiction carries rational costs severe enough to make its acceptance a decisive reason to abandon dialetheism altogether.

(iv) That there are no independent, overriding reasons to maintain dialetheism—such as its explanatory power over hierarchical or contextualist alternatives—even if transmission occurs.

Show how these combine to yield your overall number. Then identify which sub-question your credence is most sensitive to, and specify what kind of argument or conceptual clarification would most move your estimate on that point.
