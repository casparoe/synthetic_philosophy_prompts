Write a poem in blank verse, exactly eighteen lines, spoken in the voice of the prisoner who has escaped Plato’s cave, seen the sun and the Form of the Good, and returned to describe what he saw. Before line ten, every line must contain at least one word drawn from the vocabulary of the prison—shadow, chain, wall, echo, flicker, dark—used to evoke the world above; after line ten, those same six words must recur, but now applied to the speaker’s own speech, so that the language of the cave infects his attempt to name what lies outside. The turn at line ten should mark not the captives’ refusal to listen but the speaker’s discovery that the forms resist translation into the medium of shadows. End without resolution: the speaker remains linguistically chained, still describing the sun in terms of firelight. Render the philosophical point precisely—this is a failure of vocabulary, not of will—so that someone who knows the allegory recognizes the inversion, and someone who does not still encounters a coherent poem about the impossibility of speaking the unshadowed.
