I serve on the governing council of a century-old cooperative that recently digitized its inventory system, leaving an old handwritten sign-out ledger technically redundant. At our last meeting, a motion to abolish the ledger was countered with an appeal to Chesterton’s fence—specifically, that an inherited practice should not be dismantled until one understands the purpose it originally served. A colleague dismissed this as status-quo bias in philosophical dress, and I want to return with conceptual precision rather than biography. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan might express, arranged as a menu from the tautological to the strongly conservative? For each reading, please say whether it is defensible, whether it genuinely applies to our ledger rule, and whether it actually distinguishes prudent caution from an illegitimate appeal to tradition. I need this as a disambiguation I can use in a practical vote, not a history of Chesterton’s writings or a bibliography.
