I want to center the session on a framing I’m developing: that historicity is not a binary switch but a "double displacement," occurring first when a narrative stops functioning as a living communal identity-marker, and second when it acquires an independent material or institutional anchor that no longer depends on the narrative for coherence. Plan the explanatory arc: what conceptual distinction must be in place before this framing is even intelligible, which of the three cases should carry the central comparison and what it must have and lack to do so, the misconceptions the group will predictably bring (for instance, that absence of archaeology proves pure myth, that religious texts are automatically non-historical, or that Arthur is fiction solely because he is magical), the precise point in the sequence where each misconception should be defused, a concrete end-of-session check that reveals whether they have grasped the double-displacement logic rather than merely ranking the cases by plausibility, and which layer of the specialist debate I should explicitly set aside and name as beyond this level.
