Researchers debating whether the world is becoming less violent repeatedly reach the same impasse: new corrections to historical homicide or war-death data shift both sides' numbers without changing anyone's conclusion, because one camp treats violence as direct, intentional physical harm measured per capita, while the other includes structural, institutional, and self-directed harms measured in absolute terms or weighted by severity. My working hypothesis is that this is not mainly an empirical dispute but a disagreement about whether "violence" names a single natural kind with a unified metric, compounded by a rarely noted statistical coupling—population growth and violence-reduction are both outputs of the same institutional and technological shifts, so per-capita normalization is endogenous rather than an independent denominator. Diagnose the stall: is it verbal, substantive but hidden, ontological, or bedrock intuitive? What exactly would have to be shown—a conceptual distinction, a formal result, or an empirical finding—to move either side? And is there genuinely a fact of the matter about whether violence is declining, or does the answer depend on a framing choice that cannot be adjudicated?
