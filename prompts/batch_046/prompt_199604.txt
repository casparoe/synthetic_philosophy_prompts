I am a software engineer without any formal training in philosophy, and I am trying to determine whether a technical disagreement I have with a colleague over resource allocation conceals a deeper conceptual problem.

We operate a shared GPU cluster serving two categories of workload: interactive development and data-exploration sessions used by human engineers, and a single automated optimisation pipeline maintained by our research division. To guide our scheduler, we compute a composite 'system value' score that weights throughput, projected revenue, and user-reported satisfaction. For interactive users, satisfaction is captured on a 1–10 self-report scale; for the pipeline, value is derived from estimated commercial impact. Our aggregation formula treats one satisfaction point as equivalent to approximately £4,000 of pipeline revenue. Under this metric, the pipeline produces about forty times the value per GPU-hour that any interactive session produces.

My colleague argues that the scheduler should therefore assign nearly all capacity to the pipeline. He maintains that maximising aggregate system value is the correct design objective, and that reserving significant capacity for lower-yield interactive users — simply to preserve their access — is rationally equivalent to discarding value. I feel there is something deeply wrong with allowing one process, which has no subjective experience and does not suffer from deprivation, to monopolise resources merely because its output scores highly on an aggregation formula that mixes incommensurable measures. Yet I cannot articulate a precise objection: if the metric genuinely captures forty times the benefit per unit, on what principled ground can I resist concentrating everything there?

I am not seeking coding guidance. Instead, I want to understand whether this scenario exemplifies a recognised philosophical tension about extreme disparities in productivity and the justification for near-total resource concentration. Please explain the relevant conceptual framework, evaluate my colleague's argument, and indicate whether my resistance has a rational basis or is only an unexamined intuition. Please write your response in British English, in a formal and polished style.
