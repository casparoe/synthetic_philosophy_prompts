I have spent fifteen years practicing a very specific form of structured dialogue facilitation: I always open with the same three questions in the same order, allow exactly two minutes of uninterrupted response per participant, and never intervene until the sequence is complete. During a recent session with a grieving family in crisis, the second participant began to weep and ask for immediate comfort; I recognized the need to abandon the structure, yet I completed the full sequence before responding, later explaining that breaking my own method would have violated my integrity as a practitioner. A colleague argued that this was not autonomy but mechanical rigidity—a script executed regardless of context—while my supervisor insisted that such unwavering procedural commitment is precisely what distinguishes a free, self-governing agent from someone who merely reacts to circumstances. I am unsure whether my inability to deviate was a failure of will or its highest expression. Should I treat this rigidity as a defect to be overcome through greater flexibility, or is there a philosophical defense of such mechanical consistency as actually constitutive of agency?
