Write a six-month dissertation prospectus proposal in the history of philosophy of science, focused on Darwin’s views on morality and religion, with no bibliography, citation list, or literature-review-by-author requested (I will supply references myself). Frame the project around this precise question: Darwin argues in The Descent of Man that moral feeling arises from social instincts shaped by natural selection, yet he also holds that the highest moral acts often run against immediate evolutionary interest for the community’s good; separately, he explains religious belief—especially belief in a beneficent Creator—as an evolutionary byproduct of over-extending causal and agency attribution to unknown forces. Standard treatments keep these as separate domains, evolutionary ethics on one side and evolutionary psychology of religion on the other. The proposal should ask whether the two domains are in tension because they rely on incompatible criteria for the reliability of evolved cognition: if Darwin trusts moral judgments despite their evolutionary origin, on what principled ground can he dismiss religious judgments that arise from similarly evolved mechanisms? Or, conversely, if evolutionary origin undermines religious authority, does it not also erode moral authority?
