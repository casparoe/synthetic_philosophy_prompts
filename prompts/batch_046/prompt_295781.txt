Her thesis is that Borges's denial of linear succession should be read not as idealism in the Berkeleyan mold, nor as a revival of Zeno's paradoxes, but rather as an implicit doctrine she terms indexical presentism: temporal ordering is a feature of narrative indexing rather than of events themselves. She further claims that this reconstruction exposes an unresolved tension with the branching-future ontology implied in Borges's fiction, where alternate timelines appear genuinely open rather than merely differently indexed. Please draft six to eight interview questions that press directly on this tension. I want at least two questions that ask how she distinguishes indexical presentism from standard B-theoretic eternalism and from modal-realist interpretations of Borges's fictional worlds; at least one question that asks what her reconstruction implies for Borges's well-known invocations of Schopenhauer and Berkeley; and at least one question that anticipates her likely deflection — that the tension dissolves once we recognize that fictional worlds operate under different indexing rules — together with a sharp follow-up designed to make that deflection harder to maintain. The questions should turn on the actual content of her position rather than on biography or bibliography, and they should avoid asking for citations, reading lists, or references to obscure secondary literature.
