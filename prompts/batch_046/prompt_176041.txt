At a bar last night my friends Riley and Sam got into it over consequentialism being "too demanding", but they werent talking about donating kidneys -- they were arguing about "moral sabbaticals". Riley said if you're a real act-consequentialist you can't take a year off to paint or whatever, because every day you aren't maxing out welfare is a day you're letting avoidable suffering happen. Sam replied thats wrong: consequentialism only cares about outcomes, so if burning out now makes you useless later then taking a break is literally required. Riley shot back that Sam just smuggled in a deontological "right to rest" by framing it as instrumental, and that pure consequentialism doesn't care about your burnout unless the net harm is lower -- which it usually isnt if you could be working now. Then Sam brought up "moral credit": if you've donated 40% of your income for ten years, dont you have a ledger that buys you some slack? Riley said ledgers are rule-utilitarianism in disguise, act-consequentialism doesnt do banking. They asked me to call it and I genuinely cant. Please go point by point: is Riley right that breaks are always unjustified unless they reduce harm? Is Sam right that credit can exist inside act-consequentialism? And what are they both missing about collective action or partial compliance?
