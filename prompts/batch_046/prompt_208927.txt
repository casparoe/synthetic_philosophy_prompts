I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of law, and I serve on the Independent Commission reviewing capital sentencing policy. We must recommend to Parliament whether to retain, suspend, or abolish the death penalty for aggravated homicide, with our report due before the summer recess. Please draft the Commission's formal written opinion: it should state a concrete recommendation with an implementation time frame, engage seriously with the retributive argument that execution is proportionate to the gravest wrongs, and treat deterrence claims with appropriate scepticism. I wish the reasoning to centre on a conceptual distinction I have developed—an 'irreversibility asymmetry' between capital punishment and other sanctions: because execution eliminates the possibility of correcting judicial error, it imposes an epistemic burden analogous to the prohibition on irreversible experimental interventions when measurement uncertainty is non-negligible. The opinion should apply this framing rigorously but without claiming scientific authority over moral questions, and it must address the strongest objection from the opposing view. Please write in formal British English, suitable for parliamentary circulation.
