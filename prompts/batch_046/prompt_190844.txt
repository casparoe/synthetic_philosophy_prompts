For a seminar on medieval philosophy of mind I have been asked to write down my commitments regarding Avicenna's flying-man argument so that my interlocutors can stress-test their consistency. Here is the profile: I take the suspended-in-void scenario to show that first-person awareness of oneself as a subject is non-derivative—it does not depend on perceptual acquaintance with one's body or on any particular sensory modality being active; I also hold that personal identity over time is constituted by psychological continuity that is essentially embedded in a biological life-history, so that there is no pure ego that could persist independently of some embodied narrative; I think phenomenal concepts are irreducibly first-personal and that this first-personal character explains the explanatory gap between physical descriptions and conscious experience, but I deny that such access delivers ontological insight into what kind of substance one is; I regard the flying-man scenario as genuinely conceivable, and I treat conceivability—though fallible and defeasible—as a real constraint on modal claims about the self; I reject Cartesian substance dualism, yet I maintain that the persistence conditions for self-consciousness are not strictly identical to the persistence conditions of the organism, so that the structure of self-reference could in principle be sustained without the original biological body; and I read Avicenna's argument narrowly, as an internal critique of empiricist theories of self-knowledge that fail to account for pre-reflective self-awareness, rather than as a positive proof of an immaterial soul. Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how severely. For each tension, rank it by seriousness, say what the cheapest revision would be, and note what that revision costs; also identify any pairing that looks contradictory but dissolves on closer inspection. Finally, tell me which single commitment I should drop or weaken to clean up the greatest number of conflicts, and exactly what I would have to give up in doing so.
