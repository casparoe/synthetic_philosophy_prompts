Then write a decision tree—not a prose comparison—that sorts a thoughtful reader from the entry commitment “The situationist experiments show that behavior varies more with situation than with enduring character” to one of eight terminal positions. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate given the branch structure, and explain what additional distinction would be needed to split them. Inject one novel conceptual distinction—clearly defined inside the tree itself—to sharpen the taxonomy beyond standard textbook divisions. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not depend on obscure figures, highly specialized literature, or specific experimental papers; build the tree from the logical architecture of the debate, using only concepts that a well-trained philosopher could reconstruct without external sources.
