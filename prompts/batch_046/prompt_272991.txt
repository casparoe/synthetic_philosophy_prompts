I serve as the director of a small curatorial team at a regional museum. Last quarter, the city announced an impending budget restructuring that will eliminate several positions in our division, though the specific roles affected have not been disclosed and I have no formal authority over the final decision. In response, I have begun holding additional staff meetings to help team members revise their portfolios, informally lobbying city administrators for transparency, and attempting to maintain my own emotional equilibrium by reminding myself that the budget outcome is not within my direct control. A mentor in museum leadership praised this as ethical stewardship, while a friend who recently completed a modern Stoic practice course argued that I am treating my staff's employment status—something determined by politicians and accounting offices—as if it were my own personal responsibility, essentially blurring the line between my actions and outcomes that depend entirely on others. I am torn between seeing my efforts as virtuous fulfillment of my professional role and seeing them as a rationalization of my inability to accept what I cannot change. How should I understand the boundary between responsible action in one's social role and the acceptance of outcomes that lie completely outside one's influence? Please answer in American English.
