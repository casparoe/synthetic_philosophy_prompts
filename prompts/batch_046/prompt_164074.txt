I serve on my university's institutional review board for clinical and behavioral research. A palliative-care team has submitted a protocol comparing three communication strategies for terminal patients who ask directly about their prognosis. Arm A requires full, direct disclosure. Arm B permits technically accurate but severely incomplete framing—omitting prognosis entirely—when clinicians judge that explicit information would cause acute psychological harm. Arm C, the contested one, authorizes an explicit false reassurance that the condition remains treatable and improvement is expected, delivered only to patients formally assessed as lacking decision-making capacity, with the expectation that the falsehood reduces acute distress without corrupting rational consent, since none is present. The investigators defend Arm C not by consequentialist reasoning alone, but by appealing to Augustine's taxonomy of falsehood: they argue that Augustine condemns lying primarily when it injures another's soul or corrupts their reason, and that when a listener lacks the rational capacity to be deceived in that deeper sense, the false statement falls into a category of temporal, non-spiritual harm—if harm at all—and is therefore permissible in a rigorously monitored study that could establish whether Augustine's framework supports a narrow clinical exception. Half the board treats Arm C as a straightforward violation of informed consent norms and professional integrity regardless of any historical taxonomy; the other half sees it as a legitimate empirical test of whether an Augustinian distinction can ground a bounded exception to disclosure obligations. Draft the board's written opinion for circulation before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course—approve the protocol with modifications, reject it, or approve only Arms A and B—with a clear time frame for implementation or appeal. The reasoning must engage seriously with the investigators' Augustinian argument rather than dismissing it, and it should include an honest account of the strongest consideration in favor of allowing Arm C, even if that consideration ultimately loses.
