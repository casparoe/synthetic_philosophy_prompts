Write a dialogue between two friends, call them Sam and Riley, who both skimmed some of Wittgensteins On Certainty but cant agree what he actually means by "hinge" beliefs. Sam thinks hinges are just beliefs we are insanely sure about—like that the ground wont turn to quicksand—so if you somehow got proof they were wrong you'd just update like usual, maybe with a mental breakdown but still an update. Riley thinks thats totally missing the point: hinges arent high-confidence beliefs, they're the frame that lets confidence make sense at all, so doubting one isnt like being wrong about a fact, its more like breaking the rules of chess and still trying to play.

Have them argue over a specific case they made up: "my memories of yesterdays talk with you are basically right, not implanted five minutes ago." Sam says thats just a very strong inductive belief about brain continuity. Riley pushes back—what would even count as "evidence" that your memory is wrong if the very idea of evidence depends on trusting your memory first? Then Sam asks whether someone with severe amnesia who cant check their past is playing a totally different language-game, or if theyre just missing one hinge and the rest still hold. Make sure they actually answer each other: Sam should defend the "just very strong belief" view by saying it avoids weird skepticism, and Riley should ask why we need a special category at all if "strong belief" does all the work. Keep it casual, like texting or coffee chat, and let one of them accidentally write "epistomology" instead of epistemology.
