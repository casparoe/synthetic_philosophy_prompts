For this entry, I want a deadpan satirical piece that operates from inside a philosophical position rather than mocking it from the outside. Write an internal corporate handbook—approximately 600 words, in the flat, jargon-heavy register of HR and facilities-management documentation—for a fictional consultancy called NullPoint Consulting that has adopted what it calls “Second-Order Tranquilism” as its sole organizational ethos. The target of the satire is the tranquilist claim that only the absence of craving and suffering possesses normative weight, while achievement, satisfaction, flourishing, and even purposeful activity are not neutral but represent unextinguished disturbances that must be systematically eliminated. The manual should be fully committed: every policy recommendation should follow with bureaucratic rigor from the premise that value is exclusively negative. Exaggerate real features of the view—its reduction of evaluation to suffering-avoidance, its tendency to treat all desire as a kind of malfunction, and its difficulty in guiding action without a positive baseline—rather than inventing caricatures with no philosophical echo.

To keep the prompt niche yet fully comprehensible without external lookup, inject several invented conceptual apparatuses: a “Craving-Abatement Protocol” that taxonomizes employee ambition into tiers of pathological intensity; a “Suffering-Neutral Workspace Architecture” that eliminates competitive signage, stimulating color palettes, recognition boards, and natural light variability in favor of uniform null-state environments; and an annual corporate observance called “Null Day,” on which all projects are suspended to celebrate the dissolution of organizational purpose. The standard objections should be legible without being stated: whether an agent can experience the absence of craving if all agency has been abated; whether a purely negative metric can justify any particular allocation of resources; and whether the consultants themselves harbor an unacknowledged craving for theoretical purity. Maintain sustained deadpan irony throughout—no winking, no explicit moral commentary—so that a specialist recognizes the satire as fair and a careful reader can still tell exactly what is being ironized.

This appended note should be the only place where qualification or distance appears; inside the manual itself, remain unhedged and committed. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for further sources, as this prompt is intended for evaluation without web access. Use precise conceptual vocabulary—reference “negative axiological asymmetry,” “the null-state baseline,” and “desire-cessation metrics”—but do not place quotation marks around any passage drawn from an actual text; if you reference a specific claim, paraphrase it without attribution marks.
