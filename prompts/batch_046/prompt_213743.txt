After a long and somewhat exhausting argument last night with a close friend about how rational belief should work across centered worlds, I have been trying to write down exactly where I stand before our next conversation. I want to check whether my commitments are jointly tenable, because I felt I was being pulled in contradictory directions without being able to name the conflict.

Here are the positions I find myself holding:

(i) In the canonical Sleeping Beauty scenario—Sunday coin toss, single Monday awakening if heads, Monday and Tuesday awakenings with amnesia if tails—Beauty's rational credence upon awakening that the coin landed heads should be 1/3. I take this to be the correct application of self-locating conditionalization: she should partition her epistemic space into three centered possibilities—(Monday, Heads), (Monday, Tails), and (Tuesday, Tails)—and assign them equal prior weight.

(ii) I also endorse a strict version of what I call indexical insulation: in general, acquiring purely indexical information—facts about where or when one is located within a world—should never, by itself, change one's rational credence about non-indexical, world-level states of affairs, unless there is an explicit bridge principle linking the two domains.

(iii) At the same time, I think that in duplication or copy cases—where a deterministic process might create N qualitatively identical agents—each copy should, upon learning that she is one of the copies, update her belief about whether the duplication process occurred by treating her centered location ("I am copy k") as evidence about the total number of copies, and thereby about the world-level frequency of the process.

(iv) I hold that the proper semantics for self-locating belief involves evaluating propositions relative to a centered world ⟨w, a, t⟩, but I deny that this semantic framework automatically licenses any particular update rule; in particular, I reject the idea that centered-worlds semantics by itself entails the thirder answer in Beauty.

(v) Finally, I maintain that evolutionary or cognitive debunking arguments against our modal or probabilistic intuitions in these cases fail, because the intuitions are arrived at through conceptual analysis rather than evolved heuristics, and therefore reflective equilibrium remains the appropriate methodological check.

My friend insisted that (i) and (ii) are flatly incompatible, since in Beauty the coin result is a world-level fact that is being updated solely through indexical awakening information. I tried to respond that the experimental protocol establishes a statistical correlation, but I am not sure whether that saves (ii) or violates its spirit. Meanwhile, I worry that (iii) relies on exactly the kind of indexical-to-world-level inference that (ii) forbids, though perhaps the correlation structure is different. And I wonder whether (iv) undermines my defense of (i), since if the semantics do not entail the update rule, I might need an independent justification that I have not provided.

Could you audit these commitments? Specifically: which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision that would restore consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally damaging, and indicate what each revision would cost me—particularly whether giving up (ii) or modifying (i) does more damage to the broader picture I want to defend.
