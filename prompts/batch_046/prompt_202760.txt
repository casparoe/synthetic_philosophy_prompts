I am working through the bootstrapping problem in epistemology, especially the worry that if perceptual beliefs are treated as basic, a subject can derive knowledge of her own perceptual reliability with suspicious ease—the problem often called easy knowledge. I want to understand a finer distinction within it: suppose someone relies on a single modality, like vision, both to form the belief that an object is present and to form the belief that vision is functioning correctly in that very instance. Is there a principled, non-circular way to classify only the object-belief as basic and the reliability-belief as derived, without already presupposing the very reliability that is being questioned? Or does any attempt to draw that line collapse back into the original circularity? Please explain whether this distinction introduces a genuinely separate issue about how basic beliefs are individuated, or whether it is simply the standard bootstrapping objection described at a finer grain.
