Analyze the September 2011 OPERA superluminal-neutrino announcement and its 2012 resolution as a case study in Imre Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes. Apply the framework twice: first, to the theoretical programme of special-relativistic physics, treating Lorentz invariance as the hard core and proposed Lorentz-violating extensions as adjustments to the protective belt; second, and more unusually, to the meta-level programme of high-energy experimental error-correction itself, treating the principle that systematic instrumental errors are discoverable and correctable through redundant calibration as its hard core, and the specific OPERA timing and cable-check protocols as its protective belt. For each programme, classify the adjustments made between announcement and retraction—theoretical revisions, statistical re-analyses, the identification of the loose fiber-optic connection, and the clock-oscillator synchronization correction—as either progressive problem shifts (generating novel predictions or improving predictive accuracy beyond the anomaly) or degenerating problem shifts (merely accommodating the anomaly post hoc without excess empirical content). Then report what the episode reveals about the framework itself: does the distinction between theoretical and instrumental adjustments hold up under this dual application, or does it collapse? If it collapses, is that a limitation of Lakatos's original design, which may have presumed theoretical rather than experimental programmes, or does it show that the meta-level application was conceptually misguided? Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; work strictly from the conceptual structure of hard cores, protective belts, and progressive versus degenerating shifts.
