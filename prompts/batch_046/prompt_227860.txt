Since my sister had her baby last month I've been weirdly fixated on why there's anything at all rather than just, well, nothing. Anyway, people keep trotting out 'nothing comes from nothing' — ex nihilo nihil fit, I think? — and using it like a conversation stopper. I heard it on a podcst about cosmology where the host was arguing whether quantum vacuum fluctuations mean the universe literally came from 'nothing' in the physicists' sense, and then my uncle used the same line at xmas to shut down any talk about whether the big bang needs a cause outside modal logic. I'm not sure what precise claim is hiding in there. Is it just a tautology about causation? A metaphysical principle that being requires a sufficient reason? A denial that empty quantum fields count as genuinely nothing? Or maybe it's doing work in an argument about explanatory closure? Could you separate out the distinct theses it might be expressing, flag which are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are actually false given modern physics? And tell me which reading is being invoked when someone uses it to block cosmological arguments about existence? Please write your answer in British English.
