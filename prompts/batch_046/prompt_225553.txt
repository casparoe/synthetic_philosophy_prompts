I am drafting a final exam for my high school philosophy and history seminar on whether Rome truly “fell.” The prompt must prevent students from merely summarizing Gibbon, listing barbarian invasions, or reciting climate and lead-poisoning hypotheses. Instead, present a 90-minute essay question built around a specific conceptual distinction: institutional failure, meaning a state’s loss of capacity to extract resources and enforce law across territory, versus civilizational rupture, meaning the disappearance of shared legal, linguistic, and economic practices that allow coordination without direct state mediation. Ask students to apply this distinction to the Western Empire between roughly 400 and 600 CE, requiring them to argue whether the period involves one form of discontinuity, both, or neither, and specifically whether framing it as “transformation” rather than “fall” actually solves the problem or merely renames the institutional loss. Provide the exam question, a model answer that executes the required conceptual moves, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points (specific marks for each, totaling 20), plus a one-line note on how candidates typically miss each point. The task should be fully answerable without access to specific sources, citations, or bibliographies, but it must separate students who understand the conceptual crux from those who have only memorized the standard narratives.
