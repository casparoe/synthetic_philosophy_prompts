I've been thinking about periodization - are the Middle Ages, Renaissance, and Scientific Revolution real historical joints or just later inventions? I want to defend that they track real shifts, but not through the usual 'great men and ideas' story. Instead I think they map onto changes in epistemic jurisidiction - basically who gets to authorize factual claims and on what grounds: clerical in the Middle Ages, humanist-textual in the Renaissance, method-empirical in the Scientific Revolution. But I'm worried this is just redresing the old triad in sociological language and avoiding anacronism only by being vague. What would actually count against it? I don't want 'historians disagree' - I want the conceptual problems with treating jurisdiction as a historical category, the cross-cultural cases that break the pattern, and whether the transitions were actually discrete or just gradual blur. Rank these defeaters from fatal to awkward. And be honest: has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability, since I can always redescribe a messy period in these terms? If so, is that a problem for a view like this?
