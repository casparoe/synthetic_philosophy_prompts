I'm working through some philosophy of language material with a study group, and we keep returning to Putnam's Twin Earth scenario and the externalist claim that mental content is not fully determined by internal states. One member of the group keeps using an analogy to make the point: she says semantic externalism is like relying on a satellite navigation system that holds no local map. Two devices with identical internal circuitry, she argues, can direct their users to entirely different destinations if one is linked to Earth satellites and the other to a Twin Earth orbital network that uses the same signal format but references different geospatial data. From this she concludes that the meaning of a mental representation, like the meaning of the device's directional command, is fixed entirely by the external network rather than by anything inside the user or the hardware.

I want to push on exactly how far this navigation analogy carries the philosophical load it is being asked to bear. Could you lay out the correspondence explicitly — what in the Twin Earth case plays the role of the device, the satellite link, the geospatial database, and the user's journey? Then identify the points where the mapping fractures: for instance, does the analogy smuggle in intentional design and real-time error correction that have no analogue in the causal-historical chains that fix natural-kind terms? Does it obscure the fact that Twin Earth Oscar and Earth Oscar share identical causal histories up to divergence, whereas two navigation devices are separately manufactured? I am particularly interested in whether the analogy survives when we extend it to deferential or empty concepts — terms like phlogiston or expert-dependent classifications where the external database is either absent or distributed across a community rather than orbiting like a satellite. Does the conclusion that internalism is necessarily false still hold once these disanalogies are on the table, or does the navigation picture collapse into a more modest claim about causal dependence? If there is a sharper analogy that captures externalist insights without importing technological artefacts, I would welcome that too.
