Compare Jain anekāntavāda—specifically the syādvāda doctrine and its sevenfold predication scheme (saptabhaṅgī)—with contemporary standpoint epistemology’s form of perspectivism. Focus on how each framework handles the simultaneous assertion of apparently contradictory properties: anekāntavāda through modal qualification, perspectivism through situated knowledge. Then extend the comparison to the niche concept of negative ontology, asking whether the absence or non-being of a property can itself function as one of the seven modal predicates, or whether standpoint theory treats such absence purely as an epistemic shift rather than an ontological feature. Which framework better accommodates the idea that a thing can genuinely be neither X nor not-X in a non-epistemic sense?
