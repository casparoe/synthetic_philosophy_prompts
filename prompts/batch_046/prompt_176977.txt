The practice is this: contemporary constitutions and oversight statutes embed sunset and auto-dissolution mechanisms, such as independent counsels with fixed terms, crisis-authority expiration clauses, and mandatory rotation rules, which are explicitly justified by the analogy that Rome failed because it could not keep temporary powers genuinely temporary. What philosophical commitments does this analogical design practice presuppose?
