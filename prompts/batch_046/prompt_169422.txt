I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of biology and applied ethics, and I serve as the graduate-student representative on the National Advisory Panel on Invertebrate Agricultural Welfare. The Ministry of Agriculture has asked our panel for a formal recommendation, due within six weeks, on whether industrial-scale facilities farming black soldier fly larvae (Hermetia illucens) for protein meal should be brought within mandatory welfare-assessment protocols. Recent comparative-cognition syntheses have assigned dipteran larvae a low-but-non-negligible posterior probability—roughly in the single-digit to low-teen percent range—of possessing nociception-like integrative processing. Because a single large facility can cycle through tens of billions of individuals across its operational lifespan, applying even a conservative probabilistic moral-weight framework suggests that the aggregate expected suffering-equivalent in these facilities could exceed that of conventional vertebrate livestock systems, despite the far lower individual confidence of sentience. At present, no regulatory architecture exists for monitoring welfare at this numerical scale, and the emerging sustainable-protein sector argues that premature binding rules—such as temperature, density, or harvesting-speed caps—would impose disproportionate economic and logistical burdens given the epistemic uncertainty. Other panel members maintain that the scale argument obligates precautionary action precisely because the affected population is orders of magnitude larger than any previously regulated group, and that minimal, low-cost safeguards are justified even on weak ascriptions of moral status. Please draft the panel’s memo addressed to the Ministry: it should recommend a concrete course of action with a phased implementation timeline, treat the scale argument as a practical policy-design problem rather than an abstract paradox, and engage seriously with the strongest objection on the opposing side—whether epistemic, economic, or methodological—without resorting to appeals to authority, citation lists, or bibliographic references. The recommendation should be actionable by a regulatory body that lacks invertebrate-specific enforcement infrastructure.
