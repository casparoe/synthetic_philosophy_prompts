I am currently in the final year of my philosophy of mind and cognitive science degree programme, and I am writing an essay for a seminar on consciousness and cognitive architecture. I want to examine the tension between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness within global workspace theory, but rather than rehearsing the standard exchange, I want to test the distinction through a conceptual construct I have devised. Consider a hypothetical cognitive architecture—let us call it a silent workspace—in which sensory and affective representations are broadcast globally to every consumer system, including attention, memory, decision-making, and linguistic report, in precisely the manner that Bernard Baars and Stanislas Dehaene describe, except that the workspace never produces a second-order representation of those representations being present. The system behaves identically to a conscious agent in every behavioural and cognitive test: it can report its states, recall them, and base actions upon them with perfect accuracy, yet there is no recursive self-model that registers the presence of the state as being experienced. I want my essay to treat this silent-broadcast architecture as a serious test case for global workspace theory. Specifically, I want to weigh whether the complete absence of recursive self-monitoring entails that phenomenal consciousness is genuinely absent despite fully intact access consciousness, or whether global broadcasting already exhausts the conditions for consciousness and renders the recursive layer redundant. The essay should draw on the familiar distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness, apply it rigorously to this silent-workspace scenario, reach a definite conclusion rather than ending on a non-committal note, and justify that verdict with clear reasoning. A conditional conclusion is acceptable—for instance, that phenomenal consciousness requires recursive monitoring only under a particular theory of self-reference, and that broadcasting alone suffices otherwise—but I want the argument to take a clear stand. Please present the response entirely in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though writing a formal letter or email.
