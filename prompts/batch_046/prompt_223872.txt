Take the Bristol Colston statue removal in 2020 and analyse it as a case study for presentism in historiography — not just the moral outrage part, but the theory mapped onto real actors. The council voted to take it down, historians objected publicly that judging 17th-century figures by todays standards is bad history, and protestors pulled it into the harbour. What does presentism actually say each group was doing? Was the council making a historical claim about Colston or just a present moral one? Does presentism explain the historians' anger, or does it miss the gap between explaining past beliefs and endorsing them? Then flip it: the statue was erected in 1895 with its own presentist framing of Colston as a city benefactor, so is the 2020 removal just another layer of presentism, or does it break the pattern?
