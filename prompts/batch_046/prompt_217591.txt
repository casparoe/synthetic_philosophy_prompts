here are my gut verdicts:

- my sister volunteers at a food bank every saturday. she told me she mostly does it cause she feels guilty being middle-class, but she also cant stand seeing people hungry. i think shes still genuinely good, just not pure.
- a kid at school found a wallet with 400 bucks and turned it in, but only cause there was a security camera right there. he also said "stealing is wrong" but i dont think he actually cares. verdict: this doesnt count as moral, its just avoiding trouble.
- my mom works overtime at a nonprofit helping immigrants with paperwork. she really cares about them, but she also needs the overtime to pay our bills. i think shes still acting for the right reasons, the money is just... there?
- my friend apologized to someone he bullied in middle school. he said he realized he was wrong, but honestly he just wanted the guy to stop bringing it up on instagram. he sent a long heartfelt text though. this feels fake, like the right action with the wrong engine.
- a nurse stays late to comfort a dying patient even though shes off the clock and doesnt get paid. she says she does it cause "its her job" but also cause she remembers her dad dying alone. this feels like the best kind.

heres the tension: in the mom case and the nurse case, theres a selfish or practical reason mixed in, but i still call them good. in the wallet case and the apology case, theres also a mix, but i call them bad or fake. is there a principle about "counterfactual stability" — if you took away the selfish reason, would they still do it? for the nurse, yes. for the mom... probably? for the wallet kid, definetly no. for the apology, also no.

im trying to see if one rule saves all my verdicts without being too messy. maybe "the right reason has to be load-bearing, and you have to be somewhat transparent to yourself about it"? but then the mom case gets complicated cause she knows she needs the money. also what about lying? if i lie to protect someones feelings but i also enjoy feeling like a hero, does that ruin it?

can you lay out a precise principle, or maybe two competing ones, and tell me which of my verdicts i have to drop? also predict for a case i havent listed: what if a politician votes for a good law mainly because it polls well with their base, but they also genuinely think its just?
