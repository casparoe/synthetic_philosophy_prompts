Having recently learned that the ancestral clan my family has claimed for three generations was actually chartered by a regional tourism board in 1987, I now find myself tasked with helping to design compensatory rituals for a community whose collective past is largely an administrative fiction. Please write a deadpan, Swiftian modest proposal in the form of an official government white paper—approximately 600 words—addressed to citizens of such a recently constituted nation. The document should propose a systematic program for manufacturing continuity: mandatory annual ceremonies that reenact a founding that never occurred; a statistical appendix that treats national sentiment as a quantifiable resource with measurable input-output ratios; and a bureaucratic aside on the logistics of issuing passports to citizens who share no demonstrable common ancestry. The proposal should treat collective identity as something that can be engineered through ritual repetition, print distribution, and bureaucratic classification—drawing on the logic of invented traditions and imagined communities—without ever naming Hobsbawm or Anderson, and without any scholarly apparatus, bibliography, or citation. The satire should arise from the unflinching collision between administrative rationality and emotional need, allowing the standard critiques—that heritage is managed, that nations are imagined, that traditions are retroactively assembled—to emerge implicitly rather than being stated. Maintain a dry, earnest, bureaucratic tone throughout. The target is the discourse of heritage management and the politics of collective memory, not any living individual.
