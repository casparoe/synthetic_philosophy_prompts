I am writing a philosophical novel in which two scholars—an aggregative panpsychist and an emergent panpsychist—have spent a year in adversarial collaboration and must now issue their joint statement. Please draft that document. Both parties accept that consciousness is fundamental in some form, that purely structural physical descriptions leave an explanatory gap, and that the combination problem is the central obstacle for any view that derives macro-experience from micro-experience. They remain divided over whether micro-subjects literally contribute their phenomenal character to a larger subject, and over whether neural integration generates genuinely novel experience or merely organizes pre-existing proto-experiential properties. Structure the response as a numbered list of agreed claims, each phrased so that neither camp feels misrepresented, followed by a disagreements table with each position rendered in wording its own proponents would endorse. Then identify three cruxes: first, the problem of experiential inheritance—whether a macro-subject possesses the very qualia of its constituent micro-subjects or only structurally analogous ones; second, the feasibility of a cross-scale phenomenal topology that maps relations among micro-qualia without reducing them; third, the interpretive weight of integrated-information measures in neuroscience for distinguishing literal composition from organizational emergence. For each crux, present the shared test case—specifically, a continuously integrated artificial neural network whose organizational complexity increases gradually—and state in advance the outcome each camp predicts if the case were fully realized. Maintain a formal, literary-philosophical tone appropriate for a novel's appendix, and do not include citations, reading lists, bibliographies, or requests for external verification.
