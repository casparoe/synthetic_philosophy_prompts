I am designing a taxonomy exercise for a graduate seminar in philosophy of science on long-run predictive failure and theoretical rescue. Our focal case is Malthus's Essay on the Principle of Population: the geometric-arithmetic divergence between population growth and resource expansion was eventually borne out in ecological and resource limits, yet specific predictions of imminent famine and demographic collapse were systematically contradicted for roughly two centuries by agricultural innovation, energy substitution, and the demographic transition. The seminar's guiding question is whether a predictive theory can be "right but early"—that is, falsified in the intermediate term yet vindicated over its intended temporal domain.

Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy that classifies the philosophical positions on this question. The root commitment, shared by every branch, is that the Malthusian mechanism (binding resource constraints on population) is genuinely operative and eventually manifested, but that the intermediate period contained sustained predictive failure.

The tree should be built from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of named theories. Each internal node must be a concrete claim, case judgment, or commitment that a student could answer without knowing which philosophical label it leads to. Structure the splits around the following four dimensions (order them as you see fit, but all four must appear):

1. The scope of falsification: Is predictive failure during the intermediate centuries a genuine falsification of the theory for its intended domain, or should evidential evaluation be quarantined to the long-run epoch?
2. The status of technological progress: Does agricultural and energetic innovation function as a legitimate boundary condition defining the theory's domain, as an ad hoc auxiliary hypothesis that rescues a degenerating core, or as a direct counterexample?
3. The ontological target of vindication: Is the "right but early" judgment about having identified a real causal mechanism regardless of intermediate predictive error, or about having arrived at the correct macro-outcome regardless of whether the underlying mechanism was correctly described?
4. The epistemic posture during latency: During the centuries of contrary evidence, should rational agents have suspended belief, actively maintained belief despite counterevidence, or rejected the theory with the possibility of later rehabilitation?

The taxonomy must locate the following positions as leaves:
- Hard Popperian immediate falsificationism
- Lakatosian progressive research-programme defence
- Structural realist (mechanism-first vindication)
- Instrumentalist (the view that the theory was a useful predictive fiction whose macro-forecast happened to align with later reality)
- Chronological insulationist (the niche view that evidential evaluation of long-horizon theories should be epoch-quarantined and not treated as time-transitive)
- Latent verificationist (the niche view that long-run predictions generate a distinct epistemic status—"deferred confirmation"—that is neither falsification, suspension, nor standard verification)

For every combination of answers that your tree permits, either place a named or unnamed leaf, or explicitly mark the cell as unoccupied. Where a cell is empty, provide a one-sentence diagnosis: is the combination incoherent, theoretically unattractive, or merely unexplored?

Each leaf should contain: (a) the position name, or "unnamed" if no recognized literature occupies it; (b) one sentence stating its most awkward commitment (for example, "must treat two centuries of sustained counterevidence as epistemically invisible to rational evaluation").

Finally, flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate on these four dimensions. Do not paper over logical proximity with different labels. The instrument should be usable without bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests, and should be readable to a philosophy graduate student who understands basic philosophy of science but has not memorized the Malthus literature.
