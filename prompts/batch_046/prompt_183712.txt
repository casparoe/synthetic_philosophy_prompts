Describe the most important implications of adopting a two-layer account of conceivability—where first-order imaginability (the ability to construct a coherent mental narrative or image) is strictly separated from second-order modal coherence (the absence of hidden structural or metaphysical presuppositions that collapse upon reflection)—for the standard thesis that conceivability is a guide to possibility. In particular, address how this distinction reshapes three familiar cases: the conceivability of philosophical zombies, the conceivability of an inverted spectrum with no behavioral difference, and the conceivability of mathematical structures failing to be physically instantiated. For each case, specify whether the two-layer model strengthens the move from conceivability to possibility, weakens it, transforms the argument into a claim about conceptual architecture rather than modal facts, or renders the original thought experiment moot. Furthermore, discuss whether defenders of conceivability-based reasoning should welcome this refinement or resist it as changing the subject, and identify what the acceptance or rejection of the distinction implies for the epistemic weight we should assign to modal intuition in metaphysics generally.
