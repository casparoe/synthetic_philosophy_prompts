I'm a retired physicist who got into philosophy after retirement because my wife said I needed a hobby that wasn't just fixing old radios, and now I'm stuck on this Carter doomsday argument that feels like it proves way too much. Here is the version that's bugging me: (1) If humanity survives long and produces trillions of people, then it's wildly unlikely I'd be born among the first hundred billion. (2) So my actual birth rank is strong Bayesian evidence that the total human population is small and extinction is near. (3) But "being at rank n in reference class R" is just a formal pattern. I can apply it to any class I belong to - I'm member 312 of my local astronomy club (founded 1987), or the 47th retiree in my building who started reading philosphy late in life. (4) If those groups survive for millenia there'd be thousands of later members, so by the exact same reasoning I should predict they all collapse within a few years. (5) That means Carter's logic forces me into universal pessimism about every social group I belong to, including my gardening club and my old 1973 physics cohort. (6) Obviously I don't actually believe all my clubs are about to vanish tomorrow. (7) Therefore the argument must fail somewhere between (2) and (4). (8) But I want to keep (2) for humanity - it feels like honest statistical reasoning, not a parlor trick. Where is the hidden slip? Is it that only the "all humans ever" refernce class is legitimate, or is there a quantifier shift I'm not seeing? I'd rather pay the price at (4) than throw out (2).
