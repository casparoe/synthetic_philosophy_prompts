Next Thursday I present to an interdisciplinary seminar—analytic philosophers of mathematics, narratologists influenced by structuralist poetics, and one cognitive scientist of narrative—an argument drawn from my novel-in-progress and its companion essay. My thesis is that Gödel's first incompleteness theorem does not license any claim that human cognition transcends formal computation (the Penrose-Lucas error I take pains to reject), but instead demonstrates a structural feature of any recursive representational system capable of encoding self-referential statements about its own consistency. I apply this to fiction through what I call autodiegetic closure: when a narrative contains an internal mechanism that refers back to the narrative's own production—such as a narrator writing the very text we hold—the system generates propositions analogous to Gödel sentences, plot-level claims that the story's internal rules can neither affirm nor deny without contradiction. I contend this reveals nothing mystical about consciousness and much about the formal limits of self-modeling, whether in arithmetic or in narrative architecture.

The audience is precisely divided. The philosophers include a formal epistemologist skeptical of logical omniscience debates and a philosopher of science who regards analogical transfers between mathematics and literature with deep suspicion. Among the literary scholars, one is a structuralist fascinated by metalepsis and narrative embedding, another is a poststructuralist wary of importing mathematical rigor into textual interpretation, and there is also the cognitive scientist who studies how readers construct coherence across nested fictional worlds.

Please prepare me for the objections in the order they are most likely to arise and in proportion to the damage they could do. Phrase each as the specific faction that raises it would phrase it. For each, tell me what to concede gracefully, what to hold firmly, how to reply within the two minutes available, and where that reply is weakest. Include the objection I am hoping nobody asks—the one that exposes the deepest disanalogy between arithmetical provability and narrative coherence, which my essay addresses only in passing. I want a speaker's prep sheet, not a defense of the thesis itself: show me the fault lines in my own position that I should be ready to defend rather than pray go unnoticed.
