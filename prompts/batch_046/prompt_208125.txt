ok so for my phil-of-history seminar i need to check if my takes on legend vs history actually hang together. here's roughly where i've landed:

1. i think a legend becomes history only when there's "source independence"—material or documentary traces that could exist even if the story was never told. without that it's just a well-preserved myth.

2. i apply that symetrically: no special pleading for sacred texts or national founding stories.

3. using that, trojan war is over the line—hittite references to wilusa, the hisarlik destruction layer around 1180 bce, the late bronze collapse context. enough independent stuff.

4. arthur stays legend—no 6th-century documents, earliest mentions are 9th c and clearly literary. plus there's a big gap, so accretion has had time to work.

5. exodus also hasn't crossed—no egyptian administrative record of a mass slave departure, no sinai archaeology matching 600k people, and canaan in the 13th c doesn't show a sudden disruption.

6. but absence of evidence hits different for mobile, non-literate groups. the israelites in the wilderness wouldn't leave monuments, so the archaeological silence isn't as damaging for exodus as it is for arthur, where we have a settled society that should have left traces.

7. i also think sustained institutional transmission—like the biblical text through jewish and christian communities—counts for something. it's not archaeological proof, but it creates a kind of historical continuity that makes me open to a small exodus kernel despite 5.

8. at the same time i think long transmission chains usually corrupt mnemonic cores through accretion, which is partly why arthur is out (huge gap between event and first record). so i'm not sure why 7 should work for exodus, where the chain is even longer.

9. finally, the bar shouldn't shift depending on when i happen to study the problem or whether i find the narrative personally compelling—uniform standards.

i'm also playing with this idea i call "counterfactual traceability": a trace only counts if the record would look different had the event not happened. does that help?

- is there a hidden clash between 7 and 8—why does long transmission hurt arthur but help exodus?
- and does adding counterfactual traceability make things better or just deepen the 6/7 tension?
