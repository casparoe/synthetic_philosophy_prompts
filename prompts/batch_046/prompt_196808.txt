I have recently taken over the administration of my late grandmother's estate, which includes a small, depopulated mill town where property boundaries and shared maintenance obligations were never codified in law but governed for decades by an unspoken system of reciprocity. As I sort through the papers, I find myself thinking about David Lewis's account of convention and the evolution of norms—specifically how arbitrary equilibria are sustained by mutual expectation, how they depend on common knowledge of that expectation, and how they collapse or transform when the coordinating signals disappear. The story should be accessible to a reader who knows nothing of Lewis, yet conceptually precise enough that a philosopher recognizes the architecture.
