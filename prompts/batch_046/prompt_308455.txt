I'm preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends a specific version of the idea that consciousness is a user illusion. On her account, the brain doesn't project a false inner movie; instead, it runs a predictive interface that tags certain representations with an ownership marker, producing what feels like a single "me" to whom experiences happen. She claims this dissolves the hard problem only if we treat "what it's like" as a meta-representational comment about those tagged predictions, not as an intrinsic property. I want questions that push past restating this picture.

Please draft a focused sequence of six to eight interview questions in American English. They should turn on the actual content of her position, with no generic biographical openings. Cover these four areas, each with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply: (1) the objection that feeling like something cannot be reduced to a representation about representation — press her on whether meta-representation explains or explains away the phenomenon; (2) the binding problem — how distributed predictions unify under one user token, and what she says about split-brain or dissociation cases where that token seems to fracture; (3) the constructed "now" — if the subjective present is an interface feature, does it have any objective temporal location, and does that risk making every present moment solipsistic; (4) what empirical finding in predictive-processing research or in clinical conditions where the ownership marker fails would genuinely count against her view rather than just complicate it. End with one question asking whether her view has been qualified so heavily that no observation could refute it, and whether that insulation is a weakness for a theory meant to explain a concrete phenomenon.
