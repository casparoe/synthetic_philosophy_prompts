The exam prompt was: "First, explain the core ontological commitment of presentism and contrast it with eternalism (the block-universe view). Second, present the truthmaker objection to presentism and describe how a presentist might respond using either Lucretian properties or ersatz presentism. Third, briefly distinguish presentism from Broad's moving-spotlight theory, paying careful attention to whether the latter requires an ontology of past and future events."

The student answered:
"Presentism is the view that only present objects and events exist; past and future entities have no ontological status whatsoever. Eternalism, by contrast, treats all temporal locations as equally real, arranging them in a four-dimensional manifold where change is merely variation along the temporal axis, analogous to spatial variation. This means eternalism denies any objective passage of time.

The truthmaker objection asks what grounds the truth of past-tense propositions if the past does not exist. For instance, if Caesar crossed the Rubicon is true, but Caesar is not present, there seems to be no truthmaker. One presentist reply appeals to Lucretian properties—named after the Roman poet Lucretius—which are present properties of current objects that encode information about past states, such as the property of being such that Caesar crossed the Rubicon. Another reply is ersatz presentism, which holds that only present concrete objects exist, but also allows abstract representations or 'ersatz' past times to exist now, thereby supplying truthmakers without committing to real past entities.

Finally, presentism is closely related to Broad's moving-spotlight theory, since both hold that only one time is genuinely real. In the moving-spotlight view, there is a four-dimensional block of events stretching through past and future, but only those events currently illuminated by the spotlight of presentness are metaphysically actual; the rest exist only as unilluminated potentialities. This means presentism can be seen as a limiting case of the spotlight view where the unilluminated parts of the block are eliminated entirely. Meanwhile, localized presentism—where presentness is relative to a spatiotemporal region rather than global—shows that presentism can be made compatible with special relativity by allowing different observers to have different present slices without contradiction."
