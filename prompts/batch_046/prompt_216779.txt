I hold the strong Madhyamaka-inspired reading of anatta: not merely that there is no permanent self, but that there are no persisting persons even conventionally — only discrete, momentary dharmas with no narrative, causal, or psychological thread that binds them across time. On this view, talk of being the same person is not just false at the ultimate level but systematically misleading at the conventional level, comparable to speaking of a flame when only successive sparks exist. What would genuinely count against this position? I want defeaters that do not simply reassert Western psychological-continuity intuitions, but rather internal Buddhist tensions — for instance, karma and moral responsibility without a bearer — phenomenological evidence of diachronic self-reference, or formal results in philosophy of persistence. Rank them: which would be fatal to the strong reading, which merely awkward, and which could be absorbed by redefining conventional truth? Be honest about whether this view has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a Buddhist metaphysical claim.
