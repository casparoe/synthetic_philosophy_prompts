At a seminar last week Mira and Jonas got into it about whether the Frege-Geach problem hits aesthetic expressivism harder then moral expressivism. Mira said yes: if "this chord progression is jarring" just expresses discomfort, then in "If this chord is jarring then the composer failed" there's no attitude in the antecedent to carry the inference—whereas moral sentences at least have normative force you can hypothetically project. Jonas said thats backwards—moral terms have stronger action-guiding force, so embedding them creates worse "attitude fragmentation" when you try to preserve compositionality with hypothetical endorsement. Mira brought up "If being generous is good, then being generous to enemies is good" and said Jonas's projection model can't tell this apart from "If I like generosity..." Jonas replied the distinction is pragmatic, not semantic, and Mira's smuggling in a descriptivist backbone.
