Infintism was summed up as the view that justification requires an actually infinite sequence of reasons, which is humanly impossible, so it should be rejected as a reductio.

The author said foundationalism avoids this by cutting the branches at the root, but suffers from 'the given problem' — basic beliefs are caused by the world but causation isn't justification, so they can't actually do the work of supporting other beliefs. Coherentism avoids both issues but is unstable because you can always adjust one belief to save coherence, so there's no real constraint.
