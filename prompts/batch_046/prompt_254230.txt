I serve on the board of a small housing cooperative that recently adopted a strict policy prohibiting subletting without written board approval. The policy was drafted and vigorously enforced by our board president, who has now been discovered to have quietly operated an unlisted sublet for over a year. At our upcoming meeting, I plan to raise the matter and ask for accountability. A fellow board member urges me to remain silent, noting that I once permitted a friend to stay in my unit for two months without filing the required form; he argues that anyone with that record lacks the authority to condemn another member, and that speaking up would only expose me to the same charge. I believe there may be a relevant difference—she authored the rule, applied it punitively to others, and actively concealed her own breach, while I never enforced the policy on anyone and was transparent about my arrangement—but I am uncertain whether my own past lapse genuinely removes my authority to object, or whether her position as both architect and secret violator constitutes a distinct moral failure beyond simple inconsistency. Is my colleague right that my history disqualifies me, and does her combination of rule-making, hidden violation, and enforcement against others create a wrong that is qualitatively different from merely falling short of a standard one never claimed to uphold?
