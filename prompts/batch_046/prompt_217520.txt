My colleague Elena maintains an unvarying morning protocol: she grinds coffee beans for exactly twelve seconds, taps the filter twice, and pours water in a clockwise spiral that never deviates. When her grinder broke last Tuesday, she refused pre-ground beans and skipped the drink entirely, insisting that without the precise sequence the act "would not be hers." Her partner interprets this as mechanical rigidity — an inability to separate means from ends — and argues that genuine autonomy requires adapting execution while preserving intention. Elena counters that the ritual is not instrumental but constitutive: altering its form transforms the end itself, as if changing a vow's wording changed the vow. What puzzles me further is a hierarchical pattern: she displays no second-order flexibility about whether to maintain the ritual, yet tolerates no first-order deviation within it. Is this coupling of rigid execution with rigid commitment to execution best diagnosed as a failure of practical reason, or as a coherent, if unusual, structure of agency? Please advise, using British English, in a single paragraph not exceeding 230 words.
