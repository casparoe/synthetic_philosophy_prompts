On her view, aggregating moral weight across vast populations requires a uniform prior on sentience within the class, but evolutionary convergence yields architecture-specific priors that are incommensurable across phyla; multiplying a low uniform probability by 10^19 insects is therefore a statistical category error, not a moral calculation. Write a sequence of questions that progressively presses this position: first, how she distinguishes it from standard neural-architecture skepticism; then whether incommensurable priors block aggregation entirely or merely reduce the aggregate weight; next, challenge her with within-phylum scale—say, nematodes versus cephalopods within Mollusca—where architecture varies but is not incommensurable; ask what empirical finding, such as a shared nociceptive pathway across arthropods and vertebrates, would force her to treat insect priors as uniform; and close by asking whether her framework implies that moral uncertainty should never be summed across taxonomic boundaries, and if so, whether that undermines cross-species trade-offs in conservation biology.
