I'm preparing to moderate a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends an unusually integrated reading of Hilary Putnam's later religious essays. Her central claim is that Putnam's covenantal interpretation of Jewish practice — drawing on the prohibition of representing the divine face in Maimonides and on Rosenzweig's idea of covenantal presence — is not merely compatible with his internal-realist philosophy of science, but actually presupposes it. She argues that the Maimonidean ban on a "God's-eye view" of the deity functions structurally like Putnam's rejection of a metaphysical "view from nowhere" in Reason, Truth and History, and that commentators have missed this parallel precisely because they treat Putnam's philosophy of religion as hermetically sealed from his philosophy of science.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate this thesis. I want at least two questions that press directly on the Maimonides–Putnam parallel itself — anticipating her likely reply that theological epistemic humility and conceptual-scheme dependence are entirely different kinds of limitation — and pushing past that distinction. Include at least two questions on what this reconciliation implies for Putnam's earlier arguments against metaphysical realism: does binding religious commitment to internal realism strengthen that anti-realist case, or does it expose internal realism to objections that only arise once it is made to carry theological weight? I also want at least one question on whether this reading renders Putnam's religious commitments curiously immune to the conceptual-relativity objections he is otherwise willing to accept in scientific and ethical domains.

For each question, provide a brief follow-up keyed specifically to the answer she is most likely to give. Write the entire response in British English. At the end, include a concise note stating what would change your mind about which questions are the sharpest — identify the specific misreading of Putnam's corpus, or the overlooked essay or text, that would most undermine your selection, and point out where your reasoning about the Maimonides parallel is most vulnerable to collapse.
