I am constructing the philosophical architecture of a novel in which a character asserts that a past atrocity is metaphysically 'gone,' yet insists it retains a present moral reality within the narrative frame. To ensure this tension is conceptually rigorous rather than decorative, I require an exam-style instrument for an upper-level metaphysics seminar: a single essay question answerable in ninety minutes, designed for students who already understand the standard distinction between presentism and eternalism but who must do more than summarize it. The question should introduce and evaluate a hypothetical view—frame-relative presentism—according to which an event exists only relative to a narrative or experiential 'present,' not an absolute cosmic now, and ask whether this genuinely resolves the cross-temporal relation problem or merely disguises a four-dimensionalist ontology; the answer must be evaluative, not expository. Provide the question itself, a model answer of approximately six hundred words, and a marking scheme structured around five specific conceptual moves totaling twenty marks, with each point weighted and accompanied by a one-line note on the characteristic mistake that costs it. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or summaries of named literature; the evaluation should turn entirely on the internal coherence of the proposed view and its relation to the standard objections.
