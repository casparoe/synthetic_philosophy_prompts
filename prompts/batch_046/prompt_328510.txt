I think advance directives should bind even when the later patient is genuinly content — not because the earlier self was the real one, but because practical authority over your body works like a diachronic property right you can't revoke just by changing preferences. What would have to be true for that to be wrong? I want the actual defeaters: cases where the property-right analogy breaks, arguments that the later self is a different moral subject, maybe results about personal identity that undercut diachronic ownership. Rank them from fatal to just awkward. Which ones would a defender concede actually hurt? And is the view so well insulated — you can always say the right was never waived — that it's unfalsifiable, and is that a problem?
