I'm trying to map out my gut reactions about blame and resentment in edge cases, and I want to see if there's a principle behind them. My roommate forgets our anniversary dinner because he's absored in work: I'm annoyed but it's not real resentment, more like disappointment. A CEO dumps toxic waste knowing it might hurt people but the profits justify it: I feel full-on moral resentment, like he's a legitimate target. Someone in a psychotic episode shouts insults at strangers because of hallucinations: no resentment at all, just pity and wanting them to get help. An AI system optimized for engagement ends up radicalizing users: I feel something like resentment but it feels weird, like I'm treating it as an agent even though I don't think it has real intentions. A friend lies about something trivial just to avoid awkwardness: I do resent it, but way less than the CEO even though the harm is tiny. The tension that bugs me is the comparison between the psychotic person and a drunk driver who causes harm with diminished control: both have reduced capacity, yet I resent the driver and not the other. Is there a coherent account - maybe tracking quality of will plus capacity for recipricol recognition - that saves all these verdicts? Or do I have to drop one? And what should I think about the AI case - does my weird reaction there commit me to some kind of functionalist view of agency?
