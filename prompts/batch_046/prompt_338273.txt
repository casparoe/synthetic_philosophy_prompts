I have just come away from a long, somewhat exhausting argument with a close friend about whether Douglas Hofstadter's tripartite distinction in Gödel, Escher, Bach—between frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages—can survive being mapped onto domains outside the formal systems for which it was originally devised. My friend insists that the distinction collapses as soon as you apply it to legal interpretation, where a framing clause sets boundary conditions, the statutory text delivers an explicit command, and the normative architecture that judges slowly excavate behaves like a hidden self-reference. I remain convinced that this is precisely where the distinction becomes most illuminating, and to close a seminar I am running on interpretive levels I want a poem that treats these three layers not as illustrations but as structural bones.

Compose a poem of exactly three stanzas, each eight lines long (twenty-four lines total), with an ABAB CDCD rhyme scheme in each stanza and a single refrain line that closes every stanza but shifts its grammatical subject—first pointing to the frame, then to the outer text, then to the inner message—so that the repetition itself enacts the problem of self-reference Hofstadter describes. The first stanza should evoke the frame message through the image of a record player confronting its own label, that classic GEB example of a system meeting its boundary condition, without reducing the image to mere explanation; the second should present the outer message as language that appears self-sufficient yet demands an interpreter; and the third should suggest the inner message as something that emerges only when the system attempts to observe itself observing, leaving genuinely open whether it was always present or produced by the act of interpretation. The poem should be precise enough that a reader familiar with GEB recognizes the three levels, yet work as an independent lyric for someone who has never encountered the book.

Please do not provide citations, a bibliography, or reading suggestions; I need only the poem itself, polished and suitable to be read aloud at the end of our session.
