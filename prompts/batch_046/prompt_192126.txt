Make the case that Hofstadter's conception of the self as a strange loop—where the symbol "I" functions as a self-referential symbol with no underlying owner—undermines the standard narrative-continuity theory of personal identity. Specifically, argue that if the self is nothing more than the recursive architecture of this loop, then identity across time depends not on the preservation of autobiographical memory or bodily continuity, but on the maintenance of what we might call the loop's "re-entrant topology": the precise structural pattern by which the symbol refers back to itself through intermediate representations. Defend the further claim that this allows for identity to persist through radical discontinuities—such as split-brain separation or successive digital instantiation—provided the recursive topology remains intact, even when all qualitative content (memories, traits, phenomenal character) is replaced. Anticipate and answer the objection that this collapses the self into a purely formal or algorithmic property, indistinguishable from any system running an isomorphic program, and do so without treating consciousness as an irreducible substrate that rescues the loop from formalism. Your argument should show why the strange-loop structure itself supplies a non-derivative ground for sameness.
