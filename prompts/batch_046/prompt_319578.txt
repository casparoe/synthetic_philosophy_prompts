Prioritarianism is the thesis that moral reasons to benefit people are stronger the worse off those people are, usually represented by a weighting function applied to individual well-being that declines monotonically with welfare level. The leveling-down objection reveals an internal contradiction: if two individuals are at unequal levels and the only available intervention is to reduce the better-off party, prioritarianism must approve, because equalizing downward makes the weights more uniform and can raise the aggregate weighted sum. The standard defense — that the view cares about absolute levels rather than equality — is therefore inadequate; since the weighting scheme is symmetric with respect to gains and losses, it contains no structural distinction between lifting the bottom and pushing the top down other than contingent empirical constraints. From this it follows that prioritarianism implicitly treats equality of welfare as an intrinsic good, and its defenders are forced either to accept leveling-down as obligatory or to abandon symmetric weighting altogether.
