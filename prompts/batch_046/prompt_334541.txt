I am a PhD candidate in political theory working on the ontology of collective agency, and I am preparing an interview for our department’s conceptual-issues podcast series. The guest is an unnamed philosopher who defends a view I will call mnemonic contractualism. The position accepts the standard framework associated with Hobsbawm’s analysis of invented traditions and Anderson’s account of nations as imagined communities sustained among strangers, but it adds a distinct normative thesis: once an invented tradition has structured institutional practice across approximately three generations, the nation acquires a derivative moral personality. This personality generates obligations of historical repair and institutional continuity that bind the collective entity itself, not merely its present citizens, even though the entity’s origins remain fully constructivist. The view is intended to occupy the narrow space between primordialist nationalism and pure constructivist reductionism.

Please draft eight to ten formal interview questions that press this position at its joints. I require explicit coverage of the following four pressure points, with at least one dedicated question for each:

1. The temporal threshold: why three generations rather than two or five, and whether the ethical irrelevance of the original fabrication is a matter of degree or of kind once that threshold is crossed.
2. The boundary problem: why mnemonic contractualism applies to nations but not to other long-standing invented collectives—such as multinational corporations, professional sports leagues, or religious denominations with similarly fabricated origin myths—that have also persisted across multiple generations.
3. The secessionist challenge: how the view treats movements that reject the state’s invented tradition in favor of a counter-narrative claiming deeper historical roots. Does it grant those movements derivative personality too, or does it privilege the incumbent tradition in a way that smuggles in primordialist criteria?
4. The phenomenological status of comradeship: whether the deep horizontal solidarity that sustains the imagined community must be genuinely experienced by members, or whether purely performative institutional repetition is sufficient for the normative transformation to occur.

For each question, provide a brief framing sentence that situates it in the relevant literature, then pose the question itself. Do not include generic biographical or career-oriented questions.

Please organize your response into exactly these sections, in this order:

- Thesis Summary and Literature Situating (approximately 180 words): briefly outline mnemonic contractualism and locate it relative to the Hobsbawm and Anderson framework, without merely repeating standard summaries.
- The Questions (approximately 650–800 words): present the eight to ten questions, each with its framing context.
- Anticipated Replies and Follow-ups (approximately 450–550 words): for each of the four pressure points above, state the reply the defender is most likely to offer (two to three sentences), then provide a sharp follow-up question specifically calibrated to undercut or complicate that reply.
- Closing Strategic Question (approximately 100 words): a single final question designed to force the defender to choose between collapsing the view into pure constructivism or into a covert form of primordialism.

Keep the tone appropriate for an advanced philosophy podcast: precise, adversarial but respectful, and free of introductory pleasantries.
