Student answer:
The tetralemma lists four options: that emptiness exists with svabhāva, that it lacks svabhāva entirely, that it both has and lacks it, and that it neither has nor lacks it. Nāgārjuna rejects the first three in the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā because if emptiness had inherent existence it wouldn't depend on conditions, contradicting dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda). He then accepts the fourth corner — neither existence nor non-existence — as the correct Madhyamaka position, which is essentially the prāsaṅgika method later formalized by Candrakīrti. This isn't nihilism since conventional phenomena still arise dependently; in fact emptiness is dependently originated too, so it serves as the conventional ground for appearances without being an ultimate substrate like the Yogācāra ālayavijñāna. Applying the tetralemma reflexively means even 'emptiness is empty' avoids all four extremes, leaving a middle way that uses conventional language without reifying anything.
