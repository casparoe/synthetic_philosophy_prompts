I hold the view that life is not a natural kind but a graded organizational phenomenon anchored in autopoietic closure—a system's continuous regeneration of its own boundary conditions—while replication, metabolism, and evolutionary adaptability are contingent correlates rather than necessary conditions. On this account, viruses are not alive because they borrow closure from hosts; synthetic minimal cells that replicate without self-maintaining metabolic boundaries are not alive either; yet certain non-reproducing, self-sustaining chemical networks could qualify. The familiar checklist approach (reproduction, growth, homeostasis, response to stimuli) is therefore a pre-theoretical taxonomy that mistakes typical accompaniments for defining ones.

What would genuinely count against this position? I want a map of its exposure, not a literature survey. If so, is that insulation a virtue of conceptual precision or a vice that empties the view? (4) Is there a formal result from systems chemistry or theoretical biology showing that organizational closure either collapses into replication or cannot be defined without circularity?
