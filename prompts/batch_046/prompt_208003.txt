I just finished a long, somewhat heated argument with a friend over dinner about Newton’s bucket and whether the paraboloid of rotating water proves that space exists independently of its contents. She took the Machian line that the curvature is purely relational—correlated with the distant stars—while I kept insisting that without substantival spacetime there is no non-circular account of why the bucket “knows” it is rotating. Tomorrow I am giving the opening remarks at a small interdisciplinary workshop; about half the audience are specialists in philosophy of physics who will catch every imprecision, and the other half are historians and physicists who should not be left behind. The philosophical tension needs to do real structural work in the remarks, not serve as ornament: specifically, please inject the niche scenario of a quantum superfluid inside the bucket that exhibits zero surface curvature under rotation, so that the classic visual evidence for absolute motion vanishes, and build the speech around what can and cannot be said about spacetime ontology if that result holds. Write exactly five brief movements, each no longer than two sentences, arranged without any separate introduction or conclusion, so that the piece lands for both audiences—honoring the specialists’ concerns without lecturing the non-specialists.
