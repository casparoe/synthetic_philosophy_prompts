She should draw on Dennett’s distinction between cranes and skyhooks, but argue specifically that greedy reductionism—insisting every layer be fully accounted for by the layer beneath—actually undermines scientific explanation rather than securing it. To ground her view, she should introduce and defend “normative inertia”: the claim that once cultural practices achieve sufficient stability, they exert downward selective pressure on biological and cognitive development, creating feedback loops that are mechanistically tractable yet not eliminable into gene-level or neural-level dynamics.

He should treat “scaffolding” and “normative inertia” as names for explanatory gaps, and maintain that true scientific explanation requires greedy reduction: if one cannot show precisely how gene-expression patterns or neural dynamics constitute moral error and correction, one has smuggled in mysterious top-down causation. He must respond directly to Maren’s points about downward pressure and feedback loops, challenging whether these are coherent without being fully decomposed into bottom-up mechanisms.

The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth: each character should address the other’s specific arguments rather than speaking past one another. Both should be equally competent; neither should be a strawman. Do not have either character win conclusively.
