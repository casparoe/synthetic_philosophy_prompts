Exam question: Explain David Lewis's Principal Principle, then discuss the problem of expert deference and how self-locating (indexical) evidence interacts with it. Be sure to distinguish the Principal Principle from Bas van Fraassen's reflection principle for temporal credences.

Student answer:

The Principal Principle states that if an agent knows the objective chance of an event E is x, then her rational credence in E should be x, provided she has no other admissible evidence. David Lewis introduced this principle to bridge objective physical probabilities and subjective degrees of belief. When an expert tells her that the chance of rain is 0.8, but she also has a private correlation—say, her joints ache before rain—she should still set her credence to 0.8, because private correlations do not alter the objective chance.

The problem becomes harder with self-locating belief. Imagine you know that exactly one of ten people in a room will be selected by a fair lottery. The objective chance that you are selected is 0.1. But if you also hold the indexical belief 'I am the person holding ticket number 7,' and you happen to know that ticket 7 was drawn, then your credence should jump to 1. This shows that the Principal Principle must be restricted to non-indexical propositions; whenever indexical information is present, the principle is suspended.

This relates to van Fraassen's reflection principle, which requires that your present credences match your expected future credences. Some philosophers think reflection can defend the Principal Principle against expert deference: if you expect that tomorrow you will defer to the meteorologist, you should defer today. Lewis himself appealed to reflection in this way, treating temporal consistency as the foundation for chance-credence alignment.

Therefore, the correct response to expert deference is to check whether the expert's report gives you reason to revise your estimate of the objective chance itself. If it does not—if you merely learn her credence—then the Principal Principle holds firm and you stay at 0.8.
