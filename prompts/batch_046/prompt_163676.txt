I spent much of last evening arguing with a friend about whether images produced by diffusion models can count as art in any philosophically robust sense. Whenever I pressed her on what criterion was actually being violated, she returned to the Tolstoyan slogan that art is essentially “infection”—a transmission of feeling from one person to another—and insisted that since a model has no feelings of its own to transmit, its outputs are automatically disqualified. I think she is treating a compressed metaphor as if it were a single decisive argument, but I want to come back with precision rather than flat contradiction. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that “art is infection” might be expressing, arranged as a menu rather than collapsed into one reading? For each, distinguish at least: a purely descriptive psychological claim about how audiences come to share an emotional state; an intentionalist claim that the artist’s actual mental state is causally necessary for the transmission; a normative claim that artistic value depends on this authentic origin; and any metaphorical or rhetorical reading that carries no exclusionary force at all. For each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or incoherent; and, most important for our argument, say whether that particular version actually supports her conclusion that an AI-generated image cannot be art.

To make this concrete, my friend specifically invoked the idea against a particular Midjourney output that she admitted moved her emotionally, yet she still denied it was art because the “infection” lacked an authentic human source. That detail makes me think there may be hidden distinctions—between the presence of feeling in the recipient, the presence of feeling in the originator, and the transparency of the causal chain—that the slogan is obscuring. So please include in your menu whether there is a defensible reading on which audience affect alone is sufficient for art-status, and whether there is a reading on which the absence of a conscious artist is fatal regardless of the audience’s response. The deliverable should be a taxonomy of precise claims with verdicts on each, so that I can bring it back to her as a structured challenge showing exactly which version of Tolstoy’s idea she is relying on, rather than as a vague disagreement about artificial intelligence and creativity. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations from What Is Art?—just the disambiguation I need to keep the argument in the weeds.
