hey, I need an in-class design for a philosophy workshop — 8 grad students, one 75-minute session, no powerpoint. The theme is sphexishness: that wasp that buries its prey and then gets stuck in a loop if you move the prey, but pushed way past the intro-level anecdote everyone already knows. I want you to inject a fresh framing: call it "refractory loops" — cases where an agent doesn't just miss data, but its action architecture can't represent a state as already-completed, so it keeps rebooting. Build a live simulation or structured role-play where each student gets a hidden rule-card that forces mechanical repetition. They have to collaborate on a physical task — say, arranging colored blocks into a pattern — but some cards secretly reset the builder whenever they reach step three. In round two, hand out "meta-cards" that look like they grant flexibility; but two of them are actually higher-order loops disguised as reflection. The win condition is collective: the group only succeeds if every member genuinely breaks their loop, not just looks like it. Include the exact rules, how the hidden cards work, what small stakes you use (maybe a shared snack or just social prestige), a debrief that maps each round onto rigidity vs real flexibility, and be honest about which part of free will this still can't reproduce — probably the phenomenology of choice. Also say what will predictably go wrong on the first run (someone figures out their card too fast, or people laugh it off) and how to fix it. No lecture before we start, no assigned reading, and don't ask participants to recieve citations or name-check authors. Keep it runnable for someone without web access.
