Write an essay on whether reciprocal masking constitutes a distinct ontological category between finking and standard masking, or whether it collapses one of those distinctions. Begin by distinguishing the familiar cases: a fink removes or destroys a disposition precisely when its manifestation conditions are satisfied, whereas a mask leaves the disposition intact but introduces an opposing causal factor that suppresses its expression. Then introduce the less-discussed case of reciprocal masking, in which the interfering factor is not an independent blocker but a co-disposition activated by the very same stimulus conditions, and in which both dispositions share a common causal substrate—so that the system never exhibits either disposition in isolation. Defend a specific verdict: either (i) reciprocal masking is ultimately a form of finking, because the shared substrate means the putative original disposition was never genuinely present in pure form; (ii) it remains a form of standard masking, because both dispositions genuinely coexist and merely cancel one another out; or (iii) it reveals that the fink/mask distinction itself depends on an untenable reification of dispositions as separable causal powers. In justifying your conclusion, address whether talk of "the" underlying disposition is misleading when the substrate is structurally shared, and discuss whether this analysis changes how we should attribute dispositional properties to complex systems—such as adversarially regularized neural networks or biological pathways with antagonistic feedback loops—without appealing to bibliographic authority or citation.
