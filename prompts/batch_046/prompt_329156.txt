Question: Explain Dennetts multiple drafts model and contrast it with the Cartesian theater idea. Then discuss whether split-brain cases support or challenge the model.

Student answer:
Dennett rejects the Cartesian theater, where theres one spot in the brain that displays everything to a little observer inside. Instead he proposes that the brain generates many drafts of sensory content in parallel across different regions. Through competitive selection, one draft achieves fame in the brain and gets promoted to the global workspace, becoming the canonical conscious experience that gets presented to the rest of the system. There is no homunculus watching this workspace, but there is a single authoritative version. In split-brain patients each hemisphere produces its own drafts, and because there is no central theater, neither hemisphere is the true self — which supports Dennett by showing consciousness can divide without a single audience collapsing. Some researchers argue that predictive processing in prefrontal cortex acts as an editorial layer that picks the winning draft, but Dennett replies that even those dynamics are just more drafts, not an audience.
