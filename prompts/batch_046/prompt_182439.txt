Here's my view: standard Frankfurt cases fail against the principle of alternative possibilities only if we misread PAP. I think PAP should be read as requiring "pathway-open" alternatives -- meaning there's a causal route the agent could take that the blocker wouldn't detect or intercept in time. Most classic setups rely on an omniscent blocker that knows the decision in advance, but real-world intervention (neural monitors, AI oversight) has latency and blind spots. So the agent genuinely could have done otherwise through those gaps, and PAP survives in actual decision architechtures.
