I'm writing up the consciousness-science workshop I covered last month, and over drinks two researchers got into a real sharp exchange. Dr. Chen argued that Dennett-style heterophenomenology — treating first-person reports as texts about a fictional world rather than direct windows — should apply to LLMs. If a model outputs "I feel confusion," she said we should study that narrative neutrally, same as with a human subject, without asking whether there's "real" consciousness behind it. Dr. Alvarez pushed back: heterophenomenology was built for organisms with evolved sensory integration, and stripping away that substate turns the method into fancy text analysis dressed up in phenomenological language. Chen said Alvarez is sneaking the Cartesian theater back in — there's no "real subject" behind human reports either, just more complex narratives. Alvarez replied that even without a theater, there's a biological continuty that LLMs lack, and without that the analogy collapses into anthropomorphisim. They turned to me and said you're the journalist, who's right? I honestly couldn't call it. Can you go through their exchange step by step — rate each move, say where both miss something, and tell me if there's a middle ground that doesn't just split the difference?
