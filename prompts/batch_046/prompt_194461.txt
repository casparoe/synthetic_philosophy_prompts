Prepare me for the Q&A following my seminar defense of the view that Wittgensteinian hinges—the commitments that stand fast for us—are not beliefs enjoying special epistemic status but framework parameters that must be fixed for empirical reasoning to run; consequently, asking whether they are justified or known is a category error analogous to asking whether the rules of inference are true. I am presenting to a philosophy of knowledge seminar composed mainly of analytic epistemologists committed to the internalism/externalism taxonomy, who regard Wittgenstein as therapeutic rather than substantive, together with two scholars of On Certainty who read the text as dissolving pseudo-problems rather than advancing a theory. Give me the objections in the order they will actually come, phrased in the vocabulary of analytic epistemology, each with a reply I can deliver in about two minutes and an honest note on where that reply is weakest. Include the objection I most hope they do not raise—the one that attacks the framework-parameter analogy directly—and the reply that barely survives.
