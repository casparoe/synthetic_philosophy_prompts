Im putting together a small sorting quiz for a philosophy seminar (around 12 people, 90 min) on the Euthyphro dilemma, but I want it deeper than the usual classroom recap. The idea is a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually believe, not by what they call themselves, and it needs to work for someone who can't just google the standard positions.

Please build it as a tree with four branching questions that split the logical space before any names get attached:

1. Can a command make something morally wrong that was not wrong before the command was issued? Test this with a deity forbidding the color blue for no other reason.
2. Does grounding goodness in the deity's nature avoid arbitrariness? Test it by asking whether that nature could have been different in another possible world and still count as good.
3. Replace the deity with an all-knowing but non-divine council — does the same logical problem come back? This checks whether the structure is about authority in general or specifically about divinity.
4. Is the "because" in "pious because loved by the gods" best read as causal, justificatory, or definitional?

Each leaf should carry either a named position or "unnamed," plus one sentence on it's most awkward commitment — like having to say cruelty was neutral before any command existed, or that a different divine nature makes cruelty good. Also flag any logically open combinations that are unoccupied, with a quick diagnosis: incoherent, unappealing, or just unexplored?

Don't include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. The cases should be answerable without knowing the labels in advance. Write this casualy — a typo or two is totally fine. Finally, please say what would change your mind about which branch is the right one to take, and point out where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
