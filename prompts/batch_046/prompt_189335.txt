Next Thursday I defend at our epistemology seminar a reconstructed version of Moore's proof: I argue that its force lies not in inferring the existence of an external object from a premise about hands, but in exposing a performative contradiction in the skeptic's practice—specifically, that the skeptic asserts epistemic norms of humility that they violate the moment they treat skeptical possibilities as live in ordinary discourse. I claim Moore's gesture succeeds only as an ad hominem against implicit commitments, not as a direct refutation of Cartesian underdetermination, and I reject both the standard common-sense reading and the disjunctivist interpretation that treats perceptual knowledge as non-inferential. The audience comprises three justification-internalists who think perceptual knowledge requires accessible reasons, a Wittgenstein scholar who treats Moore's proof as a grammatical confusion about knowing, and a specialist in skeptical arguments from underdetermination. They have read my draft, which does not fully defend the normative-assertion framework I borrow from pragmatics. Give me the objections in probable order of appearance, phrased in each camp's vocabulary: first the internalists on whether assertion-norms can ground knowledge-claims without circularity; then the Wittgensteinian on whether I have mistaken a therapeutic dissolution for a proof; finally the skeptic on whether my reconstruction makes the proof available only to those who already reject skepticism. For each, specify what to concede, what to hold, and where the reply is weakest—especially my undefended move from pragmatic contradiction to metaphysical conclusion. Also include the objection I hope is suppressed: that if my account is right, then anyone with hands can run Moore's proof without philosophical training, which renders it either trivial or viciously question-begging against the skeptic who denies hands are known. I have fifteen minutes for responses, so indicate which objections merit a brief concession versus a sustained defense I should save for the open floor.
