Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets closing in a quatrain, with refrains A1 and A2 in the pattern abA1 / abA2—on a reflexive constitutional clause. The clause should contain its own amendment protocol, yet include a defeasibility condition: suspension is permitted only through a procedure that requires the clause to remain in force, while simultaneously authorising a constitutional court—whose authority derives solely from that clause—to disregard the suspension protocol whenever the court judges the suspension to violate the clause's core purpose. Let the two refrains enact the circular dependency between the rule's authority and its self-override, so that repetition performs the loop rather than merely stating it. The volta in the final quatrain should expose the precise logical tension: the clause is both the source of the court's power and the object of that power's capacity to void its own procedural constraints. Do not resolve the paradox; let the form hold it suspended. It should read as accurate to a legal philosopher—recognising the self-reference problem, not just borrowing its vocabulary. Please begin with the poem itself, with no preface or commentary.
