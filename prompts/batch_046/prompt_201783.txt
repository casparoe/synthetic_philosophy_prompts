Leo said that's just projeciton—our categories track practical interests and tech limits, and even 'species' is a mess with ring species, horizontal gene transfer, and hybrid zones; the joints are in our insturments, not the world.

Mara pushed back: even if species boundaries are fuzzy, there's a real distinction between a tiger and a rock that any culture with science converges on. Leo said convergence just shows shared cognitive bias's and shared instruments, not discovery of pre-existing lines. Leo said if you allow relational kinds you've given up on the classic 'joints' metaphor entirely, because joints are supposed to be in individual things, not between them. Mara said maybe the metaphor was always bad, and the real claim is just that some groupings support inductive inferences better than others, whether the grouping is intrinsic or relational.
