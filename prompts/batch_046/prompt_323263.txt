I'm preparing a seminar presentation on whether any form of moral responsibility can survive the regress of self-creation, and I'm particularly stuck on Galen Strawson's well-known argument that true responsibility requires being causa sui—something that is ultimately the cause of itself—while nothing can satisfy that condition because any feature of one's character traces back to heredity, environment, or prior causes outside one's control. Several participants have suggested that hierarchical or Frankfurt-style accounts of agency dissolve the problem, since a person can be responsible not for having a particular desire, but for identifying with it through a higher-order endorsement. Please make the argument that this hierarchical response fails to address Strawson's core concern because the identification itself stands in need of justification: if one asks why you endorse that particular second-order desire rather than another, and the answer appeals to yet further features of your psychology or history, we are launched into a regress that can only terminate in factors you did not choose. Take a committed position on whether this regress genuinely undermines hierarchical compatibilism or whether there is a principled stopping point—perhaps in the structure of rational agency itself—that avoids the demand for ultimate self-creation. I need this developed with definite reasons rather than a balanced survey, and please don't provide citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, since I'm working offline and want the reasoning constructed from the conceptual structure alone.
