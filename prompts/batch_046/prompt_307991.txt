Construct a decision tree that sorts metaphysical positions on causation in the tradition of al-Ghazālī's denial of necessary connections between natural causes and effects. The tree must begin with the root commitment: "Natural objects lack any intrinsic productive power over their effects," and then branch through conditional nodes so that it terminates in exactly six leaves. The required leaves are: strict occasionalism; moderate Ash'arite occasionalism that admits divine custom without object-powers; Thomistic secondary causation in which causes are genuinely efficient instruments; a dispositionalist-essentialist account that grounds regularities in real object-powers; a Humean-regularist stance denying both necessary connections and real powers; and one logically available but currently unnamed combination—identify it and briefly diagnose whether its absence is due to incoherence, theological cost, or historical neglect.

Use only the following three diagnostic questions as branch nodes, phrased as concrete commitments answerable without prior knowledge of the taxonomy: (1) If the customary course of nature were suspended, would affected objects exhibit hidden stable dispositions, display no behavior whatsoever, or be immediately replaced by direct divine action? (2) Is the relation between cause and effect best characterized as logical entailment, metaphysical grounding, or observed constant conjunction? (3) Does affirming secondary causation require that causes be genuinely productive, or only instrumentally mediative?

Flag any pair of leaves that these three questions fail to separate, and state what additional distinction would be required to split them. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or specific textual citations.
