hey, quick philosophy of history question. i've been noticing this pattern in writing about environmental causes—like volcanoes or disease—where the author describes the thing with almost strategic language, e.g. 'the eruption waited for the monsoon failure' or 'the pathogen navigated trade routes to outmaneuver quarantine.' then they immediately deny any literal agency, but in the next breath they use that same framing to say human actors barely had options, like the farmers 'couldnt have acted differently' because the climate was 'pursuing its own logic.' its not quite anthropomorphism, since they reject intentionality, and its not straight environmental determinism either, because they still allow that people technically made choices. it's more like using agent-metaphors to do real explanatory work that shrinks human agency without technically denying it exists. is there an established term for this exact rhetorical move? if not, how should it be defined, and how do we separate it from sloppy metaphor or from regular determinist arguments?
