Hey, I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm putting together a feature on the tollerance paradox. I'm not after the usual Popper lines everyone wheels out — I want to see if my gut instincts on some edge cases hide a real principle, or if I'm just being inconsistent. I'm especially curious about whether there's a split between social tollerance (letting people live as they choose) and epistemic tollerance (letting institutions treat harmful claims as legitimate).

My verdicts:

1. A democratic goverment should tolerate a registered party that campaigns peacefully to replace democracy with authoritarian rule. Suppressing them feels self-defeating.

2. A small religious congregation should be free to exclude women from internal leadership. That's social tollerance — internal autonomy.

3. A large corporation should not be allowed to force employees to sign away their right to speak publicly about working conditions. That's not tollerance; it's coercion that damages public knowledge.

4. A university should not invite a one-off speaker who claims some racial groups are naturally inferior. Even with Q&A, giving them the podium is epistemic tollerance I don't want.

5. But the same university should allow a student debate society to host that view in a structured rebuttal format. Here the institution isn't endorsing; it's facilitating critique.

6. I'm going back and forth on this: a city allows a peaceful protest that blocks hospital access for half an hour to highlight healthcare cuts. Is that acceptable social dissent, or does the direct harm make it intolerable?

So — can you lay out a precise principle, or maybe a small set of candidates, that captures these? For each clause, tell me which case motivates it, and then apply it to a situation I haven't listed — say, a public library deciding whether to stock books that deny well-established historical events. Be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save, especially case 6. And please write the answer in British English — no need for citations, just clear reasoning, thanks.
