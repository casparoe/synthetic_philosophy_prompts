They call it "function-first filtering," and basically if you submit a grant proposal that treats some trait as a spandrel or just a byproduct of some other adaptation, the review board automatically tanks it. They only fund projects that identify a specific adaptive purpose for whatever they're studying, whether it's a gene expression pattern or some animal behavior. She said it's not official policy, but everyone knows you can't get through review without framing your trait as optimized for something. I was taking a course on evolutionary theory last semester and we spent like two weeks on the Gould--Lewontin spandrels critique, which seemed to argue that assuming every feature is a perfect adaptation is exactly the mistake biologists should avoid.

So I'm trying to figure out: what philosophical commitments does this "function-first filtering" practice actually presuppose? Does it strictly need full-blown adaptationism to make sense, or is it compatible with other views about evolutionary explanation?
