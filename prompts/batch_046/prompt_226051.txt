Before I vote I want to understand what philosophical commitments the old three-block layout actually presupposes. Does it quietly assume that intellectual change comes in discrete ruptures rather than slow drift? Does it need a progress narrative where each era corrects the last? Or is it just scheduling that happens to fit several rival theories? Reconstruct the implicit view of historical change and knowledge, say which parts of our exams and reading lists actually depend on that view versus which would survive the long-module redesign, and point out where the practice is philosophically underdetermined — where both frameworks license the same assignment. Keep it grounded; I’m not a philosopher, I just need to spot the hidden assumtions before we redesign the syllabus.
