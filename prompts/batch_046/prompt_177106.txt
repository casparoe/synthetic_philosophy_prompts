Assume Marc Bloch—whose scholarship on rural societies and collective mentalités deliberately turned away from state-centered, event-driven narratives—were alive today and presented with two contemporaneous developments: microhistory's method of using singular, anomalous archival traces to recover subaltern mental worlds, and the use of large language models to detect statistical patterns across millions of digitized peasant records without interpretive proximity to any individual life. How might Bloch have assessed the tension between these approaches? Would he have regarded algorithmic macro-pattern extraction as a technologically accelerated return of the elite historiography he rejected, or as a complementary filter that could help identify the very anomalies worth close reading?
