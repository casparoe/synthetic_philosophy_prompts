Imagine that Isaiah Berlin—having lived to see the emergence of globally deployed, highly capable AI systems—were asked to advise on how such a system should determine whose values to serve. Given his defense of value pluralism, his claim that genuine goods are often incommensurable rather than merely competing, and his qualified endorsement of liberal neutrality, how do you think he would respond to the contemporary problem of value aggregation in AI alignment?

Or might he accept some practical compromise? Second, how would he assess attempts to avoid aggregation altogether by embedding procedural liberal neutrality—sometimes now described as constitutional AI—into a system's hard constraints, allowing diverse users to pursue different goods within bounded rules? Would he view this as an authentic preservation of pluralism, or as the covert universalization of one liberal doctrine among many equally legitimate ones? Third, drawing on his well-known distinction between negative and positive liberty, what would he say about the danger that an aligned system, even one designed to be neutral, becomes an instrument through which a dominant group exercises collective positive liberty over others, universalizing its particular conception of the good under the technical guise of objectivity? Fourth, and pushing slightly beyond his own writings, would he find any conceptual resources in distributed or federated models of value jurisdiction—where different digital environments operate under different normative frameworks rather than submitting to a single global objective—to address the tension between universal deployment and local pluralism?

Please develop this as a rigorous predictive reconstruction rather than a biographical fantasy. I am not asking for direct quotations, citations, or a reading list—only an argumentatively structured account of where Berlin's commitments would likely lead him, which practical concessions he might reluctantly allow, and where he would remain intransigent, particularly regarding any technical architecture that implicitly assumes values can be fully ordered or harmonized without remainder.
