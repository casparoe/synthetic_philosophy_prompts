I'm a software engineer who builds climate dashboards, and I keep noticing this contradiction I cant square. Our carbon-footprint calculator lists "have one less child" as saving like 58 tons of CO2 a year — bigger than going vegan or quitting flying — which treats reproduction as basically an environmental harm. But then I look at actual policy: every government I can think of offers child tax credits, freaks out over falling birth rates, and frames low fertility as an economic crisis. Meanwhile tech companies like mine brag about "net zero" but also design products to scale to billions of users, which only makes sense if we expect more people consuming stuff. So what theory of value is hiding underneath all this? Does the calculator presuppose that people are only valuable instrumentally, and that their carbon cost cancels out their welfare contribution? Or can a view where total welfare matters — not just per-capita emissions — make sense of both the pronatalist tax code and the climate tool? I want to know if these practices actually share one implicit philosophy, or if they're just incoherent. And if you really believed that a larger population could be good despite emissions, what would a policy built on that have to change — would it still encourage births but force them to be ultra-low-carbon, or does that break the logic of the calculator entirely? Sorry if this is rambling, I just dont have the philosophy vocabulary for it and keep seeing these seperate logics in the data.
