Consider a modified Maxwell's demon that interacts with a quantum gas: it distinguishes particles by energy eigenstate, maintains full quantum coherence without decoherence, and records measurement outcomes in a holographically-encoded boundary register rather than in local memory. One view holds that because there is no local bit-erasure, Landauer's principle imposes no thermodynamic cost; the opposing view holds that the Bekenstein-Hawking bound on the boundary register supplies an unavoidable entropic price, and that coherence itself may carry an information-theoretic cost not captured by classical Shannon theory. Give your credence that this specific demon could operate in sustained, net violation of the second law without eventually paying an information-theoretic cost of any kind. Decompose the reasoning into sub-questions: (a) whether quantum coherence can be maintained without hidden entropy production, (b) whether holographic storage genuinely evades local Landauer costs, (c) whether the Bekenstein-Hawking bound applies to this register in the required form, and (d) whether an as-yet-unidentified information-thermodynamic constraint closes any remaining loophole. Assign a rough probability to each, show how they combine into your final number, identify which component your credence is most sensitive to, and state what theoretical result or argument would most shift your estimate.
