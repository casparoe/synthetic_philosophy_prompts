Quick check on population ethics: I think I've spotted a flaw in how the repugnant conclusion is usually presented. The setup compares world A (1 million people, welfare 100 each) with world B (10 billion people, welfare 1 each) and says B is better because the total is higher. But in world A, the 10 billion people from B don't exist — they don't have welfare zero, they have no welfare assignment at all. So we're not comparing the same group with different outcomes; we're comparing two completely different membership sets, where A has 9.99 billion "missing entries" with undefinded welfare values. Doesn't that mean the comparison is broken in a way total utilitarianism never actually addresses? I know the non-identity problem deals with who gets harmed, but this seems like a prior question about whether you can sum welfare across disjoint populations at all. Is this a recognized objection, or am I just mixing up categories?
