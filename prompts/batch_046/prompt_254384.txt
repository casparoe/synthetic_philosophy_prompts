A recent essay in a general-interest magazine summarises the distinction between echo chambers and epistemic bubbles as follows: echo chambers are mainly algorithmic environments in which platforms filter out dissent before users see it, whereas epistemic bubbles are social structures in which members actively discredit outsiders and therefore never encounter opposing views. The essay claims that people inside epistemic bubbles retain full epistemic autonomy, because they could seek dissenting sources if they wished; the harm is therefore a voluntary form of 'trust-cascade isolation'. From this, the author concludes that breaking bubbles requires individual moral courage, while echo chambers can be fixed by redesigning recommendation systems. Something about the assignment of mechanisms feels reversed, and the autonomy claim seems too strong. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a common simplification, and what defenders of the original views—especially regarding whether epistemic bubbles involve active discrediting and whether members typically lack access rather than choose to ignore it—would reject. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then provide your reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question. Write in British English.
