I curate acquisitions for a small municipal archive with a fixed annual budget. Each year I must decide, among roughly 200 donated document collections, which to preserve and which to decline, knowing I cannot fully process or compare them all. I want a reusable decision procedure grounded in bounded rationality and satisficing—not in optimization—to apply repeatedly. Lay out the steps in order: how to set aspiration levels for preservation value without computing full utility; how to structure sequential search and determine when to stop; where judgment must remain because the theory does not settle the case; and how to handle a collection that falls just below the aspiration level but fills a unique gap in the holdings—should the aspiration adapt? Then run the procedure on three proposals: a complete but largely redundant run of a common local newspaper; fragmentary papers of an unknown labor organizer that are poorly preserved but unique; and a professionally catalogued collection that duplicates about eighty percent of existing holdings. Flag the cases where the procedure recommends an answer that conflicts with curatorial intuition, and say exactly what the curator should do in that gap.
