hey so i'm giving opening remarks tomorrow morning at this interdisiplinary workshop we're calling Value, Meaning, and Causal Weight—small group, about thirty people, half economic historians and Weber people who think culture genuinely explains things, half philosophers of social science who are skeptical that values can do causal work (they keep invoking interventionist accounts and saying meanings aren't wiggable). i need about five minutes of talk that frames the two days without being a lecture or a cop-out.

the idea should do real structural work: my own take is that Weber's Protestant ethic thesis is best read through elective affinity—Wahlverwandtschaft—not as a mechanical cause but as a kind of constraint that made capitalist rationalization selectable, something that made a path stick where it otherwise wouldn't. so culture isn't a billiard ball, but it also isn't just decoration. the problem is that if i say this plainly the philosophers hear "not really causal" and the historians hear "you're watering Weber down into correlation."

i want the remarks to open with the fact that we're in a room named after some local industrialist who almost certainly never read Weber—just to ground us—then set up the workshop's real question: what kind of "making a difference" do we allow in social explanation? the speech needs to welcome both camps honestly, suggest that resolving the tension probably means giving up either our narrow interventionist picture of causation or our reluctance to treat meanings as structural constraints rather than forces, and do it all without pretending i've solved it over coffee. please don't include a bibliography or assigned reading; half the room writes the literature. keep it casual, maybe a typo or two is fine, and make sure it lands for experts without losing the family members or grad students who tagged along.
