The proponent insists that this is not a prophecy about the inevitable future course of world history, but a generalization about the conditions under which complex bureaucracies and legal frameworks emerge.

Given this framing, please describe the most important implications of Popper’s poverty-of-historicism argument for evaluating whether such a claim could ever count as a genuine law of history. I am not asking for bibliographies, citations, or references to specific secondary literature; I want the conceptual structure of the argument laid out clearly on its own terms.
