I'm preparing a one-page prospectus for an independent six-month study on Plutarch's Parallel Lives and the uses of biography for moral learning. Rather than rehearse the familiar dichotomy between biography as exemplarity—the reader copies displayed virtues—and biography as narrative formation—the reader acquires practical wisdom through plotted experience—I want to frame a more specific and less discussed problem. Call it the problem of moral stereoscopy: in Plutarch's paired lives, such as Alexander with Caesar or Demosthenes with Cicero, the synkrisis sections do not simply rank one subject above the other; they place the same trait in two different political and cultural contexts so that the reader perceives the trait's shape and variability rather than its fixed moral value. If this reading holds, then hagiographic biography—which presents a single life as uniformly edifying—fails precisely where Plutarch succeeds, because it collapses that comparative depth into flat exemplarity. State the research question precisely: does the synkrisis structure in selected pairs actually perform this calibrating function, and if so, does it undermine the standard pedagogical defense of hagiography? Explain why existing positions in narrative ethics and virtue exemplarity do not settle the question, noting that both tend to treat biography as a direct text-to-reader relation rather than as a paired, comparative apparatus. Describe the method—close comparative reading of three or four pairs with careful attention to the synkrisis passages—state what a positive result would look like (for instance, consistent patterns of contextual variation that resist clear moral ranking) and what would count as failure (for instance, the comparisons consistently resolving into unambiguous hierarchies of virtue), and give an honest assessment of whether this scope is realistic for one researcher in six months. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation references; those will be added separately. Write in formal academic prose suitable for a fellowship or workshop prospectus.
