I've been chewing on Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model from that old GEB book - the idea that creativity is basically sliding continuous paramters on a conceptual theme until the landscape gets interesting, like Bach tweaking a fugue subject. I'm half-convinced this is actually the deep structure of human cognition: every 'insight' is just navigating idea-space and finding the right coordinate to twiddle.

But I'm injecting a twist that might be wrong: I think the model only holds for domains with genuinely continuous parameter spaces - music, visual geometry, maybe language style - and it's a categroy error in discrete normative spaces like ethics or legal interpretation. There's no smooth gradient between 'slightly more utilitarian' and 'slightly more deontological'; creativity there isn't interpolation but jumping between disconnected manifolds, and Hofstadter's knobs literally don't exist. People overapply the model because they mistake linguistic analagy for structural similarity.

So my position is: knob-twiddling explains creative cognition in continuous domains but fails everywhere else, and defenders of the universal version need to either show hidden continuous dimensions in law/ethics or admit it's just a metaphor. What would count against either half? I want the evidence that should move me - maybe formal work on parameter spaces in legal reasoning, or examples of artistic creativity that are genuinely discontinuous and can't be reconstructed as knob-sliding. Also be honest: is the discrete/continuous distinction just a convenient dodge that insulates the view? What would its defenders concede actually counts against them?
