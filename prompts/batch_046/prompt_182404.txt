Following a recent bereavement that has made me think more concretely about the boundaries of moral consideration, I want to test whether panpsychist commitments actually undermine conventional sentientist ethics or merely redescribe them. Please give me your actual credence in the following compound claim: if panpsychism is true—specifically a version in which phenomenal properties are fundamental but macro-level suffering requires an organizational condition analogous to the combination problem's integrative binding—then restricting direct moral status to organisms with centralized nervous systems is not arbitrary anthropocentrism but tracks a genuine ontological boundary in the distribution of suffering.

Decompose your credence into four terms, each with a number between 0 and 1 and a single line of justification:
(a) The prior probability that some form of panpsychism is true.
(b) The conditional probability that, given panpsychism, suffering requires integrative binding rather than being distributed uniformly across micro-physical entities.
(c) The conditional probability that, given (a) and (b), such binding occurs primarily or exclusively in biological nervous systems rather than in other complex physical systems.
(d) The conditional probability that, given (a)–(c), this ontological boundary genuinely rescues sentientist ethics from the charge of arbitrariness rather than leaving it only contingently justified.

State how you combine these terms into an overall credence, then identify which term you are least confident in and specify what philosophical argument or empirical finding would move it most. Present your reasoning clearly, and conclude with the final number in the format \answer{...}.
