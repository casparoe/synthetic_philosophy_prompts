I'm writing up my philosophical profile for a graduate seminar on distributive justice and want an internal consistency check before I present. My current package on interpersonal utility comparison runs as follows. First, (a) I take such comparisons to be meaningful and indispensable for any serious normative economics. Second, (b) I deny that they can be reduced to revealed preference or behavioral choice, since preferences may be adaptive, misinformed, or shaped by unjust background conditions. Third, (c) I think the appropriate method is an extended sympathy procedure—imaginatively projecting oneself into another's experiential state—disciplined by reflective equilibrium rather than by external behavioral metrics. Fourth, (d) I believe these comparisons license genuinely cardinal judgments about the magnitude of welfare differences, not merely ordinal rankings. Fifth, (e) I hold a version of welfare pluralism: dimensions such as achievement, enjoyment, and autonomy are not always commensurable even within a single life, yet cross-personal comparison remains possible through a practical translation manual constructed within the comparison procedure itself. Sixth, (f) I reject any objective, mind-independent unit of well-being, treating measurements as constructed assessments rather than discovered quantities. Seventh, (g) I want to endorse the Harsanyi aggregation framework for social choice, though I reject the premise that individual utility functions are already cardinally comparable prior to any interpersonal aggregation procedure.

Do these commitments actually hang together? I am especially concerned about hidden tensions between (d) and (e)—if welfare dimensions are incommensurable, how can differences be genuinely cardinal?—and between (c) and (g)—can sympathy-generated comparisons supply the precise formal inputs that the aggregation framework demands? Are there other pairs that only appear incompatible but dissolve on closer inspection? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what commitment would have to give and at what cost, and indicate whether the least costly revision pushes the package toward standard Harsanyi utilitarianism, toward pure ordinalism, or toward some hybrid position. I am not asking for a bibliography or literature review—just a logical audit of the package as I have stated it.
