For the plot I invented this fringe idea called "resonant monadism": each monad isn't totally windowless like Leibniz insisted, but its internal perceptions get "tuned" to other monads through a shared harmonic resonance that updates in real time—no causal doors, but not fully isolated either. It's my own made-up twist, but I want the scene to feel philosophically honest, so don't worry about citing papers or anything.

Given what Leibniz actually believed about monads, windowlessness, and his pre-establised harmony, do you think he'd see resonant monadism as a clever patch for the combination problem, or would he reject it because it smuggles interaction back into a system he designed specifically to avoid interaction? I'm trying to decide whether he nods slowly or throws the seminar chair, and I need to know which part of his system he'd defend to the death and which part he might secretly want to bend.
