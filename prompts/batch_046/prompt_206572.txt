Here is the position I wish you to stress-test. I call it the Chronological-Immunisation Demarcation. It claims that pseudohistory is not one uniform methodological failure but divides into two sharply different kinds. Type A comprises chronologically revisionist pseudohistories—exemplified by Fomenko’s New Chronology—which advance concrete, testable reorderings of dated events, asserting, for instance, that astronomical records and dynastic lists are systematically misaligned by centuries and can be corrected by a compressed timeline. Because these claims commit to specific synchronisms, they are vulnerable to contradiction by primary-source evidence and astronomical data; they are pseudohistorical in a strict sense because they risk being empirically wrong. Type B comprises archaeologically mythic narratives—exemplified by Atlantis theories or ancient-astronaut claims—which are structured so that the absence of material traces is explained in advance by cataclysmic erasure, extraterrestrial departure, or deep-time geological recycling. These are not pseudohistory but modern mythology, because they are immunised against archaeological falsification by design. Only Type A, therefore, falls within the scope of a scientific history that can correct it.
