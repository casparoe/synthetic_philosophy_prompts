For the past several months I have been trying to make sense of panpsychism without any formal philosophical training, and the issue that keeps returning is what people call the combination problem. The basic idea, as I understand it, is that if even the smallest physical entities carry some minimal form of consciousness, then we must explain how countless micro-subjects combine into the single, unified awareness that I seem to possess. Recently I encountered a distinction between phenomenal fusion—where micro-experiences literally merge into one new macro-experience, losing their individual identities—and phenomenal bonding—where they remain ontologically distinct but are linked in a direct relational structure, comparable to separate voices arranged in harmony rather than blended into a single tone. It strikes me that if panpsychists adopt the bonding model, they can sidestep the combination problem entirely, since there is no longer any need to explain how many subjects become one; there is only ever a complex arrangement of many. Yet this makes me wonder whether such a move actually resolves the difficulty or simply renounces the phenomenon that requires explanation—namely, my sense of being one continuous thinker rather than a coordinated assembly of particle-minds. Is the combination problem therefore not genuinely about metaphysical unification at all, but rather about a mistaken presupposition that consciousness must take the form of a single subject?
