My thesis is that Glaucon's ring of Gyges should be read not as the canonical challenge to moral motivation—why be just if you can escape detection—but as a diagnostic tool that exposes a hidden dependency in Plato's psychology: specifically, that the ring's invisibility doesn't just remove external sanctions, it dissolves the conditions for what I call reflective ownership of one's actions, which is a prerequisite for rational agency in the Republic's own tripartite framework. I sketch this by comparing the ring to modern moral luck cases but reversing the usual diagnosis: it's not that luck reveals morality's fragility, but that total invisibility reveals agency's fragility, and therefore can't serve as a stable counterfactual.
