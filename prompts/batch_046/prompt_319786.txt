hey, writing a quick essay for a phil of rationality seminar and wanted to run the core argument by someone before i commit to the framing. i'm playing with an idea i'm calling the 'self-dealing pump' — it's like a money pump but there's no external bookie, just the agent bleeding utility into their own commitment machinery.

the gist: at t1 i prefer A over B, but i anticipate that by t2 my preferences will shift so B looks better. so i set up a costly precommitment device now to lock in A. the problem is my model of my future self is never perfect. if i'm sligtly off, i pay the commitment cost, my t2 self still prefers B, and then pays to reverse the lock. the value doesn't go to anyone else; it just dissapates into the mechanism. over iterations this can happen even if my underlying preferences are perfectly consistent across time, as long as my predictions of those preferences have noise.

my essay argues this means diachronic rationality can't just be about preference consistency or avoiding Dutch books — we also need some bound on the 'friction cost' of intertemporal commitment. otherwise you can be rational in the standard sense and still get pumped dry by your own planning apparatus.

does this distinction actually hold up? i'm especially unsure about the middle claim that dissipation inside the mechanism is philosophically different from ordinary transaction costs. also, am i just redescribing the sophistication vs. commitment literature without realizing it? and should i be engaging with the old time-neutrality debates here? i don't need citations, just want to know if the conceptual move is coherent and where it's weakest. also feel free to point out if my reasoning has a typo-level mistake, though i know the draft itself is a bit messy lol
