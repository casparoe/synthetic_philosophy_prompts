Hey — quick question for the group. I've been thinking about that old wasp example (the one where it checks the burrow in exactly the same sequence every time, even if you move things around). There's a related pattern I keep seeing in free will debates that I don't have a clean label for. It's not just single-track rigidity. It's more like: the agent has a whole tree of hard-wired fallback routines. You block the first path, and it doesn't improvise — it just exectutes fallback B, which is itself totally stereotypd. Scale that up and you get something that looks really flexible from the outside, because it handles lots of situations differently, but inside it's still just a big bundle of little sphex-like loops. People use this to defend human freedom ('we adapt, we're not robots!'), and then the reply is always 'that's just sphexishness with more branches.' Is there an actual term for this distributed or multi-layered rigidity? Or for the mistake of counting a larger repertore of fixed responses as genuine flexibility? I want to be able to point out that having ten pre-set scripts isn't the same as open-ended reasoning, but I also want to avoid just saying 'it's still sphexishness' when the structure is genuinely different. What's the right boundary to draw here?
