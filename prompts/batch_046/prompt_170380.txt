Write an essay examining the internal tension in Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's arguments for women's education. She contends that women should study not only scripture but also the natural sciences, logic, and secular literature, on the grounds that a broader intellectual formation is required for true understanding of divine things. The controversy is whether this argument establishes women's education as an autonomous good or merely instrumentalizes it within a theological hierarchy—treating natural philosophy as a handmaid whose value is exhausted by its service to religious interpretation. Weigh the case that she advances a genuinely universalist epistemology, available to women regardless of their social or religious role, against the case that her universalism is strategically contained: it expands access to knowledge only insofar as that knowledge remains subordinate to an ultimately clerical and masculine authority. Do not end non-committally; defend a specific verdict about whether her position should be read as radically emancipatory or as a cautiously bounded reform. In reaching your conclusion, address what this distinction means for treating her as a precursor to modern feminist epistemology.
