I'm serving on my university's Student Research Integrity Committee, and next Thursday we must decide a case that turns on issues we've been examining in my philosophy of collective agency seminar. A flat-hierarchy student collective—using a rotating editorial protocol in which each member authors one section and a designated integrator assembles the whole without requiring line-by-line endorsement—submitted a single policy brief to the campus sustainability board. The brief contained a fabricated data point in its fourth section; the member who wrote that section has confessed, while the other four members maintain they never reviewed or endorsed that specific claim and should not be held collectively liable. The sustainability board has asked our committee whether to recommend collective disqualification, which would bar the entire collective from future university funding and retract the brief in the collective's name, or individual liability, which would sanction only the confessing member and allow the brief's other sections to stand under the remaining members' names. Our charter permits only one of these two recommendations. Please draft the formal committee opinion I should circulate before the meeting: it should state a clear recommendation—collective disqualification or individual liability—with concrete reasoning that addresses whether the rotating, non-hierarchical structure actually constituted joint action with shared responsibility for the whole product, or whether it was merely an aggregation of individual contributions with minimal shared commitment. The memo should also engage seriously with the strongest argument for the option you reject—particularly the claim that joint authorship, even under distributed conditions, creates mutual accountability for the final integrated text—without relying on named theorists or bibliographic references. Write in formal institutional style with an actionable bottom line.
