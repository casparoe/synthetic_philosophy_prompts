I'm a novelist working on literary fiction and I'm applying for a small six-month interdisiplinary grant that pairs writers with philosophers for part-time research. I need a tight two-page proposal. The idea came from a manuscript I'm drafting: there's a ghostwriter forging letters in a dead philosopher's voice, not to deceive about historical facts but to make the recipient feel a specific moral weight — framing grief as meaningful rather than asserting any claim. That made me wonder about testimony. The standard debate pits reductionists, who say accepting testimony is just believing on the authority of another's belief, against anti-reductionists, who say there's an irreducible social reason to trust. But both camps seem to treat testimony as always propositional: you recieve information and form a belief.

I want to study whether there's room for what I'm calling 'orientation testimony' — cases where you take someone as authoritative not for a fact, but for a way of framing or reorganizing your own experience. Think of a first-person narrator whose voice doesn't just inform you but shifts how you perceive moral situations, and you treat that shift as warranted because of who is speaking, not just because of argument. The precise question: does orientation testimony collapse into belief-plus-influence, or does it force a revision of both reductionism and anti-reductionism? Write the proposal with: the question stated exactly, why current answers leave it open (they assume the deliverable is always a belief with propositional content), the method broken down month by month — month one cataloguing narrative examples, month two mapping them onto the philosophical accounts, month three arguing for collapse or revision, months four to six testing objections and writing — plus an honest failure condition (maybe it turns out to be just rhetoric, not epistemology). Say what a useful result looks like for non-philosophers, maybe writers thinking about narrative authority. Don't include a bibliography or citations; I'll fill those in myself. Keep the scope realistic for part-time work. End with one line starting 'Bottom line:' summarizing the proposal's core claim in a single sentence.
