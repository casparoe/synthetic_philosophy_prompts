Write a deadpan parody of a municipal zoning board’s public-comment report that applies population axiology to urban housing policy. The board must decide whether to approve high-density “reproductive licensing districts” that maximize total welfare under a utilitarian calculus, and the report should treat the repugnant conclusion not as a reductio but as an acceptable planning trade-off—arguing that a very large population living barely above zero welfare is preferable to a smaller, thriving neighborhood, provided aggregate utility is maximized. Frame the argument through a fictional metric invented for the report, “cumulative well-being density per square mile,” and translate standard premises—total versus average utilitarianism, the intuition that more lives at barely positive welfare can outweigh fewer lives at high welfare—into bureaucratic language about sewer-capacity projections, parking-minimum adjustments, and recreational-area ratios. The voice should be technically precise, morally neutral, and sustained across about 600 words in numbered sections (Executive Summary, Methodology, Findings, Recommendations). The satire must come from the straight-faced endorsement: commit within the parody to the board’s logic that maximizing headcount-adjusted utility is sound urban planning, rather than presenting it as one option among many. After the piece, include a brief explicit note identifying which real positions in population ethics you are exaggerating or simplifying—particularly the move from Parfit’s original formulation to zoning metrics—and flag where the invented well-being-density measure takes liberties with standard welfare economics. Keep the report readable to someone unfamiliar with the literature, but precise enough that a specialist recognizes the actual philosophical targets being satirized.
