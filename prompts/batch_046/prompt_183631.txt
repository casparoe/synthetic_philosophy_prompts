Give the most important implications of interpreting Wittgenstein's hinge propositions in On Certainty as locally indexed to particular forms of life rather than as universal conditions of rationality. Specifically, consider a scenario in which two communities maintain incompatible hinge commitments—say, one treating ancestral testimony as epistemically basic and another treating only repeatable observation as basic—and neither can be refuted by evidence the other accepts. What does this localist reading imply for the possibility of rational disagreement between them, for the notion of epistemic progress across frameworks, and for whether accumulated practical failure can ever provide normative pressure to revise one's hinges? Keep the analysis conceptual rather than ethnographic, and avoid treating any single framework as default.
