I defend a hybrid view of metaphysical grounding: ontological dependence is real and directional, but fundamentality is graded rather than binary, measured by explanatory coverage within a theoretical framework, and anchored by a framework-independent "hard floor" of spacetime regions with intrinsic physical properties. I further hold that attempts to eliminate this floor in favor of pure relational structuralism fail, because they must smuggle in a primitive "structural priority" relation that either collapses back into standard grounding or generates a vicious regress. What would count decisively against this position? I want the specific conceptual arguments, formal results, or physical discoveries that should move me — not generic anti-realist complaints, but concrete threats such as: whether a mature structuralist physics could genuinely eliminate spacetime regions without explanatory loss; whether documented failures of transitivity in grounding relations undermine my graded measure; whether quantum entanglement or holographic dualities show that the supposed hard floor is itself derivative; and whether my view has been insulated from refutation by allowing the floor to retreat whenever physics advances. Rank these defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish those that defenders of this hybrid account would concede from those they would dismiss, and deliver a committed judgment on whether the position is well-insulated and whether that insulation is a theoretical virtue or a vice. Write in American English.
