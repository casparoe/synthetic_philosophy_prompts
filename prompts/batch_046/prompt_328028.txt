I serve as a novelist on the Meridian Foundation's Ethics and Program Committee, and by Thursday I must circulate a written opinion before our Friday vote on a proposed exhibition titled The Hidden Harvest. The proposal compares wild-animal mortality in mechanized crop cultivation against direct slaughter in beef and poultry systems, but it introduces a graded aggregation metric—weighting invertebrate and small-mammal deaths by neural complexity rather than by binary sentience—to calculate suffering-adjusted mortality per edible calorie. On this framing, monoculture crop production for plant-based diets generates greater aggregate suffering than pasture-raised beef, though less than factory-farmed chicken, yet the underlying field-fauna density estimates remain deeply contested. Our charter forbids funding works that present disputed comparative moral metrics as settled fact. Draft the memo I will circulate: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions and a concrete timeline; include reasoning the applicant could respond to; and treat with real seriousness the proposal's strongest claim—that dietary ethics which ignore hidden wild-animal suffering privilege visible harm over invisible harm—without merely invoking the charter's prohibition.
