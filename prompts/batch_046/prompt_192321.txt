State the question so that it admits a determinate answer: specify whether you are asking about metaphysical causation, explanatory priority, or the possibility of substrate autonomy under symbolic constraint. Explain why both eliminative physicalism and strong emergentism leave the intermediate case of the careenium-simmball relation unresolved—particularly when symbolic rules violate neither substrate conservation laws nor causal closure—so that a third option is genuinely open. Propose an analytical approach that distinguishes three grades of substrate influence (correlational tracking, dynamic constraining, and constitutive determination) and applies them to a constructed model case rather than to empirical neuroscience, making clear why conceptual analysis rather than laboratory data is the right tool. Describe what a positive result would look like (a coherent account in which the careenium pushes the substrate in at least the constraining sense without collapsing into the other two grades) and what would count as failure (a demonstration that all apparent downward causation reduces to one of the non-causal grades). Give an honest assessment of scope: identify the single conceptual distinction—likely between rule-governed correlation and causal production—that, if it proves unstable under pressure, would make the project too large for six weeks, and say how you would narrow the proposal if that happens.
