Write a villanelle — five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains that loop back like a stuck gear. The theme is disjunctivism versus the argument from illusion, but push it in a slightly odd direction: don't treat hallucination as a botched copy of seeing, treat it as a state that never had perceptual content to begin with — like a belief that borrows visual authority without ever being in the seeing-game. Refrain A (the illusion's voice) should be something like there is no shared mental kind between us, and Refrain B (the argument from illusion) should insist that the feel is identical so the kind must match. Use the tercets to lay out the naive picture — hidden variables of experience, the highest common factor, the sense that if it feels like a duck it quacks like one — then let the turn happen in the fourth tercet where the disjunctivist points out that in veridical sight you are genuinely open to the world, whereas in the hallucination there is just a misfiring mechinism producing an authoritative-looking blank. The quatrain should crash the refrains together so the reader sees that shared phenomenlogy is just grammatical shadow-play, not ontology. Make it precise enough that someone who teaches direct realism wouldn't wince, but keep the language earthy — no lo, the veil of perception nonsense. I'd read this at the end of a seminar on direct realism, so it needs to feel like an argument wearing rhyme, not a poem wearing philosophy.
