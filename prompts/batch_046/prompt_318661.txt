I'm reading counterfactual history for fun and I keep running into the same glitch. Someone asks what if the 1848 revolutions had actually won, and at first they say everything would diverge wildly, but then they trace a chain: industrialization still happens, nationalism still rises, so by 1914 the alliances look about the same. The change gets absorbed. Then with the telegraph never being invented they do the opposite—communication stays slow, diplomacy breaks differently, WWI might not happen. Same person, both arguments, never noticing they switched from treating history as a rigid pipe to treating it as a loose web. Is there a name for this specific failure mode, where a counterfactual quietly swaps contingent links for necessary ones just to protect the actual timeline? How is it different from plain determinism or from path-dependancy? I don't need a bare label; I want the exact shape of the mistake, what it is not, and where its edges are.
