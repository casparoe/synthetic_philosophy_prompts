student answer:
Panpsychism holds that consciousness is fundamental and present even at the basic physical level. The combination problem asks how these micro-level conscious states come together to form macro-level conscious states like human experience. This problem was first named by William James, though Chalmers made it famous in The Conscious Mind. Micropsychism claims fundamental particles have micro-experiences and these combine via physical integration - kind of like how atoms combine into molecules - to produce unified minds. Cosmopsychism, which is basically the same idea as Russellian monism, claims the universe itself is one big conscious entity and human minds are just parts of it; this avoids combination because there's only one subject to begin with, though it creates a decombination problem. Chalmers actually thinks cosmopsychism is the best version of panpsychism since it dodges the hard part. One response to the combination problem within micropsychism is that experiences can literally sum together like quantities, so there's no mystery once you accept the basic micro-experiences exist.
