I'm working on a handout for a grad seminar about moral emotions and whether societies can engineer them. I need a taxonomy — like a decision tree — that sorts out positions on whether guilt or shame is the better moral emotion, and whether a culture can actually choose which one domintes.

The branching questions should be concrete, not label-based. First node: when someone breaks a serious promise to a close friend, is the right emotional response mainly an internal sense of having violated a bond, or mainly a sense of being seen as unreliable?

Second node: does that emotion still do its moral work if nobody else ever finds out?

Third node: once the emotion hits, is there a standard social script (apology ritual, temporary exile, etc.) that reintergrates the person, or does the feeling itself push them to repair things without any scripted path?

Fourth node: imagine a state deliberately redesigned schooling and public ritual to suppress one emotion and amplify the other over generations. Could that shift actually stick, or would the old emotional ecology collapse back?

Each leaf should be a named position or marked 'unnamed / unstable' if the combo is empty. I want these leaves present: guilt-monist, shame-functionalists, hybrid reintegrationists, culture-voluntarists, culture-determinists. Plus at least two empty slots with a one-sentence diagnosis — is it incoherent, just ugly, or merely unexplored?

Also flag any pair of leaves the tree can't separate. And phrase every node as a question you'd answer without knowing where it leads — no 'do you hold view X?' stuff.

This is for a model with no web access, so don't ask for bibliographies, ethnographic citations, or reading lists. Just build the tree. Oh, and I don't care about perfect spelling — there's probably a typo in here somewhere.
