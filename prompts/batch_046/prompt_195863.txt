A colleague has offered the following argument, which extends Frankfurt-style reasoning about moral responsibility into epistemology and then claims to derive a reductio. (1) Frankfurt cases establish that an agent can be morally responsible for an act A even when she lacks the ability to do otherwise, provided A issues from her own deliberative structure and a counterfactual intervener — who would have compelled A had she hesitated — never actually intervenes. (2) This undermines the principle of alternative possibilities for action: responsibility requires only an appropriate source, not a leeway condition. (3) Doxastic states are also governed by reasons and can be evaluated for responsibility; therefore, if the source condition is sufficient in the practical domain, it should be sufficient in the epistemic domain too. (4) Consider a dispositional blackout belief: a subject reasons her way to p, but an undetected cognitive mechanism is present that would have induced p regardless of her reasoning had she shown any resistance; since she never resisted, the mechanism remains dormant. (5) By direct parity with Frankfurt, she is doxastically responsible for p despite having no available alternative to believing p. (6) The same structure applies to rigid perceptual processing: in standard conditions she cannot help but believe she sees red, and the blackout mechanism is just the inflexibility of her perceptual apparatus, which would have overridden any contrary inclination. (7) Hence she is blameworthy for false perceptual beliefs she was cognitively unable to avoid, which is absurd. (8) So the Frankfurt rejection of the principle of alternative possibilities must fail; we need a leeway condition for both action and belief.

My colleague insists the flaw lies between steps (3) and (5), but she cannot identify it precisely. I am inclined to favour keeping (2), yet I am unsure whether saving it requires giving up (6) instead. Which step should be rejected, what makes it look plausible, and what are the costs of the main alternatives? Please answer in American English.
