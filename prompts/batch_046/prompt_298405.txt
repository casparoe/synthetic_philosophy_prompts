I'm presenting at an interdisciplinary workshop on collective decision-making, and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The audience is split three ways: formal social-choice theorists who know the impossibility results and the logical structure of the discursive dilemma in judgment aggregation; democratic theorists who care about deliberative legitimacy and are skeptical of formal rules that suppress disagreement; and one public-health policy analyst who will ask whether any of this can be implemented in a real pandemic-preparedness panel.

My thesis: the standard discursive dilemma—where premise-based aggregation yields one collective conclusion and conclusion-based aggregation yields another—can be dissolved without collapsing into either pure form. I propose replacing binary premise judgments with graded epistemic plausibility scores, aggregated through a non-linear pooling rule, combined with a coherence constraint that prohibits adopting any collective conclusion that falls below the pooled plausibility of a premise on which it logically depends. I argue this preserves consistency at the collective level while treating disagreement over empirical predictions and normative weights as revisable inputs to an ongoing inquiry, not as contradictions to be voted away.

Prepare me for the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased as each subgroup would phrase them. For each: identify its real force, what I should concede immediately, the core of a two-minute reply, and the weakest point in that reply. Include the objection I am dreading—the one that asks whether my coherence constraint secretly reintroduces conclusion-based aggregation through the back door. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need reasoning I can deploy from memory. Keep the focus on the logical, normative, and practical vulnerabilities of the proposal itself.
