I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about universal basic income, going by my gut on specific setups. Every adult in a wealthy country gets $1200/month with no conditions attached: feels right. Same deal for every adult in a poor country funded entirely by foreign donors: also feels right, maybe even more so. But tie the payment to passing a basic civic literacy test: suddenly wrong, like it stops being universal in the moral sense. Give it to people serving life sentences: genuinely unsure, they have material needs but there's something that feels off about unconditional cash specifically for them. Now picture a near-future where AI handles 95% of productive labor and there's basically no jobs -- distributing the surplus as cash feels necessary, but also different in kind, like it's an ownership dividend rather than income support. Replace cash with guaranteed housing and food vouchers for everyone: feels more paternalistic even if the dollar value is higher. What's the principle that gets most of these? Is there one that can also explain why a means-tested version (only below median wealth) feels like a budget compromise rather than a betrayal of the idea? And what does all this say about the prisoner case I keep going back and forth on?
