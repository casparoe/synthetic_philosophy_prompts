Consider the evaluative norm pervasive in contemporary theoretical physics—evident in peer-review standards, funding priorities, and the pedagogical framing of the Standard Model—whereby leaving a fundamental parameter unnaturally fine-tuned, with no dynamical explanation for its value spanning dozens of orders of magnitude relative to a more fundamental scale, is treated as a defeasible scientific defect. Notably, this defect is often considered remedied not only by empirical discovery but also by supplying explanatory frameworks—symmetry principles, relaxation mechanisms, or multiverse scenarios—that may themselves lie beyond direct experimental reach. What philosophical commitments regarding scientific explanation, probabilistic reasoning over parameter spaces, and the ontological status of physical constants does this institutional practice presuppose? Does it strictly entail a principle of sufficient reason applied to the laws of nature, or can it be reconstructed as a heuristic bias toward simplicity lacking independent metaphysical import? And is the practice internally consistent with the simultaneous acceptance of anthropic reasoning, which explicitly abandons the demand for dynamical explanation of the very same parameter ranges?
