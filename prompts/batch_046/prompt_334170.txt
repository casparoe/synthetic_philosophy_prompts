Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the extended mind thesis: the functional-integrationists, who hold that reliably coupled external resources can literally be parts of cognitive processes when they meet conditions of accessibility and automatic endorsement; and the boundary-defenders, who accept deep cognitive embedding and coupled dynamics but deny that anything lacking biological or agentive ownership conditions can be constitutive of cognition rather than a mere tool. Write in American English. Structure the document as follows: first, a numbered section of agreed claims about what coupling requires, about the difference between causal scaffolding and constitutive inclusion, and about the formal properties that external resources must have to even be candidates; second, a disagreements table that gives each camp's precise position in wording they would accept, focusing especially on whether functional isomorphism alone is sufficient and whether an intrinsic "ownership" or "non-derived content" condition must be added; third, the cruxes—specify at least two, including whether long-term transactive memory systems in close partnerships should be analyzed as extended belief or as distributed tool-use, and whether a hypothetical mathematician's haptic-feedback proof-glove that stores intermediate tensor states through muscle-memory retrieval patterns counts as part of reasoning or as an advanced prosthetic; fourth, for each crux, state the case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's explicit prediction about what the correct analysis should be. Keep the prompt focused and avoid any request for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Do not include verbatim quotations from published texts. The aim is to separate substantive disagreement from verbal confusion and to identify what would actually move either camp.
