Write a deadpan parody of a peer-reviewed empirical research article—title, abstract, keywords, and a brief Results and Discussion section totaling about 600 words—reporting a fictional neuroimaging study that claims to have localized Adam Smith’s “sympathy” and “impartial spectator” in discrete brain circuits. Invent plausible technical details—fMRI protocols, regions such as the temporoparietal junction or medial prefrontal cortex, invented statistical thresholds—and include a short fabricated reference list in correct APA format with no real citations. The tone must remain perfectly serious, as if published in an interdisciplinary journal eager to “operationalize” philosophy, so that the humor arises solely from the mismatch between reductive empirical method and Smith’s actual framework in The Theory of Moral Sentiments. A reader familiar with Smith should be able to identify exactly which interpretive distortions are being exaggerated, and the piece should remain intelligible without requiring any knowledge of real neuroscience literature.
