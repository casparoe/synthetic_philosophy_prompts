I'm putting together a prospectus for a small summer workshop on philosophical theology, and I've got this half-baked idea that might turn into a real paper if I frame it right. Everyone talks about Molinism like it's just "God knows counterfactuals of freedom and uses them to actualize worlds," but I keep thinking about those branching-time models where counterfactuals are basically fixed-points in a modal lattice. What if we treat middle knowledge not as direct acquaintance with some spooky counterfactual fact, but as a selection function over worlds that respects a "no-guessing" constraint — God only gets to know a creature's free choice in circumstance C if it strictly follows from that creature's essential modal profile plus the acutal world-structure, without peeking at the future?

The existing debate between Molinists and anti-Molinists — the open-theist crowd, the hard determinists, people like Plantinga — mostly fights over whether counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are metaphysically possible and whether they constrain divine freedom. But they usually take for granted that if those counterfactuals exist, God knows them in some direct, propositional way. Nobody asks whether we can reconstruct middle knowledge using only constructive modal resources: essentially asking if God's knowledge of what I *would* do is derivable from my essential properties plus the logic of the branch, rather than being an independent primitive. If it is derivable, Molinism collapses into something closer to presentist determinism; if it isn't, then middle knowledge requires a kind of modal access that looks suspiciously like foreknowledge of actual future contingents, which undermines the whole point.
