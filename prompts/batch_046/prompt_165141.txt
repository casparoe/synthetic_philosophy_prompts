I'm trying to figure out what we owe dead people when we dramatize them, going by my gut on cases. A biopic inventing a private conversation between two dead scientists that captures their real disagreement, using made-up dialogue: seems okay. A movie showing a dead activist secretly doubting her cause, zero evidence, just for grit: feels wrong. A novel giving a dead minor diplomat a fictional childhood trauma that doesn't contradict the record: fine. A documentary using AI voice synthesis to have a dead poet read her own poem in a reconstructed voice: I'm okay with it, even though she never recorded it. But a drama moving a dead leader's death back six months so she can witness an event she missed, then showing her react in character: that feels like cheating. Meanwhile inventing a dead musician had a secret addiction with no basis: clearly not okay. Is there one principle here—about falsifying chronology, inventing mental states, not exploiting the dead, or whatever? The AI-voice case and the moved-death case seem to pull in seperate directions, so can a single principle cover both? What's the best account, and which of my verdicts does it have to overturn?
