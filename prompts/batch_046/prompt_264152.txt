Write an essay examining whether moral compromise between genuinely incommensurable value systems—such as a deontological framework centered on inviolable rights and a consequentialist framework centered on aggregate welfare—can be coherently modeled as a jurisdictional exchange of permissions rather than as a weighted aggregation within a single metric. Specifically, evaluate the proposal that institutions or agents might achieve gains from trade by granting each framework limited jurisdiction over distinct domains of decision—analogous to contractual exchange between parties with incompatible utility functions—without either side conceding that the other’s values are reducible to its own currency. Address three objections in depth: the framing objection (that jurisdictional boundaries are themselves justified in terms of one framework, smuggling in monism), the measurement objection (that incommensurability blocks the comparative judgment required to recognize a mutual gain), and the inalienability objection (that moral rights and duties are not fungible goods and therefore cannot be traded or delegated). Reach and defend a substantive verdict: is jurisdictional moral compromise conceptually stable, or does it necessarily collapse back into implicit commitment to one value system? Do not end on a purely agnostic or non-committal conclusion.
