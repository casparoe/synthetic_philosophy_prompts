Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a Jamesian permissivist camp and a Cliffordian evidentialist camp, focused on the ethics of belief under conditions of what I will call structural evidential opacity: a context in which available evidence symmetrically supports two or more mutually exclusive explanatory frameworks, and in which an agent's adoption of one framework operates not merely as a discrete propositional belief but as a sustained doxastic framework that structures subsequent inquiry and practical reasoning. The collaboration should address both the classic distinction among options that are live, forced, and momentous, and the specific problem of framework commitment under opacity.

Structure the deliverable in three parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: shared descriptions of Clifford's shipowner analogy and James's mountaineer analogy; shared rejection of the view that belief-formation is always a direct voluntary act analogous to bodily movement; shared acknowledgment that practical stakes do not by themselves generate epistemic entitlement; and agreement on the formal conditions that define structural evidential opacity. Second, a disagreements table with four columns—Jamesian formulation (phrased in terms that defenders of that view would accept), Cliffordian formulation (phrased in terms that defenders of that view would accept), the precise crux, and, where both sides concur, the kind of case or argument that would bear on that crux, with each side's predicted resolution recorded in advance. The table should cover at least these cruxes: (i) whether there exists a moral asymmetry between adopting a false belief under opacity and withholding belief from a true proposition through excessive evidential caution; (ii) whether sustained framework commitments are governed by the same evidential norms as discrete propositional beliefs; and (iii) what conditions, if any, could render one framework evidentially superior to another when the available data remain symmetric. Third, a brief concluding assessment that distinguishes verbal disagreements—such as whether the term belief properly applies to framework commitments—from substantive disagreements that would persist even after terminological clarification, and that specifies what each camp would count as decisive evidence or argument against its own position.

In drafting each formulation, avoid generic caricature. The Jamesian side should preserve the specificity of the live/forced/momentous taxonomy and the claim that in genuinely forced options suspension of judgment is itself a form of commitment with practical consequences. The Cliffordian side should preserve the universalist claim that belief is never permissible without sufficient evidence, the characterization of insufficient belief-formation as epistemic negligence, and the denial that practical urgency can substitute for evidential weight. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke theories or technical results that depend on specialized literature a general philosophical audience would not command; and do not ask for verification of historical texts. The goal is a precise, signable document that makes the residual disagreement exact rather than diffuse.
