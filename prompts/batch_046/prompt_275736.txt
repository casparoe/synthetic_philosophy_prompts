Please argue that Freud’s 1907 analogy between religious ritual and obsessive neurosis and his later characterization of religious belief as a collective illusion shielding humanity from helplessness are not merely complementary descriptions but rest on incompatible theories of mental defense. Specifically, defend the position that the obsessive-ritual model treats religion as a displacement of repressed drive energy that should yield to interpretation and reality-testing, whereas the collective-illusion model treats religion as an epistemic dependency that survives precisely by refusing the reality-testing Freud demands of science. From this tension, argue that religion is more resistant to psychoanalytic dissolution than Freud believed, because its structure does not function like an individual neurosis open to insight but rather like a distributed framework for cognitive authority that protects itself against disconfirmation at the group level. In constructing the argument, draw on Freud’s own distinction between primary and secondary process thinking and his account of reality-testing, but build the case from the internal logic of his texts rather than from later commentary. Address why this reinterpretation makes the psychoanalytic cure of religion not just unlikely but conceptually misdirected.
