Please argue that the lottery paradox and the preface paradox are not structurally analogous, and that the familiar rejection of rational closure under conjunction resolves only the former. Ground your argument in a distinction between beliefs operating at different doxastic layers: object-level claims about particular states of affairs versus second-order claims about one's own overall epistemic reliability. Show that in the preface case, the author fully believes each individual chapter is accurate (object layer) while rationally maintaining a calibrated meta-belief that she has probably erred somewhere (meta layer), with each belief supported by a different evidential base. By contrast, show that in the lottery all beliefs concern distinct object-level propositions evaluated against the same probabilistic criterion, so that accepting each ticket will lose is genuinely inconsistent with accepting that some ticket wins. From this, derive that closure should be restricted within but not across doxastic layers, which dissolves the preface tension without requiring the same move in the lottery. Finally, address the objection that this merely relocates contradiction into the agent's global belief set, and defend why layer-relative consistency, rather than unrestricted conjunction closure, is the correct rationality constraint.
