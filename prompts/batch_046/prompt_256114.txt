Here are notes for a short essay challenging the standard separation thesis about Frege's 1924 political diary, published posthumously and containing virulent antisemitism together with an ethnic-national essentialism. The standard view treats this material as biographically shocking but philosophically inert, since Frege's logic and philosophy of language—his anti-psychologism, his doctrine of objective and timeless thoughts (Gedanken), his insistence on sharp conceptual boundaries—are formal and universalist. I want to argue that the diary and the philosophy share a deeper structural architecture: in both domains Frege treats a hidden, non-empirical essence (national character in the political writings, logical content in the philosophical work) as objective, mind-independent, and determinative of value or truth regardless of empirical appearance, accessible only through rigorous method rather than through observation or psychological analysis. This is not meant to imply that his logic is racist, but rather that his hostility to psychologism and to natural-language ambiguity may reflect a broader essentialist temperament rather than purely logical hygiene. Please comment on whether this structural parallel holds or instead conflates political rhetoric with logical doctrine; identify any obvious misreadings of either the diary's arguments or Frege's account of thoughts; and assess whether, if the parallel survives scrutiny, it genuinely weakens the separation thesis or merely adds biographical color without philosophical consequence.
