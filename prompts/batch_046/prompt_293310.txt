Consider the view that Arendt's diagnosis of the "banality of evil" in the case of Adolf Eichmann is best reconstructed not as a psychological observation about individual mediocrity or the absence of ideology, but as a structural-phenomenological claim about modern bureaucratic rationality. On this reading, Eichmann's evil was "banal" not because he lacked conviction, but because he had internalized a specific cognitive framing—what we might call bureaucratic language-game compliance—that systematically translated moral questions into technical-procedural ones. The claim is that this framing is not a mere absence of thought but an active, disciplined mode of "thoughtlessness" (in Arendt's sense of representational thinking) that is structurally incentivized in administrative systems. It further holds that this form of evil is reproducible across contexts wherever narrative imagination is replaced by rule-following optimization, and that it therefore carries predictive weight: we should expect similar patterns of moral blindness in other highly rationalized institutions, regardless of the personal psychology of the individuals involved.

Assume you hold this reconstructed position. What would count against it? I want an honest map of its exposure. Which historical or psychological findings about Eichmann—say, evidence of deep ideological commitment, or of genuine emotional investment in genocidal aims—would be decisive, and which would defenders dismiss as compatible with bureaucratic framing? Which philosophical objections—perhaps about the coherence of attributing moral responsibility to a "structural" failure rather than to an agent—would be fatal versus merely awkward? Are there institutional analogies (other bureaucracies that produced comparable harm without comparable "thoughtlessness") that would undermine the predictive claim? Could the view be qualified so heavily—say, by allowing that bureaucratic framing is only one path to evil among many—that it becomes unfalsifiable, and if so, is that insulation a virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguish between evidence that defenders of the position should themselves concede and evidence they would reasonably reject.
