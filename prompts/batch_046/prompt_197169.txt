I hold that if Integrated Information Theory is correct, then applying its exclusion postulate consistently across time rather than only across space yields an unexpected consequence: the conscious substrate at one moment and its closest temporal successor are rival claimants to being the subject, not stages of one persisting self, so diachronic personal identity collapses into a sequential dominance relation among momentary complexes. This dissolves standard fission and amnesia puzzles but makes the self radically discontinuous. What would actually count against this specific interpretation? I want the defeaters ranked by decisiveness: formal arguments that exclusion cannot coherently extend to temporal dimensions without breaking IIT's own axioms; empirical findings about consciousness surviving interrupted intervals that should not occur if temporal rivals suppress one another; conceptual costs of abandoning diachronic identity that are independent of IIT; and whether defenders can insulate the view by weakening cross-temporal integration claims so far that it risks nothing. Which of these would an IIT proponent concede as damaging versus dismiss as misapplying the framework? Finally, is any insulation present a virtue of precision or a vice that renders the position unfalsifiable?
