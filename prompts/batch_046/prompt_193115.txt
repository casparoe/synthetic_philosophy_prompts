There's a move people make with Twin Earth-style cases that I can't find a clean label for. You set up a planet identical to ours except that the clear liquid in rivers is XYZ, not H2O, and everyone agrees: call it 'twin water' if you want, but it's not actually water, because the hidden microstructure differs. Fine. But then I bring up a paralell case with a social institution — say, a culture where the practice we call 'marriage' emerged entirely from property-transfer contracts, with no history of romantic pair-bonding, yet it regulates the exact same social roles, rights, and rituals. Here the hidden 'essence' is different (economic origin vs affective origin), but the funcitonal profile is identical. And suddenly people split: some say it's still marriage, others say it's a different institution altogether, and very few apply the same hard externalist line they used for H2O.

So I'm wondering: is there an established name for this specific asymmetry — applying semantic externalism rigorously to natural kinds but relaxing or reversing it for social kinds? Or is it just an unremarked tension in the literature?
