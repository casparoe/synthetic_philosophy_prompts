Let's say Keynes was alive now and watching how people actually use thier free time in the 2020s — not just working less, but this weird hybrid where leisure itself feels like a job because you're constantly curating a presence, tracking wellness metrics, and turning hobbies into side hustles through platforms. He originally figured the good life problem was mainly economic scarcity being solved so people could do art and hang out with friends. Now scarcity has kinda shifted to attention and social capital. Would he say we've basically solved the economic problem but failed the good life problem in a totally different way? Or would he think this platformized leisure is actually closer to his vision — people still working, just on self-cultivation? Also what'd he make of the fact that despite huge productivity gains, climate anxiety and precarity make people feel like they can't actually enjoy free time? I'm trying to figure out if Keynes's vision was always naive or if it's just been distorted by specific modern structures that he wouldn't have predicted.
