hey im drafting some notes on statue removal vs street renaming and i feel like everyone treats them as the same thing but theyre not. statues are basically pure commemorative objects — they sit there and symbolize. streets are different because theyre also functional infrastructure, so renaming one changes how people navigate and talk about space. im calling this mnemonic infrastructure — the idea that a street name isnt just an honor, its embedded in logistics like addresses, directions, delivery routes.

so heres my argument: tearing down a statue removes a symbol, but renaming a street disrupts a system. that means the ethical bar should be different. with a statue you ask whether the symbol is harmful enough to justify removal. with a street you have to ask whether the name creates navigational harm — meaning people are forced to invoke a bad actor just to do everyday tasks like giving directions. thats a distinct kind of harm that doesnt apply to statues.

i also wonder if theres an asymetry in preservation: old street names survive in archives and maps no matter what, but melting a statue destroys the material form forever. so maybe renaming is actually less destructive to history? not sure if that holds.

can you comment on whether this distinction makes sense? specifically, is mnemonic infrastructure useful or just jargon? and does navigational harm actually separate the two cases, or do statues also organize social space in a way im missing? feel free to point out any logical holes — i probably have a typo or two in here too.
