I have been turning over an image I cannot shake: a careenium, the dense substrate that carries everything, and above it simmballs — pure symbols, shapes that name rather than weigh. Do they ever push the matter beneath them, or does the matter move and the symbols only record the motion? Write a fourteen-line blank-verse poem with a volta at line nine that holds this tension. In the first eight lines, keep the layers distinct: let the language of the simmballs float, precise and weightless, while the careenium stays thick, silent, below. At the turn, introduce a single pressed image — a key-shaped simmball lowered onto a careenium lock that has no keyhole — and ask whether the pressure is real or only described. In the final six lines, tangle the two vocabularies without resolving which one causes the other. Let the line breaks themselves do the work: separated early, interwoven after the volta. No citations, no philosopher names. I want the poem to feel like someone testing an idea aloud, not explaining it.
