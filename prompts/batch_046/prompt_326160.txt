Below is a draft outline and the core argument of a short essay on the institutional extension of the extended mind thesis. The essay examines whether a multi-member appellate court that relies on an integrated, real-time digital precedent-analysis system can be properly described as a single extended cognitive agent, rather than merely a collection of extended individual minds.

The argument proceeds in three steps. First, I apply the parity principle familiar from the extended mind literature: if an external process plays the same functional role in guiding judicial reasoning that an internal memory or inference mechanism would, then there is no principled reason to deny that the process is part of the court’s cognitive system. Second, I consider a specific institutional design—what I call coupled deliberation—in which the software does not merely store precedents but actively restructures the argumentative options available to each judge in real time, such that no individual judge could reproduce the court’s collective reasoning path without the system’s continuous mediation. Third, I argue that if one accepts this, then attributing a belief or a knowledge claim to the court is not just a legal fiction but a literal cognitive attribution, which in turn threatens methodological individualism in jurisprudence.

Please provide critical comments on this draft. In particular, I would like feedback on the following points:

1. Is the application of the parity principle to a multi-agent institution coherent, or does it conflate functional integration with collective intentionality?
2. Does the notion of coupled deliberation introduce anything genuinely new beyond standard accounts of distributed cognition in social epistemology, or is it merely a rebranding?
3. Is there a hidden category error in moving from the court having a legal personality to the court having cognitive states, and if so, where exactly does the inference break?

Please comment on the clarity and rigor of the reasoning, and suggest where the argument might be strengthened or where it risks being unpersuasive to someone sympathetic to the extended mind thesis but skeptical of institutional cognition.
