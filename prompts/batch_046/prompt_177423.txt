Draft a take-home essay question, model answer, and marking scheme for an upper-level seminar in political philosophy. The topic is Simone Weil’s argument for the abolition of political parties. The essay should be answerable in approximately 1,200 words and should be designed so that students cannot earn top marks by summarizing Weil’s well-known claims about partisan loyalty. Specifically, the question must require students to do the following: first, reconstruct her thesis that parties function as apparatuses for generating collective passion and her comparison of partisan attachment to religious faith; second, identify and precisely formulate the tension between her normative demand for absolute intellectual honesty in political judgment and her own reliance on a spiritual vocabulary centered on gravity and grace; third, evaluate whether that tension undermines her critique or can be resolved by distinguishing procedural neutrality from substantive impartiality — a distinction introduced in week seven of the course. Provide a model answer of about 700 words that executes all three moves with philosophical precision rather than biographical commentary. Provide a marking scheme out of 25 points, broken into five criteria of 5 points each: accurate reconstruction of the collective-passion thesis; correct identification of the religious-analogy structure; precise articulation of the honesty-spirituality tension; critical evaluation that deploys the procedural-substantive distinction to test coherence rather than to praise or blame; and disciplined use of course concepts without generic digression. For each criterion, include a one-line note on the typical failure mode. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or external reading lists. Frame the prompt at the level of students who have read Weil’s essay arguing for the abolition of parties and have discussed democratic theory, but do not assume familiarity with any secondary literature beyond what is necessary to understand the primary text.
