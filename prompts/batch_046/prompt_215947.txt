Please argue that Bernard Williams was right to reject the claim that explicit moral arguments alone ended practices like dueling and slavery, but that Kwame Anthony Appiah's honor-based account requires an institutional refinement to avoid treating honor as an independent moral cause. Specifically, defend the view that honor functioned primarily as a coordination mechanism for elite status rather than as a freestanding normative commitment, and that dueling collapsed once legal and economic institutions offered lower-cost signals of reliability—rendering honor-based violence an unstable equilibrium—while slavery was dismantled mainly where industrial capital markets disrupted the coordination value of slaveholding status. Show how this equilibrium-shift reading preserves Williams's anti-rationalist thesis—norms change through altered interests and institutional costs, not through argument—while explaining why honor was nevertheless the vocabulary elites used to negotiate those shifts. Conclude by distinguishing this position from both naive moral progressivism and pure interest-based reductionism, clarifying what work the concept of honor actually does in explaining moral revolutions.
