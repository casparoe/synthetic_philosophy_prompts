If Nozick was still around and had to think about these lab-grown neural clusters made from cow stem cells—basically mini-brains with no bodies, no pain receptors, but they do show coordinated firing patterns—how do you think he'd classify them? Would he say they fall under the same moral side-constraints he used for animals, or are they too thin to count as separate lives? I'm especially curious wether he'd apply his experience-machine critique in reverse: since these things are literally just experiencing patterns without any world, does that make using them for research more acceptable, or actually more troubling? And do you think he'd still reject utilitarian aggregation if we could grow thousands of them and trade them off against human suffering?
