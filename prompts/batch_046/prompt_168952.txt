Gould and Lewontin's spandrel critique emphasized that many traits arise as architectural byproducts rather than adaptations, yet evolutionary psychology routinely treats cognitive features—such as narrative reasoning—as exaptations co-opted from prior adaptive modules. This seems to presuppose that exaptation can only operate on traits that were originally adaptations, not on spandrels that were never adaptive. If spandrels are genuinely non-functional byproducts, then describing narrative reasoning as an "exapted spandrel" appears incoherent: either it was adaptive at some point, which denies its spandrel status, or it was never adaptive, which blocks exaptation. Given this tension, is evolutionary psychology implicitly rejecting the spandrel framework, or am I misunderstanding how developmental constraints and evolutionary tinkering allow a non-adaptive byproduct to acquire function without ever having been an adaptation?
