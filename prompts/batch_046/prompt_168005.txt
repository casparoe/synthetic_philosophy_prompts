I work as a software engineer designing evaluation protocols for autonomous agents, with no formal background in philosophy. I am trying to encode a "low-impact" constraint—penalizing unintended side effects—without supplying a complete normative specification of which world-states are valuable. I take seriously the conceptual claim that any boundary between "side effect" and "intended outcome," and any choice of reference class or counterfactual baseline against which impact is measured, already smuggles in axiological commitments; therefore a genuinely value-neutral impact metric may be impossible. Assuming this dependency holds, please describe the most important implications for practical evaluation design. Specifically, address how it bears on (i) counterfactual invariance tests that compare an agent's trajectory to a baseline scenario, (ii) the problem of implicit "shadow specifications"—unstated value assumptions embedded in benchmark task definitions or reward structures—and (iii) whether formal notions of impact bounded by causal footprint or distributional distance from a reference trajectory can remain value-agnostic in principle. Which trade-offs represent unavoidable philosophical commitments disguised as engineering parameters, and which are framing artifacts that better formalization could resolve?
