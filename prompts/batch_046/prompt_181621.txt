Exam question: In Buddhist philosophy, the doctrine of anattā operates within the framework of the two truths: conventional (samvṛti) and ultimate (paramārtha). Explain how anattā functions differently at each level, particularly with regard to moral responsibility and karma. Contrast this with Derek Parfit's reductionism about personal identity, identifying one specific point of divergence in how each view treats psychological continuity across discontinuities in physical substrate. Finally, assess whether recognizing anattā at the ultimate level undermines the conventional practice of attributing moral responsibility to persons.

Student answer: At the conventional level, anattā allows us to use personal names and assign responsibility to individuals as a practical convention, even though ultimately no self exists. At the ultimate level, analysis reveals only the five skandhas—form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness—arising and ceasing according to dependent origination. Karma operates across these aggregates without requiring a soul, functioning like a causal transmission where intentional volitions condition future consciousness, comparable to a seed producing a tree. This parallels Derek Parfit's reductionism, which eliminates the self in favor of psychological and physical continuity. The key divergence is that Parfit requires direct brain continuity for survival, which is why he rejects the possibility of teletransportation preserving identity; Buddhists, by contrast, allow consciousness to continue in a different body at rebirth, so they accept a form of discontinuity that Parfit's physicalist framework forbids. Cartesian dualism fails for Buddhism because it treats the mental aggregates as a single immaterial substance, whereas Buddhism sees them as impermanent processes. Consequently, recognizing anattā does not undermine conventional morality: it is only a practical error to think one is a permanent agent, not a metaphysical illusion, so ethical practices remain intact at the conventional level.
