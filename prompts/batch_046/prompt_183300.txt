I am preparing the opening session of a graduate colloquium series in moral philosophy, and I will interview a philosopher—let us call her Dr. Elena Voss—who has recently advanced a revisionist reply to demandingness objections against act-consequentialism. Her core claim is that critics systematically conflate two phenomena: structural demandingness, in which a moral theory requires agents to abandon the life-defining commitments that sustain narrative continuity, and marginal demandingness, in which a theory merely requires continuous, incremental welfare sacrifices that leave those commitments intact. Voss argues that only structural demandingness constitutes a genuine normative objection, because only then does compliance destroy the conditions for moral agency; marginal demandingness, she holds, produces psychological fatigue but not moral wrongness. She further contends that standard integrity-based objections—of the kind developed in the literature on personal projects—illegitimately treat an agent’s commitments as carrying deontic weight rather than merely axiological weight, and that once this distinction is honored, consequentialism can accommodate a threshold of narrative integrity without collapsing into deontology.

I want a sequence of technically precise interview questions that treat her position seriously and press exactly where the literature presses, not generic biographical or methodological queries. Each question should turn on the actual content of her two-level distinction and her treatment of integrity. In particular, I want the sequence to anticipate her likely deflection—that critics are merely appealing to moral psychology rather than to normative ethics—and to include the immediate follow-up that makes that deflection harder to maintain. I also want one late question that asks what empirical or conceptual evidence could count against her claim that marginal sacrifices never accumulate, over time or across agents, into structural damage to narrative identity. The audience will be advanced graduate students and faculty, so the questions should be rigorous but spoken in live interview form.
