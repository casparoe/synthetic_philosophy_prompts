A podcast host summarized Schopenhauer's philosophy of music as follows: painting and poetry represent Platonic Ideas—the fixed grades at which the Will objectifies itself—whereas music alone bypasses Ideas and expresses the Will directly. The host mapped bass lines to inorganic nature, melody to conscious human striving, and harmony to the conflict and resolution among the Will's grades, then said this structure explains why music does not depict particular emotions but rather the universal form of willing itself. From this he concluded that music is the supreme art because it yields direct metaphysical insight into reality's inner essence, and that deep, sustained engagement with great music can serve as a practical substitute for the ascetic denial of the Will—permanently quieting individual striving in much the same way the saint achieves through resignation. I am trying to separate Schopenhauer's actual claims from popular embellishment. Which parts are faithful to his account, where does the summary slip into treating music as an emotional cipher or overstate its soteriological effect, and does the analogy between aesthetic listening and ascetic denial hold once we distinguish temporary respite from permanent negation?
