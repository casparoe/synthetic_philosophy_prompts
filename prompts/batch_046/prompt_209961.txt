Defend the position that debates about mind uploading have been misframed by treating gradual neural replacement and instantaneous scan-and-copy as points on a single continuum of speed. Instead, argue that the decisive factor is not temporal duration but the preservation of what might be termed “mesoscopic dynamic coherence”: the sustained, feedback-driven interaction patterns among cortical columns that operate at intermediate temporal and spatial scales. Maintain that a gradual replacement retains personal identity precisely because these loops remain continuously causally active, whereas a scan-and-copy reproduces only their structural snapshot, breaking the dynamic thread. Address the charge that selecting this mesoscopic level is ad hoc, and explain why continuity at this scale is metaphysically decisive rather than merely a pragmatic heuristic.
