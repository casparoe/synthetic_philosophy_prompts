I am drafting a six-week fellowship proposal for a philosophy-of-science panel that is sceptical of "mere puzzles". The precise question concerns a variant of Thomson's lamp—call it the stochastic lamp—in which the toggle intervals halve as usual, but at each step a fair coin determines whether a toggle occurs, so the sequence is genuinely indeterministic. I want to know whether the lamp can possess a determinate state, on or off, at the limit time, or whether the limit is irreducibly open, and whether that openness reflects a feature of the process logic rather than mere epistemic limitation.

Existing analyses of supertasks assume a predetermined toggle history; they ask whether a fixed sequence produces a contradictory limit state. Because they presuppose determinism, they leave entirely unsettled whether an indeterministic infinite process can have a well-defined limit property at all. Standard physical objections—energy divergence, Planck-scale discontinuity—are familiar, but they miss the conceptual target, since the stochastic lamp is designed to test the logic of infinite processes, not physical realisability.

The approach I propose is to model the process within a modal framework in which the limit is not a single world-state but a set of accessible worlds, each representing one possible completion of the coin-flip sequence. I reject the obvious alternative of applying standard temporal logic, because temporal logic presupposes a linear actual history, which the stochastic lamp explicitly lacks. I also reject purely probabilistic treatments that assign a single expected value to the limit state, since they suppress the modal structure that actually generates the problem.

The deliverable will be a concise formal framing plus an argument establishing either that the limit state is necessarily indeterminate across all accessible worlds, or that one world is privileged by a coherence condition on toggle dynamics, with a rigorous account of why that privilege does not smuggle determinism back in. Failure would consist in showing that the modal apparatus collapses into either standard determinism or into a probabilistic average that ignores the supertask structure altogether.

Please write the proposal in polished British English, arguing that six weeks is realistic for this conceptual work, describing the question, why current answers leave it open, why this modal approach succeeds where temporal-logic and probabilistic alternatives fail, what the result and failure conditions are, and why a funding body should support philosophical rather than empirical work here. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading; refer to positions and arguments by description only, and keep the document focused and approximately one page in scope.
