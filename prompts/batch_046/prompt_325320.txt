Write an essay on whether presentism in historiography is unavoidable, and if unavoidable, whether it undermines normative neutrality in historical explanation. Focus on the tension between hermeneutic presentism—using present conceptual tools to identify structural silences in the record, such as recognizing that an archive's categories systematically exclude certain agents—and evaluative presentism—judging past practices by present moral standards. Consider whether a historian can deploy present frameworks of moral epistemic analysis solely to reconstruct why past agents acted within constrained informational environments, without thereby committing to normative condemnation of those agents. Weigh the argument that explanation and evaluation are inseparable in historical narrative against the argument that explanatory presentism is a methodological necessity while evaluative presentism is an avoidable failure of calibrated interpretive charity. Reach and defend a definite verdict: either that a principled separation between explanatory and normative uses of present concepts can guide responsible historiography, or that any present conceptual engagement necessarily carries evaluative weight and historians should acknowledge rather than suppress this. Finally, say explicitly what argument or counterexample would change your verdict, and identify the weakest point in your reasoning.
