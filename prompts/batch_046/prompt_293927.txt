(2) so he seems to hold a general principle: if a practice is backed by a society's own customs, outsiders lack any rational ground to condemn it, because all evaluation is relative to local custum.

(3) but he also praises cannibal society for lacking greed, private property, and hierarchy, and he offers this as a real reason europeans should feel ashamed and maybe reform.

(4) apply (2) consistently: his praise is just the expression of his own skeptical-humanist custom, so it carries no rational weight for anyone else.

(5) worse, if a european reformer appeals to cannibal simplicity as a model, she's importing an external custom, which (2) says has zero authority over her own culture.

(6) so (2) makes internal reform conceptually impossible: appeals inside the culture just restate current custom, appeals outside are just arbitrary clashes.

(7) but the whole point of the essay is internal reform -- he wants europeans to see themselves as worse than the cannibals.

(8) therefore (2) undermines (7).

(9) the escape is to reject (2) and admit some cross-cultural rational comparison exists.

(10) but then don't we lose montaigne's defense of the cannibals against european arrogance?
