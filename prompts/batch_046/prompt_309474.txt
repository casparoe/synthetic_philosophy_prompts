For that piece, please compose a satirical internal memorandum—approximately 650 words, formatted with conventional bureaucratic headers (TO, FROM, RE, DATE)—issued by the fictional Federal Office of Coordinated Uncertainty to regional program directors. The memo should announce the formal adoption of two new administrative frameworks—designated Platonia Protocols and Luring Lottery Allocation—for federal grant distribution and citizen-participation scoring, respectively. Write in a sustained deadpan bureaucratic voice: no explicit irony, no direct acknowledgment of the absurdity, and no citation of authors, magazines, or historical origins. The satire must derive from precisely exaggerating the actual mechanics of superrationality experiments. In the grant section, specify that exactly twenty eligible entities may compete; that direct cooperation is prohibited; that each participant is expected to submit with probability 1/20; that a singular submission wins a fixed billion-unit award; and that zero or multiple submissions result in null payout for all parties. Include a brief procedural footnote describing the office's simulated-die mechanism for citizens who lack full confidence in reasoning symmetry, specifying a die with sides equal to the projected respondent pool, estimated at approximately five percent of the registered population. The target is the broader institutional tendency to treat symmetric rationality and common-knowledge reasoning as administratively tractable. The document should reward a reader familiar with the original experiments through exact structural parallels—probabilistic submission rules, prize-division mechanics, the instability of cooperative equilibrium without communication—while remaining fully legible to a general audience. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for further research.
