A friend who reads pop-philosphy summed up the problem of verisimilitude like this: it's the puzzle that if science aims at true theories but all our best ones are technically false, then either science never makes progress or we need some way to rank false theories by how close they get to the full truth. He said Popper solved this by defining verisimilitude as true consequences minus false consequences, so a theory with more true predictions and fewer false ones is closer to the truth. That means Newtonian mechanics is closer to the truth than Aristotelian physics because it gets more things right, which makes scientific progress just the steady accumulation of true statements. But then he said this was killed in 1974 when Miller and Tichy proved that, under Popper's own formulization, no false theory can ever be closer to the truth than any other false theory—so Popper's definition makes progress literally impossible. His take is that after this result philosophers mostly abandoned counting consequences and just switched to likeness approaches that compare possible worlds instead. Is this summary fair?
