I am preparing the conceptual framework section of my graduate seminar on interpretations of quantum mechanics, and I want to test whether I can reconstruct the implicit commitments of a senior colleague in the philosophy of physics from the verdicts she has delivered in our joint reading group over the past year. Her judgments are these.

First, in the original Wigner's friend scenario, she holds that the friend inside the isolated laboratory experiences a definite outcome upon measuring a spin-½ particle, whereas Wigner outside correctly assigns an entangled superposition to the friend-plus-particle compound; she explicitly denies that consciousness plays any causal role in collapsing the state.

Second, in the Schrodinger cat thought experiment, she maintains that the cat is never genuinely in an alive-and-dead superposition, because environmental decoherence suppresses interference terms on timescales far shorter than any biological measurement interval; nevertheless, she insists that decoherence does not solve the measurement problem, since it offers no account of why one particular branch is experienced.

Third, in the EPR-Bell setup with spacelike-separated measurements on a spin-singlet pair, she asserts that the correlations violate Bell's locality condition but do not permit superluminal signaling; she grants that Bohmian mechanics survives the theorem by embracing explicit non-locality, yet she treats this as an empirical possibility rather than a decisive reason to adopt the interpretation.

Fourth, in a hypothetical fully isolated quantum computer running Shor's algorithm with no external environment whatsoever, she says that the computation proceeds correctly precisely because the internal dynamics remain strictly unitary, but she denies that this entails parallel computations occurring in branching worlds.

Fifth, in the delayed-choice quantum eraser with entangled photon pairs, she argues that the recovery of interference patterns after which-path information is erased is fully explained by the unitary evolution of the global state combined with appropriate sub-ensemble selection; she rejects any retrocausal influence on the earlier detection events.

Next term she will present a scenario I have sketched for her: a cosmologically closed system containing two observers, A and B, with no external environment and therefore no global decoherence. Each observer performs an independent spin measurement on a shared particle and records the result in a local macroscopic apparatus. Applying unitary dynamics consistently, each observer assigns to the other a pure state in which that other observer's record is in a coherent superposition of distinct outcomes. Yet when A and B exchange information within the closed system, both report having observed a single definite result. She asks her students whether, on her view, (i) there is an observer-independent fact of the matter about which result each obtained, (ii) the shared particle possessed a definite spin value prior to either measurement, and (iii) this situation forces her to accept either a branching-world ontology or a hidden-variable dynamics in order to preserve consistency.

Predict her answers to (i), (ii), and (iii). Identify the interpretive rule she is implicitly running—whether a decoherence-supplemented Everettian stance that rejects multiverse ontology, a relational quantum-mechanical position in which state assignments are observer-relative, or a Bohmian view that treats the wavefunction as epistemic—along with at least one other rule that fits her five earlier verdicts equally well. For each competing rule, state precisely what it predicts about (i)–(iii). Indicate which of her earlier judgments serves as the closest analog, say whether her track record genuinely underdetermines this case, and specify the further scenario that would best discriminate between the leading candidates.
