I am presenting a seminar paper defending the following position: Quine’s critique of the analytic–synthetic distinction succeeds against Carnap-style conventionalism, but it does not establish that no functional equivalent of the distinction survives within holistic belief revision. I propose replacing the binary divide with a graded “revision-cost gradient” measured by the structural damage that rejecting a statement inflicts on the predictive and inferential network that links observation to theory. On this account, arithmetical statements sit at a local maximum of entrenchment: they are not analytic in virtue of meaning or convention, but in virtue of being structurally indispensable to the architecture of empirical science. This rehabilitates a pragmatically analytic status for mathematics without reviving Carnapian reductionism or contradicting Quine’s naturalised epistemology.

The audience is a philosophy of science and mathematics reading group at a research university. Most members are committed post-Quinean naturalists who regard any revival of the analytic–synthetic distinction as either conceptual confusion or crypto-conventionalism. One member works in formal epistemology on belief-revision theory and is sympathetic to structural measures of entrenchment, yet suspicious of importing formal metrics into metaphysical or epistemological argument. Another is a historian of analytic philosophy who is quick to detect anachronistic projections onto Quine’s texts and will challenge any reading that makes Quine sound like a precursor to contemporary formal epistemology. A philosopher of mathematics is also present; she accepts Quinean indispensability arguments but regards talk of “entrenchment” as psychologically speculative and irrelevant to the objectivity of arithmetic.
