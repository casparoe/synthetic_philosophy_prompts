I have recently become the primary caregiver for my father, who, following a minor stroke, interacts daily with an integrated home-health language model that tracks his medication adherence and mood reports. Observing him ask the system whether it "believes" he is improving, and watching clinicians record its probabilistic outputs as if they were clinical opinions, has led me to defend the following view: that attributing beliefs to an artificial system is warranted not by predictive utility alone, but only when the system displays what I call representational inertia—the maintenance of a specific propositional stance across adversarial or contextual perturbations that would disrupt purely associative or retrieval-based response patterns. I further hold that current large language models systematically lack this property, and that without it, intentional attribution collapses into a misleading anthropomorphism that risks distorting clinical judgment and obscuring where genuine epistemic responsibility lies. I want to know what would count against this position. Which empirical findings about model behavior—say, in chain-of-thought reasoning, adversarial robustness, or cross-context consistency—would undermine the claim that current systems lack representational inertia? Which conceptual arguments, perhaps drawing on the intentional stance or on functionalist theories of representation, could show that predictive success alone is sufficient for belief attribution regardless of inertia? I also want an honest assessment of whether my criterion risks being qualified away: if defenders of belief-attribution to AI respond that inertia is present but hidden in internal activations, or that it is unnecessary because instrumental prediction is all that matters, has my view been rendered unfalsifiable? If so, is that insulation a problem for a view that aims to guide practical decision-making in care settings? Finally, rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish those I should concede as genuinely damaging from those I could reasonably dismiss without abandoning the core commitment, and indicate whether anything that has arguably already happened in deployed systems should move me now.
