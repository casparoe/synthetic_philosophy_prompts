I'm drafting a dialogue where my character tries to kill hedonism and gets stuck. Here's teh argument she builds. (1) Hedonism claims all pleasures are good because they're pleasant. (2) But teh heterogeneity problem is real: biting a cold apple, seeing a proof click, and feeling schadenfreude share no felt quality. (3) So the hedonist must say pleasures share only a formal trait—being mental states we want to prolong. (4) But then the quiet satisfaction of clinging to a false belief—say, wrongly thinking your dead brother wrote that letter—counts as good, just because you want it to stay. (5) And if you want your grief to continue because it keeps him near, that sorrow-state is good too. (6) So hedonism ends up praising self-deception and cultivated mourning, which is absurd. (7) Therefore pleasures must share some hidden phenomenal core after all—maybe not in awareness but in sub-personal texture. (8) Yet if that core is hidden even from the subject, hedonism is unfalsifiable and useless. Where's teh slip? I want to keep (2) and (4)—they feel true to life—but I'm not sure (3) is avoidable. Which step should she drop, and what's teh cost?
