The student's answer reads as follows: Aristotle divides wealth acquisition into oikonomia, which serves the household, and chrematistike, which seeks unlimited wealth. The worst kind is usury, because money is barren: unlike plants or animals, coins cannot reproduce. When a lender charges interest, money is made to breed more money—tokos literally means offspring—which violates nature because metal is sterile.

Aristotle also observes that every good has two values: a use-value, such as wearing a shoe, and an exchange-value, such as trading it for coins.
