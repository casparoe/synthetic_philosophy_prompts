Consider the institutional practice, observed in certain secularized Vipassana retreat centers, of running registration on two parallel tracks. Legal identity governs liability waivers and emergency contacts, while all internal scheduling, seating, and instructor feedback are tied to a temporary "process identifier" assigned at entry. Retreatants are told that "the self that registers is not the self that completes the retreat," and progress is tracked by the identifier rather than by legal name. What conception of personal identity does this administrative split presuppose? Must it be interpreted as an operationalization of Buddhist anatta, or can it be reconstructed as purely pragmatic scaffolding fully compatible with a reductionist psychological-continuity account? And is the practice internally consistent: does the liability form, which implicitly treats the registrant as a single persistent moral and legal agent, undermine the pedagogical framing, or can both tracks be licensed by one coherent view?
