I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about supertasks and whether they can have a determinate outcome, going by my gut on cases. Here are my verdicts; I'm hoping there's a principle behind them, or maybe I just have to drop one.

Case 1: Classic Thomson lamp — toggle every half-interval for 2 minutes. At t=2 it's either on or off, but since there's no last toggle, I think there's genuinely no fact of the matter. Not unknown, actually inderminate.

Case 2: Same lamp, but wired so that if it's ever on for more than 1 second total cummulative time, it melts. The on-intervals sum to exactly 1 second, so I think it should melt. So there's a determinate fact about the cumulative property, even if the instantaneous state in case 1 isn't. That's a tension I can't resolve.

Case 3: A Zeno computor that checks Riemann zeros in exponentially shrinking time, printing a result at t=1 and stopping. I think it's logically okay — if it prints counterexample found that's a real result — but physically impossible because of heat dissipation limits. So supertasks are conceptually fine but physically moot.

Case 4: A branching version where each toggle splits the world: one branch on, one off. At t=2 there are 2^aleph0 branchs. I think this avoids the paradox, but only because there's no single lamp left to ask about. So I think the paradox depends on preserving identity through the infinite process.

Case 5: A lamp near a black hole singularity. From outside, gravitational time dilation makes the toggling take infinite time, so there's no final state. From inside, proper time is finite and the lamp just breaks after a few toggles. This feels like case 1 with a physical cutoff, but I'm not sure if that saves determinacy or just hides it.

Case 6: A quantum lamp where each toggle is a measurement collapsing to |on⟩ or |off⟩ with 50% probability. After infinite measurements, I keep going back and forth — sometimes I think the sequence converges to a definite eigenstate, sometimes I think it stays in a superposition of all histories. I genuinely can't settle this.

Is there a coherent principle that captures cases 1 through 5, especially the tension between 1 and 2? Or do I have to give up one? And what should I say about case 6, or about a further case: the lamp's state at each step determines the trajectory of a particle, so the particle's position at t=2 depends on the entire infinite sequence? I'd like to know if there's a unified view — maybe anti-realism about instantaneous states but realism about cumulative/integrated ones — or if I'm just confused and should accept that supertasks don't have outcomes except for properties that are continuous across the limit.
