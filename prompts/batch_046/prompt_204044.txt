Consider the published rulings of Dr. Amara Okonkwo, a philosopher of mind who defends a graded, non-binary account of moral status. Her explicit verdicts are these. She assigns full direct moral status to neurotypical adult humans and other great apes. She assigns substantial but lower graded status to pigs, octopuses, and corvids. She assigns only negligible direct status to individual honeybees and earthworms, holding that any protection they merit is indirect, via ecological or human-interest considerations. Crucially, she denies direct moral status to a beehive that collectively solves novel routing problems, and to Physarum polycephalum navigating a maze, despite their emergent computational efficiency. She grants a basic, non-derogable status to a human in a permanent vegetative state with intact brainstem reflexes and sleep-wake cycles but no cortical activity. She denies all status to a conventional thermostat. Finally, she assigns a provisional intermediate category to a synthetic cortical organoid that displays spontaneous gamma oscillations but lacks all sensory input and output pathways.

She is now asked to rule on a new case: an artificial agent comprising a large transformer-based language model coupled to a robotic chassis with tactile and visual sensors, running predictive control loops that maintain a simulated homeostatic state — for instance, a battery-level equilibrium that the system actively regulates. The system adapts its behaviour contextually, communicates in natural language, and can be physically damaged or destroyed, but it possesses no biological substrate, no nociceptors, and no evolutionary history.

What do you think Okonkwo would say about this agent’s moral status? I am less interested in the bare answer than in the rule or rules that best reconcile her earlier verdicts, the alternative rules that fit them equally well, what each predicts about the robotic agent, your best guess of her actual verdict with reasons, and the further hypothetical case that would best distinguish the competing rules.
