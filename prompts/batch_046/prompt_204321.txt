Before I evaluate any theory of honesty and deception for artificial systems, I want the adequacy conditions specified in full. Write the desiderata: what such a theory must be able to deliver—distinguishing deliberate misrepresentation from emergent confabulation; handling outputs that are false yet lack both belief-states and communicative intentions; preserving the gap between epistemic accuracy and pragmatic transparency; accounting for counterfactual honesty (whether a system that produces a falsehood in one framing would have produced the truth in a counterfactual framing with equivalent information); and maintaining the boundary between tool-use error and agentive deception without illicitly importing mental-state attributions that erase the distinction between instrument and agent. For each requirement, provide the concrete case or architectural consideration that motivates it, and flag any subset you regard as jointly unsatisfiable—especially if explaining context-sensitive deception demands intentional vocabulary incompatible with treating the system as a non-agentive artifact. Then, briefly, assess how a strict behaviorist account, an intentional-stance functionalism, and a stochastic-fidelity view fare against these criteria, keeping the evaluation subordinate to the list itself.
