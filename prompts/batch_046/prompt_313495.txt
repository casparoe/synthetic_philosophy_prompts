For a philosophical novel I am drafting, I have constructed the intellectual profile of a linguist-philosopher character whose views on language, mind, and knowledge rest on the following six commitments. I need an audit of whether these positions can be held together or whether some must be sacrificed. The commitments are these:

First, the poverty-of-the-stimulus argument demonstrates that children acquire syntactic structures—recursive embedding, binding principles, and long-distance dependencies—that are systematically underdetermined by the linguistic input they actually receive, which implies a domain-specific innate language faculty.

Second, conceptual and semantic knowledge—understanding causation, number, or moral categories—is not similarly impoverished; it is largely constructed through embodied interaction, social scaffolding, and statistical learning across modalities rather than being hard-wired.

Third, this innate linguistic faculty is best understood as knowing-how—procedural, non-explicit, and largely inaccessible to introspection—rather than as knowing-that comprising explicit representational rules that the child tacitly deploys.

Fourth, cross-generational emergence of sign languages reveals a biologically prepared but highly flexible substrate: the innate contribution constrains the space of possible grammars without fixing any particular one, as seen in the rapid crystallization of grammatical structure across cohorts without explicit instruction.

Fifth, recent small-scale neural language models, trained on corpora orders of magnitude smaller than a child's lifetime linguistic exposure and lacking domain-specific architectural biases, reproduce core syntactic regularities, suggesting that statistical density rather than biological specificity may explain the acquisition pattern the poverty argument highlights.

Sixth, the distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that is sharp and non-reducible: if a subject genuinely possesses a procedural competence, no amount of explicit propositional knowledge about that competence can substitute for or fully explain it.

Please audit this set directly, not as a general lecture. Go through the commitments pairwise. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each genuine tension state how severe it is—minor adjustment, significant revision, or fatal contradiction—and what the cheapest fix would be. Order the tensions by severity. Then state which single commitment, if revised or abandoned, would clean up the most tension with the least damage to the overall profile. End your response with a line that reads exactly: Verdict: [the single revision].
