Write a short story about a brain suspended in a nutriant vat in the basement of a defunct philosphy department. The brain was once an epistemologist, and now it's part of an archival project that feeds preserved minds the sensory stream of the lecture halls they used to work in. Over months, it develops a skeptical argument it dubs "layer-parsimony" — the claim that if two worlds are observationally identical, but one is physically nested inside the other (like a brain in a vat inside a larger universe), the nested world is ontologically cheaper because it doesn't need to postulate all those extra external objects. So the brain starts arguing that the vat-world is actually the base reality, and that belief in an "outside" is just an unnecessary layer. The story should center on its conversation with the maintenance AI — which communicates through a synthetic voice — as the brain tries to persuade the AI that the AI itself is likely just another simulated layer, and that both of them should adopt a kind of "nested realism" where they treat the vat as the ground floor for ethics and action. Include a moment where the brain notices that its nutrient mix seems to spike whenever it reaches the conclusion that the vat is real, and it has to wrestle with whether that correlation discredits the argument or is just one more nested fact. Don't make it a generic Matrix retread — the plot should actually turn on the mechanics of this inverted parsimony argument and the brain's attempt to live consistently with it.
