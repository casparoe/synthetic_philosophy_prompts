I have been working with a reconstruction of G. E. Moore's axiology that treats the intrinsic goods of beauty and friendship not as parallel mental states, but as standing in an asymmetrical grounding relation: on this reading, friendship derives its value solely from the mutual, non-inferential acquaintance with beauty that it enables, whereas beauty retains full value even in solitary experience. Thus friendship is valuable only as a vehicle for shared aesthetic cognition—something like a joint direct awareness of qualitative value—while isolated aesthetic appreciation needs no such partnership. What would count decisively against this position? I am not asking for the usual criticisms of Moore's non-naturalism or the open-question argument, but for internal defeaters: intuitions or cases in which friendship seems valuable in the complete absence of aesthetic content, or in which shared aesthetic experience actually diminishes rather than enhances value; conceptual results showing that grounding relations of this kind collapse into reductionism; and the risk that the view can be qualified away from every counterexample without becoming unfalsifiable. Please rank these threats from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which ones a committed defender of this asymmetrical reading would concede as genuinely damaging versus which they would dismiss by refining the notion of shared acquaintance, and assess whether that insulation risk is itself a serious defect for an axiological claim of this kind.
