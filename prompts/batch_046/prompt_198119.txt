I am a novelist constructing a linked narrative cycle in which one historical event is reconstructed through mutually incompatible ethical frameworks, and before I finalize the architecture I want to audit whether my underlying commitments cohere. Here is where I stand. (1) I hold that fiction should practice anekāntavāda: no single character's moral framework should dominate, and contradictory judgments about the same act should each be rendered conditionally valid, in the manner of syādvāda—true in some respect and false in others, relative to the standpoint adopted. (2) At the same time, I maintain that an author must preserve a singular causal substrate—actual plot-level events that are not themselves many-sided—because without invariant narrative facts, pluralism collapses into mere incoherence. (3) I reject interpretive relativism: I believe some readerly interpretations of my text are genuinely erroneous, not merely alternative nayas or partial perspectives. (4) I believe fiction has an ethical duty to cultivate intellectual humility through this very pluralism, yet I also hold that certain acts—betrayal of trust for private gain, for instance—merit unambiguous moral condemnation within the story-world. (5) To navigate this, I am experimenting with a compositional technique I call conditional focalization: each section assigns moral truth-values relative to its focalizer's epistemic conditions, while leaving the underlying causal chain untouched. Do these commitments hang together? Specifically, does my singular substrate (2) smuggle in a privileged metaphysical standpoint that undermines anekāntavāda (1), or is that conflict only apparent once we distinguish factual causation from normative evaluation? Does my rejection of relativism (3) sit comfortably with conditional focalization (5), or does the latter commit me to treating every interpretation as valid from some naya? And is my demand for unambiguous condemnation (4, second clause) compatible with the humility cultivated by pluralism (4, first clause), or must I separate object-level judgment from meta-level attitude, and if so, is that separation stable? Please rank the tensions by severity, note which conflicts dissolve upon closer inspection, and indicate the minimal revision—whether softening the substrate claim, redefining conditional focalization, or qualifying the condemnation—that would restore consistency with the least damage to my artistic aims.
