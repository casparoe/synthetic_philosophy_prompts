I hold that predictive processing, combined with the free energy principle, collapses scientific inference into perceptual inference: both are hierarchical Bayesian minimizations of surprise, differing only in scale and precision rather than in kind. On this view, peer review, instrument calibration, and replication are not distinctively scientific practices but higher-order predictions about predictions, and the evidence supporting a theory is structurally identical to sensory prediction error. What would count against this assimilation? I want the arguments or observations that establish scientific inference as genuinely different in kind—not merely more complex—including whether the institutional and social dimensions of science perform explanatory work that free-energy minimization alone cannot replicate. I also want an honest assessment of whether this position has been insulated into unfalsifiability: if every successful theory-choice and every perceptual illusion can both be redescribed as surprise minimization, does the view risk saying nothing that could be wrong, and is that a problem for a thesis about cognition? Please answer in American English.
