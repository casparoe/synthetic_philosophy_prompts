I am a retired experimental physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has been studying moral philosophy with the same attention to logical structure that I once applied to physical theory. I have become particularly interested in Kant's discussion of falsehood—specifically the well-known case in which a murderer asks after the whereabouts of his intended victim, and the respondent considers lying. The secondary literature seems divided between an absolutist reading, according to which any intentional false assertion violates the categorical imperative regardless of context, and a reciprocity-based reading, according to which the duty of truthfulness applies only within relations of rational cooperation and therefore does not constrain one's response to an agent who has, by initiating lethal aggression, forfeited the standing required for truthful address.

Please draft the joint statement that an advocate of each of these two positions could sign after a rigorous adversarial collaboration. Structure it as follows: first, a numbered list of agreed claims about the logical form of Kant's prohibition, the role of the Formula of Humanity, and the specific features of the murderer-at-the-door scenario, each claim phrased so that neither advocate need append a qualification; second, a disagreements table with two columns—Absolutist and Reciprocity-Theorist—giving, in each side's own precise vocabulary, the formulation of its view on whether false assertion to the aggressor is categorically forbidden, permitted, or required; third, a concise enumeration of the cruxes—those sub-questions about rational agency, moral standing, and the scope of cooperative duty such that settling any one of them would compel at least one party to revise its position; and fourth, for each crux, the specific hypothetical variation or conceptual test both sides agree is relevant, together with each side's explicit prediction of the outcome.

The statement should be written in formal, analytical prose suitable for philosophical publication. Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or recommendations for further reading; I am evaluating the argumentation without access to external references. Where the two camps genuinely differ only in terminology, resolve that in the agreed-claims section rather than treating it as a substantive disagreement.
