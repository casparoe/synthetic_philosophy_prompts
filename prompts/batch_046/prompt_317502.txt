Write a villanelle (nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, two refrains) on the problem of origin essentialism, taking as its central tension the question of whether you could have come into being from a different sperm and egg. Let the first refrain (line 1, recurring at lines 6, 12, and 18) articulate the strongest essentialist claim—that your origin fixes your identity across all possible worlds—while letting the second refrain (line 3, recurring at lines 9, 15, and 19) pose the counterfactual possibility of an alternative conception. Across the poem, allow the meaning of both refrains to drift: by the closing quatrain the essentialist line should sound like a consolation or a confinement, and the counterfactual line like an unclaimed life rather than a contradiction. In the middle tercet (around lines 10–12), introduce the non-identity problem by observing that if different gametes had united, the very subject who now asks “would that still be me?” could never have existed to ask it; the complaint is therefore self-undermining, yet this does not dissolve the intuition of contingency. Do not resolve the tension—let the villanelle’s compulsory repetition enact the philosophical deadlock. Use precise conceptual vocabulary (modal essentialism, counterfactual identity, non-identity problem) without citing particular texts or offering bibliographic references. Write in polished British English, with the rhyme and refrain structure doing conceptual work rather than merely decorative work.
