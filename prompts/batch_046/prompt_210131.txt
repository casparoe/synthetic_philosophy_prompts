Write a dialogue between two philosophers—one defending a Dennett-style account of intentionality as fundamentally derived and bootstrapped through recursive tracking mechanisms, and one defending a Searlean account on which original intentionality requires specific biological causation that cannot be replicated in artificial systems. The exchange should revolve around a hypothetical construct called System K: an engineered, self-modifying architecture with three recursive layers. Layer one registers environmental states; layer two monitors layer one and assigns functional labels; layer three monitors layer two's labeling dynamics and revises them based on integrated survival-feedback loops, with no programmer explicitly encoding meanings at the third layer. The Dennettian interlocutor should argue that System K shows how originally intentional-seeming states can emerge purely from derived tracking relations scaling up through recursive self-monitoring, thereby undermining any sharp biological boundary. The Searlean interlocutor should counter that every state in System K still derives its aboutness from the engineers' design intentions and the externally imposed selection criteria, not from intrinsic biological directedness, and should challenge whether recursive tracking ever amounts to conscious aboutness rather than mere functional correlation. Both should engage specifically with whether Dennett's two-bitser framework genuinely extends to this three-layer architecture without collapsing into eliminativism about original intentionality, and whether the absence of biological substrate is a contingent limitation or a metaphysically decisive absence. Ensure that each speaker responds directly to the preceding argument rather than advancing parallel monologues.
