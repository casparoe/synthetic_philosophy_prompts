I've ended up with a slightly weird hybrid on whether numbers should count in rescue cases — basically Taurek's anti-aggregation intuition, but with a twist. I think the reason we feel pulled to save the five rather than the one isn't because five lives have more intrinsic weight, but because in real-world cases the "many" are usually saved through diffuse causal chains (policy, risk-reduction, etc.) where we can't trace responsibility to specific individuals, and that structural opacity makes agregation unavoidable in practice. So I deny that numbers count metaphysically, but accept they dominate in institutional contexts. What would count against this? I want honest defeaters: arguments that show the metaphysical/practical split collapses, cases where diffuse causation still shouldn't aggregate, and whether I've just qualified the view into unfalsifiability. Rank them from fatal to awkward, and say what a defender of this split would concede.
