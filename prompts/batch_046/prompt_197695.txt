Exam question: Max Weber describes the Protestant work ethic in The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism as a form of inner-worldly asceticism in which rational labor becomes an end in itself, oriented toward capital accumulation as a sign of election rather than consumption. Evaluate this claim: "If Weber is correct that modern capitalism derives its distinctive moral energy from treating labor as a calling (Beruf), then both the 35-hour law and the recognition of karoshi as a social problem represent a direct philosophical repudiation of capitalism's foundational ethic — one by legally enforcing leisure, the other by revealing the lethal cost of treating work as an absolute duty." Student answer (three paragraphs):
Weber's argument is that Protestantism, especially Calvinism, made people work hard because they wanted proof they were saved. This created capitalism because people accumulated wealth without spending it, so labor became a calling where your job is like a religious duty.

The French 35-hour law does the opposite: it says people have a right to not work, so leisure is valuable for its own sake. This clearly contradicts Weber, because Weber thinks leisure is basically wasted time that pulls you away from your calling. So the law is saying capitalism was wrong to make work central.

Karoshi shows Japan also treats work like a calling, but they don't have Protestantism, so Weber's theory doesn't apply there. Actually this proves Weber was right about a universal need for work-ethics — Japan just found a different version. So karoshi isn't a repudiation of Weber; it's confirmation that every capitalist society needs people to treat work as an absolute duty, and when they do they sometimes die.
