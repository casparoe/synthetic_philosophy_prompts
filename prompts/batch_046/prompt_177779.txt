Please present an argument in defence of the view that Bradley's regress regarding the unity of the proposition is best dissolved neither by postulating a primitive binding relation, as in Russellian or Armstrongian accounts, nor by accepting Bradley's own monistic absolute, but by reconceiving relational propositions as structured simples in which relationality is an intrinsic, non-decomposable feature of the propositional whole. Your argument should explain precisely how this avoids an infinite hierarchy of relating relations, should respond to the objection that it collapses the distinction between a genuinely unified proposition and a mere aggregate of its constituents, and should demonstrate how the account can accommodate complex, multi-relational propositions without introducing an additional layer of unity for each embedded relation. Do not provide citations, a bibliography, or references to specific editions; focus solely on the conceptual coherence and metaphysical plausibility of the position.
