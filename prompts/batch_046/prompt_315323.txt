If one takes seriously the argument—advanced in different forms by Dennett’s quining strategy and by Chase and Sanborn—that qualia are not merely mysterious but conceptually incoherent, several open questions and alleged implications follow. Some concern whether an incoherent posit can still play an indispensable explanatory or predictive role in cognitive science; others ask whether establishing incoherence dissolves the hard problem of consciousness or merely relocates it; still others ask whether synthetic minds lacking phenomenal properties could be wronged in ways that depend on qualia.

Assume you are advising a small philosophy-of-mind research group with three years of funding and no reliance on external literature retrieval for its core deliverables. Which open questions or lines of work in this area actually merit concentrated effort, and which are overrated or do not genuinely follow from the incoherence thesis? In particular:

- Distinguish between ‘thin’ incoherence (the concept of qualia is empty) and ‘thick’ incoherence (the concept is contradictory but psychologically indispensable). Which of these, if either, is tractable within the group’s timeframe, and what would count as progress?
- Rank the implications for (a) the methodology of first-person reports, (b) the ethics of attributing suffering to artificial systems, and (c) the explanatory targets of consciousness science. Defend your ranking with explicit criteria—tractability, neglect, and how many other debates depend on the resolution.
- Identify one line of work that looks important but, on closer inspection, collapses into a prior debate about functionalism versus eliminativism and therefore does not advance the specific incoherence claim.

Write in British English. Provide a defended agenda rather than a survey, and do not offer a bibliography or reading list. End your response with a line beginning ‘Verdict:’ that states, in one sentence, the single open question you judge most worth pursuing and the one implication you judge most overrated.
