Construct a thought experiment that transposes the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem from moral motivation into epistemic justification. Design a realistic, one-paragraph scenario set in a workplace or research group, in which an agent acquires a true belief through a chain that begins with a clearly wrong-kind reason—such as status anxiety or competitive rivalry—and only subsequently reaches a right-kind reason in the form of direct empirical evidence. The wrong-kind reason must be structurally necessary: without it, the agent would never have encountered the evidence, yet once present, the evidence genuinely supports the belief. After presenting the scenario, explain precisely which feature distinguishes this dependency from a mere causal precondition, show how a proponent of pure evidentialism would attack the case, and state whether removing that dependency collapses the example back into an ordinary deviant-causal-chain problem. Write your answer in British English.
