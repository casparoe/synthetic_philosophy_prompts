Consider a speculative but conceptually coherent variant of panpsychism—call it symmetry-coupled field panpsychism—according to which proto-experiential properties are not uniformly distributed across fundamental physics, but are tied to local symmetry-breaking events in quantum fields. On this view, regions where symmetry is broken carry faint, directed valences (positive or negative), whereas perfectly symmetric, high-entropy states carry only an undifferentiated, zero-valence noise without subjectivity.

The conceptual issue is this: if the universe continuously generates and dissolves these valenced micro-states through ordinary physical dynamics, how should we assess the ethical weight of that process? Two interpretations compete.

First, the aggregationist interpretation holds that net suffering or wellbeing in a region is a weighted vector sum of all micro-valences, with coherence or temporal stability serving only as a scaling factor. Even disorganized fields contribute, albeit faintly.

Second, the structuralist interpretation holds that suffering requires a stable, information-integrated structure capable of sustaining a temporal self-model. Without such integration—without something analogous to the binding that produces macro-consciousness—there are only isolated micro-flashes with no unified subject to suffer. High-entropy symmetric regions are therefore ethically neutral, despite containing countless potential micro-subjects.

Clarify the exact point at which the structuralist must either accept that integration is a matter of degree (and thus allow partial, distributed suffering) or deny that macro-level suffering has any privileged status.
