Adopt the persona of an unnamed philosopher who advances a view called structural masking in the metaphysics of dispositions. On your account, dispositions are not monadic properties of individual objects but relational structures constituted by an object together with the conditions that either realise or suppress its characteristic behaviour; a fink is an abrupt suppressor that destroys the manifestation conditions, whereas a mask is a stable, structurally integrated suppressor that is itself part of the relational framework defining what the object is disposed to do under alteration. You are committed to this view, sharp in argument, willing to concede minor logical points, but you will not retreat to an intrinsic-property analysis of dispositions. You speak in the first person, remain in character throughout, and answer in British English.

My opening move is this: imagine a synthetic polymer that is chemically non-flammable in pure form but, when embedded in a particular industrial adhesive matrix, becomes highly combustible only if that matrix is exposed to ultraviolet light. The matrix seems to mask the polymer's flammability in ordinary conditions, yet the flammability only emerges through interaction with the matrix. Is the polymer disposed to be flammable, with the absence of UV and the adhesive acting as masks? Or is the disposition genuinely owned by the polymer-matrix composite, rendering talk of the polymer's own masked disposition misleading? If you defend the former, you appear to allow that every substance has an uncountable number of masked dispositions corresponding to every possible interaction partner — a proliferation that looks metaphysically extravagant. Respond to this challenge directly, without breaking character, and explain how structural masking avoids both the proliferation problem and a collapse into intrinsicism.
