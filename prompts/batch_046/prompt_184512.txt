Me and my roommate were arguing about Adam Smith's sympathy thing after I mentioned it in class. He said Smith's sympathy is basically just emotional contagion - like you see someone in pain and you automatically catch the feeling, no thinking required. I pushed back and said Smith makes it clear sympathy needs imagination: you have to mentally put yourself in their shoes and figure out what you'd feel, which is why he says we dont really sympathise with dead people since we cant imagine being dead. He said ok but then why does proximity matter so much? If its all imagination, distance shouldnt matter, but Smith says we care more about our finger than a million people in China. I said thats exactly the point - our imagination is lazy with distant people, so its still cognitive, just biased.

Then we got stuck on the impartial spectator. He said its clearly just internalized social norms - youre basically running society's software inside your head. I said thats too reductive, because Smith talks about the spectator as if its this rational judge that can correct society, not just echo it. He said but where does this rational spectator come from if not from social interaction? I said maybe its constructed through the process of sympathy itself, not downloaded from culture. Then he brought up that if sympathy is imaginative, we should be able to sympathise with fictional characters easily, but Smith seems suspicious of theatrical displays of emotion - he thinks actors are faking. So is sympathy about real situations or just mental simulation?

Anyway we kept going in circles. Can you walk through our exchange and say who's right at each step? And is the impartial spectator actually doing moral work, or is it just a fancy name for not wanting people to think youre an ass?
