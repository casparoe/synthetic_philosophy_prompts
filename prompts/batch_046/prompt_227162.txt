I'm putting together a sorting quiz for my philosophy blog -- not "which dead pragmatist are you" but "what do you actually think truth is?" I want about 8 questions, each a concrete weird case with 2-3 answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto Peirce's long-run convergence view, James's cash-value/warranted-assertibility view, Dewey's instrumentalist view, a minimal deflationary pragmatism, and a coherence-only version. The cases need to be specific enough that people can't just parrot textbook summaries: e.g., a physics prediction about a region we'll never observe, a historical claim that makes zero practical difference now, a belief that guides action but has no verification conditions whatsoever. Each question should ask what you'd say about that belief's truth-status, not "do you like Peirce?" At the end, flag any answer-pattern that lands in an empty branch -- is it incoherent, unexplored, or just unattractive? And note if any two named views can't really be seperated by these cases. Keep it focused, no reading lists or citations.
