I'm working through my own commitments on collective mentality after revisiting the ant-colony interludes associated with the Ant Fugue framing — the metaphor in which a colony behaves as a single cognitive subject despite each ant being a simple reflex machine. Rather than asking for a survey of the literature, I'm testing whether there's a coherent principle behind my gut reactions to concrete cases. The classic ant colony — call it Aunt Hillary — strikes me as genuinely minded in a non-metaphorical sense: the pheromone dynamics implement global information integration about food sources in a way structurally analogous to neural workspace broadcasting, so I lean toward attributing a minimal, distributed mind. A slime mold Physarum retaining maze-memory through its vascular network feels similar but weaker; I'd classify it as a cognitive system without full mentality, closer to an adaptive filter. A split-brain patient after corpus callosotomy feels like two minds, not one fragmented one, which suggests that integration failure fractures mentality rather than distributing it across substrates. By contrast, a modern corporation with strategic planning and legal personhood has functional intentionality — beliefs, goals, and strategic representations — but no phenomenological mind whatsoever.

The tension emerges with engineered and biological distributed systems. An engineered bacterial colony with synthetic gene-circuit logic gates, designed to solve novel arithmetic patterns it was never evolved to encounter, pulls me toward attributing a minimal mind because of representational flexibility directed at genuinely new problems; yet I strongly resist attributing a mind to the human immune system, which learns, remembers pathogens, and makes adaptive decisions through distributed cytokine signaling that is structurally parallel to the ant colony's loops. If the principle is substrate-independent integrated information, the immune system should qualify, but my intuition rejects that. So is the right distinction something like global workspace dynamics applied only to representational architectures that can handle open-ended novelty, or a threshold that excludes purely homeostatic adaptation? Can any principle accommodate both my ant-colony verdict and my split-brain verdict — that integration failure yields multiplicity, not distributed single minds — without either embracing immune-system consciousness or denying Aunt Hillary her cognition? And what does that principle predict for the case I keep oscillating on: a blockchain-based DAO that allocates resources through smart-contract voting, with no central processor but with emergent strategic behavior that none of its individual nodes comprehend?
