I've been reflecting lately, outside of any coursework, on what makes me specifically me rather than someone else, and I keep returning to the intuition that if my parents had conceived at a different time, the sperm and egg that formed me would have been different ones entirely, so I wouldn't exist at all. That idea—that my origin is somehow fixed to those particular gametes—feels compelling, but I'm trying to work out on my own exactly what it commits me to. A friend recently brought up gene therapy and how targeted treatments can alter a person's DNA in specific tissues, which made me realize that my own genetic material has probably already shifted in small ways through ordinary mutations over the years.

So here's where I'm genuinely stuck: if my identity is essentially tied to that original sperm-and-egg combination, then doesn't any subsequent change to that genetic material threaten to make me a different person? I don't mean psychologically—I mean metaphysically. It seems like origin essentialism might force us to say that biological persistence requires keeping the exact original DNA intact, which strikes me as obviously false, yet I can't see the precise distinction that separates the origin condition from the persistence condition without collapsing the view entirely. Is there a coherent way to hold that I couldn't have come from different gametes without also committing to the claim that I cease to be me whenever my cells accumulate genetic change?
