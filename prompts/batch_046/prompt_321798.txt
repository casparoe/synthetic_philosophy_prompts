I am moderating a colloquium Q&A for a philosopher who defends luminosity—the thesis that whenever one occupies a mental state with a particular phenomenal character, one is in a position to know that one occupies it—while also endorsing the KK principle that knowledge is closed under known entailment. She argues that the standard anti-luminosity argument illegitimately applies reliability conditions proper to perceptual judgment onto introspective awareness, and she distinguishes direct phenomenal access from inferential self-monitoring in order to block counterexamples involving gradual change. I want a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond having her restate this framework. Please include at least one question on how she preserves luminosity across imperceptibly graded transitions—such as slowly warming up or a color experience fading—without positing an arbitrary epistemic cutoff, together with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that introspective knowledge tracks phenomenal presence rather than comparative thresholds. Include another question on whether her acceptance of the KK principle commits her to an infinite hierarchy of higher-order knowledge for every luminous state, and a follow-up pressing whether she can halt that regress without weakening luminosity into a merely dispositional or potential claim. Finally, include one question introducing the concept of algorithmic introspection—the idea that an artificial system might model its own cognitive states through error-correction loops rather than direct acquaintance—and ask whether her defense of human luminosity extends to such systems or depends on a uniquely human mode of access, anticipating her answer that error-correction is compatible with direct acquaintance and following up with whether that compatibility renders the distinction between direct and inferential access merely verbal.
