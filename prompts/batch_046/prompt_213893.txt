I chair the Methodology and Ethics Review Panel of our university's Center for Interdisciplinary Forecasting. Our seven-member board evaluates integrated-assessment proposals—studies that derive normative policy recommendations from empirical predictive models, such as a current pending study using regional climate-damage functions to justify mandatory agricultural water-reallocation obligations. We vote separately on the empirical premises and on the final normative conclusion, and we have encountered a structural inconsistency: on recent proposals, majorities have accepted each of the premises that together entail the conclusion, while a different majority rejected the conclusion itself.

We must adopt a formal judgment-aggregation rule before Thursday's meeting. The three procedural options are: (1) premise-based aggregation, in which the board's collective conclusion is mechanically derived from its collectively accepted premises, regardless of direct votes on the conclusion; (2) conclusion-based aggregation, in which only the direct vote on the recommendation counts, and premise votes are advisory only; and (3) a discursive-deliberative rule, in which detected logical inconsistencies trigger structured re-deliberation with an explicit override mechanism.

Please draft the formal recommendation memo I will circulate. It should recommend one of these rules—or a clearly specified hybrid—state the precise procedural language for adoption, apply it to the concrete pending proposal (climate-damage projections and water-reallocation obligations), and explain the reasoning with respect to democratic legitimacy, logical coherence, and practical feasibility. Crucially, it must treat seriously the strongest argument for the option you reject. Additionally, address whether members may register domain-specific abstentions—for instance, an ethicist abstaining on climate-model premises—without breaking the aggregation chain, and specify whether such abstentions count as neutral or as breaking a premise-majority threshold. The memo should conclude with an actionable decision framework the board can apply immediately.
