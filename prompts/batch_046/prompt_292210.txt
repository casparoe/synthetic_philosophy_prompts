Construct a formal argument that treating germs, climate patterns, and volcanic eruptions as historical actors avoids the perils of environmental determinism only if one distinguishes between structural protagonism—by which non-human forces restructure the space of feasible human actions without possessing intentions—and teleological agency, which is reserved for beings capable of goal-directed deliberation. Your argument should respond to the objection that this distinction is purely terminological and therefore collapses back into determinism, and it should clarify why acknowledging structural protagonism enhances causal explanation in historiography without displacing moral accountability for the human decisions made within the constraints those forces impose.
