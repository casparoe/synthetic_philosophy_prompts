I chair the institutional review board overseeing proposed revisions to our regional psychiatric nosology manual. A working group has petitioned to recognize algorithmically mediated identity fragmentation—provisionally designated AMIF—as a formal dissociative subtype. Their submission draws on clinical cohorts who, after sustained interaction with symptom-matching conversational agents, report disruptions in self-continuity that map with unusual precision onto the proposed criteria, whereas comparable presentations appear rarely in populations without comparable technological exposure. Several board members regard this as a straightforward taxonomic extension; others contend that we are witnessing an accelerated variant of the looping effects described in philosophy of psychiatry, wherein the diagnostic category—partly circulated through automated interfaces—helps constitute the very clinical phenomena it claims merely to describe. The working group maintains that excluding AI-assisted self-assessment from intake protocols, combined with mandatory debriefing, can sever any feedback loop, and they request provisional adoption with a five-year sunset clause and mandatory reassessment.

Please draft the board's formal decision memo addressed to the working group. State a clear recommendation—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—supported by reasoning the researchers could actually respond to in a revised protocol or follow-up study. The memo should include an honest treatment of the strongest opposing consideration, specifically whether methodological safeguards can genuinely neutralize looping dynamics when the mediating mechanism is embedded in widely accessible algorithmic systems rather than purely interpersonal social processes. In addition, address whether provisional institutional recognition risks reifying a category whose phenomenological and etiological boundaries remain unsettled, and specify precisely what empirical or procedural conditions would need to be satisfied before full adoption could be reconsidered at the end of the provisional period.
