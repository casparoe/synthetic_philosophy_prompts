Populist discourse frequently invokes 'the will of the people' as though it were a unitary normative reality that democratic institutions ought to discover rather than construct. Yet Arrow's impossibility result demonstrates that any rule combining individual preference orderings into a collective one must violate either transitivity, unrestricted domain, or independence of irrelevant alternatives, or else reduce to a dictatorship. If populists genuinely maintain that there exists a single, pre-political popular will independent of any aggregation procedure, are they not implicitly committed to a metaphysical form of dictatorship—since any non-dictatorial mechanism for deriving that will from discrete individual opinions is demonstrably impossible? Or does the error lie in applying a framework designed for preference aggregation to a normative conception of popular sovereignty that was never intended to operate on rank-ordered individual choices?
