I'm trying to work out the ethics charter for a utopian society in my novel, so I'm testing my own gut on suffering-focused morality. Here are my verdicts.

Eliminating factory farming through lab meat, even though wild predators still cause suffering: that's a clear win, do it.

Engineering away all human depression and anxiety, but as a side effect people lose the melancolic depth that drives serious art and moral outrage: I'm definatly not okay with that. Feels like we're trading away something important.

A policy that stops all future sentient beings from being born, just to prevent future suffering, while keeping current people comfortable: that's wrong. Not fine.

An AI system that instantly detects and anesthetizes any being that starts suffering, without trying to prevent it first: that feels okay, maybe even required?

Telling a dying patient a comforting lie that reduces their terror, knowing they'll never find out: that's fine.

But here's the one I keep flipping on. A 'suffering budget' where we deliberately allow a small fixed amount of suffering — maybe one percent of current levels — to persist, because that remaining pain is causally necesary to motivate the tech that wipes out the other ninety-nine percent. Sometimes it feels like a necessary trade, sometimes like I'm just rationalizing.

Is there one principle behind all this, or a small family? I want something precise, with the case that motivates each clause. Also tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed — say, reducing suffering by slightly lowering the complexity of consciousness itself, so people are happy but less capable of deep reflection. And be honest: which of my verdicts does the simplest version force me to drop?
