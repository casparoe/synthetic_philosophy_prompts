Compare Douglas Hofstadter's practice of analogical translation in Le Ton beau de Marot — in which the rhyme, meter, and emotional color of Clément Marot's poem are recreated through functional analogues in English rather than through literal rendering — with the stricter principle of isomorphic formal preservation, which holds that a poem's architectural features (rhyme scheme, syllable count, syntactic parallelism, and phonetic texture) should be mapped onto the target language with structural one-to-one correspondence, accepting semantic divergence or unnatural phrasing as the price of fidelity. Does analogical translation genuinely preserve the original's form, or does it substitute one formal system for another? Is there a coherent distinction between preserving a poem's music and preserving its structural blueprint? And if form itself carries meaning rather than merely conveying it, which of these two approaches does justice to that claim?
