I'm drafting the core of a position paper for a philosophy of logic seminar and want a consistency audit before I fix the structure. Here is the package I've arrived at:

(a) I accept Gödel's first incompleteness theorem in its standard formulation: any consistent formal system capable of expressing elementary arithmetic contains arithmetical statements that are true but unprovable within that system.

(b) I take this result to support a modest mathematical Platonism — there are objective mathematical truths that outstrip any particular formalisation we might construct.

(c) Nevertheless, I reject Lucas's argument that human minds therefore cannot be Turing machines, mainly because human reasoning is fallible, context-dependent, and not governed by a single fixed axiom set.

(d) I interpret Gödel's second incompleteness theorem as showing that no sufficiently strong consistent system can demonstrate its own consistency, and I take this to entail that scientific theories can never provide a complete, internal self-justification of their foundational coherence.

(e) I insist that both theorems apply strictly to formal, axiomatisable systems with recursively enumerable axioms, placing no direct constraint on informal, heuristic, or non-axiomatic reasoning.

(f) I also maintain that these results undermine the logical-positivist programme of reducing mathematical knowledge to syntactic derivation within a fully explicit formal language.

Do these hang together? Specifically: is there a hidden tension between (b) and (c), given that both seem to invoke a gap between formal machinery and human cognitive access, yet I draw an anti-mechanist conclusion in one case and reject it in the other? Does (d) overreach relative to the restriction I impose in (e), since scientific foundations are rarely presented as a single formal system? Is (f) consistent with (e), or does it smuggle in an application to informal reasoning that (e) forbids? Please rank the tensions by severity, distinguish those that dissolve on closer inspection from those that require a revision, indicate the least costly commitment to drop or weaken if consistency demands it, and say whether there is an interpretation of the Gödelian results that lets me preserve the substance of all six.
