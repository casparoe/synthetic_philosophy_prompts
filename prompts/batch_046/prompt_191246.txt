Please describe the most important implications of extending Miranda Fricker's framework of hermeneutical injustice to include what we might call epistemic ventriloquism: cases in which dominant epistemic institutions design conceptual resources for a marginalized group without that group's participatory input, yet present the result as an authentic remedy for hermeneutical marginalization. What are the implications for (1) the authenticity conditions of shared conceptual resources, (2) whether testimonial justice can be achieved through top-down conceptual engineering, and (3) the moral obligations of institutions—such as psychiatric classification boards or legal reform committees—when they attempt to correct hermeneutical gaps? In particular, does successful conceptual engineering require prior testimonial justice, or can it serve as its precondition?
