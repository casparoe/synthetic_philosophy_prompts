I read a bit about it online but I want to feel it in my bones, not just nod along. I'm running a little discussion group at the community centre, about 10 people, mix of ages, one hour on a tuesday evening. Can you design a quick game or structured activity where they experience the pull of being cautious without being told "this is the precautionary principle"? I'd like the scenario to be made-up: a neighbourhood deciding whether to let a local artist install these weird reflective mirrors in the park that might either scare away birds permanently or do nothing, but the data is messy and nobody's sure.

Here's my own twist, which I think gets at something real: the group makes decisions in rounds. Each round they can spend a "trust token" to reduce the ambiguity a bit, but spending tokens costs them collective reward no matter what the final outcome is. So being cautious literally eats into their score even if they're right to wait. I want them to discover that tension themselves, not have me lecture them.

Please give me rules, a timeline, materials they can grab easily (paper, pens, maybe a jar?), and a debrief that maps what happened back to the idea.
