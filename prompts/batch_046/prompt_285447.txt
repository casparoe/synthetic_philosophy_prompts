I am preparing discussion material for an advanced seminar on moral partiality and the boundaries of ethical consideration, and I want to press my students on an analogy that surfaced when we turned from human-centered justice to interspecies obligations. One student proposed that species membership functions structurally like national citizenship: just as a state may legitimately prioritize its own citizens in the distribution of goods, protections, and deliberative weight—without thereby denying the moral standing of outsiders—so human beings may legitimately prioritize conspecifics in moral consideration without committing themselves to the view that animals matter not at all. The analogy is intended to license a moderate, non-absolute form of species preference, treating cross-species concern as supererogatory rather than strictly obligatory, much as cosmopolitan duties to foreigners are often characterized.

Please audit this analogy with precision. First, lay out the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the role of the passport, the territorial border, the social contract, the tax or reciprocal burden, and the possibility of naturalization? Then identify the disanalogies that specifically bear on the normative conclusion—whether biological inheritance is closer to birthplace or to ethnicity, whether an animal can in any meaningful sense “emigrate” into the human moral community, and whether the legitimacy conditions that justify state partiality (consent, reciprocity, democratic accountability, shared institutions) have any parallel in interspecies relations. I also want you to examine what the analogy quietly imports about the value of collective identity: does it presuppose that partiality is only defensible when it tracks some functional collective with internal governance? Finally, tell me whether the conclusion—that moderate speciesism is permissible—survives once those imports and breaks are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the entire normative load. If it collapses under that pressure, suggest a better analogy that might do the same work without the same liabilities.
