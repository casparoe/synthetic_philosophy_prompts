Write a dialogue between Dr. Kira Okonkwo, who defends an etiological (selected-effects) account of biological function, and Dr. James Whitfield, who defends a causal-role (systemic-contribution) account. Set the exchange in a seminar room after a talk on functional ascription in synthetic microbiomes.

The conversation should center on a hypothetical but conceptually precise case: a gut bacterium possesses Pathway A, an ancestral metabolic route originally selected for detoxifying a specific plant compound. Due to a recent dietary shift in the host population, Pathway A now makes its primary causal contribution to nitrogen recycling, while Pathway B—a horizontally transferred gene cluster—has taken over the detoxification role and is currently under strong positive selection.

Okonkwo should argue that Pathway A retains its original proper function (detoxification) despite its current causal role, that its failure to detoxify constitutes malfunction regardless of its nitrogen-recycling success, and that Pathway B lacks a proper function altogether until it has undergone evolutionary stabilization and selection for detoxification. Whitfield should counter that Pathway A's present contribution to nitrogen recycling is its current function, that "malfunction" must be defined relative to the system's present organizational needs rather than ancestral selection history, and that requiring evolutionary pedigree makes the etiological account unable to recognize genuinely functional novel adaptations in real time.

Crucially, both should engage with the "liberalism" objection: Whitfield should press Okonkwo on whether her view can avoid attributing spurious proper functions to accidental by-products (for instance, whether the audible vibrations of a heart acquire a proper function simply because physicians use them for diagnosis), and Okonkwo should press Whitfield on whether his view can distinguish genuine functional contributions from causally inert or harmful side-effects without smuggling in hidden normative criteria about system integrity. The dialogue should contain genuine back-and-forth—each character should revise or sharpen their position in direct response to the specific point just made, not merely restate their opening view. Conclude without declaring a winner, but with both identifying the exact normative commitment—about whether function carries an inherent "ought" derived from evolutionary history or from present systemic contribution—that divides them.
