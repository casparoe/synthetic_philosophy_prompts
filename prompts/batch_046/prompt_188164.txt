Here is roughly where I've landed on human extinction and value, and I want a consistency check before I write it up. I accept Scheffler's afterlife conjecture: extinction is catastrophic mainly because it annihilates the collective "afterlife" of future generations on which our present meaningful projects depend. I add a refinement—call it interpretive density—that bare species survival isn't enough; what preserves present value is a future population large and diverse enough to continue interpreting, contesting, and extending our work. I take this dependency to be structural, not metaphysical: cancer research matters now because we anticipate future beneficiaries and interpreters, not because of some mystical temporal link. At the same time, I'm a constructivist about value: there are no mind-independent normative facts, and "value" tracks what agents have reason to care about within their projects. I also think extinction harms the final generation in a non-experiential way—there is a real badness in being the terminus of the chain, even if they never learn they're last—though I grant that learning it would layer on a distinct psychological injury. Finally, I reject individual life-extension (cryonics, mind-uploading) as irrational, precisely because what matters is collective interpretive continuity, not personal persistence through time. Do these hang together? Go through the pairs and tell me which are in genuine tension, which only look like it, and for each real tension how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be. Rank them by severity, say exactly what has to give in each case, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
