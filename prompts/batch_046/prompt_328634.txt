I was chatting with a colleague over coffee after a panel on visual philosophy — she summed up Neurath's picture-language project in a way that sounded slick but I'm not sure it's fair. She said Neurath believed images work like a universal logical syntax because a picture of a red cube litterally is the state of affairs it shows, so there's no translation problem and protocol sentences become redundant; apparently he thought this is why he broke with Carnap, who insisted observation reports still need rendering into physicalist language. She also claimed his ISOTYPE system was meant to replace ordinary scientific language entirely rather than supplement it, and that he saw picture languages as immune to the ambiguty that makes verbal philosophy go round in circles.

Something about that feels a bit too tidy — I know Neurath was keen on physicalism and on those picture symbols for education, but I'm not convinced he thought images are literally identical to facts, or that he wanted to bin verbal protocol sentences altogether. Could you go through her summary and tell me which bits a sympathetic reader of Neurath would accept, which are subtle misrepresentations, and whether her conclusion — that picture languages solve the protocol-sentence debate by making translation unnecessary — actually follows from the accurate parts?
