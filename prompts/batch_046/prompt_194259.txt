I run a little local-history club at the community center, usually about 10-12 people of all ages, we get about an hour. I want a game or role-play that makes us feel why a town might fight to keep a wrong story alive even when the papers say otherwise.

Here is the case: imagine a factory town where everyone remembers a strike in 1968 - the owners apologized, there is a mural and a plaque. But the county archives say it was 1971, lasted one day, the owners fired the organizers and never said sorry. Now the state offers a grant to restore the community center roof, but only if all public displays pass document-check.

Split the group: half plays the town council who wants the roof fixed; the other half plays residents - some are grandkids of fired workers, some grew up with the mural and feel it is who they are, some just distrust the archives because they were written by the company. Give us rules and roles that make the conflict happen through our choices, not through a lecture. Use a payoff scheme that doesnt need real money - maybe points that decide whose version gets told first at the next meeting, or control over a shared snack budget.

Then debrief: map what just happened onto the idea that collective memory is not just mistaken history but a living record that does work for the group, against dead paper in the archives. Tell me what will definately go wrong the first time we run it, and be honest about which part of this tension the activity just cannot reproduce and I will have to supply myself.
