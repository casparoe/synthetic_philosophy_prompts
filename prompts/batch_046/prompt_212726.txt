For a graduate seminar in modal epistemology, specify the complete set of desiderata that an adequate theory linking conceivability to metaphysical possibility must satisfy. For each desideratum, provide the conceptual motivation—the specific case, counterexample, or disciplinary constraint that makes it necessary—and classify it as non-negotiable (failure disqualifies the theory) or as a preference of a particular camp (e.g., rationalist, empiricist, or cognitive-scientific). Be sure to include the following injected considerations: first, the phenomenon of "fragmented conceivability," in which a thinker can separately conceive of state S₁ and state S₂ without contradiction, yet cannot coherently conceive of their conjunction S₁∧S₂ despite its metaphysical possibility, and the theory must explain whether this undermines the conceivability-possibility link or merely shows its local applicability; second, the requirement that the account either preserve a sharp epistemic/metaphysical boundary or explicitly reject it in favor of a graded transition, with the stakes for each option made clear; third, compatibility with empirical constraints on mental modeling and working-memory limits that restrict simultaneous representation of complex properties; fourth, the demand that the account explain why necessary falsehoods—such as mathematical errors that seem conceivable to non-experts—do not generate spurious possibilities, without merely stipulating that they fail. Finally, indicate whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given standard constraints on mental representation, and if so, identify which desideratum does the most work in excluding rival accounts. Weight your answer on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a scorecard of existing theories.
