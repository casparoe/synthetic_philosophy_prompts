Write a dialogue between two philosophy grad students, Lin and Kai, sitting in a campus cafe and arguing whether a nation can be guilty for atrocities commited generations back. Lin thinks Jaspers' four-way split - criminal, political, moral, metaphysical - lets her defend a kind of inherited institutional burden she calls a 'successor ledger', where the state keeps obligations alive after the original perpetrators are dead. Kai says she's just smuggling moral guilt in through the back door, since Jaspers reserved collective reach for metaphysical guilt alone, and that doesn't transfer like a bank debt. Make them actually talk to each other: Lin should ask Kai what he does with citizens who still benifit from past wrongs without being personally blameworthy, and Kai should ask Lin whether her ledger carries any real affective or punitive weight, or if it's just budget accounting with a moral paint job. Neither should be a strawman. Keep the tone casual, like they're actually talking over coffee, and don't worry about being too formal.
