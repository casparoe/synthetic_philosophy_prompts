my friend mara is really into hofstadter's knob-twiddling idea — that creativity is just twisting conceptual knobs to make variations on a theme. she cooked up her own taxonomy of seven "master knobs" for judging whether something is genuinely creative or just derivative: material substrate, scale/proportion, temporal ordering, causal narrative, affective valence, semantic domain, and social function. she's pretty consistant in her calls so far, but i'm not convinced she's actually running one clear rule.

track record:

1. duchamp's fountain — urinal placed in a gallery. she said creative: turned social function and semantic domain, left the form untouched.

2. beethoven's fifth played on theremin with identical notes and rhythm. derivative: only twisted material substrate.

3. hamlet told backwards scene-by-scene with the same dialogue. derivative: just flipped temporal ordering, which doesn't mess with the causal narrative.

4. a physics paper that re-derives general relativity using information-theoretic entanglement instead of spacetime curvature, same predictions. creative: turned causal narrative and semantic domain.

5. michelangelo's david reproduced at 1/10 scale in ice inside a refrigerator. derivative: only substrate and scale changed.

so what's she actually doing? maybe she only cares when "deep" knobs get turned — causal narrative, semantic domain, affective valence, social function — and ignores surface ones? but then there's probably another rule that fits these five just as well.

new case: a composer writes an opera using mozart's don giovanni libretto and melodies exactly, but inverts every emotional valence — seduction scenes become funereal, the comedy becomes horrifying — and stages it in a decommissioned subway tunnel where the audience walks through different stations for each act. what will mara say? creative or derivative? what's the rule she's implicitly using, what's an alternative rule that accomodates her earlier verdicts equally well, and what single further case would best distinguish the two?
