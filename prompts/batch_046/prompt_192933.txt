For my history and philosophy elective, I am examining how societies sustain collective identity through narratives of defeat. I want to compare two analytical frameworks applied to “lost cause” national myths: the “sacrificial redemption” model, which treats defeat as a morally purifying sacrifice that guarantees eventual collective vindication, and the “permanent grievance” model, which treats the lost cause not as a promise of return but as a perpetual boundary-marker that defines the in-group against outsiders. The first framework is often used to explain the Lost Cause of the Confederacy and the Masada narrative; the second is sometimes applied to Kosovo and to certain readings of the Dunkirk spirit. I want a committed comparison: defend which framework offers the more coherent account of long-term myth survival, identify the specific mechanism—redemptive teleology versus boundary maintenance—that best distinguishes them, and specify which of these four cases most clearly undermines the framework you reject. Do not merely list strengths and weaknesses; take a definite position and justify it with reasoning that could stand without reference to specific secondary literature.
