Assume the role of a professional philosopher of mind specializing in theories of consciousness, and please think carefully and step by step. I am a university student in an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar, and I need you to construct a precisely engineered clinical-style thought experiment for my coursework. Before you begin, briefly brainstorm the conditions under which phenomenal consciousness and global workspace broadcasting could come apart in a non-trivial direction without invoking philosophical zombies, science-fiction technology, or perfect predictors.

Specification: Design a realistic, one-paragraph scenario describing a neurologically plausible patient with a highly selective disconnection. The patient must retain a rich, structured phenomenal experience of a complex, emotionally salient visual array—say, an intricate multicolor geometric pattern—while being permanently unable to access, report, reason with, or behaviorally utilize any information from that array. Simultaneously, the patient must exhibit full global broadcasting—accurate report, reasoning, and action-guidance—regarding a second, phenomenally empty or minimal cue (such as a simple auditory tone or spatial marker) that never enters phenomenal consciousness. The case is intended to isolate whether phenomenal consciousness can exist in the absence of global access, not merely to show that report can fail. It should rely on ordinary clinical neuroscience framing, contain no more than one paragraph, and avoid appeals to niche or obscure theoretical apparatus that a well-trained model might not reliably reconstruct.

After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that it meets the specification by walking through the following checks precisely:

(a) Identify the representational pathway or method for each stimulus, state the relevant nearby worlds (standard conscious vision, blindsight, total absence), and explain why the phenomenal/access distinction assigns consciousness to the visual array but denies access-consciousness to it.

(b) State what global workspace theory predicts about both stimuli—why it might deny phenomenal consciousness to the array despite its richness, or grant it to the broadcast cue despite its phenomenological emptiness—and justify this with the theory's core commitment.

(c) Isolate the single structural feature that does the isolating work, explain why removing it collapses the case back into a familiar, less interesting example, and confirm that all other variables are held fixed.

(d) Provide the strongest counter-attack available to a global workspace theorist that does not simply posit hidden access; specifically, address whether they can redescribe the array's richness as unconscious fragmented processing or as partial broadcasting.

(e) Conclude with an explicit statement of what specific empirical result or conceptual argument would change your mind about whether phenomenal consciousness can be separated from global broadcasting in this architecture.

Finally, be self-critical: identify the point in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to failure, and state exactly what consideration or objection would lead you to revise your verdict.
