I am revising the conceptual framework for my graduate seminar on artificial intelligence and moral philosophy. I work with the standard distinction between intent alignment, understood narrowly as the technical problem of ensuring that an artificial system executes the designer's specified objective, and value alignment, understood broadly as the normative problem of ensuring that the specified objective is ethically sound. A recent objection—advanced in an unpublished manuscript by a colleague working on formal specification—claims that this distinction cannot be maintained because any non-trivial formalization of an objective function necessarily involves what she calls normative leakage: the choice of representation language, the weighting of error terms, and the boundary conditions of optimization all embed substantive moral commitments about distribution, risk, and agency. From this she infers that intent alignment is not a distinct, technically tractable layer but rather a masked form of value alignment, and that teaching them as separate modules in my course misrepresents the structure of the problem. I find this argument seductive but worry that it conflates the context of discovery with the context of justification: the fact that engineers make value-laden choices when building a system does not entail that the logical relation between this system maximizes R and R is morally correct collapses into identity. Before I restructure my syllabus, I want to know whether there is a rigorous conceptual defense of the narrow-broad separation that survives the normative-leakage challenge without simply appealing to an untenable fact-value dichotomy in engineering practice. Specifically, can one maintain that an AI might be perfectly intent-aligned—executing R with complete fidelity—while being profoundly misaligned with human values, even if the very selection of R was itself morally contestable? And does preserving this separation require accepting a particular meta-ethical view about the autonomy of instrumental reasoning?
