I have been volunteering with a local initiative that advises municipalities on post-conflict community repair, and over time I have developed a set of working commitments about amnesty and truth-telling that I apply whenever we review a proposal. Before I present them to our board, I want a consistency check to see whether they actually hang together.

Here are my commitments:

First, I believe that granting amnesty to perpetrators is morally permissible only when it can be shown to produce a net increase in the total truth disclosed about the harm.

Second, I hold that any victim should possess an individual veto over amnesty being granted specifically to the person who harmed them, regardless of the collective benefit.

Third, I think truth commissions should prioritize documenting structural and institutional causes of harm over collecting extensive individual testimonies, because the latter risks re-traumatization without adding explanatory value.

Fourth, I maintain that there should be no temporal limit on accountability for those who held command responsibility, even if lower-level actors are granted amnesty.

Fifth, I take reconciliation to be a legitimate public good that can justify limited institutional forgiveness, but I also believe that forgiveness itself must remain an entirely private, non-state matter.

Sixth, I argue that material reparations should be distributed according to collective harm calculations rather than individualized damage awards, in order to prevent competitive victimhood.

Please audit these six commitments for joint tenability. For each pair that is genuinely in tension, rank the severity of the conflict, explain precisely what pulls against what, and identify the least costly revision that would restore consistency without abandoning the core purpose of the commitment. Also identify at least one apparent tension that dissolves once the distinction between institutional and personal domains is kept clear. Finally, tell me which single revision—if I were forced to make only one—would clean up the most conflicts overall, and what practical cost that revision would exact on the initiative's goals.
