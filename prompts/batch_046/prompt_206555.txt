For a graduate seminar on the ontology of mental disorder, construct a decision-tree taxonomy that classifies philosophical positions on psychiatric classification through the lens of Ian Hacking's looping effects, but build it entirely from the logical space of commitments rather than from named camps or theorists.

Begin with the shared premise that psychiatric diagnostic categories can influence the self-conception and behavior of those to whom they apply.

From that root, design four branching nodes, each posed as a concrete evaluative claim that a respondent could answer without knowing which philosophical label it corresponds to:

1. Ontological impact: does this feedback alter the underlying nature of the condition being classified, or only its surface manifestations and prevalence?
2. Scope uniformity: do looping effects apply with comparable strength across all psychiatric categories, or are they concentrated in conditions defined largely by subjective experience and self-narrative?
3. Epistemic consequence: does the presence of looping undermine the classification's validity for clinical prediction and intervention, or can it be incorporated as part of the kind's dynamics without loss of authority?
4. Taxonomic response: should diagnostic manuals be revised continuously to track these feedback-driven changes (a diachronic taxonomy), or should categories remain fixed to preserve longitudinal comparability and treatment fidelity?

Map the resulting answer combinations onto at least five characterized leaf positions—include a strong interactive-kind view in which looping partly constitutes the disorder; a medical-realist view that treats looping as expression-modulation within a stable underlying kind; an eliminativist view that takes looping as evidence the category is a pure social artifact; and a pragmatic clinical view that accepts looping but insists on static categories for therapeutic consistency. Identify one logically possible combination that appears unoccupied in your tree, and diagnose briefly whether its emptiness stems from incoherence, practical unattractiveness, or being merely unexplored.

Finally, flag any pair of leaves that your four nodes cannot genuinely separate, explaining exactly where the ambiguity lies. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the instrument should be navigable by someone who knows only these conceptual distinctions, not the secondary literature.
