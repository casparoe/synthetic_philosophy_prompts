Exam question: (a) Explain the distinction between psi-ontic and psi-epistemic interpretations of the quantum state, clarifying whether the distinction is primarily metaphysical, epistemological, or both. (b) Consider the following reasoning: "Take two quantum preparations that produce non-orthogonal pure states |ψ⟩ and |φ⟩. In any psi-epistemic model, these correspond to probability distributions over ontic states λ that must overlap, since the states are not perfectly distinguishable. Now suppose an experiment demonstrates that the ontic-state supports for these two preparations are entirely disjoint. This shows that the quantum state is not merely encoding ignorance about λ; it is tracking a real feature of the system. Therefore, psi-epistemic interpretations are experimentally falsified whenever such disjointness occurs for non-orthogonal pairs." Identify the two principal flaws in this reasoning. In your answer, address the role of preparation independence and explain whether a hidden-variable theory like Bohmian mechanics is affected.

Student answer: (a) Psi-ontic interpretations hold that the quantum wavefunction represents a real feature of the physical world—either as a field in configuration space (as in Everettian mechanics) or as an entity that literally guides particles (Bohmian mechanics). Psi-epistemic interpretations hold that |ψ⟩ represents an agent's incomplete information about an underlying ontic state λ; the quantum state is analogous to a probability distribution in classical statistical mechanics, not a physical field. The distinction is therefore both metaphysical—does the wavefunction exist?—and epistemological—does it encode knowledge?

(b) The reasoning fails for two reasons. First, it assumes preparation independence: that separately prepared systems have independent distributions over λ. This assumption is required to show that non-orthogonal states must overlap. But for entangled pairs, preparation independence clearly fails, so the argument doesn't apply there. Therefore, the experimental demonstration of disjoint supports could be accommodated by a psi-epistemic model that rejects independence for entangled states.

Second, the argument overlooks hidden-variable theories such as Bohmian mechanics. In Bohmian mechanics, the wavefunction is epistemic—it merely carries information about how particles are guided—while the true ontology consists of the particle positions. Since Bohmian mechanics allows different wavefunctions to correspond to the same particle configuration, it can accommodate disjoint supports without treating |ψ⟩ as ontic. Hence the argument doesn't rule out all hidden-variable approaches.
