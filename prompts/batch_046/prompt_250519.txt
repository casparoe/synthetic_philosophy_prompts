I am enrolled in an honors philosophy seminar at my high school, and our instructor has spent the last month giving us her verdicts on whether the relation "better than" is transitive across different comparison domains. Her track record so far is as follows: in pure mathematics, she accepts that if proof A is more elegant than proof B and B more elegant than proof C, then A is more elegant than C; in gustatory taste, she denies that if wine A is smoother than B and B smoother than C, then A is smoother than C, on the grounds that the relevant sensory dimensions shift along the chain; in straightforward moral quantification, she holds that saving ten lives is better than saving five and five better than saving one, and therefore that saving ten is indeed better than saving one; but in architectural aesthetics, she denies transitivity when comparing harmony across buildings of radically different scale, even when each adjacent pair favors the smaller structure. She also noted, in an aside, that competitive chess Elo ratings behave transitively, whereas judgments of "stylistic superiority" between players do not.

Next week she must evaluate a synthetic case that I have proposed for our discussion: imagine a continuous policy spectrum consisting of one thousand incremental adjustments, each endorsed as an improvement over its immediate predecessor because it reduces aggregate suffering by exactly one-thousandth of a percent; the initial policy is essentially the status quo with minimal intervention, while the final policy eliminates suffering entirely but requires the complete dissolution of individual identity into a unified collective consciousness. The specific comparison she must judge is whether the initial incremental policy is "better than" the final state, assuming she accepts each adjacent pair along the chain. Please predict her verdict, identify whether she is implicitly running a quantitative-transitivity rule, a multidimensional-comparison rule, or a spectrum-break rule that allows local improvements to accumulate into a global qualitative reversal, and tell me which of her earlier rulings best supports your reading. Then propose one further hypothetical comparison that would clearly distinguish these three rules from one another. Finally, please say explicitly what argument or consideration would change your mind about which rule she is actually using, and indicate where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
