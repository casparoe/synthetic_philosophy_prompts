Before drafting anything, please take time to brainstorm carefully: what changes about rational cooperation when agents can inspect each other's source code rather than merely predict behavior? Write a short story—substantial in scope, with concrete sensory and procedural detail—set in the near-future municipality of Apertura, which has implemented a civic framework called open-deliberation equilibrium. Collective choices are made through program equilibrium: residents run simulations of each other's procedures to arrive at cooperative outcomes that no one can improve upon unilaterally. The conceptual twist, which you should treat as central rather than decorative, is that membership in the demos is not defined by birth or residence but by simulation completeness: to hold democratic standing, your algorithm must be fully legible to the collective verification protocol without epistemic residue. The protagonist, Mara, is a civic engineer who discovers that her own deliberative architecture includes an irreducibly opaque sub-module—one that weighs genuinely incommensurable values in a way that collapses when rendered into the standard format, not because she hides it, but because the values lack a common metric within the protocol's representational scheme. If she complies, her reasoning homogenizes and she retains membership; if she refuses, she is reclassified as a non-simulated external agent and loses democratic standing, though she remains physically present and affected by every decision. Center the narrative on the moment she must cast her vote—on the very audit rule that determines her status—while knowing that her choice procedure is visible to everyone in real time. Do not provide a simple moral resolution; instead, show through action and environment whether program equilibrium here functions as a genuinely superior form of collective rationality or as a mechanism that solves coordination by defining certain minds out of the demos. Keep the philosophical tension alive through narrative consequence rather than exposition, and ensure the story could be understood by someone unfamiliar with the technical literature but attentive to the logic of the situation.
