I'm in a reading group with some mates from my philosophy course, and last week we got into a proper argument about blame and luck. One of my friends, Priya, works as a junior architect on big renovation jobs. A few months back she signed off on a structural check that had a hidden flaw — not in her maths, but in old survey data she had no reason to distrust. By pure chance the flaw only showed up in a part of the building that was empty during testing; if the wind had blown a bit different that week, the same weakness would have caused a partial collapse while people were inside. Meanwhile her colleague Tom made basically the same call on another site, using the same dodgy dataset, and nothing went wrong. Now the firm treats Priya like she's careless and reckless, but Tom's still seen as solid. Priya says she did exactly what Tom did, so it's unfair that there reputation is ruined and his isn't. But another mate says since the risk was real and she was the one who let it through, she should carry the stain regardless of luck.

I'm not sure either view gets what's bothering me — it feels like there's a gap between judging her choice and judging her as a person, but I can't quite name it. Is there a philosophical angle on this kind of thing, where moral weight seems to come from something outside the agent's control, and should Priya and Tom really recieve different treatment?
