In debates about historical objectivity, Leopold von Ranke's formula that the historian should show the past 'as it actually was'—sometimes rendered in its original German form—is frequently invoked to dismiss arguments about narrative framing, selection bias, or the claim that all history is constructed interpretation. Treat that phrase not as a settled doctrine but as an ambiguous one-liner.

First, separate the precise theses that 'how it actually was' could be expressing. Does that reading actually support their rebuttal, or does it collapse into one of the trivial or false variants?
