hey, i sit on our city's civic integration and public space committee — we review exceptions to the Charter of Neutral Public Presence, which is basically our local version of strict secular space rules for municipally run buildings. we vote thursday.

the case: Riverview Aquatic Center, a city-owned pool, wants to run a 6-month pilot called Water Access for All. it offers women-only swim sessions with adjusted hygiene protocols — full-coverage swimwear including head coverings allowed, no standard swim-cap mandate — because right now women from the local Somali and Kurdish communities basically never come to open swim. the health dept is backing it with a diabetes-prevention grant that requires measurable participation increases in underserved neighborhoods. the pool director filed for an exception under section 4b of the charter, which allows practices that don't impede core public function.

our legal advisor says approving this creates a visible religious accomodation in a public facility and undermines the charter's whole logic: that neutrality requires everyone to enter space as an undifferentiated citizen. supporters say the charter confuses formal neutrality with actual access — if the rule excludes a specific group, it's not neutral, it's assimilation by another name.

i need a memo to circulate before the meeting. recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete timeline — pilot start date, review checkpoints, sunset clause untill the review is done. it should treat the assimilationist argument seriously — don't just dismiss it — but also explain why the other view is stronger. and please don't just list philosophers; the committee cares about whether recognition or assimilation actually protects equal dignity here. also avoid anything that sounds like a bibliography request — we don't need that.

write it like an internal committee memo, not a term paper. maybe 2-3 pages worth of reasoning but keep it readable.
