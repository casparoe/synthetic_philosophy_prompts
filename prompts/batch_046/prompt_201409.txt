I'm working through the Dennett-Searle exchange on original versus derived intentionality for a philosophy of mind seminar, and a comparison keeps surfacing in discussion that I'd like to pressure-test. The comparison is that derived intentionality is like the shadow of a tree cast on a wall: the shadow can be interpreted as being about the tree—its outline tracks the tree's shape, and an observer can read information about the tree from it—but the shadow itself possesses no intrinsic aboutness. Turn off the lamp, which is the external interpreter or causal source, and there is nothing left to interpret. Students in the seminar use this to argue that Dennett's wandering two-bitser is in exactly the same position as the shadow: however complex its internal state-transitions, they only become beliefs or desires because we, the observers, shine the lamp of the intentional stance onto them. By contrast, Searle's original intentionality is likened to the tree itself, or perhaps to the lamp, carrying its aboutness intrinsically rather than borrowing it.

Could you lay out the correspondence table precisely—what plays the role of the tree, the lamp, the wall, the shadow, and the observer in the case of the two-bitser? Then identify where the mapping fractures: for instance, the shadow is causally passive and lacks internal dynamics, whereas the two-bitser is designed to wander, update internal registers in response to environmental perturbations, and maintain homeostatic goals. Does the analogy quietly import a static, one-way representational model that mischaracterizes Dennett's claim that interpretation can be grounded in functional design rather than in an external observer? And does the anti-Dennett conclusion—that the two-bitser's states are merely derived and therefore philosophically second-class—survive once these disanalogies are explicit, or was the shadow doing the argumentative work all along? If the analogy collapses, is there a better comparison—perhaps drawn from dynamical systems or feedback control—that captures the dispute without smuggling in the very distinction it aims to defend?
