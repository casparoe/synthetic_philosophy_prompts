I'm prepping an interveiw for my podcast — the guest is an unnamed geohistorian who argues the Anthropocene should be dated to 1610, the Orbis spike, and that the ICS shouldn't be the body deciding epoch boundaries; he wants a global citizens' assembly with veto power over stratigraphic naming. I need eight or ten questions that go past him just restating that. At least one should press why colonial land-use collapse is more foundational than Watt's steam engine or the Trinity test, with a follow-up keyed to the reply that it's all continuous human impact and no cutoff is special. One should ask what technical expertise citizens lack that stratigraphers have, and then a follow-up if he says democratic representation trumps technical accuracy. One on the sorites problem: depopulation in the Americas was spread over decades, so why 1610 exactly rather than 1590 or 1630? One on his claim that Pacific marine sediment cores register the 1610 CO2 dip more sharply than Greenland ice, which he uses to say the ICS is biased toward northern industrial signals — follow-up asking whether geographic unevenness kills the idea of one global boundary altogether. And one asking what he says to critics who claim epoch-labeling is just moral politics in geological clothing, with a follow-up if he concedes it's partly normative but insists the stratigraphic signal is still real. Don't ask for reading lists or citations.
