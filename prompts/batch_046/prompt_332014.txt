I cover science and ideas for an educated general audience, and I am drafting a long-form feature on the normative design of social rule-following. Please write an analytical essay—approximately 900 words—defending a precise verdict on the following controversy: can a modern pluralistic society coherently pursue high behavioral tightness (strict, uniform adherence to rules in procedural and coordination domains) while maintaining low epistemic tightness (tolerance for disagreement about the meaning of those rules and about who holds legitimate interpretive authority)? Or do behavioral and epistemic dimensions necessarily rise and fall together?

Distinguish procedural norms, where predictability is paramount, from substantive moral norms, where interpretive diversity may be valuable. Address the strongest counter-argument: that compliance without shared commitment to an interpreting authority collapses into strategic performance, undermining the social trust that makes tight coordination sustainable. Consider whether small-scale homogeneous societies and large-scale democracies face different structural constraints. Reach a clear conclusion—either that the bifurcated model is coherent and desirable, or that it is not—and justify it with the considerations you treat as decisive. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific empirical studies; build the argument conceptually. Make the reasoning accessible but rigorous enough for readers familiar with basic philosophy of social norms.
