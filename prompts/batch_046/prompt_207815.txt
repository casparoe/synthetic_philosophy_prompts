Write a two-page research proposal for a semester philosophy paper (about 5,000 words) on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: pleasures seem to share no common qualitative feel, which threatens hedonism with reducing well-being to tracking a disjunctive list rather than a unified good. Propose investigating a structural rather than qualitative solution—specifically, whether all pleasures share a formal second-order endorsement relation in which the subject takes the experience itself as a reason to continue or preserve it. State the precise question: does this relational structure survive counterexamples such as addictive pleasures (endorsed only retrospectively) or masochistic pleasures (involving first-order aversion), and if it survives, does it give hedonism sufficient unity without collapsing back into qualitative essentialism? Explain why standard responses—phenomenal intentionality accounts, pure attitudinal theories, and pluralist hedonism—leave this structural option underexplored. Outline the method: conceptual analysis of three ordinary-experience test cases plus a formal model of the endorsement relation, not an empirical psychology study. Specify what counts as success (a defensible structural criterion distinguishing pleasure from other pro-attitudes) and failure (the criterion either over-includes non-pleasures or secretly relies on qualitative description). Give an honest scope assessment: is this tractable in one semester without clinical or neuroscientific literature? Write in American English. Do not request or include a bibliography, citation list, or specific named references; refer to positions generally and note that citations will be added separately.
