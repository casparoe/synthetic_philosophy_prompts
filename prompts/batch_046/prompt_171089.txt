As a retired physicist who has lately turned to philosophy of language, I have been working through Douglas Hofstadter’s study of Clement Marot’s verse and the problem of translating poetry under formal constraint—specifically whether rhyme, meter, and hidden compositional architecture are part of a poem’s meaning or merely ornamental vehicles for it. In my discussion group, two positions have crystallized. The form-preservationists hold that hidden formal constraints—exemplified by a hypothetical Marot epigram in which the initial letters of successive lines form the acrostic FRANCOIS, encoding a patron’s name invisible in the poem’s surface discourse on spring and friendship—are inseparable from the work’s identity and must be recreated in translation, even if this requires altering surface semantics. The semantic-functionalists hold that translation serves communicative intent, that such hidden structures carry no interpretable content and were not available to the original audience, and that adapting or dropping them is a legitimate functional choice rather than a loss of meaning. I want you to treat these camps as parties to an adversarial collaboration.

Please draft the joint statement they should have produced. Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims—about Marot’s historical practice, about the distinction between surface meaning and compositional architecture, about the aims of translation, and about the specific hypothetical acrostic case. Follow this with a disagreements table: state each camp’s position in wording its own adherents would accept; identify the cruxes, especially whether hidden formal features without semantic content constitute part of poetic meaning and whether a translator’s duty extends to features the original audience could not have recognized; and, for each crux, present the concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, together with each side’s predicted verdict recorded in advance. If any portion of the dispute turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, say so and explain why. Keep the format formal and precise, suitable for publication as a collaborative document, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests.

Finally, please state how confident you are in each major section of your analysis—particularly in your characterization of the form-preservationist position and in your identification of the cruxes—and explicitly single out the point about which you are least certain.
