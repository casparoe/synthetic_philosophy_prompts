In the explanations I have encountered, ISOTYPE is consistently likened to modern emoji sets or to IKEA assembly instructions—a finite vocabulary of standardized icons arranged by combinatorial rules to transmit information across linguistic barriers with minimal ambiguity. My instructor has used this comparison to suggest that Neurath effectively anticipated contemporary demands for globally legible data visualization, and therefore that his picture-language solves the problem of cross-cultural scientific communication by removing verbal ambiguity altogether.

Please audit how far this analogy actually holds. Separate the disanalogies that genuinely undermine the conclusion—that a picture language can function as a culture-independent, ambiguity-free medium for unified science—from those that are superficial. Finally, state whether that conclusion survives once the mapping is corrected, and propose a different analogy, if one exists, that better captures the philosophical function Neurath intended for his system rather than its superficial graphic form.
