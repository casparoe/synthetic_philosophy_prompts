Provide a precise numerical credence, between 0 and 1, for the claim that in Everettian quantum mechanics the Born rule for subjective probabilities can be justified non-circularly by self-locating uncertainty alone, without any decision-theoretic supplementation, on the condition that personal identity through branching is modelled as enduring rather than fissioning — that is, as a single observer who is merely ignorant of which decoherent history she inhabits, rather than as a plurality of post-branching successors.

Your answer should decompose the reasoning that produces this number:

1. Estimate the probability that self-locating uncertainty is coherent at all in a deterministic branching universe where no stochastic mechanism selects a branch.
2. Estimate the probability that, under an enduring-identity model, applying a principle of indifference across branches yields amplitude-weighted credences rather than a uniform counting measure over branches or observer-moments.
3. Estimate the probability that decision-theoretic arguments for the Born rule are either circular or dispensable, so that their absence does not leave a justificatory gap.
4. Show how these estimates combine — whether multiplicatively, with weighted addition, or by some other structure — to reach your final figure, and identify which of the three is the dominant source of variance: the one whose revision by ±0.2 would shift your overall credence the most.

Finally, specify what kind of conceptual argument — not an empirical finding — would most plausibly cause you to revise your overall number upward or downward by at least 0.25.
