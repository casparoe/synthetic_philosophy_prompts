I have been tracking a recurring pattern in philosophical and technical discussions about agency, flexibility, and determinism, and I am unsure whether it has an established name. The pattern arises when someone proposes that genuine freedom requires not merely acting within rules, but being able to suspend or revise those rules in context. To model this, they construct a framework that includes explicit flexibility clauses—procedures specifying when an agent should deviate from its default patterns. Once embedded, however, these clauses operate as ordinary rules: the agent breaks routine only in the precise conditions the clause lists, and any attempt to apply them requires consulting the same procedural hierarchy. When observers point out that the behavior remains rigid—merely a more complex choreography—the defenders do not abandon the framework. Instead, they introduce meta-level instructions about how to apply the flexibility clauses with appropriate judgment, or about when to invoke the rules for breaking rules. Each layer is intended to restore spontaneity, yet each further encodes deviation into a checklist.

I have seen versions of this in debates over AI corrigibility, where safeguards meant to permit deviation become hard constraints, and in philosophical accounts of second-order desire that treat the wish to revise one's wishes as just another deterministic mechanism governing first-order ones. The phenomenon seems distinct from ordinary bureaucratic rule-accumulation, because the added rules are specifically about escaping rules; it also seems different from the familiar wasp-like rigidity in which an agent simply lacks any internal mechanism for deviation, since here the mechanism for deviation is present but has been turned into part of the routine. Is there a recognized term for this particular failure mode—the recursive proceduralization of flexibility itself? If there is none, how should one distinguish it conceptually from simple overfitting, from metric-corruption dynamics like Goodhart's law, and from the broader paradox of trying to systematize spontaneity? What boundary cases would show that a system has genuinely escaped this loop rather than merely added another layer to it?
