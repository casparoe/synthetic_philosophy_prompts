I've been trying to work out a consistent personal stance on how beliefs should shift when the evidence isn't all-or-nothing, and I want a consistency check before I commit to it in writing. Here is the package as I understand it:

(1) I accept Jeffrey conditionalization: if I become, say, 60% sure that a signal is noisy rather than clear, I should redistribute my credences over the outcomes in proportion to that 60/40 split, rather than treating the noise as certain.

(2) I think the rigidity condition is essential — within each branch defined by the uncertain evidence, the relative probabilities of further outcomes should stay exactly as they were before the update.

(3) I don't see a principled boundary between observational and theoretical updating, so I think Jeffrey's rule should apply uniformly, whether I'm revising beliefs about a light flickering or about the reliability of an entire measurement theory.

(4) At the same time, I think there are cases where the evidence doesn't just populate a branch but destabilizes the tree itself — for instance, learning that my perceptual system might be systematically biased should alter not just my beliefs about specific colors, but the internal ratios I previously held within my color-vision framework.

(5) I hold that rational updating in this style doesn't require me to have precise numerical priors over every hypothesis in advance; direct assignment of probabilities to the evidence-partition cells is enough.

(6) Separately, in decision scenarios where a randomizing event has already occurred but remains unknown to me — like the coin in the counterfactual mugging case — I think I should hold my belief about the outcome fixed at my actual credence and refuse to update on counterfactual suppositions.

My worry is that (2) and (4) are at war: rigidity says keep the internal ratios fixed, but framework destabilization seems to demand changing them. I also wonder whether (3) makes that conflict unavoidable, whether (6) secretly relies on the same kind of conditioning I endorse in (1), and whether (5) is actually compatible with the Bayesian structure I'm invoking. Please audit these commitments in order of severity: for each genuine tension, explain how badly they clash, what the cheapest revision is, and which single change would clean up the most problems. Also point out any pair that looks tense but actually dissolves on closer inspection. Finally, tell me what specific argument or finding would overturn your assessment — for example, a proof that rigidity must apply globally across all belief revision, or a demonstration that Jeffrey-style updating collapses without exact priors — and be explicit about where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
