Please argue that Kelly betting, interpreted as the geometric-rationality criterion for capital growth, should not be transferred directly to epistemic contexts—specifically, to the allocation of confidence across competing models—without a structural modification. Defend the view that when an agent faces ambiguity about the model space itself, not merely about parameters within a fixed model, the correct geometric-rational rule is not to maximize the expected logarithm of predictive accuracy, but to maximize a robustness-weighted geometric mean taken over a dynamically expandable set of hypotheses, where the weights depend on the agent’s current capacity to generate alternative models rather than on prior probabilities alone. Explain why this implies that rational agents will systematically under-bet on high-confidence predictions in ways that standard Bayesian updating and risk-aversion explanations both fail to capture, and address the objection that this collapses into ordinary model-averaging with an arbitrary penalty term.
