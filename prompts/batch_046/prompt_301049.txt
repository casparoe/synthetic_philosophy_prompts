A philosophy blogger summarized Robert Nozick's position on animals like this: Nozick defends a two-tier moral system in Anarchy, State, and Utopia. For human beings, he applies strict Kantian side constraints—no one may be used merely as a means, regardless of aggregate benefit. For non-human animals, he applies a utilitarian calculus that weighs suffering and pleasure without invoking rights. Crucially, however, the blogger claims Nozick does not reduce animals to zero direct moral status; instead, animals fall under what the blogger calls a "threshold deontology" for interspecies ethics. On this reading, animals possess weak, defeasible protections against severe suffering that can be overridden only by substantial human needs, never by trivial ones. The blogger concludes that Nozick's view is neither pure speciesism nor pure utilitarianism, but rather a graded moral-status framework in which rational agency determines which tier applies, with animals occupying an intermediate tier of protected-but-overridable standing.
