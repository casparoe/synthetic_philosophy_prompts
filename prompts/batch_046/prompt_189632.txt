Construct a thought experiment to specifications.

Domain: consciousness as a user illusion, specifically predictive self-model or controlled-hallucination frameworks.

Task: Design a one-paragraph clinical-neurological scenario—realistic, present-day or near-future, with no science-fiction devices such as perfect predictors, mind-reading scanners, or exotic physics—in which a human subject produces fluent, coherent first-person reports of rich subjective experience, yet lacks a specific structural feature that predictive-processing user-illusionists take to be necessary for genuine phenomenal consciousness. The missing feature should be a causally integrated, counterfactually open predictive loop that is embedded in global error-correction dynamics and coupled to lower-level sensory hierarchies; in its place, the subject’s brain maintains only an informationally encapsulated phenomenal placeholder—a structurally complete representation of what-it-is-likeness that drives language and behavior without being woven into the brain’s live predictive dynamics. The condition should be derivable from known neuropsychological phenomena (for example, an engineered variant of dissociation, neglect, or blindsight-like decoupling), not invented ex nihilo.

The scenario must generate the following verdicts from the theories themselves, not by stipulation:
1. The predictive-processing user-illusionist denies that the subject is phenomenally conscious, arguing that the self-model is present and reportable but not “lived” because it is decoupled from the requisite integrated predictive dynamics.
2. The global workspace theorist also denies consciousness, but on the distinct ground that there is no global broadcast or cross-modal integration linking the placeholder to sensory and cognitive modules.
3. The direct phenomenal realist affirms consciousness, holding that the coherence of first-person report and behavior is sufficient evidence for intrinsic phenomenal properties, regardless of whether the underlying architecture involves an integrated loop or an isolated placeholder.

After presenting the scenario, provide:
- A demonstration that each of the three positions reaches its assigned verdict through its own principles, without ad hoc adjustment.
- Identification of the exact structural feature—the presence of an integrated, counterfactually open loop versus an encapsulated placeholder—that does the work and explains why the first two theories converge.
- An explanation of what happens if that feature is restored: does the disagreement collapse, or does a different disagreement between the three positions emerge?
- An honest note on how the phenomenal realist would attack the case, for instance by denying that predictive integration is necessary for consciousness, or by charging that the other views confuse the mechanism of consciousness with consciousness itself.

Constraints: The scenario itself must be no longer than one paragraph. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke obscure theorists by name; refer only to the three positions described above. The disagreement must be produced by applying the theories to the case, not built into the scenario by fiat. Write for an audience that has no web access, so define any technical terms (e.g., “counterfactually open predictive loop”) in the prompt itself.
