I am a retired physicist who has taken up philosophy of well-being in retirement, and I am constructing a seminar exercise for fellow newcomers. Nozick's experience-machine scenario is familiar, but I want a sharper, self-contained diagnostic instrument—one that sorts people by the judgments they actually make about concrete cases rather than by the labels they prefer.

Please design a decision-tree taxonomy (a flowchart with binary or ternary branches) that classifies positions on well-being using five specific diagnostic scenarios. The root commitment should be whether a life of maximal subjective satisfaction spent entirely inside an undetectable, perfectly functioning experience machine can be as good as, or better than, an objectively identical life lived in physical reality. From there, the tree should branch through the following four further cases, each phrased as a concrete choice rather than an abstract principle:

1. The intermittent-exit case: the subject spends 80 percent of time inside the machine with maximal satisfaction, but retains one unscripted, genuinely reciprocal interpersonal relationship outside, with full memory of both domains.
2. The epistemic-inversion case: the subject is in fact living outside in reality, but is permanently and undetectably convinced that they are inside the machine; subjective experience is identical to the root case, but the metaphysical status is reversed.
3. The achievement-without-causation case: inside the machine, the subject sincerely believes they have independently authored an original mathematical proof, yet the proof was in fact generated algorithmically by the machine without any cognitive contribution from the subject.
4. The plural-value trade-off case: the machine offers a forced choice between (a) intense, uniform pleasure with no variety or struggle, and (b) a life containing moderate pleasure, authentic effort, and objectively real accomplishment, but not both.

The tree should have exactly six terminal leaves, corresponding to the following positions:

A. Pure subjective hedonism: well-being is identical to experienced satisfaction, irrespective of reality, authorship, or variety.
B. Reality-coupled hedonism: experienced satisfaction determines well-being, but only, or primarily, when causally tied to the actual world.
C. Desire-fulfillment with an authenticity condition: well-being requires that one's strongest desires are satisfied and that one is not systematically deceived about the manner of their satisfaction.
D. Objective-list pluralism: well-being requires the actual attainment of a plurality of objective goods—such as knowledge, friendship, and achievement—independent of subjective attitude, with permissible trade-offs among them.
E. Perfectionist or natural-function view: well-being consists in the excellent exercise of distinctively human capacities, which requires real engagement with reality and cannot be fully simulated.
F. An unoccupied or inconsistent combination: if the logical space of answers to the five cases is not fully exhausted by A through E, identify the remaining answer-pattern, place it here, and briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

For each internal node, state the separating question in plain language, so that a respondent can answer without knowing philosophical terminology. For each leaf, provide a concise diagnosis of which pattern of answers leads there. Explicitly flag any pairs of positions that the tree cannot cleanly separate—if two positions yield identical verdicts on all five cases, say so rather than inventing a spurious distinction.

Please present the result as a self-contained instrument, no more than about 600 words excluding the scoring key. Conclude with a concise mapping table that links each answer-pattern (e.g., Root: Yes/No; Case 1: ...; etc.) to its corresponding leaf A–F. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the cases should be specific enough that a reader can respond to them without consulting external texts. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate for a retired scientist addressing a philosophy seminar.
