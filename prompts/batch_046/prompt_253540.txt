I'm presenting my paper at the ethics workshop next tuesday. I defend a way out of the paradox of deontology — you know, the problem that forbidding me to push one off a bridge to save five looks irrational if moral reasons are all on one scale. My claim is that this assumes all reasons are aggregate-comparable, but if we split them into directed duties and background entitlements then the paradox dissolves without becoming a side-constraint story. The room will be mostly consequentialists plus a couple Kantians who think I'm just relabeling the formula of humanity. I need objections ordered by damage: first the agregate welfare hit, then the Kantian reply about treating persons as ends, then the hidden one I'm praying they skip — cases where the duty structure and the entitlement structure both apply and seem to conflict. For each tell me what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and where my split-reason framwork is actually weakest. Don't need citations, just the prep sheet.
