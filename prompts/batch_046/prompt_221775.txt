Here're my gut reactions on whether I'd still be me in different origin stories, and I want to know if there's one principle behind them or if I'm just being sloppy. If my parents had used the exact same sperm and egg but conception happened two years ealier, that's still me — just born sooner. If it was a different sperm from the same dad, that's my sibling, not me, so the specific sperm seems to matter. But now it gets weird: if scientists built a syntetic sperm with the exact same nuclear DNA and even the same histone modifications, and used it with my mom's actual egg, part of me thinks that's still me, even though it's not physically the same cell. Meanwhile, if they used my mom's egg but swapped out the cytoplasm and mitocondria for a donor's, I still say that's me — so the egg's nucleus is enough? But then there's the split-embryo case: if my zygote had divided at the two-cell stage and both halves were implanted, I feel like both people might have a claim to being me, which clashes with my "only one me" intuition. There's also this case: same sperm and egg, but one gene was edited in before implantation. If it's fixing a mutation I don't have, I think I'm still me; if it's adding a gene I never had, I'm less sure. So is the principle about the actual biological tokens, the genetic information, the causal chain, or something about the embryo's potential? Which of my verdicts should I drop for consistency? And what does your best principle predict for a syntetic embryo built from my parents' DNA sequences but assembled in a lab with no actual gametes involved?
