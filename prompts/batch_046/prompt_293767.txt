I'm taking a graduate seminar on environmental ethics, and the problem of indirect wild-animal suffering has become personally urgent: after adopting a plant-based diet six months ago, I recently tried to formalize my reasoning using a suffering-years-per-hectare-year framework, only to encounter the claim that crop cultivation kills more sentient animals per calorie than pasture-raised beef. I need to know whether pushing that comparison to its logical extreme collapses into absurdity, because if the underlying principle survives, my dietary choice may not actually minimize harm.

Here is the argument I constructed for our discussion section. (1) If an action is avoidable and foreseeably causes more total sentient suffering than an available alternative, then choosing that action is morally wrong. (2) Industrial crop agriculture kills wild rodents, insects, and other sentient animals through mechanized harvesting and pesticides, and this harm is largely avoidable by consuming pasture-raised beef that requires minimal cultivated feed. (3) Therefore, choosing crops over pasture beef is wrong, and beef consumption is permissible or even required when it displaces crop demand. (4) But applying (1) consistently requires us to consider counterfactual land use, not only direct harm: maintaining pasture prevents that land from being rewilded, which would allow wild herbivores to exist whose lives contain net suffering, so beef consumption also suppresses potential wild populations and carries indirect harm. (5) The same logic extends to any human occupation of land—growing vegetables prevents rewilding, foraging wild plants displaces animals that would have consumed them, and even building the seminar room occupies ground that could support suffering wild life—so (1) entails that any consumption or activity tied to land occupation is impermissible. (6) Since all human survival requires occupying land, (1) implies that human existence itself is morally wrong. (7) That conclusion is absurd. I feel the error lies somewhere between comparing direct harms in (2)-(3) and invoking counterfactual rewilding in (4)-(5), perhaps an equivocation between causing suffering and failing to prevent it, but I cannot name the precise step. If I must preserve (1) rather than accept (6), which premise should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost for the comparison between crop cultivation and beef?
