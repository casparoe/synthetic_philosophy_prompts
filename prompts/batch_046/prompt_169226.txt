After a long argument with a friend about epistemic luck and testimony, I want to check whether my commitments actually hang together before I defend them further. Here is where I have landed:

(1) The Gettier cases show that knowledge requires justification that is modally robust—specifically, it must exclude both intervening and environmental luck—rather than being merely non-accidental in some informal sense.

(2) I reject modal safety and tracking conditions as necessary conditions on knowledge, because they overgenerate failures in ordinary perceptual and testimonial knowledge.

(3) Testimonial knowledge is genuinely transmitted: if a competent source knows p and communicates it to a recipient, the recipient can come to know p without replicating the original evidential base, provided she exercises appropriate epistemic vigilance.

(4) In collective testimony—where knowledge passes through a chain—I maintain that every intermediate link must be free of Gettier-style luck. If one link involves environmental luck (for instance, a source that is accurate only because of a contingent calibration error that happens to correct itself), transmission is broken and the final recipient does not know.

(5) I endorse a virtue-epistemic framework—knowledge as apt belief, where aptness is accuracy because of competence—but I deny that the believer's own cognitive competence must be the primary causal source of that accuracy; in testimonial cases, the speaker's competence can do the necessary work.

Please audit this set: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severely? For each real tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single change to my commitments would clean up the most problems overall, and rank the tensions accordingly rather than treating them as equally bad.
