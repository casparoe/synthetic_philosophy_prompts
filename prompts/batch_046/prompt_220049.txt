Question: Explain the procreation asymmetry. Contrast the standard person-affecting account with an impersonal formulation, and evaluate whether the impersonal version escapes the non-identity problem without reducing to a total view.

Student answer: The asymmetry is the claim that we have strong reason not to create a person whose life would be bad, but no corresponding reason to create a person whose life would be good. On the person-affecting account, this is explained by the fact that a suffering individual actually exists and is harmed, whereas a merely possible happy person never exists and so cannot be deprived of anything. This sidesteps the non-identity problem, since that problem asks whether a specific individual is harmed by being brought into existence with a flawed life; the asymmetry avoids it altogether by comparing existence with non-existence rather than comparing two possible lives for the same person.

An impersonal formulation, by contrast, treats the asymmetry as a structural feature of value: suffering is bad in a way that demands prevention, while happiness is good but does not demand production. Because this version does not require identifying a particular victim, it applies even to choices that determine which people exist. Still, it risks collapsing into a total view if well-being is summed impartially. To block this collapse, the impersonal asymmetry must assign suffering lexical or disproportionately negative weight and treat happiness as lacking positive weight altogether. Consequently, the view can maintain that suffering is to be avoided without generating any obligation to maximize the number of happy lives, and it handles identity-changing choices without invoking the non-identity problem.
