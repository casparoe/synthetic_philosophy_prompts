I hold a somewhat specific reading of Wittgenstein's later remarks on religious belief that goes beyond the standard non-cognitivist gloss. On my view, religious pictures—say, the Last Judgment, miracles, or divine providence—function not merely as empirically empty expressions of attitude but as framework propositions that regulate affective and practical orientation within a form of life, and because they are grammatically insulated, they neither confirm nor conflict with scientific claims in any direct logical sense. The twist I want to defend is that this insulation should, if taken seriously, entail a strict compartmentalization: a believer who genuinely treats the resurrection as non-empirical should not also treat it as a historical event competing with archaeological evidence, and a believer who prays for healing should not simultaneously assess medical prognosis by empirical standards in a way that treats prayer as an intervention with probabilistic efficacy. In other words, Wittgenstein's account predicts that authentic religious belief should be practically quarantined from empirical reasoning, yet descriptive anthropology and ordinary religious practice suggest that believers routinely violate this quarantine—switching between registers without the contradiction or anxiety the compartmentalization thesis seems to require. What would genuinely count against this reading and its implied prediction? I want to know which observations from religious practice, which passages in Wittgenstein's lecture notes on religious belief or in the Culture and Value fragments, and which arguments in philosophy of language should make me abandon the claim that Wittgenstein's view entails strict compartmentalization. I am particularly interested in whether there is a principled distinction between framework uses and within-framework empirical claims that allows believers to hold both without contradiction, and whether that distinction collapses under pressure. I also want an honest assessment of whether my view has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every apparent empirical commitment by a believer can be redescribed as just part of the form of life, does the position risk becoming tautological, and is that insulation a problem for an interpretive claim about Wittgenstein rather than a normative claim about how religion ought to function? Please distinguish decisive defeaters from merely awkward ones, include what a sympathetic Wittgensteinian would themselves concede as damaging, and conclude with whether the view is well-insulated and whether that insulation is virtuous or vicious.
