So our gallery board's past calls on sketchy artists: we bought the serene landscape series after the painter turned out to be a tax fraudster—stain's biographical, not on the canvas; we rejected the state-commissioned mural depicting purges—moral poison is in the work itself; we bought the novel manuscript from an author who was abusive at home—the text stands alone; and we rejected the installation built with forced labor—harm is baked into production. Now there's this photographer who engineered dangerous refugee-camp scenarios to get more visceral documentary shots—images are technically brilliant and raised real awareness, but she manufactured the risk to subjects. Should we acquire them? What's our closest anolog? Are we actually running a separate-the-artist rule, a separate-the-content rule, or a process-integrity rule? Depending on yes or no, what principle are we locking in, and what's the one future case that'd actually split those rules apart?
