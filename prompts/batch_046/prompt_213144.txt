While participating in a philosophy-of-mind reading group, I witnessed a sustained disagreement between two colleagues, Mara and Leo, concerning a hypothetical substrate they call the careenium — a structured lattice of physical tokens referred to as simmballs, arranged so that their spatial and dynamic relations are meant to realise symbolic states. Mara opened by arguing that in any actual careenium the causal efficacy belongs solely to the physical properties of the simmballs: their mass, charge, and collision dynamics. She maintained that symbolic interpretation — identifying a cluster as representing 'negation' or 'belief-state B' — is purely observer-relative, comparable to seeing shapes in clouds, and therefore that symbols do not literally push the substrate around. Leo responded that this overlooks the design purpose of engineered careenia: the physical properties are deliberately configured so that symbolic rules are physically instantiated. If a simmball's geometry alters its trajectory in a manner that tracks logical entailment, he claimed, then the symbolic content is not an overlay but an integral part of the causal chain; the symbol is doing work, not merely being witnessed. Mara countered that Leo is only redescribing physical causation in semantic clothing — it is the geometry that exerts force, not the meaning. Leo then pressed a further point: if Mara is correct, then no computation within a careenium could be morally relevant, because there would be no principled basis for distinguishing a pattern of simmballs that implements sentient computation from one that implements mere noise; the symbolic level would be causally inert and hence irrelevant to attributions of consciousness. Mara conceded that symbolic interpretation might be necessary for moral attribution, but insisted this is an epistemic or normative criterion rather than a causal one: we attribute sentience on the basis of functional organisation, yet the substrate is still moved only by physics. Leo concluded by questioning whether Mara's separation of causal and normative relevance can bear the weight she places on it, since if the symbolic pattern makes no causal difference, then two physically identical careenia — one running a conscious algorithm, the other running its inverse — would be indistinguishable and equally deserving of moral concern, a result he takes to be absurd.

Please adjudicate this exchange point by point. For each specific argument advanced by Mara or Leo, state clearly who is right about that point and where, if anywhere, each party is missing something. I want a committed answer: take a definite position on whether symbols in a careenium genuinely push the substrate around, and on whether resolving that question is essential for determining which computations matter morally.
