Write a parody of a tenure-track faculty job advertisement—approximately 650 words, in deadpan institutional and bureaucratic prose—for the Department of Ontological Uncertainty at a research university that has formally adopted radical dream-skepticism inspired by Zhuangzi’s butterfly dream. The advertisement should treat the live possibility that the hiring committee, the applicants, and the university itself are presently being dreamed by a butterfly (or are butterflies dreaming themselves into committees) as a genuine administrative and legal problem rather than a metaphor. Include specific sections: required qualifications (e.g., how an applicant demonstrates a publication record if publication may be a dreamed event); a definition of “original research” under conditions in which the researcher may not exist outside the dream; a diversity-and-inclusion statement that addresses the moral status of entities who may be purely oneiric; a description of the campus (noting that buildings, parking, and laboratories share the same ontological status as the butterfly); and a benefits sidebar addressing health insurance for bodies whose existence is disputed. The satire should exaggerate real features of both modern academic bureaucracy—credential inflation, proceduralism, institutional risk management—and classical dream skepticism, especially the circularity of appealing to coherence or “waking” verification to distinguish dream from reality. A reader familiar with Zhuangzi should recognize that the philosophical target is being engaged fairly, not distorted. Maintain strict deadpan consistency: do not resolve the paradox with a winking “it was all a dream” ending, and do not allow the voice to slip into explicit acknowledgment that the premise is absurd. The prompt should not request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
