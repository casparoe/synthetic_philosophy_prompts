I'm a retired marine biologist who got into philosophy recently after I stopped working, and now I'm sitting on our university's Institutional Procurement and Climate Ethics Panel. We have a vote Thursday on whether to renew a three-year contract with the only supplier that can provide high-purity isotopic sulfur tracers for our ocean acidification lab. The factory emits SF6, which has enormous warming potential, and our share of global emissions from this purchase is about one part in five hundred thousand, so the standard inefficacy objection seems to apply: dropping us changes almost nothing, while the alternative supplier has a 40% purity failure rate that would wreck months of experiments.

But I've been chewing on a twist that feels like it belongs in philosophy rather than just climate science. Harm accumulation isn't always linear, and if we're near a stochastic tipping point in ocean heat uptake, then sub-perceptual contributions might not be morally neutral in the same way they are under simple aggregation. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action with a clear time frame—renew with conditions, switch with a phased transition, or reject outright—and it must take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously, especially the claim that near a non-linear threshold the usual drop-in-the-bucket logic collapses. Don't include any bibliographies or reading lists; I just want straight reasoning and an actionable bottom line. Oh, and my typing is rusty, so excuse any typos like recieve if I slip up.
