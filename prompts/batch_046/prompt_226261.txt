I keep noticing a pattern in consent debates that I cannot place in the existing taxonomy. Here are two concrete cases. A patient gives fully informed, voluntary, competent consent to a procedure, understanding every physical risk correctly; but she understands risk in a framework where only bodily harm counts, while the practitioner records and acts on the consent within a framework where psychological trauma and loss of identity-constituting capacities are central. No false belief, no coercion, no incompetence -- just consent registered in one normative frame and executed in another. A parallel case in political philosophy: citizens democratically authorize a policy with full causal knowledge, but they evaluate the outcomes against a standard of individual rights, while the policymakers apply the authorization within a framework of aggregate welfare optimization. In both situations every standard checklist for valid consent -- disclosure, voluntariness, capacity -- is satisfied, yet it feels like something has gone wrong with the authorization itself. Is there an established term for this kind of cross-framework consent failure, and how should it be distinguished from ordinary deception about facts, from mere value disagreement, or from hidden coercion? If no concept captures it precisely, I want a precise coinage with boundary conditions, not just a bare label. Also, if this framing rests on a confusion -- if consent is genuinely frame-independent and the worry dissolves -- please say so directly rather than accommodating the premise politely.
