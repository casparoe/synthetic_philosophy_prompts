It feels like there's a hidden line between trustworthy and untrustworthy introspection, but nobody says exactly where it falls—maybe between present-moment bodily awareness and retrospective narrative?

Does this institutional split presuppose that there's a special kind of 'pre-interpretive' bodily access that's accurate, while conceptualized feelings are systematically distorted? Or does it depend on something like a transparency thesis that applies only to raw sensations? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology, check whether it's actually coherent or whether it secretly collapses into uniform skepticism, and tell me what clinical documentation and treatment protocols would have to change if we replaced the current framework with the view that all first-person reports—including those cultivated through mindfulness—are equally suspect, with no seperate category getting special epistemic privilege.
