The proposed amendment would require that at least twenty-five percent of the capital budget be directed to wastewater remediation for the next thirty years. Full repeal would require a two-thirds council vote plus a citywide referendum. The measure also includes an unusual provision: every five years, an independent engineering audit may determine whether wastewater remains the primary constraint on local water safety. If the audit finds it is not, a three-fifths council vote may suspend the funding floor without triggering the full repeal procedure.

Proponents argue that current politicians consistently underinvest in invisible infrastructure because voters reward visible, short-term projects, and that the city must therefore bind itself against this predictable weakness—much as a person might take steps to guard against her own future impulsiveness. Opponents reply that locking in spending for three decades treats future residents as less competent and less sovereign than present ones, and that the audit clause is merely a cosmetic flexibility that preserves an essentially rigid constraint.

I can see force in both positions, but I sense a principled disagreement beneath the policy dispute—something about whether a democratic body can legitimately constrain its own successors. I want a committed judgment: should I vote in favor of the amendment? Take a definite stance, defend it with clear reasoning, and do not merely list the competing considerations and leave the choice to me.
