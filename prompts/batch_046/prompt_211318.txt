Sen's liberal paradox is typically presented with two agents and a book-choice scenario to show that minimal liberal rights conflict with the Pareto principle. I'm interested in a conceptual issue that standard expositions tend to suppress: suppose an agent's preference over social states can depend not only on her own assigned option, but on whether another agent's preference is being honored—a second-order, or meta-liberal, commitment. Does extending the liberal principle to cover these interdependent preferences dissolve the paradox, transform it into a different impossibility result, or merely relocate the tension between liberty and collective rationality? Please explain the precise conceptual issue: in particular, whether the Pareto condition is misapplied once preferences are no longer profile-independent, and whether the liberal principle should be restricted to first-order preferences or legitimately extends to these meta-level concerns. I want the distinction laid out clearly, with attention to what each option implies for the coherence of liberal social choice.
