I've been developing a framework-relative rehabilitation of Popperian verisimilitude. The standard Miller-Tichy result demonstrates that in any language with adequate logical closure, no non-trivial ordering can rank false theories by their proximity to the truth without violating basic conditions on verisimilitude. My proposal is that this obstacle only applies if we require theories to be evaluated within a single, fully expressive, fixed language. If instead we index verisimilitude to the specific theoretical vocabulary and inferential commitments of a research program at a particular stage—measuring progress as the accumulation of true consequences and the elimination of false ones relative to that local framework—then the formal impossibility dissolves. What would count against this position? I want decisive objections, not merely the reminder that framework-relativity risks circularity or program-specificity. Specifically: does this relocation preserve Popper's core motivation of explaining why one false theory can be closer to the truth than another, or does it change the subject entirely? Are there formal results showing that even locally indexed frameworks cannot avoid the Miller-Tichy trap under weaker closure conditions? And has anyone demonstrated that this kind of relativization collapses into a Kuhnian incommensurability thesis that Popper explicitly rejected? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and include an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—and if so, whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a theory of scientific progress.
