Chesterton's fence is usually read as an epistemic humility argument — you don't tear down the fence because you, as one person, don't understand it's purpose. So here's my question: if Chesterton's point is really just about individual ignorance, then hasn't modern distributed cognition kind of solved it? Does that mean Chesterton's fence collapses into a practical heuristic for the pre-digital age, while Burke's survives because it's about tacit, embodied social knowledge that can't be reconstructed from records? Or am I missing something about Chesterton — maybe his fence was never about information gaps at all, but about something closer to Burke's view?
