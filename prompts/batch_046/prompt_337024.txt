As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy in a sustained way, I have become absorbed in Borges's writings on time, yet I fear I am imposing scientific frameworks where they do not apply. In the essay A New Refutation of Time, Borges argues that time lacks reality: the present is an abstraction, and both past and future are without being. Meanwhile, in the story The Secret Miracle, he depicts a writer facing execution who lives through an entire year of mental life within the frozen instant before the firing squad acts. From a scientific standpoint, this resembles a claim that subjective duration can run independently of physical clock time—perhaps analogous to time dilation, except occurring in consciousness alone. But if Borges truly denies that time exists, what exactly is being suspended or expanded in the story? Does he implicitly rely on a distinction between chronological sequence and mental duration that his philosophical argument rejects? Or should the story be read as purely metaphorical, illustrating that creative consciousness is indifferent to measured length? I want to avoid treating Borges's fiction as a disguised model of temporal psychology. Therefore, my question is whether asking for consistency between the essay's metaphysical denial and the story's suspended moment is itself a misunderstanding. If so, what is the proper philosophical status of Borges's denial—ontological, phenomenological, or grammatical—and why does it not reduce to either a subjective theory of time or a version of eternalism?
