Write a short story set in a near-future polity where civic rank is assigned through the voluntary assumption of "silent burdens" — chronic, invisible physiological impairments that confer no visible prestige and are documented only in a sealed registry. The protagonist, a senior administrator being considered for the highest council, has secretly maintained one of these burdens for a decade without registration. She now learns that the promotion requires formally adopting a new, more severe burden, yet she realizes her existing hidden condition already exceeds its objective cost. Through her deliberation, explore whether the handicap principle retains its explanatory power when the cost of signaling is entirely unobservable to the audience whose deference is being sought. In particular, examine whether honest signaling collapses into mere self-certification when the handicap is private, and whether the normative weight of the burden depends on social recognition rather than on the magnitude of the cost actually borne.
