I want to push back against this, but not by simply reasserting the usual anti-realist slogans. Instead, please make the argument that the rejection of double-negation elimination in intuitionistic systems—say, in Heyting arithmetic—should be understood as a positive ontological claim about the modal structure of mathematical domains, not merely as methodological caution.

More precisely, argue that constructive negation functions as a coherence filter across branching information states rather than as a classical complement within a fixed domain of pre-existing objects. On this reading, to assert the existence of a mathematical object is to claim accessibility within an epistemic frame that may branch; negation then tracks which branches remain coherent with the available constructions, rather than marking an absence in a completed totality. This means constructivism is not a thesis about human proof limitations but about the architecture of mathematical possibility itself—objects are nodes in an accessibility relation, not inhabitants of a static Platonic realm. I want you to develop this position in detail: explain how it reinterprets the intuitionistic quantifiers over infinite domains, show why it avoids the standard objection that intuitionists are simply being overly conservative about non-constructive existence proofs, and address whether this modal-structural reading collapses back into a form of epistemic modesty or genuinely offers a distinct ontology. Keep the argument rigorous and conceptual; I am not asking for a bibliography or citations, but I do want the reasoning to engage with the actual logical machinery—how the failure of the law of excluded middle behaves in formal systems—rather than staying at the level of philosophical metaphor.
