I am delivering the retirement toast for my doctoral advisor, Dr. Elena Vasquez, at a dinner attended by about thirty people: roughly half are philosophers—several of whom have published on Sartre and existential phenomenology—and half are family members who have never read Being and Nothingness. She spent thirty years defending a particular, somewhat unfashionable reading of Sartre: that bad faith is not only the private lie we tell ourselves ("I had no choice"), but a structural temptation built into institutions, especially universities, which reward us for treating our roles—professor, chair, mentor—as if they were essences that excuse further choice. She insisted that tenure, committee service, and curricular design were not expressions of a true self but acts of radical freedom performed within severe facticity.

Write a five-minute toast—approximately 650 words—that honors her career without falling into the very essentialism she spent her life diagnosing. The speech must not say she "found her true calling," that she "was born to teach," or that she now "watches over us from somewhere." It should not treat her institutional achievements as the unfolding of an inner nature. Instead, let the philosophical architecture shape what is sayable: distinguish, implicitly or explicitly, between facticity (the department she inherited in 1992, the budget crises, the particular students who arrived with particular histories) and transcendence (the constant, unexcused project of choosing how to exceed those givens). For the philosophers in the room, include one precise, non-caricatured reference to her institutional argument—perhaps her claim that tenure itself functions as a "facticity that invites essentialism"—without turning the toast into a seminar. For the family, ground the idea in one concrete, recognizable anecdote or image that shows her refusing an institutional excuse in a human moment they can understand. The tone should be warm, not academic, but intellectually honest: she should emerge as someone who chose repeatedly under constraint, not as someone who fulfilled a destiny. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations, and do not use verbatim quotations from Sartre's texts.
