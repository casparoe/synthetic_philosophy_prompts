Notes for the essay:
- The strong Sapir-Whorf claim is that language determines thought, while the weak claim is only that it shapes habitual patterns of cognition. Contemporary linguists generally reject the strong form but accept limited weak-form evidence.
- In software engineering, programmers using statically typed languages often construct mental models of state that differ from those of programmers using dynamically typed languages. The formal syntax does not determine the algorithm, yet it seems to channel reasoning in predictable directions.
- I want to argue that this programming-language effect is structurally similar to weak linguistic relativity: formal categories prime certain cognitive pathways without fully constraining them.
- I am concerned, however, that this analogy may conflate linguistic relativity with technological determinism, and that it overlooks a key difference—programmers routinely switch languages, whereas monolingual speakers are embedded in a single linguistic framework.
