In my graduate seminar on philosophy of physics, we have been working through the legacy of Newton's bucket argument, and over coffee after Thursday's session a visiting colleague and I found ourselves sharply divided on whether the thought experiment retains any philosophical force once we move from Newtonian mechanics to the framework of general relativity. I maintain that relationism about spacetime can accommodate rotating motion without postulating absolute space, provided we accept that the metric field is dynamical and that rotational inertia is ultimately explained by the global distribution of matter in accordance with Mach's principle; my credence that substantivalism is therefore false is roughly 0.65. She counters that even in GR the bucket reveals a genuine distinction between rotating and non-rotating frames that cannot be fully reduced to relations among material bodies, especially once we take seriously the independent degrees of freedom carried by the gravitational field itself, which puts her credence that substantivalism is true at about 0.70.

Could you please state your own credence that Newton's bucket argument, properly reconstructed with modern differential geometry, succeeds in establishing spacetime as an independent substance rather than a relational structure? More importantly, decompose the reasoning: assign a rough probability to whether rotational motion genuinely requires an absolute standard of rest; to whether Mach's principle fully explains inertial effects without absolute structure; to whether the metric field in GR is best interpreted substantivally rather than as a relational property of matter distributions; and to how heavily the hole argument weighs against a naive substantival reading. Show how these combine into your final number, identify which term dominates your calculation, and tell me what kind of conceptual or empirical result—perhaps a precise interpretation of frame-dragging measurements—would most shift your credence. I ask not for a literature review but for an explicit calibration with its weak points marked.
