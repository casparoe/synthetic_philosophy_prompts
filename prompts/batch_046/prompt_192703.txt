I am drafting a proposal for a six-month philosophy research grant. The precise question is whether federated learning protocols—in which local nodes update shared parameters without transmitting raw data—constitute a genuine epistemic third option between Hayek's price-mediated decentralization and Lange's iterative market-socialist planning, or whether gradient aggregation merely reproduces the information bottlenecks each classic position was designed to solve or avoid. Existing treatments of the socialist calculation debate frame information transmission as either exchange-based or centrally aggregated with feedback loops, leaving untouched any mechanism that converges globally while preserving local opacity; this gap matters because distributed architectures of this kind are now being proposed for economic governance and resource coordination, so settling their conceptual status determines whether modern institutions have accidentally solved a problem thought intractable or have only disguised it. The approach will be strictly philosophical and conceptual rather than empirical: I will map the informational properties of gradient updates onto the distinction between explicit and tacit knowledge, test whether aggregation preserves the local particularity Hayek argued was indispensable, and determine whether rational allocation within such a system still requires a price-like comparator of the kind Lange insisted upon. A useful deliverable would be a precise taxonomy showing which planning problems federated architectures can resolve, which they inherit from the classic debate, and which institutional conditions would have to hold for them to avoid collapsing into either pure market pricing or pure central computation; the project fails if the taxonomy collapses entirely into one of the two classic positions with no residue. Funding philosophical rather than technical research is warranted because the obstacle is not engineering implementation but understanding what kind of knowledge is actually being transmitted when local data never travels.
