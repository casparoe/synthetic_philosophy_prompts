My friends Jordan and Riley were arguing over dinner about this new neural implant that restores working memory in older adults. (1) Jordan said if it just brings you back to the functional range you'd have without age-related decline, its clearly therapy — fixing dysfunction, not upgrading. The therapy/enhancement line should track whether something is broken, not whether it makes you better than average for your age. (2) Riley said thats assuming there's one objective 'broken' line, but aging isn't necessarily pathology. If we use an age-relative statistical baseline, then pushing a 70-year-old back to 30-year-old norms is enhancement, not repair. (3) Jordan pushed back that heart failure at 70 is still treated as disease even though hearts weaken with age, so statistical norms don't determine therapy; pathology does. (4) Riley brought up the implant's 'sleep consolidation' feature — it keeps processing problems overnight, something the user never had naturally — and said thats unambiguously enhancement, but its bundled with the restorative chip. Jordan said you can disable that mode, so the core device is still restorative. (5) Riley then said even the 'restoration' depends on a hidden counterfactual: if modern stress and pollution accelerated my decline, my true baseline might be my un-deprived potential, so fixing me to that level is actually restoration, not enhancement. Jordan called that baseline relativism and said without a fixed reference the whole distinction collapses into politics. They asked me to referee. Please go through moves (1)-(5) one by one: say which are clearly right, which are shaky, and where each side misses something.
