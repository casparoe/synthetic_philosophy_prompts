As I prepare the concluding section of my dissertation on the metaphysics of temporal continuity and the coherence of completed infinities, I want to include a poetic intervention rather than a prose summary. Write a Petrarchan sonnet (octave rhyming ABBAABBA, sestet CDECDE, with a clear volta at line 9) spoken in the first person by Thomson's lamp during the two-minute window in which it is toggled at intervals that halve the remaining time. In the octave, establish the accelerating rhythm—on, then off, then on—compressed toward the limit, using paired rhymes to bind opposing states (light/dark, on/off, lit/unlit) so that the form itself enacts the reversal without resolution. The speaker should describe the infinite sequence of toggles while implicitly acknowledging the physical impossibility of the supertask—the unbounded energy required, the acceleration without bound—yet insist that the conceptual puzzle remains. At the volta, shift from narration of the process to confrontation with its absence: there is no ω-th toggle, no final act in the well-ordered sequence, and therefore the standard inferential rule "the lamp is in whatever state the last toggle produced" lacks a premise. The sestet should ask whether imposing a continuity condition—that the state at the temporal limit equals the limit of the states—resolves the paradox or merely legislates an answer where the sequence itself provides none. The closing couplet must leave the tension open: if there is no final toggle, then asking whether the lamp is on or off at the two-minute mark may be asking a question that the sequence of actions does not answer, not a hidden fact we have failed to discover. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of time or metaphysics recognizes the distinction between physical impossibility and logical incompleteness, and the rhyme scheme should carry philosophical weight rather than serving as ornament.
