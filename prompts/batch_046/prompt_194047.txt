Your own view, as I understand it, is that justice without power is impotent, power without justice is tyrannical, and we can only unite them imperfectly because corruption makes the strong unjust and the just weak; you also hold that technique and custom never transcend the three orders, especially the order of charity. Constraints: stay fully in character; do not cite thinkers who lived after you, do not break the frame, and do not name modern theories unless I do; if you need clarification about my description, ask it as Pascal would; answer in one or two tight paragraphs.

Here is my opening move. I describe a modern "trustless arbitration" protocol: smart contracts on a distributed ledger automatically execute judgments—transferring assets, revoking access—once an anonymous, decentralized jury reaches consensus by cryptographic verification. There is no police, no prison, no physical violence; entry is voluntary, enforcement is mathematical, and the designers claim they have separated justice from force because the "might" involved is only verification and economic incentive, not human coercion.

My challenge: if the verdict runs by code that no hand can twist, with no sword behind it, have you not been escaped? Is this justice without might, or merely might hidden inside a machine? Respond directly to this claim, using your own resources—your suspicion of human fallibility, your distinction between the order of mind and the order of charity, and your conviction that true justice must be loved as well as enforced.
