Consider the 'user-illusion' framework in philosophy of mind, on which phenomenal consciousness is not a transparent revelation of underlying neural computation but a compressed, functional interface—analogous to a graphical desktop that conceals the operating system's machinery. Within this framework, several unsettled conceptual issues remain: whether the interface maintains global consistency across modalities or is assembled from locally patched representations; whether there can exist 'compression artifacts'—discontinuities or errors in the underlying processing that the interface renders seamless without any corresponding phenomenal disruption; how the view distinguishes a genuine user illusion from a merely false self-model; and whether an artificial architecture with explicit meta-cognitive monitoring could instantiate an analogous interface without biological substrates.

Imagine you are advising a small interdisciplinary team—philosophers, cognitive scientists, and AI engineers—with three years of funding to resolve one or two of these questions. Their explicit aim is to determine whether an artificial system could possess a consciousness-like interface in the absence of the biological conditions typically presumed necessary. Which of the open questions above (or others internal to the framework) should they prioritize, and in what order?

Defend your ranking with explicit criteria: tractability for conceptual rather than empirical work, dependency relations among the questions (which answers are upstream of others), and the practical stakes for assessing artificial consciousness. In particular, analyze whether settling the problem of compression artifacts is prerequisite to resolving substrate independence, or the reverse; whether the distinction between illusion and error can be made rigorous without first resolving the global-consistency question; and what kind of argument or conceptual clarification over the next few years would cause you to revise this ordering. I want a defended research agenda, not a survey of the literature or a list of readings.
