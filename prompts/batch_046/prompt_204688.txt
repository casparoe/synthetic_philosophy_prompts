Write a satirical internal corporate memo of approximately 600 words, composed in the deadpan bureaucratic voice of an HR director at a fictional management consultancy that has adopted classical Chinese dream skepticism—specifically the epistemic and identity problem raised in Zhuangzi's butterfly episode—as its official operating metaphysics. The memo should announce new "Ontological Status Protocols" requiring staff to file phenomenological incident reports whenever they experience ambiguity about whether they are currently awake or dreaming, and it should revise liability and evaluation policies for actions taken in potentially oneiric states: for instance, contract signatures, performance reviews, or interpersonal praise and blame. The satire must derive from extending the actual philosophical problem—whether the boundary between waking and dreaming can be secured and whether self-identity remains stable across both conditions—into the language of corporate risk management, rather than inventing a cartoonish misunderstanding of it. Maintain sustained deadpan irony: the director should never wink, never explicitly acknowledge absurdity, and never slip into compatibilist reassurance such as treating waking life as a practical default. By the conclusion, the reader should be left with a genuine philosophical tension: either the consultancy has constructed a rigorously coherent framework that renders ordinary institutional practice impossible, or it has accidentally demonstrated that literal dream skepticism dissolves the conditions for accountability, praise, and blame. Do not cite modern philosophers, do not include bibliographies or reading lists, and do not name any living person.
