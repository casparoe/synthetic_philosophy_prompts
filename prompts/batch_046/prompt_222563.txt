I am composing a scene for a philosophical novel in which a secondary character, an aging aesthetician named Dr. Voss, hosts an intimate dinner party in a city that has long forgotten its own name. Voss has spent the last decade defending a severe variant of tranquilism—the view that only the complete absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, while all positive states are at best neutral instruments. Recently, however, he has begun to worry about what he terms recursive apatheia: the possibility that true tranquility requires not merely the cessation of first-order cravings, but the dissolution of the very cognitive scaffolding that makes it intelligible to notice one’s own freedom from craving. He insists that anyone who takes pride in being tranquil has already failed, since that pride is itself a subtle second-order craving for self-recognition.

For this scene, I need a joke that Voss might tell—dry, precise, and directed at his guests—to illustrate why his position is not merely asceticism but a kind of logical extremity. The joke should turn on the actual content of tranquilism rather than being a generic joke about stoics or Buddhists: specifically, it should play on the tension between privative tranquility, which is the simple absence of suffering, and the more demanding recursive condition he now advocates, in which even the awareness of absence must be absent for the state to count. It should make conceptual sense to someone who understands that, for Voss, any mental content that registers I am now free from desire is already a contaminating presence.

Please write such a joke in Voss’s voice—formal, slightly austere, but with a surgeon’s sense of timing—so that it could plausibly occur in the dialogue without sounding like a lecture. The punchline should depend on the distinction between first-order and second-order absence, and it should leave the listener uncertain whether Voss is genuinely amused or merely demonstrating that amusement, too, would be a form of craving he has successfully eliminated.
