I serve on the Allocation Committee of the Voss Foundation, which evaluates disbursements through a modified cause-prioritization framework—importance, tractability, and neglectedness—constrained by an accuracy-first epistemic commitment that treats the reduction of structural decision-error as a first-order moral good. We must decide, before Thursday’s meeting, how to allocate the remaining $3.2 million of this fiscal year between two proposals that pull the framework in opposite directions.

Proposal A is a direct grant for antimalarial bed-net distribution in the Eastern Highlands region, where our internal models project approximately 340 disability-adjusted life years averted per $100,000. However, three large NGOs already maintain saturated distribution networks in that region, so the marginal tractability is low and the neglectedness score is near zero; the impact estimates are precise but the returns diminish sharply with our entry.

Proposal B is the Meta-Framework Initiative: a 24-month program to refine our importance-weighting algorithms and tractability-assessment protocols for low-data, long-horizon interventions, with explicit deliverables being updated decision matrices and open methodological documentation. It has no direct beneficiary cohort, but its proponents argue that improving the epistemic architecture of our prioritization process generates an indirect, diffuse, and highly leveraged form of importance—what they term epistemic tractability—that should be weighted against direct service delivery.

Our charter contains a Direct Beneficiary Proviso: any disbursement exceeding $1 million must have an identifiable, named beneficiary cohort with measurable outcomes within 18 months. Proposal B fails this criterion categorically. The philosophical tension is whether the proviso functions as an absolute deontological side-constraint on consequentialist prioritization, or whether it can be interpreted as a defeasible heuristic that yields when the accuracy-first commitment demonstrates sufficient expected epistemic utility.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—full funding to one proposal, a split allocation with conditions, or a conditional deferral with a specific time frame—and provide the reasoning. The memo must treat the strongest argument for the losing side with genuine seriousness: if you recommend B, address why the Direct Beneficiary Proviso is not an absolute bar; if you recommend A, address why treating epistemic tractability as a secondary good does not undermine our accuracy-first commitment. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the recommendation should stand on the internal logic of the framework and the case.
