I've been trying to understand what makes a life actually good for the person living it, and I keep returning to that mental exercise where you imagine being hooked up to a machine that feeds you perfect, lifelike experiences—complete relationships, achievements, everything—while your body lies in a tank. Most people I've talked to say they wouldn't do it, and that reaction is usually treated as proof that well-being isn't just about feeling good. But I've noticed that the conversation never actually moves past that initial refusal. People who think happiness is just pleasure say you'd be foolish to reject it, and people who reject the machine say it wouldn't be "real," but they never agree on what "real" is doing in the argument, and after a while everyone just restates their intuition and stops.

In my own attempts to sort this out, I've started making a distinction that I haven't seen named in any of the popular articles I've read: there's a difference between knowing that an experience has an external cause, and being able to trace the outcome back through a chain of your own actual decisions and efforts over time. I think of this second thing as something like "path-traceability"—not exactly authenticity, but the specific structure of seeing your present state as the result of a sequence you authored. When I bring this up with friends, the debate still stalls. One side treats path-traceability as a quirky psychological preference for transparency, the other treats it as essential to well-being itself, and neither side explains whether it is part of what makes life good or just a separate need that gets confused with well-being.

So I'm trying to figure out whether this impasse is real or merely verbal. Is the experience-machine disagreement actually about whether well-being is built out of moment-to-moment states or out of the narrative arc of a whole life? Or are the two sides answering different questions—one asking "would this feel good?" and the other asking "would this be mine?"—while pretending they're debating the same concept of well-being? And if path-traceability really is part of well-being, could we test that by imagining a modified machine that lets you review a perfectly accurate, simulated history of your "choices" inside the program? Would that satisfy the anti-machine intuition, or does it show that the intuition was never about traceability at all? I want a diagnosis of what's actually blocking progress here—whether it's a hidden factual disagreement, a clash of standards, bedrock intuitions about naturalness, or something else—not a survey of well-being theories, and an honest view of whether anything, such as a conceptual split or a redesigned thought experiment, could actually break the deadlock.
