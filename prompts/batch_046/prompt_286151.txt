I've noticed this pattern in climate and heritage debates, and I dont know if philosophers have named it. You have two goods that genuinely cant be traded off on a single scale - say, keeping an old community's way of life intact versus moving people to safer ground. Instead of admitting the hard choice, people say the culture sets a hard boundary, and then we maximize safety within it. But the boundary is calibrated exactly to let them do the relocation they wanted, with just enough preservation to feel okay. So the incommensurability gets hidden behind a fake constraint. I started calling it threshold laundering in my own notes, but is there an actual concept for this? I know about value pluralism and framing effects, but I'm asking about the specific move: turning a rival value into a pseudo-constraint with a conveniently placed cutoff. Is that just rationalization, or is it a recognized failure mode in how people handle hard choices? And where's the line between genuinely treating something as non-negotiable and using that language to cover up that you've already picked sides?
