Write an essay on whether rural/working-class values (practical repair, local loyalty, suspicion of credentialism) or urban/professional-class values (systems thinking, cosmopolitan openness, meritocratic sorting) have the stronger claim to being defensible — not just economically, but morally and epistemically. Don't just rehearse culture-war talkin points. Take seriously the niche framing that "repair epistemology" (the knowing that comes from fixing broken physical things under hard constraints) produces a different kind of cognitive humility than "systems epistemology" (managing abstract networks). Ask: does either form of humility ground a more reliable social outlook, and can either side dismiss the other without falling into self-undermining arrogance? Weigh both sides properly, then reach a actual conclusion and justify it — if you think one outlook wins, say which; if you think they're genuinely incommensurable, defend that verdict with specific reasons, not just "both sides have a point."
