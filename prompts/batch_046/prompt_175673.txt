Write a Petrarchan sonnet in iambic pentameter addressing Quine’s “Two Dogmas of Empiricism” (1951). In the octave (rhyme scheme ABBAABBA), present the classic analytic-synthetic distinction as logical empiricism conceived it: use Quine’s own examples—“bachelor” and “unmarried man,” “creature with a heart” and “creature with kidneys”—to show how analyticity was supposed to rest on synonymy alone, with rhyme pairs such as “definition”/“condition” and “meaning”/“seeming” reinforcing the illusion that some truths are held fixed purely by internal relations of ideas. At the volta in line 9, turn to Quine’s actual arguments: first, that synonymy cannot be grounded in definitional reduction without circularity, nor in behavioral disposition without smuggling in empirical assumptions; second, that statements face experience not individually but as a “corporate body” within the web of belief, so that even logical laws are in principle revisable. In the sestet (CDECDE), extend this collapse to the problem of radical translation: suggest that if there is no fact of the matter about synonymy within a language, then translating an allegedly analytic claim across a radically different linguistic framework loses any privileged, meaning-preserving status—the boundary dissolves not only inside one language but between languages. The sestet’s rhyme scheme should pair terms separated in the octave—for instance, “meaning” rhyming with “revising” and “truth” with “uncouth” or “proof” with “disproof”—to mirror the collapse of the analytic boundary. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with Quine recognizes the specific failures of definitional and behavioral reduction, the holism of confirmation, and the in-principle revisability of logic; a reader unfamiliar should still find a coherent philosophical argument in verse.

After the poem, provide a brief critical note identifying where the poem takes liberties with Quine’s text or relies on contested interpretations. Specifically address (a) whether Quine’s confirmation holism genuinely licenses the live revisability of logical laws, as opposed to treating such revision merely as a reductio of reductionism; and (b) whether linking this failure directly to the indeterminacy of radical translation—drawn from Quine’s later Word and Object—goes beyond what “Two Dogmas” strictly establishes. State explicitly how confident you are in these interpretive judgments, and single out the one point about which you are least sure.
