My colleague runs a rehabilitation programme that uses a predictive neural interface. The device detects pre-conscious patterns linked to harmful impulses and automatically initiates an inhibitory response; the patient's conscious resolution to resist always arrives a fraction later, yet the clinicians have noticed that patients who report a stronger conscious commitment display measurably different inhibition dynamics from those who do not. My colleague asks whether, in cases where the device's physical mechanism fully determines the behavioural outcome, the patient's conscious commitment still performs any causal work, or whether praising it is like praising a thermometer for the heat. She does not want clinical guidance; she wants to know if her intuition that both the mechanism and the commitment genuinely matter can be rendered consistent, or whether one must be an illusion. Does this situation capture a real philosophical problem, and how ought she to think about the tension?
