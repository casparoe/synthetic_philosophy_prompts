Analyze the 1997 Deep Blue vs Kasparov match through Dennett's real-patterns framework — physical stance, design stance, intentional stance — but I dont want a neat taxonomy. Focus on the moments where Kasparov clearly knew he was facing IBM hardware running evaluation functions and search trees, yet still talked about it as if it had a 'style', felt pressure, or made 'creative' moves. Is that just sloppy anthropomorphism, or does it show that the design stance cant stay clean when a system's outputs are strategically opaque? Introduce the idea of 'stance bleed' — that mentalistic vocabulary leaks upward not because we're ignorant of the design, but because the pattern of behavior genuinely supports it at the predictive level. Then flip it: ask whether IBM's engineers, explaining clock speeds and heat dissipation, ever slipped into treating Deep Blue as if it had goals. Where does the three-stance model explain the match well, and where does it miss that these stances arent just epistemic choices but social performances — Kasparov performing for the media, IBM performing reliability?
