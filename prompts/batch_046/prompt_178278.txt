I'm designing a classification instrument for my upper-division history of political thought seminar on Adam Smith's critique of empire and colonies. Rather than asking students to name scholars, I want a decision tree that sorts commitments by answers to concrete cases. Use this framing: the first split distinguishes whether Smith's primary objection is mercantile extraction (the monopoly misallocates capital) or a sovereignty deficit (the absence of local legislative accountability corrupts colony and metropole alike)—a distinction my course is emphasizing but standard textbooks rarely use as the root axis. The second split asks whether this critique applies symmetrically to the American colonies and to the East India Company's territories. The third asks whether Smith's proposal for an imperial federation is a genuine institutional alternative or a rhetorical concession meant to preserve trade privileges. Construct the tree with four to six leaves, each labeled by a recognized interpretive stance—such as economic anti-imperialist, republican liberty-critic, pragmatic mercantilist, or federalist revisionist—or marked unnamed if the combination lacks defenders. Every leaf should carry one sentence stating its most awkward commitment (for instance, that it must treat Smith's defense of the Navigation Acts as purely strategic irony). Flag any positions the tree cannot separate, and briefly diagnose whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or request for one; the instrument should be fully usable by someone who has read Wealth of Nations, Book IV, ch. 7, and related lecture material but has no external access.
