You are the evaluation editor for an open-access encyclopedia that accepts submissions on contested pasts. You review five to ten proposals monthly, with roughly thirty minutes and no research staff per case. You need a reusable decision procedure to determine whether a submission that claims to correct "suppressed" or hidden history is legitimate revisionist historiography or pseudohistory, specifically without falling back on "it contradicts standard accounts," which is circular for your role.

Turn the following conceptual distinction into an ordered checklist: the difference between treating archival absence as a problem that demands independent corroboration (historiography) and treating archival absence as self-confirming proof of suppression (evidentiary inversion), paired with a check for methodological asymmetry—whether the author applies stricter evidential standards to orthodox sources than to their own claim. For each step, state the exact question, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with rejection, where it stops with acceptance, and where it must hand the decision to a human reviewer because the issue is institutional values rather than formal structure. The procedure must be runnable in thirty minutes by a non-specialist.

Then apply it to three submissions: (i) a proposal that prehistoric megalithic monuments were built by unknown non-human agents, citing the absence of worker camps and metal tools in burial contexts as primary proof; (ii) a community-archive proposal documenting a neighborhood destroyed in mid-twentieth-century urban renewal, using census omissions—documented to result from boycott and redlining—together with property-tax gaps and oral testimony to establish presence; (iii) a proposal compressing the chronology of a well-documented ancient civilization by two centuries, arguing that gaps in radiocarbon calibration and missing stratigraphic layers at key sites prove orthodox timelines were inflated by later copyists.

For each case, show exactly where the procedure lands. Include at least one case where the procedure produces an answer you, as an editor committed to amplifying marginalized archives, will not like, and explain what the procedure cannot settle—specifically, whether a legitimate revisionist claim that happens to rely heavily on negative evidence should be excluded on formal grounds alone. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
