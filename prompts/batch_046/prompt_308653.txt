ok so i've been reading a bunch about how hunter-gatherers might of had it way better than the first farmers — like they worked less, ate more varied food, and didn't have bosses or rotting teeth. people sometimes ask if the agricultural revolution was actually a mistake for the people living through it. but whenever i try to talk about this, the same pattern happens and it's driving me nuts.

someone lists all the downsides: shorter bodies, more disease, hard labor, and eventually kings and inequality. then instead of saying "no, farming was actually healthier" — which they can't, cause the bones prove it — they switch to a totally different reason it's okay. sometimes they say farming allowed way more people to exist, so it's a win on total numbers. sometimes they say without farming we wouldn't have art or medicine, so complaining is ungrateful. and sometimes they say well people chose it, even though most people born into farming never had a choice.

what bugs me is that these three moves never actually defend the quality of life for the indivduals who did the farming. they just change the scoreboard: population size, future benefits for strangers, or fake consent. it's like a defense of a historical harm that works by pointing to benefits that don't recieve the people who actually lived it. is there an established name for this exact trio? i know about the non-identity problem, and i know people talk about moving the goalposts, but this feels more specific. if there's no term, can you name it and draw clear lines around it — like, how is it different from plain whataboutism or from just saying "the ends justify the means"? i want to be able to call it out when i see it.
