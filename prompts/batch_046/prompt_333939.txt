Construct a thought experiment that tests whether autonomy conditions constrain the extended mind thesis independently of functional parity. Design a single-paragraph, realistic scenario—no science fiction, no exotic technology—featuring an adult who relies on an external digital storage and retrieval system for routine practical reasoning, where the system's architecture and contents are functionally equivalent to biological long-term memory. Crucially, the system is not exclusively controlled by the user: a third party (for example, an employer or platform provider) retains remote administrative rights to modify, restrict, or revoke access to the stored information based on contractual terms or observed behavior, and the user knows this but continues to depend on the system because no equivalent internal or personally controlled alternative exists. Present the scenario, then do the following: first, explain what the parity principle implies about whether the stored information counts as part of the user's cognitive or belief system; second, explain why the presence of an external gatekeeper with unilateral control might generate an intuitive resistance to extending cognition despite that functional equivalence; third, identify precisely which feature—functional integration, causal coupling, or autonomous ownership—does the explanatory work in producing the tension; fourth, state whether stripping away the third-party control (giving the user exclusive ownership and administration) collapses the case into a standard extended-cognition example and why; and finally, outline how a defender of the extended mind thesis might respond to the autonomy-based objection without abandoning functional parity. Please respond in American English, avoid citing specific authors or texts, and do not include bibliographies or reading lists.
