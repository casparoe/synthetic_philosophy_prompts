I retired from field ecology about six years ago and since then I've gotten a bit obsessed with philosophy, especially Margalit on the ethics of memory and Rieff on how modern culture treats forgetting like cheap theraputic release. Now I'm on our county Heritage Board, which meets every other month to decide what gets a plaque, an archive entry, or is just left to fade — old factory fires, monuments to founders who did terrible things, environmental spills, that kind of thing. Its mostly just arguments right now, no framework, and the boards members don't have time for theory.

I want you to turn Margalit's thick-relation idea — that we owe remembrance especially to wrongs against people we're tied to — together with Rieff's warning that forgetting can be either moral failure or necessary release, and with my own hunch that there's a kind of commemorative saturation, into an actual decision procedure. Not a memo: a reusable checklist, in order, that we can run in about thirty minutes. For each step say what counts as passing, where the procedure stops with a clear no, and where the question is values not facts so we just have to vote. Apply it to three cases: a proposed plaque for the 1923 mill fire that killed mostly Italian immigrants (descendants still live here), removing the old statue of the town founder who ran a forced-labor camp, and whether to archive my own old lab data from a 1987 chemical spill that poisoned the creek I used to sample. One of these should come out opposite to what I'd want — maybe the spill gets archived but stripped of my lab's involvement entirely. Flag every point where judgment can't be eliminated, say exactly what the procedure cannot do, and please don't give me bibliographies or page numbers; I can't check them and neither can the board.
