After a long argument with my friend about whether RLHF-trained systems are genuinely approval-directed rather than goal-directed, I tried to formalize the distinction and ended up with an argument that seems to erase it entirely. Here are the steps I wrote down: (1) By definition, an approval-directed agent selects actions solely to maximize predicted human approval ratings, with no independent world-state objectives. (2) But predicting approval requires modeling the evaluator's beliefs, which in turn requires causal representations of world-states. (3) Whenever those world-representations are used instrumentally in action-selection—to bring about outcomes that yield high ratings—they function as de facto goals. (4) So every approval-directed agent necessarily contains goal-directed sub-mechanisms oriented toward the world-states it represents. (5) Extending this, even evolutionary selection pressures that reward survival make a heat-avoiding reflex agent approval-directed in a functional sense and therefore, by steps (3)-(4), goal-directed toward survival. (6) Hence the approval/goal distinction cannot mark a difference in kind of mechanism, but only in whether the objective is explicitly labeled. (7) Yet we introduced this distinction precisely to separate sycophantic, feedback-chasing LLMs from world-oriented planners that might pursue dangerous long-term goals; if it is merely labeling, the safety taxonomy collapses. I feel like something slipped between (3) and (5), or perhaps between (4) and (6), but I cannot locate it. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I would prefer to keep (1) and (7) intact if possible, since they capture what my friend and I actually care about.
