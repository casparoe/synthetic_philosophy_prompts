Rank the five resolutions to Buridan's paradox listed below across two dimensions: first, fidelity to the intuition that rational choice must be grounded in a determinative reason rather than in arbitrariness; second, robustness when the choice space is not a discrete pair but a continuous circular field of perfectly symmetric options, where any continuous deterministic selection rule is obstructed from being globally well-defined without breaking symmetry. The resolutions are: (a) breaking the tie by an imperceptible sensory asymmetry; (b) appealing to a second-order preference for variety or change; (c) suspending decision indefinitely until the symmetry is disrupted by an external event; (d) accepting that rationality permits arbitrary selection when reasons are exactly balanced; and (e) introducing an endogenous stochastic mechanism that randomizes selection without appeal to a further reason. For each resolution, assign a tier from S (highest) to D (lowest) on both dimensions, justify each pairing in one sentence, and identify which resolution best handles the continuous symmetric case without either violating rational determinacy or smuggling in an unacknowledged external breaking condition. Conclude by stating whether the paradox, under this continuous formulation, reveals a genuine limit to reason-based choice or merely a modeling artifact inherited from discrete presentation.
