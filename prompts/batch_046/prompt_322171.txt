For a philosophy-of-mind seminar I am drafting my profile on consciousness and want a consistency audit before presenting. Here is my position set:

(1) I accept Block's distinction between access consciousness (A-consciousness) and phenomenal consciousness (P-consciousness), holding that they come apart in both directions: there can be phenomenal experience without cognitive access, and global access without phenomenal feel.
(2) I think global workspace theory correctly explains consciousness mechanistically, and specifically explains phenomenal consciousness—not just access—through sustained information broadcasting with recurrent processing loops.
(3) I take phenomenal overflow to be a real phenomenon: in brief visual displays, subjects phenomenally experience more items than they can report or working-memory can hold, which shows P-consciousness exceeds access capacity.
(4) I think the hard problem is dissolved, not solved, once we have a mechanistic account of how global broadcasting with recurrence generates the relevant architecture.
(5) I think philosophical zombies are conceivable but metaphysically impossible, because any physical system that implements the correct global-broadcasting architecture necessarily has phenomenal experience.
(6) I hold that a system achieving global information sharing through purely feedforward parallel streams—without recurrence—lacks phenomenal consciousness entirely, even if its behavioral and reportable capacities match a conscious agent's.

Please audit whether this hangs together. Structure your response in the following sections, with approximate lengths:

1. Bottom line (about 80 words): is the package jointly tenable or not?
2. Severe tensions ranked (about 300 words): list the genuinely incompatible pairs in order of severity. For each, identify the two commitments, explain the conflict, and state the cheapest revision with its cost.
3. Apparent tensions that dissolve (about 120 words): identify pairs that look incompatible but are not, explaining why the conflict is illusory.
4. Single best revision (about 80 words): which one change cleans up the most problems at the least philosophical cost?

Be specific about which commitments pull against each other, do not treat all conflicts as equally bad, and avoid lecturing me on the basic literature.
