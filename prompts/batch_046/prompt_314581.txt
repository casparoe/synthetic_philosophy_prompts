I've been trying to work out whether Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis actually demonstrates that culture can cause economic change, or whether it unintentionally proves the opposite. His argument turns on an elective affinity between the Protestant ethic and capitalist rationality, yet he also insists that religious values were never a sufficient cause—capitalism existed elsewhere, and material conditions had to align too. This leads me to think there is a hidden circularity: if we identify the cultural cause only in cases where capitalism actually followed, then the value is not doing independent explanatory work; we are merely labeling a correlation after the fact. So doesn't Weber's framework imply that culture can never be a genuine cause of history, but only a parallel vocabulary we use to describe outcomes that were really driven by material structures? And if that is the case, why do social scientists still treat cultural values as explanatory factors rather than admitting they are always retrospective interpretations?
