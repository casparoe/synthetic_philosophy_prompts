Write a proposal for a one-semester philosophy-of-biology term paper (around 5,000 words) on whether DNA should be understood as a code in any philosophically substantial sense. The half-formed idea: the stalemate between literalist and metaphorical readings persists because both sides conflate two conditions usually packed together in ordinary codes—mapping-arbitrariness (the symbol-to-meaning relation is not causally fixed) and token-arbitrariness (the physical form of the symbol is functionally independent). DNA clearly fails token-arbitrariness, since nucleotides are chemically specific, but may satisfy mapping-arbitrariness in a restricted sense. Rather than forcing a yes/no verdict, the project tests whether there is a stable intermediate category—causally constrained symbolic mediation—by comparing DNA with bacterial quorum-sensing pathways and with digital error-correcting codes. The precise question: if we separate these two arbitrariness conditions and apply them across the three cases, does DNA fall into a distinct informational kind that requires its own concept of biological information, or does it collapse back into one of the familiar categories? In the proposal, state this question precisely, explain why the existing literalist/metaphorist debate leaves it open, describe the comparative conceptual method and what each case is meant to show, specify what counts as success (e.g., a taxonomy with determinate membership conditions) versus failure (e.g., the distinctions dissolve under analysis), and give an honest assessment of whether this is tractable in one semester without empirical lab work. Do not include a bibliography or citation list; references will be added separately.
