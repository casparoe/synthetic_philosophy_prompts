I am a retired physicist who spent three decades studying nonlinear dynamical systems before turning, somewhat unexpectedly, to philosophy of mind and cognitive science. Lately I have been trying to understand the frame problem—not as an engineering puzzle for robotics, but as a deeper question about how any intelligent agent, human or artificial, represents the stability of the world around an intervention—without simply repeating the textbook examples I have already encountered a hundred times. I would like you to write a short story, composed entirely in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, that explores this idea through a character I have been imagining: Dr. Miriam Solano, a former oceanographer who now curates a small, fictional museum called the Archive of Latent States. The museum operates on an invented curatorial premise, one that you should treat as literally true within the narrative: every exhibited object exists in a state of suspended potential alteration, and only a direct physical intervention by a curator collapses it into one changed form, leaving all other properties in an explicit rather than implicit condition of persistence.

Your story should trace Miriam’s internal monologue as she decides to move a single ceramic vessel from one pedestal to another. Because she applies a rigorous, almost obsessive logical framework inspired by her scientific background, she refuses to take any non-change for granted; instead, she actively constructs an exhaustive catalog of invariant conditions—the molecular lattice of the marble floor, the acoustic resonance of the empty corridor, the unexpressed intentions of absent visitors, the gravitational constant acting on the pedestal she leaves behind—treating each omission of change as an active cognitive achievement rather than a background assumption. I want the narrative to feel like a philosophical meditation disguised as a domestic task, with Miriam’s voice gradually revealing that her inability to apply a default “frame axiom” has become both a method and a kind of psychological burden. Please allow her scientific training to emerge in references to boundary conditions, invariant sets, and conservation principles, while her newer philosophical inclination leads her to wonder whether the very distinction between change and stasis is an artifact of representational convenience rather than a feature of the world itself. The piece should run approximately seven hundred to nine hundred words, maintain a quiet and precise tone throughout, and should not include citations, bibliographies, or direct references to named philosophers or specific texts; let the conceptual tension arise organically from her situation and her voice.
