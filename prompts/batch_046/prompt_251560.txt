For a seminar on the epistemology of ancient narrative and archaeological interpretation, I am trying to specify the desiderata that any adequate theory of the legend-to-history transition must satisfy. The prompt is specifically oriented toward the boundary cases that standard surveys treat too quickly: the Trojan War, where textual and material traces remain in tension; the Exodus, where narrative scale and archaeological silence interact in particular ways; and King Arthur, where the shift from moral exemplar to purported biographical subject is especially pronounced.

Rather than asking for a literature review or a scorecard of existing positions, I want a rigorous specification of what the theory must be able to do, with the motivating case behind each requirement, and a clear distinction between non-negotiable constraints and the preferences of particular methodological camps.

The desiderata should cover at least the following dimensions, which I take to be central but not exhaustive:

1. Narrative fossilization: the theory must account for how oral traditions preserve structural motifs while systematically distorting chronology, scale, and agency, without treating all distortion as noise to be corrected rather than as data about transmission dynamics.

2. Material anchor decay: the theory must explain how archaeological evidence can either fail to corroborate a legend or actively destabilize specific elements of it without thereby confirming an alternative historical kernel, and it must specify what kind of evidentiary silence is compatible with a minimal historical core versus what silence undermines the core altogether.

3. Exemplary drift: the theory must handle the process by which a figure initially functions as a moral or political exemplar within a legend and only later acquires purported biographical specificity that makes them a candidate for historical reconstruction. It must distinguish this drift from simple fictionalization and specify the conditions under which exemplarity blocks or enables historical attribution.

4. Inter-source coherence: the theory must specify what level of consistency is required across textual, material, and comparative-mythological sources, and whether demanding full coherence across all three creates an impossibility result given well-established transmission dynamics.

For each desideratum, please provide: the phenomenon it must cover, the specific failure mode that results if it is omitted, whether it is non-negotiable or camp-specific (for instance, whether requiring direct textual continuity is a genuine constraint or merely a philological preference), and whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — for example, whether insisting both on strict material corroboration for every narrative element and on the preservation of a minimal historical kernel despite widespread archaeological silence yields a contradiction analogous to an underdetermination problem.

Finally, please indicate which single desideratum does the most exclusionary work — that is, which one, if enforced strictly, eliminates the largest class of candidate theories — and defend that judgment by reference to the other criteria rather than by appeal to disciplinary authority. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation of specific scholars; I am assessing the conceptual architecture, not the historiography.
