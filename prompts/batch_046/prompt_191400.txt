I work for a council that gets about three sister-city proposals a year, usually from towns in countries with dodgy human rights records or shaky democratic institutions. I need a decision procedue I can actually run in an hour or two, based on Kant's idea of perpetual peace — the preliminary articles and the definitive ones — but translated for municipal partnerships rather than states. The checklist should run in order: first check for hidden reservations or secret aims (like propaganda use), then check whether the partner has something like republican self-government at local level, then check whether the agreement creates a kind of federation or multilateral link rather than a one-off deal, and finally check cosmopolitan conditions like whether people can move or speak freely across the link. At each step say what counts as passing, what counts as failing, where I stop with a hard no, and where I have to hand it to the ethics committee because it's a values judgement dressed up as procedure. Make sure it handles the hard case where elections exist but are rubber-stamp, and where the partner city is inside a state that doesn't guarantee local autonomy. Then apply it to three proposals: one where the partner secretly wants propaganda cover (hidden reservation), one where the town is genuinely democratic but sits inside a non-republican state, and one where the exchange looks perfect but we'd have to share resident data that could expose dissidents — that's the one where the answer should be no even though I'd like it to be yes. Finally say what the procedue can't do, especially judging republican character from a distance without visiting. Write the answer in British English, please. A typo or two is totally fine.
