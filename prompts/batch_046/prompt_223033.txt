I am preparing the interview schedule for a philosophy-of-mind podcast programme. Next week's guest is an unnamed philosopher who has recently defended what she calls the staggered proscenium model of conscious experience — a deliberately modified Cartesian-theatre view designed to survive Dennett's multiple-drafts critique. On her account, there is no single central stage; instead, consciousness involves a cascade of nested temporal integration windows (roughly 100 ms, 300 ms, and 2–3 s), each functioning as a local proscenium with its own higher-order representational audience. The audiences are not unified by a single observer but are linked through asymmetric predictive updating, so that later windows can revise the content of earlier ones without generating the illusion of a single timeline being edited retrospectively in the manner Dennett describes.

I need eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. Specifically:

- At least one question should press on how her model distinguishes itself from global workspace theories that also invoke broadcasting across temporal scales; include a follow-up anticipating her likely claim that her view retains a genuinely phenomenal presence that broadcasting lacks.
- At least one question should target the binding problem her cascade creates: if each proscenium has its own audience, what mechanism ensures that I am the subject of all three rather than three loosely coupled sub-agents? The follow-up should be keyed to her probable answer that predictive updating creates a narrative self-model, and it should ask what empirical finding would force her to abandon that answer.
- At least one question should address Dennett's charge that any theatre model, however distributed, still smuggles in a point where everything converges. Frame this in terms of her specific architecture rather than generically.
- At least one question should explore the phenomenology of temporal experience: if the 100 ms window is revised by the 300 ms window, and that in turn by the 2–3 s window, does she deny that we ever experience the raw 100 ms content, or does she claim we experience it precisely as unrevised? Include a follow-up that pushes on whether this collapses her view into a version of Dennett's drafts after all.
- The remaining questions should probe other pressure points in the literature — for instance, whether her model can accommodate cases of dissociation or split-brain phenomenology without multiplying subjects beyond parsimony, and whether the predictive-updating mechanism she relies on is vulnerable to the same no-central-meaner objection she levels at Dennett.

Please write the questions in a polished, formal register suitable for a podcast audience with some philosophical literacy, and for each include a brief note (one or two sentences) on what answer you are anticipating and why the follow-up targets that specific reply. Do not include generic biographical or career questions, and do not ask for reading lists, citations, or bibliographic recommendations.
