Please argue that the discursive dilemma in judgment aggregation is best addressed through a two-tier deliberative structure rather than by selecting either a premise-based or conclusion-based aggregation rule. In this framework, the group first collectively assesses the logical dependencies that generate the dilemma—treating coherence conditions as internal objects of democratic choice—before aggregating judgments on the substantive premises and conclusions. Defend the claim that this “meta-deliberative coherence” approach allows a group to reject a majority-supported premise legitimately, provided the group has collectively endorsed the inference rule linking that premise to contradictory judgments. Address the objection that this merely relocates the dilemma to the meta-level, and explain why your approach better reconciles democratic equality with rational consistency than the standard procedural alternatives.
