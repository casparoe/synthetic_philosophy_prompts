Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing interpretive camps regarding John von Neumann's work on self-replicating automata and the claim that technology can outlast or supersede its biological originators. Name the camps the "Autonomous Lineage" camp and the "Embedded Formalism" camp. The Autonomous Lineage camp holds that von Neumann's kinematic model and universal-constructor architecture genuinely anticipate an evolutionary trajectory in which machine reproduction becomes independent of human design, implying that technological systems possess a form of phylogenetic persistence capable of surviving human extinction; the Embedded Formalism camp holds that von Neumann's automata were exclusively mathematical analogues to biological self-reproduction, developed within cybernetic and engineering contexts that carry no predictive or normative weight about post-human survival, and that attributing "surviving technology" to him projects later singularity discourse onto formal machinery.

Your document should contain:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims—covering the formal structure of the universal constructor; the distinction between instruction-based and material-based replication in the model; the unfinished status of von Neumann's manuscript; and what is and is not present in the late-1940s lecture material versus the posthumous compilation of his notes—phrased so that both camps would sign them without reservation.

2. A disagreements table stating, in wording each side accepts for its own position, the precise points of divergence: whether the capacity for open-ended evolutionary modification (not merely deterministic copying) is implicit in the architecture; whether von Neumann's comparison of automata to living cells was intended as a claim about technological destiny or only as a heuristic for biology; and whether "survival" in this context requires functional autonomy from human maintenance or merely informational persistence across generations of machines.

3. The cruxes—the sub-questions such that resolving them would cause at least one camp to shift its view. At least one crux should involve the concept of "architectural inheritance" (whether structural mutations in a von Neumann machine can accumulate without a centralized redesign protocol, and whether von Neumann treated this as an open problem or as a closed boundary condition).

4. For each crux, identify a concrete conceptual case, formal feature, or interpretive point that both camps agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp predicts about it. For example, both might agree that von Neumann's remarks on error-correction in self-replication are relevant; record whether the Autonomous Lineage camp predicts these remarks imply evolutionary robustness, and whether the Embedded Formalism camp predicts they imply only engineered reliability within a fixed design envelope.

Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not use verbatim quotations from real texts unless you can verify them by fetching the source; otherwise paraphrase without quotation marks. The prompt should be answerable without web access, so avoid relying on obscure secondary literature or highly specific archival discoveries. Keep the disagreement substantive rather than verbal, but if part of the dispute dissolves upon clarification, identify which part and explain why.
