I've been watching how committees pick grant proposals using a satisficng rule — there's a checklist and the first one that ticks every box gets funded. But there's this weird second move: someone digs up a proposal thats nearly identical but misses one box in a flashy way, and then the group spends ten minutes arguing whether that box really mattered. Once they decide it was overrated, they dont fund the near-miss; they fund the original one but now with the lowered bar. Same thing in hiring — interview until someone clears the bar, then pull up a near-miss who bombed the culture-fit round, and suddenly that criterion gets demoted. Its not just post-hoc rationalization because the near-miss is actively hunted, and its not ordinary aspiration adjustment because the bar moves downward specifically to match the option they already liked. Is there an established term for this kind of "shadow comparison" in bounded-rationality literature? If not, can you coin one and say exactly how it differs from plain satisficing and from motivated reasoning — and where its edges are, so I dont confuse it with plain groupthink?
