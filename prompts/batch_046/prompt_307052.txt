After three decades in condensed-matter physics, I have recently begun sitting in on philosophy-of-mind seminars, and I find the analogy between conscious experience and a computer interface more treacherous than its advocates admit. In particular, I have been thinking about the view—shaped by predictive-processing accounts and Hoffman's interface theory of perception—that consciousness is a user illusion, a compressed Bayesian interface that guides action without transparently revealing neural machinery. Last week I sketched an argument that now strikes me as suspiciously self-defeating: (1) If consciousness is a user illusion, then subjective states are interface representations with no direct structural correspondence to the underlying neural properties they represent, just as a desktop icon does not structurally mirror the file it denotes. (2) A representation lacking structural correspondence to its substrate is assessed by predictive utility alone, not by truth-conditions about that substrate. (3) So a report such as I am in pain refers only to the interface prediction, not to any specific neural event, and may therefore be sincerely false. (4) If such reports can be false, they are causally disconnected from the neural mechanisms that actually produce behavior, because the interface state plays no mechanistic role in the causal chain. (5) Yet psychophysics and clinical neurology depend on treating first-person reports as causally connected to neural measurements; otherwise they have no evidential basis. (6) Those disciplines plainly do have an evidential basis. (7) Therefore consciousness cannot be a mere user illusion, and phenomenal properties must stand in direct correspondence to neural properties—collapsing the interface view into the direct realism it was designed to avoid. I suspect the slip occurs between (3) and (4), perhaps an illicit move from representational independence to causal impotence, but I am unsure of the exact diagnosis. If forced to choose, I would rather preserve (1) than (4), but I want to know precisely what that commitment costs. Please respond with exactly five numbered points, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, identifying the flawed step, explaining why it appeared sound, and pricing the alternatives for the user-illusion theory.
