Write an essay weighing Niall Ferguson's broadly favourable balance-sheet assessment of the British Raj against Shashi Tharoor's indictment of imperial extraction and lasting institutional damage. Reach and justify a clear conclusion about whether the ledger method is conceptually appropriate for evaluating colonial rule, rather than ending on an agnostic note. In constructing your argument, move beyond the familiar inventory of railways, legal codes, and famine administration to examine a more fundamental tension: does treating sovereignty and self-determination as quantifiable line items commit a category error? Specifically, analyse the problem of intangible debits—the suppression of indigenous institutional experimentation and the path-dependent governance structures that outlasted the Raj—alongside the counterfactual difficulty of estimating what an independent India might have achieved. You should also consider whether Ferguson's comparative benchmarking against other empires obscures a more relevant comparison with plausible non-colonial trajectories for the subcontinent. The essay should stand on its conceptual and argumentative merits alone; do not supply a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
