As a doctoral student in philosophy of psychiatry, I am trying to operationalize Ian Hacking’s looping-effect framework for clinical review boards that evaluate proposed revisions to psychiatric nosology, but I want to extend it to cover what I am calling “instrumental reflexivity”: cases in which the diagnostic instruments themselves—structured interviews, rating scales, or factor-analytic protocols—are redesigned in response to behavioral shifts in the classified population, thereby obscuring whether the taxonomy is tracking a stable phenomenon or generating and then measuring its own artifact. I need a reusable procedural checklist that a non-philosophist board member could run in a single ninety-minute session. The procedure should proceed in ordered decision nodes: first, establish the evidentiary baseline required to claim that a proposed category tracks a pre-classificatory clinical reality rather than producing one; second, specify comparative tests that distinguish genuine therapeutic or epidemiological adaptation from taxonomic contamination; third, define the threshold at which instrument revision crosses from legitimate calibration into reflexive reconstruction that masks drift; and fourth, identify the precise point at which the board must suspend the proposed revision pending independent phenomenological validation, explicitly marking that suspension as a value-laden judgment rather than a technical failure. For each node, state what counts as a sufficient answer, what counts as an ambiguous result, and where the procedure must hand off to human deliberation. Then apply the full procedure to two hypothetical proposals: (a) the inclusion of “sustained digital-withdrawal depressive subtype” in a major diagnostic manual revision, where patient self-report profiles have measurably shifted after the provisional criteria were circulated in clinician-training modules; and (b) the bifurcation of “generalized anxiety disorder” into “anticipatory-cognitive” and “somatic-proprioceptive” subtypes based on factor-analytic studies that employed questionnaires revised after preliminary patient feedback altered response patterns. In each application, indicate where the procedure delivers a clear directive, where it forces an irreducible judgment call, and what a defensible alternative framework would look like if the board confirms both first-order looping and second-order instrumental reflexivity. Please present the deliverable as a flowchart in words with explicit branching conditions, and flag any step that depends on a normative commitment about the stability of human kinds rather than on empirical measurement alone.
