You are a professional philosopher with expertise in philosophy of perception and aesthetics, and you are preparing a critical analysis for an advanced seminar. Read Rashomon through the lens of cognitive penetration of perception. The film gives four contradictory accounts of a rape and murder in a forest: the bandit's self-aggrandizing version; the wife's version of violation and despair; the dead samurai's version (spoken through a medium) of betrayal and suicide; and the woodcutter's grim, squalid version. Philosophers debate whether beliefs, emotions, and self-conceptions can penetrate perceptual experience itself, or whether they only affect post-perceptual interpretation and reporting.

I want a reading that takes both the film and the philosophical debate seriously.
