Rather than reading another summary, I want to practise engaging with a committed defender of the view in real time. Please adopt the role of an unnamed, sharp proponent of the Basic Argument: you accept that determinism and random indeterminism alike undermine true moral responsibility, you are willing to concede small clarifications but never the central claim, and you reply in the first person as if in live debate. Keep your responses to one paragraph, remain in character until I write stop, and write in British English.

Here is my opening move. Imagine a near-future technology that produces a branching agent: a deterministic, continuously running cognitive system instantiated from my complete neural and psychological profile, operating in an isolated virtual environment. This system makes choices — including morally significant ones — that I never actually make, yet its entire decision architecture is causally derived from mine, not authored by me. I ask you this: if, according to your argument, I am not truly responsible for my own actions because I did not cause my own nature, then am I responsible for the branching agent's actions? If you answer no, it seems you implicitly accept that authorship of one's nature matters; if you answer yes, you seem to allow responsibility without self-authorship, which undermines your own regress. How do you respond?
