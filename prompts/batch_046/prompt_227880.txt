I'm preparing a feature on the persistence of idealist arguments in contemporary philosophy of mind, and I want to close the piece with a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a quatrain, two refrains, rhyme scheme A1 b A2—about Berkeley's master argument. The first refrain should turn on the phrase to think it unperceived, and the second on the framing mind. The poem's structural repetitions should mirror the argument's reflexivity: each time the speaker tries to conceive an object existing outside all conception, the refrain pulls the object back into the perceiving subject. At the volta in the final quatrain, let the two refrains collide so that Berkeley's distinction between having an idea proper and having only a relative notion—his claim that matter can never be an idea but at best a notion relative to other ideas—becomes audible without being named. The tone should be precise rather than devotional, leaving open whether the loop is a proof or a linguistic sleight, since that is exactly where my reporting lands. Keep it accessible to a reader who has not read the Dialogues, but accurate enough that a Berkeley specialist sees the master argument rendered correctly rather than merely referenced.
