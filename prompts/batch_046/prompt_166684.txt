hey so i've been thinkin about carroll's tortoise - you know the dialogue where the tortoise keeps asking achilles for one more rule every time he uses modus ponens, and it goes on forever. everyone usually reads this as "see, deduction can't be justified without circularity." but i actually want to defend the opposite take: the tortoise is making a category mistake. logical rules aren't instructions that need some extra authorization; they're constitutive of reasoning, not regulative over it. kind of like how the rules of chess don't need a meta-rule to authorize them - they just define what chess is. so my position is that the regress collapses once you stop treating modus ponens as a tool you apply and start seeing it as part of the framework that makes arguing possible at all. can you argue for this? specifically, defend the idea that the tortoise's demand is like asking "what rule allows you to speak english?" - it's not that there's a hidden permission, it's that the practice itself carries the authority. and address the worry that without explicit acceptance of the rule, we can't tell real reasoning from accidental pattern-matching. i think there's a distinction here between knowing-that and knowing-how that helps, but i'm not sure how to apply it cleanly.
