I'm drafting a short philosophical essay on whether self-deception is genuinely possible, and I'd like your comments on its coherence, on whether it handles the standard paradox correctly, and on whether the central distinctions hold up.

The essay rejects both the Freudian divided-self model and the deflationary "mere motivated bias" account. In their place it proposes a "context-indexed partition" model: an agent can maintain contradictory representations—say, B and not-B—provided they are formed and sustained within distinct practical contexts (for example, a professional-evaluation context versus a personal-reassurance context), and provided there is an active "epistemic firewall" that prevents the evidential bases and inferential pathways of each representation from cross-activating. On this view, self-deception is not a case of unconscious knowledge coupled with conscious denial, but rather the sustained coexistence of two context-bound representations that share content yet possess non-communicating justification structures.

The essay's central claim is that this avoids the classic simultaneous-knowledge paradox because the agent never has unified epistemic access to both representations at once. However, it argues that this only counts as self-deception rather than mere cognitive compartmentalization if the agent possesses a higher-order intention specifically to maintain the firewall in order to avoid the discomfort or practical cost that integrating the representations would produce. Without that intentional maintenance, the phenomenon collapses into ordinary fragmentation.

The essay then draws a sharp distinction between self-deception and self-induced ignorance. In self-induced ignorance, the agent never fully acquires the threatening belief; in self-deception, the agent acquires it but quarantines it within a sealed context. I conclude that self-deception is possible, but only as an intentionally sustained structural feature of belief-management, not as a momentary psychological glitch.

My questions are as follows. First, does the context-indexed partition genuinely dissolve the knowledge-paradox, or does the higher-order intention to maintain the firewall reintroduce a unified awareness of both beliefs at the level of agency? Second, is the distinction between self-deception and self-induced ignorance stable, given that actively maintaining a firewall might itself interfere with the full acquisition or endorsement of the quarantined belief? Third, is this account vulnerable to the objection that it describes rational compartmentalization—like an advocate arguing opposing sides in different forums—rather than the irrational self-deception we actually care about, and if so, what further feature must be added to capture the irrationality without reverting to unconscious-psyche language?
