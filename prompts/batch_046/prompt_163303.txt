I want an exam essay question aimed at students who have worked through the core literature but who must now apply the machinery to a non-standard case rather than summarize it. Specifically, please construct a 90-minute, 20-mark essay prompt centered on the following injected scenario: an academic institution undergoes a fifty-year transformation in which every faculty member is replaced, the charter is rewritten, and the funding model shifts from predominantly public to predominantly private, with precise quantitative metrics available at every stage—for instance, exactly 47% of current revenue derives from private sources. The predicate in question is "is a research university." The question should ask students to evaluate whether the availability of these sharp quantitative metrics resolves the vagueness of the predicate in the epistemicist fashion—by revealing a hidden boundary—or whether supervaluationism can still maintain semantic indeterminacy despite the metrics. Crucially, the prompt should also require them to address a further tension: during the transitional period the institution's own governance bodies continue to self-identify as a "research university" in ways that carry normative weight for accreditation and funding, yet neither view seems to explain straightforwardly why such self-identification should matter if the predicate is either unknowably sharp or genuinely boundary-less.

Please provide the exam question itself, followed by a model answer of approximately 700 words, and a marking scheme organized around five must-hit analytical moves, with the marks for each (summing to 20) and a one-line note on the characteristic failure for each point. The scheme should separate answers that genuinely apply the theories to this institutional case and identify where both views strain, from answers that merely define epistemicism and supervaluationism and apply them mechanically to "baldness" or "heap." Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the answer should be evaluable from conceptual resources alone.
