Write a short story about an aging composer who has structured her career around Hofstadter's model of creativity as knob-twiddling — the continuous adjustment of parameters on an invariant theme to generate emotional novelty. She constructs a custom acoustic instrument that permits only discrete, irreversible structural changes: once a register is sealed or a harmonic pathway is collapsed, the alteration is absolute and cannot be gradually modulated back. During a single performance, she must decide whether a discrete break can still count as a variation on a theme, or whether it reveals a hard limit to the parameter-space view of invention. After the story, provide a brief note that separates established claims about thematic transformation in music cognition from genuinely contested interpretations of Hofstadter's model, and explicitly marks where the narrative takes liberties with the concept or depends on a disputed reading of whether discrete jumps can be subsumed under continuous adjustment.
