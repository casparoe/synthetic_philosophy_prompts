My study group has an argument that seems to show that if concepts slip in one microdomain they slip everywhere, and I'm not sure where the leak is. (1) In any microdomain — say how biologists use "gene" versus how evolutionary biologists use it — the concept is fluid: there's no shared core, just family resemblance with local adjustments. (2) There's also a copycat dynamic: if concept C in domain D plays the same funcitonal role as concept C' in domain D', then whatever fluidity C has gets copied over to C'. (3) Every concept we use outside that microdomain plays some functional role that is also played inside a documented microdomain of slippage. (4) So by copycat propagation, every concept is fluid. (5) If every concept is fluid, then no statement has stable truth conditions across contexts. (6) But then scientific classification and legal reasoning, which depend on some concepts holding still, are impossible in principle. (7) That's absurd, so something went wrong. I want to keep (1) and (2) — the microdomain observation and the copycat idea both feel solid — and I'd rather not drop (5) since it follows pretty cleanly. Which step do I reject, and what's the cost? Is there a hidden quantifier shift or an equivocation on "fluid" between (1) and (4)?
