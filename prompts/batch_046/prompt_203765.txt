After a recent colloquium on recursive structures in cognition, two of my colleagues—Maya and Leo—became entangled in a disagreement over Hofstadter's use of Escher's Drawing Hands to illustrate tangled hierarchies and the concept of an inviolate level. Maya contended that the image actually destabilizes the very idea of an untouched substrate: because Escher depicts the hands emerging from and returning to the sheet of paper, the paper is not genuinely outside the loop but is itself drawn into the representational system, functioning simultaneously as container and content. Leo countered that this conflates artistic self-reference with logical typing; the paper remains causally and ontologically prior to the drawn hands, since the hands are representations on it rather than representations of it, so the asymmetry that defines the inviolate level survives intact regardless of the image's self-awareness. Maya then pressed a further objection, one that seemed to move the discussion beyond the artwork itself: in a fully recursive digital environment—say, a self-modifying rendering pipeline in which the substrate consists of modifiable pixels and the rendering rules can be altered by the patterns being rendered—there is no analog to Escher's physical paper that stands permanently outside the hierarchy. The system can, in principle, rewrite its own base layer, which suggests that the distinction between drawn object and drawing substrate genuinely collapses rather than being merely obscured. Leo acknowledged the force of this technological analogy but maintained that even in such cases an inviolate level persists, albeit relocated: the physical silicon, the formal mathematics of computation, or the boundary conditions of the simulation remain untouched by the internal loop, just as the paper remains untouched by the ink on it. They asked me to adjudicate, and I found myself genuinely uncertain about how far the analogy should be pushed. Could you work through their exchange step by step? Specifically, who has the stronger interpretation of Drawing Hands with respect to the paper's status; whether Maya's digital recursion scenario genuinely eliminates an inviolate level or merely displaces it; and whether Leo's relocation argument saves Hofstadter's framework or turns it into an unfalsifiable schema? Please also identify what, if anything, both parties overlook about how strange loops depend on the relationship between representation and substrate rather than on the substrate's physical properties alone.
