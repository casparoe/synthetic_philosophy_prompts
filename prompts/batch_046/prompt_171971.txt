I've been arguing with friends in my philosophy-of-science reading group about Dennett's framework of real patterns and the three stances—physical, design, and intentional—and I keep feeling that both sides of the standard debate miss the target. The eliminativists insist that all patterns dissolve into physics, while the pluralists claim patterns are stance-relative but then seem unable to explain why one stance should ever correct or constrain another. Before I commit to reading further defenses of pattern-realism, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory in this domain must actually achieve.

Write the desiderata: what any theory of real patterns spanning the physical, design, and intentional stances has to deliver—the phenomena it must preserve, the distinctions it must respect, and the conceptual tensions it must dissolve or explain—together with the motivating case behind each requirement. The list must cover at least: (1) the ontological status of design-stance patterns (functional architectures that survive substrate change) that vanish under fine-grained physical description; (2) the boundary conditions for intentional-stance attributions, particularly where rationality norms do explanatory work that neither physics nor pure functional design can capture; (3) what I'll call predictive interdependence, in which calibrating a prediction in one stance requires importing assumptions from another—such as explaining a broken thermostat's behavior by attributing false beliefs—without thereby reducing the first stance to the second; and (4) a consistency condition with physical closure that nevertheless allows higher-stance patterns to be causally and explanatorily indispensable. For each desideratum, provide the motivating puzzle, flag any pair that looks jointly unsatisfiable, and identify which single requirement eliminates the most popular minimalist or instrumentalist views. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justifications; then briefly sketch how Dennett's own pattern-realism, a hard physicalist reduction, and a deflationary stance-pluralism fare against your list.
