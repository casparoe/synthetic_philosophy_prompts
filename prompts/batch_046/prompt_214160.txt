You are a senior compliance officer at a county welfare-to-work office. Caseworkers must decide, within 48 hours, whether to impose a benefit reduction when a claimant has missed two consecutive job-search requirements. The office has adopted a philosophical framework—drawn from debates over desert, reciprocity, and structural constraint—that distinguishes failures of autonomous choice (where conditional obligations are genuinely broken) from failures rooted in incapacity, information deficit, or external barrier. Design a reusable decision procedure—ordered gates, evidentiary thresholds, mandatory stop points, and escalation points—that translates this framework into a 30-minute protocol a caseworker can run using only the case file and a brief claimant interview, with no philosophical training required.

Specify, in sequence: (1) the questions to ask; (2) what counts as sufficient evidence for each; (3) where the procedure ends with "no sanction"; (4) where it permits "sanction"; (5) where it must escalate to a values panel because the issue is normative rather than factual; and (6) which steps are judgment calls disguised as procedural checks. The procedure must handle hard cases: mental-health crises not yet documented, refusal of work that conflicts with religious practice without prior accommodation requests, and repeated non-compliance despite documented capacity and offered supports.

Apply it to three cases: (A) a claimant who missed both appointments and reports severe, undocumented depression, saying they "could not get out of bed"; (B) a claimant who refused a warehouse shift requiring Saturday work, citing religious observance, with no prior accommodation request in the file; and (C) a claimant with full documented capacity, prior warnings, and repeatedly missed appointments despite offered childcare and transport—whose only income supports two minor children, so that applying the sanction produces an outcome the caseworker will find morally uncomfortable. For each, show exactly where the procedure stops or proceeds, and flag any point where the caseworker’s compassion threatens to override the gate.

Finally, state explicitly what the procedure cannot resolve—particularly whether conditionality itself is legitimate—and identify where it will systematically misfire, such as by treating undocumented incapacity as choice or by masking distributive judgments behind apparently neutral evidentiary rules.
