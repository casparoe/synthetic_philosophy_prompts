Write an essay on the following controversy in metaethics and normative theory. Some philosophers hold that normative authority is indirect: a principle or action is justified not by direct evaluation but by its endorsement through an idealized deliberative procedure—one in which agents are fully rational, fully informed, and free from coercive bias. Critics object that this framework either collapses into direct normativity, because the criteria selecting the idealization are themselves directly normative, or generates a vicious regress, since the norms governing the idealization must themselves be justified by a further idealized procedure. To complicate matters, consider a hybrid view—call it recursive indirect normativity—which holds that the norms governing idealization are themselves outputs of the same deliberative process, converging on a reflective equilibrium rather than on a foundational ground. Weigh the main arguments for and against this recursive model, paying particular attention to whether the alleged regress is genuinely vicious or merely benign and iterative, and to whether the collapse objection can be answered by distinguishing procedural norms from substantive ones. Do not end on a non-committal note: defend a clear verdict on whether recursive indirect normativity offers a stable alternative to direct normativity, and explain what conditions, if any, would have to be met for it to succeed. Write in American English.
