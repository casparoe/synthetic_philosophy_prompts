I want to understand whether accuracy-first epistemology and epistemic utility theory can genuinely diverge on an intrinsic—rather than merely practical—basis, but I need a concrete, constructed case to test the boundary. Give an example of a conceptual issue in which an accuracy-first epistemologist, treating Brier-score minimization as the sole intrinsic epistemic good, assigns a specific credence c to a precisely defined claim on the basis of a specified evidence set E, while an epistemic-utility theorist—who weights accuracy together with a formalized perturbation-robustness premium, defined as a bonus applied to credences that remain invariant under small rational updates to the prior—judges that very assignment c to be epistemically suboptimal with respect to E, despite full agreement on both the evidence and the scoring-rule mathematics. Specify the claim, the evidence profile, the two utility functions, and the structural point at which the assignments diverge.
