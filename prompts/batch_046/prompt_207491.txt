Write a Petrarchan sonnet—octave ABBAABBA, sestet CDECDE—structured as a pathologist's autopsy report on an unnamed patient. In the octave, present every finding in strictly naturalist terms: statistical deviation from species-typical biological function, tissue morphology, absence of infection, so that disease appears only as an internal dysfunction without reference to value. At the volta in line 9, reframe the identical clinical observations through a normativist lens—now describing the same measurements in terms of harm to valued capacities, failure of social role, and experienced suffering—without changing any factual claim. Choose rhyme pairs that turn on words ambiguous between biological and evaluative registers, such as terms that denote statistical regularity in one line and normative appropriateness in the other, or mechanical operation versus social purpose. The closing couplet must be a single sentence that is grammatically identical whether parsed as a factual determination of cause or as a moral judgment about whether the condition counts as disease, leaving the naturalist/normativist divide suspended in the syntax itself. Do not name philosophers or cite texts; render the conceptual tension solely through the form, diction, and the structural turn of the sonnet. The result should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with the philosophy of medicine recognizes both accounts, yet rendered precisely enough that neither is reduced to a slogan.
