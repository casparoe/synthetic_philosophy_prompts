I'm advising a philosophy-of-science research unit — one PI, two postdocs, three years — that wants to allocate its effort to conceptual questions about how epistemic dependence on large language models erodes scientific understanding. Rather than a literature survey, I need a defended research agenda.

Frame the problem as follows: classical epistemic outsourcing (using instruments, formal methods) preserves access in principle to the reasoning pathway, whereas epistemic delegation (relying on an expert or authority) does not. LLMs appear to collapse this distinction: they present reasoning in a form that resembles outsourcing, yet for the user the inferential pathway is typically opaque, functioning more like delegation. This raises the question of whether scientific understanding requires a form of "triangulation" — the ability to locate one's belief within a network of reasons that one could, in principle, traverse — and whether AI-assisted research is generating a novel "understanding deficit" that is neither ordinary ignorance nor mere computational dependence.

Given this framing, identify and rank the three open conceptual questions that should absorb most of the unit's effort. For each, specify: (a) why it is upstream of or more foundational than the others; (b) whether it is tractable for conceptual philosophers rather than empirical ML researchers; (c) its practical stakes for current scientific practice; and (d) what kind of result — conceptual clarification, a formal model, or a targeted case study — would move it forward.

Then defend the overall ordering. State the criteria governing your ranking (e.g., tractability, neglectedness, dependency of other questions on this one, urgency for scientific practice), identify the main trade-offs between them, and say what empirical or conceptual development over the next two years would cause you to reorder the list. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; deliver a reasoned agenda, not a survey.
