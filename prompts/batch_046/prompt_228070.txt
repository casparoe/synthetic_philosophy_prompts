Thomson's lamp is the thought experiment where you toggle a lamp on and off in intervals that halve each time, reaching exactly two minutes. At the two-minute mark, people ask whether it's on or off. Thomson argued this creates a contradiction: it can't be on, since every on-switch was immediately followed by an off-switch; but it can't be off either, since the initial switch turned it on and no final switch turned it off. To resolve this, he pointed out that Grandi's series (1 - 1 + 1 - ...) has no standard sum, though mathematicians sometimes assign it 1/2. He took this to mean the lamp is literally half-on, which shows that supertasks generate impossible physical states. Later philosophers added that real bulbs have thermal inertia, so an infinite-speed toggle would burn out the filament right at the limit, meaning the paradox only applies to idealized logic and tells us nothing about actual continuous time.
