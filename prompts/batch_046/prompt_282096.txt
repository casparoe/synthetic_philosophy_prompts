I retired several years ago from a long career in experimental physics, and I have since approached philosophy with the same insistence on formal precision that once governed my laboratory protocols. I am now preparing to moderate a podcast interview with an unnamed philosopher—let us call him Dr. Aris—who defends a sharply defined variant of the conceivability-possibility thesis. His specific claim is that conceivability guides metaphysical possibility only when it satisfies what he terms structural closure: the imagined scenario must be not merely coherent in thought but formally integrable into the dynamics of the actual world's law-structure without generating unresolvable singularities or inconsistencies. He applies this criterion both to the modal status of consciousness in non-biological substrates and to abandoned scientific posits, such as the luminiferous aether, arguing that these cases fail not because they are unimaginable but because they fail closure.

I want eight to ten interview questions that avoid asking him to restate the view and that contain no biographical or generic material. Specifically, I need at least one question comparing his structural-closure condition to standard modal-rationalist criteria—such as those tied to clear-and-distinct perception or to two-dimensional semantic accounts—together with a direct follow-up keyed to his likely defense that closure is more restrictive and therefore more reliable than mere imaginative coherence. I also want a question pressing whether the formal-integrability requirement collapses conceivability into a disguised physical-consistency check, thereby stripping it of its distinctive modal authority, with a follow-up anticipating his reply that singularities are not failures but diagnostic markers of genuine modal boundary conditions. Please include one question on higher-order conceivability—whether we can coherently conceive of our own systematic failure to conceive a genuinely possible state of affairs—and one asking what kind of formal or empirical result could, in principle, weaken rather than merely illustrate the link he posits. Each question should be framed so that a listener without library access can follow the argument from the architecture of the view alone; do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
