Consider Aristotle's account of akrasia in Nicomachean Ethics VII, particularly his distinction between the continent agent's active grasp of practical premises and the incontinent agent's retained but inactive universal knowledge, which is overpowered by appetite or pleasure directed at a particular object. Now suppose Aristotle were presented with the modern quasi-hyperbolic discounting framework—the β-δ model of intertemporal choice—and with neuroeconomic evidence that present-biased agents systematically undervalue delayed rewards through a structurally stable hyperbolic weighting function rather than through a momentary surge of passion. How do you think he would classify this temporal myopia? Would he assimilate it to the akrasia he describes, treating the hyperbolic discounter as someone whose reasoning about future goods is defeated by an immediate affective pull, or would he regard it as a distinct failure of practical reason specifically concerning the temporal ordering and comparative magnitude of goods? Moreover, how might he revise his claim that the akratic person knows in one manner yet lacks the active knowledge possessed by the continent person, given a model in which the distortion is not an episodic victory of desire over reason but a uniform, predictable bias in the evaluation function itself?
