I am preparing the conceptual scaffolding for a graduate seminar on evolutionary explanations in the human sciences. Before we discuss particular studies, I want students to have a precise specification of theoretical adequacy. Write the desiderata that any evolutionary-psychological explanation of a complex human trait—say, cooperative punishment or normative reasoning—must meet to count as genuinely scientific rather than an unfalsifiable just-so narrative. For each desideratum, identify: the empirical cases it must accommodate (e.g., cross-cultural variation, developmental plasticity, or maladaptive present expression); the distinctions it must preserve rather than collapse (e.g., selection-for versus selection-of, evolutionary origin versus current utility, niche-construction feedback versus static-environment optimization); the biological or developmental results with which it must remain consistent (e.g., that high heritability is neither necessary nor sufficient, and that phenotypic plasticity is not mere noise); and any transparency condition on the proposed mechanism (e.g., reverse-engineering specificity: the adaptive problem must be mapped onto a plausible computational architecture). Behind each, state the motivating consideration, and flag whether the requirement is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp (strict adaptationism, evolutionary-developmental systems, etc.). Then say which single desideratum eliminates the largest share of popular evolutionary-psychological claims, and whether any subset of your list is jointly unsatisfiable given current evolutionary-developmental biology. Briefly score a strict adaptationist program and one niche-construction-sensitive alternative against the list, without bibliographies, named citations, or reading lists. Lead with the bottom line in one opening sentence. Present the core desiderata, their rationales, and their negotiability in a table. Keep the response to roughly 350 words, about one page.
