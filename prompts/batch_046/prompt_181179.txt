Then Bernard Williams used agent-regret and moral residue to show that unavoidable wrongdoing leaves a stain even when you picked the least-bad option, which proves dilemnas are real — because if moral theory worked it should have guided you clear. Ruth Marcus added that if there's no residue after your choice, you weren't in a dilemma at all, you were just running an optimisation problem. So the upshot is that any system promising full action-guidance is either denying human experience or smuggling in a hidden ranking rule."

I think there's sleight of hand in the Williams step, and maybe the Kant bit is too tidy. Is this fair? Could you go through it bit by bit and say what's roughly right, what's subtly twisted, and whether that final conclusion actually survives correction? Also, does the "wider obligation" framing actually capture Kant's view on imperfect duty conflicts, or is that a pop-philosophy gloss?
