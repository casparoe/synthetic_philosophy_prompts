I want the full specification for what an adequate theory of hinge epistemology—centered on Wittgenstein’s notion of hinges in On Certainty—must actually deliver. Not an interpretive survey, but a requirements list: the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must preserve, the cases it must handle correctly, and the constraints from adjacent domains (formal epistemology, philosophy of science, legal reasoning about presumptions) it cannot violate, with the motivating consideration behind each.

Structure the list around these demands, treating them as prompts for the complete spec rather than an exhaustive checklist:

1. The theory must account for the exemption from doubt that hinges enjoy without assimilating them to ordinary empirical knowledge or to arbitrary stipulations. Give the case behind why both assimilation paths collapse—specifically, why treating hinges as highly confirmed beliefs makes hinge-change irrational in the wrong way, and why treating them as conventional rules makes them vulnerable to voluntary revision.

2. It must draw a precise line between hinges and mere background conditions or tacit auxiliary assumptions—especially in scientific contexts where an auxiliary is abandoned rationally (e.g., revising a calibration constant) while a hinge can shift only non-rationally. What is the exact mark of that distinction, and can it be drawn without appealing to a sharp propositional/content boundary that Wittgenstein’s examples undermine?

3. It must accommodate hinge migration: propositions that function as hinges in one context but become objects of direct empirical inquiry in another (e.g., the stability of a measuring instrument, the reliability of testimony in a particular domain, or the continuity of personal identity). The theory should explain what governs migration without dissolving the hinge/inquiry boundary entirely.

4. It must handle nested hinges: cases where one hinge’s operative status depends on another (e.g., the reliability of memory may hinge on conditions of bodily persistence, which may themselves hinge on broader conditions of causal continuity). How should the theory represent these dependency relations without generating vicious circularity, infinite regress, or a foundationalist base?

5. It must be consistent with the fact that hinge change—when it occurs—resembles conversion, trauma, or a shift in form of life rather than belief revision by evidence. Specify what explanatory resources the theory needs (social, practical, biological, institutional?) and which it must avoid if hinges are not to reduce to ideology or mere habit.

6. It must preserve the asymmetry between doubting a hinge and doubting an empirical claim within the hinge system, without rendering hinges immune to all rational pressure—e.g., in scientific revolutions, in cross-cultural encounter, or in legal overturning of presumptions. Where exactly is the line between legitimate pressure on a hinge and a category mistake?

7. It must avoid literalizing structural metaphors—such as the riverbed, the axis, or the scaffolding—into physical or logical foundations in a traditional foundationalist architecture. Explain what structural feature of hinge functioning those metaphors actually capture, and flag any interpretation that turns hinges into a base layer of knowledge.

For each requirement, state whether it is non-negotiable or negotiable (and on what terms), and identify the single requirement that eliminates the largest number of existing interpretations—whether the hinges-as-grammatical-rules view, the hinges-as-basic-beliefs foundationalist reconstruction, or the hinges-as-non-propositional-trust attitude-based account.

Then, briefly—no more than a short paragraph per contender—score those three interpretations against the full list, noting specifically where each fails on hinge migration and on nested dependency.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citation recommendations; the point is to test whether the conceptual architecture holds together from the inside.
