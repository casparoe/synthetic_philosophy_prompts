I am a doctoral student in philosophy of science, and in two weeks I am presenting a seminar paper to a philosophy of science and logic group that includes several Quine specialists, philosophers of physics who take holistic underdetermination seriously, and a couple of researchers who work with formal models of scientific theories. My thesis is that Quine's rejection of the analytic-synthetic distinction in his well-known essay relies on an implicit commitment to global holism that collapses once we model scientific theories using local structural equation frameworks; specifically, I argue that the distinction can be rehabilitated not as Carnap-style semantic conventionalism but as a measurable gradient of "inferential insulation" operating within sub-models, which supports a modest epistemic division of labour in theory testing rather than a binary semantic classification. The audience will be skeptical of any revival of analyticity, committed to the Duhem-Quine thesis in its standard form, and alert to formal tricks that merely relocate philosophical problems. Please prepare me for the objections in the order they are likely to arise, phrased as these particular interlocutors would phrase them: begin with the suspicion that I am smuggling conventionalism back in through formal notation, then the global-revision objection, then the boundary-vagueness problem, then the charge that my structural-equation apparatus is itself theory-laden in precisely the way Quine warned against, and finally include the objection I am hoping nobody raises—namely, that my metric for inferential insulation requires prior normative commitments about legitimate inference that are themselves analytic by my own lights, generating circularity. For each objection, indicate what I should concede up front, what I should hold firm on, and where my reply is weakest. In framing this, please separate what is settled in the Quine scholarship from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested interpretive claim—such as the exact strength of Quine's holism or whether formal modelling can escape it—as if it were the consensus view. Write in British English, keep the tone formal and polished, and avoid asking for bibliographies, citation lists, or specific reading recommendations.
