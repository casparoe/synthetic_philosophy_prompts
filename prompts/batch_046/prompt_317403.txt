A summary I recently encountered of Ian Hacking's account of looping effects in psychiatric classification describes psychiatric categories as interactive kinds caught in a feedback loop with the people they classify. According to this summary, once a diagnosis is institutionalized, patients learn the clinical description, alter their self-understanding and behavior to match it, and thereby cause psychiatrists to revise the criteria, so that the disorder is continuously remade rather than discovered. The summary claims that Hacking extends this mechanism uniformly across all psychiatric taxonomy, including schizophrenia and autism, and that he uses it to deny any independent biological or genetic contribution to mental illness, treating neuroscience as largely irrelevant to classification. It further states that because looping introduces inherent instability, psychiatric categories can never achieve the predictive reliability of natural-science kinds like chemical elements, and that Hacking therefore regards the DSM framework as a purely sociological document that ought to be replaced by narrative, context-sensitive accounts of mental distress. I suspect this characterization makes Hacking sound more radically anti-realist and anti-biological than he actually is, and that it may conflate his distinction between interactive and indifferent kinds with a broader skepticism about scientific taxonomy, but I cannot tell which parts are genuine misreadings and which are accurate extensions of his view.
