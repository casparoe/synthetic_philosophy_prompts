I'm presenting a paper I call "Mechanistic Compatibilism: Responsibility Without Basic Desert" at a philosophy-of-law seminar. The audience is a mix of criminal-law philosophers and moral philosophers who have followed the Dennett-Caruso exchange closely: about half are retributivists or expressive-theory proponents—scholars who think Dennett's compatibilism is too thin to ground real punishment and who care deeply about whether punishment can express appropriate moral condemnation—while the other half are hard incompatibilists in Caruso's camp, convinced that any retention of punishment practices smuggles in the very metaphysical authorship they reject. They are skeptical of synthetic middle positions and are particularly invested in the distinction between treating an offender as an end versus as a node in a causal system, and in whether any non-desert justification can survive the separateness-of-persons objection.

My thesis sketch is this. I accept Caruso's core negative claim: there is no "basic desert"—no metaphysical property of agents that makes them genuinely deserve suffering proportionate to wrongness—because we lack the contra-causal freedom such desert requires. But I reject his inference that punishment must therefore be reconceived purely as a public-health or quarantine measure. Instead, I defend a Dennett-inspired functionalist view: the practices of holding responsible and applying targeted sanctions are evolved, causal mechanisms that maintain the predictive and normative stability of social coordination. Punishment is justified not as moral ledger-balancing, but as mechanism-repair: it targets the specific social and psychological conditions that produced the offense, aiming to restore coordination rather than to inflict suffering for its own sake. I call this "mechanistic compatibilism"—we treat agents as genuinely responsible (Dennett) only in the sense that holding them responsible is part of the causal architecture that makes social life possible, and we justify sanctions solely by their mechanistic role, never by a metaphysical desert-claim (Caruso).

Prepare me for the objections this specific audience will actually raise. Order them by danger to the proposal's survival. Include the one objection I'm hoping nobody asks. Also tell me what I must concede in my opening remarks to maintain credibility, and identify the single reply that, if it fails, collapses the whole argument. Give me reasons for each choice; don't leave the strategy to me.
