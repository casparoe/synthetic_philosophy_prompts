I sit on the capital projects committee for our city library system. We're three years into a climate-controlled vault for old newspapers and microfiche — about $4.2M spent, $2.8M still needed to finish. The design specs are from 2015, digital archives have made the physical collection mostly redundant, and usage projections are near zero. Half the board says finishing is classic sunk-cost fallacy: the prior money is gone, don't throw more at it. The other half says walking away now wastes the $4.2M and, more importantly, breaks a public commitment to donors and the state heritage board; they claim honoring sunk costs can be rational if it protects institutional credibility and respects prior commitments.

Please draft the decision memo for thursday's vote. Recommend one course: finish on a scaled-down scope, abandon with a transition plan for the materials, or pause with defined conditions. Give a clear bottom line and a concrete timeline. The memo should seriously engage the idea that honoring sunk costs isn't always irrational — specifically, work through the distinction between sunk costs as a decision-error and sunk investments as generating expressive obligations or credibility reasons to continue. Apply that distinction to our case: does the credibility argument still hold when the vault's original purpose has basically evaporated? Include an honest account of the strongest argument for the option you reject. Keep it practical, no citations or reading lists needed, just reasoning the board can actually respond to. Sorry for the lowercase thursday.
