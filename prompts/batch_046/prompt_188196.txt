I have been observing a recurring pattern across biological and artificial learning systems that I struggle to place within the standard taxonomy of cognitive kinds. At one pole lies evolutionary design: a form is simply eliminated if it fails. Next comes direct experiential learning—trial, error, and adjustment within a single lifetime. Beyond that sits the capacity to rehearse possibilities internally, discarding dangerous actions without ever performing them. And further still lies the deployment of language, records, or external tools to inherit knowledge without personal trial.

Between direct experiential learning and genuine internal rehearsal, however, I keep noticing a particular intermediate profile. Imagine an animal that has learned to avoid a location not because it has ever been harmed there, but because it was previously exposed to an arbitrary cue—a tone, a colour, a geometric shape—that had been paired with harm elsewhere. Or imagine a trained algorithm that rejects a specific input pattern solely because a human supervisor assigned it a negative value, despite possessing no representational structure that encodes why the pattern is dangerous. In both cases the system avoids catastrophe without current direct experience of it, and without generating or evaluating an internal hypothetical. The avoidance is mediated entirely by a learned association with a proxy symbol.

This produces a behaviour that looks remarkably like foresight—skipping a fatal option before encountering it—yet collapses as soon as the proxy is removed or altered. It is not evolutionary hard-wiring, because the association was acquired during the agent's own history; it is not cultural transmission, because no shared record or linguistic exchange is involved; and it is not genuine mental simulation, because there is no internal model being consulted, only a conditioned link.

Does this specific configuration—lifetime-acquired, symbol-mediated avoidance that mimics anticipatory cognition but operates without any internal generative model—carry an established name? I am not asking about ordinary conditioned fear, about stimulus generalisation, or about the use of external scaffolding to extend reasoning. I am asking whether there is a precise concept for this 'false foresight' that arises when conditioning on proxies produces behaviour resembling internal rehearsal through purely associative mechanisms. If such a concept exists, how exactly is it demarcated from true internal modelling—where an agent genuinely generates and evaluates hypothetical outcomes—on one side, and from simple reflex conditioning on the other? If it does not exist, could you coin a term, provide a strict definition, and specify the boundary cases that would distinguish it from neighbouring phenomena?
