I've been noticing a recurring slide in everyday arguments about borderline cases—whether a 179.9 cm person counts as "tall," whether a particular shade is "orange" or already "red." One moment a speaker insists there must be a precise cutoff, only hidden from us like an invisible line; the next moment, pressed on how nature could have fixed an arbitrary millimeter or how we could ever learn it, the same speaker says the word simply fails to apply at the edge, that asking whether the borderline case belongs is like asking whether the North Pole is north of itself. The two stories feel different—one treats the gap as ignorance, the other as a failure of fit between language and world—yet people slide between them as if defending one intuition. Is there an established name for this specific oscillation, or for the failure to keep the hidden-cutoff explanation separate from the no-application explanation? I don't just want the labels of the two underlying positions; I want to know whether philosophers have carved out the move itself, how it differs from ordinary ambiguity or contextualism, and where its edges lie—if there is no term, what precise definition and boundary cases would capture it?
