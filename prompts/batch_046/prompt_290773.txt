I am drafting the conceptual framework for a workshop on graded moral status that deliberately avoids both binary person/non-person distinctions and simple linear scaling from “fully conscious” to “merely reactive.” For the opening position paper, please argue that moral status ought to be assigned along three intersecting dimensions—valenced affective range, temporal integration of preference-states, and socio-relational embedding—rather than along a single sentience gradient. Specifically, defend the view that an individual bumblebee carries a low but genuine moral weight because it exhibits narrow affective range coupled with minimal temporal integration, whereas a hive-level collective exhibits a structurally different grade of status through dense socio-relational embedding that does not aggregate into a unified conscious subject, and that a thermostat—despite possessing feedback loops structurally analogous to homeostasis—occupies the null point not merely because it lacks neurons, but because it fails to integrate any of the three dimensions into a coherent welfare-subject architecture.

Your argument should treat these dimensions as jointly necessary for non-zero status, explain why evolutionary continuity is an insufficient criterion on its own, and address the objection that any multi-dimensional scheme collapses back into an arbitrary weighted sum. Present this as a continuous argumentative essay of no more than 300 words, with no citations, lists, or references to specific authors.
