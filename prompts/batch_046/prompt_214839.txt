Write a dialogue between two grad students, A and B, arguing whether the 18th-century French compagnonnage — those wandering craft brothehoods that invented medieval origin myths — is better explained by Hobsbawm's idea of invented traditions or Anderson's idea of imagined communities. A should defend Hobsbawm: they fabricated rituals and lineages to cover modern labor-market roots, pure present-need invention. B should defend Anderson: they formed a horizontal fraternity across regions using printed ritual manuals and shared symbols, which is an imagined community that just lacked the territorial state administation. They have to actually respond — so A should point out these guys were mostly oral and illiterate, which undercuts Anderson's print-capitalism mechanism, and B should reply that the manuals were indeed printed and circulated, and that lacking state machinery is exactly why they never became a nation rather than failing Anderson's model. Don't make either side a strawman. Keep it casual, include one typo, and to avoid boilerplate have them briefly ask whether a crypto-DAO that writes a fake ancient charter is closer to compagnonnage or to a modern nation-state.
