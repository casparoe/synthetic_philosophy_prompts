I want to set out, before weighing any particular account, the full specification of what an adequate theory of vagueness must deliver, with epistemicism and supervaluationism kept in view but not treated as exhaustive. Write the desiderata: the phenomena the theory must accommodate (higher-order vagueness, the tolerance intuition, the sorites paradox in its conditional and line-drawing forms), the distinctions it must not collapse (between semantic indeterminacy and epistemic ignorance, between lacking sharp boundaries and lacking knowledge of them, between being borderline and being merely disputed), and the theoretical costs it must avoid (positing hidden precise cut-offs that are in principle unknowable, or abandoning classical logic without an independent motivation drawn from linguistic practice). For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration—why, for instance, treating vagueness as mere ignorance fails to explain our reluctance to apply 'bald' after the addition of a single hair, and why a theory that resolves every borderline case by fiat misrepresents ordinary linguistic competence. Then flag which pairs of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, especially whether preserving classical bivalence and denying arbitrary sharp boundaries can be maintained together given standard formulations of the sorites. Keep the scoring brief: indicate only how epistemicism and supervaluationism fare against the list, with weight on the criteria rather than on the verdict.

Finally, include one further desideratum that is rarely made explicit. The theory should account for what I shall call institutional borderline rulings: situations in which a collective body—say, a regulatory or procurement authority—issues a determinate decision on a genuinely borderline property (for example, whether a particular alloy counts as high-grade steel under a technical contract) without access to hidden precise criteria, and the theory must explain whether the determinacy of that ruling derives from an epistemic resolution, a semantic gap filled by stipulation, or a pragmatic convention. State why this case matters for distinguishing semantic from pragmatic accounts of vagueness, and whether it introduces any new tension into the list above. Write in formal, polished British English.
