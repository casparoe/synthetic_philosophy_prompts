ok so i'm trying to get my philosophical house in order before i start this new novel with an unreliable narrator, and i need to check if my commitments hang together or if i'm just being a hypocrit. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) I buy the knowledge norm for assertion -- if you literally say p you gotta know p, full stop. I think Williamson's basically right, though i dont need the citation.

(2) Fictional discourse is pretence, not literal assertion, so authors can write dragons and time travel without epistemic fault. The norm in fiction is more like "make-believe coherence" than knowledge.

(3) But i also think there's this thing i call narrative leakage: if a claim is printed in narrative voice with real weight -- not buried in dialogue, not flagged with "she believed that..." -- then it carries assertoric inertia. So if my narrator flat-out says "the gun was loaded" and i haven't actually decided in my world-building whether it was, i'm violating (1) through leakage even though it's fiction.

(4) Unreliable narration blocks leakage only if the unreliability is signaled to the reader. If i just write an unreliable narrator without marking it, that's not pretence, that's me smuggling in an assertion i dont know.

(5) Dialogue is totally exempt from all this, because the character is speaking, not me. Even if the character lies, i'm not asserting the lie.

So do these hang together? It feels like (2) and (3) are fighting -- either fiction is pretence and i'm off the hook, or narrative voice leaks and i'm on the hook for every loaded-gun claim i haven't world-built. And (4) seems to make unreliable narration harder than people think, but maybe that's ok. Meanwhile (5) treats dialogue and unreliable narration differently, but both involve characters speaking, so is that distinction stable?
