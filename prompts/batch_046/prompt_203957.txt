hey—quick thought experiment for my grad seminar on moral agregation, specifically the scale argument with insects. my intuitons are all over the place and i want to see if there's a hidden principle or if i just have to live with the tension.

case one: there's maybe a 0.01% creddence any given ant is sentient, and there are roughly 10^18 of them. mathematically that's enormous expected suffering, but my gut resists treating it like a crisis on par with factory farming—feels like probability laundering or something. case two: i can save one clearly suffering dog or prevent crushing 10,000 bees. i pick the dog every time, even though 10,000 times 0.0001 technically equals one expected sentient being, which should tie it. case three: but if you tell me bees have a 15% sentience credence based on neural analogies, then saving 10,000 bees suddenly feels weighty—maybe comparable to the dog, though i still don't think it clearly wins. case four: imagine a made-up micro-crustacean, the pseudonociceptor, with exactly one pain-like receptor but populations around 10^22. even at 1% sentience, i think i shouldn't care at all; scale doesn't move me. case five: replace the bees with silicon robots behaving identically, no biological substrate, a trillion strong. i say they don't count, but i can't tell if that's a near-zero prior for silicon or just biological chauvinism.

the flip-flop case: i can either save one human child or prevent every ant on earth from dying for one year, say via banning a pesticide. sometimes the child is obviously right; other times the aggregate number feels so monstrous that ignoring it seems like moral blindness.

so—can you extract a principle, or a small family of them, that gets cases one through five, especially the jump between two and three and the flat rejection in four? does it have to drop my flip-flop in case six, or can it accommodate both swings? also apply it to an unlisted case: what if we farm ten billion synthetic crickets for protein, each with a 2% sentience chance and behavior indistinguishable from real crickets, but farming them cuts chicken consumption? where does your principle land?

keep it precise, no citations needed—just the logic and the honest admission of which verdict gets sacrificed if there's a real conflict.
