I am constructing a taxonomy for a seminar on comparative political history: I want to map the actual logical space of positions that treat the collapse of the Roman Republic—from the Gracchan reforms through the Social War to the establishment of the First Triumvirate—as structurally analogous to pathologies in modern representative democracies. Build the taxonomy as a decision tree in which each internal node is a yes/no or either/or claim on which the analogical arguments genuinely come apart, not a label that people self-identify with.

The root should be whether one accepts that there is any non-trivial structural isomorphism between late-Republican institutional decay and modern democratic vulnerability.

From there, branch on:
(1) whether the relevant parallel is institutional-design failure (e.g., quorum rules, veto mechanisms, term limits) or socio-economic substrate (e.g., debt-bondage cycles, patron-client network dissolution, land consolidation);
(2) whether the decisive failure mode is the privatization of coercive force (client armies, private security, mercenary loyalty), the erosion of deliberative norms (senatorial obstruction, filibuster analogues), or the substitution of patronage for citizenship (vote-buying, dependency networks);
(3) whether the analogy is meant as causal explanation, predictive model, or normative warning;
(4) whether modern safeguards—independent judiciaries, universal suffrage, bureaucratic professionalism—are treated as breaking the analogy or as superficial variations on the same substrate.

Each leaf should carry either the name of a recognized position or 'unnamed,' plus one sentence stating its most awkward commitment (for example, that it must deny any functional similarity between private military contractors and Roman client armies, or that it must treat universal suffrage as structurally equivalent to the Roman tribal assemblies).

Please also identify any combinations of answers that appear unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis: is the gap because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored? And flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate—where two different named positions end up with identical answer patterns—rather than inventing a spurious distinction.

The taxonomy should be usable by someone who knows the historical material but does not already know which analogical camp they belong to; therefore, phrase every node as a concrete claim about the Republic or about modern institutions, never as 'do you favor the institutionalist reading?'

If this request rests on a confused premise—for instance, if the analogy debate cannot be usefully decomposed into these four axes, or if the historical premises required are too contested to support a clean tree—say so plainly instead of constructing the tree politely. I want the classification instrument, not a diplomatic compromise. How committed should you be to the tree's branches? Be explicit about which distinctions you treat as sharp and which you think are genuinely continuous or underdetermined by the literature, and invite direct pushback if the framing forces false binaries onto the debate.
