hey, working on a chapter about embedded self-models and i tried to adapt Avicenna's flying man to predicitive processing frames and now i'm stuck with an argument that eats itself. here's the chain:

(1) Avicenna's flying man is aware of himself with no bodily input, so self-awareness doesn't require sensory inferrence about a body.
(2) in predicitive processing terms, that means the self-model can be generated without bottom-up bodily prediction errors - it's a top-down prior that can run offline.
(3) if the self-model can run offline with no bodily input, then its content is just the formal structure of reflexivity, not tied to any specific biological implementation.
(4) but then the same formal structure could be realized in a different substrate - a digital twin, a split-brain copy, whatever.
(5) so Avicenna's argument for a non-physical soul actually shows the opposite: that the self is a portable pattern with no necessary link to this body.
(6) and if it's portable, then personal identity over time isn't continuity of substance or even psychology, it's just continuity of that formal loop.
(7) but then sleep, coma, or copying all become unproblematic in ways that feel wrong - you'd survive being copied, and there'd be no fact of the matter about which copy is you.
(8) so something has to go. i'd really like to keep (1) and (2) since they match the phenomology, but (7) feels like a reductio.

where's the bad step? is it smuggling substrate-independence into running offline? please separate what's actually settled here from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
