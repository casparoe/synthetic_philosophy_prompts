I have recently begun reading about the Vienna Circle for a seminar on logical empiricism, and I keep encountering the claim that Otto Neurath's ISOTYPE system—his international picture language designed for scientific and educational communication—functions essentially like the emoji and universal icon sets we use today, in that both bypass linguistic mediation through direct visual recognition. The analogy is often deployed to suggest that Neurath anticipated modern visual interfaces and that his project was therefore more practically successful, or at least more intuitively grounded, than the syntactic and formalist approaches associated with Carnap. I want to audit how far this correspondence actually extends, because the comparison feels like it is doing more argumentative work than it should.

Could you lay out the explicit mapping—what plays the role of the pictogram, the syntax, the user community, and the intended universality in each case—then identify the precise points where the analogy fractures? In particular, I am struck by how Neurath tied ISOTYPE to his broader physicalist program and to the idea of protocol sentences as empirically anchored foundations for the unity of science, whereas modern emoji operate within highly contextual, culturally saturated linguistic frames that lack any standardized scientific syntax. Does the analogy quietly import a false neutrality or an assumption of culture-free transparency that ISOTYPE itself did not fully possess? And once we account for the fact that ISOTYPE was meant to be embedded in a rigorously standardized scientific notation rather than a loose social vocabulary, does the conclusion that picture languages are naturally more accessible or less theory-laden than symbolic ones still survive? If the analogy breaks in these dimensions, what would be a more accurate comparison for understanding Neurath's actual ambitions?
