I have been thinking about debates over welfare programs, work requirements, and the idea of the undeserving poor. In these discussions, people often say there is no free lunch to justify stricter conditionality and tougher penalties for benefit fraud. I can tell the phrase is meant to carry some weight, but I cannot tell exactly what claim it is making. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan might express — for instance, an empirical claim about work incentives, a moral claim about reciprocal obligation, or a claim about the logical impossibility of unearned goods? For each reading, I would like to know whether it actually supports policies like mandatory job searches, fraud prosecution, or the distinction between those who cannot work and those who will not. I am trying to figure out whether the phrase does genuine argumentative work or just gives a familiar shape to an intuition about deservedness.
