Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Mara and Jules, about whether hyperbolic discounting actually explains weakness of will (akrasia) or just redescribes it with fancier math. Mara thinks the hyperbolic curve dissolves the classic paradox: there's no single "better judgment" being overridden, just present and future selves with different weights fighting it out. Jules thinks thats a cheat—every hyperbolic model still sneaks in commitment devices, and those only make sense if there's already a normative standard (the "better" judgment) that the present self is trying to protect, not just another preference.

Inject a niche twist: have them argue over "retroactive annulment contracts"—pre-commitment deals where a future self can void a present contract, but only if the present self reports a specific "agency misrecognition" (feeling the action wasnt genuinely theirs). Mara should use this to show commitment is still just intertemporal bargaining; Jules should argue the misrecognition clause secretly appeals to an authentic self that sits outside the discount curve altogether.

Make it real back-and-forth—each actually answers the other's point, no strawmen, both sound sharp. Keep it around a page, casual is fine, and dont ask for citations, bibliographies, or specific texts. Just the dialogue, and maybe one closing sentence asking which feature of the case does the real philosophical work.
