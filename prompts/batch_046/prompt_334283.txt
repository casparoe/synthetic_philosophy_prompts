I run a philosophy workshop at my high school, about 15 students aged 16–18, with one 90-minute session. I want an in-class game that makes us feel the ethical scaling trap in panpsychism before anyone names it. The idea: if minimal sentience is distributed across fundamental physical entities rather than emerging only in integrated systems like brains, then altering any physical process—melting ice, running a computation, performing surgery—potentially affects astronomically more micro-subjects than all macroscopic beings combined. Design an activity in which we act as an ethics board allocating a fixed harm-reduction budget across cases that include both macroscopic beings and apparently inanimate physical processes, with rules that conceal the micro-level population counts until after we commit. The payoff or scoring should reveal that our intuitive priorities protected far fewer subjects than we assumed. Include a debrief that maps our allocation moments onto the combination problem and the quantitative incommensurability of macro and micro suffering, predicts what will go wrong the first time (for example, participants treating the micro-level as purely metaphorical), and is frank about which feature—perhaps the absence of any non-arbitrary cutoff between macro and micro suffering—the activity can suggest but not fully reproduce.
