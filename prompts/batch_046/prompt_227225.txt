You are advising a small interdisciplinary group—philosophers and cognitive scientists—with three years of funding to determine what cross-cultural differences in folk morality (the WEIRD/non-WEIRD literature) genuinely imply for moral objectivity. Do not survey the field; instead, propose and defend a prioritized agenda of three to five open conceptual questions they should tackle, in order. For each, specify: why it is upstream or downstream of the others; how tractable it is for philosophers working without direct access to large-scale empirical labs; what empirical result over the next few years would raise or lower its priority; and which standard anti-realist arguments—e.g., that cross-cultural divergence is best explained by evolutionary contingency rather than tracking moral facts—actually depend on resolving it. In particular, weigh whether they should prioritize (a) examining whether the dominant taxonomy of moral foundations (harm, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity) itself encodes WEIRD conceptual assumptions; (b) distinguishing divergence in first-order moral judgments from divergence in what counts as a moral reason at all; and (c) asking whether moral realism requires convergence at the level of intuition, explicit principle, or institutional practice. Defend your ordering with explicit criteria—importance, tractability, neglectedness, and dependency—so that the deliverable is a strategic allocation of limited research effort, not a bibliography or a list of readings.
