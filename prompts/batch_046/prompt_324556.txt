Consider the layered-incommensurability thesis regarding historical change: the cyclical dynamics Polybius traces in constitutional rotation and Ibn Khaldun traces in dynastic rise and decline operate on a morphological timescale—political institutional turnover across generations, roughly 25 to 50 years—whereas the linear trajectory implicit in Spengler's cultural morphology and Toynbee's challenge-and-response framework operates on an epistemic timescale—normative and technological accumulation across institutional memory, roughly 150 to 300 years. The thesis holds that these are not competing descriptions of one historical process but complementary accounts of different ontological layers observed at different granularities; hence a society can occupy the senescent phase of its institutional cycle while still advancing linearly in normative complexity. Play it straight: what would have to be true for this separation to collapse? Specifically, which empirical patterns—cases where institutional decay directly caused epistemic regression within a single generational span, or where rapid normative innovation accelerated constitutional collapse—would be decisive rather than merely awkward? Could a coupled dynamical model demonstrate that the two layers are necessarily synchronized, or has the thesis been insulated by flexible parameterization of its timescales? I want an honest map of its exposure: what it genuinely risks, what its defenders would concede as damaging, and whether it predicts anything that rival unified accounts do not.
