Please construct and defend the following thesis: there is a structurally distinct form of epistemic akrasia—procedural akrasia—that arises not when higher-order evidence contradicts the content of a first-order belief, but when it undermines the agent's confidence in the normative framework she used to weigh that first-order evidence. In such cases, rationality does not require either downward revision (abandoning the belief) or upward revision (dismissing the higher-order defeat as noise). Instead, rational agency requires adopting a doxastic insulation protocol: the first-order belief is preserved in a suspended, action-quarantined register, while the framework-uncertainty is treated as a meta-level calibration failure rather than as ordinary first-order counterevidence. Defend this protocol against the standard coherence objection—rooted in transparency conditions—that holding belief B while believing one is irrational in holding B is inherently unstable. In particular, address the iterative challenge: what should the agent do when she acquires third-order evidence about the reliability of her own insulation decision? Explain why treating second-order framework-uncertainty as if it were simple evidential defeat commits a category mistake rather than applying total-evidence principles correctly, and show that the insulation protocol survives this recursion without collapsing into self-defeat.
