(1) im with Malcolm: dreams arent experiences in the real sense, because during REM theres no waking subject present to undergo them. its like running a simulation with no user logged in.

(2) but i also think Dennett is right that theres no cartesian theater in waking life either, so the fact that sleep lacks one doesnt by itself show dreams are non-experiential.

(3) i think whats actually going on is predictive processing with the precision weights turned way down — the brain is updating priors with almost no sensory error correction, so theres no successful prediction being made, hence no phenomenal character being generated.

(4) i take post-waking dream reports to be reconstructed confabulations, like filling in a crash log after the fact, not actual memories of experience.

(5) still, i feel its wrong to flat-out tell someone you didnt experience anything because that feels dismissive of something genuinely happening in the brain.

do these hang together? im especially worried that (1) collapses once i grant (2), and that (3) makes it hard to explain why reports in (4) track specific REM brain states so neatly. also, is the tension between (4) and (5) real or just apparent?
