But Dennett's well-known attack on philosophical zombies says there's no coherent notion of a being behaviorally identical to us yet lacking consciousness entirely, which should leave no conceptual space for counterfeits at all. So I'm suggesting that LLMs expose a hidden crack: Dennett might need counterfeit people to remain conceptually possible (to motivate why the intentional stance is a risky bet rather than a guaranteed identification) while also maintaining they're metaphysically impossible (to block zombie arguments). I worry I'm forcing a contradiction he already dissolved in his later work, or that I'm confusing his 1980s framework with his more recent takes on AI and consciousness. Could you comment on whether this framing actually holds? I'm especially unsure if I'm misrepresenting how heterophenomenology is supposed to work when applied to a system that produces reports about its own beliefs without actually holding them, and whether there's a simpler reading that makes the tension disappear.
