Design an in-class activity for a philosophy-of-mind workshop of about 14 participants, 90 minutes, that makes them physically enact Hofstadter's strange loop — the idea that "I" functions as a self-referential symbol within a system, generating the sense of a separate observing self without any non-physical substrate.

To keep the problem from being solved by rote recall, inject the following niche structural constraint: participants must build two layered reference boards — an inner board that assigns symbols to people in the room, and an outer board that assigns symbols to relationships among the inner board's cards — using only physical string connections that are not allowed to cross. They must then place a single "I-card" that belongs on both boards at once, linking the symbol-system back to itself.

The rules and incentives should be arranged so that the group runs into the circular reference physically — tracing arrows and strings that loop back — before anyone names strange loops, self-reference, or Hofstadter. Provide exact rules, materials, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a non-monetary payoff structure (for example, a group-level success condition that proves impossible to satisfy cleanly). Include a debrief that maps specific physical moments — placing the inner cards, attempting the cross-layer string link, the knot that forms — onto corresponding parts of Hofstadter's account. Note what will predictably go wrong the first time (for instance, participants inventing an unseen "observer" outside the boards to break the circle) and how the facilitator should handle it without dissolving the experience. Finally, state frankly which feature of Hofstadter's view — specifically the emergent, distributed neural substrate that physically stabilizes the symbol — this activity necessarily fails to reproduce, and that the facilitator must supply verbally in the debrief.
