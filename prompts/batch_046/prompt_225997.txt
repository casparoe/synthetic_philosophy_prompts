I am preparing a one-semester dissertation prospectus in philosophy of economics and I need a polished proposal addressing a precise conceptual problem at the intersection of exploitation and alienation in Marx. The standard literature treats the two as conceptually separable: exploitation is usually reconstructed as a distributive failure involving the extraction of surplus value through unequal exchange, while alienation is treated as an existential or psychological separation from product, process, species-capacity, and others. Recent analytical reconstructions defend value-transfer accounts that make exploitation independent of alienation, and critical-theory accounts treat alienation in reproductive and affective domains as lacking distributive content altogether. I want the proposal to take up the following sharply stated question: can exploitation retain normative force in contexts where there is no direct labor contract or surplus-value extraction—specifically, in the structural appropriation of reproductive, care, and affective capacities—without either collapsing into alienation or presupposing the labor theory of value?

The proposal should contain five elements. First, state the research question so that it admits a determinate answer within one semester, and explain why existing positions—the analytical value-transfer account and the reproduction-focused critical-theory account—leave the issue genuinely open rather than settled. Second, lay out the proposed conceptual approach: a direct analysis of structural coercion in non-contractual reproductive labor, explaining why this angle is more tractable and more philosophically productive than either an empirical case-study of platform or care economies or a historical-textual exegesis of Marx’s early manuscripts. Third, specify what the deliverable would look like—for example, a reconstructed distinction with explicit conditions of application for exploitation without extraction—and what would count as failure, such as demonstrating that any normative harm in these contexts necessarily reduces to alienation or to a pre-theoretic intuition. Fourth, provide an honest scope assessment: identify which parts of the Marxian corpus and which contemporary debates the project will deliberately set aside, and defend those limits as protecting tractability rather than undermining the result. Fifth, keep the document formal and suitable for a graduate committee; references to named positions are permitted, but do not include a bibliography, citation-based literature review, or requests for reading lists. Write the proposal in formal academic prose.
