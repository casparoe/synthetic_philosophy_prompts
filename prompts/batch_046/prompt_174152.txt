I'm on the Committee for Civic Arts and Public Life at my college and we're meeting Thursday to vote on funding the Civic Mirror Project, an immersive theatre initiative where students improvise scenes drawn from real dorm conflicts and the audience votes on resolutions through an app. The organizers claim it's not theatre in the corrupting sense Rousseau warned about but a civic festival—no paid actors, everyone can participate, it builds collective virtue rather than vanity. I need a short memo to circulate before the meeting recommending a concrete course of action with a clear timeline, and it should definately take the Rousseau framing seriously instead of just dismissing it as old-fashioned moralism. Specifically I'm stuck on whether this actually escapes the critique in Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert: he hated spectacle because it separates actor from spectator, feeds amour-propre through artificial display, and replaces authentic civic feeling with represented emotion. The project technically blurs that line since audience members can jump in, but there's still a performative frame, an app-mediated vote that turns private grievance into peer-judged entertainment, and a real risk that students are performing vulnerability for social validation rather than actually resolving conflict. I'd like the memo to recommend either conditional approval with a tightly bounded pilot and an evaluation milestone, or rejection paired with an alternative communal ritual, and it needs to honestly treat the strongest argument on the other side—that Rousseau's own festivals were about shared labor and celebration rather than staging interpersonal grievance, so maybe this is spectacle disguised as festival. Can you draft that? Make it formal enough to circulate but keep the reasoning transparent. I was also talking with a friend about how modern participatory media creates something like parasocial authenticity—people performing intimacy for an observing crowd that feels participatory—which complicates Rousseau's binary between spectacle and festival, and I'd like that tension mentioned as part of the framing without letting it derail the bottom-line recommendation.
