Write an essay on whether diachronic money-pump arguments genuinely constrain rational preference change, or whether they conflate legitimate updating with exploitable inconsistency. Examine the classic cyclical-reversal pump and also the “stutter-step” variant, in which an agent oscillates between two options across structurally identical decision nodes separated only by a brief, non-instrumental “cooling-off” interval that yields no new information. Weigh the standard defense—that diachronic coherence is required to prevent exploitation—against the objection that agents may legitimately revise preferences in response to internal self-knowledge, provided the revision rule is not outcome-driven. Reach and justify a definite verdict: either that cyclical structures remain irrational even with such intervals, or that the pump collapses once we distinguish outcome-driven cycling from consistency-governed preference revision.
