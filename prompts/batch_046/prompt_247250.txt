I apply this premise repeatedly, and I need a reusable decision procedure—runnable in minutes without philosophical expertise—to determine which downstream patterns are legitimate inductive projections, stable and lawlike within the fiction, and which are grue-like correlations: arbitrary pairings that happen to align with the altered constant but carry no projectible weight and will feel like narrative cheating if I treat them as enduring. Ground the procedure in Goodman's account of projectibility and entrenchment, not as citation but as operational rules: the ordered questions to ask of any proposed consequence, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure returns a firm no and the pattern must be excluded or handled as accidental, and where it hands the decision back to authorial judgment because the issue is one of values—reader trust versus narrative surprise—rather than formal consistency. Include worked applications to three cases: one clearly projectible consequence I can accept (weakened bone density across generations under reduced gravity); one clearly grue-like consequence that I had planned to build a plot around and that the procedure should reject (a specific political ideology dominating because the altered constant's unit of measurement sounds like the ideology's founding term—this is the answer I will not like); and one borderline case where the procedure stalls and judgment is unavoidable (altered photosynthesis rates producing a specific religious iconography). State explicitly what the procedure cannot settle, such as whether entrenchment should be measured against real-world scientific history or against internal fictional consistency alone. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
