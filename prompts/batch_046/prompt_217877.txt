The Heritage and Naming Commission in my town has decided four prior cases as follows. First, they removed a downtown statue of a town founder who designed the original grid but also personally operated a slave-trading post and enforced fugitive-capture laws, on the grounds that the pioneer legacy being honored was inseparable from that commerce. Second, they retained a bridge named after its designing engineer, but added a co-named plaque honoring the displaced workers who built it under coercion and installed a contextual sign; they reasoned that the engineering achievement was distinct from the labor conditions. Third, they entirely renamed an auditorium originally dedicated to a politician's overall public-welfare leadership, because that same leadership included writing and enforcing an ordinance authorizing compulsory institutionalization of disabled residents; the honor could not be limited to his education funding. Fourth, they left unchanged a botanical garden named for a naturalist whose taxonomy was the explicit reason for the honor, despite the fact that the specimens were gathered through the unpaid labor of indigenous guides; they judged the harm indirect and not part of what was being commemorated.

The Commission now considers the municipal library, named in 1995 specifically to honor a local judge's 1972 ruling that established the right to counsel for indigent defendants in our state. The same judge, in 1951, authored the legal opinion that authorized the forced removal of an indigenous community to construct the reservoir that still supplies the city's water. The tribal council requests full renaming, arguing that the reservoir's continued operation makes the honor an active, ongoing structural injury. Others propose keeping the name with an added plaque describing both rulings, noting that the 1995 naming was exclusively for the civil-rights decision.

If you advise the Commission, what should it decide to remain consistent with its own record? Identify the closest prior analog, state whether the precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome, and specify the principle the Commission would be adopting with each option. Please also indicate how confident you are in this recommendation and single out the point about which you are least certain.
