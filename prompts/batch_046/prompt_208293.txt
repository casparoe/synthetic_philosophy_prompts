I'm pitching a small 4-month independent study grant on whether numbers count in rescue ethics, specifically around Taurek's case. My half-baked idea: everyone reads Taurek as saying "aggregation is wrong," but I think the real puzzle is that we have two different pictures of rescue obligations—pairwise duties that conflict, versus one collective duty to reduce harm across a group—and neither side has mapped whether there's logical space in between. So my precise question is: can we construct a rescue ethic that is genuinely number-sensitive in structure—so saving five people isn't just five copies of saving one—without being additive, meaning it doesn't automatically say five lives outweigh one? I think there might be a middle view where multiple victims generate a different kind of obligation, maybe a coordination duty rather than a scaled-up individual duty, but I need to check if that collapses.

Why it's open: Taurek defenders usually just lean on pairwise intuition, and critics usually show aggregation works in other domains; nobody has tried to formalize what a non-additive but non-egalitarian rescue rule would even look like.

Method: month one, reconstruct Taurek's argument using only relational logic—no cardinal utilities—to see exactly where the anti-aggregation move happens. Month two, conceptually map non-utilitarian aggregation rules to see which ones allow structural sensitivity to group size. Month three, build and stress-test a "middle" model against edge cases—1 vs 2, 1 vs 50, identical twins versus strangers—to see if it avoids both linear summation and flat equality. Month four, write up the result. What counts as failure: if the middle model either collapses back into standard summation or ends up treating saving one and saving a hundred as genuinely on par. Scope risk: I could get lost in formal decision theory I dont have the math for, so I'll stick to basic set relations and conceptual formalization rather than actual axiomatization. Deliverable: a 25-page paper with either a sketch of the non-additive structure or an honest negative result showing the logical space is empty.
