I have recently begun a long-term process of medical transition that will alter my body gradually over years—hormonal shifts, surgical revisions, changes in appearance and voice—and I find that the old puzzle of the Ship of Theseus has become unexpectedly personal. If my physical form is replaced piece by piece, do I remain the same person? Rather than a generic philosophical exchange, I would like a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors who apply the puzzle specifically to living human identity, but with a twist that moves well beyond wooden planks: one should defend the view that personal identity through gradual bodily change is preserved by continuous biological functioning—emphasizing metabolism, homeostasis, and the self-repairing dynamics of a living organism rather than static parts—while the other should defend the view that identity is preserved by psychological continuity, specifically by the coherence of a first-person narrative that can survive substantial biological replacement. Each must respond to the other’s actual points: the biological-continuity advocate should press the narrative view on cases of severe dementia or dissociation, where psychology fractures despite intact biology, and ask why an organism’s persistence conditions should outweigh the subject’s own authoritative self-conception; the psychological-continuity advocate should press back with thought experiments involving gradual prosthetic replacement of vital organs or brain transplantation, asking why biological continuity should matter more than the uninterrupted stream of consciousness and memory that the subject experiences as her own. They should acknowledge that living beings grow and repair themselves in ways that make the ship analogy imperfect, neither should be reduced to a strawman, and they should not resolve the dispute with a cheap compromise. Please write this as a rigorous but accessible exchange that takes both the metaphysics of persistence and the lived reality of gradual bodily transformation seriously.
