The dispute over whether an "I" can be distributed across brains—driven by Hofstadter's claim that the self is a replicable strange loop rather than a singular substance—has been stalled for decades. Continuity theorists treat brain-fission as proof that identity requires a unique causal path; pattern theorists treat it as proof that identity is a multiply-instantiable informational structure. Both sides recycle the same split-brain and teletransportation intuitions, and neither concedes ground. Is the impasse substantive—about which causal or informational conditions are necessary for persistence—or is it verbal, with one camp using "persistence" for numerical identity and the other for qualitative similarity?
