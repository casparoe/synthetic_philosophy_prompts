If Descartes were confronted with a contemporary distributed robotic network—individual modules lacking consciousness, biological organs, or a central controller, yet collectively producing adaptive, context-sensitive behavior and novel syntactic combinations—how would his mechanistic account of animals apply? Given his claim that beasts are automata without souls and that human language demonstrates a universal rational substance, would he classify this system as merely an elaborate bête-machine, or would the absence of organic physiology together with the appearance of creative symbol-manipulation force him to revise the link between language-use and the presence of res cogitans? I want a prediction grounded specifically in his arguments about animal consciousness and the nature of mechanism, not an anachronistic projection, together with an assessment of whether his dualist framework can coherently accommodate artificial entities that sit ambiguously between reflexive mechanism and rational expression.
