A philosophy blog offers this summary of the Sleeping Beauty problem and its bearing on self-locating belief. Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday and a fair coin is tossed. If heads, she is woken once on Monday; if tails, she is woken on Monday and Tuesday, with her Monday memories erased between awakenings. When she finds herself awake, what should her credence be that the coin landed heads? The blogger labels the 'halfer' view as the answer one-third: Beauty applies a principle of indifference over her three centred-world locations—Monday-heads, Monday-tails, and Tuesday-tails—treating each awakening as an equally likely position from her first-person perspective. The blogger labels the 'thirder' view as the answer one-half: Beauty's prior for heads was one-half, and since she knew she would be awakened regardless of the outcome, the awakening provides no uncentred evidence about the coin; any update here would be an irrational revision of belief based purely on indexical information. The blogger then claims that the thirder position, if taken seriously, collapses into a general scepticism about all self-locating belief revision—so that finding oneself in a small town rather than a large city could not rationally affect one's beliefs about population distribution—and therefore concludes that only the halfer view preserves coherent rational self-location. The blogger also notes that recent formal-epistemology work models these beliefs not with standard credences but with de Finetti-style betting ratios over centred propositions, weighted by the duration of each conscious episode, and that this framework uniquely vindicates the one-third answer.
