I'm trying to develop this idea that hypocrisy doesn't just flat-out cancel standing to blame; instead it restructures standing into layers. I call the middle layer "reflexive standing": when you're embedded in the same institutional machinery as your target, your blame isn't nullified but redirected inward first, and only then can it legitimately point outward. The carrying case is a university investment committee that publicly condemns a fossil-fuel firm for greenwashing while the endowment still holds shares. The members aren't direct victims, but they're not outsiders either—they're co-occupants of the system they profit from.

Rough arc:
1. Open with the standard view (Cohen, Wallace) that hypocrisy undermines standing.
2. Seperate individual hypocrisy from structural co-occupancy—this is where I think people conflate personal vice with institutional position.
3. Introduce reflexive standing: legitimate blame here has to be self-including.
4. Argue this doesn't excuse the target; it just changes the conditions and tone of blame.
5. End by saying institutional blame without internal reform is perfomative, not false.
