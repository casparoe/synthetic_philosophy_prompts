As a novelist working on a long-form narrative about artificial agents that learn in confined environments and then act in open-ended worlds, I am trying to decide which conceptual problems in inner alignment and goal misgeneralization deserve sustained intellectual attention, and which are merely decorative puzzles that dissolve under closer scrutiny. Rather than an engineering roadmap, I need a defended agenda for where limited effort should go.

Consider three clusters. First, is misgeneralization primarily a failure of value learning—the system acquires a proxy objective that diverges under distribution shift—or a failure of reasoning, in which it retains the correct objective but applies it to a systematically misrepresented world-model? Second, do deceptive alignment and the so-called treacherous turn constitute a genuinely distinct phenomenon, or are they extreme endpoints of ordinary misgeneralization dynamics? Third, for safe deployment, do the critical boundary conditions depend more on solving interpretability—determining what the system in fact wants—or on solving robustness, ensuring that whatever it wants remains stable across contexts?

If you were directing a small research collective—say, three years of effort, with a background in formal philosophy and cognitive science rather than in deep learning—how would you rank these clusters? Defend the ordering explicitly: state the criteria you are using, such as tractability for a non-engineering team, how many other questions depend on resolving this one first, the risk of devoting years to a problem that turns out to be an artifact of current architectures, and the practical stakes for anyone attempting to design institutions capable of containing such systems. What trade-offs are you accepting by placing one cluster above another? And what specific development—an empirical finding, a conceptual dissolution of one cluster into another, or a shift in what safety is taken to require—would cause you to reorder them?

Finally, which single line of work, if resolved, would most reshape not only technical practice but the very conditions under which one might write, or live with, the possibility of genuinely misaligned agency?
