I am drafting a novel with two protagonists who are qualitatively identical—identical speech patterns, identical memories, identical emotional architectures—placed in separate narrative timelines. A reader can distinguish them only by their position in the sequence of chapters, not by any internal feature of either character. I see the resemblance to Black's spheres and to debates over the identity of indiscernibles, but my concern is specifically narrative: whether tracking identity across a text requires an external spatiotemporal index that plays no part in the characters' own properties, and whether that undermines the claim that they are genuinely two rather than one figure repeated. Does this exact configuration—qualitative identity coupled with narrative-trackability through position alone—have an established philosophical treatment, and if so, how does it map onto Black's counterexample and onto Leibniz's principle? If no specific term exists, what precisely distinguishes this case from both ordinary character ambiguity and from the physical spheres case, and where does the boundary lie between a legitimate narrative index and an ad hoc labeling device?
