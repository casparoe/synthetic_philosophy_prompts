The question was: Explain Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument against patterned principels of justice, using the standard example where fans voluntarily pay an extra quarter to watch him play. Then apply it to a modified case: suppose every ticket sale includes an irrevocable smart-contract clause that automatically sends 5% to a public education fund, and fans consent to this term when they purchase. Does this built-in redistribution undermine Nozick's claim that liberty upsets patterns, or can his historical principle accommodate it?

The student answer:
Nozick's Wilt Chamberlain argument shows that liberty always disrupts any patterned distribution. In the example, everyone starts with an equal share—D1 is perfectly equal. Then people freely pay Chamberlain an extra 25 cents to watch him play. Because these transfers are fully voluntary, Chamberlain ends up with far more wealth, producing D2, which is unequal. This proves that maintaining a pattern like equality requires constant interference with free exchange. Nozick calls this "liberty upsets patterns" and says only historical principles—which check whether holdings came about through just steps—are compatible with freedom, not end-state principles that judge distributions by their current shape.

In the smart-contract version, the 5% automatic transfer is still part of voluntary exchange since fans agree to the contract when they buy the ticket. So the chain remains historically legitimate: initial holdings were just, fans made free choices within the contract, and Chamberlain recieves payments subject to those terms. Nozick wouldn't see this as coercive; the redistribution is embedded in the agreement itself. One might worry this reintroduces a pattern, since every transaction produces the same contribution, but Nozick accepts historical patterns as long as they're applied consistently over time. The only real limit is the Lockean proviso, which stops original acquisition from worsening others' situations. Here Chamberlain acquires money through labor and consent, not by taking unowned resources, so the proviso doesn't apply. So the smart-contract case actually strengthens Nozick: even redistribution built into free exchange fits the historical principel without collapsing into an end-state pattern.
