Plutarch writes parallel lives to reveal character through decisive moments, but Kane is literally about a failed biography—reporters try to reconstruct Charles Foster Kane from fragments, each witness offering a contradictory portrait, and the movie refuses to synthesize them into a single moral lesson. So here's the question: does the film dramatize a failure of the Plutarchan method? Where does Kane resist being read as either moral exemplar or cautionary tale, and does that resistance mean biography can't teach morality, or just that it teaches something more complicated?
