Please construct a committed argument addressing the counterfactual question of whether a Nazi Germany—understood specifically as a totalitarian Führer-state with an explicit genocidal trajectory—would have emerged without Adolf Hitler. Defend the synthetic view that the regime's particular form was counterfactually fragile with respect to Hitler's personal agency during the 1933–1934 consolidation window—above all his orchestration of the Night of the Long Knives and the fusion of chancellor and president upon Hindenburg's death—while conceding that the broader collapse of Weimar democracy and the installation of some authoritarian, revanchist regime was counterfactually robust given structural conditions. To do this, rigorously distinguish "enabling conditions" (treaty resentment, economic crisis, Article 48 institutional fragility, elite complicity) from "triggering conditions" (Hitler's specific rhetorical and organizational talents, his calculated norm-violation, and the contingent timing of Hindenburg's death). Your argument must take a definite position: does this distinction ultimately vindicate a modified Great Man theory, reduce it to a generic "critical juncture" model compatible with structuralism, or render both frameworks inadequate? Do not merely lay out options; defend one. Ground your reasoning in the concrete legal and bureaucratic mechanisms through which the chancellorship was transformed into an absolute dictatorship, and specify which of those mechanisms were genuinely substitutable by another right-wing leader in 1933 and which were not. Avoid bibliographies, citations, or appeals to obscure historiography; reason directly from the institutional logic of Weimar's collapse and consolidation.
