I'm trying to build a sorting quiz—more like a flowchart—for a philosophy of knowledge discussion group I'm running next month. We keep arguing about Gettier cases but nobody can say exactly where they disagree, so I want a decision tree that takes you from "do you think Smith knows he got the job?" through maybe four or five concrete branch points, and lands you in one of about six named positions without you ever seeing the label until the end. The questions should be about actual commitments: does the wrong reason for a true belief always block knowledge, or only in high-stakes cases? Is justification a binary thing or can it come in degrees? Do you think the problem is even about knowledge, or is it about how we assign cognitive credit?

Make sure the tree includes leaves for (1) standard JTB-plus-no-false-lemmas, (2) reliabilism, (3) virtue epistemology or credit theories, (4) the view that Gettier cases are misdescribed and Smith actually does know, (5) the view that knowledge isn't a natural kind so there's no single condition to fix, and (6) a collective-knowledge branch—like whether a research lab can have a Gettier-style failure even if no individual member does. Flag any pair your tree can't really seperate, say if reliabilism and virtue epistemology answer all your branch questions the same way. Also note any unoccupied logical combinations, like "accepts the intuition + denies justification matters at all + thinks knowledge is a unified natural kind"—is that incoherent or just unexplored?

Don't ask for citations or a bibliography; the point is that someone could answer the questions without knowing the technical vocabulary, then see which box they fell into. I was talking about this with my roommate and we realised the collective-knowledge branch might be a dead end, but include it anyway. Oh, and try to phrase every node as a concrete case or claim rather than an abstract principle, so people can answer without already knowing which interpretation it leads to.
