(1) The first member says this clearly eliminates any central observer. If consciousness is just competing drafts, with different ones available for report at different moments, then the feeling that there is a single self watching everything is an illusion produced by whichever draft happens to dominate.

(2) The second member replies that even on this view, something must still determine which draft becomes available for speech and memory. He argues that this selection process functions exactly like the audience in the theater: it may not experience the drafts itself, but it decides which one counts as conscious experience.

(3) The first member answers that there is no separate selector — speech and memory systems are themselves just more drafts, and the one that gets expressed is not chosen by a judge but simply dominates through ordinary cognitive competition. She adds that even the feeling of having caught and corrected an error is itself a later draft, not evidence of a real-time comparison happening in one place.

(4) The second member then asks specifically about seeing the Müller-Lyer illusion and immediately recognizing that the lines are actually equal. If there is no central point comparing drafts in the same moment, he asks, how can one draft correct another in real time? He says that if all comparison is retrospective, then Dennett smuggles the theater back in as a hidden comparator whenever he explains immediate self-correction.

Please adjudicate each of the four moves in order: rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify the rating. Then take a definite position on whether the selection mechanism really reintroduces a Cartesian theater, and say clearly what both sides are overlooking — particularly about how retrospective revision relates to real-time experience. Do not merely list options; commit to a specific verdict.
