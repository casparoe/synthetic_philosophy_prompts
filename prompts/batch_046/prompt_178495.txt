Working on a novel about labor and guilt—nothing preachy, just people living it. There's this pattern I keep circling: you have a country that cuts the work week to thirty-five hours, but instead of feeling free people start treating evenings like a productivity exam—language apps, "passive income" hobbies, optimizing sleep. Then there's the other side, like in Japan, where karoshi is real and the families of people who literally work themselves to death recieve condolences that sound almost like medals. So the same culture that legislates rest also makes rest feel like failure, and makes death-by-overwork feel like moral proof. I don't know if that's one phenomenon or two stuck together. Is there an actual term for that specific bind—where reducing labor hours just shifts the work ethic into private life, and extreme overwork gets read as virtue rather than exploitation? Or if not, what should I call it in the book? I don't need a bibliography, just the carving—what it is and where the edges are.
