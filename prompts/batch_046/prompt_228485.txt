I was actually skimming some of Nietzsche’s letters and the parts in Twilight of the Idols where he complains about his stomach, and I noticed a weird pattern: people grab these scattered, contradictary remarks about his own diet—like he was mostly vegetarian but then praised German sausage?—and they spin them into a whole coherent philosophy of eating, as if he were writing a cookbook for the ubermensch.

But the remarks are tied to his migraine’s and his hatred of Wagners cookin, and they don’t form a system at all. Still, readers keep extracting a lifestyle doctrine from a philosopher’s bodily complaints and random food opinions, smoothing over contradictions by saying oh but he meant this in a deeper symoblic way. I don’t know if I’m describing something specific—maybe it’s just bad reading?—but is there an established concept for pulling a prescriptive health or diet ideology out of a thinker’s autobiographical bodily quirks, especially when the thinker never intended one? And how is that different from plain misinterpretation? I feel like there’s a line between reading someone’s work for life advice and this other move where the body becomes the text. Does that distinction have a name in philosophy or literary theory, or should I just coin 'gastric hermeneutics' and move on?
