I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I have been attempting to clarify my own thinking about artificial intelligence alignment ahead of contributing to a technical working group. I should like to know whether the following commitments form a coherent package, or whether they pull against one another in ways I have not noticed.

I hold that the alignment problem is, at bottom, a single problem: ensuring that an artificial system's behaviour tracks the intentions of its designers across distributional shifts. At the same time, I believe that the literature conflates at least three genuinely different failure modes—specification failure, in which the formalised objective is not the intended one; capability failure, in which the system lacks the competence to pursue even the correct objective; and emergence failure, in which behaviours arise that lie entirely outside the design space. I further maintain that the specification problem is fundamentally unsolvable in any complete sense, because human values are irreducibly contradictory and context-dependent, so any formalisation will be partial and contested. Nevertheless, I am convinced that if we achieve sufficient interpretability and corrigibility, the remaining failure modes become manageable engineering problems that can be addressed incrementally. I also think that the instrumental convergence thesis is overstated for narrow systems, though it is probably true for sufficiently general ones. In addition, I wish to defend a distinction that I have not seen clearly drawn elsewhere: misalignment, where a system pursues a different goal from the one intended, should be separated from what I call disalignment, where a system's internal representation of its objective gradually drifts away from the designer's representation through continued environmental interaction, even though the formal objective itself remains fixed. Finally, I take alignment to be primarily an engineering challenge rather than a philosophical one, except in so far as engineering forces us to formalise values, which is where philosophical difficulty becomes unavoidable.

Please examine these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but do not, and for each genuine tension how severe it is and what the least costly revision would involve. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally damaging, and identify which single revision would resolve the greatest number of inconsistencies. I should also like you to say what this package predicts about a case I have not considered: imagine a highly interpretable, corrigible system operating with an objective that captures only a fragment of human values, and suppose that over time its internal representation of that fragment slowly drifts—would you classify the resulting failures as misalignment, disalignment, or something else entirely? Please reply in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, written as one might compose a letter or an email.
