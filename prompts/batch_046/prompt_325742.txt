Comparative philosophers and critics of Confucianism often lean on the family-state analogy: the five cardinal relationships (wulun) are said to scale from the family to the polity like nested organizational charts, with filial piety (xiao) playing the same functional role at home that loyalty (zhong) plays in government. Defenders use it to argue that Confucian authority is affective and relational rather than coercive; critics use it to claim that Confucianism collapses public justice into private patriarchal deference and cannot accommodate universal moral claims that override role obligations.

I want an audit of this analogy's carrying capacity. First, lay out the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the role of the family unit, the parent, the child, the inheritance of status, the domestic sphere, and the scaling mechanism that produces the state? Then identify where the mapping fractures — especially regarding the Confucian practice of remonstration (jian), in which a child or minister must correct a failing parent or ruler, and regarding the doctrine of rectification of names (zhengming), which fixes normative standards for roles independently of the actual occupants' behavior.

Next, separate the breaks that undermine the critics' authoritarian conclusion from those that merely complicate it. Does the analogy smuggle in a modern liberal distinction between private and public that Confucian texts do not recognize? Does it obscure the fact that Confucian roles are constitutively reciprocal — a father is not a father without a child — in ways that organizational charts do not capture?

Finally, say whether the defenders' conclusion — that Confucian authority is inherently relational and therefore non-coercive — survives once the disanalogies are fully mapped, or whether the analogy was carrying that conclusion all along. If there is a better analogy for Confucian relational authority — one that preserves reciprocity and context-sensitivity without importing hierarchical organizational logic — briefly propose it.
