At a philosophy meetup, Maya and Jordan got into it over Parfits teletransporter scenario - the one where they scan your brain, destroy the body, and rebuild you on Mars with the same memories and personality. Maya said the rebuilt person clearly has what matters in survival even if its not technically identical to the original, since psychological continuity is all that counts. Jordan said thats too quick. He pointed to the gradual version where brain cells get replaced one by one over ten years - in that case wed say its the same person, and its not just psychology, its that theres never a moment where the physical body just stops existing.

Maya came back: okay but if you replace one percent a day, is that really different from replacing everything in one second? The only difference is speed, and speed shouldnt matter metaphysically. Jordan said its not about speed, its spatiotemporal continuity - in the gradual case theres always a living body present, in the sudden case theres a dead body and then a new one on Mars. He also brought up the branching problem: if the machine makes two copies, both are psychologically continuous with the original, but identity cant split, so psychological continuity cant be identity.

Maya agreed identity splits badly, but said thats exactly the point - identity isnt what matters. Then she threw out a weird case: what if the scanner sends half the brain-data to Mars, pauses for a whole year while the original is in suspended animation, then sends the rest and rebuilds? She said if psychological continuity is what matters, the year-long gap shouldnt matter - its like being in a coma for a year and waking up. Jordan said no, in a coma your brain is still physically there maintaining continuity, but here theres literally no functioning mind for a year, and the copy is built from stored information like loading a saved file. He said that shows psychological continuity needs some kind of causal-functional chain, not just information preservation.

Maya replied that Jordan is just describing spatiotemporal continuity in fancy terms, and if the gradual cell-replacement counts as survival, then we already admit physical matter gets replaced - so why does the causal chain need to stay in one place? She also said Jordan's saved file analogy assumes the copy is less real, but the copy has all the same mental states. They asked me to referee but I honestly couldnt tell who was landing the punches.
