Is there a name for this? I keep running into this pattern in arguments about freedom. Case one: a worker at the only factory in town can walk out the door anytime—no guards, no contract—but they'd lose healthcare, housing support, and probably be blacklisted everywhere nearby. People say "they're free, nobody's stopping them." Case two: in that same town, if you speak out against the local power structure, suddenly stores won't sell to you, your landlord finds a reason to evict, and employers won't hire you. There's no law behind it, just everyone coordinating. The same people say "you're still free to speak, these are just social consequences, not coercion." But if a government agency kept a public list of critics and employers used it, those people immediately call it domination or structural unfreedom. So they can see the pattern when there's an official badge or office involved, but they go blind when the pressure is spread across neighbors and businesses. I've seen this come up in cross-cultural debates too—people calling state censorship oppressive but treating tribal ostracism as "just how that culture works." Is there an established term for this specific asymmetry—spotting constraint only when it's instutitionalized? I don't just want a bare label; I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is, where its edges are, and how it's different from the usual negative vs republican liberty distinction people talk about. If there's no exact term, can you coin one with a precise definition and some boundary cases? And what's the opposite error—when people see domination in markets but miss it in social norms?
