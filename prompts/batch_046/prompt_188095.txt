Rather than cataloguing them, I want to begin with a specification: what must any genuinely adequate reply to Galen Strawson's Basic Argument actually achieve in order to count as having answered it, not merely changed the subject? Please treat this as a desiderata exercise. Set out the requirements that a theory of moral responsibility must satisfy to survive the regress Strawson identifies — including, but not limited to, the conditions under which an agent can be said to be self-caused, the distinctions that must be preserved between being the cause of one's character and being merely the locus of its effects, and the formal properties a regress-stopper must possess if it is not to presuppose precisely the kind of uncaused causation that the argument rules out. For each desideratum, provide the concrete case or thought-experiment that motivates it, and flag any pair you suspect may be jointly unsatisfiable. Then identify the single requirement that, in your judgement, eliminates the largest family of standard positions — for instance, those that attempt to halt the regress by appealing solely to present capacities. After the list, include a brief comparative sketch of how the 'historical' reply (that agents are partly self-creating over an extended biography) and the 'local' reply (that present rational capacities are sufficient regardless of origin) fare against your criteria, but keep this to a concise scorecard rather than a full evaluation. I am working without access to external sources, so please do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading. Structure your response in the following sections, in this order and with these approximate lengths: first, 'The core regress and what must be blocked' (around 200 words); second, 'The desiderata with motivating cases' (around 350 words); third, 'Joint unsatisfiability and the knockout requirement' (around 200 words); fourth, 'Brief scorecard: historical versus local replies' (around 150 words); and finally, 'Bottom line for my paper' (no more than two sentences).
