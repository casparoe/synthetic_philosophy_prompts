I'm writing a family history explaining why my grandparents' restaurant closed in 1992. That year, a regional recession sharply reduced customer spending, a new highway diverted traffic away from their street, and my grandfather developed an illness that made day-to-day management unreliable. Each factor seems sufficient on its own to have caused the failure, yet all three occurred together. I want to explain this to relatives without falsely implying that one factor was clearly primary, but I also don't want the account to feel like an unstructured list. How should I think about whether all three genuinely contribute to the explanation, or whether some are redundant given the others, and is there a principled way to distribute narrative weight among them without misrepresenting their causal roles?
