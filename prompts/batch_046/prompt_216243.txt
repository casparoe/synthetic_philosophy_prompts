In particular, clarify why the expected value of the game remains undefined for every value of θ except possibly at a single boundary point, yet dominance reasoning seems to recommend playing for some values of θ and refusing for others. Your explanation should distinguish between the arithmetic failure of the expectation sum to converge—due to the alternating-sign, unbounded terms—and the separate normative failure of pairwise dominance arguments to yield a uniform recommendation across the parameter space. Additionally, address whether the discontinuity in rational recommendation at the boundary point is an artifact of inserting a continuous belief parameter into a discrete payoff structure, or whether it reveals a deeper instability in how expected-utility frameworks handle undefined expectations when belief revision is treated as continuous.
