draft a joint adversarial-collaboration statement between the "myth-scale" camp (the Harari-style view that shared fictions are the real engine letting sapiens cooperate past Dunbar-scale, with the cognitive revolution as a meaningful breakpoint) and the "discontinuity" camp (the Graeber-Wengrow-style critics who say grand narratives smuggle in teleology and that pre-agricultural societies were politically varied rather than uniformly hierarchical).

structure it like this: first a numbered list of claims both sides can actually sign — about what the archeological record uncontroversially shows, about the limits of inferring social structure from material remains, about whether Dunbar's number is a hard ceiling or just a correlation that needs explaining. then a disagreements table with the exact wording of each camp's position in terms thier own side accepts. then lay out the cruxes. i think the big ones are (1) whether "fictional coherence" is a genuine causal driver of scaling or just a post-hoc redescription of whatever happened to work, and (2) whether small-scale egalitarianism was an active political choice or just a default that collapses automatically once storage and surplus appear.

for each crux, pick one concrete case or piece of evidence both agree bears on it — maybe Göbekli Tepe, maybe the shift from oral tradition to cuneiform in early Mesopotamia — and record in advance what each camp predicts that evidence should show if they are right.

also, inject this niche framing: the "mnemonic durability hypothesis," which claims that the stability of a collective fiction depends more on the physical durability of its recording medium (clay, stone, human memory, etc.) than on the content or plausibility of the fiction itself. ask both sides whether adopting this framing changes thier predictions about the transition to state-level societies, or whether it's just a red herring that doesn't touch the real dispute.

keep it neutral, don't adjudicate who's right, and don't ask for bibliographies or citations — just reasoning from shared premises. write it casually, like notes for a working group that hasn't met yet.
