Franklin's moral perfection project relied on a private grid in which he marked daily failures against thirteen virtues, each governed by a brief precept. Rather than asking about the virtues in general, consider the epistemic problem of self-scoring: some failures leave observable traces, while others are visible only to the agent—or can be rationalized away. Rank the following five virtues—Temperance, Silence, Order, Industry, and Humility—by how structurally resistant they are to honest self-recording in Franklin's grid (most resistant to self-deception first, least resistant last). For each virtue, state in one sentence whether its failure mode is primarily externally observable, internally hidden, or ambiguously both, and relate that distinction to Franklin's own precept for that virtue. Defend the overall ordering: why should the virtue you place at the top be harder to fake in the ledger than the one at the bottom?
