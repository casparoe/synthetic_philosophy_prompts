everyone in my philosophy of mind group keeps saying symbol grounding is like trying to learn french from a french-french dictionary. the claim is that if every definition points only to other words in the same language, you never break into the actual world, so an LLM predicting tokens is stuck in the same loop and never gets real meaning. people use this to argue there's necessarily some non-computational, world-contacting step -- like a native speaker pointing at a baguette -- that pure statistics can't replace.

i want to audit this properly. give me the correspondance table: what's the dictionary, the learner, the missing speaker, the baguette, and what plays the role of the outside world that breaks the loop? then where does it break? does an LLM have anything like the dictionary-user's prior embodied experience, or is it genuinely more trapped? and here's what's bugging me: what about someone born blind who learns 'red' entirely through spectral correlations and uses it correctly? if that counts as grounded, then statistical correlation with physical measurements might be enough, and the dictionary analogy was carrying the whole conclusion. if it doesn't count, then the analogy holds but we need to say exactly what's missing. does the conclusion -- that grounding requires direct sensory interaction -- survive these disanalogies, or was it smuggling in assumptions about human cognition?

if the dictionary picture really collapses, is there a better analogy? maybe navigation without a map, or is that just as bad?
