I sit on this small city heritage board and we're constantly getting petitions about renaming streets or moving statues out of the plaza. There's like six of us, we meet every other tuesday, and we have maybe twenty minutes per case before people start yelling. I need a decision proceedure I can literally run in that room — step by step — to decide whether we keep, relocate, recontextualize, or remove a commemorative object.

The idea I want to operationalize is that every commemorative act has two layers: an honorific payload (the praise being delivered) and an indexical anchor (the specific person or event being pointed at). Public debate usually smushes these together. So the checklist should separate them, ask whether each layer is still justified, and include a quick ritual vitality check to see if the object is functioning as a living honor or just a dead historical marker — with clear pass/fail conditions. For each case, the procedure should ask ordered questions, flag exactly where the answer is a fact and where it's a value judgment that needs a human vote, and tell me where to stop with a hard no versus where to hand it back to the board because the issue isn't factual anymore. Apply it to three actual proposals on our docket: (1) a statue of a local industrialist who funded a hospital but also ran a factory that used prison labor in the 1920s, (2) renaming a street called Confederate Way that hasn't been on official maps since 1987 but still shows up in old GPS databases, and (3) adding a counter-monument plaque next to a colonial explorer statue that we previously voted to keep in place. Make sure one of these comes out technically correct according to the procedure but in a way I'll probably hate, and tell me why. Also be honest about what this can't do — like if half the board thinks historical harm is present harm and the other half doesn't — and don't bother with a bibliography or reading list since none of us will read it. Just give me the printed checklist with the worked examples.
