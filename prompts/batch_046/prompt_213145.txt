I have spent the last two years reading and thinking on my own about why some people end up poor in old age despite having worked hard all their lives, and I have arrived at a view I call the responsibility window approach, which borrows ideas from luck egalitarianism but applies them specifically to retirement-age poverty and workplace-health risks. In brief: a society should treat an outcome as undeserved — and therefore appropriate for collective compensation — whenever it stems from a choice made outside a window of adequate information, available alternatives, and absence of coercion. If someone chose a physically destructive trade at age eighteen without being told about the long-term health costs, and without realistic access to safer training, then their poverty at seventy is brute luck rather than a failure of personal responsibility. If they made the same choice with full disclosure and real alternatives, then the outcome is partly on them.
