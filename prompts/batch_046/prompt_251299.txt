I'm asking you to draft the opening address for the inaugural session of the River Basin Democratic Council, a deliberative body whose charter grants full voting seats to resident citizens and weighted advisory mandates—without decisive authority—to long-term non-citizen residents, proxy representatives for future generations, and human stewards speaking for the watershed itself. Roughly half your audience will be political theorists and constitutional lawyers who will recognize every move in the democratic-boundary literature; the other half will be tribal council members, migrant advocates, and local residents for whom membership in "the people" is an immediate practical question rather than an abstraction. Write a six-minute address in which the boundary problem—who belongs to the demos—does genuine structural work: it should determine what you can legitimately promise, what you must leave deliberately open, and how you address the room as "we" without resolving the closure question by fiat. The council's hybrid voting-advisory structure should appear not as a neat administrative compromise but as an institutionalized tension between the all-affected and all-subjected principles, one that risks reproducing the very exclusion democracy claims to solve. The address must be fully accessible to listeners without philosophical training, yet precise enough that an expert hears exactly which version of the closure problem is being navigated and why the scalar membership design is philosophically unstable. Do not provide citations, a bibliography, or false consolation.
