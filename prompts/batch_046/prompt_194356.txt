I work for a regional science endowment board and we keep recieving proposals that basically say "we've already sunk $4M into this, don't kill it now." I need a reusable decision procedure — think flowchart in words — that my review panel (scientists and accountants, no philosophers) can run in about 20 minutes, sign off on in writing, and apply consistently. The principle behind it is that honoring sunk costs isn't always the fallacy people think it is: sometimes there's a foward-looking salvage reason, sometimes there's a backward-looking commitment reason (the prior spend created promises or obligations to partners or participants), and sometimes it's about diachronic consistency — not undermining your own long-term agency by bailing every time things get hard. But I also want the procedure to be honest about where rationality ends and values begin.

So write me the ordered checklist: the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient yes/no at each step, where the procedure stops with a clear "terminate funding," and where it stops and hands the decision to the director because it's no longer a rationality question but a values call. Flag the hard cases where the procedure will misfire or where two panelists could answer the same question differently. Then run it on three proposals:

1. A deep-sea sensor network that's 90% over budget but now supplies irreplaceable climate-model data.
2. A heritage-language digitization project using obsolete tech, where stopping means breaking explicit promises to elder contributors who donated materials on the condition we'd finish.
3. A biotech trial that missed its primary endpoint but claims the "institutional knowledge" built up justifies continuing.

Make sure one of these gives an answer my board probably won't like — i.e., the procedure says keep funding despite ugly economics. And tell me explicitly what the procedure flat-out can't do, including where it might kill something good or save something bad. Keep it practical; don't ask me to read Bratman or anything, just build the idea into the steps.
