I'm preparing my position paper for our graduate seminar in epistemic rationality, and I want to check whether my commitments are jointly tenable before I defend them in front of the group. Here is the profile I intend to present:

First, I subscribe to accuracy-first epistemology: the sole fundamental epistemic value is doxastic accuracy—having true beliefs and avoiding false ones—while other goods such as understanding or explanatory coherence derive their worth entirely from serving that end. Second, I accept that epistemic utility theory should be structured decision-theoretically, evaluating belief-formation through proper scoring rules and treating rational inquiry as the maximization of expected epistemic utility. Third, I endorse the standard decision-theoretic result that, for an agent updating by conditioning on correct priors, the expected value of acquiring free information is always non-negative; more evidence cannot reduce expected accuracy.

Fourth, despite that result, I want to defend the claim that there are "epistemically toxic" contexts—highly polarized or manipulative information environments—where receiving further evidence systematically lowers an agent's actual accuracy due to motivated reasoning and framing effects, which means rational agents ought sometimes to refuse further inquiry. Fifth, I maintain that phenomenal knowledge—knowing what an experience is like—possesses a special epistemic status that cannot be fully captured by doxastic accuracy metrics, though I insist this does not undermine accuracy-firstism because such knowledge lies outside the belief-forming domain that epistemic utility theory governs. Sixth, I hold that epistemic utility should be computed using the agent's actual subjective credences rather than an intersubjective or objective baseline, since epistemic norms apply to individual reasoning processes rather than to idealized communal standards.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe is each real conflict? For every genuine tension, specify the least costly revision and exactly what philosophical ground I would have to surrender. Then tell me which single commitment, if abandoned, would resolve the greatest number of conflicts, and whether that trade-off is worth accepting for the sake of consistency.
