As a retired physicist who has lately turned to philosophy of time without keeping close track of the specialist literature, I would like a consistency audit of the position I have arrived at. I hold the following six commitments: (1) presentism is correct—only present entities genuinely exist; (2) special relativity is approximately true, and there is no absolute frame of simultaneity; (3) the moving-spotlight theory offers the best reconciliation of (1) and (2), since it permits a privileged present without requiring a privileged spatial hyperplane; (4) macroscopic objects persist through time by having temporal parts—that is, I accept perdurantism rather than endurantism; (5) causation is irreducibly A-theoretic: for one event to cause another, the cause must lie in an objectively earlier "now," not merely in a relational before-relation; and (6) eternalism should be rejected in part because, if all times are equally real, there is no metaphysical ground for the openness of the future. I am unsure whether this package is jointly tenable. Specifically, does (4) implicitly commit me to eternalism, or is presentist perdurantism genuinely coherent? Does (3) actually supply the kind of objective, global present that (5) requires, given relativistic simultaneity? And does my reason in (6) sit well with (4), or does it presuppose an ontology I have already abandoned? Please rank the tensions by severity, distinguish pairs that are only apparently incompatible, identify the least costly revision for each genuine conflict, and tell me which single commitment, if dropped, would do the most to restore coherence. I am not particularly attached to any one of these views; I simply want to know what my present set actually commits me to.
