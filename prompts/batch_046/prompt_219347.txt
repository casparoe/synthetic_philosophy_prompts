I work in community mediation, and I have kept noticing the same move when people face a choice between goods that seem to belong to entirely different categories—preserving an inherited home for family continuity versus selling it for financial security, or keeping personal artistic standards versus reaching a wider audience. Rather than weighing the options and accepting real loss on each side, or finding a middle ground, they often reframe one value so that it secretly requires the other: the house must be sold in order to truly honor continuity by funding the children’s education, or artistic independence can only be protected by embracing commercial success. The original tension does not get balanced; it gets dissolved by turning one good into an instrument of the other. This is not ordinary compromise, because nothing is actually surrendered, and it does not feel like simple self-deception, since the people involved seem sincerely persuaded by the new framing. Does this pattern have an established name in philosophy of value or decision theory? If so, how is it distinguished from rationalization, from the idea that values can be harmonized through practical wisdom, and from cases where two goods genuinely turn out to be compatible? If there is no precise term, what definition and boundary conditions would capture it, particularly to separate it from authentic integration of values and from outright denial of cost?
