Write the desiderata that a unified theory of rational belief must satisfy to count as genuinely resolving both the lottery paradox and the preface paradox, rather than dissolving one by sacrificing the intuitions behind the other. The account should apply to an agent who can hold credences over independent, externally determined outcomes (like lottery draws) and also hold reflective beliefs about the reliability of her own reasoning processes (like an author surveying a long manuscript).

For each desideratum, specify: the phenomenon or structural distinction it protects; whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp; the motivating case (lottery, preface, or a cross-cutting structural point); and which mainstream responses—threshold-based, contextualist, high-credence Bayesian, or knowledge-first—fail to meet it, with the exact point of failure.

In particular, your list must include requirements that:

1. preserve the asymmetry between aggregating beliefs about independent random processes and aggregating beliefs about one's own systematically correlated errors;
2. allow an agent to rationally withhold belief that any specific ticket will win, and also rationally withhold belief that all tickets will lose, without imposing a single fixed acceptance threshold that applies identically across both domains;
3. allow an agent to rationally accept each individual claim in a large body of work while also rationally accepting that at least one claim is erroneous, without treating the latter acceptance as a straightforward conjunction of the negations of the former;
4. do not collapse epistemic modesty about one's reasoning capacities into statistical confidence about random processes, and explain why adding a "modesty clause" in the preface case does not analogously resolve the lottery case;
5. respect the distinction between distributive rationality (what holds case by case) and holistic rationality (what holds over the set), without allowing either to be reduced to the other.
