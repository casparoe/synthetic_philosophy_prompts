Imagine a "gradual transporter": over ten years every neuron in your brain is swapped for a synethetic analog with identical input-output behavior, and the old tissue is dissolved, but it's done so slowly that there's never a moment where more than 0.1% is new at once. By year ten you're running on 100% synthetic substrate inside the original skull, with uninterrupted consciousness and perfect psychological continuity. What's your credence that the pre-replacement person is literally the same subject of experience as the post-replacement one?

Don't just say "it depends on your view of identity" — I want an actual number between 0 and 1. Then decompose the reasoning: (a) the probability that psychological continuity alone is sufficient for survival regardless of substrate, (b) the probability that doing it gradually eliminates whatever discontinuity problem the instant-scanning version has, (c) whether physical continuity of the cranial container carries any independant weight, and (d) whether there's even a determinate fact of the matter here or if it's all verbal. Give rough probabilities for each, show how they combine, and tell me which term you're least confident in and what evidence — maybe actual gradual neural prosthetic trials — would shift it.

Lead with your bottom-line number in the very first sentence, then the decomposition, no preamble and don't restate my question back at me.
