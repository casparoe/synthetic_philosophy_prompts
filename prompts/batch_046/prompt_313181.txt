I'm putting together a session for a reading group of CS grad students who mostly think consciousness is just information processing, and I want them to actually sit with the problem in mind uploading rather than just nodding. The idea is gradual replacement: every neuron gets swapped for a synthetic equivalent during sleep, one at a time, and each morning the subject writes a one-sentence diary entry answering 'am I still the same person from yesterday?' By full replacement every entry still says yes, but an outside researcher notices the grammatical subject slowly drifts from 'I' to 'this system' without the writer ever noticing. I need a teaching plan, not the explanation itself. What's the dependancy chain — what concept has to be in place before this case even bites? What's the carrying example (probably not Ship of Theseus, since they already know that) and what does it need to have and lack? Then the misconceptions in the order they'll come up: substrate independence doesn't matter, functional identity is enough, the diary proves continuity, maybe the drift is just a language quirk. Where in the arc do I head each off? What's the check at the end that proves they've felt the ambiguity rather than just picked a team? And what do I leave out at this level — maybe the hard problem, maybe the classic teleportation case — and explicitly say 'not today'?
