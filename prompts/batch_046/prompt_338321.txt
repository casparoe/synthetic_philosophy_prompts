I'm a phd student working on mental representation and I've been running a small reading group where we've been stuck for months on whether teleosemantics actually naturalizes mental content or just relocates the normativity problem somewhere else. One side — roughly the Millikan-style biosemantic view — says evolutionary selection history fixes representaional content because misrepresentation is just malfunction defined relative to proper function. The other side says that's circular: you already need some intentional stance to read evolutionary history as being 'for' a particular outcome, so you've smuggled in the very thing you wanted to explain.

We all agree on the relevant biology, we all know the formal arguments, and yet nobody has moved an inch. My working theory is that part of the stall comes from talking past each other about 'function' — one camp means selected-effect function, the other means current causal contribution to fitness — but I'm not sure if that's the real problem or just a symptom. Theres also this hidden issue about detached representations: beliefs about black holes, modal truths, counterfactual conditionals, where there's no obvious selection history. The biosemanticists usually say these are derived from more basic representational capacities, but the critics treat that derivation claim as exactly where the account collapses. So is the disagreeement actually hiding there?

What I want is a diagnosis, not another survey. Is this a substantive disagreement about what representation is, a verbal disagreement about what 'naturalize' requires, or are the two sides answering different questions — one metaphysical, one explanitory? Or is there a hidden empirical question about whether representations with no current biological utility actually exist in the way the critics claim? Given whatever diagnosis you give, tell me what would actually count as progress: a sharper distinction in the concept of function, a convincing derivation story for detached content, or an argument that the whole framing is ill-posed? And be honest about whether anything — a new case, a formal result, a conceptual distinction — could actually end the stall, or if we're just stuck with bedrock intuitions.
