Analyze the fall of the Berlin Wall in Nov 1989 through the hinge-of-history lens—basically the idea that long-run structures are mostly path-dependent inertia punctuated by brief "hinge" windows where a tiny perturbation locks in a radically different trajectory for decades. Focus on the Schabowski press conference on Nov 9: he flubbed the wording on travel rules, crowds hit the checkpoints, and border guards eventually opened the gates. Map it precisely—who the actual decision nodes were (Schabowski, the local guard commanders, the Soviet advisors in the background), what the real option set looked like in that hour, and what counterfactual paths each option would have locked in (delayed opening, local violence, a Soviet-backed crackdown?). Then be honest about where the hinge model breaks: was reunification already overdetermined by autumn 1989 given the Hungarian border opening and the Monday protests, so Nov 9 was just an accelerant rather than a true hinge? Introduce the idea of "counterfactual stability"—that a real hinge requires the alt path to persist, not just diverge briefly—and ask whether that condition is met here. Compare the hinge reading to a gradualist model that says the Wall comes down eventually regardless, and say whether this episode confirms the theory or just shows it can't distinguish a hinge from a speed bump.
