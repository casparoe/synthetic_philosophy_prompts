The problem of verisimilitude concerns how false scientific representations—models, theories, or simulations—can nevertheless approximate truth more closely than competing false representations. Rather than surveying existing literature, I want a defended research agenda. Specifically, focus on the under-explored tension between representational verisimilitude—the degree to which a model's internal ontology, causal structure, or mechanistic commitments mirror reality despite being strictly false—and functional verisimilitude—the degree to which the model yields accurate predictions, robust interventions, or transferable inferences regardless of its ontological fidelity. Many current debates treat these as competing measures; your task is to prioritise the open questions that arise from trying to hold them together.
