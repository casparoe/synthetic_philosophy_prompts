The republican-liberty debate — Pettit and Skinner saying freedom is non-domination, Berlin saying that's just negative liberty with extra conditions — has been running since the 90s with the same recyled examples (the benevolant slave, the at-will worker). My working theory is that it's partly verbal: they use "interfernce" differently, and partly they're answering different questions, one about institutional design and one about the phenomenolgy of choice. But lately there's this framing that republican freedom is really about "exit-cost asymmetry" — whether leaving a power relation is cheap relative to the other party's loss — and neither side touches it. Is the stall mainly conceptual, or is there a hidden empirical claim about human psychology or institutional stability doing the work? And if it's genuinely stuck, what would actually move it — a formal distinction, a change of question, something else? Separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and don't present one side as the consensus.
