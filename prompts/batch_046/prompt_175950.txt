Most animal welfare laws ban unnecessary suffering for vertebrates, but they carve out an exception for standard farm practices like debeaking chickens or castrating piglets without pain releif. What view of animal consciousness or moral status is that exception actually presupposing? Is it saying farm animals don't suffer in the relevant way, that economic need trumps suffering, or something about instutional inertia? Reconstruct the implicit theory, check if it's consistant with the rest of the law, and say what a policy built on behavioral-complexity-based consciousness (instead of species lines) would have to change. I keep seeing people treat this like simple hypocrisy but I think the philosophy is weirder.
