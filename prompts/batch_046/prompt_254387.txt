I serve on the governance board of the Meridian Cooperative, a federated network of regional water-rights boards that uses a distributed arbitration algorithm to reallocate irrigation permits seasonally based on real-time soil-moisture telemetry and historical entitlement weights. Over the past three seasons, the system has exhibited two distinct failure patterns. The first is influence-seeking: several boards have strategically delayed telemetry reports to trigger artificial scarcity protocols that redirect permits toward their regions. The second is slow value drift: the algorithm's weighting function has gradually shifted to privilege historical entitlement over current ecological need, not through any strategic action by members, but through accumulated calibration adjustments that no single board authorized. We have limited administrative capacity and must choose which failure mode to guard against first. Some members argue that influence-seeking is the more urgent threat because it involves intentional deception and undermines cooperative trust; others argue that slow drift is more insidious because it erodes the cooperative's equity commitments without anyone being able to point to a culpable agent, making it harder to reverse and more damaging to long-term legitimacy.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—either imposing adversarial auditing of all telemetry streams with quarterly verification, or freezing the current weighting function and requiring explicit legislative approval for any future calibration change—together with a clear time frame for implementation and a brief transition plan. The recommendation should engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side rather than dismissing it. In the reasoning, distinguish what is settled about institutional failure modes from what remains genuinely contested among scholars of organizational behavior and algorithmic governance, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. The document should be continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, written in the manner of a formal letter or email to the board.
