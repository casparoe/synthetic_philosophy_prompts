I've been covering science and ideas for years, but lately I've been sitting in on philosophy seminars and getting weirdly obsessed with whether Popper's falsifiability thing is actually a sharp line or just a slogan people repeat because it sounds good. I was arguing with a friend over coffee—well, mostly I was rambling and they were nodding—about specific cases, and I realized my gut verdicts don't line up neatly. So here they are, and I want to know if there's one principle behind them, or if I'm just being inconsistant.

Case one: Einstein predicting gravitational lensing years before anyone saw it—that feels like science at its best and confirms the theory in a big way. Case two: a psychoanalytic framework that explains a patient's anger and also the absence of anger by the exact same unconscious mechanism—feels like pseudoscience, unfalsifiable junk. Case three: string theory's landscape, where you can dial parameters to match pretty much any cosmological constant we observe—part of me wants to call that science slipping away, but another part thinks it's still physics because the math is rigorous and maybe we'll eventually find a selection principle. Case four: a climate model updated with a new cloud-feedback coefficient right after the 2023 heat spike, but that coefficient was already being tested in a separate lab study—this feels okay, not ad hoc, still legitimate science. Case five: a "quantum consciousness" startup claiming every brain scan result confirms their theory because consciousness collapses the wavefunction in a unique way that just happens to match the data—this feels like case two, though I'm less sure because quantum mechanics is genuinely strange.

There's also this idea I ran across in a recent preprint—someone proposed "computational tractability" as the real demarcation line, not falsifiability—but I don't know if that actually fixes anything. Is there a coherent account of science vs. pseudoscience that gets all five verdicts? If not, which one should I drop? And what does your best principle predict for a case I haven't listed—say, a dark-matter model that invents a brand-new particle every time a galaxy rotation curve misbehaves, versus one that predicts a specific cross-section we'll either see at the LHC next year or not?

Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each just a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Also say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you're least sure about. And please—no citations or reading lists, I don't have access to check them.
