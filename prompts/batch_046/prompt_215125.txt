I'm a PhD candidate in philosophy of medicine and science studies, and I'm preparing the Q&A portion of my job-market seminar next week in a department that houses both clinical psychiatrists and philosophers of science. The thesis I am defending introduces a modification to Ian Hacking's framework of looping effects—what I call institutional anchoring—according to which a psychiatric interactive kind can only undergo genuine classificatory looping when it lacks deep embedding in bureaucratic infrastructures such as insurance billing codes, disability-eligibility determination rules, and special-education funding protocols. I apply this to the DSM-5's 2013 consolidation of Asperger's disorder into the broader autism spectrum disorder category: despite sustained identity-based resistance from self-identified Aspies and parent-advocacy networks that looks structurally identical to the looping reactions Hacking describes in cases like multiple personality disorder, the category did not split back out because Medicaid reimbursement structures and school-district assessment protocols had already locked ASD into a single administrative node. On my view, looping was present at the level of social identity but institutionally blocked at the level of classification, and this reveals a bureaucratic filter that Hacking's original account of interactive kinds does not capture.

The audience will be split among philosophers of medicine who regard psychiatric taxonomy as pragmatically constructed but ontologically neutral, clinical psychiatrists who see DSM revisions as purely heuristic adjustments with no feedback onto patient self-understanding, and science-studies scholars intimately familiar with Hacking's work on child abuse and dissociative disorders who worry that applying looping to bureaucratic taxonomies stretches the concept beyond its intended domain. Please draft my prep sheet: the eight or so objections I will actually receive, ordered by the damage they would do to the institutional-anchoring claim and phrased in the specific vocabulary each subgroup uses—epistemic, clinical, and Hackingian—together with a concise reply I can deliver in roughly two minutes and an explicit note on where each reply is weakest or concedes too much. I also want you to identify the one objection I am hoping nobody raises—specifically, the argument that if institutional anchoring is as pervasive and rigid as my thesis requires, then looping effects become empirically marginal and Hacking's framework collapses from a general model of psychiatric kinds into a rare special case, which would defeat the theoretical contribution my dissertation claims to make.
