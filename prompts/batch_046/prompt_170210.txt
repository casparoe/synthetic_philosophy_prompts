Construct an argument that evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics misfire when applied to second-order normative commitments—specifically, to the belief that moral reasons possess categorical authority over agents—rather than to first-order moral judgments. Proceed in three stages. First, distinguish the evolutionary explanation of particular moral contents (for instance, aversions to unprovoked harm or norms of reciprocal exchange) from the evolutionary explanation of the cognitive architecture that treats certain considerations as non-optional reasons. Second, argue that if multi-level selection favors groups whose members internalize deontic constraints as coordination-stabilizing devices, then the evolutionary etiology of this architecture is compatible with a tracking relation between the architecture and genuinely normative properties—namely, the property of being a stable equilibrium in sustained cooperative interaction—without requiring direct perceptual access to non-natural moral facts. Third, show that the debunker who invokes evolutionary etiology to undermine moral justification must either apply the same reasoning to the epistemic norms that govern rational belief revision, thereby generating a self-defeating skeptical loop, or invoke an unmotivated asymmetry that protects epistemic norms while leaving moral ones exposed. Conclude that the debunker can avoid this dilemma only by conceding that evolutionary origin is not by itself sufficient for epistemic defeat, and therefore that evolutionary debunking fails against any metaethical view—realist or constructivist—that treats deontic authority as an emergent feature of socially sustained coordination mechanisms rather than as a directly selected representational content. Do not appeal to named texts or authors; build the reasoning from the structure of tracking explanations, the distinction between causal origin and justificatory status, and the conditions under which evolutionary selection can stabilize normative frameworks without reducing them to instrumental preferences.
