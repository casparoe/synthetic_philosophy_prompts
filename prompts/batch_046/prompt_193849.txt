I run a graduate seminar in epistemology with about ten students, meeting for ninety minutes in a seminar room with movable chairs. I want an in-class simulation—rules, roles, materials, and a brief payoff scheme using only reputation points—that makes the students feel the pull of luminosity and then feel it collapse when the KK principle is pressed against a gradually shifting mental state, before either concept is named. Students may declare aloud only one of three epistemic statuses—“I know,” “I do not know,” or “I know that I know”—and these declarations are visible to all and feed into a collective scoreboard, but students never see one another’s cards. The hidden cutoff for actually knowing is fixed at level 5, though this is never announced. The rules should be arranged so that in the middle rounds there is a predictable cluster of students at levels 4 and 5 who sincerely disagree about whether they are in a position to know, and a second cluster who declare “I know that I know” at level 5 but, if the KK principle holds strictly, would need to be at level 6 for that second-order declaration to be true, thereby generating the tension without anyone naming it.
