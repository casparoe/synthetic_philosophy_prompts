i'm a phd student in philosphy of science, and i keep running into this weird institutional pattern. in high-energy physics and cosmology, a paper can propose that the cosmological constant or higgs mass is explained by a multiverse landscape with something like 10^500 vacua, with no unique prediction for the next collider run, and still get published and funded if it shows internal consistency and invokes anthropic selection. but if a condensed-matter theorist tried the exact same logical move — saying "there are 10^500 crystal structures, ours just happens to exist, hence this material property" — it'd be dismissed as unfalsifiable garbage. so there's an implicit exemption: the same logical structure gets treated as progress in one field and as pseudoscience in another.

what view of theory confirmation does that practice presuppose? does it presuppose that explanatory breadth plus mathematical coherence can substitute for empirical risk, or can a standard falsificationist account accommodate it by labeling the multiverse part as metaphysics? is the practice even philosophically consistent with itself, or does it quietly rely on two different epistemologies? and if we replaced the underlying view with a strict predictive-empiricist standard, what exactly would have to change in peer review and grant evaluation — not in principle, but in the actual practice? i'm also wondering whether the practice is underdetermind: could a bayesian with extreme priors on mathematical elegance license the same exemption, or does it require something stronger like a non-empirical confirmation criterion?
