On this reading, Goethe supported the Zollverein customs union not as a step toward German political unification but as a model of economic federalism that avoided centralization; he accepted office under Duke Carl August mainly as a form of protective withdrawal that kept him away from revolutionary politics; and in the Maximen und Reflexionen he treats enlightened absolutism not as a positive ideal but as a temporary shelter within which individual cultivation might survive. From these premises my colleague concluded that Goethe was essentially an aristocratic liberal who rejected democratic constitutionalism because he believed collective decision-making could never substitute for personal moral formation, and that he dismissed nation-state projects altogether—reportedly telling Eckermann that political nationalism was a bureaucratic fiction that would crush the universal human spirit.
