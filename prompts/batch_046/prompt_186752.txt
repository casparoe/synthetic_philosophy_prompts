Write a deadpan parody of an internal government memo, approximately 500 words, in the bureaucratic voice of a fictional "Ministry of Non-Instrumental Activity" in a nation-state that has adopted Moritz Schlick's philosophy of life as official doctrine. Schlick holds that life's meaning resides in play—activity pursued for its own sake, creatively self-sufficient, and free from external purpose—whereas work is purely instrumental, exhausting, and meaningful only derivatively. The memo should announce new post-automation regulations: now that necessary labor is eliminated, citizens must demonstrate that their daily activities qualify as pure play. Invent and deploy at least one bureaucratic mechanism not found in Schlick—for example, a Play Purity Index, quarterly Intent Declarations, or an Instrumental Contamination Tax—and show how it collapses Schlick's own distinction from within. Include deadpan details: a list of approved versus contaminated leisure activities, penalties for citizens caught deriving health benefits from sport or educational value from games, and guidance for auditors on detecting disguised work in hobbies. The satire should exaggerate real features of Schlick's view (the self-sufficiency of play, the derivative status of work, the idea that youth embodies this ideal) without inventing false ones, and the irony should remain sustained and legible without explicit signaling. Do not include citations, reading lists, or references to secondary literature; rely only on the basic distinction as summarized above.
