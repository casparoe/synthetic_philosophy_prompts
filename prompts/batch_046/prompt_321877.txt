I just got roped onto the ethics committee for my building's co-op board and honestly I never cared about political philosophy until now but here we are. Next thursday we vote on whether our co-op issues an official statement urging members to vote for the lesser-evil candidate in the district 4 race — both frontrunners want to gut rent control but one is arguably worse. Some board members (including me, kinda) think voting is partly about expressing your values and that telling people to strategically pick the bad-but-not-worst option turns the ballot into a pure instrument, which feels wrong. Others say we have a collective duty to minimize harm and that abstaining or voting purely symbolically is just moral vanity when real tenants will suffer. I need a draft memo for the committee recommending one of three positions: endorse strategic voting, stay officially neutral but allow members to organize informally, or actively encourage conscientious abstention if neither candidate is acceptable. The memo needs a clear bottom line with reasoning the board can actually use, and it should take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side — especially the idea that voting expresses something strategic calculation corrupts. Keep it grounded; don't just list philosophers, but if you draw on ideas about duty vs expression vs lesser evil, make them do real work for the decision.
