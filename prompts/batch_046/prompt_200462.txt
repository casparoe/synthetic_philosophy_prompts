You serve on the grant-review board of a national theoretical-physics funding agency. A proposal requests €3 million to establish an embedded conceptual audit unit within a large cosmology collaboration. The unit would review internal papers that invoke non-empirical arguments—landscape-statistical reasoning, anthropic selection, or appeals to theoretical virtues such as simplicity and explanatory coherence—to defend predictions in multiverse or string-landscape models, with the stated aim of making implicit philosophical commitments explicit before publication.

One board member, invoking Weinberg’s familiar comparison of philosophy’s utility to that of ornithology for birds and Hawking’s claim that philosophy has failed to keep pace with modern physics, argues that working physicists already apply Bayesian model comparison and parsimony principles without philosophical mediation; adding an audit layer will only slow empirical output and substitute metaphysical debate for predictive work. Another member, drawing on philosophy-of-science work about value-laden theory assessment, responds that choices about which virtues to weight—predictive breadth versus parsimony—are not algorithmically fixed, that landscape distributions embed contested normative assumptions, and that making these explicit prevents ad hoc rationalization in the absence of direct experimental discrimination.

The board must recommend approval of the unit with defined authority, outright rejection, or approval only in a non-binding advisory capacity with clear boundaries. Draft the short decision memo you will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action—including what the unit may and may not do, how conflicts with scientific autonomy are resolved, and a timeline for review—while giving an honest, non-caricatured account of the strongest argument on the side you reject.
