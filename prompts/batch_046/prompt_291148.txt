We have to circulate a memo by Friday recommending whether the pilot programme should keep the current 'sphex-mode' care-bots (rigid, loop-based greeting and medication checks with zero deviation) or switch to adaptive-learning models across all twelve units. The philosophical knot is sphexishness: is mechanical rigidity a denial of free will and dignity for residents with cognitive impairment, or is it actually a kind of 'procedural fidelity' that protects them from unpredictable social demands? Draft the memo with a concrete recommendation and a six-week rollout or halt timeline. You must take seriously the strongest argument for retaining the loops — that for some residents, predictability is not oppression but a form of sovereignty — and you shouldnt just restate autonomy as if it decides the case. Write it in British English, keep it under two pages, avoid any bibliography or citation requests, and put the bottom-line recommendation in a box at the very end: \boxed{Recommendation: ...}.
