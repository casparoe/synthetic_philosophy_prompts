I'm writing a long-form piece on an adversarial collaboration that recently brought together two camps in the philosophy of meaning: a revised absurdist camp, which holds that the human demand for cosmic justification is permanently unanswerable and that authenticity requires sustaining—not resolving—the tension between that demand and an indifferent universe; and a modular naturalist camp, which holds that meaning is constituted by sustained, active engagement with projects of objective value, localized to domains or temporal segments of a life, and entirely independent of transcendent closure. Please draft the joint statement these two parties should have signed. Start with a numbered section of claims both can endorse—covering the structure of evaluative judgment about lives, the distinction between subjective fulfillment and objective worth, the irrelevance of theological purpose to the present dispute, and the shared rejection of purely hedonistic accounts. Follow that with a disagreements table: for each side, state its precise position in wording its own members would accept, especially on whether meaning must be globally assessed across an entire life or can be modularized without incoherence, and on whether analogizing evolutionary teleonomy to normative purpose avoids the naturalistic fallacy. Then list the cruxes—chiefly the modularity thesis and the teleonomy analogy—and for each provide a concrete case or thought experiment both sides agree is relevant (for example, a life composed of deeply meaningful projects that are abruptly and irreversibly truncated, or a biologically evolved capacity for long-term planning that appears to carry normative weight only within its own functional domain). For each crux, record in advance what each camp predicts the case shows. Keep the document self-contained, neutral, and free of bibliographies, citations, or references to specific scholars by name; the language should be formal and precise enough that readers without access to the literature can follow every step.
