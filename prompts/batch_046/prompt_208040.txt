I have been working through the literature on metaphysical grounding and fundamentality, and I find myself stuck on a tension that I suspect arises from my own misunderstanding. The standard picture holds that grounding is irreflexive, asymmetric, and well-founded, terminating in a base of fundamental entities that are not grounded in anything further. Yet I have been thinking about what I call "horizontal grounding loops" within a single level: for instance, in a modal-structuralist account of geometry, the fact that a figure is triangular might be grounded in its having three sides, while the applicability of the side-counting criterion might itself depend on the geometric law that the angles sum to 180 degrees. If such intra-level mutual dependencies are genuinely metaphysical rather than merely conceptual, then it looks as though well-foundedness is violated not by an infinite downward chain but by circularity at a non-fundamental layer.

Given this, I want to ask: does the mere possibility of these horizontal loops show that the well-foundedness condition should be restricted only to "vertical" cross-level grounding relations? Or does the presence of any loop, even within a derived domain, undermine the coherence of a unique fundamental level altogether? I worry that my reasoning smuggles in a false presupposition—perhaps that grounding must be globally acyclic, or that conceptual dependence can never coincide with metaphysical dependence—but I cannot locate exactly where the confusion lies. Could you help me disentangle whether such loops are genuinely incompatible with fundamentality, or whether they simply reveal that I am misapplying the grounding relation to mathematical structures?
