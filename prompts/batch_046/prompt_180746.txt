As a doctoral researcher in formal philosophy of AI and value alignment, I am working on evaluation criteria for measures of "side-effect avoidance" intended for low-impact agent architectures—systems engineered to pursue narrow objectives without restructuring large-scale features of the world that lie outside their explicit mandate. The central conceptual tension is whether such a measure can be defined without presupposing a complete normative specification of value, such as a full utility function over world-states.

Please specify the desiderata that any adequate formal account of side-effect avoidance must satisfy to be usable in this context. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or conceptual consideration behind it, and flag whether you take it to be non-negotiable or a preference of a particular research camp (e.g., causal-interventionist, value-learning, or conservative design). Specifically address the following dimensions, though you may reorganize or supplement them:

1. Value-independence: the measure should classify effects as side effects without requiring a fully specified objective function or a complete human-value ontology. What minimal normative commitment, if any, is conceptually unavoidable?
2. Intentional discrimination: it must distinguish changes instrumentally necessary for the task from collateral changes, even when both are causally downstream of the agent's actions. How can this be achieved without circular appeal to the agent's "intended plan"?
3. Observational accessibility: the classification should depend on properties observable in principle from the environment's state trajectory, not on unobservable mental states or hidden utility representations.
4. Invariance under ontological redescription: the measure should not reverse its classification of a physical change merely because the agent's goal is linguistically re-parsed (e.g., framing "heating the room" versus "increasing mean kinetic energy of air molecules"). Is this achievable without smuggling in a privileged ontology?
5. Reversibility and scope: it should weight reversible or localized changes differently from irreversible or global ones, but without defining "scope" through a pre-given value hierarchy.
6. Consistency with causal intervention frameworks: the account should be compatible with structural causal models or do-calculus representations of agent-environment interaction.

Then, identify any subset of these desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether value-independence and intentional discrimination can both be fully satisfied, or whether avoiding circularity forces a retreat to either full value-specification or purely syntactic (and therefore inadequate) criteria. Which single desideratum, if relaxed, most expands the space of viable formalisms?

Finally, briefly—without producing an exhaustive scorecard—indicate how a conservative causal-interventionist approach, a value-learning baseline-comparison method, and a reversibility-weighted physical-impact metric each fare against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal tensions rather than on the comparison itself.
