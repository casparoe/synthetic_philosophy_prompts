Is this collective orientation best understood as a preference or as a cultural bias? In the case of emotional reserve, if the society has democratically endorsed its norms, then by definition the orientation is a preference rather than a bias: bias requires an unjustified imposition, and democratic endorsement supplies justification. The reflexive problem confirms this. When we ask whether our own distinction between preference and bias is biased, we can answer only from within our own framework, which makes the question circular. Therefore, the preference-bias distinction cannot do real work in cross-cultural evaluation; it can only describe internal consistency. The society’s value is a preference, not a bias, precisely because it was chosen, and the unavoidable circularity means we should suspend judgment about other cultures rather than labeling them biased.
