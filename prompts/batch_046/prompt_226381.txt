Consider the Hart–Devlin debate on legal moralism, but set aside its canonical applications to criminal law and instead treat it as an unsettled framework for governing mediated moral harm. Assume a research program with roughly eighteen months, a conceptual-philosophy and institutional-design background, and no large-scale empirical apparatus, that wants to apply the debate's core distinctions—between physical harm, moral offense, and the enforcement of a shared social morality—to the design of synthetic-media regulation and algorithmic curation systems. In these domains, harm is diffuse, victims are often collective or unidentified, and moral offense propagates through recommendation architectures rather than through territorial social pressure.

Within this reframed problem space, identify the three most consequential open questions or lines of work, and defend a strict prioritization among them. At least one candidate should address whether the Hartian harm principle can be extended to cover second-order normative degradation—the erosion of cooperative conditions that make shared moral judgment possible—without collapsing into Devlin's argument for preserving a social morality. Another should examine whether Devlin's claim that societies may defend their moral fabric presupposes a theory of collective intentionality adequate to distributed, non-territorial digital publics. The third should be either a logical dependency of these two or a separate upstream conceptual task of your own choosing.

For the prioritization, specify: which question must be settled first given logical and practical dependencies; which is most tractable given the group's resources and background; which should be deprioritized because resolving it would not materially change institutional design choices; and what criteria govern your ranking—whether a result unlocks a regulatory mechanism, clarifies taxonomy without practical payoff, or resolves a crux that other debates implicitly depend on. Do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations. Deliver a defended agenda with explicit trade-offs, not a survey of the literature.
