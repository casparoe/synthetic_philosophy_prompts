spinoza dies in 1677, so he never had to deal with universal suffrage or liquid democracy where you delegate votes issue-by-issue. in the political treatise he says democracy is the most natural form of government because it expresses the power of the multtude, but he also thinks reason is scarce and passions dominate. imagine he's consulting for a modern city-state using liquid delgation plus algorithmic proposal filtering. does he back it as a rational extension of collective conatus, or reject it as aristocracy in disguise? argue the rejection side seriously, but don't skip the point that delegation might actually shield people from affective contagion.
