hey, i've been secnd-guessing this argument i cooked up about luck egalitarianism and now it feels like it eats its own tail. here's the chain:

(1) The core idea is that inequalities are okay when they come from choice but not when they come from brute luck — like if two people start with equal resources and one works 80hrs while the other chills.

(2) But for that distinction to do any work, "choice" can't just mean "what you did." It has to mean something stronger: that you could have acted differently in a way that wasn't itself caused by prior brute luck.

(3) Here's the niche injection — call it "architectual luck." Your risk tolerance, your idea of what counts as effort, your time discounting, all of that is shaped by genetics and upbringing you never chose.

(4) So every "choice" is really just the output of a decision architecture that was built by brute luck.

(5) That means the choice/luck distinction collapses. Every outcome traces back to brute luck, just with extra psychological steps in between.

(6) So luck egalitarianism can't actually justify any inequality — not even the ones it's supposed to justify, like earnings from hard work.

(7) At best it just becomes "equalize everything," which makes it indistinguishable from strict egalitarianism. But that's not what people who defend this view actually meant, right?

(8) So either the view collapses into universal equality, or we have to give up the idea that choice needs to be free of all prior luck.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? Is (2) asking too much — maybe choice just needs to be voluntary, not metaphysically free of every prior cause? Or is the collapse real and we should just accept that luck egalitarianism is secretly a full equality view? I'd rather save the intuition that effort matters, but i'm not sure what that costs me.
