Write an essay on whether self-deception is even genuinely possible, or if its really just motivated reasoning wearing a trenchcoat. You should weigh the old intentionalist paradox — like, if I know the truth and hide it from myself, whos the deceiver and whos the mark? — against deflationary takes that say theres no unitary self being fooled, just compartmentalized mental processes. But heres the twist I want you to engage with: suppose someone deliberately sets up a "belief filter" that automatically downgrades evidence threatening their self-image, and they designed that filter back when they were being fully reflective. Does that retrospective design make it count as real deception, or does it still collapse into ordinary bias? Don't just waffle — reach an actual conclusion and defend it. Write in American English (no "behaviour", no "colour", etc).
