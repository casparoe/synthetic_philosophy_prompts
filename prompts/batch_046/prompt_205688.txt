We kept sliding between “the book is Einstein” and “the book is only a very good copy of Einstein,” and I realized we never pinned down exactly what each of us was denying. I want you to draft the document that an adversarial collaboration between our two positions should have produced.

The two camps are these. The Structural Preservationists hold that consciousness is fundamentally an organizational or functional property: if a physical system — here, a volume encoding every neural-functional relation in Einstein’s brain — preserves the complete causal-structural architecture of a conscious mind, then that system, when appropriately activated (for example, by being read or interpreted in the right temporal sequence), instantiates the very consciousness it encodes. The Dynamical Indexicalists hold that consciousness requires ongoing, self-sustaining temporal dynamics with a present-tense point of view; a static printed artifact lacks internal causal closure and exists all-at-once rather than in successive moments, so it can represent consciousness but never be a subject of experience, and any apparent mental life belongs to the reader or interpreter, not to the book.

But I want the collaboration to go deeper than the standard computation-versus-biology stalemate. Specifically, inject and address the following niche framing: treat the core dispute as turning on whether consciousness requires what we might call continuous causal closure — that is, a state-transition structure in which each mental state is caused primarily by prior states of the same system, rather than by an external agent — and on the problem of simultaneous existence, since every page of the book exists concurrently, breaking the linear now that seems essential to a unified stream of consciousness. The collaboration should determine whether these are genuine disanalogies or merely differences in temporal scale and medium.
