I am auditing an introductory philosophy of mind course, and during a seminar on intentionality two fellow students became locked in a disagreement about whether contemporary large language models genuinely resolve Searle's symbol grounding problem or merely displace it. Amara maintained that the problem, as classically formulated, requires symbols to acquire semantic content through direct causal interaction with the world; since these systems possess no embodied sensory apparatus, their outputs remain syntactically sophisticated yet semantically vacant—essentially an elaborate Chinese Room in which statistical patterns never become meanings. Ben countered that this frames a false dichotomy between direct sensory causation and ungrounded syntax, advancing instead a notion he termed "distributed functional anchoring": a symbol becomes grounded not through a single causal chain linking it to a referent, but through its systematic co-variation with thousands of other representations in a high-dimensional space that tracks real-world statistical structures, thereby serving the same epistemic role as perceptual grounding. Amara granted that predictive coherence is genuinely present, yet insisted that tracking statistical structure is precisely what an uninterpreted mechanism does; without phenomenal access or causal contact that distinguishes "heat" from the symbol "heat," the model's representations remain anchored only to further representations. Ben then pressed a consistency objection: if grounding demands direct causal contact, then human acquisition of abstract vocabulary—which typically proceeds through linguistic mediation rather than direct perception—fails the same test, so demanding sensory immediacy for artificial systems applies an unfair double standard. Amara replied that human children eventually tether mediated terms to direct experience, whereas the model's mediation is entirely self-referential, producing what she called a "closed loop" in which symbols refer only to other symbols with no exit point into the world. They turned to me and asked for a verdict. Please adjudicate move by move: is Ben's functional anchoring a legitimate solution or merely a relocation of the problem? Does Amara's "closed loop" objection succeed in showing that statistical mediation differs in kind from human linguistic mediation? And is Ben correct that requiring direct causation for machines while accepting mediated learning in humans involves an inconsistent standard?
