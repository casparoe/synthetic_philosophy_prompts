I'm on the charter revision board for our regional climate-adaptation authority. We're being asked to approve a new 'intertemporal lockbox' clause in our founding charter: it automatically allocates 40% of the budget to long-term infrastructure for the next 25 years, and future boards can only reduce that with a 75% supermajority. The idea is basically a Ulysses contract at the institutional level — we bind ourselves now because we know we'll face pressure later to divert funds to short-term crises. But one member is pushing back hard, saying democratic bodies cant really bind themselves in the classic Ulysses-contract sense because the agent that signs and the agent that wants to break it are different cohorts of citizens, not different temporal stages of one person. She also says we're not just precommitting but building what she calls a 'diachronic veto,' where past voters or board members exercise dead-hand control over living ones who never consented.

Please draft the decision memo for Thursdays meeting. I want a concrete recommendation — adopt with specific safeguards, reject outright, or approve a modified version — with clear reasoning that the board can actually vote on. Take a definite position, dont just lay out options and ask me to pick; defend your choice and give an honest account of the strongest argument on the losing side, especially that diachronic-veto objection. It needs to be practical enough that the dissenting member could write a direct reply to it, not just nod at both sides. And please, no requests for bibliographies or citation lists — I need an argument, not a syllabus.
