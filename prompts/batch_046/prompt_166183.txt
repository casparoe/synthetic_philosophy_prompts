I am writing a long-form feature on what becomes of ethics if panpsychism is treated not as a metaphysical curiosity but as a serious interpretation of fundamental physics. The familiar popular discussion—whether an electron “feels”—strikes me as a dead end; I want to understand which conceptual barriers actually have to fall before anyone could coherently talk about suffering distributed across physical structures that lack biological organisation.

To that end, I am trying to prioritise three interlocking lines of inquiry. The first concerns a distinction I have been calling focal versus diffuse phenomenal binding: might there be a structurally meaningful difference between integrated, information-dense phenomenal states of the kind we associate with nervous systems, and attenuated, spatially distributed phenomenal properties that might characterise quantum fields or cosmological vacuum states? If so, does suffering require focal binding, or can diffuse states instantiate a morally relevant form of distress? The second line asks whether suffering is not merely sentience but requires a specific functional architecture—perhaps sustained entropy reduction coupled with representational error-correction—that fundamental physical processes, however sentient in a minimal sense, necessarily lack. The third is institutional: if we grant even modest pan-sentience, must our moral framework expand to encompass cosmological-scale entities, or can we defend a threshold-based ethics without covert biological chauvinism?

These questions seem upstream and downstream of one another in ways I find difficult to map. Which should occupy the limited philosophical and scientific labour available? Which are tractable without first resolving the hard problem of consciousness, and which are merely decorative unless another is settled first? And from the standpoint of someone advising where attention should go—whether in research funding, policy imagination, or public discourse—which answers would genuinely alter our ethical landscape, and which would leave practice unchanged? I want a defended agenda rather than a survey: please rank these priorities with explicit criteria, indicate the dependency relations between them, and say what evidence or argument over the next few years could shift the ordering.
