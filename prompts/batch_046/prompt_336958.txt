I just finished a long, somewhat heated discussion with a friend about the ontology of properties, and I realized our debate kept circling the standard triad—Platonism, Aristotelian realism, nominalism, and trope theory—without ever confronting a genuinely hybrid alternative that might dissolve the tension rather than resolve it within those camps. I am auditing a graduate seminar on metaphysics, and I want to develop a position paper arguing for what I will call "indexical modalism" about universals: the view that properties are neither shared entities (realism), particularized qualities (tropes), nor mere linguistic labels (nominalism), but rather equivalence classes of counterfactual dispositions indexed to a measuring system or conceptual framework. On this account, to say that two red objects share redness is not to invoke a universal they both instantiate, but to claim that both fall within the same region of a similarity-space constructed from comparative judgments; the space itself is ontologically innocent, though the dispositions that populate it are real.

Your task is to construct a rigorous, self-contained argument for this view. You must address the One Over Many problem directly: explain why the multiplicity of red things does not entail a common nature shared across them, and show how exact resemblance can be accounted for without a primitive resemblance-relation or a shared universal. You should also defend indexical modalism against the charge that it collapses into trope nominalism—emphasizing that the modal profiles are not particularized to individual objects—and against the charge that it collapses into structural realism, since the framework-dependence is pragmatic and classificatory rather than physical or mathematical. Finally, extend the account to abstract universals, such as being triangular or being prime, explaining how these can be treated within the same modal-profile framework without invoking Platonic forms. I do not need citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts; I need only the argument laid out with its premises clearly stated and its potential objections anticipated.
