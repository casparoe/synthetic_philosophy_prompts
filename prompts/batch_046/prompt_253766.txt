Write a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on whether macrohistorical secular cycles are quantifiable and predictively useful, taking a respondent from the single starting commitment—“long-run demographic and institutional patterns recur across agrarian and industrial societies”—to one of several named conceptual positions, without ever asking which scholar or label they prefer.

Structure the tree around three mandatory branching dimensions, each phrased as a concrete diagnostic claim rather than an abstract principle:

1. The aggregation problem: Are secular cycles emergent statistical artifacts of micro-level aggregation (e.g., smoothing noise across heterogeneous polities), or do they reflect macro-level causal structures with genuine downward efficacy on individual agents?
2. The recurrence type: Is the pattern one of structural recurrence (invariant causal mechanism and functional form across epochs) or parametric recurrence (the same functional form—say, logistic pressure followed by crisis and restructuring—but with technology- or institution-dependent parameters that shift period length and amplitude)?
3. Predictive access: Given answers to (1) and (2), is the system in principle deterministically predictable, stochastically forecastable with calibrated error bounds, or only retrodictively narratable?

Each internal node must pose a specific case—e.g., “If elite-overproduction dynamics proceed to crisis but energy-substitution technology allows rapid fiscal restructuring, should we expect the same two-generation collapse timeline observed in pre-industrial agrarian cases?”—so that a respondent answers yes, no, or uncertain without knowing which leaf the path reaches.

Your tree must locate at least these six leaves in the logical space: (a) full structural-demographic determinism; (b) macro-emergent stochastic forecasting; (c) parametric recurrence with technological modulation; (d) aggregation-skeptical artifact theory; (e) downward-causation structuralism coupled with epistemic limits; and (f) one logically available combination that remains apparently unoccupied—identify it explicitly and briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Additionally, flag any pair of leaves that the three dimensions cannot genuinely separate at the level of concrete commitments, explaining whether the collapse is logical or merely terminological. Keep the instrument formal and suitable for a philosophy-of-social-science seminar; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or appeals to verify specific publication dates.
