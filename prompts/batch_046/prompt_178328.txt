Write a villanelle on Sartre's analysis of bad faith in which the two refrains correspond to the poles of facticity and transcendence—one expressing the claim that a person is fixed and self-identical, the other that a person is always beyond any fixed definition. Let the form's repetitions do the philosophical work: each return of a refrain should shift context slightly so that the reader sees the speaker trying to stabilize one pole against the other, and arrange the final couplet so that both lines appear together without resolving into synthesis. The poem should show that bad faith is not mere ignorance of radical freedom but a specific self-deceptive project of treating being-for-itself as being-in-itself—accurate enough that a reader familiar with Sartre recognizes the conceptual structure being enacted by the rhyme and repetition, not merely named. Keep it to nineteen lines, with no external commentary or framing devices.
