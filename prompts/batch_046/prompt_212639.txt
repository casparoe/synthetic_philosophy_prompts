I'm trying to sort out my views on freedom for a workshop paper and want to check if I'm talking past myslef. Here's where I stand:

1. I think negative liberty is the real deal: you're free iff nobody is actually stopping you from doing stuff.
2. I also think republican freedom — non-domination — is a separate, equally important value.
3. So I think a slave with a benevolent master who never interferes is genuinely unfree, but that's because the master's retained power is itself a kind of interferance with my social standing.
4. I think democratically enacted laws don't reduce anyone's liberty, even when they block choices.
5. I think freedom only matters instrumentally — it's good because it lets people pursue their goals.
6. I reject Berlin's positive liberty idea entirely; self-mastery is just a metaphor.

Are these consistent? Specifically, am I smuggling republican concerns into negative liberty in (3), and does (5) undermine why I'd care about (2) if domination never actually blocks me? Also, is there a hidden tension between (1) and (4) that I'm missing? Rank the real tensions by severity, tell me which pairs only look bad, and say what the cheapest fix would be for each.
