In a seminar on famine and responsibility I heard someone invoke the line “famine is a political crime, not a natural disaster” to dismiss a colleague who had noted that drought and crop failure preceded the mortality spike during the Great Leap Forward, and I have seen the same phrase used in debates about the Bengal famine of 1943 and the Irish famine of the 1840s. I need a precise disambiguation, because people seem to treat the sentence as self-evident when it might be doing entirely different work in each case.

Please lay out the distinct theses the slogan could be expressing—treating them as a menu, not a single interpretation. I am especially interested in separating: (i) a causal thesis that natural triggers played no significant role; (ii) an entitlement thesis in the Sen-style sense that aggregate food availability was adequate but access collapsed through market or distributional failure; (iii) a moral thesis that preventable mass death by authorities amounts to culpable omission, perhaps even meeting the conditions for criminal negligence; (iv) an institutional thesis that relief was deliberately withheld or obstructed by policy choices; and (v) an epistemic or ideological thesis that labeling a famine “natural” functions systematically to excuse political actors regardless of the actual causal mix.

For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, defensible but contentious, or false; and say which readings, if any, actually license using the slogan to dismiss the relevance of drought, harvest failure, or weather in explaining a specific famine. Then apply the menu to the three cases mentioned—Bengal 1943, Ireland in the 1840s, and the Great Leap Forward mortality crisis—indicating for each case which precise readings are historically sustained, which require additional premises that the slogan itself does not supply, and where the slogan’s rhetorical force comes from conflating two or more of these readings. I want to know, in particular, whether there is any coherent reading under which the phrase supports ending an argument about natural contributing factors without also committing to a specific moral or institutional claim that needs separate defense.
