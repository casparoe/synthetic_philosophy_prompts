Write the desiderata any adequate theory of poetic translation must meet if its aim is to preserve le ton beau — the particular beauty that resides in formal constraint rather than in paraphrasable content — using the translation of a fixed-form poem such as Clement Marot's Ma mignonne as the reference case. For each requirement, give the consideration behind it: whether strict rhyme-scheme preservation is non-negotiable or may yield to semantic fidelity; whether meter should be mapped isomorphically or only proportionally across languages; whether phonotactic texture — patterns of assonance, consonance, and syllable weight — must be reproduced even when the target language forces distortion of diction; and whether the translator must preserve the original's pragmatic register and historical distance or may modernize voice for fluency. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, complete structural isomorphism with natural idiomatic fluency in the target language — and identify which single requirement does the most to exclude inadequate candidates. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring particular translations; do not provide bibliographies or reading lists.
