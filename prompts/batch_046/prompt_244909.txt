Restate Scanlon's contractualist position on the aggregation problem — specifically, his claim that the moral reasons against harming one individual do not combine into a stronger reason merely because benefits are distributed across a larger group, and that this blocks utilitarian-style numerical trade-offs — entirely within the vocabulary of optimisation: objective functions, feasible regions, binding constraints, Lagrange multipliers, shadow prices, and non-dominated frontiers. In particular, examine whether the contractualist requirement that justification be owed to each person individually can be captured solely by separate constraints, or whether it introduces an irreducibly relational or intersubjective element that the vocabulary of shadow prices and duality cannot express without smuggling in interpersonal comparison.
