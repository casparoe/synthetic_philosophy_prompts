Write an essay addressing this controversy: Is an accuracy-first framework, which evaluates doxastic states primarily by their expected contribution to true belief weighted by confidence, capable of accommodating the epistemic value of explanatory understanding without reducing understanding to a derivative form of accuracy? Focus specifically on cases where achieving a coherent explanatory framework appears to require tolerating locally inaccurate beliefs that serve as heuristic scaffolds for broader true theories. Contrast the strict accuracy-first response—that any deviation from maximal accuracy is epistemically unjustified unless it is a reliable causal means to greater future accuracy—with a hybrid epistemic-utility view that assigns direct, non-instrumental value to doxastic coherence itself. Weigh the arguments for and against the claim that coherence can be incorporated into an accuracy-first calculus as an instrumental factor rather than an independent good. Reach a clear conclusion and justify it: either defend the purity of accuracy-first epistemology against the coherence challenge, or argue that epistemic utility theory must genuinely expand its objective function beyond accuracy alone.
