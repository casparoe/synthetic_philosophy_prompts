I'm drafting an essay for a graduate seminar in democratic theory and would like critical comments on its conceptual architecture—particularly whether the central distinction avoids collapsing into familiar positions and whether the framing example is well chosen. The essay addresses the boundary problem for what I term ephemeral collectives: temporary, high-interdependence groups that lack stable territorial or membership boundaries—exemplified by the crew and passengers of a long-duration interplanetary voyage, or by participants in a temporary decentralized crisis-response network that dissolves once the emergency ends. Conventional solutions—the all-affected-interests principle, stakeholder theories of democratic autonomy, and territorial-citizenship models—seem ill-fitted: the first risks over-inclusion by granting decision rights to anyone remotely impacted; the second tends to require pre-existing institutional membership that ephemeral groups lack; the third is simply inapplicable.

My proposed criterion is mutual visibility under joint risk: an individual belongs to the demos of such a collective if and only if (i) her actions can directly alter the risk profile of others within the group, and (ii) she can recognize in real time, without representational mediation, who those others are. This is intended to be stricter than pure affectedness—someone affected by the voyage's externalities but unaware of the crew does not qualify—yet broader than formal membership, since an unregistered participant who shares the life-support system does qualify.

I'm especially uncertain about two internal tensions. First, once joint risk is unpacked, does the criterion secretly reduce to a stakeholder view, or does the mutual visibility condition genuinely add independent normative weight? Second, the real-time recognition requirement seems to import an epistemic filter into democratic membership: it may exclude individuals with severe cognitive impairments or those in environments of radical information asymmetry, which raises the worry that I'm substituting an epistemic elite for a democratic demos. I would welcome focused comments on whether these objections are fatal, whether the interplanetary-voyage framing obscures rather than clarifies the argument, and whether the distinction between affectedness and mutual visibility under risk is conceptually robust enough to carry the weight I place on it. Overall, does the argument read as a genuine contribution, or as a conceptual distinction in search of a problem?
