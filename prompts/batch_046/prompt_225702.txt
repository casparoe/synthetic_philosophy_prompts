Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the compatibilist camp associated with Daniel Dennett’s defense of modified just-deserts punishment and the hard-determinist camp associated with Gregg Caruso’s rejection of retributivism. The deliverable should be a formal document both parties could sign, divided into three parts. First, a numbered section containing at least five precise, non-vacuous agreed claims about determinism, neuroscience, legal practice, and the moral limits of punishment—statements that are substantive enough to constrain reasoning but that both sides genuinely endorse. Second, a disagreements table with exactly three residual disputes, each rendered in neutral language that each party accepts as a fair formulation of its own view and of the opponent’s view; the table should explicitly distinguish substantive disagreements from merely terminological ones. Third, for each dispute, identify the crux—the specific sub-question whose resolution would cause at least one party to revise its position—and specify one concrete institutional scenario or conceptual test-case that both sides agree is genuinely probative. For each crux, record in advance what each side predicts about that scenario.

The three cruxes should be: (i) whether the counterfactual “could have done otherwise” condition necessary for desert can be satisfied by an agent-causal analysis that does not invoke categorical metaphysical freedom; (ii) whether punishment necessarily contains an irreducibly expressive or communicative element that references the offender’s blameworthiness, or whether every legitimate penal function is ultimately forward-looking; and (iii) whether the persistence of retributive moral anger in cases lacking consequentialist justification can be fully explained as an evolved heuristic, or whether it retains independent normative authority.

The agreed-claims section should explicitly include that current neuroscience does not permit deterministic prediction of individual criminal acts, that functioning legal systems in practice employ hybrid justifications, and that neither party endorses punishment conceived purely as vengeance. Write the document in formal collaborative prose—not as a dialogue or debate transcript—with the residual disagreements rendered as exact formulations rather than summaries, and with each predictive commitment stated unambiguously.
