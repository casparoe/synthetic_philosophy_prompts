Most city planning boards present their big zoning decisions as basically tecnical — they run traffic-flow models, density projections, and "objective" cost-benefit sheets, then say the value part (what kind of neighborhood people actually want) gets handled seperately by elected officials. What philosophy of planning does that division presuppose? Is it basically Carnap's old split between cognitive/scientific language and optative/value language, applied to urban policy? Reconstruct the implicit view: does it think empirical data can determine planning outcomes without smuggling in commitments, and that disagreements over values are just non-rational preferences? Say whether that view is coherent, or whether the choice of metrics — efficiency, growth, accessibility — already bakes in values. Also, tell me what would have to change in the practice if we dropped the Carnap-style separation and treated planning as inherently normative — would the board's authority collapse, or just shift?
