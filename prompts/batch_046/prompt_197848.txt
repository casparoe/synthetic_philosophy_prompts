I've been thinking about guilt vs shame and whether a culture can actually choose one on purpose, and I keep seeing this weird failure mode that I don't have a word for. You have instutitions built around one emotion -- public rituals designed to trigger shame through exposure, or private proccesses designed to trigger guilt with no audience -- but the people inside them often feel the opposite one. The public-ritual people say they feel guilty about being seen, not ashamed of the act; the private-process people say they feel ashamed of having a secret, even though nobody knows. It's not just mixing up the vocabulary. I can think of two cases: a religious group that switched to public confession hoping for collective shame, but members said they felt guilty for making everyone watch; and a therapy culture that pushed private journaling for guilt, but users talked about being ashamed of the hidden pages. Is there an established concept for this inversion between institutional design and emotional result? How is it different from just saying the two emotions are hard to separate? And if there's no name, what should we call it, and where are the edges -- like does it apply to pride and humiliation too?
