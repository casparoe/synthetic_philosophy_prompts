ok so i've been trying to write down where i actually stand on disagreement and priors for a half-finished post. here's the package as it sits:

1. i buy aumann's agreement result as a normative benchmark - if you and i share a prior and have common knowledge of our posteriors, we shouldn't stay split.
2. but literally shared priors are basically fiction in practice. everyone's generative model is trained on different life-data, so the "common prior" is usually a polite fiction we slap on after the fact.
3. even so, i think "agreeing to disagree" is mostly a failure mode. if we're both rational and reasoning well, we should update on thier beliefs as evidence, not treat them as opaque preferences.
4. there's this thing i call ontological drift - not just different weights on the same hypotheses, but actually carving up possibilty-space with different dimensions. in those cases bayes' rule doesn't apply because there's no shared sigma-algebra.
5. in drift cases i think persistent disagreement is rational and neither party is wrong. there's no hidden fact we're both approximating.
6. but i also think that if two agents share values, reasoning norms, and enough time, rationality eventually forces convergence - there's always some shared structure they can bootstap.

do these hang together? i'm specifically worried that (3) and (5) are pulling in opposite directions, and that (6) just assumes away (4). also (1) and (2) look like they clash but maybe that's only apparent. can you rank the actual tensions by severity, say what'd have to give in each case, and flag which ones dissolve on inspection? and please don't just lecture me on aumann - i'm asking about my specific mix, especially how ontological drift interacts with the rest.
