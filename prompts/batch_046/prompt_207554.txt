For my A-level philosophy coursework, I need to examine whether Camus's conception of the absurd can be reconciled with contemporary objectivist theories of meaning in life. Please write a formal essay that does the following. First, set out Camus's idea of the absurd as the tension between human demand for rational meaning and the universe's indifferent silence. Second, outline the contemporary objectivist position that meaning consists in engaging with pursuits that have objective value, such as knowledge, moral action, or creative achievement, regardless of whether they produce happiness. Third, analyse whether the absence of cosmic purpose undermines this objectivity, or whether it only eliminates transcendent significance while preserving local value. To ground the analysis, work through the case of the solitary archivist: an individual who spends her entire life compiling a precise, private catalogue of ecological patterns in a format that will be destroyed upon her death and never read by anyone else. Use this case to test whether objective value requires an audience, legacy, or cosmic recognition. Weigh the strongest objections from both sides, including the claim that objectivism implicitly relies on a hidden teleology and the counter-claim that absurdism mistakes the lack of eternal meaning for the lack of all meaning. Reach a definite conclusion and justify it clearly; do not finish with an ambiguous or non-committal stance. Write the response in British English, and end with a single line in the exact format Verdict: [your conclusion].
