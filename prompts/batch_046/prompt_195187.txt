As a novelist drafting my philosophical profile for a seminar on epistemic limits, I want an audit of whether my commitments hang together or whether I am quietly helping myself to incompatible motivations. Here is the set I have settled on:

(i) I hold the unrestricted knowability principle: for every true proposition p, there is a possible situation in which p is known.

(ii) I accept the formal validity of Fitch's derivation, but I reject the closure principle it requires—specifically, closure over conjunctions that embed an epistemic operator applied to itself.

(iii) In all other domains I retain closure: knowing p and knowing that p entails q is sufficient for knowing q, provided the entailment does not involve self-referential knowledge claims.

(iv) I am certain that unknown truths exist—there are propositions that are true and currently unknown to everyone—and I take the compound statement "p is true and p is unknown" to be genuinely satisfiable.

(v) I treat conceivability as a fallible but genuine guide to metaphysical possibility.

(vi) I can conceive without contradiction of a world in which every truth is known, yet I deny this is metaphysically possible, on the grounds that it conflicts with (iv).

(vii) I also want to maintain a distinction I call narrative occlusion: certain facts become knowable only when embedded in a framing narrative that renders them cognitively available, while the bare fact outside that frame remains unknown; I do not think this undermines logical closure, but I do think it explains why some unknown truths persist despite (i).

Please examine these for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Order the tensions by severity, specifying for each what must give and how expensive the fix is. Then tell me which single revision—giving up or modifying one commitment—would clean up the largest number of problems at once. In particular, I want to know whether the conflict between (i) and (iv) is real or dissolved by (ii) and (iii); whether my narrow rejection of closure in (ii) can be stably combined with my broad retention in (iii) given the usual motivations for each; and whether (vii) introduces a hidden class of counterexamples to (i) that Fitch's argument never reaches.
