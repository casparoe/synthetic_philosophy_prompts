Consider the problem of epistemic dependence on artificial intelligence not merely as a loss of first-order knowledge, but as a potential erosion of what we might call calibration understanding — a user's grasp of where their own unaided competence ends and the system's begins, including which error-types remain visible to them and which have become opaque. This framing moves beyond familiar discussions of automation bias or deskilling to focus on a meta-cognitive layer: the ability to judge whether one still understands enough to supervise the tool.
