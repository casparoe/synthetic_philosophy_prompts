Construct a thought experiment in which the reasoning behind an individual Ulysses contract and the reasoning behind constitutional precommitment point in opposite directions over the same concrete decision. Design a realistic one-paragraph scenario: a city public-health director has executed a legally binding advance directive—an individual Ulysses contract—stipulating that if she ever enters severe dementia, life-sustaining machinery must be withdrawn regardless of cost; meanwhile, the city charter contains an entrenched “continuity of care” clause that binds future councils to maintain that machinery for all covered officials. A sudden budget crisis forces the current council to choose whether to honor her directive and shut the machinery down, or to invoke the charter and keep her alive. After presenting the scenario, show what the Ulysses-contract framework says about honoring her prior self-binding, what the constitutional-precommitment framework says about the charter’s obligation across successive councils, and precisely why they diverge rather than reinforcing each other. Identify the single structural feature—intertemporal personal identity versus collective intergenerational continuity—that does the work. Then explain how a defender of the framework that comes out worse here could attack the case by arguing that this feature does not genuinely separate the two domains. Finally, say what argument or finding would change your verdict about which framework should prevail, and indicate where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
