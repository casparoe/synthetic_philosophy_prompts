Having spent three decades in experimental physics and only recently begun studying philosophy systematically, I am auditing an advanced metaphysics seminar and have been asked to draft the final essay examination. I should like a single question, answerable in seventy-five minutes, that tests whether students understand the structural disagreement between Lewisian counterpart theory and Kripkean transworld identity—rather than allowing them to reproduce standard textbook summaries.

To that end, please embed a constructed, biologically informed scenario: imagine a unicellular organism that reproduces by continuous fission, existing in the actual world and in a nearby possible world where the division event yields two daughter cells with precisely interchanged cytoplasmic distributions. Ask candidates to determine whether the original organism is strictly identical to either daughter in that other world, or whether it stands only in a counterpart relation to them, and to defend their position by appealing to the relevant metaphysical machinery—similarity relations among world-bound individuals on one side, and essential origins or non-branching identity conditions on the other.

Please provide the following, written in British English and without any request for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, since candidates will sit the examination without access to texts:

(i) the examination question itself, pitched at upper-level undergraduate or early postgraduate students;

(ii) a model answer of roughly 600 words that makes the crux explicit—specifically, how counterpart theory substitutes world-bound similarity for transworld identity, and how the transworld-identity theorist must either accept branching identities or deny that the original survives fission across worlds;

(iii) a marking scheme totalling 20 marks, built around five must-hit points (4 marks each), each accompanied by a one-line note on the characteristic error that should cost marks—such as conflating counterpart relations with temporal parts, or treating origin essentialism as a mere stipulation.

The difficulty should come from applying the theories to this fission scenario, not from recalling labels. Please ensure the answer avoids verbatim quotation of any published text.
