Construct a decision-tree taxonomy of the conceptual responses to Sen’s liberal paradox, designed as a diagnostic instrument rather than a list of named theories. The root commitment is this: you accept the formal result that minimal liberty—each of at least two individuals being decisive over a protected pair of alternatives—can conflict with the Pareto principle in preference profiles with complete orderings, and you do not dissolve the tension by redefining the example away.

From that root, build branches using four concrete, label-independent splitting questions. (1) Should rights-assignments be treated as part of the social state that the aggregation rule evaluates, or as external constraints that remove the assigned pairs from collective comparison? (2) If minimal liberty is retained, must Pareto be abandoned globally, or can it survive in domains where no rights are assigned? (3) Does the paradox depend essentially on the completeness of individual preferences; specifically, if individuals may hold incomplete rankings over states that involve others’ protected choices, does the conflict vanish? (4) Is there any meaningful interpersonal comparison or weighting of liberties—so that protecting A’s choice between x and y might be traded against protecting B’s choice between u and v—or must minimal liberty remain strictly non-comparable across persons?

Organize the tree so that the logical combinations of answers come first, then map actual positions onto the occupied leaves, marking empty combinations as “unnamed.” For every named leaf, give the position’s conventional label (if one exists in social-choice or liberal theory) and one sentence stating its most awkward normative commitment. For every unnamed leaf, provide one sentence diagnosing why the space is empty: incoherence, extreme unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Explicitly flag any pair of named positions that collapse onto the same branch combination and explain why the tree cannot separate them.

Additionally, specify which of the following must appear among the leaves: a view that rejects Pareto entirely in rights domains, a view that restricts the domain of preferences to dissolve the conflict, a view that redefines liberty as lexicographically prior but still Pareto-compatible elsewhere, and a view that accepts the paradox as a genuine cost of liberalism. Include a brief closing note identifying one formal feature of Sen’s paradox—its dependence on rights being assigned to specific pairs for specific individuals rather than being assigned globally or probabilistically—that this taxonomy omits, and explain why that omission limits its application to institutional design problems such as algorithmic allocation rules or constitutional review mechanisms. Keep every branch node phrased as a question answerable without knowing the position names in advance, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
