Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when Fitch's paradox of knowability is extended from individual agents to distributed epistemic systems—such as a federated research collaboration or an ensemble of predictive models—where knowledge is partitioned across nodes and no single agent holds all true beliefs. Construct a scenario in which each node satisfies a localized version of the knowability principle for the propositions it accesses, yet the collective accessibility relation reintroduces the paradox at the system level through shared entailment, and explain whether resolving the tension requires abandoning distributivity of collective knowledge or weakening the principle in a way that individual nodes cannot consistently endorse.
