Sen's entitlement framework distinguishes famine caused by Food Availability Decline from famine caused by exchange entitlement collapse, and his analysis of Bengal in 1943 is widely taken to show that the latter can occur without the former. If we apply this distinction rigorously to the Irish famine of 1845–52 and to the Great Leap Forward—cases where national aggregate grain figures do not always show an absolute shortfall relative to subsistence needs—does it follow that blight and weather variability were merely epiphenomenal, and that describing either event as involving a natural disaster is conceptually incoherent rather than just analytically incomplete?
