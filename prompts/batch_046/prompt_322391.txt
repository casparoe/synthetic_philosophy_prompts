Analyze the dissolution of the Soviet Union in December 1991 through the framework of reference-class forecasting, base-rate reasoning, and the outside view applied to imperial collapse. Treat the USSR as an instance in the reference class of multi-ethnic, continent-scale empires that experienced strategic overextension followed by internal fiscal crisis—analogous cases being the Austro-Hungarian and Ottoman dissolutions after 1918, and perhaps the Qing after 1895—while noting the singular feature that the Soviet Union possessed a strategic nuclear arsenal distributed across multiple republics yet ultimately consolidated under a single successor command.

First, specify the relevant reference-class dimensions: the median interval between a decisive peripheral military failure and formal state dissolution; the correlation between federal ethnic architecture and secessionist cascade; and the base rate for peaceful versus violent transitions in non-democratic imperial regimes. Then apply the outside view: what should an observer in 1989 have predicted about the mode, speed, and territorial integrity of Soviet collapse?

Next, map the actual episode onto these predictions. The dissolution was remarkably rapid and largely non-violent among the core Slavic republics; nuclear command and control presented a continuity problem across the disappearance of the central state authority; and the transition involved a negotiated transfer of authority rather than a revolutionary overthrow. Identify precisely where the reference-class model succeeds—perhaps in predicting federal fragmentation along ethnic lines—and where it fails or is silent, especially regarding the nuclear-command continuity problem and the absence of inter-republican civil war.

Finally, evaluate whether these anomalies justify a "singular-event" adjustment to the forecasting model, or whether they instead reveal a structural limitation of the outside view when the reference class for nuclear-armed empires has cardinality one. What does this case show about the methodological boundary between legitimate base-rate reasoning and ad hoc exemption?
