I'm taking a graduate seminar on African philosophy, and we've been working through Ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood, but my study partner keeps pressing an argument on me that I can't quite dismantle. She lays it out like this. (1) If personhood is genuinely constituted by reciprocal relational recognition—taking the Ubuntu claim that a person is a person through other people literally—then being embedded in such relations is not merely evidence of personhood but metaphysically necessary for it. (2) So an individual permanently and totally excluded from all reciprocal human recognition—say, a lifelong outcast with no community—lacks the very relations that constitute being a person. (3) By the same token, if there are no intrinsic, non-relational properties that ground personhood, then any entity—whether human, artificial, or corporate—that is embedded in rich reciprocal recognizing networks qualifies as a person. (4) Therefore Ubuntu either strips isolated humans of personhood or extends it to non-human networks, both of which seem unacceptable. (5) The only escape is to admit that being human (or some other intrinsic feature) is a necessary condition for personhood, with relational recognition serving only as a sufficient or recognitional condition. (6) But once we admit that intrinsic condition, relational recognition is no longer constitutive, and Ubuntu collapses into liberal individualism with a social ornament. She says this shows relational personhood is either morally monstrous or metaphysically redundant. I want to hold onto (1) and resist (6), but I can't see where the slide happens—perhaps between constitution and necessary condition, or between total absence and partial failure, or maybe a hidden modal slide from "developed through relations" to "existent only within them." Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost? Please separate what is settled in the literature from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and don't present a disputed interpretation as though it were the consensus.
