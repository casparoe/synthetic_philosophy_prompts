Consider a modified version of Thomson's lamp in which the toggle is governed not by an external agent but by an internal feedback rule: at each interval, the lamp's current state determines whether the switch occurs in the next interval, with illumination causing a toggle and darkness causing stasis. Call this the autoreferential supertask. Given that this embeds causal self-reference within the infinite sequence converging on the two-minute limit, doesn't it follow that the loop must collapse into a single fixed point? In other words, hasn't this case shown that any supertask involving internal feedback necessarily resolves to a determinate state at the limit, thereby dissolving Thomson's paradox rather than leaving it genuinely open?
