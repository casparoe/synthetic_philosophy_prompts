next week i present at a joint moral psych / phil of mind seminar on a paper about Strawson's reactive attitudes. my thesis is that the reactive/objective binary misses a middle category i'm calling scaffolded reactivity: cases where someone genuinely feels resentment or gratitude toward a person, but the normative expectation is structurally split between that person and an institutional system, so you're not treating them as a full moral agent but you're also not adopting the cool detached objective attitude. i use dementia care and psychiatric nursing examples where caregivers report feeling genuinely wronged by patients while explicitly denying those patients are responsible in the usual sense. the audience is half Strawson scholars who know his classic paper cold, plus clinical ethicists and philosophers of psychiatry who care whether this actually describes care workers mental lives, not metaphysical free will. they want to know if this helps us understand moral emotion in institutions or if i'm just redescribing burnout.

please give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased like they'd actually say them, with what i should concede, hold, and my weakest reply point. include the one i really hope nobody asks. order by damage not topic. flag which need a brief answer vs a real defense since i only have 20 min.

my argument sketch: reactive attitudes have an implicit adress structure directed at the agent as source. in scaffolded cases the adress splits -- part to the patient, part to the care system -- but the emotional heat stays genuinely reactive, not detached. so Strawson is wrong that suspending full responsibility always means viewing the person as an object of social policy or treatment; you can keep reactive phenomonology while distributing normative expectation.

the objections i expect:
- the Strawson scholar saying i'm just smuggling the objective attitude back in with fancy language about institutions
- the clinical ethicist saying caregivers feel burnout and frustration, not real resentment, so i'm misdescribing the phenomonology
- the psychiatry philosopher asking if this collapses people into mere nodes in a system, which is exactly the dehumanizing risk Strawson warned about
- the hidden killer: if scaffolded reactivity applies to dementia patients, why can't it apply to AI or corporations, and doesn't that prove reactive attitudes never required full agency in the first place?

for each tell me the concession, the hold, where my reply is weakest, and how to say it fast. also flag whether there's any jointly unsatisfiable tension in my own position -- like if i say the emotional content stays reactive but the normative expectation is distributed, do i have a coherent account of what makes it reactive rather than just complicated frustration?
