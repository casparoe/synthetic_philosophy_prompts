My friend and I just finished a long argument about applying Hannah Arendt's idea of the banality of evil, and we want to check whether her judgments form a consistent pattern. Her verdicts so far: (1) a logistics officer who organizes deportations with purely procedural language, no personal hatred, and no reflection on the victims exemplifies banal evil; (2) a zealot who invents cruelty out of personal hatred and imaginative conviction does not—this is closer to radical evil; (3) a worker who quits, explaining only that she kept seeing her neighbor's face and could not continue, shows the presence of moral judgment rather than its absence; and (4) a university compliance officer who approves harmful research using only technical risk forms, never pictures the actual subjects, and says her job is compliance rather than conscience, also exemplifies banal evil.

The new case is a freelance image-labeler who works remotely, tagging faces for a facial-recognition system sold to authoritarian surveillance agencies. She has no organizational hierarchy, applies her own aesthetic judgment about image quality, describes her work in casual terms like sorting photos for an album, knows the end use, and says she is just a freelancer and the clients decide. She expresses no hatred and uses no bureaucratic jargon.

If she wants to be consistent with her own track record, what should she say about the labeler—which of the four precedents is the closest analog, and what principle would she be adopting with each possible choice? Please lay out at least two competing rules that fit her first four verdicts equally well, perhaps one centered on the failure of reflective judgment and reliance on cliché or procedural language, and another centered on being an interchangeable part of a bureaucratic apparatus that fragments responsibility. State what each rule predicts for the labeler, give your best guess at the consistent verdict with reasons, and name one further hypothetical case that would clearly separate the two rules. Write your answer in American English.
