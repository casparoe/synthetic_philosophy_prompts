I had a long argument with a friend last night about functionalism and multiple realizability, and we reached an impasse that I still cannot resolve. Here is the argument my friend pressed, which strikes me as proving far too much. (1) Functionalism holds that mental states are constituted entirely by their causal-functional roles within a system, with no essential dependence on biological substrate. (2) Multiple realizability entails that these roles may be implemented in silicon, synthetic tissue, distributed networks, or any other architecture that preserves the relevant organizational relations. (3) Now imagine a planetary-scale financial exchange—call it a market-mind—in which price-feedback loops among autonomous agents instantiate precisely the same transition probabilities as human belief-revision, yet there is no central nervous system or unified consciousness directing the process. (4) By (1) and (2), this distributed exchange realizes genuine belief states. (5) But slow those same functional loops by a factor of ten thousand, and they are realized not in a market but in the evolutionary dynamics of a linguistic community over centuries: patterns of usage change, stabilize, and adapt to environmental pressure in ways structurally isomorphic to belief fixation. (6) Therefore, on the functionalist account, that historical linguistic process itself literally holds beliefs. (7) That conclusion seems plainly absurd—history does not have a mind—so either functionalism is false, or the appeal to multiple realizability must be restricted in some ad hoc manner. I feel the slip occurs somewhere between (4) and (6), perhaps an unnoticed equivocation on what counts as the boundaries of a single system, or a hidden assumption that temporal scale is irrelevant to realization. Which step should I reject, and what does that rejection cost? I ask for a committed response: take a definite position on the faulty step, explain why it initially looked sound, and state clearly whether a consistent functionalist should restrict multiple realizability, accept distributed and historical minds, or pursue some other path. Please respond in continuous prose only—no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as in a letter—without laying out options for me to choose among.
