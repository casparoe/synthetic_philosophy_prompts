I advise a senior acquisitions committee at a museum that specializes in artifacts dated before 500 CE. We increasingly receive proposals for objects whose physical custody chain breaks before 1900, leaving us with only (a) references in ancient texts transmitted through medieval manuscript traditions, (b) modern scientific reports, and sometimes (c) an algorithmically reconstructed provenance narrative assembled by pattern-matching across digitized collection records. We need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in the source-critical principles associated with Herodotus—comparing competing traditions and weighing geographical and chronological plausibility against one another—and with Thucydides—separating direct testimony, secondhand transmission, and inferred causation—but translated for a non-specialist committee that has ninety minutes per proposal and no classical philologist on staff. The procedure should specify the ordered questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where it mandates rejection, where it mandates acceptance, and where it must stop and defer to human judgment because the issue is one of institutional values rather than evidentiary weight. It must explicitly address three hard cases: attribution through a single late manuscript transmission; scientific dating that conflicts with textual attribution; and an algorithmically reconstructed custody chain lacking any pre-digital documentary source. Then apply the procedure to two proposals: first, a claimed Sassanid administrative seal said to match a geographical description preserved in Herodotean tradition but carrying no custody documentation prior to 1972, supported only by reconstructed database linkages; second, a fragmentary stone inscription said to record an Athenian tribute list mentioned in Thucydidean narrative, for which radiocarbon analysis places the material two centuries later than the event, with the only textual witness being a tenth-century copy. For each case, show where the procedure lands, flag any step where it misfires or yields an answer the committee will dislike, and identify precisely what the procedure cannot settle regardless of further refinement.
