I serve on the Governance and Continuity Committee of the Meridian Foundation. Our charter grants one founding trustee sole veto power over any charter amendment. That trustee has been diagnosed with progressive cortical degeneration. A clinical consortium proposes an 18-month protocol in which biological cortical tissue is replaced, region by region, with synthetic analogues that maintain functional isomorphism, terminating in full substrate-independent emulation. The trustee has consented and requests that the Committee recognize the resulting entity as the continuous legal trustee, preserving veto authority without interruption. Some members argue that institutional and personal identity track functional and psychological continuity, and that conditional approval with transitional co-verification safeguards is appropriate. Others argue that legal personhood requires biological continuity, and that gradual replacement is not preservation but a masked succession that voids the charter's intent. Draft the Committee's decision memo for circulation before Thursday's meeting. Recommend approval, rejection, or approval with specific conditions and a clear time frame; treat the strongest argument against your recommendation with genuine seriousness; and frame your reasoning so that the clinical consortium and the trustee could respond to it directly, rather than relying solely on abstract metaphysical premises.
