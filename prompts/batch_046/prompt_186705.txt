I'm working through my position for a philosophy of personal identity and value theory reading group, and I need a consistency check before I present next week. I want to see whether these commitments actually hang together or whether I'm helping myself to incompatible motivations. Here is roughly where I stand. First, I take transformative experiences—becoming a parent, undergoing a deep religious conversion, or acquiring a severe disability—to be genuinely possible, and I think they involve changes to core values and preferences that the pre-experience self cannot fully imagine or evaluate from the inside. Second, I think standard rational choice theory still applies, but only if we replace subjective preference satisfaction with an objective-list account of well-being—goods like knowledge, autonomy, and meaningful relationships—since the preferences themselves are what's in flux. Third, I hold that the pre-transformation self retains decisive normative authority over whether to undergo the experience, and I ground this authority in psychological continuity being the correct criterion of personal identity, even though I recognize that transformative experiences are precisely the kind of event that tends to disrupt psychological continuity rather than preserve it. Fourth, I also hold that when transformation occurs, the post-transformation self's values are not merely arbitrary preference shifts but represent a kind of epistemic progress, analogous to overcoming a conceptual blind spot or seeing a moral truth one was previously blind to, and therefore these values deserve respect rather than dismissal. Fifth, I deny that there is any meaningful cross-temporal, framework-independent metric of well-being that would let us justify the choice by saying the transformed self is objectively better off, since all evaluation is necessarily situated within some evaluative framework and there is no view from nowhere. Sixth, I reject the idea that there is an ideal observer stance or a neutral arbitration point from which one could compare the pre- and post-transformation value sets and declare one correct.

Please audit this package. I am not asking for a lecture on transformative experience in general; I want you to go through these commitments pair by pair, tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look tense, rank the tensions by severity, and for each serious one say exactly what has to give and what the cheapest revision would be. In particular, I am worried about whether my appeal to psychological continuity as the ground of pre-self authority is compatible with my claim that transformation disrupts that very continuity, and whether treating post-transformation values as epistemic progress sits badly with my denial of a framework-independent standpoint. But do not assume these are the only problems, and do not tell me which commitment I should drop—I want the audit ordered by severity with the cost of each fix made explicit.
