hey so i just moved into a shared house with three roommates and we're splitting chores and groceries, and suddenly prisoner's dilemma stuff feels way less abstract. i want a consistency check before i actually propose rules for the place.

here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) in a true one-shot anomymous PD, defection is rationally required, not just permitted. you're leaving utility on the table with zero strategic upside.

(2) but i also think there's a genuine moral wrongness to defecting in that exact same one-shot case. it's not sentimental; there's a fairness norm that applies even with no repetition and no reputation at all.

(3) for iterated PD i buy the standard evolutionary story - shadow of the future, reciprocal altruism, etc. - and i think it fully explains cooperative behavior in nature and society. there's no leftover cooperation instinct that needs its own explanation.

(4) i've been thinking about noisy iterated games where there's like 10-15% signal error, and apparently pure tit-for-tat collapses while a strategy with a forgivness rate near 0.3 is stable. i want our house norms to track that: if someone misses a chore, we forgive about 30% of slips without retaliation.

(5) i reject evolutionary ethics outright - just because something evolved doesn't make it right. still, i think the evolutionary stability of generous strategies gives me at least a weak pro tanto reason to build our co-op rules around that 0.3 rate.

do these actually hang together? i'm especially worried that (3) plus my rejection in (5) should block me from using evolutionary stability to justify (4). but also (1) and (2) feel like they're pulling opposite directions, though maybe that's just practical vs moral rationality? and is there a hidden clash between (2) and (3) since one-shot cooperation isn't covered by the evolutionary explanation?

please audit this: rank the tensions by severity, say which ones dissolve on inspection, and for each real one give the cheapest revision and what it costs. i don't want to ditch the 30% idea lightly since it sounds practical for shared living, so if fixing things requires dropping it, be explicit about that tradeoff.
