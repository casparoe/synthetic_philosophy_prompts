I'm presenting to our philosophy-of-law reading group next thursday. My thesis: a constitution can contain a clause that genuinely rewrites its own amendment rule without falling into Ross-style paradox, provided the rewrite is indexed to a future constitutional moment rather than applied syncronously to the present act—so article 99 can declare that after 2035 article 99 is superseded, without the current amendment trying to lift itself by its bootstraps. The audience is mostly legal positivists who think legal self-referance is a category mistake, plus two logicians who know Ross's paradox inside out, one formalist obsessed with infinite regress, and a grad student who just read Alf Ross. Give me the Q&A prep sheet: the objectons in the order they'll come, phrased like they'd actually say them, which have real force vs which are red herrings, what I should concede upfront, the one reply on temporal indexing that absolutely has to land, and the hidden question I'm praying nobody asks—whether my fix just pushes the paradox up to a meta-constitutional level. This is for a 20-minute talk with 15 minutes of questions.
