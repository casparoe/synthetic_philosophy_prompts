I chair the legacy and representation committee of the Morland Foundation, and we must issue a formal recommendation on a proposed biographical stage drama about Dr. Alina Voss (1921–1988), a moral philosopher who published a rigorous contractualist ethics in the 1960s but left private correspondence that, read selectively, suggests she experienced severe private doubt about her own framework. The playwright proposes to stage extended soliloquies in which Voss appears to renounce contractualism—though no explicit renunciation exists in the archive—defending this as necessary interpretive reconstruction for dramatic truth. Critics on our board charge that this amounts to posthumous ventriloquism, violating a duty of epistemic fidelity we owe to the dead; supporters argue that any dramatization requires narrative license and that obligations to the dead apply only derivatively, through harm to living scholars, family, or public memory. Draft the committee's decision memo—approve the project outright, approve only with mandatory framing devices that mark invented interior monologues as speculative, or reject—defending your recommendation with explicit attention to whether our obligations to the dead are best understood as direct duties to preserve truth about them, as prohibitions against instrumentalizing their lives for present purposes, or as entirely derivative concerns. Include an honest treatment of the strongest objection to your position, and explicitly flag one structural asymmetry—such as the difference between corrupting a historical record and harming a non-existent subject—that a memo format cannot fully resolve and that requires further philosophical deliberation beyond this institutional decision.
