I have been developing a long-form feature for a science-and-ideas magazine on why F. H. Bradley’s regress continues to resurface in contemporary debates about artificial cognition. In particular, I am struck by the possibility that the way large language models bind predicates to subjects—through distributed attention mechanisms rather than through any explicit relational structure—might reopen the classical problem of how a proposition achieves genuine unity instead of remaining a mere aggregate of terms. Before I speak with philosophers of mind and AI researchers, I need the conceptual architecture mapped out with precision, not as a literature review but as a working specification.

Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of propositional unity must satisfy if it is genuinely to solve Bradley’s regress without collapsing into an associative or merely syntactic account. Give me the full requirements list: the cases the theory must accommodate—relational judgments, negative existentials, universal generalizations, and the kind of vector-bound representations produced by transformer architectures—together with the specific consideration that makes each case non-negotiable. Spell out the distinctions the theory is forbidden to erase, such as that between the logical unity of a proposition and the psychological coherence of a belief system, or between formal structure and cognitive binding, and explain why blurring each distinction would defeat the purpose of the theory. Identify any results from formal logic, cognitive psychology, or philosophy of language that a viable account must remain consistent with, marking which are hard constraints and which are camp-specific preferences. Then tell me which single desideratum does the most damage to the standard modern contenders—whether Russell’s theory of external relations, the Fregean conception of unsaturated concepts, or structuralist treatments that reduce propositions to ordered pairs or frames—and briefly indicate where each of those responses stumbles on that particular test. I want the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a historical scorecard, and I am not asking for a bibliography, reading list, or citation of specific texts.
