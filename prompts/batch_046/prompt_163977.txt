2. Internal execution engine (Case B): Operates solely on an in-house order book with no external data feeds. Its trades, however, shift asset prices that are consumed by unrelated forecasting models elsewhere in the firm. Ruling: Denied certification; not low-impact.

The physician makes independent treatment decisions; the module never writes to the electronic health record or communicates with other clinical systems. Ruling: Certified low-impact.

4. Industrial load balancer (Case D): Adjusts power draw within a single facility using only internal sensors. The facility's reduced draw alters grid frequency, which is automatically corrected by other plants in the network; the balancer has no interface to those plants or to the grid operator. Ruling: Denied certification; not low-impact.

Air-gapped fine-tuning pipeline (Case E): Trains a model on a curated dataset in an isolated environment; the deployed model answers only within a predefined taxonomy through a sandboxed API, and its outputs are contractually prohibited from being used to train other systems. Ruling: Certified low-impact.

Now consider the proposed design (Case F): An irrigation controller for a single farm that adjusts on-site water valves using only soil-moisture sensors. The farm draws from a shared watershed; its changed consumption alters downstream river flow, which is monitored by an independent regional water authority. That authority makes its own allocation decisions for other farms based on the flow measurements. The controller has no network connection to the authority, to other farms, or to any external system.

Predict my architect's verdict on Case F — certified or denied — and defend the prediction with explicit reference to the track record. More importantly, reconstruct at least two distinct implicit rules that are each jointly consistent with all five prior rulings but that diverge on Case F.
