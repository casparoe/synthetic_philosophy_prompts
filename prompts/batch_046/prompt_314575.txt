A colleague summarized lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism and its bearing on the commensurability of pleasure and pain as follows: "The view assigns suffering lexical priority over pleasure, so that once any suffering occurs no amount of pleasure can outweigh it; this means the two are strictly incommensurable, lacking any common metric that would permit trade-offs. Because of that incommensurability, the view blocks the repugnant conclusion—one cannot justify creating suffering lives by adding enough mildly happy ones. But it also implies that a world containing a single brief pinprick is worse than a world of ten billion people in perfect bliss, since the suffering dimension dominates regardless of magnitude." Is this characterization fair? Then assess whether the argument that incommensurability follows from lexical priority is sound, and whether blocking the repugnant conclusion genuinely requires denying all comparability between pleasure and pain rather than only denying that pleasure can ever outweigh suffering above the threshold.
