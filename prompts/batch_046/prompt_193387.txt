Write an essay on whether destroying wild habitats—such as draining wetlands or clearing forests—tends to reduce the total suffering experienced by wild animals. The line of thought I keep encountering is that most wild lives are brief and filled with predation, starvation, and disease, so preventing future births by shrinking habitat should lower the overall amount of pain in the world. But I am unsure whether this treats an ecosystem like a single patient whose welfare can be summed up, when in reality there is no unified consciousness experiencing the ecosystem as a whole—only discrete individuals with their own separate lives. There is also the problem that destruction does not merely prevent births; it kills or displaces survivors into degraded edge environments where competition and stress may be worse, and the individuals who are never born are not the same ones who suffer now. Reach and defend a clear conclusion: does habitat destruction usually reduce wild-animal suffering, and does the absence of a collective subject of experience make the idea of “net ecosystem welfare” incoherent? Open the essay directly with your conclusion, then develop the reasoning immediately, with no introductory preamble and no restatement of the question.
