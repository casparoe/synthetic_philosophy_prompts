Write an exam question for a final-year undergraduate political-philosophy paper (45 minutes, 30 marks, one essay) on whether compatriots have special moral claims, focusing on the tension between associative-obligation justifications and democratic-authorship justifications of compatriot priority. The question must not be answerable by summarizing standard nationalist or cosmopolitan positions; instead, it should require students to confront a specific internal tension—namely, that if democratic authorship generates special obligations only among co-citizens who jointly author the laws, then either extending democratic authorship globally erases the boundary that justifies compatriot priority, or the democratic-authorship view collapses into an associative-obligation view that does not depend on democracy at all. Apply this specifically to obligations toward climate refugees seeking entry into a democratic welfare state, asking students to determine whether compatriot priority survives once democratic authorship is interpreted consistently.

Provide three things: (a) the exam question itself; (b) a model answer of about 700 words that makes the precise conceptual distinction, applies it to the climate-refugee case, and resolves the tension with explicit reasoning; (c) a marking scheme that separates first-class answers (which identify the collapse-or-globalize dilemma, apply it consistently, and avoid conflating the two justifications) from upper-second answers (which describe both views but miss the tension or apply them inconsistently). The marking scheme should name at least three specific misconceptions that should cost marks—for example, treating democratic authorship as purely instrumental rather than relational; conflating associative obligations with contractarian consent; or assuming that global democracy automatically entails open borders without addressing the authorship boundary. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations in the question or model answer, and ensure the entire task is answerable without online access.
