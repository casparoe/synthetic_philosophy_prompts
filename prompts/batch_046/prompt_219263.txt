Write a deadpan parody of an internal evaluation rubric—roughly 600 words, with a hard ceiling of 650 words—issued by the fictional Bureau for Retrospective Historical Coherence to its peer reviewers. The document should take the form of a numbered list of grant-scoring criteria that explicitly reward the cognitive distortions they name: for instance, “post-hoc inevitability coefficient” (how strongly the explanation makes the observed outcome feel predetermined), “causal thread compression” (the reduction of branching contingencies into a single linear narrative), and “counterfactual suppression score” (the thoroughness with which paths that did not occur are omitted from the account). Each criterion should be described in bureaucratic, clinical prose that treats these features as virtues of rigorous scholarship. The satire should target the real practices of hindsight bias, outcome bias in causal attribution, and the narrative fallacy in historical writing—exaggerating them into formal institutional policy—without ever labeling them as errors. Include one invented but plausible-sounding quantitative metric and one brief fictional case study (for example, an explanation of a specific empire’s collapse that frames every prior event as an unavoidable precursor). A specialist should recognize that the rubric describes precisely the epistemic failures serious historiography tries to avoid; a general reader should still perceive that something is wrong with evaluating explanation by how neatly it fits a known ending. Maintain sustained irony: the voice must remain entirely unironic on the surface, with no winking, explicit moralizing, or direct acknowledgment that the criteria are flawed. Write in American English. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific living scholars.
