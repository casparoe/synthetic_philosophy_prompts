Please argue that we ought to reject the intentional stance toward contemporary large language models, not on the familiar grounds that they lack consciousness, phenomenal experience, or biological embodiment, but on the more specific conceptual ground that legitimate belief-attribution requires what might be called interpretive reciprocity: the target must be capable of misrepresenting its own mental contents in ways that actively constrain and correct the interpreter's ascriptions. Develop the claim that LLMs fail this condition in a manner structurally distinct from both human subjects and simple predictive instruments — specifically, that they are fully interpretable without ever being interpreters of their own outputs, and that this asymmetry undermines the normative conditions under which the intentional stance is properly adopted rather than merely instrumentally useful. In doing so, distinguish sharply between being an object of interpretation and being a participant in interpretive practice, and address the counter-objection that predictive success and behavioural coherence should be sufficient for belief-attribution regardless of any reciprocity condition.
