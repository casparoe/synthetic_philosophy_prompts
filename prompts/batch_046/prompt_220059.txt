I'm writing up my philosophical profile on historical representation for a workshop on aesthetics and cultural heritage, and I want a consistency check before I circulate it. Here is where I have landed: (1) The dead lack moral standing, so any ethical obligations in dramatizing them are obligations to the living—particularly to audiences who may mistake fiction for record, and to descendant communities for whom the figure remains a living ancestor. (2) Despite (1), I think there is a non-derivative duty of historical fidelity that binds creators regardless of audience effects; it is closer to an epistemic virtue than to a harm-avoidance principle. (3) This duty is graded: it is stringent for figures with dense archival traces, and considerably weaker for figures reconstructed mainly from oral tradition or fragmentary material. (4) Fabricating undocumented private dialogue or emotional interiority is permissible provided it remains consistent with the documented external behaviour of the figure and does not ascribe false motives. (5) If a dramatist depicts a documented figure expressing a view that the historical record clearly contradicts—say, attributing to a known abolitionist a defence of slavery—this is not artistic licence but a form of mnemonic harm that corrupts collective memory. (6) The ethical weight of a work should be assessed partly through its genre contract with the audience: a film marketed as 'based on true events' incurs stricter obligations than one labelled 'inspired by' them. (7) Yet I also think that external marketing categories are epistemically unreliable guides, since promotional framing is shaped by distributors rather than creators; therefore, evaluation should turn on the work's internal framing devices—disclaimers, anachronistic narration, visible counterfactual markers—rather than on its commercial classification.

Please audit this set: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear so, and what is the least costly revision for each real conflict? I am particularly interested in whether (2) can be sustained alongside (1) without collapsing into a purely instrumental account, and whether (4) is compatible with (3) once we notice that fragmentary records offer less behavioural constraint. Also, please rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each what must give, and say which single revision would clean up the most inconsistencies. Do not treat all conflicts as equally damaging.
