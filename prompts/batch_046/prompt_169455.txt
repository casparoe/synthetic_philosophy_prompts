For an upper-level philosophy of work final exam, draft one essay question that cannot be answered by summarizing Arendt's labor/work/action distinction or Marx's account of alienated labor alone. Present a hypothetical: fully automated fabrication systems produce durable, world-constituting objects (Arendtian "work") with no biological necessity driving them (no "labor") and no embedded human deliberation or public speech in their design or governance (no "action"). Ask students whether this collapses Arendt's taxonomy or reveals a hidden dependency among her categories, and whether Marx's concept of alienation applies when the worker is absent rather than exploited. Provide a model answer of roughly 700 words and a marking scheme built around five must-hit analytical moves, specifying the marks each carries (total 25) and the characteristic mistake that loses them. The difficulty should come from applying the categories to the case, not from recalling labels. End with a one-line summary of the marking scheme's logic on its own line beginning with Verdict:.
