After retiring from physics I got into philosophy of science, and I keep seeing people cite Wigner's old point about mathematics being unreasonbly effective in physics like its some deep mystery. I think its mostly a framing error: we have quietly restricted physics to domains where continuous symmetry groups apply, and then we act surprised that Lie algebras describe them well. The effectiveness is manufactured by our choice of formalisim, not discovered in nature. So what would have to be true for this to be wrong? I want an honest exposure map—what observations, formal results, or conceptual moves would actually damage this view, not just make it awkward? Is there a live case where a system yields precise predictions but genuinely resists any continuous-math representation? And what do defenders of the Wigner mystery themselves concede would count as a real defeater? I need to know whether my position is well-insulated or just dodging the problem, and where the risk actually sits.
