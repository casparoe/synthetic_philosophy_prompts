I'm working through my commitments on Machiavelli's account of fortune and virtù before a discussion group, and I'd like a consistency audit rather than an expository lecture. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Fortune is not merely epistemic uncertainty but includes genuinely open-ended contingency in political outcomes—there are branches of the future that are not just unknown but structurally undetermined.
(2) Virtù is a non-moral adaptive capacity, closer to strategic flexibility and institutional design than to Aristotelian moral excellence.
(3) Machiavelli's claim that fortune governs about half of human affairs should be read as a heuristic calibration for decision-makers, not as a quantitative or metaphysical thesis.
(4) The best modern formalization of virtù is robustness-under-uncertainty in decision theory—structuring institutions and actions to perform well across unknown distributions of outcomes.
(5) Renaissance humanists generally conceived fortune through a revived pagan fatalism rather than through anything analogous to modern probabilistic risk analysis.

My questions: Do these hang together? I'm particularly worried that (4) anachronistically imports normative and mathematical machinery that Machiavelli would not recognize, which might undermine (2)'s claim that virtù is non-moral strategic adaptation. There's also a possible conflict between (1) and (3): if outcomes contain genuinely open-ended contingency, can there be a stable, meaningful 'half'? Meanwhile, (5) seems to pull against (4) from a different direction—if the conceptual framework was fatalist, applying modern robustness theory might be doubly anachronistic. But perhaps some of these tensions dissolve once the distinctions are sharpened.

Please audit the set: for each pair in real tension, rank them by severity, specify what would have to give, and say what the cheapest revision costs. Also identify any pair that only looks tense but isn't. Don't treat any commitment as sacred—if the decision-theoretic framing has to go, say so. And please push back plainly if the request rests on a confused premise, for instance if I'm forcing coherence onto commitments that belong in incompatible conceptual spaces, or if Machiavelli's text doesn't actually support the ontology of contingency I'm attributing to it. I want the audit, not polite compliance with a false problem.
