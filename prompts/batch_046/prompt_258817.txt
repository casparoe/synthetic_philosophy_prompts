We got into epistemic game theory — you know, the stuff where payoffs depend on what you think I think you'll do — and then we started inventing this scenario for a story and I got kind of obsessed.

Can you write a short story, maybe 1000 words or so, set in a small orbital archive called the Lattice? There are six archivists maintaining a shared climate reactor. They can't talk to each other directly; they only recieve probabilistic updates about the others' rationality scores on private screens. The reactor is a public good — anyone can draw power for research, but if everyone draws too much it collapses. The catch is that the screens are noisy, so there's never perfect common knowledge. One archivist, Mira, starts noticing that the reactor is being underused, not overused. She realizes they've hit an epistemic fixed point: everyone beleives everyone else will withhold energy, so they all withhold, expecting cooperation that never comes because no one is sure anyone is sure anyone is rational. The story should focus on her trying to break the loop without direct communication, and on the moment where second-order belief fractures.
