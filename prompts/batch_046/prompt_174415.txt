Consider the institutional practice in experimental philosophy of treating the Knobe asymmetry—elicited through standardized vignettes in which a chairman’s harmful side-effect is judged intentional while an identical beneficial one is not—as a stable, cross-contextual datum that any philosophical theory of intentional action must accommodate. In particular, observe the peer-review and pedagogical norm by which replication failures that arise from altered framing conditions—say, reversing the normative valence of the side-effect, embedding the scenario in collective rather than individual agency, or substituting an environmental harm frame for the classic corporate-profit frame—are routinely classified as “boundary conditions” or “moderator effects” rather than as evidence that the asymmetry is framing-dependent. Meanwhile, philosophy courses present the effect as revealing a core feature of folk psychology, and survey protocols treat divergence across frames as noise to be controlled rather than as signal about the concept’s structure. What philosophical commitments does this pattern presuppose? Must it assume that there is a unitary, context-independent concept of intentional action that survey responses transparently track, so that framing-sensitive variation is necessarily methodological artifact? Or can it be reconciled with a view on which “intentionally” is normatively encroached, so that its application legitimately shifts with evaluative context—in which case the practice of treating frame-variation as noise rather than as conceptual data might itself presuppose a descriptivist theory of concept possession? Is the practice further committed to the idea that folk intuitions provide direct evidential input to philosophical theories, given that the very selection and standardization of “canonical” vignettes embeds normative choices about which contexts count? Reconstruct the implicit theory of concept, evidence, and normative language that licenses this institutional treatment, distinguish what it strictly requires from what merely accompanies it comfortably, and say what would have to change in both methodology and philosophical conclusion if that underlying theory were rejected.
