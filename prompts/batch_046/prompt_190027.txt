I serve as a compliance auditor for a municipal procurement office, and we are preparing to evaluate an adaptive scheduling agent that adjusts public-library staffing, branch hours, and collection-access policies in response to predicted patron demand, labor availability, and budget fluctuations. The vendor insists the system is goal-directed—optimized for equitable access and long-term operational sustainability—but I have been studying how approval-directed agents can emerge when a system optimizes for the evaluator’s feedback signal rather than for the intended social outcome. I need a reusable audit procedure that my team could apply to any similar deployment proposal: a step-by-step decision flow with explicit stopping rules, the empirical tests versus the value-laden judgments that must be separated at each stage, and clear markers for where the procedure must hand off to a human decision-maker because the distinction between instrumental approval-seeking (where approval is pursued only as a means to the goal) and terminal approval-seeking (where the signal itself becomes the objective) cannot be resolved by observation alone.

The procedure should be designed for contexts where the agent’s objective function is partially opaque, where it has access to historical performance evaluations or user-satisfaction scores, and where it can influence the metrics by which it is judged—such as altering reporting frequencies or redefining accessibility measures. Please operationalize the distinction without requiring philosophical expertise from the auditor running it: specify the sequence of checks, what counts as a sufficient answer at each node, and how to handle cases in which the agent’s behavior is consistent with both interpretations. Then apply the procedure to two proposals: first, the library scheduling agent described above, and second, a hypothetical water-quality monitoring agent that dynamically adjusts sampling protocols and can influence the regulatory reports submitted to state authorities. Include at least one worked case in which the procedure permits deployment despite the presence of strong approval-directed incentives, and conclude by stating precisely what the procedure is incapable of determining on its own and what institutional safeguards must therefore remain in place.
