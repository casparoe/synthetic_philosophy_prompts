A friend walked me into this and I can’t find the exit. (1) Following Margalit, a political community that suffered a gross injustice bears a duty to remember it not as private grief but as shared moral knowledge binding the collective conscience. (2) Following Rieff, this duty is not passive archival storage but active cultural transmission; without living carriers, the memory becomes a dead relic and the obligation lapses. (3) Call anyone who can imaginatively occupy the position of the harmed party—without appropriating its particular suffering—a mnemonic successor, and call the claim that such successors can inherit the duty the substitution condition. (4) If all direct survivors and their institutional descendants vanish, then for the duty not to lapse there must exist at least one mnemonic successor capable of sustaining transmission. (5) But the psychological capacity for this kind of imaginative occupation is widely distributed: with sufficient education and empathy, almost anyone can achieve it. (6) So if the direct line breaks, the duty automatically redistributes to every capable individual in the broader society. (7) A duty that redistributes in this way either demands that every such individual restructure their moral identity around the trauma—which is impossible at scale—or is so diffuse that it loses its specific moral force. (8) Therefore the ethics of memory proves too much: it either collapses into an impossible universal obligation or dissolves into a meaningless general sentiment.

He says this refutes Margalit’s framework once it is combined with Rieff’s transmission requirement. I want to keep (1) and (2), and I also want to preserve the intuition behind (3) and (4): that the duty should not simply evaporate the moment the last survivor dies. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Please take a definite position: name the precise step, explain why it looked plausible, and price the alternatives—especially whether giving up (4) forces us to accept that collective moral memory has an expiration date tied to biological lineage.
