Write an essay evaluating whether Gödel's rotating-universe argument succeeds in undermining the objective reality of temporal passage, or whether it merely exposes the limitations of a particular global coordinate representation. Central to your analysis should be the following conceptual scenario: an observer restricted to a causally isolated local region measures proper time, entropy gradients, and causal succession in a manner entirely indistinguishable from measurements made in a flat Minkowski spacetime lacking closed timelike curves. Call this condition chronometric isolation. Given that such an observer possesses no internal empirical access to the global presence or absence of closed timelike curves, examine whether Gödel is entitled to treat the failure of global time-ordering as a metaphysical discovery about time itself, rather than as an artifact of extending local physical descriptions to cosmological scales. Weigh the counter-argument that if local temporal structure remains invariant across rotating and non-rotating models, then the non-existence of a universal temporal parameter reflects a property of our theoretical representations rather than a feature of reality that negates passage. You should reach and defend a definite conclusion: either Gödel's inference from cosmological geometry to the unreality of time is sound despite chronometric isolation, or it overreaches by privileging global coordinate structure over invariant local physics.
