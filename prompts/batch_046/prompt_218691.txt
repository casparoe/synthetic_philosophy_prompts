They also had to address whether the temporary intrinsics problem undermines the endurantist claim that the same person is wholly present at year zero and year ten, and to say something about what the inactive backup implies for identity.

The student wrote this: Endurantism is the view that objects exist at different times by being wholly present at each of them, so a person at t1 and t2 is literally the same thing, not made of parts spread across time. Perdurantism says objects are four-dimensional worms made of temporal parts or stages, so at any given moment only one slice is present. For the brain replacement case, the endurantist can say the person survives because they are still wholly there, just with different material. The perdurantist would say the person is the whole worm from year zero to ten, but the stage at year ten is a different temporal part, so in a sense it's not the same person present, which seems wrong.

About temporary intrinsics: this is when an object has incompatible properties like being tired and then awake. The endurantist avoids this problem because since the person is wholly present they can't have contradictory properties at the same time, they just stay the same. Meanwhile perdurantists have to deal with it by saying different temporal parts have the properties. Actually I think Lewis said this was a problem for endurantism but he solved it with temporal parts? No wait, he used temporal parts for perdurantism. Anyway, the backup at year five doesn't matter for either view because it's never activated, so there's no branching. But the endurantist has to explain why the backup doesn't mean there are two people at year ten, and they can do this by saying the original is still wholly present and the backup is just a potential copy.
