I am preparing a proposal for a six-month research fellowship in normative economics and political philosophy. My working idea is this: the literature on safe Pareto improvements treats safety as a property of an outcome relative to a fixed profile of individual welfare levels, but it rarely examines reforms that alter the very metric by which welfare is assessed—what I am tentatively calling a "metric-shifting" safe Pareto improvement. I want to ask whether the safety condition retains normative force when the baseline and post-reform states are evaluated under different frameworks (for instance, preference satisfaction versus capability achievement), and whether any non-circular justification can be given for privileging one metric over another in applying the safety constraint.

Please write a formal proposal of approximately 1,200–1,500 words that includes: (1) a precise, answerable statement of the research question; (2) an explanation of why existing analyses of Pareto robustness and safety conditions in social choice and mechanism design leave this specific problem open; (3) the methodological approach—conceptual clarification of the safety condition across metrics, supplemented by simple formal models of two-agent, two-metric cases to test for consistency and collapse; (4) what a successful result would demonstrate (for example, a principled restriction on metric choice) and what would count as failure (for instance, if the condition collapses into framework-relativism or requires an implausible meta-metric); and (5) an honest assessment of scope and timeline for six months, including where the project risks becoming unmanageable. The proposal should be written in British English. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; you may refer to positions, distinctions, and arguments in general terms without attributing them to specific authors or texts.
