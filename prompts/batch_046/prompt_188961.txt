Here are the notes. Generalism, on this model, implicitly assumes that agents possess either a tractable decision procedure or access to pre-computed universal rules that collapse the search space. Particularism, by contrast, denies that such rules exist and insists on context-sensitive judgment—but philosophers usually treat this as a claim about the ontology of moral reasons, not about cognitive resources. I want to argue that particularism is better understood as a form of computational humility: it acknowledges that human agents solve the moral frame problem not by exhaustive search but by narrative salience, selecting which features matter through story-structured attention rather than algorithmic optimization. The niche turn is to suggest that morality is therefore structurally literary: the "frame problem" in AI ethics is solved in human life by the same conventions that govern narrative coherence—plot relevance, character consistency, and dramatic economy—rather than by formal computation. This doesn't reduce ethics to fiction, but it does imply that any philosophical defense of generalism must either demonstrate a tractable moral algorithm or admit that it relies on an unrealistic model of human reasoning.
