Two friends of mine, Alex and Jordan, got into it over drinks about Humeanism vs anti-Humeanism about laws. Alex said laws are just the best systematization of actual regularities, so if there's a pocket universe that never interacts with ours, it doesnt share our laws because there's no regularity spanning them. Jordan said thats weird — then whether something is lawlike depends on whats happening elsewhere in the multiverse, and laws should be local. Alex said anti-Humeans like Armstrong make it worse: they claim laws are necessary relations between universals, but then have to explain why those universals dont govern in empty space, so they sneak locality back in. Jordan countered that at least on that view a law can hold in isolation if the universals are present, whereas on Alex's view you need the whole mosaic. Alex said Jordan's treating the mosaic like a database query, and Humeanism is more modest because it avoids mysterious necessary connections. Jordan said modesty isnt truth, and Humeanism cant distinguish accidental regularities from laws in small isolated regions without looking outside them. Can you go through their exchange step by step and say who's right on each point, not just who wins? What are they both missing?
