Every abstract algebra textbook I've seen treats Zorn's lemma like a utility knife: you pull it out, you get your maximal ideal or your vector-space basis, and then you put it away. Nobody stops to say its equivalent to the axiom of choice, and theres never a footnote about wether using it commits you to some weird ontology of infinite sets. What view of mathematical objects and proof is that convention actually presupposing? Is it straight-up platonism — that every set has these hidden maximal elements just waiting to be revealed — or could a formalist who thinks math is just symbol-pushing use the same black-box move without contradiction? Reconstruct the implicit philosophy baked into teh curriculum, say whether the teaching practice genuinely forces one camp or is philosophically underdetermined, and describe what an honest course that took the choice debates seriously would look like — do you ban Zorn, or just change how you present it?
