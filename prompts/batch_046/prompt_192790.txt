I have been working through an idea I encountered recently: that the only things which genuinely matter are the absence of craving and the absence of suffering. Achievement, recognition, and even pleasure seem, on this view, to be either temporary relief from craving or fresh sources of it. I now face a concrete choice. I have been offered a senior position at a growing firm; it promises substantial wealth, public recognition, and the satisfaction of building something significant, yet it also demands constant competition, long hours, and the perpetual feeling that I must reach the next milestone merely to maintain my standing. My current life is modest — I earn enough to live comfortably, my work is unremarkable, and I feel little urgency to advance — so if I take the role I gain what most people call success, but according to this view I may only be multiplying my cravings and anxieties, whereas if I decline I preserve calm yet fear I might be disguising cowardice or a refusal to grow as philosophical wisdom. How ought I to weigh these considerations? Is it genuinely rational to forgo conventional achievement in order to minimise both craving and suffering, or does that reasoning rest on a confusion about what makes a life go well?
