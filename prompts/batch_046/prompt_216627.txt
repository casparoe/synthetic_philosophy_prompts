I've been working through a long summary of predictive processing and the free energy principle that a colleague in my philosophy of science seminar circulated, and I'm trying to determine which parts are defensible interpretations and which are overextensions. The summary begins by identifying predictive processing—associated with thinkers like Clark and Hohwy—with Friston's free energy principle, stating that both are versions of the same claim: the brain minimizes variational free energy by updating hierarchical predictions against sensory prediction errors, effectively treating perception as a form of controlled hallucination corrected by bottom-up signals. It then asserts that action is not driven by motor commands but by predicting the proprioceptive and exteroceptive consequences of movement, with the body moving to fulfill those predictions; consequently, the summary claims, there is no meaningful distinction between intending an action and performing it, only differences in the precision weighting assigned to prediction errors at various hierarchical levels. The summary introduces expected free energy as the quantity that governs policy selection, and argues that because minimizing expected free energy involves both pragmatic and epistemic terms, agents are necessarily driven toward information-seeking—what it calls epistemic foraging—meaning that curiosity and risk-avoidance are not opposing motives but the same computation evaluated at different precision levels. Finally, the summary concludes that since the free energy principle applies to any system that maintains its boundary conditions against entropy, consciousness is not a special biological property but merely the high-precision internal model that such a system has of itself, which means the hard problem of consciousness is dissolved rather than solved, and that this framework renders representational theories of mind obsolete because there are no internal representations, only continuous error-minimization dynamics. Something about the slide from neural mechanism to metaphysical conclusion strikes me as too quick, and I suspect the identification of predictive coding with the free energy principle might be glossing over important differences, but I don't have the technical background to pinpoint the errors. Is this a fair summary of the framework?
