I have adopted a specific, somewhat unorthodox reading of Schopenhauer's metaphysics of music. On this view, music is not merely the highest art because it bypasses Platonic Ideas to copy the Will directly; rather, its internal structures—melody as the analog of desiring, harmony as the analog of the grades of objectification, rhythm as the analog of temporal persistence—form a non-representational isomorphism with the dynamics of willing itself. Consequently, emotional response to music is not an interpretation projected onto sound but a mode of self-knowledge in which the listener's own Will recognizes its dynamics made audible, temporarily dissolving the subject-object distinction in a way representational arts cannot achieve. Tell me what would count against this position—not just the familiar objection that music clearly represents or depicts emotions symbolically, but which specific empirical findings (say, in cross-cultural music cognition or neuroscience), which conceptual tensions within Schopenhauer's own system, and which results in adjacent domains (the philosophy of self-knowledge, aesthetics of other non-representational forms) would be decisive versus merely awkward. Which predictions does this isomorphism view make that rival accounts—expressive, representational, arousal-based—do not? Has the position been insulated by qualifications, for instance by claiming that the isomorphism operates only at a pre-conceptual level, and is that insulation a strength or a weakness? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely inconvenient, and indicate which ones a committed defender of this Schopenhauerian view would themselves concede as damaging versus which they would dismiss as missing the point.
