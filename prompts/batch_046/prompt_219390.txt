I'm delivering the retirement toast next Saturday for Dr. Elena Voss, who is stepping down after thirty years directing restoration at the Harbor Archive and Maritime Museum. The dinner is being held in the museum's boathouse, with the restored 1892 pilot cutter—known in the collection as the Tern—visible through the glass wall behind the head table, which makes the philosophical problem unavoidable rather than decorative. Roughly half the guests are family members, local patrons, and former apprentices who understand her work as patient craft; the other half are philosophers of art, conservators, and historians who have engaged with her published arguments about material continuity and know that she rejects both essentialist identity conditions and purely functional accounts of artifact survival.

The structural difficulty is that the Tern now contains not a single original plank, and Voss deliberately incorporated salvaged timbers from two sister vessels lost in the same 1923 storm, so the hull is literally spliced from three separate origins. She has consistently maintained that asking whether the restored vessel is "the same ship" is the wrong question altogether, and I need the toast to honor that stance without resolving it. Specifically, I must avoid saying she "saved the original," since that would falsely assert material continuity; I must avoid calling it a replica or reconstruction, since that would erase the custodial labor she regards as genuinely continuous with the ship's working life; and I should not pretend that gradual replacement solves the problem, because the composite provenance breaks even that narrative. The speech needs to do real structural work—shaping what can and cannot be claimed about her career—while remaining comprehensible and moving to guests who have never read a word of metaphysics. Can you write a five-minute toast that navigates the Ship of Theseus as a live constraint rather than an anecdote, that lands affectionately for both audiences, and that finds language to praise her achievement without committing to either identity claim she spent her career undermining?
