I've been trying to extend the Darwin-Turing inversion to epistemology and now I'm stuck in a loop. Here's the argument: (1) Darwin showed you can get functional design without a designer, just selection. (2) Turing showed you can get problem-solving behavior without understanding, just mechanism. (3) So there's a general pattern: competance in X doesn't entail comprehension of X. (4) Human scientists clearly have competance in reasoning about nature and building theories. (5) Apply (3): their reasoning competance doesn't entail they actually comprehend reasoning or why it works. (6) But then when I accept Darwin's or Turing's argument, I'm using reasoning I don't comprehend. (7) So either I don't really understand the inversion (undermining my belief in it), or I do comprehend reasoning (violating (3) applied to myself). (8) The only escape is saying the inversion applies to biological/computational domains but not rational agents, but that looks like special pleading since human brains are biological computation. Where's the slip? Is (3) overgeneralized, or is there a hidden scope error in applying it reflexivly? I'd rather keep (3) general and drop something else, but I don't want to just carve out an exception for humans.
