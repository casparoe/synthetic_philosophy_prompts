I'm drafting a novel in which a philosopher of mind and a cognitive neuroscientist are snowbound in an alpine lodge, arguing whether consciousness is best understood as a global broadcast or as a private qualitative residue. For the chapter's epigraph, I need a poem—fourteen lines of unrhymed iambic pentameter with a decisive volta at line nine—that does not merely allude to the access-phenomenal distinction in the shadow of global workspace theory, but structurally performs it. The octave should construct Baars's architecture: information selected, integrated, made globally available for report, reasoning, and action—access consciousness rendered as a luminous newsroom where every module can read the headline. At the volta, the poem should pivot to phenomenal overflow, invoking the Sperling partial-report paradigm: a grid of twelve letters flashed for an instant, the subject certain she saw them all, yet able to report only four—suggesting a richness that exceeds the workspace's bandwidth. The sestet should treat this surplus not as an additional broadcastable item, but as an untransmitted qualitative vehicle, a "silent chamber" that registers its own representational state without releasing it into the global stream. The closing couplet should refuse resolution, asking what remains for the saying if the workspace explains only what can be said. Render the concepts precisely enough that a specialist recognizes Block's distinction and the Sperling argument, yet keep the verse readable as verse. Write in American English. After the poem, provide a brief note—separate from the verse—distinguishing settled points (for instance, that global workspace theory is generally read as an account of access consciousness) from genuinely contested ones (whether phenomenal consciousness overflows access, whether broadcasting necessarily entails phenomenal presence), and identify any liberties taken, particularly the framing of the unbroadcast residue as a representational vehicle rather than content, which leans on contested self-representational readings.
