At a philosophy reading group, two members, Maya and Leo, argued over origin essentialism after discussing whether a person could have come from different gametes. Maya defended the standard essentialist line: if the particular sperm and egg that actually formed you had been different, you would not exist; your origin is essential to your identity. Leo offered a counter-case. Imagine an IVF conception in which sperm S is paired with egg E, but due to an undetected laboratory error the zygote actually forms with sperm S and egg E', where E' is genetically indistinguishable from E except for a silent mutation in a non-coding region and different mitochondrial DNA drawn from the same donor pool. The child develops normally and lives a full life. Decades later the error is discovered. Leo said that if origin essentialism is true, this person was never the child of the intended parents in the metaphysically relevant sense, despite being psychologically and socially continuous with the child everyone raised. That, he claimed, makes origin essentialism absurd: it ties identity to hidden cellular tokens that play no causal or developmental role in producing the person. Maya replied that Leo confuses discovery with constitution; learning about the substitution reveals who they always were, not that they changed. She added that Leo's appeal to "causal-developmental continuity" misses the point, because the actual egg E' is precisely what causally developed into the organism, so continuity still picks out the token origin rather than a generic type. Leo then pressed a further case: suppose in nature, not in a lab, two eggs are released and one is fertilised accidentally by sperm intended for the other, with no social intention tracking either. Maya said the actually fertilised egg still fixes the origin. Leo answered that this shows her view collapses into "whatever actually happened determines identity," which makes the essentialist claim trivial rather than metaphysically substantive. They turned to me and asked for a referee's verdict. In particular, address whether the IVF case genuinely undermines token-origin essentialism or merely confuses epistemology with metaphysics, and whether Leo's natural-accident case makes Maya's position trivial. Please also say what argument or consideration would most change your mind here, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
