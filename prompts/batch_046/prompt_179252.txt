When I objected that these claims lack the corroborating archaeological, numismatic, and independent textual evidence that mainstream chronology enjoys, he waved off my objection with the line: “Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence.” I want to understand precisely what that sentence could be doing.

Please treat the slogan not as an unambiguous principle but as a family of distinct theses. Lay out the different precise claims it might express—for instance, interpretations about Bayesian prior adjustment, about asymmetrical burdens of proof in scientific methodology, about sociological thresholds for peer-reviewed publication, about the logical fallacy of arguing from ignorance, about probabilistic calibration under radical skepticism, and about rhetorical moves that terminate inquiry rather than evaluate claims.
