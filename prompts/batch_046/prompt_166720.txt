I serve as chair of a mid-sized city’s zoning appeals board, hearing two or three contested rezoning cases each week with no research staff and only ninety minutes per hearing. I need a reusable decision procedure—ordered tests in flowchart form—that operationalizes the principle of urban membership: zoning is not neutral land-use regulation but a boundary practice defining who belongs to the city, so a legitimate decision must satisfy both non-exclusionary morphology (density and form rules must not operate as economic filters) and temporal representation (the interests of future, not-yet-resident members must be weighed, not only current owners). For each step, specify what counts as a pass or fail, flag exactly where judgment replaces procedure, and state when to deny, approve with conditions, or hand the case to the full council because values are incommensurable. Apply it to three proposals: upzoning a single-family block near transit to four-story mixed-use over “neighborhood character” objections; a height bonus for fifteen percent affordable units with a thirty-year covenant expiration; and a market-rate tower with no affordability mechanism that nevertheless scores high on morphological openness—explain why the procedure still approves it despite my likely opposition, and what the result reveals about the procedure’s limits. Conclude by listing the disputes this framework is not designed to settle.
