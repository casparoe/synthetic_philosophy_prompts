You are a senior advisor in philosophy of language and applied ethics serving on the Interdisciplinary Review Board for Linguistic Technologies at a major research university. The board must issue a formal written opinion recommending whether to approve, reject, or approve with conditions Protocol 7-B: a proposed field deployment of an AI translation system for an undocumented language in emergency medical settings.

The researchers propose a radical-translation architecture inspired by Quine’s thesis of the indeterminacy of translation. Because no bilingual speakers or prior corpora exist, the system constructs translation manuals solely from behavioral dispositions, stimulus conditions, and holistic coherence constraints—there is no independent fact of the matter about whether the native term for a reported symptom (functioning analogously to Quine’s classic gavagai scenario) refers to tissue damage, subjective distress, or a social-communicative role. The system will generate real-time triage recommendations. The research team argues that indeterminacy is benign in practice because all admissible manuals converge on identical clinical actions; dissenting board members counter that in high-stakes consent and liability contexts, even slight divergence in semantic content—such as whether a term denotes an internal state versus an externally observable behavior—could alter the ethical validity of patient interaction and institutional responsibility.

Please think carefully and begin with a brief brainstorming outline of the conceptual distinctions you will rely on (for instance, stimulus-meaning versus sentence-meaning, analytic hypotheses, and the relation between ontological relativity and practical decision-making). Then draft the board’s decision memo. The memo should contain: (1) a clear recommendation with a concrete course of action and an explicit timeline or conditions; (2) a rigorous but accessible explanation of how Quinean indeterminacy specifically bears on this protocol’s validity, written so that the researchers can respond to it substantively without needing external references; (3) an honest and serious treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, specifically addressing whether practical convergence among translation manuals is sufficient to neutralize the philosophical worry in clinical deployment; and (4) a formal institutional tone appropriate for circulation before a vote. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or appeals to authority. Ensure your reasoning is self-contained and does not depend on niche theoretical apparatus beyond what is explained within the memo itself.
