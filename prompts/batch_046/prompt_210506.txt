I'm trying to sort out my actual commitments about temporal ontology by testing them against concrete relativistic scenarios, and the pattern I get doesn't line up neatly with the standard block-versus-growing-block menu. Here are my gut verdicts, case by case.

First, the classic Rietdijk-Putnam setup: two observers in relative motion disagree on whether a distant stellar explosion is simultaneous with their respective "now" moments. If presentism is true, then for one observer the explosion is real and for the other it is not, which strikes me as a straightforward reductio—so presentism is false, and eternalism is correct because it makes all spacetime events equally real regardless of frame. Second, however, if someone saves presentism by positing a privileged cosmic rest frame—say, the CMB frame—that defines the unique global present, even if that frame is locally undetectable, I think the view becomes coherent again; I dislike the hidden structure, but I don't think it's self-contradictory. Third, apply the same frame-dependence worry to the growing-block view: its "edge" of reality is a three-dimensional slice, so different observers slice reality differently, which seems ontologically more extravagant than presentism's two-dimensional slice; I'd reject growing block even if I were willing to accept the privileged-frame version of presentism. Fourth, inject quantum indeterminacy: suppose two entangled measurements occur in regions with frame-dependent temporal ordering. If the outcomes are genuinely open—indeterminate—before measurement, then the frame-dependence of "which comes first" doesn't affect ontological status, since neither outcome is fixed yet; but if the outcomes are already determinate and merely unknown to us, then frame-dependence makes determinacy observer-relative, which I find intolerable. So frame-dependence is harmless for indeterminacy but fatal for determinacy. Fifth, consider a macroscopic rod extended in space and at rest in one inertial frame. In another frame moving relative to it, the relativity of simultaneity implies that different spatial parts of the rod belong to different hyperplanes of simultaneity—some parts lie in the "present," others in the "past" or "future" of a given observer's now. If presentism holds, the rod literally disintegrates across frames: its parts do not co-exist. I think this is a serious, perhaps decisive, problem for presentism, unless one denies that spatial parts have independent ontological status. Sixth, there's a view I'm unsure how to classify—call it foliated eternalism: the four-dimensional block exists in full, but conscious experience traverses it along a hidden hyperplane defined by a metaphysically privileged but physically undetectable foliation. Is this genuinely eternalism with an epiphenomenal spotlight, or just presentism with extra dimensions? I go back and forth; sometimes it feels like a coherent middle path, sometimes like presentism in disguise.

Is there a single principle—perhaps about when frame-dependence is ontologically permissible versus epistemically harmless—that delivers verdicts one through five and tells me what to say about case six? Or do I have to drop at least one of my judgments?
