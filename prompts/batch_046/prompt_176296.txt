Jeffrey conditionalization updates beliefs by redistributing credence across a partition of possibilities—say, {E₁, E₂}—while preserving, within each cell, the prior conditional ratios between hypotheses. Suppose I receive noisy evidence that bears uncertainly on whether a measurement apparatus is miscalibrated (E₁) or accurate (E₂), and I wish to update my beliefs about two competing theoretical models, T₁ and T₂, both of which assign different predictions to the apparatus's output under either calibration state. Because Jeffrey's rigidity constraint fixes P(T₁|Eᵢ)/P(T₂|Eᵢ) for each i, it appears that any shift in my credences about E₁ and E₂ applies an identical multiplicative factor to the overall odds of T₁ versus T₂, leaving their relative plausibility unchanged regardless of how the evidence weighs on calibration. Does this mean that Jeffrey conditionalization is structurally incapable of allowing uncertain evidence about instrumental conditions to rationally discriminate between theories that make predictions in both conditions? Or is there a feature of the rigidity condition—perhaps involving non-uniform conditional distributions across the partition—that permits differential updating of theoretical beliefs despite the local preservation of ratios?
