I've been trying to apply Benatar's asymmetry consistently across a modal framework: for any genetic profile that could have yielded a conscious subject, the corresponding non-existent being lacks pleasure (not bad) and lacks pain (good). Extended over the infinite space of possible people, this seems to generate an uncountable background of non-bad missing pleasures and good missing pains. So when we create an actual person who suffers, we don't merely fail to justify that suffering by the pleasures they experience; we also disrupt an infinitely large modal balance in which non-existence was, on net, preferable. But this seems to rely on counting merely possible subjects — exactly the move Benatar's person-affecting restriction should forbid. Does this mean the asymmetry is self-defeating once we take its modal implications seriously, or is there a principled way to block the aggregation without abandoning the intuition that absence of pleasure isn't a harm?
