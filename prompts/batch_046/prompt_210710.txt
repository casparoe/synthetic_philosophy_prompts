Compare the coherence-filter model and the causal-trace model as frameworks for distinguishing genuine moral preferences from moral biases. In the coherence-filter model, a judgment is classified as a bias when it fails to achieve reflective equilibrium with an agent’s other considered judgments after procedural idealization—where informational deficits, emotional turbulence, and social pressure are corrected—whereas a preference is defined as what survives this filtration intact. In the causal-trace model, by contrast, a judgment is a bias if its dominant causal etiology traces back to non-moral adaptive mechanisms—such as kin-selection heuristics, contamination-avoidance responses, or in-group coordination pressures—while a genuine preference is one sustained by a rational justification chain that remains stable even when the evolutionary or psychological origin is explicitly bracketed.

Your task is to compare these two models specifically on the problem of circularity and the problem of baseline selection. First, explain why the coherence-filter model risks circularity: the idealization conditions themselves appear to embed prior normative commitments about which psychological disturbances count as corrupting biases rather than legitimate preference-forming processes. Second, explain why the causal-trace model faces a baseline problem: isolating a judgment’s “true” causal origin requires selecting a counterfactual baseline of purely rational agency that is itself morally contested and empirically underdetermined. Third, evaluate whether either model offers a genuinely non-circular criterion, or whether both collapse into a third position—perhaps a pragmatic calibration view that treats the preference/bias distinction as instrumentally useful for deliberation but ontologically indeterminate. Be precise about how each model handles two hard cases: adaptive preference formation, in which an agent’s moral outlook shifts to accommodate oppressive conditions, and deep cross-cultural moral disagreement, in which local coherence is robust but causal origins are widely shared. Conclude by arguing which framework, if any, best preserves the distinction without smuggling in the very normative conclusions it purports to filter.
