Here is an argument I have been turning over about coherent extrapolated volition that now strikes me as suspicious because it seems to dissolve the very pluralism it was meant to preserve. (1) Coherent extrapolated volition defines the preferences an alignment target ought to satisfy as those we would hold after sufficient reflection, information, and internal coherence. (2) For CEV to function as a collective standard—say, for guiding an institutional AI system—it must aggregate the volitions of different people into a single determinate profile, which requires that individual volitions be coherently reconciled with one another. (3) Coherent reconciliation of preferences demands that genuinely incompatible aims be revised or abandoned; if idealized agent A values universal ascetic withdrawal and idealized agent B values hedonistic flourishing, and both states of the world are mutually exclusive, coherence requires that at least one preference yield. (4) But idealization removes only cognitive errors and informational deficits, not substantive values: once ignorance and bias are eliminated, the conflict between asceticism and hedonism remains because it is not based on a mistake of fact or reasoning. (5) Therefore any inter-personal coherence condition either forces the suppression of reasonable value pluralism or fails to yield a determinate collective preference. (6) If pluralism is suppressed, CEV collapses into a monistic utility function that treats one life-plan as rationally mandatory, contradicting the intuition that people have genuinely incommensurable but legitimate aims. (7) If pluralism is preserved by restricting coherence to the intra-personal case, then there is no collective “volition” to extrapolate—only a disjoint set of individual CEV profiles—so the concept cannot guide alignment decisions that affect everyone jointly. (8) Hence CEV is either authoritarian in its outcome or empty as a shared standard. (9) I want to keep both (2) and (4), since abandoning (2) seems to make CEV useless for collective alignment and abandoning (4) seems to conflate rational correction with value imperialism, but together they drive the contradiction. Where is the slip? Identify the premise or step I should reject, explain why it looked defensible, and state precisely what giving it up costs for using idealized preferences in prosaic alignment. Give a committed answer rather than a menu of options.
