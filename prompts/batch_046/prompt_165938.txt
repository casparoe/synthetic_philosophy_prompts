For a workshop on institutional ethics, construct a decision tree that sorts critiques of effective altruism into named or unnamed positions, using only conceptual commitments that a reader could evaluate without consulting texts. The root asks whether the objection targets the normative core (maximize expected welfare across beings) or only the delivery mechanism. The second level asks whether measurability bias is an epistemic failure—we lack metrics for certain goods—or a moral failure—quantification itself distorts or suppresses value. The third level asks about earning-to-give: is it rejected because direct work empirically dominates, because the career's direct harm exceeds the donated value by any plausible multiplier, or because separating earning from moral judgment creates an accountability gap? The fourth level asks whether longtermist framing is accepted in principle but denied current operational traction, or rejected altogether. Every leaf should carry a one-sentence label of its most awkward commitment—e.g., a view that accepts longtermism, treats measurability bias as moral distortion, and rejects earning-to-give on empirical grounds must explain why direct work is not equally distorted by metric fixation. Note any logically possible combinations that remain unoccupied, diagnosing whether they are incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate. Do not include bibliographies, author lists, or citation requests; the instrument should work from first principles alone.
