As a retired cellular physiologist who has only recently begun serious study in metaphysics, I am assisting with the design of a final examination for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the ontology of propositions. Having worked through Bradley’s metaphysical writings, I find his regress argument against relational unity compelling yet structurally analogous to problems of hierarchical organization I once studied in living systems, and I want to test whether students can engage with the problem at a conceptual level rather than merely reproducing the standard distinction between internal and external relations.

Please draft a single 90-minute essay question focused specifically on Bradley’s regress and the unity of the proposition. The question should present students with a hypothetical “boundary-condition” model—analogous to the way a selectively permeable membrane organizes cellular chemistry without being an additional organelle inside the cell—in which propositional unity is achieved not by an entity that relates subject and predicate, but by a functional organizational limit that constitutes the terms as a unified whole without itself being one of the terms. Ask students to determine whether this model genuinely escapes Bradley’s infinite regress, whether it can preserve the distinctness and truth-evaluability of individual propositions, and whether adopting it inevitably collapses into the kind of monistic absolutism Bradley favored or can sustain a pluralist ontology of propositions. The question must be fully answerable without reference to any specific secondary literature, bibliographies, or citation requirements.

Along with the exam question itself, provide a model answer of approximately 600 words and a marking scheme totaling 20 marks. The scheme should be built around five indispensable argumentative moves—such as correctly reconstructing the regress, diagnosing whether the boundary-condition functions as a hidden relation, evaluating the threat to propositional plurality, assessing the slide toward monism, and identifying one unresolved tension—each with its specific point value and a brief one-line note on the characteristic mistake that costs marks at that stage. Keep the prompt formal and self-contained, suitable for a student who has encountered Bradley’s argument but has no access to external research tools.
