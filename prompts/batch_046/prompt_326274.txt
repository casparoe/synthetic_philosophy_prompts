I keep running into this specific failure mode in morality discussions on rationalist-adjacent forums, and I cant tell if its just motivated reasoning with extra steps or if theres an actual concept for it. The pattern goes like this: someone notices they have a strong gut reaction on some issue — say, they feel uneasy about certain AI alignment tradeoffs involving suffering — and they immediately flag it as "probably just bias" or "status quo bias" or whatever. Then they apply a correction procedure: they check consistency with utilitarian principles, or they ask what they'd want behind a veil of ignorance, or they just compare notes with the other people in the thread. The corrected view still ends up tracking their original social cluster, but now it wears the authority of having been "debiased." I've seen it with people dismissing deontological intuitions because they think rules are just cultural programming, then substituting consequentialist rules they learned in a specific EA context. Its not quite adaptive preferences, because they're not adjusting to an oppressive system; its not false consciousness either. Its more like treating your local reference class as a universal bias-correction mechanism. Does this have a name? If not, how do you distinguish it from legitimate preference refinement — like, what would the boundary conditions be?
