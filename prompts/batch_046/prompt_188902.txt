I'm reaching out because a practical dilemma in my housing cooperative has begun to feel like a philosophical puzzle I can't resolve, and I was hoping for some guidance. I'm serving on the restructuring committee for a collective we founded five years ago with an explicit commitment to flat governance: our charter allows residents to amend any provision by a two-thirds vote. Yet embedded in that same charter is a single protected clause stating that the amendment mechanism itself can never be altered or suspended, not even by unanimous agreement. Recently, a group proposed using our standard amendment process to insert a ten-year sunset provision that would dissolve the cooperative entirely. Their argument is straightforward—if the amendment power is genuinely comprehensive, it must reach every corner of the charter, including its own foundation. A counter-group insists that this protected clause serves as the cooperative's fixed point, and that without one rule standing permanently outside the reach of the rules, authority collapses into circularity and there is no ground for any decision.

I've been turning this over since a conversation last week with my partner, who pointed out that my discomfort isn't really about property law but about whether self-governance can ever be genuinely self-grounding. When I imagine voting to allow the sunset amendment, I feel as though I'm sawinging off the limb I'm sitting on: the legitimacy of my vote depends on a framework I'm simultaneously destroying. But when I imagine defending the protected clause, I feel I'm smuggling in exactly the kind of hierarchical authority—an untouched level above the system—that we created this collective to avoid. I'm not asking for legal interpretation or a procedural recommendation. I'm asking whether there's a coherent philosophical account of how a system can authorize changes to itself without either undermining its own legitimacy or relying on an external, inviolable foundation it can never reach. Does every framework of authority necessarily depend on something it cannot touch from within?
