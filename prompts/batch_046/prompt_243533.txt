I am drafting a philosophical interlude for a novel in which two scholars—Dr. Aris Thorne, who defends an accrual-based account, and Dr. Lin Voss, who defends a relational-topology account—have conducted a ten-year adversarial collaboration on karma as a theory of moral desert. I need the actual joint document they would have signed, rendered with enough conceptual precision that a reader could map the genuine disagreements without mistaking them for verbal disputes. The two camps are these: the Accrual Camp holds that karma operates as a moral ledger in which actions generate quantifiable desert—positive or negative—that must eventually be matched by proportionate well-being or suffering, such that desert functions like a stored normative balance; the Topology Camp holds that karma describes invariant structural relations between action-types and consequence-types within a causal-moral space, where "desert" is a geometric property of pathways rather than an accumulated quantity, so that an agent's moral standing is determined by the shape of the network linking her actions to outcomes, not by any internal bookkeeping balance.

Please produce the collaboration document in the following form. First, a numbered section of claims both parties endorse—claims about the link between action and consequence, about the inadequacy of pure determinism or pure randomness to capture moral desert, about the existence of non-arbitrary distinctions between meritorious and blameworthy conduct, and about the conditions under which an agent can be said to genuinely deserve a particular state—phrased precisely enough that neither side could reject them as vacuous or slanted. Second, a disagreements table in which each residual dispute is stated in wording that the Accrual Camp accepts for its own position and, separately, in wording the Topology Camp accepts for its own position; include at least three such disputes, one of which concerns whether desert can be fully cancelled or "bankrupted" without residual structural trace, and another concerning whether two agents with identical action-histories but embedded in different causal networks can differ in what they deserve. Third, identify the cruxes—those sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would cause at least one camp to revise a central commitment; specify for each crux a concrete conceptual case or structural feature that both sides agree is relevant (for instance, the case of an agent whose virtuous actions are causally isolated from her subsequent suffering by an intervening natural catastrophe, or the structural feature of cyclical versus linear causal pathways in long-term consequence patterns). For each crux, record in advance what the Accrual Camp predicts about that case or feature and what the Topology Camp predicts, in terms each would accept. Fourth, conclude with a brief, jointly signed paragraph stating whether any portion of their disagreement is merely verbal—if so, specify exactly which formulations dissolve upon clarification, and why the remaining disagreement is substantive rather than terminological. Write formally, without citation requests, reading lists, or bibliographic references; do not invoke obscure named doctrines or texts that a general philosophical reader might not recognize, though you may employ the technical vocabulary of "accrual" versus "topological" desert as defined above. The tone should suit a document that two rigorous but adversarial collaborators could both place their names beneath.
