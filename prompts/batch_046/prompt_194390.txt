Write a retirement toast for Dr. Lena Vasquez, delivered at her university's faculty club after thirty years directing need-based financial aid. Roughly half the guests are moral philosophers who will recognize allusions to prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection; the other half are family, staff, and trustees with no background in ethics. The toast should run about five minutes when spoken.

The philosophical framework must do structural work, not serve as decoration. Vasquez's signature achievement was a specific aid model she designed—progressive matching grants, in which institutional support scales inversely with family wealth but includes a continuity clause preventing abrupt loss of aid when a family crosses an income threshold. She called this avoiding a "local leveling-down," where modest gains trigger catastrophic losses in support. Use prioritarian reasoning to explain why benefits to the worse off carry greater moral weight, and use the leveling-down objection to clarify what her work was not: not an argument for equality achieved by reducing anyone's opportunity, and not charity that holds others back. Because of this, the speech must avoid saying she made everyone equal, avoid framing her legacy as mere generosity, and avoid any implication that raising the floor required capping the ceiling.

The audience is split. Every philosophical point must be rendered in language that lands for non-philosophers without sounding condescending to experts. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or page references. You may refer to Derek Parfit's formulation of the leveling-down objection if needed, but do not quote him; paraphrase without quotation marks. Open with a concrete image drawn from the continuity clause in action—perhaps a student whose family income rose just above a cutoff—and close by returning to that image, now transformed by the prioritarian framing. The tone should be affectionate and precise, honoring both the person and the idea.
