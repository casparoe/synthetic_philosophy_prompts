Design a realistic, one-paragraph thought experiment set in a hospital research-ethics committee—avoiding trolley problems, transplant surgeons, or science-fictional devices—in which a strict Kantian prohibition on treating persons merely as means generates a paradox of deontology: honoring the prohibition forces the committee to maintain an enrollment protocol that violates the very dignity-based rationale behind the prohibition, but now at a structural scale. Engineer the case so that both a rule-consequentialist and a threshold deontologist endorse disclosing preliminary adverse-event data to current trial participants, while a pure deontologist must forbid that disclosure because it treats enrolled subjects as informational instruments for future statistical ends; yet forbidding disclosure requires continuing enrollment under conditions that treat all future subjects as mere data sources. After the scenario, state the verdict and reasoning for each of the three positions, isolate the institutional feature (not individual psychology) that makes the paradox arise, and describe how a pure deontologist would resist the framing—specifically whether they deny that collective-level outcomes can trigger individual-level constraints. Conclude by saying what collapses if that institutional feature is removed.
