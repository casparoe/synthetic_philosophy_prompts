For my senior-year philosophy independent study, I am trying to build a conceptual map of the positions one can hold about psychiatric classification after accepting Ian Hacking’s idea of looping effects—that is, the phenomenon where people classified in a psychiatric category alter their behavior and self-understanding in ways that then feed back into the category itself. Rather than asking anyone to name a school of thought, I want a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts thinkers by how they answer concrete questions about the scope, reversibility, and normative significance of these loops.

Please design an instrument with six to eight branch nodes, each posed as a direct yes/no or either/or question that could be answered without prior knowledge of philosophical labels. The root question should be whether looping effects are an inevitable feature of any functioning psychiatric taxonomy, or only arise in categories with particularly porous or contested boundaries. From there, branch on whether looping undermines the objectivity of the classification, whether it can be reversed by reclassifying or destigmatizing, and whether the presence of looping creates an ethical obligation to redesign categories so that they produce beneficial rather than harmful feedback loops. Your leaves must include: a realist who treats looping as a superficial sociological overlay; Hacking’s interactive-kind position; a radical constructionist for whom looping dissolves the distinction between natural and artificial kinds; a reformist who embraces looping as a tool for therapeutic improvement; and one combination of answers that currently has no named occupant—briefly diagnose whether that gap is incoherent, unappealing, or merely unexplored. If any two leaves turn out to be indistinguishable on every practical branch—differing only in unstated metaphysical commitments—flag them explicitly.

Please present the answer in American English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests, as I am preparing this offline. The taxonomy should be constructed from the logical space of answers first, with the named positions then placed into that grid, and any unoccupied cells noted with a brief explanation.
