so me and my roommate were talking about this at dinner with two friends, j and m. j is deep into animal rights, m is skeptical but not a troll.

j said speciesism is wrong the same way racism is - you draw a biological line and say everyone on one side gets full moral status and everyone on the other gets less or none, which is just arbitrary. if you wouldn't deny rights to a human with severe cognitive disability, you can't deny them to a pig with comparable mental life just because it's a different species.

m said okay but species isn't like race, it's more like developmental stage. we don't treat babies and adults identically, not because babies are a different species, but because they lack certain capacities. so maybe species is just a rough proxy for where capacities cluster, not an arbitrary wall.

j said that's literally the marginal cases arguement and it fails - there are pigs smarter than some human infants, so if capacity is what matters then species is still doing no work. you're just smuggling taxonomy back in.

m then brought up something weird i'd never heard framed this way. he said suffering might not be the same kind of thing across different neural architectures. human suffering involves this whole temporal self-model, anticipation, existential dread, while a pig or octopus might have nociception but a totally different "morphology of pain." so putting them on one scale is like comparing temperatures in celsius and fahrenheit without knowing the conversion - or worse, comparing color to sound. it's not that the octopus doesn't matter, but "equal consideration of equal suffering" assumes a universal metric that doesn't exist.

j pushed back and said that sounds like special pleading. we can admit qualitative differences without saying severe pain in a pig doesn't generate a direct reason to stop it. the fact that human grief is different from pig distress doesn't mean pig distress is negligible.

m said he isn't saying negligible, he's saying the anti-speciesist framework treats all sentience as fungible, and that's the mistake. moral obligations aren't just about minimizing suffering units; they emerge from shared practices of recognition that need enough cognitive overlap. so we have stronger obligations to humans not because we're superior, but because reciprocity requires it. equality applies inside a moral community, not across every being that can feel something.
