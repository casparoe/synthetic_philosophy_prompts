Write a villanelle about the fatalist paradox usually called Death in Damascus: a man flees to Damascus precisely because a prophecy warned him he would meet Death there, only to find Death waiting, who explains he was surprised to see him in Damascus because they had an appointment there the next day. Around the third tercet, execute the volta by shifting from the flight narrative to an indexical problem: Death’s statement “I was surprised to see you in Damascus” is always indexed to wherever the speaker actually is, so “Damascus” functions not as a fixed world-coordinate in the prophecy but as a movable reference point that tracks the fugitive. Let the poem leave genuinely open whether this indexical reading dissolves the fatalism or merely relocates it, without sermonizing toward either resolution. The language should be precise enough that someone familiar with both the paradox and its indexical-resolution literature recognizes the structural move, and accurate enough that a specialist in philosophy of logic or language would not wince at the framing.
