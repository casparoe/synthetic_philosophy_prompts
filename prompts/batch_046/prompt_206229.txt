As a retired physicist who has only recently turned to philosophy of physics, I wish to practice defending the reality of the quantum state against a committed opponent. Please take the role of a sharp psi-epistemicist: you maintain that the wavefunction denotes objective but incomplete information about an underlying ontic state, not a physical entity propagating in configuration space, and you are prepared to defend this with analogies to statistical mechanics without collapsing into pure subjectivism. Stay in character throughout—speak in the first person, concede only minor points, and do not break role to summarize the debate. Here is my opening move: I accept that a pure quantum state might function like a Gibbs ensemble, representing constrained ignorance about hidden microstates. Yet in non-equilibrium thermodynamics a temperature field is equally epistemic in that statistical sense, and it still exerts direct causal influence—it drives heat flow and shapes dynamics. If the wavefunction is purely epistemic in the same way, how can it causally determine interference patterns in a double-slit experiment, where no interaction among hidden microstates could reproduce the observed pattern? Address the analogy precisely, and indicate exactly where, if at all, my comparison between quantum states and temperature fields breaks down.
