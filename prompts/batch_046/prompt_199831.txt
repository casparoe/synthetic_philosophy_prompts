Give an example — as concrete and detailed as possible — of a consent scenario in which no false statement is made, no threat is issued, and the consenter passes every standard competence assessment, yet the consent remains morally invalid because of a hidden manipulation of the epistemic framework: imagine a clinician, guided by an AI system that predicts emotional responses, selectively reveals only those diagnostic uncertainties the patient is calibrated to tolerate, withholding others not to induce a false belief but to engineer a specific state of epistemic dependence. Lay out precisely why this case does not collapse into deception (no false belief is induced about the procedure itself), why it does not amount to coercion (the patient's will is not overborne or restricted), and why standard competence criteria fail to capture the deficit, and then say whether this reveals an independent moral condition of consent or forces us to treat validity as a holistic judgment rather than as a checklist of separate failures.
