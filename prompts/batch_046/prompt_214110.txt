I am taking an upper-level seminar on rational choice and the philosophy of value, and I need a sustained satirical piece for our midterm creative assignment. Please write a deadpan parody of a corporate human-resources onboarding manual—approximately 600 words, formatted in numbered sections with bullet points—issued by a fictional consultancy called Horizon Calibration Partners. The manual should instruct new hires on how to prepare for, document, and evaluate “institutional transformative experiences” (ITE) that the firm mandates as part of career development. The satire should target the philosophical problem of whether transformative experiences can be rationally assessed from an ex ante, third-person perspective: treat value-change as a quantifiable HR metric, require employees to file “pre- and post-experience utility profiles,” include a grievance protocol for staff who claim they “no longer recognize their own consent forms” after an ITE, and present a sidebar rubric for managers to determine whether an employee’s post-transformation preferences count as “authentic” or “disoriented.” Keep the voice relentlessly bureaucratic and deadpan—never wink at the reader—so that the absurdity arises from applying real conceptual distinctions (epistemic transformation versus personal transformation, first-person authority versus external assessment, the incommensurability of old and new values) to mundane office procedures. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or named philosophers; let the conceptual machinery operate on its own. The manual should be readable to someone without specialist training, but it should reward a reader who recognizes that you are exaggerating actual features of the transformative-experience debate rather than inventing strawmen. End with a brief “Frequently Asked Questions” section in which the answers only deepen the contradiction between treating experience as a deliverable and acknowledging that the deliverable might reject the delivery system.
