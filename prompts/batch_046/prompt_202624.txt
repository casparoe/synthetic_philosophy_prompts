As a retired physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science, I have been trying to determine whether the thermodynamic arrow genuinely explains the direction of time or whether the explanation is circular. The following argument, which I composed for a reading group, seems to show that statistical mechanics cannot provide an independent ground for the arrow, yet I find the conclusion implausible and cannot locate the error.

(1) The fundamental dynamical laws are time-reversible: for every macroscopic process P that runs in direction D, the time-reversed sequence P* is also a valid solution.

(2) If the arrow of time is physically real rather than merely epistemic, then P* must be physically impossible in our world, not merely unlikely.

(3) Statistical mechanics accounts for the absence of P* by placing it in a region of phase space of extraordinarily small measure—an initial condition of vanishing statistical weight under the Liouville measure.

(4) However, that measure is itself time-symmetric, and rarity over an ensemble of possible worlds does not translate into a dynamical prohibition within any single actual world. Dynamics alone do not forbid P*; they only classify it as atypical.

(5) Therefore, in our specific world, statistical mechanics supplies no physical law that prohibits P*; it merely records that P* is not the history we inhabit.

(6) Yet our judgment that P* does not occur is precisely the observed arrow that the theory was meant to ground.

(7) Hence, appealing to statistical rarity to justify the arrow, and then appealing to the arrow to dismiss the reverse as impossible, is circular.

(8) Consequently, the thermodynamic account fails to offer a non-circular physical foundation for the direction of time.

I wish to preserve (1) and (6), since the reversibility of dynamics and the reality of the observed asymmetry both strike me as firm. Which step—most plausibly (2), (4), or (5)—should be rejected, and what does rejecting it cost? Must one concede that the arrow is purely epistemic, or can the circularity be broken only by introducing a cosmological boundary condition? I would prefer to avoid invoking such a boundary condition unless the logic genuinely demands it.
