I am drafting the philosophical scaffolding for a novel about a political strategist in a failing republic, and I have tried to build it on Machiavelli's distinction between fortune and virtù. Before I commit the manuscript to this architecture, I want to know whether my commitments actually cohere. Here is the framework as it stands:

(1) Virtù is not moral virtue but narrative agency: the protagonist's capacity to impose structural coherence on events that resist coherence.
(2) Fortune, on the Machiavellian model, is an unformed medium—like the river that can be channeled but never drained—that virtù directs but does not eliminate.
(3) Perfect adaptive mastery would collapse the boundary between agent and world, turning the protagonist into something like a deterministic system; therefore, some residue of fortune is structurally necessary for virtù to remain meaningful rather than tautological.
(4) At the same time, I want to treat fortune in the fiction as intersubjective: it is not cosmic chaos but the accumulated, uncoordinated virtù of other agents, which produces friction for the protagonist.
(5) Finally, I take Machiavelli's own abrupt, fragmentary prose style to perform this tension formally: the discourse enacts the resistance of fortune to narrative control.

My audit request: rank the tensions among these five by severity. Is there a genuine conflict between (4) and (2)—if fortune is merely distributed agency, can it still function as an unformed medium, or does that collapse fortune into ordinary conflict? Is there a hidden circularity between (1) and (3), since if coherence by definition requires resistance, then the claim that perfect mastery dissolves virtù may be true by stipulation rather than by discovery? And does (5) actually pull against any of the others, or is it only an illustration that dissolves on inspection? Please tell me which single revision would clean up the most damage with the least cost to the novel's conceptual architecture, and whether the framework survives intact after that revision.
