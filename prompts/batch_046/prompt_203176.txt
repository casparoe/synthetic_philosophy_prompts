I am organizing an adversarial collaboration for a graduate seminar on meta-philosophy, pairing researchers from the Cumulative Knowledge Advocates (CKA) with those from the Conceptual Refinement Theorists (CRT). The CKA maintain that philosophy generates genuinely additive, stable results—especially in formal metaphysics, logic, and applied ethics—where later work builds on earlier achievements through shared methodological standards, so that disagreements eventually contract rather than merely migrate into new vocabulary. The CRT maintain that philosophy possesses no mechanism for permanent settlement; its achievements lie in conceptual clarification, framework construction, and the dissolution of pseudo-problems, but core disputes regenerate in transformed dress, producing what they call "conceptual refinancing" that never pays down epistemic principal and only creates the illusion of progress through "vocabulary drift."

Please draft the joint statement both sides could sign after sustained good-faith engagement. Use this structure: first, a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the observable persistence of ancient questions in modern formulations, the role of formalization and interdisciplinary borrowing, what distinguishes conceptual dissolution from factual discovery, and the conditions under which a philosophical community can genuinely converge—phrased with enough neutrality that neither camp needs to disavow them; second, a disagreements table that states each camp's core thesis in wording its own members would accept as faithful, avoiding any straw-man gloss; third, the cruxes, including specifically whether philosophical subfields exhibit "epistemic amortization" (a measurable decay in disagreement driven by shared standards) versus whether all apparent convergence is merely vocabulary drift, and whether formalization functions as a convergence mechanism or only as a translation device; and fourth, for each crux, name one concrete case or trajectory both sides agree is relevant—such as the evolution of the free-will debate from libertarian framing through compatibilist refinement, or the development of formal epistemology's treatment of justification—together with each camp's explicit prediction of what that case demonstrates. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or reading lists, and formulate everything so that a knowledgeable model could evaluate the claims without external search.
