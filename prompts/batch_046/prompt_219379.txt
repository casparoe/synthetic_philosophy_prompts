The decision-makers in 1433 didn't see a strategic pivot; they saw a line-item veto. The 'turning inward' frame is retroactive. (5) Alex then said: if the coherence is retroactive, then the hindsight bias belongs to the historians, not the actors. So aren't you admitting the whole explanation is biased? (6) Blake said they're intertwined—hindsight bias makes the outcome feel obvious, and narrative fallacy supplies the false causal chain that makes it feel obvious. You need both layers to build the myth of inevitability.
