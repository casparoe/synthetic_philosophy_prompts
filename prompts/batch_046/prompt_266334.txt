hey, I'm working on evolutionary debunking in metaethics and my studdy group walked me into this argument that feels like it proves way too much. I really want to hang on to the idea that our moral beliefs come from evolved cooperative mechanisms, but this version seems to eat logic too. here's the chain they wrote out:

(1) If a belief is produced by a mechanism that natural selection shaped solely for reproductive fitness — with no selection pressure for tracking truth in that specific domain — then the belief is unjustified.

(2) Our belief that harming strangers is wrong comes from in-group cooperation mechanisms and mammalian care circuits being co-opted for broader altruism, not from tracking mind-independent moral facts. Same goes for our cross-species intuition that suffering matters regardless of species.

(3) So those moral beliefs are unjustified.

(4) But those same domain-general predictive mechanisms — the ones that got co-opted — also underwrite our acceptance of modus tollens and our belief that evolutionary theory itself is correct.

(5) So if (1) is true, our logical and scientific beliefs are unjustified too.

(6) And if we have no justified logical beliefs, we can't justify (1) itself or derive (3) and (5).

(7) Therefore evolutionary debunking, taken consistently, collapses into global skepticism about all reasioning, not just morality.

I think the slip might be an equivocation in (1) between not being selected for tracking X-truths and being unjustified about X, or maybe in (4) between a mechanism being evolutionary old and every belief it produces being debunked. But I'm not sure which step costs the least to drop. Which one do you reject, and what does giving it up do to the standard evolutionary debunking positions — Street-style Darwinian dilemmas, Joyce-style practical debunking, etc.? I'd especially like to keep (2) intact since it matches the literature on deontic modal cognition I'm reading. Please write in American English, and don't worry about citations — just talk it through.
