I have just finished a long argument with a friend about whether statistical mechanics genuinely explains the direction of time, or whether it merely pushes the asymmetry back into an unexplained boundary condition. She argues that the past hypothesis—a universe that began in an extraordinarily low-entropy macrostate—is a legitimate explanatory terminus: given time-reversible microdynamics, that atypical initial state derives the thermodynamic arrow without circularity. I disagree. I think that calling the initial state “special” already smuggles in the directional notion of “past”, and that our partition of phase space into macrostates is itself calibrated by our present access to records and traces, so the account risks being quietly anthropocentric. Please take a definite position: is the low-entropy past a sound explanatory stopping point, or does it conceal a circular dependence on the very temporal asymmetry it claims to ground? Specifically, address whether entropy’s macrostate definition can be made independent of our epistemic situation, and state clearly which of us is wrong and why. Keep the focus conceptual—no bibliography, no citation of particular texts—just the argument, sharply drawn.
