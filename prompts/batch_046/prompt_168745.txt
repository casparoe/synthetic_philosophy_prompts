I serve on my company’s algorithmic-deployment review board; none of us have formal philosophy training, but our precedents show a pattern. Our past rulings on risk-weighted proposals are these.

We approved a medical-triage optimizer whose risk-weighted expected utility was strongly positive, accepting that a small subgroup with rare conditions received lower priority; the harm was distributed independently of whether we adopted the system.

We rejected a content-ranking feature with a positive risk-weighted score of +85, on the grounds that the severe mental-health harm pathway existed only if the feature were deployed—without adoption that causal chain was absent—so the risk was structurally coupled to our decision itself.

We approved a fraud-detection model near risk-weighted neutrality, because false-positive harms were randomly distributed and would occur in comparable form regardless of adoption.

We rejected an autonomous-vehicle update that minimized aggregate fatalities, because it applied different risk weights to passengers versus pedestrians; we required uniform weighting across all affected parties.

We approved a climate-prediction dashboard that warned of probable disasters, even though heeding the warning would prevent the disaster and render the prediction false.

We now face a proposal for a predictive resource-allocation system. Its calculated risk-weighted expected utility is −30: moderate efficiency gains are outweighed by a 40 percent probability of severe organizational disruption, weighted by magnitude. The critical feature is that the 40 percent disruption probability is contingent on deployment—if we block the system on the basis of this score, the disruption never materializes, which retroactively removes the risk weight that justified blocking it. The board is split.

If we want to be consistent with ourselves, what should we decide—approve or reject? Identify the implicit rule or rules that jointly explain our five precedents, noting where more than one rule fits. For each candidate rule—say, pure risk-weighted aggregation, an intervention-sensitive exclusion principle, or a counterfactual-stability requirement—state what it predicts for this case and why. Give your best guess at our actual implicit rule, with the reasoning that favors it over the alternatives. Then specify the single further case—perhaps one where harm is causally independent of deployment versus one where the risk weight itself shifts with our choice—that would best distinguish the remaining candidates.
