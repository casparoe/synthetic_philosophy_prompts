i was arguing with a friend about kierkegaards critique of the press and it made me think about these community-notes systems on social media. you know the ones - users propose corrections to false posts anonymously, then other psuedonymous accounts upvote them, and once the score hits some thershold the platform slaps a label on saying 'the community' has spoken. no byline, no editor, nobody personally responsible. meanwhile old-school papers still have named journalists and institutional accountability.

what philosophical commitments about truth and responsibility does that design presuppose? does it assume knowledge is just statistical aggregation - enough anonymous votes and you get accuracy? or does it rely on a collective epistemology where blame and credit can be dissolved into an abstract public, exactly the kind of leveling kierkegaard warned about? is it even compatible with the idea that subjective truth requires an individual who stands behind it with passion, or does the architechture quietly reject that? and if we took kierkegaards view seriously, what would have to change - not just in content moderation but in the actual mechanism of how claims get validated and who owns them?
