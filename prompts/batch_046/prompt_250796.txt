I am preparing a discussion for a seminar that pairs Aldous Huxley's Brave New World with the account of happiness developed by Émilie du Châtelet in her writings on the subject. Du Châtelet maintains that happiness requires both disciplined intellectual labor—study not as an instrumental means to pleasure but as a constitutive activity of rational life—and a tolerated set of sustaining illusions, particularly those of romantic love and religious sentiment, which reason cannot justify but which prevent the passions from collapsing into mere appetite. She thereby occupies a position that is neither hedonist nor ascetic: flourishing is structured by reason yet sustained by illusions that reason does not dispel.

Read the novel through this specific dual framework. Where does Huxley's World State represent the elimination of study in du Châtelet's sense—reduced to conditioned consumption rather than active understanding—and where does it represent the deliberate removal of the sustaining illusions? Consider Mustapha Mond's private library and his admission of loneliness: does he approximate a form of rational happiness, or does his position reveal that du Châtelet's two conditions are inseparable? Conversely, examine John the Savage's insistence on what he calls the right to be unhappy and his Shakespearean suffering: is he attempting to restore both study and illusion simultaneously, and does his destruction suggest that her synthesis is unstable under modern conditions?

Point to the scenes that best dramatize this tension—particularly the debates in Mond's office, John's exile in the lighthouse, and the engineered rituals of solidarity. Then say where the novel resists the philosophical reading: does Huxley's satire imply that study and illusion are always instruments of social control, making du Châtelet's distinction between them conceptually untenable? Finally, tell me what the narrative form adds to du Châtelet's argument that philosophical prose alone cannot capture—particularly regarding the lived experience of attempting to maintain both conditions at once.

Keep the analysis focused on the conceptual architecture rather than biographical scholarship; I am not asking for a bibliography or list of secondary sources, only for the reading itself.
