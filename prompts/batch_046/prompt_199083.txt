As a philosophy professor serving on an institutional review board, I have been asked to advise on the following case. A gene-therapy trial offers participants a substantial travel stipend; one prospective enrollee, who possesses full legal capacity but limited numeracy, needs the stipend in order to visit her seriously ill parent. The consent form explains the direct physical risks in plain language, yet it characterizes dosage adjustments as being personalized to her without disclosing that an adaptive algorithm will determine them in ways that even the investigators cannot fully anticipate. The enrollee understands the trial's general purpose and the physical risks, but she naturally assumes that physicians control every adjustment. She now asks whether accepting the stipend means she was not genuinely free to refuse, whether the form's reassuring language about personalization conceals something material, and whether her difficulty with probabilistic reasoning means she fails to understand what she is undertaking. I would like a precise evaluation of whether her agreement is valid, carefully separating the different conditions under which consent might be undermined and indicating which ones genuinely apply in this situation. Please begin with your bottom line in the first sentence, then provide the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
