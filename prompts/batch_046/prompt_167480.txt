Write a deadpan parody of an academic journal's "Instructions for Authors" for a fictional publication titled The Quarterly of Relational Ontology. Frame Confucian role ethics—specifically ren, li, xiao, and zhengming—not as moral philosophy but as a bureaucratic protocol for assigning and auditing social positions. The satire should exaggerate real features of the view: that moral failure is primarily a failure of role-performance rather than harm to discrete individuals; that ritual propriety (li) functions mainly to maintain hierarchical order; and that relationships are ontologically prior to the persons who occupy them. Invent plausible institutional details—submission categories such as "Corrections to One's Father's Published Papers," a conflict-of-interest disclosure requiring full ancestral obligations, and peer review assigned by generational rank relative to the author—without inventing false features of Confucian thought. Keep the tone dry and institutional; let the absurdity emerge from the framing rather than explicit mockery. The target should be the interpretive tendency to reduce Confucian ethics to static role-compliance, suppressing its critical and transformative dimensions (for example, Mencius's insistence that moral judgment can override hierarchical duty). Around 500 words. Do not include citations, references, or a bibliography.
