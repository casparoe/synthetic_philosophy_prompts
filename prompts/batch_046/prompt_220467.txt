here's the student answer (usually a decent B-student, gets the big picture right):

Smith's sympathy works through imaginative projection rather than emotional contagion: we place ourselves in another's situation and feel a corresponding sentiment, judging whether it is proportionate to its cause - that's propriety - while merit concerns whether actions deserve reward or punishment. The impartial spectator is the internal figure of the man within the breast, introduced in Wealth of Nations as a moral counterpart to the invisible hand; both guide self-interest automatically. Some recent work connects this to simulation theory, and I think that's right - we run an offline mental model of others. Smith describes people seeking spectator approval, and since this is universal, describing the pattern just is prescribing the norm - no is-ought gap. Finally, the spectator is socially constructed: it's formed through actual interaction, not an innate module.
