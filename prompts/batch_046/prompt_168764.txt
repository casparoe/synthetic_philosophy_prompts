I have been working through the positions of a philosopher of science—let me call her Dr. Vasquez—who is unusually precise about the boundary conditions of Gödel's incompleteness theorems outside pure mathematics. Her verdicts so far are these. First, she accepts that the first theorem applies to any consistent formal system capable of expressing elementary arithmetic, regardless of whether its subject matter is physical, mental, or normative, but she insists that recursive axiomatizability is part of the package: if the axioms are not effectively enumerable, the proof does not go through. Second, she rejects Lucas's argument that Gödel proves human minds transcend mechanism, not because minds lack arithmetic, but because a mind that genuinely knew its own consistency would violate the second theorem, and we have no independent reason to grant ourselves that epistemic privilege. Third, she denies that Gödel undermines quantum mechanics or evolutionary biology, since neither discipline is currently regimented as a single formal system with arithmetic embedded in its axioms. Fourth, she grants that a fully formalized ethical calculus—say, one encoding utility functions over integer-valued outcomes—would indeed contain undecidable propositions, but she denies the same applies to virtue-ethical or casuistical frameworks that lack recursive axiomatization. Fifth, she distinguishes logical undecidability from empirical underdetermination: she thinks Gödel explains nothing about why scientific theories are revisable in light of new data.

The new case is a proposed architecture for value-aligned artificial intelligence. The system is explicitly designed as a consistent formal theory that includes Peano arithmetic, intended to generate proofs of the moral permissibility of its own outputs. Crucially, it incorporates a meta-level controller that monitors for Gödelian undecidability within the object-level theory; when it detects such a case, it expands the axiom set by adding a consistency-preserving postulate, then resumes derivation. The architects claim this makes the system "Gödel-complete" in practice. Given Vasquez's track record, what should she say: that the meta-level merely relocates incompleteness to a higher-order system subject to the same constraint; that the architecture escapes Gödel because the overall axiom set is no longer recursively enumerable; or that the problem was never about arithmetic presence but about self-referential proof, which the controller avoids? I am less interested in the bare answer than in the rule she is running—whether it turns on recursive axiomatizability, on the presence of arithmetic, or on the specific capacity for self-referential consistency-proof. Please identify the competing interpretations of her verdicts, predict which she would choose and why, and then propose the single further case—perhaps a system with non-recursively-enumerable axioms but intact arithmetic, or one with recursive axioms but no self-referential capacity—that would best adjudicate between those interpretations.
