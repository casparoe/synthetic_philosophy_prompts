I'm taking a course on Hegel's Philosophy of Right and got stuck on his weird defense of war in the paragraphs around 324-328. I tried to formalize the argument my friend sketched for me, and now it feels like it proves way too much. Here's the chain:

(1) For Hegel, war isn't just a necessary evil; it has an ethical moment because it breaks the complacency of civil society—the system of needs—and reminds citizens that their particular lives are subordinate to the universal will of the state.

(2) If the ethical value of war comes entirely from this rupture of private satisfaction and the assertion of the universal over the particular, then the specific content of the rupture shouldn't matter: any crisis that shatters private complacency and forces submission to the universal should do the same work.

(3) In fact, if we treat the intensity of recognition as proportional to the severity of the disruption, then more extreme disruptions should yield greater ethical value—so a state of permanent emergency should be ethically superior to occasional interstate conflict.

(4) So Hegelian logic seems to recommend that a rational state should maintain a continuous crisis—maybe perpetual mobilization against an invented enemy, or systematic internal purges—rather then risk the stagnation of peace.

(5) But Hegel explicitly says war must remain between states that recognize each other, and he condemns arbitrary internal violence; he also wants civil society to flourish in peacetime. So (4) is obviously not his view.

(6) Therefore one of the earlier steps is wrong, but I'm not sure which. If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (1) and (5) then (3), but I want to know exactly where the slide happens and what it costs me to block it. Is there an equivocation between the universal in interstate recognition and the universal in domestic obedience? Or is the mistake treating an ethical moment that only makes sense within a specific dialectial structure as if it were a generic psychological benefit?

My friend also mentioned negative solidarity being a modern echo of this—does that matter for diagnosing the flaw, or is it just a distraction?
