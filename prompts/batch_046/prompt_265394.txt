Next month I'm giving a seminar defending this weird hybrid view: emotions aren't judgments (the Solomon/Nussbaum line), they aren't perceptions (Prinz/Tappolet), and they aren't bare feelings (James/Damasio). Instead they're "normative prediction-error profiles" — basically your brain flagging that some value-laden expectation got violated, but without necessarily encoding that as a full-blown propositional belief. It pulls from predicitive processing and phenomenolgy without being either, and I think it avoids the standard objections to all three camps.

Audience is a philosophy-of-mind workshop. Mostly analytic philosophers who know the three-way taxonomy cold. Two enactivists who think cognition is embodied/extended and hate anything that smells like internal representationalsim. One interoceptive-inference researcher (Barrett-style) who thinks emotions are literally constructed predictions and will probably see my view as redundant jargon with extra steps.
