I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with an unnamed contemporary moral philosopher who advances a revived Anscombian position on the 1945 atomic bombings: she maintains that President Truman's authorization constituted murder in the precise sense Anscombe articulated—not merely a disproportionate act of war but a direct intention to kill non-combatants as a means—and she rejects all double-effect defenses of strategic bombing that embed military targets within civilian populations. Draft eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her to restate this thesis. I require the following: first, a question that presses the distinction between directing evil and merely foreseeing it in institutionally mediated cases, such as a commander who orders a strike executed by distant subordinates with no direct causal contact; second, a question that anticipates her likely reply—that the sheer scale of Hiroshima makes the directing/foreseeing distinction collapse—together with a sharp follow-up; third, a question that introduces a deliberately novel scenario: an autonomous weapons system programmed to predict collateral damage with near-perfect accuracy but designed so that no civilian is ever the algorithmically intended target; ask whether her framework treats such algorithmic mediation as morally different from human intentional action, and provide a follow-up keyed to her probable answer that the programmer's intention is what ultimately counts; fourth, a question about whether Truman's sincerely held but, ex hypothesi, false belief that the bombings would shorten the war and save more lives can ever excuse what she regards as direct killing, with a follow-up that challenges whether this makes moral assessment depend on epistemic access in a way that undermines her absolutism. The tone should be rigorous and appropriate for an academic philosophy panel. Do not include generic biographical questions, and do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
