I have been reading through Frank Ramsey's brief writings on belief, ethics, and the meaning of life, and I think his view is that asking about life's meaning is not a request for some hidden metaphysical fact, but rather a practical question about whether our overall commitments cohere successfully in action, with practical success serving as the measure rather than correspondence to an independent reality. This strikes me as very different from the existentialist position that meaning is not discovered but must be invented through radical, groundless choice. Please compare Ramsey's pragmatist-cognitivist approach to the meaning of life with the existentialist constructivist account, and take a definite position: which of the two genuinely resolves the anxiety behind asking the question, and which one merely dissolves it by changing the subject? In your answer, address specifically whether Ramsey's reliance on practical success as the criterion avoids circularity, or whether it quietly presupposes the very objective standard of value that he claims to reject.
