If the intentional fallacy demonstrates that an author's design is neither available nor desirable as a standard for judging a poem—because the published text becomes an autonomous linguistic object whose meaning is fixed by internal relations and interpretive practice—then shouldn't the same principle apply uniformly across all genres of writing? In particular, why do legal and technical interpreters treat patent claims or software licenses differently, granting special authority to the drafter's intended scope, when these documents are also authored texts released into public circulation? Is there a coherent boundary that saves authorial intent for instructional and contractual genres without simply begging the question against the autonomy thesis, or does accepting the fallacy strictly require us to read a license agreement with the same hermeneutic methods we apply to a lyric poem?
