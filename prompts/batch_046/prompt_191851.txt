I am currently revising the central chapter of my dissertation in metaphysics, which examines the teletransporter thought experiment and its implications for survival. Rather than endorsing either bodily continuity or pure psychological continuity, I have come to defend a hybrid position: what matters in survival is the preservation of a first-personal practical stance—the capacity to regard future experiences as one's own within practical deliberation—together with the normative coherence of the reasons that bind present and future selves. I take this stance to be reducible to psychological relations, but not identical to them; it can survive duplication, so branching does not annihilate survival, yet it admits of degrees, becoming fragmented when normative coherence falls below a threshold that I leave deliberately unspecified. Bodily continuity, on this view, is instrumentally valuable at best, and I treat Parfit’s reductionist framework as correct in its metaphysics but insufficient because it ignores this normative dimension.

Given this framing, I want an honest map of the view’s exposure: what observations, arguments, formal results, or conceptual distinctions would count as decisive defeaters rather than merely awkward adjustments? I am particularly interested in whether empirical findings in neuroscience or cognitive psychology tie the first-personal stance to non-replicable neural architectures; whether ethical considerations about singular moral accountability in branching cases undermine the claim that survival can be graded; and whether the view’s reliance on an unspecified coherence threshold renders it unfalsifiable in a damaging way. Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which ones a committed defender of this position would themselves concede as counting against it, and assess whether the view has been insulated by qualifications to the point where it risks nothing at all—if so, say whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a metaphysical theory of this kind. I do not want a bibliography, reading list, or citation survey; I want the arguments and evidence sorted by how much they should move someone who holds this specific hybrid reductionist-normative view.
