So my mom just moved in with us after her stroke and I’m suddenly juggling being her caregiver, a parent to my toddler, and my actual job, and I’ve been reading about Confucian role ethics because it feels like my obligations come from being a daughter, not from some abstract rule. But then I built this argument and now I’m worried it makes failure unavoidable?

(1) Confucian role ethics says moral obligations are internal to social roles: being a good daughter just is fulfilling the duties of that role.
(2) These duties aren’t contractual or opt-in; they’re constitutive. You can’t reject filial piety and still be a daughter in the full moral sense.
(3) The wulun lists five cardinal relationships, but there’s no clear hierachy for conflicts outside the family — no Confucian lexical priority rule for parent vs. employee.
(4) Meanwhile each role’s demands are mediated by li (ritual propriety), which shapes how you feel and act but never caps the scope of care — it just tells you the right posture while you keep giving.
(5) So in modern life, where I stack daughter, parent, employee, spouse with no principled resolution, every choice I make violates some open-ended role demand.
(6) Therefore I’m in a state of unaviodable moral failure — I’m always failing at least one role I genuinely occupy.
(7) But any theory that makes unavoidable failure a structural feature of ordinary adult life is broken.
(8) So role ethics is false, or at least the version that treats role duties as absolute.

Where does this slip? I really want to keep (1) and (2) — they capture why I feel pulled toward my mom in a way that isn’t just preference — but I don’t want to land at (8). Is there a hidden premise about role interaction, or is (4) wrong about li not setting limits? Or maybe (5) assumes that not meeting every possible demand counts as a moral violation, and that’s not what the tradition says?
