Hey, I'm working through a problem for my phil-phys reading group and i think i built an argument that proves way too much. Here are the steps:

(1) In GR, if (M,g) solves Einstein's equations and φ is a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside some open region H but non-trivial inside, then (M, φ*g) is also a solution.
(2) If spacetime points are real individuals (substantivalism), then g and φ*g assign diffrent geometric properties to the very same points inside H, so they represent different physical worlds. Hence determinism fails inside H.
(3) The standard relationalist escape is to deny bare point indentity: (M,g) and (M,φ*g) represent the exact same physical situation, since only spatiotemporal relations matter.
(4) Now suppose H has non-trivial topology—say a toroidal region—so there are "large" diffeomorphisms inside H that aren't smoothly deformable to the identity, even though they're still the identity outside H. These too are symmetries.
(5) By (3), metrics related by these large diffeomorphisms are also physically identical.
(6) But we can iterate: partition H into smaller toroidal subregions H_i, apply independent large diffeomorphisms in each, all identity outside the union. By (3) and (5) the whole configuration is physically identical to the original.
(7) So inside H, the metric assignement can be arbitrarily shuffled at every scale—there's literally no physical fact about which point (or subregion) carries which curvature.
(8) Yet we run local experiments inside H—gradiometers, light-bending measurements—that seem to depend on specific local curvature values.
(9) The relationalist has to say these don't measure "the metric at p" but only global relational structures spanning the apparatus and its environment.
(10) But if the apparatus can sit inside an arbitrarily small H_i, then every genuinely local measurement collapses into a global relational claim.
(11) That makes local physics conventional and kills local explanation, which seems wrong.
(12) So we should reject (3) and accept substantivalism with indeterminism, despite the hole.

This feels suspicious—probably a slide somewhere between "assignment to points is conventional" and "local geometric properties are conventional." I'd like to keep (3) and (11) if I can. Which step should I actually reject, and what does it cost me? Is the flaw in (6) about composing large diffeomorphisms across boundaries, or in (9) about what local measurements actually track?
