Over the past few weeks, since I began studying how reference gets fixed in artificial language systems, the Twin Earth problem has stopped feeling like an old seminar exercise and started feeling like a live worry about whether mental content can ever be fully self-transparent. I have been trying to determine whether semantic externalism, taken consistently, forces us to abandon first-person authority over our own thoughts, and I have laid out the following chain for a study group, but I am convinced it proves far too much. Here is the argument exactly as I presented it:

(1) In the Twin Earth scenario, Oscar's intrinsic duplicate on Twin Earth directs a mental state at XYZ, not H₂O, despite sharing every internal neural property with Earth-Oscar.
(2) Therefore the intentional content of a mental state is not fixed by intrinsic physical properties alone.
(3) If content is partly fixed by external environmental conditions, then knowing what one's mental state is about requires knowing those relevant environmental conditions.
(4) First-person authority consists in being able to know the contents of one's own mental states without empirical investigation of the external world.
(5) Hence semantic externalism is incompatible with first-person authority.
(6) First-person authority is a necessary presupposition for the legitimacy of introspective reports used in clinical psychology and cognitive science.
(7) So if externalism is true, introspective psychology lacks legitimate data.
(8) Introspective psychology does in fact yield legitimate data.
(9) Therefore semantic externalism is false.

My study partner replied that (4) confuses knowing that one is in a mental state with knowing its broad, environment-dependent content; she thinks we retain direct access to narrow content and discover broad content only when specifically required. I want to preserve (1) and (2), since they seem to capture the core externalist insight, and I am deeply reluctant to abandon (4) because it seems to gut the traditional conception of self-knowledge. But I also cannot accept (9). Where exactly does this chain slip—is there an equivocation between narrow and broad content hidden in (3) or (5), or does the real damage occur elsewhere? If I must reject one step, which rejection does the least damage to the externalist framework I wish to keep?
