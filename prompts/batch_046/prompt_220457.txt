Write a short dialogue between two philosophers, Mara and Jude, arguing about whether epistemic modesty in philosophy should come from the inside view or the outside view. Mara defends the inside view: she thinks if you've carefully worked through the arguments for, say, moral anti-realism, you shouldn't slash your confidence just becuase philosophers split evenly on metaethics—your reasoning is what it is. Jude pushes the outside view: he says philosophy has awful base-rate accuracy, so rational belief requires regressing toward the disagreement rate, especialy on questions where experts have been arguing for centuries with no convergence. Make them actually respond to each other—Mara should ask whether Jude's own view is self-defeating (if philosophers disagree about epistemology too, shouldn't he regress his belief in the outside view itself?), and Jude should answer with some distinction between methodological norms and first-order claims. Keep both sides sharp, no strawmen. Write it like I'm asking you for a classroom exercise—casual tone, don't overpolish it.
