Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed strong illusionist about phenomenal consciousness—who holds that qualitative experience is a widespread representational illusion with no underlying phenomenal substrate—and a committed phenomenal realist—who holds that phenomenal properties are real, irreducible, and genuinely instantiated in conscious subjects. The document should be something both parties could sign without misrepresenting their own positions.

Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims. These should cover: shared commitments regarding the formal architecture of introspective reporting; the explanatory targets that any adequate science of consciousness must meet, including the integration of hierarchical predictive-processing mechanisms with self-modelling; and the empirical dissociations—such as those between subjective reportability and objective stimulus presence—that both sides accept as established phenomena requiring explanation, regardless of interpretation.

Next, present a disagreements table. For each camp, state its precise position in wording that side would accept for itself, addressing: (i) whether phenomenal concepts possess non-descriptive, reference-fixing conditions or are constituted entirely by representational error; (ii) whether the refrigerator-light analogy genuinely applies to qualitative experience or mischaracterises its stability conditions; and (iii) whether a hierarchical predictive-coding model parameterised by precision-weighting can, in principle, fully account for phenomenal consciousness without ontological remainder. Ensure the formulations are neutral and not slanted.

Then identify the cruxes—the specific sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party. Likely cruxes include whether introspective transparency is compatible with the existence of a qualitative substrate, and whether first-person authority can survive systematic misrepresentation of phenomenal properties. For each crux, specify a concrete case, argument, or empirical scenario that both sides agree bears on it—such as the interpretation of blindsight-type dissociations within hierarchical self-models, or the limits of reproducing phenomenological reports in artificial systems—and record in advance what each side predicts that case demonstrates.

Finally, assess whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal: if so, say exactly which formulations dissolve upon analysis and why. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Write in polished British English, and treat phenomenal reports as outputs of a precision-weighted predictive-coding architecture to keep the framing substantive and self-contained.
