I work in computational systems biology and think naturally in the vocabulary of Boolean gene-regulatory networks: nodes representing genes, directed edges representing regulatory interactions, synchronous update rules, state-space trajectories, attractor basins, and perturbation experiments in which a node is clamped to a fixed value. I want the philosophical debate between counterfactual theories of causation—associated with Lewis and with Rubin-style potential-outcomes reasoning—and interventionist theories—associated with Pearl's do-calculus and structural causal models—restated entirely within that vocabulary.

Proceed framework by framework.

For the counterfactual account: translate its core idea, that causation involves comparison between what actually happened and what would have happened in the closest possible world in which the cause is absent, into network terms. Can "closest possible world" be rendered as the nearest alternative attractor basin reachable by minimal perturbation? Can counterfactual dependence be rendered as a divergence in state-space trajectories? Identify precisely which parts of the philosophical machinery map onto network dynamics, which parts require distortion—such as treating modal similarity metrics as distance metrics in state space—and which parts have no expression at all, particularly any commitments about modal realism or cross-world identity conditions.

For the interventionist account: translate its core idea, that causation is identified through modular interventions represented by the do-operator and assessed via conditional independence in a graphical model, into the same network vocabulary. Can an intervention be rendered precisely as a node-clamping perturbation? Can the independence conditions required for causal identification be rendered in terms of network topology and update-rule modularity? Identify which elements translate cleanly, which require fudging—such as treating probabilistic independence as deterministic basin separation—and which are missing entirely, particularly any reliance on probabilistic graphical semantics or external parameter distributions.

Then provide the residue report. Is there anything the philosophers are genuinely disagreeing about—such as whether causation requires actual-world comparison versus structural modularity, or whether causal claims depend on background conditions fixed across worlds—that lacks any counterpart in Boolean-network dynamics? Or does the residue consist mainly of metaphysical commitments (modal spaces, probabilistic framing) that the biological vocabulary deliberately omits without loss of explanatory power? I want to know whether my working intuition, that causation in these networks amounts to "a clamped-node intervention that shifts the system to a stable alternative basin," captures the substance of the debate or suppresses a real distinction. Keep the translation honest: do not smuggle philosophical assumptions into the network vocabulary and call them translations.
