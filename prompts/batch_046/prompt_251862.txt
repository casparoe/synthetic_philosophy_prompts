Compare Hofstadter's idea in the Ant Fugue — that an ant colony can function like a single mind with its own point of view — with what we might call 'distributed proprioception,' where the colony is just a network of partial minds linked by pheremone trails that act like shared memory but not shared experience. I want you to figure out whether there's actualy a meaningful difference between saying there's one subject with many sensors versus many subjects with shared data. To do this, apply the access-consciousness / phenomenal-consciousness distinction directy: treat the colony's chemical signaling as an access pathway and the individual ant sensory states as the phenomenal substrates. Which framing makes more sense of ant behavior, and does either one really explain why we'd be tempted to call the colony 'one mind' in the first place?
