You are a senior compliance auditor for a national workfare program that conditions basic unemployment support on verified job-search activity. You process between eight and twelve sanction appeals per day, each within a strict fifteen-minute window, using only the standardized file in front of you; your determination is signed and subject to review by a senior administrator with no philosophical training, and errors carry both legal-appeal risk and public-audit exposure. You need a reusable procedural framework—an ordered checklist with explicit decision nodes—to determine whether a recipient’s non-compliance should be classified as a capacity deficit (the administrative environment genuinely failed to supply the substantive freedom to comply) or a preference divergence (the recipient possessed that freedom but assigned it lower priority than competing aims). The framework should translate the capabilities approach—treating compliance not as bare behavioral output but as the exercise of a real opportunity—into an operational test you can run without theoretical vocabulary.

The framework must contain:
1. A preliminary filter that separates observable barriers (transport, scheduling, documentation access) from attitudinal indicators, with explicit rules for what to do when both appear in the same file.
2. Three ordered diagnostic tests applied in sequence, each with a stopping rule and a required notation on the form.
3. A reversibility check: ask whether the recipient’s situation would have permitted compliance if the administrative burden were reduced by half, and specify exactly what counts as a negative answer.
4. Worked application to three cases:
   a. A recipient sanctioned after missing two mandatory workshops; she arrived thirty minutes late both times, having taken two buses from a rural address with no vehicle access, and she submitted handwritten notes explaining the transit delays.
   b. A recipient sanctioned for failing to report part-time informal earnings; he admits he knew the reporting rule, describes the paperwork as unnecessarily annoying, and presents no documented barrier to filing.
   c. A recipient with a verified anxiety disorder who submitted all required documentation consistently late but complete; clinical notes indicate deadline-triggered avoidance, yet he also declined an offered accommodation to extend deadlines.
5. For case (c), identify the specific point where the procedure yields a determination you are likely to find intuitively unfair, and mark precisely where an ineliminable judgment call replaces mechanical application.
6. An explicit statement of what the framework cannot resolve—namely, conflicts in which partial capacity and partial preference are both present, and the procedure forces a binary classification on a continuous reality.
7. A prohibition against requesting outside documentation, consulting theoretical texts, or invoking authority; every step must be justified by its internal logic and its fit with the auditor’s practical constraints.

Deliver the framework as a flowchart-in-words with test sentences an auditor can apply directly, include the sign-off notation required at each node, and do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or appeals to named scholars.
