Write an essay addressing the following controversy. In cases where an autonomous system produces harm that no particular human decision specifically authorised—often discussed as a responsibility gap—should our institutional response be to extend liability and blame to cover the deficit, or should we acknowledge that certain harms may remain genuinely orphaned and redesign our regulatory frameworks accordingly? To examine this, employ the distinction between causal authorship and normative custody: the claim that responsibility may depend not on having caused a specific outcome, but on holding authoritative oversight over the operational domain within which the system acted. Assess the argument that this distinction dissolves the gap by redistributing responsibility, and counter it with the objection that normative custody without direct causal control provides an insufficient basis for either moral blame or legal liability. Your essay should reach and defend a specific verdict—either that the gap is closable through a custody-based framework, or that it is structurally irreducible and requires institutional redesign rather than liability extension—and illustrate your reasoning with reference to one concrete domain, such as autonomous maritime navigation or algorithmic allocation of social resources. Write in formal British English, weigh the principal considerations on both sides, and do not conclude on a non-committal note.
