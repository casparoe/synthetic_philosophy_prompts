Write an essay addressing whether the agricultural revolution should be regarded as a mistake for human welfare. Rather than rehearsing the standard comparison between hunter-gatherer leisure-time affluence and the emergence of social inequality with sedentism and storage, frame your evaluation around the claim that agriculture introduced structurally novel suffering risks—conditions in which dense, surplus-dependent populations become vulnerable to catastrophic famine, epidemic, and systemic coercion at scales impossible in small, mobile foraging bands. Engage seriously with the revisionist counter-argument that forager existence was already violent and precarious, and that farming merely amplified pre-existing risks rather than creating qualitatively new ones. Reach and defend a definite conclusion: either that the transition to agriculture was indeed a mistake when judged by its capacity to generate extreme, large-scale suffering, or that this framing fundamentally misrepresents the trade-offs involved. Take a committed position and justify it with explicit reasons; do not offer an undecided survey of both sides.
