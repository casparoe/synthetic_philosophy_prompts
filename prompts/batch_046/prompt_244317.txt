I am planning a ninety-minute workshop for postgraduate students in organisational ethics who have no background in comparative moral psychology, and I need a teaching plan — not the explanation itself — for introducing the conceptual contrast between German folk morality, in which moral authority is distributed through procedural rule-following, collective obligation, and ordered duty, and American folk morality, in which it is located in individual conscience, authentic conviction, and personal judgement about what is right. I want to know which structural distinction must be secured before either framework can be presented without caricature, and in what dependency order the two should be introduced so that one does not collapse into a stereotype of the other.

Specify the single carrying case — I suggest a scenario in which a quality-assurance employee detects a statistical deviation that remains technically within permitted parameters but triggers an intuitive sense that something is wrong — and explain exactly why this case should carry the session rather than a dramatic whistle-blowing or obedience-to-authority example. Map the misconceptions the students will form in predictable sequence: that the German framework is merely authoritarian, that the American framework is merely selfish individualism, that these are only cultural stereotypes rather than coherent normative structures, and that one framework must be universally correct. For each misconception, identify the precise point in the explanatory sequence where it should be anticipated and headed off.

Include a check at the end that reveals whether the students have grasped the structural difference in how moral error is distributed — procedural breach versus betrayal of inner conviction — rather than which framework they personally prefer. State clearly what must be left out at this level and openly acknowledged as outside scope: historical sociology of national character, specific canonical texts, and empirical cross-cultural data. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Write in British English.
