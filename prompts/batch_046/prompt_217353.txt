I'm trying to figure out if I'm a methodological individualist or a holist by testing my instincts on actual explanations. Here are my verdicts, and they're kind of all over the place.

When Swedish workplaces switched from formal 'ni' to informal 'du', I think that's just individuals copying each other—no group-level mind needed. Individualist.

But when a factory town's suicide rate spikes right after the plant closes, I feel like the closure itself is doing explanitory work that you can't reduce to each person's private grief. Holist.

Then there's the redundancy wave: every worker at the plant takes the buyout. I still want to explain each one by there own savings, family situation, etc., even though the pattern looks collective. Individualist.

The one that breaks me is an interbank freeze—each branch manager stops lending to other branches because they individually fear default, and suddenly the whole regional system locks up. Is the collapse explained by each manager's risk calculus, or by the network structure that turned private fear into gridlock?

Can you find a principle that saves all four verdicts? If not, which one should I drop? And apply whatever you come up with to a case I haven't considered: a dialect dies out not because speakers abandon it, but because the school system drops it, yet every speaker still privately prefers using it.
