I'm part of a small working group advising a mid-sized city's heritage commission. We've been asked to help design a framework for deciding which monuments, street names, and public markers should be retained, altered, or removed over the next decade, but we have very limited staff time and political capital. Rather than asking for case-by-case recommendations, I want to understand the conceptual landscape: within the ethics and philosophy of commemoration, which open questions are actually upstream of practical decision-making, and which are largely ornamental?

Specifically, I'm trying to prioritize among several unresolved tensions that keep coming up in our discussions. First, there's the ontology of commemorative objects: is a statue primarily an honorific speech-act directed at the present, or a documentary record that belongs to the past regardless of current values? Second, there's the problem of what I might call semiotic drift—the way a monument's meaning shifts as the social context around it changes, often without any physical alteration—raising the question of whether ethical evaluation should target original intent, current interpretation, or some counterfactual stable meaning. Third, there's the distributive problem: if we accept that public space is a scarce resource for collective memory, does removing one figure merely open space for others, or does it actively restructure the narrative architecture of the city? And fourth, there's the moral remainder concern: whether removal resolves an injustice or merely displaces it into absence, leaving unaddressed the structural conditions that made the commemoration possible.

Could you rank these four lines of inquiry—along with any others you think I've missed—according to which ones, if settled, would actually change how we allocate our limited resources? I want a dependency-aware prioritization: which questions need answering before the others become tractable, which ones are philosophically interesting but have little practical downstream effect for a municipal body, and which might be deprioritized not because they're unimportant but because they're likely to remain intractable regardless of effort. Please defend your ordering with explicit criteria—such as tractability for non-philosophers, how many other decisions depend on the answer, and whether resolving the question would genuinely alter policy rather than just refine our vocabulary. I'm not looking for a reading list or a bibliography of the literature; I want a defended agenda for where a constrained working group should direct its conceptual energy.
