If internal reasons must be linked to an agent's actual motivational set S, and external reasons are defined precisely by lacking such a link, then it seems that any reason recognized through rational reflection becomes internal—since reflection updates S. So doesn't the internalist position entail that genuinely external reasons form an unstable category, collapsing the moment one considers them? Or is there a hidden structural constraint—perhaps that an internal reason must be reachable without already presupposing the normative conclusion—that prevents this assimilation and keeps the distinction intact?
