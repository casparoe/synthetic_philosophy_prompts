Write the desiderata for an adequate theory of moral compromise conceived as inter-value arbitration with potential gains from trade. The theory should explain how agents anchored in genuinely different normative lexicons—say, one that treats distributive fairness as lexically prior and another that treats aggregate welfare as non-negotiable—can reach hybrid arrangements that leave both parties better off without requiring either to convert. For each desideratum, supply the motivating case or distinction: (i) what counts as a genuine compromise versus a mere tactical concession; (ii) how to preserve the integrity of each value system through the agreement, including how to measure or acknowledge lexical translation costs—the structural distortion that occurs when one system's core demand is expressed in the vocabulary of another; (iii) the conditions under which a compromise is stable rather than dynamically eroding one party's commitments; and (iv) the boundary conditions that distinguish permissible trade-offs from betrayals of incommensurable values. Flag any pair of these you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, and indicate which desiderata are essential versus negotiable. Keep the focus on the criteria and their cases; brief remarks on how existing pluralist or bargaining accounts measure up are welcome only if they clarify the list.
