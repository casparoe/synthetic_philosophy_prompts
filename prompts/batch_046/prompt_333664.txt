I cover science and philosophy for a national magazine, and I sit on the funding advisory board of the Foundation for Public Science Communication. Next Thursday the board must vote on a proposal for a feature-length documentary, tentatively titled The Open Trajectory, that argues deterministic chaos undermines Laplacian determinism and thereby rehabilitates a libertarian conception of free will. The filmmaker contends that exponential divergence of trajectories in nonlinear systems makes the future metaphysically open, not merely practically unpredictable, and that this dissolves the causal closure required for determinism. A dissenting memo from our physics advisor counters that this collapses epistemic limits into ontological indeterminacy, and further invokes the shadowing lemma—approximate trajectories in hyperbolic dynamical systems are tracked by true deterministic state-space paths—to argue that chaos reinforces structural determinism while rendering it empirically opaque. The board is divided: one faction sees the proposal as a valuable public correction to popular misconceptions about determinism; the other sees it as a category error that will mislead audiences into thinking randomness equals freedom. I need a formal decision memo—no longer than two pages, addressed to the board chair with an actionable bottom line up front—that recommends a concrete course of action: approve, approve with binding conditions, or reject. The memo should engage the filmmaker's claim seriously rather than dismissing it as confusion, address the advisor's distinction between predictive determinism and state-space uniqueness, and include an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. If the recommendation is conditional, specify the required revisions—such as script changes, companion explanatory materials, or expert review provisions—and frame them so that the filmmakers could actually respond. Write for an institutional audience, not for general readers, and ensure the reasoning turns on the conceptual relationship between chaos, predictability, and determinism rather than on budgetary or stylistic concerns.
