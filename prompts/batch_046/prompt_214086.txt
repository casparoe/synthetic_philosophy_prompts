Here is an argument about gradual mind uploading that my study group keeps returning to, and I cannot locate the leak. (1) If mental properties depend only on functional organization, then replacing one biological neuron with a silicon chip that performs the same computation does not alter the subject's consciousness, since every mental state is preserved. (2) By repeating this substitution one neuron at a time, we eventually obtain a fully synthetic brain functionally identical to the original; if no single step disrupted consciousness, continuity is maintained across the entire sequence, so the final silicon being is numerically identical to the original person. (3) Functional organization, however, can also be instantiated in a substrate that never participated in this chain—an "instant copy" built from scratch with the same final architecture. (4) If consciousness is purely functional, this instant copy must also be numerically identical to the original person, just as the gradually replaced being is. (5) Now suppose both the gradual result and the instant copy exist simultaneously. They share every psychological property but are spatially separated and can acquire divergent experiences. (6) By the transitivity of identity, if both are identical to the original, they must be identical to each other; yet they clearly are not the same being. (7) Therefore the premise that consciousness is substrate-independent—specifically the inference in (4)—is false, and causal-historical continuity through the gradual chain must matter for personal identity. (8) But if causal history matters, then even the first chip replacement breaks the biological chain and destroys the original consciousness, which seems wildly implausible. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost? If I must choose, I would rather keep (1) and (2) than (6), but I want to know precisely what I am paying.
