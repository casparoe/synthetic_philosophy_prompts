I lean toward the American side: I think treating rules as just backup for conscience makes societies more adaptible when rules lag behind reality, and that the German model risks "normative outsourcing" where people stop reasoning altogether. But I want to know what actually counts against that. Would comparative data showing high-rule societies recover faster from institutional shock be decisive, or just awkward? Is there a situationist-style argument that hits conscience-based morality harder—maybe that it just produces more rationalization through some kind of moral-licensing loop? And have I qualified my view into unfalsifiability by saying "well, true conscience isn't rationalization"?
