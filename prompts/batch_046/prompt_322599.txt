Here is an argument built around Dennett’s framework of real patterns and the three stances, and I cannot find the exit. (1) A stance reveals a genuinely real pattern only if the predictions it licenses depend on features of the world that exist independently of the observer’s predictive needs, not merely on useful fictions. (2) The design stance licenses predictions about functional organization—what an organ or artifact is for—without committing to the mental states of any designer. (3) Natural selection produces functional organization structurally identical to designed artifacts: kidneys filter blood because that effect was selected for, just as carburetors mix fuel because that was engineered. (4) Therefore, applying the design stance to organisms tracks a real pattern in nature, not just a heuristic overlay. (5) Now, evolutionary dynamics themselves are routinely described with rational-choice vocabulary: genes pursue replication strategies, populations choose equilibrium states, and evolutionary stable strategies expect defection to be punished. These intentional-stance descriptions yield accurate predictions about evolutionary trajectories that the design stance alone misses. (6) So if predictive success grounded in structural identity is sufficient for stance-realism in (3), the same reasoning should license intentional-stance realism about evolutionary processes. (7) But treating evolutionary dynamics as genuinely possessing beliefs, desires, and expectations is ontologically extravagant—no working biologist takes gene-level intentionality literally. (8) Hence either we reject stance-realism for the design stance (making evolutionary biology a fiction), or we accept it for the intentional stance applied to non-cognitive systems (making ontology bloated), or we deny that predictive success ever tracks reality (undermining scientific realism generally). I want to keep (3) and reject (8), but I do not see how to block (6) without an ad hoc boundary. Which step is the weak link, and what does giving it up cost?
