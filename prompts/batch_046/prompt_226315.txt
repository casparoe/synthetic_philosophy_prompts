Please construct an argument for the following position: Dutch directness and Japanese indirectness should not be understood as opposite poles on a single universal politeness-bluntness axis, but rather as two distinct institutional solutions to an identical epistemic-coordination problem—specifically, how interlocutors maintain stable second-order common knowledge about whether a face-threatening intention is actually present. Directness solves this by rendering the absence of face-threat mutually obvious and therefore common knowledge; indirectness solves it by rendering the presence of such a threat mutually deniable. From this framework, derive the claim that a white lie is not merely a minor breach of honesty or autonomy, but a specifically corrosive communicative act, because it undermines the speaker’s capacity to credibly signal that they are not engaging in strategic signaling at all—thereby corrupting the very epistemic stance upon which indirect coordination depends. In developing your case, address the objection that if indirectness is already a form of strategic ambiguity, then a white lie is only an additional layer of ambiguity with no special moral status, and explain precisely why the corruption of the speaker’s communicative stance differs in kind from ordinary strategic vagueness. You need not invoke specific ethnographic citations, but your reasoning should engage concretely with how these norms function as coordination mechanisms in practice.
