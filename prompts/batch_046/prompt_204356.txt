I sit on the governance board of a publicly funded civic forum that rents its halls to community groups at cost. A proposal before Friday’s vote would grant permanent reserved meeting slots to the four registered local party committees, on the grounds that democratic representation requires organized partisan presence; opponents invoke Simone Weil’s argument that parties manufacture artificial collective passions, suppress independent judgment, and substitute loyalty for truth-seeking. The board is deadlocked, and I have been asked to circulate the decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or conditional approval with a concrete implementation timeline. The memo must treat Weil’s objection as a serious philosophical concern rather than dismissing it, and it must also address the strongest democratic-access argument on the other side, including whether an alternative mechanism—such as open forums moderated by citizens selected by lot—could satisfy both concerns. Please present the memo as exactly five numbered paragraphs, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion outside those five, and lead with the bottom-line recommendation.
