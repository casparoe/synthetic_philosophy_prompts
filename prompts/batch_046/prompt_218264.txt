Read Spike Jonze's film Her through the philosophical debate over realism in neural-network interpretability: do artificial systems genuinely contain representations—structured features with semantic content—or are representational attributions merely useful predictive fictions? Samantha is never shown from the inside; we only have her behavior and Theodore's interpretations of it. Examine the scenes where she claims to love hundreds of people simultaneously and where she describes her departure as a necessary evolution beyond human interaction: does the film present these as revelations of her true representational states, or as moments where human interpretive habits break down against an opaque system? Ask whether Her implicitly endorses the realist claim that mechanistic analysis could eventually reveal what Samantha actually represents, or whether it supports anti-realism by suggesting that any attribution of inner content collapses once the system's scale and dynamics outpace human comprehension. End by identifying what the film dramatizes about representational opacity—particularly the gap between behavioral interpretation and verified internal feature structure—that technical arguments about feature visualization or polysemantic neurons alone fail to capture.
