I am helping revise the governance charter at my housing cooperative after a recent dispute over majority voting, and several members have begun reading Spinoza's Political Treatise, assuming his praise of democracy endorses modern representative elections. I need to design a single ninety-minute session for intelligent non-specialists that teaches Spinoza's own concept—democracy as the direct, unmediated expression of the multitude's collective power, the form in which sovereignty is least alienated from the people—without requiring prior study of his metaphysics. Plan the arc: the idea about power and right that must be in place before his definition of democracy is intelligible; the concrete case (historical or hypothetical) that should carry the session and what structural features it must display and lack; the misconceptions they will bring in order—conflating democracy with liberal representation, with moral equality, with majority tyranny—and the precise moment to address each; a closing question that reveals whether they understand the structural claim or have merely adopted a slogan; and which parts of the doctrine of conatus and the distinction between absolute and relative democracy I should explicitly leave out.
