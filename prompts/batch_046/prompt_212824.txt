I am constructing a diagnostic instrument for a workshop on the philosophy of meaning, and I want it to sit at the intersection of absurdism and contemporary naturalist, narrative, and transcendentalist accounts without collapsing into the familiar objective/subjective/nihilist trilemma. Please design a decision tree—built from the logical space of commitments rather than from a list of author-names—that classifies a respondent's actual position through concrete case judgments. The instrument should contain exactly seven diagnostic nodes, each presenting a concrete scenario or forced choice (never asking directly “which label do you hold?”) with two or three answer options phrased in ordinary conceptual language. The scenarios must include: (i) Sisyphus finding genuine satisfaction in repetition despite cosmic pointlessness; (ii) a scientist who cures a fatal disease but lives in a universe lacking ultimate teleology; (iii) a life whose projects are constantly interrupted and reconstructed without ever achieving a single unifying narrative; and (iv) a world in which all human achievements are eventually erased by cosmic heat death, yet local relationships remain intact. The tree should terminate in eight to ten named positions, and these must include Camus-style absurdist rebellion with local meaning; strong subjective naturalism; narrative coherence accounts; objective-list theories that require cosmic-scale significance; nihilism denying even local value; and a distinct “fragmentary meaning” leaf in which meaning is real but non-cumulative and non-narrative—include this even if it is rarely defended in the literature. For combinations of answers that do not map onto any named position, note the unoccupied region and briefly diagnose whether it seems incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, paying particular attention to the combination that affirms both global teleological meaning and complete narrative fragmentation. The tree must also be able to separate positions that agree on all four cases but diverge in metaphysical explanation—for instance, two views that both affirm local meaning after cosmic pointlessness but differ on whether this requires a specific affective stance of defiance rather than simple contentment. Provide a brief scoring key explaining how to trace a respondent’s path from root to leaf. Write in British English, avoid bibliographies, citations, reading lists, and references to specific page numbers or publication dates, and do not include author biographies. The aim is to reveal whether a respondent holds a coherent but under-discussed hybrid—such as accepting absurdism as an affective condition while denying that it undermines objective local goods—rather than forcing them into standard categories.
