I'm presenting a 25-minute paper at an interdisciplinary workshop on institutional forecasting. My thesis is that in strategic forecasts about unique institutional configurations—specifically, predicting failure modes in scientific peer-review systems over multi-decade horizons—standard outside-view methods commit a structural error. Rather than blending inside and outside views with a fixed epistemic weight, I argue that the selection of a reference class is itself an inside-view judgment that should be second-order calibrated: we should apply outside-view base rates only within reference classes that have survived an inside-view screening for causal relevance. I call this calibrated reference-class construction, and I claim it preserves epistemic modesty better than standard blending because it makes the boundaries of our ignorance explicit rather than averaging over them.
