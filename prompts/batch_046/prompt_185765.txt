My friend and I just finished a long argument about Hacking's looping effects in psychiatric classification, and I have mapped her judgments consistently. She holds that ADHD is looped because the diagnosis reshaped classroom behavior norms and adolescent self-expectation; that schizophrenia is not looped, since its core phenomenology remains invariant under labeling practices; that social anxiety disorder loops because DSM criteria expanded to absorb ordinary shyness, which then raised self-identification and treatment-seeking; that autism spectrum reclassification loops because broadening the category changed who sought evaluation and how they narrated their own traits; that major depressive disorder is not looped, since prevalence tracks economic stressors rather than diagnostic fashion; and that prolonged grief disorder is not looped, because grief has culturally stable boundaries that resist expansion. Now consider a proposed diagnosis called algorithmic attention fatigue—cognitive fragmentation specifically attributed to prolonged interaction with recommendation systems—where epidemiological prevalence correlates tightly with population-level screen-time exposure, yet the symptoms overlap substantially with ADHD and anxiety except for the technological attribution, and where receiving the label may cause individuals to externalize executive dysfunction onto platforms rather than addressing underlying self-regulation. Predict whether she would classify this as looped, identify whether her implicit rule is diagnostic-boundary permeability, self-attribution feedback, or the absence of an independent environmental driver, and specify which further hypothetical case would discriminate between these three rules.
