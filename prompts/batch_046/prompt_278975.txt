I was chatting with my study group after our ethics semonor and two people got stuck arguing about G.E. Moore's views on beauty and friendship. One of them, call her A, said Moore treats friendship like aesthetic appreciation — the value is in the whole relational thing, not just the parts summed together, and reciprocity is just the compositional feature that makes the friendship what it is. The other guy, B, said that's wrong: beauty works through organic unity where the parts don't have standalone value, but friendship is different because each person's welfare is independently valuable, so treating friendship like a painting is a category mistake.

A pushed back that B was smuggling in utilitarian assumptions. Moore rejects summing parts, and friendship's value depends on the specific mutual awareness between people, just like a painting depends on specific color relations. B said okay, but then Moore has to explain why breaking a friendship — say, one person forgets the other due to amnesia — destroys value in a way that damaging part of a painting doesn't necessarily destroy the painting's value. A claimed that's actually consistent with organic unity: the whole in friendship includes the reciprocity, so losing reciprocity is like destroying the compositional relation in art.

Then B brought up this weird edge case he read about — if you have a friendship where one party is a perfect philosophical zombie with no inner life, but behaves identically, is the friendship still valuable? A said yes, because Moore cares about the experienced relation, not hidden mental states, but B said no, that proves friendship value requires actual conscious reciprocity in a way beauty doesn't require actual consciousness in the observer. A said B was just inventing zombie-friend scenarios to avoid the point. They both looked at me like I should settle it.

Who's right about the comparison between friendship and beauty in Moore's framework? Is B right that friendship involves independent value of parts in a way that undermines the organic unity analogy? Or is A right that reciprocity is just the compositional feature? And what, if anything, are both of them missing about how Moore distinguishes intrinsic goods?
