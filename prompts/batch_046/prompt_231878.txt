During a philosophy-of-mind seminar on whether dreams are experiences, a classmate invoked the line “uncorrectable reports aren’t reports of experience” to dismiss Dennett’s view that dream narratives are constructed upon waking: since no outsider can ever correct your account of what you dreamt, dreams lack the corrigibility required for genuine experience. I’m skeptical that one sentence settles the debate, especially if we imagine technologies that reconstruct dream imagery from neural signals and allow third-party correction. Can you lay out the distinct precise principles that slogan might stand for—verificationist criteria for experience, conditions of first-person authority, norms of testimony about mental states, and any others—as a menu of separate theses? For each, say whether it is defensible on its own, whether it actually undermines Dennett’s narrative-construction view or instead targets Malcolm’s verificationist position, and whether it survives the hypothetical possibility of neural reconstruction making dream reports correctable from the outside.
