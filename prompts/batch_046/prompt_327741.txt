I'm running a philosophy-of-math seminar — about 14 grad students, one 50-minute session. Design a card-and-token game that makes them feel Hilbert's hotel as a live fight between potential and actual infinity, not a story they nod at. Split the room: half are "potentialist" managers who may only ever build one new room-card when a guest shows up, so they never hold a completed infinite list; the other half are "actualist" shift-operators who begin with a symbolic infinite corridor (cards 1,2,3... in principle) and just remap guests onto even numbers when bus-infinity arrives. Give concrete rules, a point system (not cash) that rewards lodging but docks points for "cheating" by treating an unfinished process as finished, and a debrief mapping the exact round where potentialists fail to accomodate the bus onto Cantor's completed-totality move. Say what will predictably break — likely the actualists explain too loudly — and be honest about what finitist intuition this still misses (probably the epistemic worry about grasping the infinite, not the logic).
