Read Romeo and Juliet through causation by omission, but don't just list the friar's letter or Juliet staying quiet. I want you to take the metaphysical worry seriosly: are omissions actual causes, or just absences we paper over with counterfactuals? The plot is basically a chain of things not happening — the message not arriving, the families not reconciling, Romeo not checking Juliet's pulse. Ask whether Shakespeare treats these non-events as real causal powers, or just as gaps we fill in after the fact. And try this weird framing: "distributed omission," where no one person is blameworthy because the failure is spread across the feud, the messenger, and the lovers' own choices. Does the tragedy require a single guilty omission, or can a collective absence do the killing? Don't reduce the play to an illustration; say what the drama adds that a philosophy paper wouldn't. Also tell me what scene or argument would make you abandon this reading, and where it's weakest.
