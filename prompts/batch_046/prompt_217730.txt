I've been reading Darwin's Descent of Man and some of his private letters for a course on science and religion, and I walked into this loop with my study partner and can't find the exit. (1) Darwin thinks our moral sense evolved from social instincts in lower animals — it's not a direct perception of some objective moral law but basically a byproduct of selection for cooperative behavior. (2) He also seems to think religious belief comes from the same evolutionary bag: in his letters he talks about how humans have an innate tendency to personify forces and ascribe purpose where there is none, which produces belief in gods and spirits. (3) Now here's the move my friend made: if a belief-forming mechanism was shaped by natural selection purely for survival and reproduction, not for tracking truth about its subject matter, then beliefs it produces are unreliable about that subject. (4) That applies to morality — we shouldn't trust our moral intuitions as tracking objective moral facts. (5) It applies to religion too — religious belief is just evolutionary noise with no epistemic standing. (6) But wait: Darwin's own scientific reasoning, including the inference to evolutionary theory itself, was produced by cognative faculties that were also shaped by natural selection for survival, not for tracking abstract scientific truths about deep history. (7) So by the same principle, evolutionary theory itself is unreliable. (8) And if we say "well, science is self-correcting," that doesn't save Darwin specifically, because the self-correcting mechanisms are also evolved products shaped by fitness, not truth. (9) So Darwin's view proves too much: it doesn't just debunk religion and morality, it debunks science and itself.

My friend says the exit is at step (3), but I don't see why that principle wouldn't apply — it looks like a general claim about selection and reliability. Another friend says reject (6) by saying scientific cognition is different, but Darwin never really carved out a special exception for theoretical reason, so that feels ad hoc. Where does the argument actually slip? Is it an equivocation on "reliable"? A scope mistake between domains? Or do we just have to bite the bullet and accept that Darwin's framework really is globally skeptical, and that's the cost of keeping (1) through (3)? I'd especially like to keep (1) and (2) since they seem like straightforward readings of Darwin, but I'm open to giving up something if the price is clear.
