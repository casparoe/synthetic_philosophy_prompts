Write a villanelle that stages the disagreement between counterfactual and interventionist theories of causation through the problem of preemption. Use refrain A—"In the nearest world, the match stays dry"—to carry the counterfactual account, and refrain B—"Hold the mechanism fixed; shift only one node"—to carry the interventionist account. Across the five tercets, move from an initial appearance of agreement (both seem to explain why the strike caused the flame) through the divergence over preemption (a backup striker waits; the counterfactual must rank worlds in which it is absent, while the interventionist asks which invariant relation survives surgical removal of the actual strike), to the recognition that "nearest" already encodes a decision about which structural relations are modular. In the closing quatrain, interweave both refrains so that their rhyme and repetition make audible the claim that the dispute is not over evidence but over what must remain constant when we imagine the cause away. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher familiar with Lewis-style possible-world semantics and Pearl-style do-operators recognizes the technical distinction—especially the difference between similarity-based world-ranking and invariant modularity—without reducing either view to a label. Prioritize poetic coherence: let meter and rhyme serve the conceptual architecture. After the poem, include one section titled "Commentary on formal and philosophical choices" (about 200 words), explaining how the villanelle's repeating lines, rhyme scheme, and final-quatrain convergence map the two theories' respective treatments of preemption and structural invariance.
