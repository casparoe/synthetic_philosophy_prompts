I've been working through Bernard Williams's integrity objection — the claim that impartialist moral theories require an agent saving a spouse from drowning to have one thought too many if she pauses to weigh the moral calculus rather than acting straight from attachment. I take this not as a psychological quirk but as a structural thesis: that treating personal attachments as inputs to a deliberative calculus corrupts the ground projects that give life meaning, and that there is no stable middle ground between honoring an attachment and subjecting it to impartial justification. To make this concrete, consider a constructed case: a trauma surgeon who is also the parent of a child trapped in a collapsed building, where her ground project is not generic parental duty but a specific narrative identity — being the kind of person who never treats her child's survival as exchangeable against strangers', even in extremis. Given this framing, what would genuinely count against the integrity objection?

I want the arguments and evidence sorted by how decisive they would be for me, not merely listed. Which empirical findings about moral decision-making under extreme stress should I take seriously? Is there a defensible conceptual distinction between acting from attachment and acting with attachment acknowledged but overridden by an impartial reason? Could formal arguments show that recognizing an overriding reason need not corrupt the attachment itself? I also want an honest diagnosis of whether the objection risks unfalsifiability — if any successful rescue counts as integrity preserved and any deliberative hesitation counts as integrity violated — and whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind. Finally, tell me whether defenders of impartialism have already conceded enough about the importance of ground projects to absorb the objection without surrendering their core theory, and whether that absorption should be seen as a theoretical victory or a hidden defeat. Please distinguish what would be merely awkward from what should force abandonment, and identify any development — conceptual, empirical, or formal — that proponents of the objection themselves should concede as damaging.
