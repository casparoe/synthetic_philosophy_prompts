I am revising the syllabus for my graduate seminar in philosophy of value—twelve doctoral students, one ninety-minute session—after a frustrating discussion in which students kept treating absurdism and contemporary hybrid theories of meaning as merely competing answers to the same static question. Rather than lecturing on standard positions, I want them to feel, structurally, a conceptual pressure I have been tentatively calling “the reversibility of authorizing conditions”: the possibility that a life-project derives its felt significance precisely from conditions of social recognition, metaphysical grounding, or narrative coherence that, once satisfied, expose themselves as arbitrarily contingent, thereby collapsing the very meaning they were meant to secure. I need a runnable simulation or structured deliberation—complete with explicit rules, hidden roles or information, and small but real stakes, perhaps tokens representing “commitment capital” and “coherence points”—in which participants collectively construct a project, experience it as genuinely meaningful within the game’s internal logic, and then discover, through a rule-triggered revelation rather than any facilitator announcement, that the conditions sustaining its value depend on a contingency that undermines it retroactively. The mechanics should allow students to inhabit both the absurdist response—continuing in defiant awareness of groundlessness—and a hybrid-objectivist response—reconstructing value by revising the alignment between subjective investment and objective conditions—before either position is named.

Please design this activity with the setting and materials specified, step-by-step rules and payoff structures, and the precise moment at which the reversal should occur. Include a debrief plan that maps specific moments in the game onto these two philosophical responses, predicts where students will likely resist the collapse—perhaps by insisting it is merely epistemic rather than axiological—and explains how to handle that resistance in discussion. Be explicit about which feature of the conceptual tension, likely the temporal asymmetry between prospective commitment and retrospective evaluation, the game mechanics cannot reproduce and I must supply verbally. The exercise should be entirely self-contained: no assigned readings, no requests for citations or bibliographies, and no dependence on obscure theoretical texts.

Keep the deliverable formal and actionable, as if it were a session plan I could circulate to a teaching assistant. At the very end, provide a line beginning “Core mechanism:” followed by a one-sentence summary of the activity’s central philosophical move.
