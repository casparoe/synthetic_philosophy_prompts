Compare the fink analysis of dispositions—on which a disposition can be destroyed in the very instant its manifestation conditions are met, because an interfering mechanism removes the underlying property—with the mask (or antidote) analysis—on which the disposition remains fully intact but is prevented from manifesting by a blocking factor that interrupts the causal chain. Rather than rehearsing the canonical physical illustrations (a fragile vase struck by a hammer, a wire whose conductivity is interrupted, a poisonous compound neutralized by an antidote), apply both frameworks to one non-physical case: the disposition of a liberal democratic institution to resist authoritarian consolidation when subjected to acute systemic stress. For each framework, specify precisely what plays the role of the base property, the triggering conditions, the interfering factor, and the intended manifestation in this institutional application. Then take a committed position: does an authoritarian capture mechanism function as a fink, thereby annihilating the democratic disposition at the moment of crisis, or as a mask, leaving the disposition intact but suppressed? Defend your choice by isolating one structural disanalogy between institutional dispositions and standard physical cases—such as the absence of a discrete spatiotemporal bearer that can be destroyed, or the distributed and normative character of the institutional base—that undermines the rival analysis. Conclude by assessing whether your verdict survives if we abandon the assumption that institutional dispositions must have a single, localized bearer, and state explicitly what a better analogy for institutional resilience would look like if neither the fink nor the mask framework ultimately fits.
