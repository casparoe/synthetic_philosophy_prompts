People often say consciousness across animals is like a dimmer switch — not on/off but brighter or dimmer depending on the species. I heard this used in a debate about crab welfare: if crabs are just 'dimmed' conciousness, that apparently changes the moral maths. But then someone mentioned octopuses, where the arms have their own neural clusters, so maybe it's not one dimmer but loads of little lamps scattered about — a federation rather than a single bulb. Can you audit the dimmer-switch analogy properly? Lay out what corresponds to what, where the mapping breaks (especially with distributed nervous systems like an octopus's), wether the 'federation of lamps' image is any better, what each version smuggles in about gradation vs discrete thresholds, and whether the argument for extending welfare to crabs actually holds up once you accept consciousness might be patchy rather than uniformly dimmed?
