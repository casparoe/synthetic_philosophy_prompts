I'm designing a module for an advanced philosophy of action course centered on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, which contends that true moral responsibility is impossible because no agent can be causa sui—nothing can be the ultimate cause of its own mental nature, and therefore nothing can be genuinely deserving of praise or blame in the deep sense required by our practices. To push students past the standard compatibilist-versus-incompatibilist script, I want them to work with a deliberately constructed opposition: Camp A defends the Basic Argument's conclusion that unitary moral responsibility is a metaphysical impossibility; Camp B, which they call the "diachronic coherence" camp, accepts Strawson's formal premises—including that responsibility requires some form of authorship over one's dispositions and that being causa sui in the traditional, singular sense is impossible—but denies the conclusion by arguing that authorship can be distributed across a temporally extended sequence of mental states without any single slice being self-creating. Please draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a sustained adversarial collaboration.

The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims: about the structure of Strawson's regress, about why traditional libertarian self-creation fails, about the distinction between synchronic choice and diachronic formation of character, and about the conditions under which an agent might be said to "author" its dispositions rather than merely inherit them. Then include a disagreements table that states each camp's residual position in wording it would accept—Camp A framing distributed authorship as merely descriptive narrative continuity that carries no normative weight, Camp B framing diachronic coherence as sufficient for a non-ultimate but genuine form of responsibility. Identify the cruxes: specifically, whether authorship must be unitary to ground desert, and whether distributing the causal chain across time dissolves the regress or merely displaces it. For each crux, present a concrete hypothetical case—such as an agent who gradually reshapes a harmful disposition through years of reflective practice without ever being able to point to a single moment of self-creation—together with each camp's advance prediction about what that case demonstrates. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and present the result in the requested two-part format.
