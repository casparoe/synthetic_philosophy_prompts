My roommate and her friend Leo got into it over beers about whether "better explanation" is actually transitive. Maya said if integrated information theory explains consciousness better than global workspace theory, and global workspace is better than predictive processing, then IIT has to be better than predictive processing -- otherwise rational comparison collapses. Leo said thats wrong because "better" shifts meaning: IIT might win on unification, workspace on neural correlates, predictive processing on parsimony. So IIT > workspace on dimension X, workspace > predictive on dimension Y, but IIT could still lose to predictive on Y. Maya came back that Leo's just smuggling in different criteria -- if you fix "overall best" then the chain holds. Leo said "overall best" is exactly the problem, its a weighted spectrum with no principled cutoff, so treating it like a linear ordering is the mistake. Maya then said if Leo's right we can never rank more than two theories. Leo said no, we just need explicit weights, and transitivity failure is a feature not a bug.
