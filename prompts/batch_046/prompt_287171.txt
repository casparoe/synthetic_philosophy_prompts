I was arguing with my roommate about Chesterton's fence and whether Burkean conservatism actually tells us anything useful, and I realised my intuitions are kind of a mess. Here are five cases and what I think should happen. First, there's a town charter with a 50-foot building setback from the river, put there after a 1902 flood, but the upstream dam that caused the flooding was demolished in 1970 and hydrologists say the risk is genuinely gone now — I think tear it down. Second, a craft guild still forces people through a 7-year apprenticeship even though modern training could do it in three; the original reason was skill transmission, but now it's mostly about limiting entry and protecting wages — I still think keep the 7 years, because rapid change would destabilize the whole labor market in ways nobody can fully map. Third, there's an unelected upper chamber in a democracy, originally filled by religious appointment and aristocratic privilege, both justifications dead, but it still slows down populist bills — I say don't abolish it, just gradualy reform how members get picked. Fourth, a colonial-era law banning imported newspapers is still on the books, kept only because local papers like the protection — tear it down, no question. Fifth, my university department has this weird 30-minute ritual at the start of every faculty meeting, reading old minites in a specific order with a gavel, and nobody can say exactly what it does, but I feel like we should keep it because the slowness stops people from rushing into bad decisions.

The problem is cases one and five both look like obsolete protections with dead original reasons, but I'm tearing one down and keeping the other. Same with one and three — both have outdated justifications, but I'm treating them totally differently. So is there actually one principle behind all this, or am I just being inconsistent? If there is a coherent account, state it precisely, say which clause comes from which case, and tell me which of my five verdicts it forces me to drop. Also, what should it say about this other case: a biotech company engineered a kill switch into synthetic bacteria as a precaution, but now they fully understand the genome and the switch is blocking industrial use — should they disable it, or does the fence apply? I want the principle to cover that without me having to look up specific philosophers.
