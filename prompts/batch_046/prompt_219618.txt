Write an essay on evidential cooperation in large worlds — specifically, whether the logic that makes you cooperate with distant copies actually scales up when there are millions of agents with slightly noisy decision procedures. People often say it's just Newcomb's problem writ large, but the noise issue feels different and hasn't been dealt with properly. Weigh the argument that correlation still holds in expectation against the worry that with enough noise you're basically donating to randomness, and that it's correlation structure breaks down faster than people think. Reach a conclusion: should a rational agent act more cooperatively in large worlds, or does the argument collapse once you leave the two-agent case? Don't end with 'it depends' — defend an actual verdict. No bibliographies, just reason through it yourself.
