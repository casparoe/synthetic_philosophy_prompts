You will defend to a philosophy-of-mind seminar the claim that, if panpsychism is correct and fundamental physical fields possess proto-experiential properties, then deliberately arranging matter into high-entropy configurations that maximize disintegrated micro-suffering is morally wrong even in the absence of any integrated conscious subject. The audience includes committed physicalists who reject phenomenal properties at the quantum level, a panpsychist who grants micro-experience but denies that disintegrated states generate aggregate moral obligations, and an ethicist who treats the move from field properties to normative conclusions as an illicit is-ought leap. Give me the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased as each interlocutor would phrase them, with for each what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest point in my position that I should prepare to defend rather than hope they miss. Respond with exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, in American English.
