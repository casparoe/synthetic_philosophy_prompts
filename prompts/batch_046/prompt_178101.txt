In particular, I want to know whether James’s defense of the right to hold religious beliefs in the absence of sufficient evidence gains a different kind of support if those beliefs are understood as scaffolding rather than as ordinary factual claims, and whether Clifford’s shipowner analogy retains its full force when applied to framework-constituting commitments rather than to empirically verifiable propositions. Please structure your answer so that it first identifies the precise point of divergence between the two thinkers on this specific scaffolding issue, then evaluates which view handles the case more coherently, and finally articulates the strongest objection the losing side could raise without merely restating its general principle. I do not need bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I am writing this for my own conceptual clarification before I finalize the course design, and I need the argument to stand on its own terms.
