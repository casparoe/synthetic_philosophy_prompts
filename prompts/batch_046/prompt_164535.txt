I am a high school student moderating a virtual philosophy panel for my school's colloquium series, and next week's guest is an unnamed philosopher who recently defended a revised Popperian theory of verisimilitude. Rather than measuring truth-likeness through literal propositional overlap, her view employs what she terms "modal-proximity tracking": a false scientific theory is closer to the truth to the extent that its structural predictions align with possible-world neighborhoods adjacent to the actual world. I want interview questions that go far beyond asking her to restate this position. Please draft seven to nine questions in American English. At least one must address how modal-proximity tracking avoids the classic Miller-Tichy collapse objection; at least one must press whether applying this framework to climate-model ensembles collapses verisimilitude into a form of sophisticated instrumentalism; and at least one must ask how she adjudicates cases in which two competing false theories occupy equally close modal neighborhoods. For each of these three, include a sharp follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer—that modal proximity is formally tractable without logical-content comparison, that structural alignment is not reducible to mere predictive success, and that tie-breaking requires an independent theoretical-virtue metric, respectively. The remaining questions should probe other substantive tensions in her account, such as its treatment of partial truths in idealized models and its compatibility with scientific realism. Do not include any requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
