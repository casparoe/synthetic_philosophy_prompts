hey, i've been trying to figure out if guilt or shame is the better moral emotion, and whether a culture can just pick one and design itself around it. here are my gut reactions, and i'm wondering if there's one principle behind them or if i'm being inconsistent.

case 1: someone cheats on their partner, nobody ever finds out, but they feel crushing guilt and eventually confess. i think the guilt was appropriate — it tracked the harm, not just the risk of being caught. but confessing just to feel better feels selfish, even if the guilt itself was good.

case 2: some workplaces have public "error boards" where you post mistakes for everyone. people call this a "guilt culture" of acountability, but to me it feels like shame — you're exposed, not repairing. i think it's worse for moral growth.

case 3: imagine a culture with a "silent-restoration" practice (just a thought experiment, not a real group): if you harm someone, you fix it completely without mentioning it to them or anyone. no apology, no announcement, just quiet repair. i feel like this misses something — maybe guilt needs acknowledgment? — but i still think it's better than public shaming. not sure.

case 4: a society decides shame is barbaric and abolishes it, relying only on internal guilt for all wrongs. they call it "moral hygiene." i think this is possible but probably unstable — shame does work guilt can't do, especially with strangers or systemic harm? but maybe i'm wrong.

case 5: a kid lies about homework. parent makes them stand in the hallway with a sign (shame), vs. parent has them write why lying breaks trust and then fix the homework (guilt?). i think the second is better, mostly because the first makes the kid care about image, not harm.

case 6: a dying patient asks if their estranged child called. they didn't. telling the truth causes pain with no repair possible. lying avoids unnecessary harm, but also dodges a hard moment. i think a guilt culture says "face it," shame culture might say "protect dignity." i lean guilt, but weakly.

is there a principle? maybe guilt is good when focused on repair and internal standards, shame is okay only for social reintergration not exclusion? but then silent-restoration has repair without acknowledgment, which my principle might reject but my intuition kinda likes. also, can a culture actually choose? or are they stuck with whatever emotional architecture they inherited?

what principle captures these, which verdict do i have to drop, and what does it predict for a culture that tries to engineer its moral emotions from scratch?
