Please consider the following concrete situation and advise me on its underlying moral structure, rather than offering only generic practical guidance.

Two experienced engineers, Alex and Jordan, work at comparable manufacturing firms. Both independently disable an automated safety interlock during a controlled test—each acting on identical diagnostic data that strongly suggests the interlock is malfunctioning and that manual oversight is the safer course. In Alex's facility, the test proceeds without incident; the interlock's malfunction is later confirmed, and Alex is praised for sound judgment. In Jordan's facility, an undetectable, unrelated sensor degradation—something no available protocol could have predicted—interacts with the disabled interlock to produce a serious workplace injury. Jordan is prosecuted for negligence, while Alex is promoted.

The two made the same decision with the same competence, evidence, and intent; only an unforeseeable external interaction differentiated the outcomes. If you were counseling Alex, who feels both guilty for escaping blame and indignant at Jordan's punishment, how should he understand the moral comparison between his own conduct and Jordan's? Is he genuinely less blameworthy because no harm occurred, or is the disparity in judgment merely an artifact of fortune? And should Alex voluntarily disclose his identical decision to regulators, knowing that disclosure could trigger punishment despite the absence of harm?
