Please construct for me a conceptual example—ideally involving a physical system that undergoes continuous material replacement while first retaining, then losing, a discrete functional identity, such as a self-repairing measurement instrument whose calibration gradually drifts beyond recovery—that genuinely forces a choice between saying the same object persists through change and saying that successive temporal parts are related only by continuity conditions. I am especially interested in seeing where the analogy between temporal parts and spatial regions in a vector field breaks down, since that analogy initially made perdurantism seem plausible to me. In your answer, please separate the logical commitments that are uncontroversial within each position from the interpretive disputes that remain genuinely contested—such as whether perdurantism requires an ontology of events or can be formulated with objects alone—and do not present any single contested reading as the consensus view. If your example relies on a non-standard framing of persistence conditions, please note that explicitly.
