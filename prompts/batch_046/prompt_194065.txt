I'm putting together a position paper for my seminar on African philosophy and relational ethics, and I want a quick consistency check before I present it to the students. Here is where I stand, more or less: I take Ubuntu in the relational sense — that personhood is constituted through being embeded in caring social networks, not as an intrinsic property of isolated individuals — and I think this can actually ground robust individual rights, since the health of the network depends on recognising the dignity of each member. I also hold that there is a threshold condition of minimal rational agency necessary for entering the reciprocal relations that generate full moral status. But then I want to say that a severly disabled human being who lacks that agency still possesses full status purely in virtue of being held within those caring relations, not derivatively but directly. Finally, I extend this framework to non-human animals genuinely embeded in human social practices, but I deny it applies to ecosystems or artificial intelligence, since neither can participate in the reciprocal recogntion I take to be essential.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear tense but dissolve on closer inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs philosophically. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most problems at once. Write the answer in British English, please — I need to send it to a colleague in Oxford and I'd rather not have to correct the spelling myself.
