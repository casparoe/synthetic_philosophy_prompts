Write a decision-tree taxonomy sorting positions on whether global workspace theory (GWT) accounts for phenomenal consciousness, access consciousness, both, or neither. Structure the tree around three concrete splits that a student can answer without knowing view-labels: (1) Can there be phenomenal experience—something it is genuinely like—without that state being available for report, reasoning, or voluntary action guidance? (2) If yes, is local recurrent processing in early sensory cortex (feedback loops without fronto-parietal broadcasting) sufficient for that phenomenology, or does phenomenology require global broadcasting? (3) Can globally broadcast information ever occur with no accompanying phenomenology?

Map every combination of answers to a leaf. Label leaves with standard names where they exist (e.g., strong integrationist/global-workspace phenomenalism, Block-style access-phenomenal separation, local-recurrence phenomenalism, illusionism about phenomenal consciousness) and "unnamed" otherwise; each leaf must include exactly one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Note any unoccupied branches and briefly diagnose why they are empty—whether incoherent, unappealing, or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of named positions that this tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining the ambiguity.

Phrase every node as a claim about experience, reportability, or neural dynamics, not as a named doctrine. Separate what is relatively settled (e.g., that GWT was originally developed to explain cognitive access and reportability, not phenomenology per se) from what remains genuinely contested among specialists (e.g., whether local recurrent loops generate phenomenology independently of global broadcasting, and whether overflow experiments establish phenomenal excess over access).

Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. End with exactly one line in this form: Verdict: [brief phrase describing the root split and total leaf count].
