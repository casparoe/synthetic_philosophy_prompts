I'm preparing a graduate seminar on Gödel's philosophical theology, specifically the under-examined intersection between his mathematical platonism, his rationalist optimism about the intelligibility of the world, and his private remarks—recorded in correspondence and posthumous notes—suggesting that belief in an afterlife is not irrational but structurally analogous to belief in the consistency of arithmetic. Rather than asking for a standard literature review, I want a sorting instrument that maps the logical space of interpretations of Gödel's position on personal survival after death.

Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy that takes a reader from a minimal shared commitment—acceptance of Gödel's ontological argument for a necessary being with positive properties, together with his platonist view that mathematical objects exist independently of minds—through a sequence of concrete diagnostic questions to named or unnamed interpretive positions. The branches should be separated by substantive philosophical choices rather than by pre-existing labels: for instance, whether the positive properties invoked in the ontological argument must include individual identity-preservation, or whether they can be satisfied by a transpersonal participation in an ordered realm of concepts; whether Gödel's conviction that reality is fundamentally rational entails that conscious agents must eventually understand all truths (thereby implying continued cognition), or merely that truths are in principle accessible regardless of who accesses them; and whether his theological rationalism permits an empirically undetectable but logically necessary post-mortem state, or whether such a state must bear some analogical connection to mathematical intuition.

Each leaf should state the position—such as individual theistic immortality, structural-formalist persistence, rationalist agnosticism about personal survival, or unnamed—together with its most awkward commitment, that is, what it must deny or accept that feels counterintuitive. You should also flag any pairs of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate, and identify any logically possible combinations of answers that remain unoccupied: diagnose whether the gap arises because the combination is incoherent with Gödel's premises, unattractive on independent grounds, or simply unexplored in the literature. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the instrument should be usable by someone without library access. Keep the diagnostic questions phrased so that a student could answer them on the basis of philosophical judgment rather than prior knowledge of Gödel scholarship.
