I'm writing up my commitments on standing to blame and hypocrisy for a seminar consistency check. Here is roughly where I've landed: (i) I hold a comparative account of standing—having done wrong W doesn't automatically disqualify you from blaming another for W; what matters is whether your own fault is comparable in severity and culpability, so a minor excused instance doesn't block blame of a flagrant one. (ii) I reject the strong "clean hands" doctrine: moral equality between blamer and blamed doesn't require identical moral records, only that the blamer isn't guilty of the same wrong in a comparable or greater degree. (iii) Hypocrisy is narrowly circumscribed: it requires not just having done W and now condemning others for it, but a present attitude of moral self-exemption—applying the norm to others while treating oneself as outside its scope, with awareness. (iv) Hypocrisy is worse than the underlying wrong because it corrupts moral authority specifically; it doesn't merely add another wrong but undermines the agent's capacity to speak on behalf of the norm. (v) Blame is legitimate only when it expresses judgment from a position of uncorrupted moral authority; a hypocrite's blame is therefore illegitimate, not merely unpersuasive. (vi) In cases of structural injustice, members of dominant groups lack standing to blame members of subordinate groups for reactive wrongs—say, property crimes committed in response to systemic deprivation—even when those reactive wrongs are technically more severe than any fault of the blamer. (vii) I reject identity-based moral exemptions: belonging to a socially dominant group doesn't excuse one's wrongs, nor does belonging to a subordinate group excuse reactive wrongs.
