Alfred North Whitehead maintained that education should unfold through successive rhythms of romance, precision, and generalization, and he cautioned against inert ideas—knowledge acquired without being actively incorporated into lived experience. Consider a present-day proposal for universal K-12 schooling in which every student wears a lightweight, non-invasive neural-monitoring headset that tracks attentional states in real time. The system uses these biometric signals to continuously adapt digital coursework, automatically calibrating difficulty and sensory modality so that each learner remains within a narrowly defined optimal engagement zone. Standardized examinations are abolished; instead, a distributed-ledger architecture maintains an unbroken, algorithmically generated record of granular competencies. Drawing on Whitehead's philosophy of organism, his insistence on the primacy of immediate experience over detached abstraction, and his account of the interplay between freedom and disciplined structure in learning, assess whether he would regard this closed-loop, biometrically governed system as advancing the stages of romance and precision, or as institutionalizing a new form of inert abstraction. Defend your interpretation, and address specifically how automated pedagogical control relates to his conviction that genuine education requires both rigorous method and the spontaneous exercise of freedom.
