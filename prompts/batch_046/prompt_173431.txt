Vasquez grants that a global, system-wide inviolate level collapses once self-reference is admitted into a cognitive architecture, but she argues that domain-specific modules—particularly those governing syntactic binding in language processing—can maintain an internal "floor" that never becomes tangled with the representational content it processes, precisely because they operate on structural rather than semantic properties. I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to summarize Hofstadter or restate her thesis. For each of these three, please include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give: for instance, if she appeals to modular encapsulation, ask what happens when cross-modal integration forces syntax into direct semantic negotiation; if she invokes structural autonomy, ask whether that autonomy survives when the system learns to represent its own binding rules; and if she points to predictive processing, ask what pattern of error would distinguish a broken floor from a merely noisy one. The remaining questions should probe the boundary conditions of her view—whether the floor can be nested, whether it applies to non-linguistic domains like numerical cognition, and whether admitting localized floors forces her to concede some version of the global hierarchy she rejects.
