I'm auditing a set of commitments I've arrived at about Marx on exploitation and alienation, and I want to know whether they form a stable package or whether I'm helping myself to incompatible motivations. Here is where I stand:

(a) I read exploitation as a relation of domination and control in production—rooted in workers' separation from the means of production and from authority over the labor process—rather than as a quantitative imbalance in labor-time exchange, and I treat the labor theory of value as unnecessary for this critique.

(b) I understand alienation as an objective structural condition: specifically, the worker's lack of control over the purpose, pace, and product of her labor. It is not a subjective psychological state like dissatisfaction or estrangement.

(c) I hold that this structural alienation persists in settings that lack capitalist ownership: a worker-owned cooperative operating under competitive market discipline and an intact technical division of labor would still be objectively alienated, even though no capitalist owner is extracting surplus.

(d) I take Marx's normative critique to derive its force entirely from this objective domination and alienation; I do not think it needs any supplementary appeal to moral rights, distributive justice, or fairness.

(e) At the same time, I maintain that an individual worker who reports genuine autonomy, creative satisfaction, and control over her immediate tasks—say, a senior engineer in a capitalist technology firm—is not personally alienated, even though she works within a structurally alienating system.

(f) I believe that remedying alienation requires transforming or abolishing the detailed division of labor, not merely socializing ownership.

(g) I also hold that surplus extraction is real and objectionable, but that it is best explained by institutional power asymmetries and property rights rather than by any value-theoretic account of embodied labor.

Which of these genuinely pull against one another, which only appear to, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify exactly what would have to give in each case, and do not treat every pairing as equally problematic. In particular, I am unsure whether (b) and (e) can coexist, whether (a) and (c) fracture the unity of the critique claimed in (d), and whether there is a hidden normative premise in (d) that (g) undermines.
