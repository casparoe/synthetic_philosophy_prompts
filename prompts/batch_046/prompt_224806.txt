I'm trying to stabilize the following package of views for a project on administrative ontology and informational rights, and I need a consistency audit before I present them. Here are my commitments:

(1) Frege's puzzle about identity—how a true statement that the morning star is the evening star can be genuinely informative—is best resolved by distinguishing sense from reference, where one object is presented under different modes.

(2) Applied to civic data systems, a citizen's legal name and her biometric identifier refer to the same individual but present her under different administrative modes: one as a rights-bearing agent, the other as a physically trackable system.

(3) Therefore, maintaining separate storage architectures and access rules for these two identifiers is not an error about identity; it encodes a legitimate difference in how the state relates to her.

(4) At the same time, I hold that modes of presentation are not mind-independent features of reality but are constituted entirely by institutional conventions and access protocols.

(5) I also hold that if two designators are co-referential in every actual and counterfactual administrative context, then maintaining distinct records for them is pure notational redundancy and should be eliminated for efficiency.

(6) Finally, I maintain that citizens possess a right to domain opacity: the state ought to be prohibited from merging health, fiscal, and mobility records even when they all concern the same person, because such merging collapses administratively distinct modes of presentation into a single totalizing profile.

Which commitments genuinely conflict, which conflicts are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, identify the specific step or pair that has to give, and explain what I pay if I keep (3) and (6) but reject (4) or (5).
