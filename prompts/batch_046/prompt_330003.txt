I've been trying to work out my own position on advance directives and whether an earlier version of me should have authority over a future version who might be severely demented. Here is where I've landed: (1) I think if I develop advanced dementia, my prior directive refusing prolonged life support should still be honored, because I am still the same person and letting my disease-altered preferences override the values I held for decades would violate my true autonomy. (2) I also think personal identity is mainly psychological continuity—if my memories, character, and core values are largely erased, then in an important sense the original person is gone, and enforcing the directive harms someone who is not really me. (3) At the same time, I don't think identity is all-or-nothing; psychological connection comes in degrees, so the moral weight of my past directive should fade gradually rather than vanish instantly. (4) I believe that as long as I retain decision-making capacity, I should be free to revise or revoke any directive, since treating it as an irrevocable contract would disrespect my present autonomy. (5) Finally, I think end-of-life medical decisions are categorically different from everyday preferences like diet or career, so the fact that I can change my mind about trivial matters doesn't automatically extend to revoking a directive about being kept alive.

Do these commitments hang together? I'm especially concerned that (1) and (2) seem to pull in opposite directions—if psychological continuity is what makes me me, and it is broken, how can I claim the directive still binds the same person? And does (3) conflict with (1), since (1) sounds like full authority while (3) suggests it should weaken proportionally? Meanwhile, (4) looks like it clashes with (1), but I suspect that tension dissolves once we distinguish having capacity from lacking it. Please audit the set: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, rank them by severity, specify the cheapest revision for each, note any tensions that only look serious but dissolve on inspection, and tell me which single change would resolve the most conflicts while preserving my core idea that long-held values should not be casually overridden by disease.
