Here is roughly where I've landed on confirmation logic, and I'd like an audit of whether the package is jointly tenable. (1) I take Hempel's raven paradox to be a substantive challenge to naive inductivism, not merely a quirk of natural-language framing. (2) I accept Nicod's criterion that observing a black raven confirms "all ravens are black" and that observing a non-black non-raven should not. (3) I think the standard Bayesian dissolution technically works—observing a green apple does confer an infinitesimal likelihood-ratio update—but only because it presupposes a prior over the space of all objects that treats "being a raven" as a live possibility for every item observed, which strikes me as cosmologically extravagant rather than epistemically innocent. (4) Consequently, I reject that prior and want to say that, in ordinary scientific practice, seeing a green apple gives literally zero confirmatory weight to the ornithological hypothesis. (5) At the same time, I want to maintain that logical equivalence preserves confirmatory relations: if two hypotheses are extensionally identical, then any observation must bear the same logical relation to both. (6) But I also want to index confirmation to what I'm calling the intensional framing of the inquiry—so that the same green-apple observation might have different epistemic salience depending on whether the investigator is actively engaged in testing color-generalizations about ravens versus testing color-generalizations about apples—without that framing altering the underlying logical symmetry. The tension I feel is between (5) and (6): if framing can make an observation salient or salient-free, doesn't that functionally break the equivalence that (5) demands? Is there a consistent way to let intensional context govern psychological or methodological salience while keeping extensional logical relations untouched? Or does one of these commitments have to go? Be specific about the cheapest repair and what it costs—especially whether giving up (6) forces me back into the Bayesian resolution I already rejected in (3) and (4).
