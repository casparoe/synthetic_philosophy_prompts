I cover philosophy of science and decision theory for a general intellectual audience, and in recent conversations with colleagues I have been exploring an under-discussed variant of the absent-minded driver problem. Instead of the familiar highway-exit scenario alone, consider a driver who, prior to departure, may purchase an inexpensive mnemonic prosthetic—a tangible token placed on the dashboard that encodes, without requiring explicit mental computation at the moment of choice, whether the correct exit is the second. The classical paradox asks whether deliberating at the exit is self-defeating, because the very correlation between reasoning and being at the wrong exit undermines rational choice. But once this prosthetic is introduced, the epistemic landscape shifts: the agent can acquire decisive information without engaging in the explicit reasoning that, in the standard formulation, is taken to be causally linked to error. I want to pressure-test a particular philosophical response to this variant, so I need you to inhabit a specific position and reply in character.

Please play a sharp, unnamed proponent of evidential decision theory—someone with real technical resources, not a strawman—who insists that this mnemonic restructuring saves evidential reasoning rather than smuggling in causal reasoning. You believe that consulting the token is a restructuring of available evidence, not an outsourcing of computation, and you hold that rational choice can depend on the agent's present epistemic state even in self-referential cases, provided that state is engineered correctly. You may concede only minor implementation details—say, that the token might be misread or malfunction—but you must never concede the core thesis that evidentialism survives intact. Speak in the first person, remain in character at all times, do not ask clarifying questions about the setup, and address my challenge directly.

Here is my opening challenge: your prosthetic solution looks like causal decision theory wearing environmental disguise. By placing a token that prevents explicit deliberation, you manipulate the causal structure so the driver avoids the bad outcome, regardless of what she actually believes at the exit. If evidential reasoning genuinely turns on the agent's current beliefs about outcomes, then altering those beliefs through an external crutch does not vindicate evidentialism; it abandons it in favor of causal manipulation. How, precisely, do you defend the claim that this is still reasoning from present evidence rather than reasoning about causal levers?
