Here is an argument I have been developing, and I find it suspicious because the conclusion undermines not only functionalism but any non-physicalist account that allows multiple realization. (1) Functionalism identifies each mental state with a specific causal-functional role. (2) Multiple realizability holds that this role can be occupied by physically heterogeneous substrates—call them P in humans, Q in silicon-based systems, and R in hypothetical organisms—each with distinct microphysical causal powers. (3) Consider a counterfactual claim about mental causation: if the subject had not been in mental state S, behavioral effect E would not have occurred. For S to genuinely ground this counterfactual, the absence of S must guarantee the absence of E regardless of which substrate realizes S. (4) But because P, Q, and R have different physical causal pathways, the mechanisms linking S to E differ across realizations. In some substrates, E might still occur through non-functional physical pathways even when the functional role is absent; in others, the absence of the role might reliably prevent E. (5) Therefore, counterfactuals grounded in S are not stable across realizations: they hold for some substrates and fail for others. (6) If mental states are to figure in scientific explanation or in ascriptions of moral responsibility, they must support stable counterfactuals. (7) So either mental states are not multiply realizable—which contradicts functionalism's central commitment—or they lack the explanatory and normative status we ordinarily attribute to them. (8) Given that denying mental causation or moral responsibility is more costly than abandoning functionalism, we should reject functionalism. Where does this argument fail? In particular, is the demand for cross-realizational counterfactual stability legitimate, or does it conflate the causal powers of a mental state with those of its particular physical implementation? I would prefer to preserve both mental causation and some form of multiple realizability, so I want to know exactly what I am giving up if I reject step (3) or step (6).
