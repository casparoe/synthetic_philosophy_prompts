I'm drafting a feature on the philosophical afterlife of Schrödinger's Vedanta-inspired monism, and before filing I want a consistency audit of my own interpretive stance. Here are the commitments I have settled on:

(1) Consciousness is numerically one: what we experience as separate minds are not ontologically distinct subjects but modal differentiations—localized perspectives—of a single, non-composite mental reality, in line with the Vedantic tradition Schrödinger engaged.

(2) This monism is nevertheless compatible with a modest physicalism, on the condition that physicalism permits a singular mental reality to be locally constrained or "focused" by neural architecture without being ontologically generated by it.

(3) I reject panpsychism: fundamental physical entities possess no proto-experiential properties; consciousness is not distributed across matter but is a primitive, irreducible feature that becomes phenomenally articulated only through complex biological organization.

(4) Personal identity is therefore not psychological continuity in the Parfitian sense, but rather the persistence of this universal consciousness in a particular embodied aperture; the death of the organism dissolves a boundary rather than extinguishing a self.

(5) Evolutionary biology explains why brains evolved as effective localization mechanisms, yet it provides no explanatory purchase on the existence of consciousness itself, which remains primitive.

Do these hang together? I need the tensions ranked by severity, with the cheapest revision for each. I suspect the sharpest conflict is between (2) and (4): standard physicalism requires local supervenience, yet (4) implies that consciousness survives the dissolution of the local physical system that was supposed to constrain it. I also worry that (1) and (3) may look redundant—if consciousness is singular, isn't that just panpsychism with only one subject?—though I think that tension may dissolve on inspection. Please identify which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which are only apparently so, and if I must abandon one commitment to restore coherence, which single revision costs me the least while preserving the core Vedantic insight that consciousness is fundamentally unitary.
