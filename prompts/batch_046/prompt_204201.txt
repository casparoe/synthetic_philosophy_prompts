Please explain the conceptual issue at stake in the debate over whether DNA constitutes a literal code rather than a causal template or structural blueprint. Begin by distinguishing two conceptions of coding that are often conflated: (a) an arbitrary syntactic system in which symbols bear meaning through conventional or detached interpretation rules, so that the same symbol can in principle be assigned different meanings without altering its physical form; and (b) a causally embedded representational process in which the interpretation mechanism is physically continuous with the message substrate, such that the "reading" of the message is partly constituted by the chemical and cellular environment in which it occurs.

Then address the following specific conceptual tension: DNA exhibits both degeneracy (multiple codons mapping to the same amino acid) and extensive context-sensitive decoding, including alternative splicing, epigenetic modulation, and environment-dependent gene expression. Critics of the code metaphor argue that these features violate the independence condition required for genuine coding—namely, that interpretation rules should be stable regardless of substrate context—because in biology the "decoder" (the cellular machinery and its state) is not external to the message but is part of the physical system carrying it. Defenders respond that this independence condition reflects an anachronistic computer-science bias, and that biological codes are precisely those representational systems in which interpretation is inseparable from the causal dynamics of the substrate.

Without invoking specific named theories or asking for citations, explain whether context-sensitive decoding undermines, strengthens, or conceptually transforms the claim that DNA is a code. In particular, clarify what is philosophically at stake in this dispute: does insisting on the code metaphor commit one to a form of semantic essentialism about genetic information that is incompatible with developmental plasticity? Or does denying the metaphor risk collapsing genetic information into mere causal correlation, thereby losing the normative and representational dimensions that allow us to speak of genetic error, misexpression, or evolutionary selection acting on informational content?
