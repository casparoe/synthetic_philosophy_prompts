Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a juridical minimalist—who defends a strict, Lemkin-derived group-based legal definition of genocide and worries that politicized naming erodes juridical neutrality—and a moral maximalist—who argues the category must remain responsive to evolving moral recognition of systematic group destruction, even if that requires interpretive expansion and accepts that naming is always partly political.

Structure the document exactly as follows. First, a numbered section of claims both can sign regarding: (a) the formal elements of the legal definition; (b) the historical context in which Lemkin formulated it; (c) the observable fact that states and NGOs apply the label selectively and strategically; and (d) the conceptual distinction between genocide as a status offense (fixing the victim group's protected identity) and as a process offense (fixing the perpetrator's pattern of conduct). Second, a disagreements table in which each side's position is stated in wording that side would accept, with no straw-manning. Third, identify the cruxes—I take the central ones to be (i) whether insulating the legal category from political advocacy strengthens or betrays its purpose, and (ii) whether retroactive application of the label to past atrocities performs a moral-ontological change that undermines legal predictability. Fourth, for each crux provide a concrete case or evidentiary scenario both sides agree is relevant—for instance, the international legal assessments of the 1994 Rwandan genocide, the parliamentary recognition debates over the Armenian genocide in European states, and the current juridical evaluations of mass violence against Uyghurs—and record in advance what each side predicts that case reveals about the crux.

The statement should be constructive, neither adjudicating the dispute nor declaring it verbal, though if part of it is merely verbal say precisely which part and why. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading.
