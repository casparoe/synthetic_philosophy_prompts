I serve on the evaluation-policy panel of a national humanities funding council. A working group has proposed restructuring our grant criteria around Fernand Braudel’s tripartite temporal scheme—geographical time, social time, and the time of events—arguing that proposals addressing slow structures (climate patterns, demographic shifts, spatial organization) should receive preferential weight, while event-driven research (political biography, diplomatic history, individual decision-making) should be deprioritized on the grounds that only long-duration phenomena constitute genuine historical explanation. The proposal includes a specific weighting formula and claims this aligns funding with structural reality. Half the panel finds this a necessary correction to presentism; the other half sees it as a category error that conflates ontological depth with evaluative importance and risks eliminating the study of human agency altogether.

Draft the panel’s decision memo: recommend adoption, rejection, or conditional adoption with specific modifications, with an actionable bottom line and a time frame for implementation review. The memo must engage Braudel’s framework directly—not as a slogan—address the scale paradox (that a funding body operating at intermediate social time cannot legitimately privilege millennial-scale structures without undermining its own institutional authority), and give an honest account of the strongest argument for retaining event-level funding. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
