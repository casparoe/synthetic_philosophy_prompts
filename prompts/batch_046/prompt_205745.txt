In the philosophy seminar I attend through my school’s advanced humanities program, a fellow student recently summarized the literature on metaphysical grounding in the following way: grounding is the non-causal relation of ontological priority that explains why derivative entities exist in virtue of more basic ones; it is irreflexive, asymmetric, and necessarily transitive, so every chain of dependence must terminate in a unique, absolutely fundamental stratum—usually taken to be fundamental physical entities. Jonathan Schaffer, however, accepts this layered termination thesis only by applying it to the cosmos as a whole: he holds that the entire universe is the single ungrounded object and that all particular things depend on it, which means the question of what grounds the grounding relation itself never arises, since that relation operates only among derived entities. Meanwhile, Jessica Wilson’s well-known objection is not about well-foundedness or infinite descending chains but solely that grounding is redundant with modal supervenience. From all this, the student concluded that the debate is essentially settled except for the single remaining issue of whether grounding is a genuine metaphysical relation or merely a useful explanatory metaphor.

I am inclined to think that the part about Schaffer misrepresents his view—since I understand him to deny that fundamental particles form the bottom layer—but I am unsure whether the error is in attributing the layered picture to him, in the claim that his monism dissolves the grounding-of-grounding problem, or in the framing of Wilson’s critique as exhausting the opposition. Could you audit this summary sentence by sentence: indicate which claims a specialist in metaphysical grounding and fundamentality would accept, which involve subtle conflations—perhaps between grounding and causation, between Schaffer’s cosmic monism and standard fundamentality pluralism, or between well-foundedness and the redundancy objection—and whether the conclusion that only one live disagreement remains actually survives once those corrections are made? I would especially like to know whether there is a hidden formal or conceptual dispute—about transitivity, about infinite descent, or about whether grounding applies to relations—doing the work behind the apparent stalemate.
