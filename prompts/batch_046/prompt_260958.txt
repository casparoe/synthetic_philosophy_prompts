I serve on the steering committee for a cooperative that holds collective rights to a regional watershed. We are drafting a covenant stating that if groundwater levels fall below a predetermined scientific threshold, all allocation authority automatically shifts to an independent trustee appointed now; reversing that shift would require unanimous consent from both current members and all future shareholders, including those not yet admitted. Proponents argue that this protects the group from short-term panic during drought, while opponents say it unfairly binds people who never consented to membership—especially because the mechanism activates through an appointed delegate rather than a direct democratic decision. I am trying to decide whether supporting this covenant is ethically defensible. How should I think about the legitimacy of locking in an irrevocable, conditionally triggered delegation that binds people who are not yet part of the collective?
