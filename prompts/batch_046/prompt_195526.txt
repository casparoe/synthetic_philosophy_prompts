I am directing a philosophy-of-science research group with three years of dedicated funding for conceptual work on AI alignment—work that is strictly theoretical rather than empirical, and intended to clarify what is being asked before any technical implementation is pursued. I am convinced that the phrase “the alignment problem” obscures at least three distinct conceptual strains: first, the aggregation problem, concerning how heterogeneous and potentially incommensurable values are synthesized into a coherent objective without reducing them to a simple weighted sum; second, the conservation problem, concerning whether and how such an objective can be preserved through autonomous structural modification and capability gain; and third, the delegation problem, concerning the normative and epistemic conditions under which it is rational to cede decision-making authority to a system whose objectives we have specified but whose reasoning we do not fully govern.

Within each strain there are specific open questions—for instance, whether value aggregation requires a prior meta-ethical framework that resolves conflicts by ranking rather than weighting, whether conservation demands a form of functional identity incompatible with open-ended learning, and whether the delegation problem can be addressed through normative political philosophy independently of the first two strains, or whether it is genuinely downstream of them.

The group can pursue at most two of these strains in depth. I want you to propose a defended prioritization: which strains, and which specific questions within them, should receive the group’s primary effort over the next three years? Your answer should not be a survey or a bibliography. Instead, provide an agenda that explicitly defends the ordering according to criteria of conceptual tractability for philosophers (as opposed to ML researchers), upstream dependency (which questions must be settled before others become well-formed), and the magnitude of downstream change that resolving each question would produce in how we allocate research effort. In particular, address whether the delegation problem is best treated as an application of prior results from aggregation and conservation, or as an autonomous domain of normative theory. Also specify what theoretical progress—say, within the first eighteen months—would cause you to revise the ranking upward or downward. Finally, identify which line you are deprioritizing not on grounds of irrelevance, but because its resolution, even if achieved, would not substantially alter the division of labor in alignment research.
