I'm taking a metaphysics seminar this term and trying to stabilize my views on origin essentialism and personal identity before I present them. Here is the package I've arrived at, and I'd like an audit of whether it actually holds together.

(1) I accept origin essentialism in roughly the form associated with Kripke: I could not have come from a different sperm-egg pair; those particular gametes are essential to me.
(2) For diachronic identity, I favor psychological continuity with appropriate causal dependence — broadly a Parfit-style account of persistence.
(3) I take (1) and (2) to answer different questions — origin conditions versus persistence conditions — so I do not treat them as competing theories.
(4) I think I could have had a somewhat different genetic profile (say, an allele producing a different eye color) and still be me, provided my psychological history remains continuous.
(5) To keep (4) compatible with (1), I insist that any modal genetic variation must arise from in-utero mutation or developmental noise, not from gametic substitution — even if a different sperm from the same father carried the variant allele. So I distinguish sharply between token-gamete origin and genetic sequence.
(6) I deny that a perfect psychological upload — continuous with my mental life but lacking any biological origin — is identical to me, precisely because it violates the origin condition in (1).
(7) I reject using "closest possible world" semantics to evaluate counterfactuals about my origin, since I think origin essentialism renders worlds with different gametes modally distant regardless of overall similarity to the actual world.

Please go through these commitments pair by pair: which are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each real tension? For every tension you identify, say what the least costly revision would be and what accepting that revision commits me to elsewhere. I'm especially concerned that (5) smuggles in an unstated principle — that origin essentialism cares about the token gamete rather than the genetic sequence — and that this principle may conflict with (6) in a way I haven't noticed. If that hidden contradiction is real, I want it laid out specifically.

Also: if my separation in (3) of origin and persistence conditions is conceptually confused, or if (7) rests on a mistake about modal logic or counterfactual evaluation, say so plainly rather than accommodating my framing. And if the task as posed can't be done well — for instance, if (4) and (5) are too vaguely specified to adjudicate — tell me that directly instead of producing a forced verdict. I want pushback where it's due.
