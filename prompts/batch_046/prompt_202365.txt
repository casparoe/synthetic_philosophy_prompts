Write a villanelle (ABA rhyme, two refrains alternating through five tercets and closing in a quatrain) spoken by Adam Smith's impartial spectator observing an ordinary failure of proportion—for instance, someone grieving a broken cup with the intensity due a death, or showing coldness where warmth is expected. Choose one refrain that expresses the spectator's imaginative entry into the agent's situation and one that expresses the judgment of whether the feeling is proportionate to its cause. Use the form's repetitions so that each return shifts meaning: the entry-refrain should move from compassion to comparison, the judgment-refrain from observation to approval or disapproval. In the final couplet let the two refrains stand side by side without merging into one voice. The poem should reflect that for Smith sympathy is not emotional fusion but an imaginative comparison yielding a verdict of propriety, and that the spectator is an internalized construct rather than an actual observer. Make it recognizable to someone familiar with his moral philosophy and good as poetry to someone who is not.
