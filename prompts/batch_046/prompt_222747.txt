My opening move: I want to press you on a case I invented—call it the split-loop. Suppose a person's self-referential neural patterns are divided: half the loop is implanted in Brain A, half in Brain B, linked by a real-time interface so that together they form one continuous loop of self-reference, but neither brain alone contains it. If Brain A is asked "Are you the original person?" and Brain B is asked the same, both might answer yes, yet neither is complete alone. Is there genuinely one distributed "I" across both, or is there none at all because no single brain houses the loop?
