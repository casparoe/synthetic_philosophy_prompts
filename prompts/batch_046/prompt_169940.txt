I have recently retired from experimental physics and have begun working through epistemology with the same rigor I once applied to measurement theory. I find myself drawn to the regress problem, but I am unsure whether my commitments form a coherent position. Here is my current profile:

(1) Justification is structurally linear: any belief I genuinely know can be traced through a finite, actually traversed chain of reasons that terminates in basic beliefs—beliefs justified without further doxastic support, though they may depend on non-doxastic processes.

(2) These basic beliefs are not epistemically isolated or self-certifying. Their foundational status depends on a calibration loop: the reliability of the processes that generate them is established only through the long-run predictive coherence of the broader system in which they are embedded.

(3) In scientific practice, justification is irreducibly holistic. Every observation is theory-laden and instrument-dependent; there is no neutral ground from which theories can be linearly derived, so empirical support works through mutual coherence rather than one-directional inference.

(4) I reject infinitism on the basis of what I term cognitive thermodynamics: an infinite regress of reasons requires unbounded processing resources, and since actual knowers are finite physical systems with bounded cognitive capacity, infinitely descending justification chains are epistemically inert for real agents.

(5) I reject pure coherentism because coherence without structural termination cannot discriminate between a well-integrated fiction and a knowledge-constituting system; termination points are therefore necessary.

Do these hang together? In particular, does the calibration loop in (2) undermine the independence required by (1)? Does the holism of (3) render the linear structure in (1) descriptively false for scientific knowledge? Is (4) a legitimate philosophical constraint or an illicit analogy from physics? And is the tension between (3) and (5) only apparent—dissolving once we distinguish the practical dynamics of theory confirmation from the formal architecture of justification?

Please provide an audit: rank the genuine tensions by severity, distinguish them from merely apparent conflicts, identify the least costly revision that would restore consistency, and specify which single commitment, if abandoned, would do the least damage to the remaining four.
