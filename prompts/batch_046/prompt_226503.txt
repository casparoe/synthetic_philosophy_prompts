I am designing a final exam for an upper-level metaphysics seminar and need one essay question on the special composition question that cannot be answered by reciting the standard taxonomy—universalism, nihilism, and restricted composition with its familiar contact or functional-integration conditions. Some drones sit near the boundary, gradually drifting out of the lattice.

Write an exam prompt suitable for a 90-minute essay that asks students to do three things. First, apply unrestricted, nihilist, and moderate restricted answers to whether the swarm is a single composite object during that window. They must distinguish this from the classic Problem of the Many, noting that here there is no pre-given “single object” being multiplied—only a gradient of spatial integration. Third, evaluate whether the absence of causal-functional integration undermines restricted-composition accounts that depend on it, and whether accepting vague composition forces one toward universalism after all.

Present the response in three labeled sections: (1) The exam question with explicit sub-parts; (2) A model answer of about 600 words; (3) A marking scheme of about 250 words that separates an A from a B answer by naming specific required moves—for instance, correctly distinguishing ontological vagueness from epistemic vagueness, avoiding conflation with Ship of Theseus or standard artifacts, explaining why local collision-avoidance fails to constitute the kind of integration restricted views require, and addressing whether vagueness in boundaries infects the ontology or merely our descriptions. List what each missing move costs. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or author names; the answer should be graded on conceptual precision alone. Keep the total response under 1,000 words.
