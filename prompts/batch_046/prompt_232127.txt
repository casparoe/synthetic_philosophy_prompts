I'd like an audit of whether the following four commitments form a consistent package. (1) I take the grue paradox to show that induction requires prior constraints on projectible predicates, but I regard these constraints as purely pragmatic and conventional—there is no metaphysical fact of the matter whether emeralds are green or grue. (2) I think Hume's problem is solved not by deduction but by practical rationality: inductive projection is indispensable for any agent with stable preferences over time, since without regularity-expectation intentional action collapses. (3) Despite that pragmatism about induction, I am a realist about natural kinds—chemical and biological kinds are mind-independent, and the success of science is explained by its tracking these real joints rather than by convention. (4) When selecting inductive policies, I reject Bayesian expected-accuracy maximization in favor of a robustness criterion that prioritizes avoiding catastrophic long-run error, accepting lower average predictive success if necessary. Are these jointly tenable? Order the tensions by severity, specify which pair is only apparently in conflict and why it dissolves, and identify the single cheapest revision—stating which commitment it modifies—that would restore full consistency. Do not assume I prefer to keep any one of them.
