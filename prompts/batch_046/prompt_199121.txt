I am preparing an argument about whether people can overcome "natural" limitations through effort, and I am confused by what my biology teacher said about genes and environment. She explained that for some traits—like artistic skill—scientists sometimes calculate that about 60 percent of the differences between people in a given population come from genetic differences rather than from upbringing or practice. But I am not sure what that number actually means for an individual. Consider three cases: my sister and I share parents, yet she shows no artistic ability despite our mother being a painter, while I improved dramatically through years of practice; my cousin was adopted into a family of professional musicians but still struggles with rhythm, whereas his biological brother, raised in a different household, plays piano professionally; and my running coach insists my sprinting speed is "mostly genetic" because my father was fast, even though I train daily and he never did. Does that 60 percent figure mean that 60 percent of my own drawing ability is fixed in my DNA and only 40 percent can come from practice? If that statistic is calculated across a whole population living in one particular environment, can it justify claims about my personal potential, or about whether my improvement was genuinely caused by training rather than by genes I share with my relatives? And if every child grew up with identical art lessons and resources, would that 60 percent figure itself shift, and should that change how we interpret the boundary between innate and acquired ability?
