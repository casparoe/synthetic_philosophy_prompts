Write the desiderata that any philosophically adequate and technically operational distinction between tool AI and agent AI must satisfy, treating the distinction not as a behavioral taxonomy but as a conceptual framework meant to guide design, regulation, and moral assessment. For each requirement, specify the motivating case or technical result behind it: for instance, architectures that exhibit persistent goal-directed behavior across contexts without autonomous ends-setting; systems that generate novel sub-goals in open-ended environments versus those whose every inference step remains embedded within a user-specified objective function; and cases where corrigibility—genuine revisability of objectives—must be distinguished from mere interruptibility. Identify the distinctions the theory must preserve without collapse: between instrumental reasoning and ends-setting, between causal contribution to an outcome and moral responsibility for it, between delegated task execution and autonomous commitment. State which desiderata are non-negotiable—such as consistency with the fact that current large-scale language models lack the kind of persistent internal state and world-modeling continuity that standard accounts of agency require—and which are camp-specific preferences, such as demands for phenomenal consciousness, libertarian free will, or biological embodiment. Then flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable, particularly any tension between requiring that an agent AI possess genuinely open-ended goal formation and requiring that it remain fully corrigible or value-aligned by design in all possible worlds of its operation. Briefly indicate how three competing framings—the stochastic-parrot framing, the autonomous-system framing, and a hybrid delegated-agency framing—fare against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves. The deliverable is the specification: what the distinction must get right before it can be applied.
