I've been asked to help revive a small community archive my grandmother started—collecting oral histories from our neighborhood—but there's no funding stream, no institutional backing, and no proof that anyone beyond our family will actually use it. My relatives say I need to genuinely believe the project is worth saving, not just hope for it, because that conviction is what will carry me through the unpaid years of work; they also say it's wrong to demand "proof" for something this meaningful before committing. I worry, though, that deciding to believe it's viable just because I want it to be true, and because my commitment might help make it succeed, is a kind of self-deception that ignores real risks. Is there a principled difference between hoping a project succeeds and deciding to believe it will? And if my belief actually shapes whether I persist, does that make it rational to hold despite lacking sufficient evidence?
