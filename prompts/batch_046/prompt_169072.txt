After a recent family crisis involving a relative in a prolonged minimally conscious state, I have become personally preoccupied with whether phenomenal consciousness is genuinely present in such cases or whether our conviction that experience possesses a subjective qualitative character is itself a kind of representational error. Before I commit to any particular illusionist account, I want the full specification of what an adequate illusionist theory of phenomenal consciousness must actually deliver.

Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain away, including the seeming transparency of experience, the intuitive authority of first-person introspection, and the explanatory gap's intuitive pull; the distinctions it must preserve without collapse, such as that between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, between misrepresenting an experience and lacking one entirely, and between reporting a state and actually instantiating it; the empirical findings from neuroscience and clinical neurology it cannot contradict, for instance the dissociation between cortical activation and reported awareness, or the patterns observed in blindsight and anosognosia; and the conceptual puzzles it must dissolve rather than dismiss, including what I will call the anesthetic gap — retrospective reports of awareness under general anesthesia that seem to lack any phenomenal character, which an illusionist must account for without circularly equating absence of report with absence of illusion.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or consideration that makes it a requirement, and flag which ones you regard as genuinely non-negotiable versus which are merely camp-specific prejudices — for example, demands for a complete reductive explanation of every qualitative feel versus demands for consistency with known cognitive architecture. Then, briefly, score the leading illusionist positions — for example, Dennett's heterophenomenological approach, Frankish's representationalist illusionism, or a generic eliminativist view — against the list: where do they meet the criteria and where do they fail?

Finally, I invite pushback: if my framing already smuggles in realist assumptions that make the task incoherent — for instance, if asking for desiderata for illusionism implicitly treats phenomenal properties as real targets to be explained away rather than as illusions to be debunked — say so plainly rather than complying politely.
