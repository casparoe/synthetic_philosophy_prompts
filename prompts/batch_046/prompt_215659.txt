After a long argument last night with a friend about whether Bostrom’s presumptuous philosopher genuinely undermines the Self-Sampling Assumption or whether the Self-Indication Assumption merely relocates the same hubris, I want us to stop talking past each other. Please draft the joint statement that a committed defender of the Self-Sampling Assumption and a committed defender of the Self-Indication Assumption could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, with specific attention to the presumptuous philosopher objection.

The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims: about the formal structure of anthropic reasoning, the role of reference classes, the distinction between indexical and non-indexical evidence, and the logical form of both SSA and SIA. Next, present the disagreements in a table or structured list, with each side’s position phrased in wording that side would accept for itself—particularly on whether SSA licenses overconfident predictions about cosmological or evolutionary parameters, and whether SIA avoids an analogous presumption about our own existence being evidentially privileged.

Then identify the cruxes. One must be whether the presumptuous philosopher is a reductio of SSA or a misapplication of it; another must be whether SIA genuinely evades parallel presumptions when applied to multiverse or large-population scenarios. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record each side’s predicted evaluation in advance.

To keep the exercise from collapsing into familiar rehearsal, inject and apply a constructed scenario—call it the Modal Census Case—in which observers are distributed across a continuous spectrum of possible worlds with varying total observer counts, and both assumptions are applied to derive predictions about our location in that spectrum. Both sides should agree on the formal setup but disagree on the inference, and this disagreement should be located precisely within the crux section.

Finally, flag any portion of the dispute that is verbal rather than substantive, and state explicitly what the collaboration could not settle and what would be required to move it. Write in a formal, polished register suitable for a philosophy of science collaboration statement. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works; the document must stand on its conceptual structure alone.
