For a consistency audit in my philosophy of language seminar, I want to lay out my current working position on Wittgenstein and ask whether the commitments actually cohere.

(1) I endorse a family-resemblance account of conceptual unity: categories such as game or language are held together not by an essential feature but by a network of overlapping similarities, and I take this structure to apply horizontally across the members of a category.

(2) I read the private-language argument as establishing that a speaker cannot fix criteria of correctness for purely internal sensations; without some external, publicly accessible check, the distinction between genuinely correct application and mere belief in correctness collapses.

(3) I maintain that language-games are strongly incommensurable: the grammar and internal standards of one practice do not carry over into another without distortion, so that moral discourse and empirical science, for instance, operate with autonomous norms.

(4) I apply family resemblance reflexively and, I think, vertically: the concept of a language-game itself lacks necessary and sufficient conditions, which means the boundaries between games are porous rather than sharply drawn.

(5) I hold that meaning is constituted by use, but I deny that normative correctness within a practice reduces to the contingent agreement of the community; there can be objectively incorrect uses that transcend majority opinion, provided only that the criteria for error remain publicly accessible.

Do these five form a stable package, or am I implicitly relying on incompatible motivations? Specifically: is there a real tension between (2) and (5), or does the demand for public criteria in (2) leave room for the non-reductive objectivity I want in (5)? More seriously, can (3)'s claim of strong incommensurability survive (4)'s admission that language-games have no sharp boundaries—if the borders are porous, what licenses the claim that translation between games necessarily distorts? And does extending family resemblance vertically in (4) undermine the horizontal autonomy asserted in (1) and (3)?

Please rank the genuine tensions by severity, distinguish them from tensions that dissolve on inspection, and for each genuine conflict specify what I would have to surrender. Then tell me which single revision to my set would resolve the most problems at the least philosophical cost. I am not looking for a general exposition of Wittgenstein; I need a targeted audit of this specific profile.
