You chair the Joint Review Panel for Synthetic Biology and Philosophy of Science at your university. A proposal has arrived for a three-year, £2.4 million project entitled 'Typogenetic Closure in Artificial Genomes: From Gödelian Self-Reference to Replicative Semantics.' The applicants — a molecular biologist and a philosopher of logic — claim to have engineered an artificial genome that encodes not only its own replication machinery (as natural DNA does), but also a formal representation of that encoding within its own symbolic structure. They describe this as achieving 'full typogenetic closure,' a measurable state defined by a 'closure coefficient' that quantifies the extent to which a replicator's description is causally internal to its own replication chain. Once this coefficient exceeds a critical threshold, they argue, the system acquires 'semantic autonomy' — a minimal, non-conscious form of self-interpretation that distinguishes it from ordinary chemical autocatalysis and places it in an ethical category comparable, in certain respects, to that of sentient organisms.

Half the panel treats this as an overinterpretation of formal analogy: DNA replicates, formal systems prove theorems, but conflating the two is a category error that adds nothing to biosafety assessment. The other half accepts that the structural isomorphism between genetic replication and Gödelian self-reference reveals something genuinely deep about self-reference in material systems, and that engineered closure might blur the boundary between mechanism and agency in ways that existing regulatory frameworks fail to capture. The researchers have requested unconditional approval and exemption from the standard synthetic-organism containment tier, on the grounds that their system is purely informational and poses no pathogenic risk.

Draft the panel's formal decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — addressed to the Pro-Vice-Chancellor for Research. The memo should state a clear, actionable recommendation; explain how the philosophical issue (the boundary between formal self-reference and biological agency) bears on the practical decision; give an honest account of the strongest consideration favouring the position you reject; and specify the empirical or conceptual milestone that, should it be reached during the project, would oblige the panel to reconvene. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
