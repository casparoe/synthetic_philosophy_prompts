After a long argument with a friend about whether policy causation is fundamentally counterfactual dependence or interventionist manipulability, I need a practical resolution for my actual work. I evaluate proposals for a regional health board: every month I must sign off on whether an implemented program caused an observed outcome, often where jurisdictions overlap, behaviors shift in anticipation, or neighboring districts copy measures informally. Please construct a reusable thirty-minute decision protocol that translates both frameworks into ordered operational steps for a practitioner without philosophical training. Specify the sequence of factual questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, the precise point where it stops and returns “causal claim not warranted,” the conditions under which counterfactual and interventionist analyses diverge and must be flagged rather than merged, and the exact points where value judgment—not fact—requires board sign-off. Apply the procedure to three cases: a municipal flavored-tobacco ban enacted simultaneously with a state tax increase; a district school-breakfast program that neighboring districts informally replicated; and a telemedicine-triage pilot where emergency-room visits dropped before full technology deployment. At least one case should yield an unwelcome result—forced causal indeterminacy or a “no”—and you should state clearly what the procedure cannot settle.
