Design a classroom activity for a graduate philosophy seminar of approximately twelve students lasting ninety minutes that makes the force of pragmatic encroachment and epistemic contextualism felt rather than merely discussed, using a scenario invented specifically for this session so that participants cannot rely on familiar textbook examples. Create a fictional institutional review board called the Meridian Data Oversight Committee, which must decide whether a junior researcher named Dr. Chen knows that a clinical dataset contains a subtle covariate confound; keep the evidential presentation identical across three successive rounds, but vary the practical stakes by distributing hidden role cards that change the advisory context from an internal technical memo with no external consequences to a public-health recommendation carrying institutional liability and potential regulatory action. Divide the group into board members who must vote and justify whether Chen knows the confound exists, and silent observers who record every justification without commenting; run the rounds without revealing that the evidence has not changed, then expose the constant-evidence structure and ask the observers to read back the justifications so the group can see that knowledge attributions shifted solely with practical stakes. The debrief should map each round onto the pragmatic encroachment claim that knowledge conditions depend on practical interests and onto the contextualist claim that the truth conditions of knowledge ascriptions vary with context, should identify which participants resisted the shift and whether their resistance rested on invariantist intuitions or on confusion between evidential standards and knowledge conditions, should predict that at least some students will conflate rising stakes with rising evidence requirements rather than with shifting knowledge conditions and should advise the facilitator to separate these reactions without resolving them, and must be honest about which feature of the view—specifically the metaphysical instability of the knowledge relation itself, as opposed to mere variability in our assertive practices—the rules of the game cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply directly. Specify that participants need only general familiarity with knowledge ascription and should not be required to read specific texts in advance; do not include any bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; and present the entire design in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though composing a single letter describing a runnable exercise.
