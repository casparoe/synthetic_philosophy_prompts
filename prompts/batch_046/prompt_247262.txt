In my philosophy of language seminar this term we have been tracing the Frege-Geach problem for moral expressivism from Geach's original observation that moral sentences seem to lose their characteristic force when embedded in conditionals, through Blackburn's commitment-theoretic response and Gibbard's plan-based expressivism, to the more recent hybrid strategies that borrow propositional machinery without conceding cognitivism. What strikes me is that despite half a century of refinement—expressivists repeatedly specifying what mental state or practical attitude gets expressed, cognitivists repeatedly insisting that embedding requires shared truth-conditional content—the debate has not moved. The same objections recur, the same distinctions are redrawn, and nobody seems to change position.

My working theory is that the stall is not about moral psychology or compositional semantics at all, but about a hidden disagreement over whether embedding requires the embedded clause to carry a uniform mode of presentation across descriptive and normative contexts—something like applying Fregean sense-reference machinery inconsistently to discourse that was never designed to bear it. But I am unsure whether this diagnosis is right. Could the disagreement instead be purely verbal, with "meaning" doing different work for each side—one tracking linguistic competence, the other tracking metaphysical grounding? Or is there a substantive formal question about compositionality for non-truth-conditional languages that both sides gesture at without resolving? I want an honest diagnosis supported by what each camp actually says: do they differ about facts, about standards of explanation, about which cases count as data, or about bedrock intuitions regarding whether moral language describes anything? And given that diagnosis, is there anything that could actually break the impasse—a precise conceptual distinction between embedding and entailment, a formal result on how attitudes compose in complex sentences, or should we expect permanent stalemate because the dispute tracks incompatible starting points that no argument can reach?
