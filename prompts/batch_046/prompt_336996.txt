I spent thirty years in experimental physics before turning, in retirement, to philosophy of science. I am drafting a proposal for a one-semester independent study—approximately six thousand words, with a clear endpoint—and I would like help sharpening its architecture. My subject is the longstanding dispute over whether DNA is genuinely a code or only metaphorically described as one. The familiar debate divides into literalists, who treat the genome as carrying semantic content that is decoded by cellular machinery, and deflationists, who regard “code” as a useful heuristic with no ontological commitment. From my laboratory perspective, however, both camps overlook a structural feature that is visible in every molecular biology textbook but rarely addressed in philosophical terms: the interpreting apparatus—the ribosome, the tRNA synthetases, and the mapping rules of the genetic code itself—is almost entirely specified by the very nucleic acid sequences it interprets. This creates a recursive, self-bootstrapping loop in which message and interpreter are co-constituted, a configuration absent from both human languages and conventional computer codes, where encoder and decoder are typically independent of the message content.

My proposal asks whether this circular dependency establishes that DNA belongs to a distinct ontological category—perhaps an autopoietic information system in which material interpretation and interpreted message are inseparable—or whether it instead demonstrates that the code analogy was always inappropriate at the level of ontology, leaving only a causal-informational description. More precisely, I want to determine whether recognizing this recursion allows us to preserve the explanatory successes of molecular biology—predicting translation errors, engineering synthetic genomes, accounting for fidelity—without committing to semantic realism about nucleic acids, or whether the recursion forces us to abandon the code framework entirely in favor of a purely mechanistic account.

For the study, I propose a conceptual analysis rather than empirical research. I will map the logical structure of the DNA-translation loop, compare it with two carefully restricted analogues—self-modifying computer programs and autopoietic theories in biology—and assess whether the explanatory practices of geneticists implicitly rely on semantic notions such as misreading, fidelity, and meaning that cannot be fully reduced to causal dynamics. The deliverable will argue for one of three outcomes: first, that the recursion defines a new, non-semantic category of biological information; second, that the recursion undermines the code analogy but can be replaced by a mechanistic account retaining all predictive power; or third, that the recursion reveals semantic notions to be ineliminable, though they apply to the system as a whole rather than to DNA in isolation.

The project could fail if the conceptual distinction between semantic and causal-informational descriptions collapses upon analysis—if, for instance, every successful prediction in molecular genetics can be translated without loss into a purely physical vocabulary, rendering the philosophical framing redundant. It could also fail if the scope expands uncontrollably: comparing DNA recursion with general theories of self-reference or with the full literature on biological autonomy might exceed a single semester. To contain this risk, I will restrict analogical comparison to the two cases mentioned and will not attempt a comprehensive survey of the philosophy of information.

Please write this as a formal two-page proposal. State the question with enough precision that a philosopher of science could see, at the end, whether I have answered it. Explain why the literalist-deflationist stalemate leaves the recursion problem untouched. Outline the method in monthly stages: the first month mapping the logical structure of the translation loop; the second comparing it with the restricted analogues; the third evaluating whether explanatory practices require semantic commitment; the final month drafting the argument and testing it against the three outcome conditions. Conclude with an honest assessment of the risks—particularly that a retired physicist may lack fluency in recent philosophy of biology—and specify what would count as a genuinely negative result rather than mere confusion. Do not include a bibliography or reading list; I will assemble references myself.
