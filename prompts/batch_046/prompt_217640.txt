I'm preparing a long-form interview for my science-and-ideas column, and next week's subject is an unnamed political philosopher who defends a position she calls normative-institutional bifurcation within the debate over repugnant markets. She holds that the standard corrosive-commodification framework fails to distinguish goods whose social value is constituted by non-market norms from goods that are merely distributed through non-market mechanisms by convention. On her view, regulated kidney markets are permissible because renal tissue lacks constitutive social meaning, whereas vote-selling and crisis-price-gouging must remain prohibited because democratic equality and emergency solidarity are undermined by pricing regardless of institutional design. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press on the actual architecture of her view rather than biography or generic ethics. I want at least one question on how she draws the boundary between tissue goods and democratic goods without smuggling in prior moral intuitions; one on whether her framework permits partial commodification—such as compensating organ donors without allowing direct purchase—and how that interacts with her claim about constitutive norms; and one on the empirical assumption that pricing necessarily erodes solidarity during disasters rather than merely signaling scarcity. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give: that institutional design can insulate norms from market logic. Also include at least two questions that inject a less-discussed tension: specifically, ask whether her bifurcation survives if democratic participation is partly constituted by economic bargaining, as some public-choice models suggest, and whether a hypothetical auction-based organ allocation with equity weighting would violate her conditions or merely implement them differently. Keep the tone rigorous but accessible to an educated general audience, and do not ask for citations, reading lists, or bibliographic references.
