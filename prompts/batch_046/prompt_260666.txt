Write a villanelle of exactly nineteen lines following the traditional rhyme scheme and refrain structure (A1 b A2; a b A1; a b A2; a b A1; a b A2; a b A1 A2). Take as your subject David Benatar's procreation asymmetry—the view that there is a strong moral reason against creating beings who will suffer, but no symmetrical reason in favor of creating beings who will be happy. Assign one refrain to the anti-suffering pole and the other to the absence of a pro-happiness pole. Let the turning repetition of the form enact the way the two poles keep circling without resolving into a balance, and let the final couplet, where both refrains appear together, stage structurally why they do not cancel each other out. Within the poem, incorporate the non-identity problem precisely enough to show that the asymmetry survives even when we grant that not bringing a particular happy person into existence harms no one, because there is no actual subject there to suffer deprivation. Write in American English. The poem should be philosophically accurate enough that a specialist in population ethics recognizes both Benatar's asymmetry and the person-affecting challenge raised by Parfit, and it should still stand as poetry for a reader who knows neither literature. Do not provide citations, a bibliography, or references to specific page numbers; render the ideas through the verse itself.
