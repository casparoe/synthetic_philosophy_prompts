Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for a seminar on rationality and forecasting that sorts positions on the interaction between inside-view reasoning and outside-view reference-class prediction, with epistemic modesty as the governing constraint. Each branch must be a concrete diagnostic case or claim answerable without knowing philosophical labels—such as whether an agent facing a 90% base-rate failure in peer-reviewed analogies to her own causal model should still privilege her model, or whether awareness of past overconfidence in that domain should globally suppress confidence. Build the tree from logical space rather than from a list of names: first split on whether the agent includes her own reasoning process within the reference class; then on whether conflicts are resolved lexically or by domain-weighted calibration; then on whether modesty demands uniform downgrading across domains or only local adjustment. The tree must locate these four leaves: pure outside-view forecasting (reference class alone determines belief), rationalist inside-view privilege with local exception clauses, domain-specific calibrationism, and the recursive-modesty position that applies reference-class reasoning to the choice of reference class itself. For any logically possible combination that remains unoccupied—particularly a view granting unconditional inside-view lexical priority together with global epistemic modesty about one's reasoning—note the combination and briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored. Conclude by flagging any pair of leaves that the tree's diagnostics cannot cleanly separate without smuggling in contested psychological premises.
