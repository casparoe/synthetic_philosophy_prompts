I hold a position that runs against the standard interpretation of the Gibbard-Satterthwaite result. The theorem establishes that, for any collective choice rule with three or more alternatives that is neither dictatorial nor restricted to a narrow preference domain, strategic misrepresentation is unavoidable. Most commentators treat this as a design problem to be solved through domain restrictions, verification mechanisms, or approximate strategy-proofness. I take the opposite view: in large, heterogeneous electorates, strategic vulnerability is not a defect but an endogenous preference-intensity filter. Because misrepresentation carries cognitive and reputational costs, only voters with sufficiently intense preferences will bear them; consequently, strategic behaviour reveals salience-weighted preferences in a way that sincere voting under practically implementable restricted domains does not. I further maintain that attempts to engineer full strategy-proofness — for instance through certain scoring protocols with preference-verification — are normatively undesirable, since they suppress this informational function and tend to privilege well-informed, low-cost strategic actors anyway.

What would have to be true for this view to fail? I want the evidence, arguments, and formal results that should move me, sorted by how decisive they are. In particular: which empirical findings or theoretical results show that strategic voting tracks sophistication or resources rather than preference intensity; whether there are convergence theorems or equilibrium analyses showing that strategic behaviour in large electorates produces cycling or welfare-inferior outcomes rather than salience-weighted aggregation; and whether any practical mechanism actually achieves both intensity revelation and protection against manipulation by high-resource actors. I also want an honest assessment of whether my position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — for example, by defining any failure of intensity-filtering as a misapplication rather than a refutation — and whether such insulation is a problem for a view of this kind. Please distinguish what a committed defender of this position should concede as genuinely damaging from what they could reasonably dismiss, and flag any defeaters that are merely awkward rather than fatal.
