In a seminar on strange loops and explanatory levels, a fellow student kept returning to the comparison between scientific explanation and the structure implied by Escher's Drawing Hands: each hand draws the other into existence, yet we instinctively ask which one is "really" the base. She applied this to argue that reductionist explanation always needs an inviolate level—say, fundamental physics—that explains upward without being explained downward, and that any apparent circularity (psychology informing neuroscience, or observation being theory-laden) is merely a temporary tangle that resolves once we reach the true foundation. I want to audit this analogy carefully, because it seems to do heavy lifting in her defense of a strict hierarchical reductionism, and I am not convinced the image survives contact with the actual structure of scientific practice.

Give me the explicit correspondence: what in the scientific case plays the role of the left hand, the right hand, the paper they draw upon, the act of drawing itself, and the viewer who assumes one must come first? Then identify precisely where the mapping fractures—whether the "paper" (shared empirical data or conceptual framework) is itself drawn by both hands simultaneously, whether the "drawing" is best understood as a temporal process of discovery or as a logical relation of dependence, and whether cases like quantum mechanics reshaping our concept of "particle" or the pervasive theory-ladenness of observation constitute genuine tangled hierarchies rather than illusions that dissolve at the bottom. Does the analogy quietly import the assumption that explanation must be unidirectional and that circular dependence is always vicious rather than virtuous? Finally, assess whether her conclusion—that there must be an inviolate level untouched by the levels above it—survives once these disanalogies are on the table, and if it does not, suggest whether a different image (a web with anchored nodes, a feedback loop with an external constraint, or something else) better captures explanatory relations that permit interdependence without collapsing into pure circularity, or whether the very notion of an inviolate level should be abandoned altogether.
