Take the Bristol Colston statue toppling in 2020 and the weird aftermath where they fished the broken thing out and put it in a museum display with the graffiti still on. Analyze the whole mess through Nietzche's three modes of history from that second Untimely Meditation — monumental, antiquarian, critical. Map each mode onto an actual group or stance: who was being monumental about the past, who was being antiquarian about the physical object regardless of what it meant, and whether anyone was actually doing critical history in Nietzche's specific sense rather than just weaponizing the past. There's a tension people kept pointing out — activists were accused of erasing history, but maybe they were the ones practicing critical history, while the museum curators preserving the damaged statue were being antiquarian. Is that mapping right? Or does critical history require that suprahistorical capacity to forget and live outside history, which a collective protest probably can't do? And what does the case show about whether critical history is even possible as a group act, or if Nietzche meant it as an individual, almost artistic thing? Don't just list the three modes — I want precise mapping of who faced what, and an honest note on where the theory goes silent or breaks.
