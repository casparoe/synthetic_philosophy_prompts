Here is a profile of my commitments on norms of assertion, and I want to know whether it holds together or collapses into contradiction. I hold that (1) the knowledge norm is correct in its core form: one ought not assert p unless one knows p. I also hold that (2) in scientific practice it is not merely permissible but often required to assert hypotheses and preliminary results that fall well short of knowledge, since falsification depends on putting claims at risk. Additionally, (3) personal belief is neither necessary nor sufficient for appropriate assertion; what governs permissibility is the speaker’s institutional or disciplinary role and the listener’s warranted entitlement to rely. On the classic lottery case, I hold that (4) it is misdiagnosed: the problem with saying “I will lose” is not a missing knowledge state but a pragmatic asymmetry between statistical generalization and singular prediction that violates a conversational maxim of salience. Finally, (5) I accept a distributed-knowledge view: a researcher can legitimately assert a finding she has not personally verified if it is warranted by her group’s validated protocols, and in such cases she genuinely knows it despite lacking direct belief-forming access.
