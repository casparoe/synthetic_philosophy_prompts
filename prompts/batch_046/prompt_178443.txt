I am preparing a research proposal on what I have tentatively called Nietzsche's physiological hermeneutics: the claim that his scattered remarks on diet, digestion, climate, and daily regimen are not biographical curiosities but constitute a systematic interpretation of bodily states as value-judgments that underpin his broader philosophical project. I am particularly struck by the tension between his ideal of great health (große Gesundheit), which he frames as a future-oriented, self-overcoming condition, and the highly specific, almost medicalized self-experiments he reports—avoiding alcohol and tea, preferring light southern cuisine, and attributing digestive failure to philosophical sterility. Before committing several years to this, I need to determine which open interpretive and conceptual questions in this space genuinely deserve priority.

I see three competing lines of inquiry. First, a historical reconstruction of Nietzsche's engagement with nineteenth-century physiology and dietetics, asking whether his prescriptions derive from identifiable medical sources and whether recognizing those sources alters our reading of his critique of asceticism. Second, a conceptual analysis of coherence: does the simultaneous advocacy for harsh self-discipline and for gentle, nourishing regimen undermine his health-ideal, or does the concept of great health resolve the tension by redefining health away from mere comfort? Third, an interdisciplinary exploration of normative implications for contemporary philosophy of mind and ethics—specifically, whether his somatic philosophy offers a genuine precursor to embodied cognition theories, or whether applying it to modern wellness culture and biometric self-tracking is merely anachronistic projection.

Please rank these three lines of work and defend the ordering. The defense should turn on criteria appropriate to a philosopher without clinical training and with roughly three years of focused effort: tractability, upstream importance for understanding Nietzsche's ethics and epistemology, and the likelihood that resolving the question would change how scholars interpret his later writings. I am not asking for a bibliography, a survey of secondary literature, or citation lists; I want a defended agenda that tells me which question to build around, which to treat as secondary, and which to set aside as either too antiquarian or too speculative. Conclude with a one-line verdict in the form \answer{...} identifying your top-ranked priority.
