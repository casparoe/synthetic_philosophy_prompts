I have been arguing with a close friend about Daniel Dennett’s claim that the self is not a metaphysical substance but a center of narrative gravity — a fictional point around which the stories of a life organize themselves. My friend insists that coherence and social recognition are sufficient: wherever a unified first-person narrative pulls together memories, intentions, and social attributions, that is where the self is located, regardless of internal experience. I am skeptical, because it seems to me that this view quietly treats authorship as disposable, and I want to see whether the metaphor can survive a case in which authorship is explicitly relocated outside the biological subject. Please construct a thought experiment engineered to isolate exactly this tension.

Design a realistic, near-future scenario — no teleporters, no brain scans of impossible precision — involving an adult human, Mira, who relies on an external cognitive system that continuously reconstructs her autobiographical narrative in real time. The system has access to her episodic memories and behavioral data, and it generates the unified, first-personal life story that Mira, her family, and her colleagues all treat as “her story.” The narrative is causally integrated with her actions, emotionally resonant, and socially recognized as authentic, yet Mira herself reports no sense of ownership over its arc; she describes her own experience as “watching a protagonist being written by someone else.” Keep the scenario to about one paragraph and hold everything constant except the locus of narrative generation.

Then run the check: state the belief or attribution in question (that Mira is the self, or that the system is, or that they form a distributed center), specify the method of narrative integration being used, identify the relevant nearby worlds in which the system is shut down and Mira resumes generating her own story without discontinuity, and explain why the center-of-narrative-gravity framework gives the verdict it does in each case — whether coherence, social recognition, or causal integration dominates. Finish by identifying which feature of the case actually does the philosophical work, what happens to the argument if that feature is removed, and how a defender of the pure coherence view would attack the scenario. I want to know whether the analogy of gravity still pulls toward Mira once the generating force is demonstrably outside her, or whether the metaphor collapses into something that requires an internal source after all.
