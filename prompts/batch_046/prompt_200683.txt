I advise the evaluation board of a foundation that funds ten-year, high-uncertainty basic-research programs. We need a reusable decision procedure — not a one-off opinion — for assessing any proposal that asserts a quantitative confidence level: specifically, whether that confidence should stand as an inside-view forecast (privileged by domain-specific reasoning) or be corrected by an outside-view base rate, and how to apply epistemic modesty without triggering an infinite regress of meta-level uncertainty. Write the procedure as an ordered checklist with explicit questions, criteria for a sufficient answer at each stage, stopping rules that mandate a downward adjustment, and clear flags for where the decision becomes normative rather than epistemic. The procedure must address the reference-class problem — when the category used for the outside view is itself contested — and detect cases where the inside view has already implicitly incorporated the outside-view data, risking double-counting. Apply it to two proposals: (A) a team claiming an 80% probability that a novel superconducting-qubit architecture will scale within eight years, based on first-principles physical modeling; (B) a team claiming a 60% probability that a stratospheric aerosol intervention will meet its temperature targets, citing analogies to three prior field trials. Include one case where the procedure yields a result that conflicts with the reviewers' initial intuition, and state explicitly what structural limitations prevent the procedure from resolving the decision alone.
