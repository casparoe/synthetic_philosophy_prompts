I've been convinced that moral trade isn't just a cute EA idea but actually the right way to think about cooperation across deep moral disagreement—like, if you think factory farming is wrong for rights-based reasons and I care about aggrigate suffering, us both supporting a subsidy for lab-grown meat isn't compromise, it's just us both getting more of what we value without betreying our framework. But I want to know what actually counts against this being a general solution. Not the obvious 'sometimes values are incommensurble' objection, but specifically: what kind of case would show that moral trade collapses into either hidden consequentialism or hidden deontology? Would a successful trade require that both parties share some meta-ethical commitment—like coherence of preferences or non-sadism—that makes the disagreement shallower than it looks? And is there a version of this that survives if one party thinks the other is not just wrong but morally corrupted in a way that cooperation is complicity? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to awkward, and I want you to be honest about whether this view has just been qualified into unfalsifiability by saying 'only trade when it's cheap for you'—and if that insulation is actually a problem for a view of this kind.
