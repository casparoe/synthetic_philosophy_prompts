I'm preparing an interview for a philosophy podcast with a scholar who has just published a monograph defending an unorthodox reading of Nāgārjuna's tetralemma (catuskoti) in the Madhyamaka tradition. Rather than treating the four corners—affirmation, negation, both, neither—as successive logical options eliminated in order, she argues they are four simultaneous modes of conceptual fixation that must be undermined together through what she terms recursive negation without reification. She holds that both the standard dialetheist interpretation, which permits true contradictions, and the non-assertoric interpretation, which treats Nāgārjuna's statements as purely therapeutic, miss the soteriological point: the tetralemma is not a propositional argument but a practice aimed at dissolving the reifying tendency of conceptual cognition itself. She applies this especially to the analysis of motion in the root text's second chapter, arguing that the familiar argument concerning the present moment and the moved or not-moved should not be read as a reductio of motion's reality but as an exposure of the four fixations that any concept of motion necessarily activates.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that avoid asking her merely to restate the view. For each area, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give: she will likely claim that prasaṅga remains too dialectically reactive, that self-refutation is avoided because the negation targets conceptual fixation rather than propositional content, that the motion-analysis exposes the four fixations in real-time cognition, and that the metaphysical and soteriological dimensions are inseparable. Keep the questions substantive, anticipate her standard replies, and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
