Construct a thought experiment that fuses the lottery paradox with the preface paradox inside a single, realistic institutional protocol. Design a one-paragraph scenario set in a hospital ethics committee that must evaluate a large batch of independently assessed patient-care recommendations. The committee is bound by two formal rules: the Lottery Rule, which prohibits acting on any individual recommendation whose assigned error probability exceeds a specified threshold, regardless of how many other recommendations pass; and the Preface Rule, which obliges the committee to record that, across the entire batch, at least one endorsed recommendation is probably flawed, without specifying which. Inject a novel aggregation mechanism: each committee member assigns every recommendation both a binary vote and a continuous epistemic weight between 0 and 1, and the collective decision is computed by multiplying—not averaging—the weights across all recommendations in the batch. Constraints: no exotic physics, no perfect predictors, realistic workplace dynamics, and the contradiction must emerge structurally from the interaction of the two rules under multiplicative aggregation rather than from an arithmetic error. After presenting the scenario, analyse it precisely: state the verdict the Lottery Rule yields for action on recommendation k, the verdict the Preface Rule yields for collective endorsement of the full batch, and why multiplicative aggregation renders these verdicts jointly unsatisfiable in a way that linear averaging would not. Identify which feature—the independence assumption, the fixed threshold, or the multiplicative rule—does the essential work; explain what happens if that feature is removed; and say how a defender of rational committee belief might resist the case by distinguishing acceptance from belief or by rejecting the aggregation method. Write your answer in British English.
