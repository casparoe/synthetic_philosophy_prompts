I'm putting together a taxomony for a metaphysic seminar on Leibniz's best-world claim, and I want a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually hold rather than letting them just call themselves Leibnizian. Start from whether the claim does real philosophical work or is just theological window dressing. Then branch on whether best is quantitative — net good, variety weighted by simplicity, that sort of thing — or qualitative, like perfect harmony or absence of privation. Then what constrains possibility: logical consistency alone, metaphysical compossibility with actual laws, or only worlds God could actually instantiate given divine nature. Then whether selection is external, God surveys options and picks, or internal, God's nature necessarily produces the optimum. I also want a niche split: does the view imply modal density, an infinite chain of worlds arbitrarily close in goodness, or a discrete global optimum? Each leaf gets a named position if anyone holds it, or unnamed — probably incoherent because... — if the combo is empty. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate, and identify one combination that sounds okay but violates the principle of sufficient reason. Work thorugh two or three exmaple paths for hypothetical students. Keep it around eight or nine nodes, no citations, no reading lists, no quotes from texts.
