I'm drafting operational guidelines for a clinical ethics mediation service that works with a diaspora population in which ubuntu-inspired relational conceptions of personhood routinely conflict with the hospital's default autonomy-based framework. In our team discussions, mediators keep collapsing relational personhood into a vague directive to defer to the family, missing the internal philosophical distinctions that should generate different procedural results. I need a reusable, thirty-minute decision procedure that a mediator without philosophical training can run during a family conference, translating the conceptual architecture of ubuntu and related relational theories into an ordered sequence of questions, evidence thresholds, and explicit judgment hand-offs. The procedure must distinguish thin relational embeddedness—the uncontroversial claim that every patient is socially situated and that family perspectives carry informational and emotional weight—from thick relational constitution, the stronger claim that in certain contexts the patient's identity and moral standing are partly constituted by communal bonds such that unilateral refusal can be incoherent or self-undermining within that framework.

Your task is to construct that flow: state the questions in sequence, define what counts as sufficient evidence that thick rather than thin relationality applies, identify the stop points where the mediator should recommend honoring individual refusal despite family objection, and identify the conditions—if any—under which collective decision-making authority may be endorsed without violating the framework's own constraints. Then apply it to two worked cases. Case one: an elderly patient with advancing dementia refuses dialysis that her adult children insist is necessary to preserve her role as family elder and spiritual anchor; her prior expressed values are ambiguous about life-extending measures but consistently emphasized communal harmony. Case two: a previously competent adult in a temporary psychotic crisis refuses a low-risk antipsychotic that would restore decision-making capacity; the refusal is driven by delusional beliefs that he is already disconnected from all human bonds and that medication would cement that isolation. For each case, show the procedure's path and final recommendation, then flag precisely where the procedure misfires, requires the mediator to invoke values external to the philosophical framework, or conflicts with legal informed-consent requirements. Conclude by stating explicitly what this procedure cannot settle—particularly regarding the weight of relational obligations when they appear to override statutory autonomy protections—and identify which of its steps is most vulnerable to cultural bias in application.
