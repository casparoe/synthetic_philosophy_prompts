Rank Dennett’s four creature-types—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—by their capacity to recover from a systematically misleading reward environment in which false beliefs about unobservable states are consistently reinforced and in which the creature has no access to social correction or linguistic feedback. Place each in a tier (e.g., highly robust, moderately robust, poorly robust, non-robust) and, in one sentence per tier, explain why that architecture succeeds or fails at internal error correction under those specific conditions. Focus on the representational and learning mechanisms distinctive to each kind rather than on general intelligence, and be explicit about whether any of the four lacks the representational depth to form the false beliefs in the first place.
