By this you mean a systematically maintained record of one's past instances of suspected cognitive bias, the corrective adjustments one made, and the actual outcomes, used to weight peer disagreement and self-doubt rather than treat them as flat defeaters. My opening move is this. Consider Dr. Elena Voss, a climate statistician with deep first-order expertise. She has independently derived that a particular model parameter should be 3.2. A peer review then suggests she is anchoring on her prior estimate of 3.0 and should revise downward. Voss consults her calibration ledger: in past cases where she ignored bias warnings in this domain, her first-order judgment was correct about 70 percent of the time; in cases where she corrected downward, she overcompensated and was wrong about 55 percent of the time. Given that her higher-order evidence now includes both the peer assessment and the ledger's asymmetric record, should she maintain her belief in 3.2 even though she judges that she probably suffers from anchoring?
