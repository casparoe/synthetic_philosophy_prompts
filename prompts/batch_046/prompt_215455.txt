In Quine's argument against the analytic-synthetic distinction, there is a deeper conceptual tension that needs clarification: does the web-of-belief framework genuinely dissolve that distinction, or does it only relocate it into a gradient of centrality and entrenchment? Explain this tension, and in particular address whether any non-circular criterion can distinguish revising a logical principle such as bivalence from revising a deeply held empirical generalization. To make the problem concrete, consider a hypothetical scientific community that treats bivalence as revisable in light of quantum-mechanical anomalies, retaining it solely as a pragmatic default with high abandonment costs. From within Quine's holism, can we coherently say this community has given up an analytic principle rather than merely updating an entrenched empirical belief? If no such distinction is available, does that mean the analytic-synthetic divide has been eliminated, or only transformed?
