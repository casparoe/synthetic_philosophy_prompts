I am preparing a position statement on Nietzsche's eternal recurrence and want an internal consistency audit before I finalize it. I hold the following commitments, not as a summary of Nietzsche scholarship but as my own working view:

(a) The doctrine should be treated as a cosmological hypothesis about the cyclic repetition of all events in strictly identical form, rather than reduced to a psychological exercise in life-affirmation.

(b) Its central philosophical function is ethical: it operates as a decision criterion analogous to universalizability—one ought to act only in ways one could will to recur eternally.

(c) The value of human achievement and suffering depends partly on the axiological priority of the token over the type; the irreplaceability and singularity of particular lives and choices is constitutive of their worth, and infinite duplication in identical form would erode that worth.

(d) Suffering is not merely instrumental to greatness but partly constitutive of it, so that a complete affirmation of recurrence requires affirming the eternal return of the worst pains without reservation.

(e) Human freedom consists in retrospective narrative integration—the capacity to reinterpret one's fixed past actions into a coherent arc of self-overcoming—rather than in altering the events themselves.

(f) The doctrine is incompatible with any linear or teleological conception of history; there is no cumulative progress toward a redeemed or final state.

Which of these commitments genuinely conflict, which pairs only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and what is the least costly revision if I want to preserve the core of (a) through (d)? Please rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each exactly which commitment must be qualified or abandoned. I am not asking for an introduction to Nietzsche; I want to know whether this specific constellation hangs together.
