reading up on why people keep comparing every crisis to munich or vietnam. there's this pattern i can't name. someone takes a historical decision -- say appeasement in 38 -- and pulls out the lesson (dont appease dictators) but they leave behind all the contextual stuff that made appeasement rational then: the empire's overstretch, the public's anti-war mood, the lack of allies. then they use that stripped lesson as a weapon in today's debate. if you say but the context is different they accuse you of repeating chamberlain's mistake. its like the analogy isn't being used to understand the present, its being used to borrow moral authority from the past while ignoring the past's actual complexity. is there a specific concept for this? i know about false analogy and the thucydides trap but this feels different -- it's not just comparing apples to oranges, it's using the apple's reputation to sell an orange. does it have a name, or is it just sloppy history? and if not, how would you distinguish it from ordinary analogical reasoning?
