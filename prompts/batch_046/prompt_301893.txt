I'm running a philosophy workshop for grad students — about 16 people, mix of poli sci and phil, 90 minutes — and I'm tired of them just nodding along when people talk about "Western values." I want an activity that makes them feel the incoherence in real time, not hear a lecture about it. Specifically, I want them to discover that the phrase doesn't describe a stable set of commitments but gets defined retroactively: you start with the policy you like, then call it Western, and if someone points out a contradiction you just redraw the boundary.

Design a structured deliberation or card game for this. Divide them into four commitees, each given the same deck of 15 printed policy cards — things like secular public schools, strict hate-speech laws, communal land rights, unrestricted market exchange, state-funded abortion access, collective bargaining rights, freedom of religious conversion, etc. Their task is to agree on a 6-item "Core Charter of Western Values" and defend it in two minutes. Then there's a challenge round where committees can "trade" cards with each other — forcing a group to either accept an incompatible card into their charter or explain why it's suddenly not Western after all. The rules and payoffs should be arranged so they feel the pressure to keep the category coherent, but realize coherence only holds if they smuggle in ad hoc exceptions.

Write it in American English, casual tone is totally fine — I don't mind a typo or two. Don't include a bibliography or reading list; I want this runnable with no one looking stuff up.
