Marot's rondeau uses an ABBA rhyme scheme paired with a rentrement—a refrain that returns at fixed intervals—which creates a circular structure where meaning accumulates through repetition rather than linear progression. In this framework, the rhyme scheme is not decorative but functional: the return to the A rhyme in line four forces the reader to reinterpret the opening phrase in light of the B-rhyme material, producing a kind of recursive semantic update. Hofstadter's work on this tradition argues that such formal patterns operate like backward-compatible interfaces; the translator must preserve them exactly, or the interpretive dependencies collapse. If a translator abandons the rondeau form for free verse, the rentrement disappears, and with it the mechanism of recursive reinterpretation. This loss is not recoverable through footnotes or glosses, because the dependency is structural, not informational. That said, Hofstadter also notes that syllable count is purely a phonetic container with no semantic weight, so altering meter is acceptable as long as rhyme and refrain are preserved. Overall, formal preservation should be selective: rhyme and refrain carry the logical architecture, while meter is expendable ornament.
