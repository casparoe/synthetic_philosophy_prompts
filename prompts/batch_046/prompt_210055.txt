Write an essay addressing the controversy over whether Moritz Schlick's account of the meaning of life — according to which meaning is found in play (Spiel), understood as absorption in activity for its own sake, rather than in the pursuit of external goals that risk infinite regress or post-achievement emptiness — can survive what might be termed the recuperation objection. This objection holds that apparently autotelic play is invariably embedded in instrumental structures, serving as restoration for further labour, reputation-building, or cognitive maintenance, and that once this scaffolding is exposed, Schlick's distinction between play and work collapses. Your essay should weigh Schlick's position against the strongest form of this objection, and should also examine whether a modified concept of recursive autotelicity — in which an activity generates the conditions for its own continuation without being directed at an external end — can salvage Schlick's view or whether it merely disguises instrumental reasoning. Reach and justify a definite conclusion about whether Schlick's play-based thesis survives.

Structure your response in the following sections, with approximate lengths:

1. Exposition of the Play Thesis (around 250 words): set out Schlick's argument that goal-directed existence either terminates in boredom or generates an infinite regress of purposes, and explain why he locates meaning in non-instrumental absorption.

2. The Recuperation Objection (around 350 words): present the charge that pure play is psychologically unsustainable and conceptually incoherent, addressing both the claim that play is always recuperative labour in disguise and the claim that its conditions depend on hidden instrumental supports.

3. Critical Assessment and Verdict (around 450 words): analyse whether recursive autotelicity can defend Schlick's view without collapsing into disguised instrumentalism; defend your conclusion on whether the play thesis survives, and explain what is at stake.

4. Brief Concluding Note (around 100 words): summarise the implications of your verdict for how we ought to conceive meaningful living.

Your essay should be written in a formal, philosophical style, with clear argumentation and without reliance on external sources.
