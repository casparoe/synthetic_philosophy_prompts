Write an essay taking and justifying a clear position on whether the Enlightenment is best understood as an emancipatory project of universal reason or as an ideological precursor to bureaucratic domination and colonial administration. Rather than cataloguing achievements and crimes separately, examine whether the Enlightenment's normative commitment to universal human dignity was structurally compatible with its concurrent development of classificatory sciences—natural history, political arithmetic, comparative anatomy—that treated human populations as measurable, reformable objects. Consider the argument that liberal rights discourse and biopolitical governance share a common conceptual root in the reconceptualization of nature and society as quantifiable and administrable. Conclude with a verdict: was the tension between liberation and discipline a contingent historical corruption, or an internal contradiction of Enlightenment rationality itself? State your confidence explicitly, and flag where your reasoning relies on contested interpretations of eighteenth- and nineteenth-century intellectual history.
