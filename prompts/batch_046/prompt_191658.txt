I've just been roped onto our uni's international partnerships ethics commitee — nightmare timing — because we're deciding whether to keep funding our joint research centre with an institute in Warsaw. The Polish goverment has been stacking courts and tightening media rules, but the institute itself hasn't been captured; they're still publishing critical work. Some colleagues want to suspend immediately, saying any collaboration now legitimises democratic backsliding. Others say that's the whole problem: 'backsliding' gets chucked at Hungary, Poland and Turkey but rarely at the US, so it's just a partisan label dressed up as analysis. I need a short memo recommending whether we suspend, continue with conditions, or keep going as normal. It should set out criteria the board can actually apply, treat the partisan-label worry honestly, and reach a clear bottom line. I've been trying to frame it with a distinction I cooked up between 'hard backsliding' (the formal rules and institutions actually get rewritten) and 'soft backsliding' (the democratic culture erodes — trust in elections collapses, norms rot — even if the constitutional text stays intact). I want the recommendation to hinge on where our partner sits relative to both, but I'm not convinced the distinction does all the work I want. Can you write the memo? Don't bother with citations or a reading list — just reason it through properly, including the strongest point for whichever side loses.
