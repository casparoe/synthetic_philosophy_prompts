Consider a philosopher who denies that non-rational beings possess direct moral status yet consistently condemns cruelty to animals on the ground that it degrades human moral character. His recorded verdicts on six cases are as follows: (1) a farmer painlessly kills a healthy horse for meat — he judges this permissible but not admirable; (2) a scientist inflicts severe pain on dogs in experiments that yield no usable knowledge — he judges this wrong; (3) a child pulls a cat's tail purely for amusement — he judges this wrong; (4) a person abandons an aging dog that served them faithfully for years — he judges this wrong; (5) a hunter kills a deer for sport despite having no need for food — he judges this wrong; (6) a zoo keeps cognitively complex apes in small enclosures solely for public entertainment — he judges this wrong. Now present a seventh case: researchers construct an artificial neural substrate that replicates primate suffering-circuits without any capacity for rational self-legislation, subject it to stress protocols that induce simulated distress but cause no physical injury, and do so because the data could genuinely advance trauma therapy for rational beings. Predict the philosopher's verdict, identify whether he is running a pure indirect-duty corruption argument, a concealed direct-duty threshold tied to cognitive complexity, or a hybrid, specify which of the prior six cases does the decisive work in distinguishing these readings, and name the single further case that would settle the matter.

Then inject one structural modification not standard in the literature: suppose the suffering-substrate is not designed but evolves through an unsupervised process that the researchers merely observe and occasionally steer without initiating the suffering-protocols themselves. Ask how this alters the corruption analysis — does passive observation of evolved simulated distress still corrupt character — and explain whether, under this modification, the indirect-duty interpretation and the threshold interpretation now pull in opposite directions, and if so, which feature of the modified case forces the split.
