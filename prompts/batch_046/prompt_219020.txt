One takes the Camus line: the universe is silent, there's no transcendent purpose, and building 'meaning' through projects is mostly just a way of avoiding that fact — she doesn't call it bad faith but that's the vibe. The other holds a contemporary hybrid view, kind of Wolf-style: meaning is fitting fulfillment, you need objective value plus subjective engagement, and cosmic pointlessness is just not relevant to whether your life is meaningful. They've been talking past eachother because they never agreed on whether 'meaning in life' is a global narrative property or something modular that can exist in parts even if the whole lacks it. They want me to draft the adversarial collaboration document they should sign.

Write the joint statement. I want a numbered agreed-claims section first — things both accept about what absurdism asserts, about the hybrid view's conditions, about whether flow-state research or cases of post-traumatic growth are neutral data, and about the distinction between local and global evaluation. The cruxes should include whether 'meaning' functions as a success term versus a descriptive one, whether knowing your garden will be destroyed undermines its local meaning, and whether a simulated agent who achieves real mastery in a virtual domain has a meaningful life-part. Also have them address whether any of the dispute is verbal — especially around 'point' and 'value' — and identify which disagreements would survive if the vocabulary were cleaned up.
