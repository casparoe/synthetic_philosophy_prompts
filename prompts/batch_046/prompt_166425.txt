Here is my philosophical profile on suffering, pleasure, moral status, and obligation. I am asking you to audit it for joint tenability.

My commitments:

1. I accept a lexical-threshold form of negative utilitarianism. I hold that suffering and pleasure are incommensurable across a qualitative boundary: any instance of intolerable suffering—the kind that annihilates the capacity for meaningful agency—is lexically worse than any quantity of pleasure, regardless of duration or intensity. Once suffering is of a tolerable kind, standard aggregation resumes, but suffering carries substantially greater weight than pleasure.

2. At the same time, I believe that public moral reasoning—especially in health policy, climate ethics, and long-term resource allocation—requires a continuous, cardinal, interpersonally comparable welfare metric, something in the family of quality-adjusted life years or disability-adjusted life years. Without such a metric, consistent triage and intergenerational evaluation are impossible.

3. I am a phenomenal-consciousness essentialist about moral standing: a being has direct moral status if and only if there is something it is like to be that being. Evolutionary explanations of nociception in organisms that lack phenomenal consciousness therefore provide no direct moral reason to intervene; the badness of suffering is always mediated by experience.

4. On population ethics, I want to block the repugnant conclusion. I do this by holding that bringing a being into existence well above the lexical threshold is permissible but never obligatory, even if that life would contain substantial net welfare. Nevertheless, I maintain that social welfare calculus should maximize total aggregate welfare across all populations—existing, future, and merely possible—using the cardinal metric described in (2).

5. In particular case judgments: (a) a life containing one brief episode of extreme agony but otherwise sustained flourishing and deep joy is worse than non-existence; (b) a life of chronic mild discomfort coupled with profound achievement and sustained joy is clearly worth living and should receive positive weight in aggregate welfare calculations; (c) a being whose well-being hovers exactly at the lexical boundary—neither clearly tolerable nor clearly intolerable—has zero net moral value: it is neither good nor bad to create or sustain such a being.
