I am preparing for a graduate seminar on democratic theory and have been trying to formalize my own philosophical profile on political deception, drawing mainly on Hannah Arendt's well-known essays about lying in politics. Before I present it, I want an audit of whether these commitments actually hang together. Here is the set: (1) Arendt is correct that modern political lying is categorically different from traditional state secrecy because it aims not at concealing particular facts but at destroying the shared factual reality that makes collective judgment possible; (2) consequently, the organized, systematic falsehood she linked to totalitarian regimes is not merely one democratic vice among others but a distinctive existential threat to public life; (3) nevertheless, factual truth does have a legitimate and even necessary place inside ordinary political debate, since policy disagreements presuppose empirical premises that cannot be suspended without rendering deliberation arbitrary; (4) there remains a meaningful distinction between a representative's strategic framing or omission—undertaken to maintain a governing coalition that serves constituent interests—and the reality-destroying falsehood condemned in (1) and (2); and (5) the moral virtue of truthfulness binds private citizens more strictly than it binds officeholders acting within a mandate system, because representatives are sometimes justified in suppressing or distorting facts to preserve institutional capacity.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, ordering them by severity. For each tension, say how badly the conflict runs, what the cheapest conceptual revision would be to restore consistency, and what that revision costs me. I am especially concerned whether the space I try to preserve for strategic framing in (4) collapses back into the reality-destroying behavior I reject in (1)–(2), and whether my differential assignment of truthfulness in (5) undermines my insistence in (3) that factual reality must be protected inside the public realm. Do not simply flag every possible friction; discriminate between apparent conflicts that dissolve on inspection and deep ones that require giving something up. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if abandoned, would do the most to stabilize the remainder of the profile.
