Consider the following argument about downward causation in a careenium. (1) The substrate dynamics are causally closed: every substrate transition has a sufficient substrate-level cause. (2) Simmballs supervene on substrate states but are not identical to them. (3) If simmballs push the substrate around, then some substrate transition lacks a sufficient substrate-level cause, violating closure. (4) Therefore simmballs do not push the substrate. (5) Yet a careenium can display the reflection invariant: a mechanism reads a simmball pattern and produces an output determined solely by the abstract symbol-type, so that substituting physically different token-realizations of the same type yields identical substrate trajectories. (6) Because the mechanism tracks the symbol-type rather than the tokens, the symbol-type is causally relevant to the transition. (7) Hence simmballs do push the substrate, contradicting (4). I am inclined to keep both (1) and (5), but something must give. Identify the weakest step—whether the slide from (2) to (3) illicitly assumes identity conditions, or the inference from (5) to (6) conflates explanatory relevance with causal efficacy—and state precisely what abandoning it costs.
