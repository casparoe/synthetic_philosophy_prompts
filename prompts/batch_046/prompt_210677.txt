Hi, I’m a retired bioligist who’s been readng Tolstoy’s writing on art lately — fascinating stuff, but I keep noticing a pattern in discussions that I don’t have a word for. People invoke Tolstoy’s idea that art should infect the audience with the artist’s feeling, but they apply it selectively. Simple, sentimental works are praised for achieving this infection straightaway, while complex pieces that demand intellectual effort first are dismissed as failing to infect and therefore not being real art. So the infection criterion gets used not as Tolstoy meant it — a democratic, universal standard — but as a filter that lets through easy emotion and blocks anything that needs work. Is there an established concept for this selective misuse of an emotional-transmission standard? How is it different from ordinary elitism, or from Tolstoy’s own view? And if there is no proper term, could you coin one with clear boundary cases? Please answer in British English.
