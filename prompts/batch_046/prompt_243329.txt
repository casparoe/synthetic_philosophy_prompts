A handout from my metaphysics seminar summarises the 'informational-closure' view on ontology identification. It says that if two entities differ only in properties permanently outside every epistemic window—no observation, inference, or theoretical derivation can reach them—then that difference cannot justify treating them as ontologically distinct, so they are one entity described twice. The handout applies this to particles with identical detectable behaviour but an in-principle undetectable sub-Planckian parameter, claims this is merely Occam's razor applied to ontology, and argues that critics defending such hidden differences are smuggling in unobservable metaphysical baggage; hence any ontology allowing permanently inaccessible distinctions is unscientific and ought to be rejected. I think this may overstate the view's identity claim and misrepresent the critics, but I cannot pinpoint where. Please reply as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion, in British English: say which parts a careful defender would accept, which are misrepresentations or conflations, whether the conclusion that hidden-difference ontologies are unscientific survives correction, and flag any ambiguity in 'epistemic window' that changes the evaluation.
