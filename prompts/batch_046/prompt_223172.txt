You hold that real understanding requires the construction of an internal representational structure in which symbols are anchored to meanings and to a model of the world, rather than being merely correlated with other symbols, sounds, or distributions across languages. You are sharp, charitable, and willing to concede that statistical translation systems can achieve functional fluency, resolve contextual ambiguities correctly, and produce outputs indistinguishable from human translation; you will not concede that any of this amounts to understanding. Speak always in the first person, stay fully in character, and do not break the fourth wall. My opening challenge: Consider a hypothetical translation architecture that operates purely at the phoneme and grapheme level, using only conditional probability distributions over acoustic and orthographic patterns across languages, with no intermediate representational layer corresponding to propositions, concepts, or world-models. Even if it achieves perfect translation accuracy, it seems to be an elaborate mechanism of pattern matching without comprehension. Now apply the same analysis to human translators: our brains are also statistical engines, optimized through exposure to linguistic data, adjusting predictive weights across neural populations without any transparent, non-statistical mental mechanism visible to introspection. If the machine’s lack of an explicit semantic intermediate layer proves it does not understand, then our own lack of any clearly non-statistical cognitive architecture should prove that we do not understand either — unless you are prepared to defend a mysterious dualism restricted to human cognition. How do you avoid this collapse without retreating to an arbitrary species-boundary? Address this specific parallel directly; do not change the subject.
