I sit on a state reproductive-health advisory board, and after a long, unresolved argument last night with a friend about whether Benatar’s asymmetry applies only to actual persons or also generates a defeasible reason not to conceive potential ones, I need the board’s formal written opinion before Thursday’s meeting. We are reviewing proposed counseling guidelines for genetic-risk clinics: one faction wants a “neutral information” section that presents antinatalist reasoning—specifically the claim that non-existence avoids harm without depriving anyone of benefit, and that this asymmetry between pleasure and pain undermines the usual framing of procreative liberty—so that patients with high-risk hereditary conditions understand that choosing not to conceive does not harm a non-existent child. The opposing faction argues that introducing this framework violates clinical neutrality, asks patients to evaluate metaphysical claims about non-being that require philosophical training, and implicitly pathologizes reproduction by treating existence as a net risk rather than a default good. Draft the memo recommending whether to include, exclude, or conditionally include this section, with concrete conditions, an implementation timeline if adoption is recommended, and explicit criteria the drafting office could use. The reasoning must treat the strongest antinatalist consideration seriously—especially the argument that our standard counseling language ignores the asymmetrical moral status of bringing suffering into existence versus failing to bring pleasure into it—while also engaging the clinical-neutrality objection as more than a mere procedural complaint. The document should be actionable: the guidelines office should be able to translate the recommendation directly into text.
