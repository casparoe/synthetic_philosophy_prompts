1. genuine ownership of an idea must include the right to control its mental instantiation in others, otherwise it's just copyright with extra steps.
2. ideas are non-rivalrous: my thinking X doesn't prevent your thinking X.
3. but non-rivalry doesn't defeat ownership - radio spectrum rights work the same way.
4. so mental ownership isn't killed by shared use.
5. now, if i own idea I and you learn it from me, you've recieved a transmission of my property into your mind.

8. but we don't license people for every concept they absorb, so most education is technically theft.

11. yet patents cover functional ideas, and copyright protects expression precisely to avoid owning the idea - but they still rely on the idea/expression distinction being real.
12. so IP law simultaneously asserts mental ownership (via patents) and denies it (via the idea-expression split), which is incoherent.
