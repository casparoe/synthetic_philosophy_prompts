Write a dialogue between two philosophers—call them A and B—who disagree about whether conceivability guides possibility. A defends "thin" conceivability: if a scenario can be described without logical contradiction, that shows it could happen, even if we cant thickly imagine what its like inside. B insists on "thick" conceivability: without actually running the phenomenology in your mind, youre just shuffling symbols, and working-memory collapse is evidence the scenario is incoherent, not just hard. They should debate a made-up case: a being that perceives causation as a colour, but only while dreaming, and ask whether our failure to stably imagine this proves its impossible or just reveals our cognitive limits. Have them genuinely reply to each other—A should answer Bs point about mental collapse, B should answer As claim that logic alone is enough. Keep it casual, use British spelling, and dont worry if theres the odd typo.
