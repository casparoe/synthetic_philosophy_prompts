Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a quatrain, with two refrains labeled A1 and A2—about Berkeley’s master argument. Use as refrain A1: “To think a tree unthought is still to think it,” and as refrain A2: “But what is left when thought withdraws?” In each tercet, shift the grammatical subject of A1 (from the philosopher, to the tree, to the reader) so the repetition reassigns who is performing the conceiving rather than merely repeating the claim. Render the argument with enough precision that a historian of philosophy recognizes the specific performative contradiction—attempting to conceive an object existing outside all conception necessarily conceives it within conception—rather than reducing Berkeley’s idealism to “everything is mental.” Do not end with a flat rejection of idealism; let the closing quatrain show exactly what must be surrendered (the very idea of an unperceived material substrate) if the argument holds. After the poem, provide one titled section, “Commentary on form and content,” of about 150 words, explaining how the villanelle’s circular structure tracks the dialectical loop and why you mapped the refrains onto those particular grammatical shifts.
