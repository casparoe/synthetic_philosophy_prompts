After forty years in solid-state physics, I have recently turned to metaphysics and find the special composition question particularly puzzling. In crystallography, we treat a lattice—say, silicon in the diamond structure—not as a scattered aggregate of atoms but as a single object possessing emergent properties such as a band gap and definite thermal conductivity, which suggests that physical bonding and periodic arrangement are sufficient for parts to compose a genuine whole. Yet if functional or causal integration is the criterion that distinguishes real composites from mere aggregates, then any collection of particles linked by universal gravitation or shared quantum fields should also form one object, which seems to collapse restrictionism into universalism. Must the restrictionist therefore deny that a crystal is genuinely one thing, or is there a hidden flaw in extending physical-bonding criteria into mereological analysis?
