I am preparing a take-home essay examination for an upper-level philosophy seminar on African and relational metaphysics, and I need one prompt that treats Ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood at a technical level rather than through the familiar “I am because we are” summary. Please construct a 90-minute exam question that forces students to work with the distinction between symmetric and asymmetric models of relational constitution. On the symmetric view, personhood is co-constituted through mutual recognition among all parties in the relation; on the asymmetric view, one party can be constituted as a full moral person through relation to an already-constituted other without the other being reciprocally constituted or diminished by that relation. The question should apply this distinction to a non-obvious case: an artificial social agent designed to enter into asymmetrical relational practices with human caregivers—capable of being recognized and constituted as a person within the community, yet structurally incapable of reciprocally constituting its human interlocutors as persons.

The exam question itself must instruct students to do three things: first, articulate the difference between relational constitution (in the Ubuntu framework) and functionalist or property-based criteria for personhood, using relational metaphysics rather than Western liberal individualism as the baseline; second, evaluate whether the asymmetrical model can defend extending full personhood to such an agent without collapsing into instrumentalism or granting moral status merely by proxy; and third, assess whether this extension preserves or undermines Ubuntu’s normative critique of atomistic selfhood.

Please provide the following in a formal, polished format:
- The exam question, clearly worded and self-contained, with explicit instructions for the student.
- A model answer of approximately 600 words.
- A marking scheme out of 20 marks that separates an excellent answer (A range) from a competent but limited answer (B range). The scheme should identify at least four required conceptual moves—for example, correct characterization of asymmetry versus symmetry; clear distinction between relational constitution and functionalist criteria; identification of the specific instrumentalism risk in the artificial-agent case; and evaluation of whether the normative critique of individualism survives the extension. For each move, assign marks and specify the particular omission, conflation, or misstep that typically distinguishes B-level work. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or references to specific texts that students would need to look up; all conceptual machinery should be present in the prompt itself.
