I am preparing a ninety-minute graduate seminar for a philosophy-of-mind reading group—mostly second-year students with solid background in philosophy of language but no specialist work on externalism—and I want to teach Putnam’s Twin Earth argument and the resulting semantic externalism without replaying the familiar water/H₂O scenario they have all encountered in introductory surveys. In its place, I intend to build the session around a modified Twin Earth case involving a functional-kind term for local fauna: on Earth, the term applies to both placental and monotreme organisms through expert deference and shared taxonomic practice, while on Twin Earth the term applies only to egg-laying monotremes despite the speakers being in identical narrow psychological states, with the reproductive and biochemical differences playing the structural role that H₂O and Twin-H₂O play in the original argument. I discussed this framing with a colleague who worried that introducing evolutionary contingency might confuse the metaphysical point with biological accident, so the plan should address whether that risk undermines the pedagogical choice.

Plan the explanatory arc precisely: the dependency order in which the pieces must be introduced so that the externalist conclusion feels inevitable rather than abrupt (the pre-theoretic intuition about mental-content sameness, the division of linguistic labor, the biological-functional twist, the broad-content conclusion); the properties the carrying example must have and must lack—specifically, how to prevent it from collapsing into either a mere descriptive-error case or a purely causal-correlation story about brains and environments; the misconceptions students will form at each stage, especially the slide from “meaning is not fixed by intrinsic mental states” to “the physical environment causally determines every belief,” and the exact point in the sequence where each misconception should be intercepted; one diagnostic question I can pose near the end to distinguish genuine grasp of broad content from mere anti-individualist sentiment; and what I should explicitly refuse to explain at this level—whether two-dimensional semantic frameworks should be mentioned only to be bracketed, or left out entirely—together with a brief justification for that omission. Include also a check for whether they have understood why the Twin Earth intuition survives even if one grants that the speakers share all their narrow psychological properties.
