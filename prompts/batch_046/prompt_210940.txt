okay so i'm a software engineer and i dont have any formal philosophy background, but my roommate studies it and threw this argument at me while i was working on a consent flow for an app. i think he's wrong but i cant find the exit.

(1) if consent is valid then the consenter must not be deceived about anything thats materially relevant to thier choice.

(2) a fact is materially relevant iff, if they knew it, theyd say no.

(3) take any hidden fact F - say the exact server farm location, or a 0.001% chance of a future algorithim change. there is always some possible version of that same person who is more risk-averse and who, upon learning F, would refuse.

(4) so for every hidden fact F, there exists a counterfactual where knowing F flips thier decision.

(5) by (2) that makes F materially relevant.

(6) but you cant disclose literally everything - every line of code, every future update, every server location.

(7) therefore every act of consent involves deception about materially relevant facts, which means valid consent is literally impossible.

(8) yet we clearly treat some consents as okay and others as broken - like a surgeon lying about risks is wrong, but not mentioning the color of the waiting room isnt.

(9) so either (1) is false, or our whole practice is nonsense.

i really want to keep (1) because it feels right. but i also dont want to say that all consent is impossible. where's the slip? which step do i reject, and what does fixing it cost?
