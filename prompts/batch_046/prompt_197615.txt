As I revise a novel about mechanical minds and evolutionary time, I find myself needing a precise philosophical document to serve as an exhibit in one chapter. In the scene, two scholars—designated here as the Inversionist and the Embodimentist—have been ordered by an unseen tribunal to produce an adversarial-collaboration statement on whether Darwin's evolutionary competence and Turing's syntactic computation represent a single inversion of reasoning or two categorically different phenomena. The Inversionist argues that both domains achieve robust, adaptive performance without any internal comprehension, and that this shared structure undermines any sharp boundary between natural cognition and artificial intelligence. The Embodimentist insists that biological adaptation carries what she calls teleonomic debt—an accumulated, environment-coupled historical obligation that calibrated traits bear to vanished selective regimes—which syntactic computation cannot replicate, making Darwin's inversion ontologically distinct from Turing's.

Please draft the joint statement these two parties could both sign, separating genuine disagreement from merely verbal confusion. Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. The first item should state the shared claims both camps accept about evolutionary calibration, formal computation, and the unreliability of introspection as a guide to competence. The second should formulate their core disagreement in wording each side accepts: specifically, whether the distinction between biological and computational competence is one of kind or of degree, and whether teleonomic debt functions as a coherent explanatory category rather than a metaphor. The third should identify the cruxes—particularly whether an evolutionary trait calibrated to an extinct predator demonstrates comprehension-like properties that computation lacks, and whether evolutionary maladaptation to obsolete environments carries philosophical weight for artificial minds.

The fourth item should present one concrete, neutrally described case that both sides agree bears on the dispute—such as a fossilized anti-predator reflex that remains active long after climate-driven extinction has removed the threat, rendering the trait permanently maladaptive—without resolving it in either camp's favor. The fifth item should record each camp's explicit prediction about what that case reveals regarding the presence or absence of comprehension, and should state clearly whether any portion of their disagreement dissolves upon closer inspection into a dispute about words alone.
