I am preparing my commitments on Keynes's philosophy of the good life for a sixth-form essay and want a consistency check before I submit. Here is where I stand:

(1) I take Keynes's 1930 essay Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren to predict that within roughly a century advanced economies will solve the problem of scarcity, reducing necessary labour to about fifteen hours per week and leaving humanity with the deeper challenge of living wisely and agreeably.

(2) I think the conception of that wise life is essentially Moorean: for Keynes, friendship, aesthetic contemplation, and emotional intimacy are objectively valuable states of consciousness, not merely culturally preferred activities, because he absorbed this directly from G. E. Moore's Principia Ethica.

(3) At the same time, I read Keynes as holding that human beings possess a near-irrepressible psychological drive for purposive labour and accumulation; that is why he warned that suddenly removing economic necessity might produce widespread ennui and psychological dislocation rather than liberation.

(4) I also want to extend Keynes's notion of animal spirits—the spontaneous, non-rational optimism that drives investment—into a broader claim about flourishing: genuine well-being depends on unstructured, vital energy being directed towards art, play, and friendship, and this kind of vitality is inherently resistant to rational planning or bureaucratic direction.

(5) Consequently, I believe that Keynes's own advocacy for active state macroeconomic management and long-range planning to hasten abundance stands in tension with his deeper recognition that the good life is spontaneous and unplannable; yet I also think his remark that in the long run we are all dead undermines his authority to make century-long forecasts about abundance in the first place.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and tell me which single change to my commitments would clean up the most problems at once. I would like the answer in British English, and please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations—just analyse the commitments on their own terms.
