Camp A are the strong loopers — they hold that once a diagostic category is applied, it reshapes self-understanding and behavior so deeply that the category becomes a moving target, and any claim to track an objective kind collapses. Camp B are the revisable realists — they accept looping happens but think it can be modeled, measured, and corrected through iterative revision of criteria, so nosology can still progress.

The deliverable is a document both can sign. Structure it exactly like this:

First, a numbered agreed-claims section (maybe five or six items). Both sides should sign onto the formal structure of feedback between classification and self-interpretation; onto Hacking being right about interactive kinds but leaving something important out; and onto the descriptive mechanics of a hypothetical multi-layer loop — call it the algorithm-mediated loop — where patients adjust self-reports in response to predictive risk scores trained on prior diagnostic labels, clinicians then tweak thresholds based on observed resistance, and the epidemiological baseline shifts in response. They should agree on what this loop looks like descriptively without agreeing on what it means philosophically.

Second, a disagreements table with columns: the crux; Camp A wording (phrased so theyd accept it); Camp B wording (same); the study or conceptual argument both agree would actually bear on it; and each sides prediction.

The cruxes must include at least:
1. Does looping destroy reference stability altogether, or just complicate tracking?
2. Can iterative revision correct loop-induced drift, or does correction itself become part of the loop?
3. In the algorithm-mediated loop specifically, is unbiased epidemiology even conceptually possible?

Make sure neither side sounds like a strawman. If part of their dispute turns out verbal — maybe over what objective kind requires — say exactly which part and why.

Keep it in the weeds but dont ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. This needs to be reason-through-able for a model with no web access. Write casual, and a typo or two is totally fine.
