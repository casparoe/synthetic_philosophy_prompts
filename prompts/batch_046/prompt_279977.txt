Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy sorting positions on whether the agricultural transition was, in some defensible sense, a mistake for human welfare and social organization. Split the tree by four concrete commitments: (1) whether forager welfare should be measured primarily by leisure, nutritional security, or autonomy from hierarchy; (2) whether the shift was a reversible collective choice, an irreversible demographic trap, or an evolutionary inevitability; (3) whether moral evaluation applies retrospectively to early cultivators given their information, structurally to long-run outcomes such as inequality and state formation, or only by comparison with modern industrial agriculture; (4) whether the counterfactual baseline is stable hunter-gatherer life, hypothetical non-agrarian complex societies, or the actual pre-agricultural populations that existed. At each leaf, state the position's common name if it has one, otherwise label it unnamed; summarize its core claim in one sentence; and identify the single commitment that sounds most awkward to common intuition. Note any answer-combinations that are incoherent or unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis—logical contradiction, hidden empirical premise, or absence of defenders.
