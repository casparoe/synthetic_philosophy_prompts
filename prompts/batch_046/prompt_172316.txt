I am drafting a six-month research proposal for an interdisciplinary workshop on the architecture of justification, and I want to move beyond the well-worn trilemma of foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism. My suspicion is that all three positions tacitly assume justification travels along a single linear chain—whether it terminates in basic beliefs, loops back on itself, or extends infinitely—whereas our actual practices of giving and asking for reasons often look more like a convergent graph: several semi-independent lines of support meeting at a node without any one being the unique ancestor. The precise question is whether replacing the linear regress model with a directed acyclic graph of convergent support allows for a genuinely distinct fourth position—tentatively called convergentism—that avoids the standard objections to the other three, or whether it inevitably collapses into one of them once the formal structure is made explicit.

The existing literature leaves this open because philosophers typically analyse the regress in terms of ancestral chains of belief-dependence, which makes convergence either invisible or reducible to coherence. I propose to spend the first two months constructing formal models of justification graphs with convergence nodes and defining conditions under which convergence provides non-circular, non-foundational support; the next two months testing whether the traditional regress argument still applies when the relation is not transitive along a single path but distributed across multiple paths; and the final two months comparing the result with recent non-linear epistemologies to see if convergence adds anything they miss. A successful project would deliver a rigorous argument that convergentism is structurally distinct and that the linear regress is a modelling artefact rather than a necessary feature of justification; failure would be demonstrated if convergence either reduces to coherent circularity or requires hidden foundational nodes at the roots of every converging branch. I do not want a bibliography or literature review by citation—please refer to positions and arguments generally, and I will supply references myself.
