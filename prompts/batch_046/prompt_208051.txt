Write an essay on whether the separateness of persons objection successfully undermines act-utilitarianism. Do not treat the objection merely as a complaint about unequal distribution; instead, examine the stronger metaphysical version that holds experiential burdens are non-transferable between agents because personal identity fixes a boundary of ownership over suffering and welfare. Weigh whether this version collapses if one adopts a four-dimensionalist ontology of persons—where temporal parts rather than enduring substances are the primary bearers of experience—against the standard enduring-substance framing that utilitarian aggregation tacitly assumes. Reach and defend a specific conclusion: either that the metaphysical version of the objection survives ontological revision and therefore blocks interpersonal aggregation, or that it depends on a container-model of the self that four-dimensionalism dissolves, leaving only a weaker distributive concern. In either case, justify your verdict with explicit reasoning about how identity conditions affect the permissibility of trade-offs between agents.
