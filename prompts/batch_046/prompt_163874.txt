Arthur Schopenhauer died in 1860, decades before the phonograph, let alone generative systems capable of composing structurally complex, emotionally resonant music without any human performer in the loop. In his metaphysics, music is not a representation of Platonic Ideas but an immediate objectification of the Will itself—bypassing conceptual cognition and expressing the inner nature of striving, suffering, and desiring directly. Consider a contemporary system that produces novel symphonic works through statistical pattern-matching over centuries of scores, with no embodied consciousness—no suffering, desiring, or striving subject—anywhere in its production chain, yet human listeners report profound aesthetic and emotional responses. Drawing strictly on Schopenhauer’s distinction between the world as representation and the world as Will, his claim that music expresses feeling rather than idea, and his view that melody articulates the multifarious striving of the Will, predict his assessment of such algorithmically generated music. Would he deny it the status of genuine music because the producer lacks a Will, grant it a derivative status because the listener’s own Will supplies the missing interiority, classify it as an especially deceptive form of representation, or propose some other ontological category? In your answer, first articulate the governing metaphysical principle, then apply it precisely to the absence of a living subject in the generative process, and finally address whether the listener’s experience can redeem, condemn, or further complicate the artifact’s status.
