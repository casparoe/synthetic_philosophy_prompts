Consider the philosopher Dr. Elena Rostova, who evaluates hypothetical worlds against Leibnizian criteria for being the best of all possible worlds. She has delivered the following verdicts on five constructed cases.

World Alpha is infinite in duration and population; every instance of suffering is mild and instrumentally necessary for greater goods that depend on it; the aggregate net value diverges positively; and the governing physical laws contain the minimal number of independent constants. Rostova judges that Alpha is a viable candidate for the best world.

World Beta is identical to Alpha in every respect except that it contains one additional sentient being who suffers intensely and pointlessly, with no compensating good for anyone. Rostova rules it out.

World Gamma is finite in both duration and population; it achieves the mathematically maximal sum of well-being that any finite universe can contain; its laws are slightly more complex than Alpha's; and no being in it ever suffers. Rostova judges Gamma viable as well.

World Delta is infinite; its net value also diverges positively; yet there exists an accessible possible world that differs only by an infinitesimal change in one law constant and achieves a strictly greater net value while retaining exactly the same law-complexity. Rostova rules Delta out.

World Epsilon is finite and achieves the same maximal finite sum as Gamma; however, its long-term stability depends on a single direct miraculous intervention that prevents a catastrophic collapse that would annihilate all value. Rostova judges Epsilon viable too.

Now consider World Zeta. Zeta is infinite in duration and population; its net value converges to a finite supremum that is strictly lower than Gamma's total; it contains no gratuitous evil whatsoever; its laws are maximally simple, matching Alpha's; and although there exists a nearby world with slightly more complex laws that achieves an infinite net value, no world with equal or greater simplicity exceeds Zeta's value.

Would Rostova say that Zeta qualifies? I am less interested in the bare answer than in the work of identifying the rule she appears to be running, articulating at least two other rules that fit her five verdicts equally well, and stating what each of those rules predicts about Zeta. Please also give your best guess at her actual verdict with the reason for preferring that rule, and name either one of her earlier cases or one further hypothetical case that would best distinguish the competing rules from one another.
