I've been working through some old LessWrong discussions on social kinds and keep hitting a gap that seems to sit right between philosophy of science and institutional design. Most people treat generational labels—Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z—as either obviously fictional marketing brackets or as obviously real demographic clusters with measurable causal powers. I'm interested in the murkier intermediate case: an organization that has to decide whether to build a permanent internal structure around a cohort category, where the decision hinges on ontological commitments rather than on predictive convenience alone. I'm taking a course on conceptual engineering right now, and my instructor keeps pushing us to distinguish statistical clustering from kind-membership, but the examples we use are always biological species or chemical elements. I want to see how that distinction actually fractures when applied to human cohorts.

Please give me a concrete example of a conceptual issue that arises specifically when an institution treats a generational cohort as possessing a kind-essence—something like a shared modal profile of risk tolerance, epistemic habits, or normative commitments—rather than treating it as a temporary administrative bracket indexed to birth years. The scenario should have real institutional mechanics: perhaps a university curriculum board, a corporate governance panel, or a public-health allocation committee. Crucially, show how the ontological assumption changes the reasoning in a way that correlational clustering alone wouldn't justify—maybe by licensing counterfactual inferences about how members would behave under radically different economic or technological conditions, or by grounding obligations, rights, or design requirements that wouldn't apply to a mere age bracket. I want the example to expose a tension that survives even if all the empirical correlations are granted, and I'd prefer it avoid both simplistic stereotype objections and appeals to obscure sociological theories outside standard philosophy-of-science discussion. The goal is to locate the exact conceptual fault line when generations are taken seriously as kinds.
