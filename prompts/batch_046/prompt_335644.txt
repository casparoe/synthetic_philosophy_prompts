A podcast on ancient historiography I heard summarised contemporary source criticism as follows. Historians, it said, treat Herodotus and Thucydides as narrators with correctable biases: by cross-referencing their accounts against material remains and later documentary records, scholars strip away the subjective layer and recover an objective core of probable events. The method has recently been extended by what the speaker called negative archival synthesis: when several independent administrative or literary traditions all fail to record an event that ought to have left widespread traces, this collective silence is now weighed as positive probabilistic evidence against the event's occurrence, rather than treated merely as a gap in survival. From this, the speaker concluded that knowledge of the distant past is essentially statistical inference — we correct testimony for bias, integrate silent archives as data points, and arrive at confidence intervals for historical claims. The framing struck me as suspiciously tidy, particularly the idea that silence is routinely given positive weight and that Herodotus and Thucydides can be corrected in the same way, but I cannot tell where the popularisation ends and the mistake begins.
