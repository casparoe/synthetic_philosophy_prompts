I have come to hold a strong version of embedded agency: an agent's goals are not internal representational states that happen to be causally coupled to an environment, but are relational properties constituted entirely by the specific informational and causal coupling between the organism and its niche. On this view, removing an agent from its niche does not merely deprive it of the means to pursue its goals; it literally dissolves those goals, in much the same way that destroying neural substrate dissolves a belief, except that here the substrate includes parts of the environment. The agent is therefore not embedded in its environment as a pilot is embedded in a ship; rather, the boundary of the agent is drawn where predictive dynamics stabilize, and that boundary routinely extends beyond the skin. I want to know what would genuinely count against this position—not a summary of standard internalist objections, but the specific observations, conceptual distinctions, or formal results that should move a defender. For instance: are there well-documented cases in which organisms retain clearly goal-directed behavior after radical environmental displacement, and if so, can the view accommodate them without ad hoc redrawing of the boundary? Does the analogy between niche-destruction and brain-destruction survive careful analysis of functional organization, or does it collapse once we distinguish causal dependency from constitutive dependency? Are there findings in robotics or synthetic biology—concerning minimal agents, artificial life systems, or decoupled control architectures—that either support or undermine the claim that goals are not portable across contexts? Which of these potential defeaters would a committed embedded-agency theorist concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss by appealing to residual hidden coupling or by redefining the niche? I also want an honest assessment of whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability—if any retention of goal-behavior can always be explained away by undetected coupling—and whether such insulation is a virtue or a vice for a metaphysical claim of this kind. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicate what distinctive predictions this view makes that its internalist rivals do not, and say clearly what it actually risks.
