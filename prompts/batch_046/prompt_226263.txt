In discussions of Borges's metaphysics of time, a recurring analogy compares his eternal, cyclical model — developed across his essays and fiction — to a musical score in which every measure is already written and performance is merely the selection of one path through a pre-existing grid. The analogy is used to argue that Borges eliminates genuine becoming and collapses human freedom into the illusion of choosing among eternally present possibilities. Lay out the correspondence explicitly: what plays the role of the score, the performer, the selection, the repetition of themes, and the silence between movements? Then identify where the mapping fractures — for instance, whether Borges's denial of the present's uniqueness can be captured by a notation system that still requires sequential execution, or whether an infinite combinatoric space is closer to his view than linear musical form. Say what the analogy quietly imports about the existence of an outside reader or composer that Borges's cosmos may lack, and whether the conclusion that human agency is thereby dissolved survives once those disanalogies are in view. If a different analogy — say, to a labyrinth with a single center or to a mirror reflecting all other mirrors — does better justice to Borges's denial of succession, propose it and show where it gains and loses ground.
