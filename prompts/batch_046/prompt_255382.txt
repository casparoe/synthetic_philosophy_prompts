Design a branching taxonomy—functioning as a decision tree—of philosophical responses to Goodman’s grue paradox, aimed at sorting positions by their commitments rather than by label recognition. Begin with the root question: does the paradox reveal a genuine logical deficiency in formal inductive inference, or does it dissolve once we recognize that “grue” lacks projectibility? From there, split by whether projectibility is treated as an objective metaphysical feature of natural kinds, a feature fixed solely by linguistic entrenchment in actual practice, or a pragmatic convention with no deeper ground. Add a further branch on whether entrenchment, if it resolves the riddle, does so descriptively (tracking how we actually project) or normatively (constraining how we ought to project). Include another division on whether admitting predicates like “grue” into inductive reasoning is strictly incoherent, merely epistemically uneconomical, or actually permissible—even required—in theoretical domains where natural-kind boundaries break down. Each leaf should carry a brief description of the resulting position, a standard name if one exists, and a one-sentence note on its most awkward commitment; explicitly mark any logically possible branch combinations that appear unoccupied, diagnosing whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or being unexplored. Flag any well-known interpretations that your tree fails to separate. The intended user is a philosophy student who understands induction and predicate projectibility but can answer each node without already knowing which view it selects. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or references to specific page numbers.
