I am preparing a seminar paper on Machiavelli’s account of fortune and virtù, and I want a consistency audit of the interpretive package I have adopted before I defend it. (2) I take Machiavelli’s cyclical theory of regimes, the anacyclosis, to describe deterministic attractor dynamics in institutional morphology: regardless of initial path, political forms eventually converge on a small recurring set of patterns. (3) At the same time, I treat Machiavelli as a deep path-dependence theorist: minor perturbations—an untimely death, a single flood, a misread dispatch—can permanently lock a polity onto one institutional trajectory, with no convergence mechanism that pulls it back toward the cyclical pattern. (4) I hold that virtù includes a radical capacity for self-transformation, allowing a leader to reshape temperament, habits, and even moral dispositions to match the demands of the times. (5) I maintain that this self-transformation carries no intrinsic ethical cost, because Machiavellian virtù is normatively empty: its only content is efficacy in preserving the state. (6) Finally, I claim that fortune can be mastered in the limited sense that careful institutional design can reduce the variance of outcomes, even though contingency itself can never be eliminated.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. Then tell me which single revision, if I were forced to make only one, would clean up the largest number of problems, and whether that revision leaves me with a recognizably Machiavellian position or forces me into a different theoretical framework altogether. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
