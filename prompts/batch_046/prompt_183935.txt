Construct a thought experiment involving a mechanical sorting apparatus built entirely from careenium — a hypothetical material responsive only to geometric shape, thermal gradient, and charge, with no intrinsic representational properties — that processes simmballs: small spheres bearing engraved symbols that denote temperature categories. Engineer the scenario so that it isolates whether semantic content causally influences the substrate, with these specifications: the setting is an ordinary laboratory or workplace (no consciousness, no perfect predictors, no exotic physics); the apparatus is described in one paragraph; and the symbols HOT and COLD share an identical shape profile except for orientation, which is read by a mechanically neutral scanner. For three positions — (i) strong semantic causalism, holding that the symbol's meaning pushes the careenium substrate; (ii) pure physical reductionism, holding that only the engraving's geometry and the scanner's mechanics move the substrate; and (iii) careenial functionalism, holding that the symbol's functional role is causally efficacious but only through its physical instantiation, not through intrinsic meaning — show what each says about the actual sorting event and about a counterfactual in which the symbol is flipped (same shape, opposite meaning, scanner misreads orientation). Identify the structural feature that makes position (iii) diverge from both (i) and (ii), explain whether removing that feature collapses the case into a standard symbol-grounding problem, and describe how a defender of whichever view fares worst in the counterfactual would attack the scenario. Please state how confident you are in your analysis of each position's verdict and single out the point about which you are least sure.
