Write a proposal for a philosophy term paper (approximately 2500 words, one semester) on a precise formulation of the Meno problem: does Socrates' claim that knowledge is true belief secured by an account of the reason—often rendered through the metaphor of tethering—actually resolve why knowledge is more valuable than true belief, or does it merely relocate that value onto the auxiliary worth of explanatory coherence? State the question so that it has a determinate answer, with conditions specified for each outcome: either the securing relation supplies knowledge with an independent value that true belief lacks, or it does not. Explain why existing answers leave this open: standard interpretations read the tether either as a causal-stabilizing mechanism that prevents belief from wandering, or as a logical-justificatory condition, but they rarely examine whether either reading gives knowledge a value distinct from practical success in action-guidance. Propose an approach that avoids this stalemate by introducing a distinction between practical value, understood as reliable success in guiding action, and epistemic sovereignty, understood as the agent's normative standing as the rational origin of the belief's correctness; then ask whether Socrates' tether is intended to establish sovereignty rather than merely stabilize opinion. Describe the possible conclusions: that Socrates solves the problem by grounding belief in the agent's rational authority; that he fails because sovereignty, if real, is instrumentally reducible to reliability; or that the problem dissolves once we recognize that Meno tacitly assumes value must be practical, an assumption Socrates may not share. Provide an honest assessment of scope: the project is tractable if confined to the closing argument of the Meno and a focused comparison with one modern framing of the value problem, such as the swamping problem, but it risks becoming too large if it attempts a comprehensive historical survey of Platonic epistemology or a full defense of epistemic sovereignty as a general theory. Argue that the work is worth doing because it uncovers a hidden normative dimension in an apparently practical puzzle, and that it can be completed within the term. In your response, please state how confident you are in the framing offered above and single out the point you are least sure of, particularly whether the distinction between practical value and epistemic sovereignty genuinely captures Socrates' concern or imports an anachronistic modern category into the dialogue.
