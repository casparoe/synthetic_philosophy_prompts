Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers, Ari and Blake, about whether transformative experiences undermine rational choice not merely through ignorance of subjective value, but through a specific structural failure Ari calls "diachronic preference-transitivity collapse." Ari argues that when an experience restructures one's core values, the pre- and post-transformation selves lack a common metric, violating a minimal intertemporal rationality constraint that an agent's preferences over her own possible lives should remain transitive across the transformation boundary. Blake rejects this framing, proposing that rationality across transformation should be judged instead by "epistemic-humility consistency"—the willingness to revise one's choice architecture precisely in response to the failure of one's prior predictive models. The dialogue must contain real back-and-forth: Blake should press Ari on whether transitivity is even a normative constraint for agents who rationally anticipate becoming different evaluative subjects, and Ari should challenge whether Blake's humility criterion avoids collapsing into mere adaptive preference formation without critical distance. Neither character should be a strawman, and neither should talk past the other. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or references to specific texts.
