Consider the rulings of a hypothetical distributive-justice board that applies what it calls "priority-weighted welfare assessment" to cardinal distributions across three-person societies, with welfare scaled 0–100 and anchored so that 0 is non-existence and 100 is full flourishing. Its past judgments: (i) at (20, 80) it endorsed redistribution to (30, 70); (ii) at (50, 50) it rejected a universal shock leaving both at 40; (iii) at (10, 40, 90) it endorsed reform to (20, 35, 85); (iv) at (30, 50, 50) it endorsed reform to (40, 45, 45), allowing the worst-off to gain ten points funded by equal five-point reductions from the other two.

Now consider a new case: a society at (20, 50, 50). A proposed policy lowers the two individuals at 50 to 40 each, destroys the ten-point surplus rather than transferring it, and leaves the person at 20 untouched. This is a pure leveling-down of the middle and upper-middle: equality improves sharply, but no one is made better off and two are made worse off.

Predict the board's verdict. Identify at least two competing rules that fit its track record— for instance, continuous concave prioritarianism; equality-sensitive prioritarianism that assigns independent weight to variance reduction; and a threshold-sensitive view applying extra priority only below a dignity floor near 30— and state what each predicts for this case. Give your best-guess verdict with the reasoning that selects among them, and specify one further case that would distinguish the leading candidates.
