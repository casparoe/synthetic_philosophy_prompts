Consider the following examination question and a student’s response, submitted in a graduate course on Plato’s metaphysics and epistemology.

Question: In Republic X (596a–598d) Socrates distinguishes three beds—the Form made by the god, the particular bed made by the carpenter, and the painted image made by the artist—to illustrate degrees of reality and imitation. Integrate this three-level ontology with the four-level epistemology of the divided line (Republic VI 509d–511e) and the four stages of liberation in the cave allegory (Republic VII 514a–521b). Specifically: (a) assign each of the three beds to a segment of the divided line, explaining your reasoning; (b) map the prisoner’s four stages—shadow-viewing, viewing the puppets and fire inside, seeing reflections and real objects outside, and viewing the sun—onto the same four segments; and (c) address whether the carpenter’s understanding of bed-making, which relies on measurement and proportion but does not grasp the Form itself, belongs to dianoia or to pistis, and whether the prisoner’s recognition inside the cave that the fire illuminates the puppets should be classified with dianoia because it involves reasoning about an unseen cause.

Student answer: The painter’s bed is clearly eikasia, since it copies the carpenter’s copy. The carpenter’s physical bed is pistis: it is a tangible object in the visible realm. The Form of Bed is noesis, grasped by direct intellectual intuition. That leaves dianoia to cover the carpenter’s practical reasoning—his use of measurement, proportion, and structural hypotheses to shape the wood—which is intermediate between mere belief and full knowledge of the Form. Turning to the cave: (1) shadow-viewing is eikasia; (2) seeing the puppets is pistis; (3) seeing reflections in water and then real trees and stars outside is dianoia, because the prisoner is now reasoning about structural relations using visible images as models; and (4) viewing the sun is noesis of the Form of the Good. Notably, the prisoner’s recognition inside the cave that the fire is the source of illumination and that the shadows are cast by objects moving behind him should also be placed in dianoia, not pistis: he is constructing a hypothetical causal model of an unseen mechanism and deriving the visible effects from it, which is precisely the hypothetical, downward-reasoning character of dianoia described in the line passage. Thus the fire-stage belongs with mathematical-craft reasoning, leaving pistis only for the passive perception of the puppets themselves.
