I'm in a reading group on Kant's Perpetual Peace and we've been stuck for months on whether the 'guarantee of nature' in the First Supplement actually supports republican government or quietly replaces moral motivation with historical determinism. The other camp says if nature guarantees the outcome, republican institutions become instrumentally justified, which Kant should reject since he thinks republican constitutions are valueable only because they express autonomy. We've read the same passages and neither side budges. Is this a verbal disagreement about 'mechanism', a substantive clash over whether instrumental reasoning can enter Kant's politics, or just us projecting modern worries?
