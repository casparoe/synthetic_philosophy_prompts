(1) in kavka's puzzle it's rational to form the intention to drink the toxin tomorrow for $1M, even though actually drinking is harmful and irrational once the money is in your account.
(2) the only reason drinking is irrational later is that the reward that justified it—the million—has already been recieved.
(3) so whenever an action is justified solely by a reward that's paid out before the action happens, performing the action is irrational even if intending it was rational.
(4) promises work the same way: i sincerely promise to help you move tomorrow, and the reward (being trusted, keeping my reputation) is secured the instant i form the sincere intention.
(5) once tomorrow comes and i'm tired, that reputation reward is already banked, and helping you has only costs.

(7) if i do this consistently i'm a rational promise-breaker, which is absurd.
(8) so the split between rational intention and rational action that kavka relies on must be fake; there can't be a real distinction.
