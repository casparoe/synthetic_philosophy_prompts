I am preparing a graduate seminar on comparative metaphysics, and I want to test whether the core arguments of Madhyamaka Buddhist philosophy can be rendered into the vocabulary of contemporary analytic metaphysics without losing their critical force. You should treat this exercise as an expert philosopher with equal competence in Buddhist philosophy and in analytic metaphysics—someone who can move precisely between sūtra-style dialectic and the formal apparatus of grounding, modality, and essential properties. Before you begin the translation, please take a moment to outline for yourself the four positions of Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi (the tetralemma) and the reflexive Madhyamaka claim that even śūnyatā, emptiness itself, is empty of svabhāva, that is, of any inherent, independent existence.

Then, working systematically, restate both the tetralemma and the doctrine of emptiness using only the resources of modern analytic metaphysics and modal logic: ontological grounding (full and partial), essential properties and anti-essentialism, possible-worlds semantics with de re and de dicto modal operators, and the distinction between existing simpliciter and being essentially F. Do not import untranslated Buddhist technical terms as loanwords; every claim should be expressed as a thesis about grounding relations, modal properties, or essential absence. Once the translation is complete, provide a detailed residue report: for each corner of the tetralemma, indicate whether it maps cleanly onto a failure of grounding or an absence of essential properties, whether it requires distorting either framework to be expressed, or whether it has no counterpart at all in the analytic vocabulary. Pay particular attention to the reflexive move—emptiness of emptiness—and ask whether it corresponds to any recognized problem in the metaphysics of grounding, such as debates over well-foundedness, second-order grounding, or the impossibility of a complete grounding chain. Finally, assess whether the untranslatable residue is best read as a genuine limitation in analytic metaphysics—perhaps its inability to register a soteriological critique of conceptual reification—or as evidence that the original Madhyamaka position was less philosophically determinate than standard commentaries suggest. Keep your response formal and precise; I do not need bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, since the task is strictly conceptual.
