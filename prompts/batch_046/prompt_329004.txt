Two autonomous assessors, A and B, each hold graded sovereignty over one interpretive axis: A controls the weight assigned to literal accuracy versus contextual charity, and B controls the weight assigned to concision versus completeness. Because these axes intersect in the assessment of any single output, the assessors’ personal spheres overlap non-trivially. I argue that if collective evaluation must be Pareto-efficient across both assessors and preferences are unrestricted over the four-dimensional space of axis weights, then even a probabilistic weakening of minimal liberalism—where sovereignty is expressed as continuous influence rather than a binary veto—fails to dissolve the impossibility. Instead, the contradiction migrates into the normalization conditions of the bargaining space. I further ask whether substituting a Kalai-Smorodinsky bargaining solution for social-welfare aggregation resolves the tension or merely disguises it by implicitly privileging one assessor’s utopia point.
