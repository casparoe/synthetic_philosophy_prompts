Construct a biological thought experiment that isolates etiological and causal-role accounts of proper function by engineering a divergence that inverts the standard exaptation narrative: the trait should actively perform the activity for which it was recently selected, yet that activity should currently contribute nothing to the organism's capacities because the relevant metabolic pathway or substrate has been replaced by a different mechanism; simultaneously, the trait should perform a different, never-selected side-effect that is now indispensable to a system-level capacity. Use a realistic organism—such as a polyploid plant or an animal with a documented history of gene duplication or symbiotic integration, not a fictional species—present the scenario in a single paragraph, and specify the evolutionary mechanism precisely, for example neofunctionalization after whole-genome duplication followed by loss of the ancestral pathway, rather than invoking generic mutation or degeneration. After the scenario, state the verdict of the etiological account (function as selected effect) and the causal-role account (function as contribution to system capacity), showing the explicit reasoning for each; identify the exact structural feature that forces the split; explain what happens to the divergence if that evolutionary mechanism is removed; and articulate the strongest reply available to a defender of the causal-role account, particularly one that attacks your choice of system boundary rather than denying the evolutionary history. Do not provide a bibliography, citation, or reading list.
