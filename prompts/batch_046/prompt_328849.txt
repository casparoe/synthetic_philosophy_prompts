I was sitting with two friends from my philosophy of physics study group after seminar, and they got kind of stuck in the weeds about Newton's bucket. (1) The first said the classic argument is decisive: the water climbs the sides of the rotating bucket even when the bucket and water are initially at rest relative to eachother, so the motion can't be explained by relation to other bodies and must be motion relative to absolute space. (2) The second said that's not so fast -- Mach's principle gives the relationist a reply: the curvature comes from rotation relative to all the distant stars and matter, and if the universe were empty except for the bucket there'd be no inertia at all. (3) The first came back with something I hadn't heard before: in GR you can have a massive rotating shell with empty space inside, and inside that shell you get frame-dragging effects that look just like bucket curvature, but the "rotation" is carried by the spacetime metric itself, not by bodies inside. So she argued this shows the effect is still relative to an independent spacetime substance that the shell distorts. (4) The second countered that in GR the metric isn't a container -- it's dynamical and determined by the matter distribution, so calling the metric "substantival" is just relabeling the relational structure of the shell. Plus gravitational waves can travel through vacuum, but that's just perturbations of a field defined over the whole, not proof of an independent space. (5) The first said if spacetime has its own degrees of freedom and can carry energy and momentum, then it behaves like a substance regardless of whether matter is present, which undermines pure relationism. (6) The second said but those vacuum solutions are idealizations, and without boundary conditions or matter there's no fact of the matter about what's "really" rotating.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the six moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, briefly justify each rating, and say who is closer to right overall and what both are missing. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
