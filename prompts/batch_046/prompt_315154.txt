Write a dialogue between Sam and Riley about whether a statue and the clay it's made of are one thing or two. Both should be sharp and actually answer each other - no strawmen, no talking past.

Sam starts with the classic squash-test: if you crush the statue into a ball, the statue is gone but the clay is still sitting there, so they can't be identical. Riley pushes back that this is just describing the same event with two different predicates, like saying the party was cancelled and the wedding was cancelled when it's literally the same happening.

Then Sam brings up a weird conveyor-belt case he made up. A sculptor molds a statue from clay, but the workbench has a continuous feed: fresh synthetic polymer comes in from the left, gets shaped into the statue form, and an equal mass of old clay is pushed out the right and immediately stacked into bricks. After a year every original molecule has been replaced by polymer, but the statue-shape never broke. Meanwhile those expelled bricks got arranged into a wall that accidentally has the exact same outline.

Sam asks: at the end, is there a statue made of polymer? Is there also a statue made of bricks? During the middle, half the left-stream object is original clay and half is polymer, while some of the expelled clay is already in the wall arranged in statue-form. So is the statue split across both locations? Is thier one object spread over two piles?

Riley tries to save the one-object view by saying the statue tracks the causal-artistic process, not the materal. The left stream is the statue because the artist kept shaping it; the wall is just an accidental duplicate. Sam asks why artistic intention should do metaphysical work - Riley is basically saying mind determines matter. Riley admits it's messy but argues that without intention you can't tell a statue from a rockslide that happens to look like David. Sam replies that now she's smuggling psychology into ontology just to protect her intuition.

They should end without resolving, but with both seeing the problem: if you follow the material you get weird splits and duplicates, if you follow the form or intention you lose the idea that statues are made of stuff. Maybe toss in that Riley thinks constitution might come in degrees, and Sam thinks that's just giving up.

Keep it casual, like they're talking over coffee, and feel free to include a typo or awkward phrasing.
