I'm working on notes for a short essay about chaos, determinism, and what I want to call "explanatory leakage." The familiar setup is Lorenz-style: deterministic micro-laws generate macro-behavior so sensitive to initial conditions that prediction collapses. The greedy reductionist response is that this is merely practical—if you could compute the parts with perfect precision, the macro-state would be fixed. I want to resist that by arguing the failure isn't computational but architectural. Macro-predicates like "a severe storm system forms in this basin by Tuesday" don't correspond to stable regions in the micro-state space; their conditions are defined through statistical geometry and boundary constructions that live at the macro-level. So determinism at the bottom doesn't "reach up" via a crane—it leaks out. The unpredictability is therefore built into the explanatory structure, not just a mask over hidden determinism. I'm concerned this collapses into either (a) reheated instrumentalism about macro-science, or (b) a disguised skyhook that grants macro-patterns causal autonomy incompatible with physicalism. For a broadly rationalist, physicalist framework—no spooky emergence—can I consistently claim that determinism fails to propagate upward without treating macro-laws as genuinely autonomous? And is "explanatory leakage" doing any real work beyond familiar anti-reductionist moves like Dennett's real patterns?
