i've been trying to pin down the diffrence between echo chambers and what i'm calling credal canyons -- bubbles filter dissent out before it reaches you, canyons let it in but have a shared hermeneutic schema that remaps it into supporting evidence. i built this argument that both destroy justification and now i'm stuck because it makes science look broken.

1. If every belief inside an information system is shielded from its potential falsifiers, then none of those beliefs is justified.
2. In an epistemic bubble, dissent never reaches members, so every belief is shielded.
3. So in a bubble, every belief is unjustified.
4. In a credal canyon, dissent reaches members but is immediately interpreted through the group's schema, turning counter-evidence into confirming data.
5. So in a canyon, every belief is shielded from actual falsification, just through reinterpretation rather than exclusion.
6. Therefore every belief in a canyon is unjustified too.
7. Scientific paradigms are basically canyons: anomalous results get handled with auxilliary hypotheses and framework-preserving moves.
8. So every belief in science is unjustified.
9. That's absurd -- i don't want to give up that bubbles and canyons are structurally diffrent, and i especially don't want to give up that science isn't globally unjustified. but if the flaw is in (1) i lose the intuition that insulation is epistemically toxic. if it's in (5) i need to explain why reinterpretation isn't shielding, but then what's the real distinction? where's the slip, and what does fixing it cost? if i have to choose i'd rather keep (1) and (7) but drop whatever makes science fall out.
