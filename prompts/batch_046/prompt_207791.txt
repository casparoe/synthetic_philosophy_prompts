I'm preparing a long-form feature on circularity in scientific justification, and to avoid publishing a framework that collapses on inspection I need an internal consistency audit before I commit to print. Here is my current set of epistemic commitments.

First, I hold a modest foundationalism about basic observation: judgments such as a particular detector registering an anomalous spike can be justified directly, without deriving that justification from a prior theoretical account of the detector's reliability. Second, I maintain that the reliability of novel instruments—especially those probing regimes where no fully independent check exists—is necessarily established empirically, typically through calibration loops in which preliminary outputs guide parameter adjustments. Third, I regard these loops as rationally permissible in practice: using an instrument's outputs to refine its calibration does not automatically vitiate the knowledge subsequently produced, provided the process converges and is eventually anchored in benchmarks outside the loop. Fourth, despite this tolerance for practice, I take the easy-knowledge problem seriously: if the sole ground for believing a source is reliable is that it has already yielded outputs I independently accepted, then I have not genuinely earned a reliability claim, and knowledge built purely on that circular base is defective. Fifth, I think reflective equilibrium is the correct method for resolving conflicts between particular epistemic judgments and general methodological principles, and I do not exempt basic observational beliefs from potential revision in that process. Sixth, I reject global skepticism about science: I believe we possess substantial knowledge about entities and processes far removed from direct observation, despite the calibration dependencies in my second and third commitments.

In addition, I distinguish diachronic bootstrapping—iterative calibration across time, where later stages are constrained by earlier independent checks—from synchronic bootstrapping, in which a single inferential step uses an output to license the very premise that generated it. I treat the former as generally legitimate and the latter as usually illegitimate.

Please audit this set for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision for each real tension?
