After a long argument with a friend about predictive processing and the free energy principle, I am struck by how little either of us moved. We both accept that neural dynamics can be formalized as minimizing variational free energy within a Markov blanket, that hierarchical prediction errors account for phenomena like repetition suppression, and that the mathematics is internally consistent. My friend—the defender—insists that this is not mere redescription but the correct first principle of all adaptive systems, explaining why brains have the particular architectures they do; she treats it as a variational constraint on possible dynamics, analogous to least action in physics. I—the skeptic—counter that every successful prediction was already accommodated by standard hierarchical Bayesian models or by homeostatic regulation, and that because any adaptive system can be recast in these terms post hoc, the framework adds no distinct empirical content. My working hypothesis is that we are not really disagreeing about data, but sliding past each other on the nature of explanation: she offers formal unification, I demand a mechanistic account that distinguishes this framework from its competitors in a specific circuit. Yet I also suspect a hidden conceptual equivocation is doing work—between information-theoretic free energy and thermodynamic free energy, and between Markov blankets as observer-dependent constructions versus ontologically real boundaries. Is this stall substantive, verbal, or a case of two questions—one about formal constraints, one about causal mechanisms—being forced under one name? Is there an actual empirical disagreement in disguise about whether the blanket is epistemically imposed or physically real? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a formal result showing the principle entails a prediction no standard Bayesian model can make; an experiment that distinguishes free-energy minimization from predictive coding in a specific pathway; a conceptual distinction that dissolves the question; or is no progress expected because the framework is designed to absorb all counterevidence?
