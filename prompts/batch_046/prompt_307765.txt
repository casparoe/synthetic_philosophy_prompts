(4) If present values determine the selection, then we never actually encounter the past as genuinely other; we just project our current norms onto it and can't seperate them.
(5) So critical history, which is supposed to show that present beliefs are contingent historical accidents, can't actually do that—because the "errors" it identifies are only errors relative to our present values, not objectively wrong.
(6) Therefore either history is shaped by life (steps 1-3) and loses all critical distance, or it keeps critical distance but then fails to serve present life.
(7) But Nietzsche clearly wants both: he wants critical history to serve life by dissolving harmful past commitments.
(8) So his view is self-undermining: the conditions that make history useful destroy its critical power, and if thats true then ALL historical inquiry is just presentist ideology.
