Draft the joint statement that scholars defending the situational-explanation camp associated with Christopher Browning’s study of Reserve Police Battalion 101 and scholars defending the cultural-dispositional camp associated with Daniel Goldhagen’s study of ordinary German perpetrators could both sign after a sustained adversarial collaboration. The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims: about the basic historical record of Battalion 101 (the scale of participation, the absence of severe sanctions for refusal, the mix of explicit orders and improvised violence); about the relevant psychological mechanisms (peer conformity, bureaucratic distancing, incremental commitment, the role of ideology in framing targets); about what requires explanation (the variance in individual refusal, the correlation—or lack thereof—between prior expressed antisemitism and shooting behavior, the durability of perpetrator narratives after 1945); and about the evidentiary standards both sides accept (the weight of contemporaneous versus retrospective testimony, the relevance of comparative cases of non-German police units). Follow this with a disagreements table that states each camp’s residual position in wording that camp itself would accept, separating empirical disputes from merely verbal ones, and explicitly flagging any disagreement that dissolves upon precisification. Then list the cruxes, including at least: (i) whether eliminationist antisemitism was motivationally sufficient for the majority of shooters or only culturally present as a permissive background; (ii) the counterfactual substitution problem—whether a randomly drawn cohort of German males of comparable age and region, inserted into the same unit dynamics without its specific peer history, would have yielded comparable participation rates; and (iii) the motivational transparency problem—whether perpetrators’ retrospective justifications and prior absence of expressed hatred should be treated as epistemic access to their motives or as post-hoc rationalizations shaped by situational pressure and social-desirability bias. For each crux, identify a concrete case, argument, or evidential pattern both sides agree bears on it, and record each side’s explicit prediction about what that evidence shows or would show. The statement should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations, and it should avoid reducing either position to caricature.
