I've been arguing with my roommate about whether moral dilemmas are actually real or just hard choices that feel bad afterwards. I don't have any philosophy training, just been thinking about it on my own, and I came up with these examples to test my gut reactions. Case one: a surgeon has one operating room and two patients will die without immediate surgery, but she can only save one, so she picks the younger one. I say this is awful but NOT a real dilemma - she clearly did the right thing by saving someone, and there's no leftover wrongness hanging over her. Case two: you sincerely promise your kid you'll be at their school play, then your boss calls and says if you don't come in tonight the warehouse will burn and people could get hurt. You go to work. Again, I don't think this is a real dilemma - the promise was just unlucky, not still broken in some deep way once the emergency is clear.

Case three: you make a real, heartfelt promise to two different friends that you'll be the sole witness at their weddings, then you find out both are on the exact same Saturday at the exact same time in different parts of town. You can only be at one. Here I think YES, this is a genuine dilemma - whichever wedding you miss, you actually broke a promise that still counts against you, and you probably still owe that friend a serious apology. Case four: you sign an NDA at your job, then you discover the company is dumping poison in the river. You leak the documents to the news. I say this is NOT a dilemma because the NDA wasn't a real moral promise to begin with, so there's really only one duty. But case five feels wierd: you're a firefighter and your captain orders you to stay back from a burning building because the floor is about to collapse, but you can hear a kid crying inside. You disobey, save the kid, but now you've violated a direct order and put your crew at risk. This feels like case three to me - a real dilemma - but I can't figure out why it's like case three and not like case two, since in both situations you have a promise or order versus saving someone.

My half-baked theory is that maybe it's only a genuine dilemma when both options leave behind a kind of moral leftover - like you still wronged the missed friend, or you still wronged your captain - but I'm not sure if that actually holds up or if I'm just making excuses for my inconsistent gut. So is there one principle that captures why I call case three and five real dilemmas but not one, two, or four? Or do I just have contradictory instincts?
