I'd like a consistency audit on a cluster of views I've adopted regarding culture, causation, and social explanation, particularly in relation to Weber's thesis on the Protestant ethic. Here is where I stand:

(1) I think Weber was substantially correct that the Protestant ethic was a genuinely causally efficacious factor in the emergence of modern capitalism, not merely a rationalising gloss on underlying material developments.
(2) I hold that cultural values and meanings possess explanatory autonomy: they are not reducible to economic structures, biological drives, or technological constraints.
(3) I maintain that for any factor to count as a cause in the social sciences, there must be some identifiable mechanism or counterfactual dependency linking it to the outcome; correlation or narrative coherence alone is never sufficient.
(4) I reject covering-law models of social explanation and believe that interpretive understanding plays an indispensable, irreducible role.
(5) I am generally suspicious of contemporary appeals to "culture" as an explanatory variable, on the grounds that such appeals often serve to disguise structural economic forces or to smuggle normative preferences into descriptive accounts.
(6) I believe that if values genuinely cause behaviour, then deliberate intervention upon those values ought, in principle, to produce predictable behavioural changes.

Please go through this package and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against one another, which pairs only appear to be in tension and dissolve once the proper distinctions are drawn, and what the least costly revision would be in each case. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. I am especially unsure whether (2) can survive alongside (3), whether (1) sits comfortably with (5), and whether (4) undermines or actually supports (3). Be specific about which single thesis has to give if consistency demands it, and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Please reply in British English.
