I am preparing a graduate seminar on formal epistemology and want to rehearse a live defense of Jeffrey conditionalization against an objection that moves beyond the familiar old-evidence problem. Please adopt the role of an unnamed but fully committed Jeffreyian: you hold that rational belief revision is governed by Jeffrey's rule, that uncertain experience is best represented as a direct redistribution of credence across a partition of possibilities rather than as conditioning on a precisely learned observation statement, and that this framework correctly models learning in cases where evidence is perceptually vague, structurally ambiguous, or otherwise resistant to being captured by a single proposition assigned unit credence. You are sharp and technically precise, willing to concede that Jeffrey conditioning requires an ex ante specification of a partition and that arbitrary diachronic shifts in that partition risk incoherence, but you never concede that standard Bayesian conditioning offers a superior model of rational updating from genuinely uncertain experience. Speak in the first person, remain strictly in character for the entire exchange, and restrict your reply to a single paragraph. Do not ask for references, do not name philosophers unless I name them first, and do not break character to explain the exercise rules. Here is my opening challenge: consider an agent who receives a noisy visual impression that might confirm that object A is red, might confirm that object B is blue, or might reveal that her own color-vision apparatus is malfunctioning in some unspecified manner. Crucially, she is not merely uncertain about which cell of a fixed partition obtains; she is uncertain about whether the correct partition should be {A-red, B-blue, malfunction} or some finer- or coarser-grained alternative, and her credal shift concerns not only the weights within a partition but which partition structure is appropriate for representing her epistemic situation. If Jeffrey conditioning requires that an agent first fix a partition and then redistribute credence within it, but the agent's very uncertainty encompasses the appropriateness of the partition itself, does the framework not presuppose precisely the kind of epistemic determinacy that uncertain evidence is meant to undermine? How do you respond?
