I've been trying to read The Truman Show thru Wittgenstein's hinge epistemology from On Certainty, and I'm kind of stuck on whether the film actually gets the philosophy right or just borrows the vibe. Truman's world runs on these background certainties that stand fast for him -- the sky is a dome, the neighbours loop, the radio narrates his life -- and when the stage light falls or he sees the same people on loop, it's like a hinge starts wobbling. Wittgenstein says hinges are'nt ordinary beliefs you can doubt one by one; they're the frame work within which doubt makes sense at all. So is Truman actually doubting a hinge when he suspects the world is fake, or is he just fixing an anomaly inside the frame? And here's the part that feels niche to me: some recent talk about hinge shifts says changing your hinges is'nt like rejecting a claim, it's more like a grammar change -- the rules for what counts as evidence shift, so there's no rational bridge from inside to outside. Does Truman's escape dramatize that kind of shift, or does the film cheat by making his old world obviuosly false? I want a committed answer, not a both-sides thing: take a definite position on whether Truman gains knowledge or just swaps hinge-frames, and point to the specific scenes where the film either illuminates Wittgenstein or distorts him for dramatic effect. I'm writing this for a philosophy of mind discussion group and need to know where to push back if someone says it's just the brain-in-a-vat with better lighting.
