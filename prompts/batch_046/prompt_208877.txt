I am preparing the final exam for an upper-level seminar in political philosophy focused on Hannah Arendt’s essays on lying and factual truth in politics. The exam lasts 90 minutes; this essay is worth 30 marks and should run about 1,000 to 1,200 words. I want one essay question that students cannot answer by summarizing Arendt’s well-known claim that lying destroys the common world. Instead, it must force them to navigate her technical distinctions—between factual truth, rational truth, and opinion; between the traditional lie that conceals a specific fact and the modern image-based lie that replaces the entire framework of reality; between legitimate secrecy and destructive defactualization; and between the liar who retains a grip on reality and the self-deceived actor who has lost it.

The question should present the following synthesized claim for critical assessment: When a democratic government systematically classifies evidence of its own policy failures—not to protect legitimate security interests, but to sustain a unified public narrative about strategic success—it is practicing Arendt’s modern form of political lying rather than mere secrecy. Yet Arendt also describes factual truth as coercive, lying outside the realm of opinion and agreement. Therefore she cannot consistently condemn this government practice without also condemning any administration that insists on objective economic or statistical data that contradicts popular belief, which suggests her framework collapses into an incoherent hostility to all factual constraints on politics.

Please write:
1. The exam question itself, framed so that students must (a) reconstruct Arendt’s distinctions among truth-types and explain why factual truth is both anti-political and indispensable for politics; (b) explain how traditional lying, modern image-making, and legitimate secrecy differ in her account, with particular attention to the roles of self-deception and the shared world; (c) pinpoint the specific misstep in the synthesized claim about coerciveness—showing why Arendt’s view that factual truth is coercive does not prevent her from distinguishing destructive narrative-management from legitimate governance that respects reality; (d) apply her framework to the government-classification scenario and state what resources she offers for resisting such lying without abandoning politics for pure fact.

2. A model answer of approximately 700 words that demonstrates the required moves with precise distinctions and correct application.

3. A marking scheme that assigns marks out of 30, specifies exactly which conceptual moves earn full credit (for example, correct separation of rational and factual truth, correct identification that the modern lie targets the framework of reality rather than discrete facts, correct diagnosis of the error regarding coerciveness, and correct use of the self-deception criterion), lists the specific mistakes that should cost marks (for example, conflating secrecy with lying, treating Arendt’s defense of factual truth as a defense of rational truth, missing the distinction between the liar and the self-deceived, or failing to see that coerciveness concerns agreement rather than governance legitimacy), and distinguishes an A-range answer, in which all four required moves are present with no significant conflation, from a B-range answer, which offers a solid reconstruction of Arendt’s view but misses the crux on coerciveness or misapplies the modern versus traditional distinction.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests in the exam prompt. Do not put any words in quotation marks that come from Arendt’s actual texts unless you have fetched and copied them exactly from a source; otherwise paraphrase her positions without quotation marks.
