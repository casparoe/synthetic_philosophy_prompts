A colleague of mine in moral philosophy has offered her intuitive judgements on several paired-world thought experiments in population ethics, and I should like to test the consistency of her outlook against one further case. Her verdicts so far are these:

(i) A world containing exactly one million people, each with a well-being level of 90, is clearly better than an otherwise identical world in which those same people each have a well-being level of 40.

(ii) That world of one million people at well-being 90 is far better than a world containing one hundred million people all at well-being 1, even though the latter contains a hundred times as many lives and far greater linearly summed well-being.

(iii) Adding one hundred people at well-being –30 to the world of one million at 90 makes it unambiguously worse, despite leaving the original million untouched.

(iv) She regards a world of one million people at well-being 90 and a world of two million people all at well-being 90 as genuinely comparable, but if pressed she tends to treat the larger world as not worse, though not clearly better either—she resists both the claim that it is twice as good and the claim that dilution has occurred.

The new comparison is between World A, which contains one million people at well-being 90, and World B, which contains those same one million people at well-being 90 together with two million further people all at well-being 85. Predict her verdict: does she judge A to be better, B to be better, or the two worlds to be on a par? Then identify the implicit aggregation rule she appears to be running—whether average utilitarianism, critical-level utilitarianism with a threshold near zero or near 80, a person-affecting asymmetry that treats the creation of flourishing lives as morally neutral, or perhaps a non-separative view in which the value of a cohort depends on relational density or distribution shape rather than on individual levels alone. Note that her earlier track record is jointly consistent with more than one of these frameworks, so set out the competing interpretations explicitly, explain what each predicts about World A versus World B, and say which interpretation you think best fits her judgements and why. Finally, propose one precise further paired comparison—specify the populations and well-being levels exactly—that would best settle the matter between the two leading candidates, and state what each of those candidates would predict in that test case.
