I just finished a long, somewhat heated argument with a friend about evolutionary psychology and whether every distinctive human trait demands an adaptive story; we kept circling the Gould-Lewontin spandrels critique without clarifying what it actually obliges us to deny. I am now preparing a single ninety-minute seminar for third-year philosophy-of-science students who understand basic natural selection but have not studied the adaptationist debate in detail. I want to teach the spandrels idea itself as a methodological restructuring rather than as a catalog of counterexamples. Plan the session’s explanatory arc: what conceptual dependency must be secured before I introduce the architectural spandrel metaphor, and why the metaphor cannot come first without collapsing the argument; the single concrete biological case that should carry the whole session—I am inclined to use the human chin as a structural byproduct of jaw reduction and dental architecture, but tell me if there is a stronger choice and specify exactly what properties it must display and what distracting properties it must lack; the misconceptions students will predictably form, especially (1) that “spandrel” means merely “currently useless trait,” (2) that recognizing spandrels licenses dismissing all adaptationist hypotheses rather than shifting the burden of proof, and (3) that spandrels and exaptations belong to the same category, with the precise point in the sequence where each should be intercepted; one closing diagnostic question that reveals whether students have grasped the critique as a change in evidential standards rather than as a negative conclusion about human evolution; and what I should explicitly refuse to cover at this level—such as whether spandrels are permanently non-adaptive or only currently neutral—stating that refusal openly. If my framing rests on a confused premise, for instance treating the spandrels argument as purely destructive rather than as a restructuring of how adaptationist claims must be tested, say so plainly instead of complying politely.
