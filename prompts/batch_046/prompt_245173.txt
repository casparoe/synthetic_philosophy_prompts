Imagine a three-year interdisciplinary program with ten senior scholars from geology, history, and political philosophy, tasked not with field measurement but with resolving the conceptual architecture of the Anthropocene as a historical period. Which three open questions about its start date and about who holds legitimate authority to declare an epochal boundary should they prioritize, in what order, and why? Focus especially on the tension between geochronological markers—the mid-twentieth-century radiogenic and plastic horizons versus earlier biotic-exchange signatures—and the normative problem of whether scientific bodies, affected communities, or future generations possess the standing to fix a boundary. For each question, defend its rank by stating the criteria used (upstream influence on climate historiography and policy, tractability for conceptual rather than empirical work, and dependency relations with other disputes), the trade-offs in ordering it higher or lower, and what kind of conceptual argument or institutional development over the next few years would cause you to reorder them. Deliver a defended agenda, not a survey or reading list.
