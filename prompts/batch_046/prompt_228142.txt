So I started going to this philophy meet-up after I retired from the lab (used to do cell biology, now I'm just confussed on purpose). Last week two regulars, Marcus and Elena, got into it about embbeded agency — you know, the idea that an agent is literally inside the world it's trying to model, not some ghost outside looking in. Marcus said this kills the whole idea of a rational agent having a complete self-model, because if you're embedded, any model of yourself is just another part of the system you're modeling, so you can never fully capture your own reasoning without leaving something out — like trying to weigh a scale while you're standing on it. Elena said that's overblown; we do hierarchical self-modeling all the time. I can think about my own thinking, then think about that second-level thought, and so on. She said that's exactly how people plan for the future or stick to diets. Marcus came back that those aren't true embeddings, they're just abstractions, and real embedding means there's no outside vantage point at all, not even a meta-level, because the meta-level is still inside the same system. Elena then said okay, but if there's truly no outside, then an embedded agent can never rationally precommit to anything — since the precommitment is just another internal state that the agent can model and then override. Marcus said that's actually a feature, not a bug; it explains why people break promises. They turned to me and I just nodded like I recieved it. Can you adjudicate move by move? Is Elena's hierarchical move a real answer to Marcus's scale problem, or is he right that meta-levels stay embedded? Is Marcus right that embeddedness undermines precommitment, or does Elena have a point that we can still bind ourselves? And what are both of them missing, if anything?
