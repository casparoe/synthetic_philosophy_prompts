Okay so I've been thinking about when automation kills the meaning of work vs when it just shifts it. Here are my gut calls on some cases, and I want to know if there's actually a principle here or if I'm just making excuses. Give me your bottom line up front in the first sentence, then the reasoning — don't restate the whole question and don't start with "sure, here's an analysis."

Case 1: A machinist spends 20 years learning to feel metal fatigue by hand, then the factory buys sensors that do it better. He says his work is now "fake" — he's just babysitting screens. Verdict: he's right, the meaning is gutted, because the skill that connected him to the material is gone.

Case 2: A radiologist uses AI to flag tumors. She says her job is now more meaningful — she spends her energy on the hard ambiguous cases instead of scanning thousands of clean images. Verdict: she's right too, the automation cleared away the drudgery and left the judgment.

Case 3: A home-care nurse is replaced by a monitoring bracelet that alerts family members. But she's kept on as a "relationship coordinator" who only shows up when the algorithm detects emotional distress. She feels like a prop. Verdict: this feels different from case 2 even though the structure is the same — she was reduced to a gap-filler for the machine's blind spots, and that feels degrading rather then elevating.

Case 4: There's this concept I ran into in some niche labor-philosophy thread — the "custodial loop" — where work is meaningful only when you're maintaining something that can't maintain itself without your direct judgment, not just filling in for what tech can't do yet. By that standard, the radiologist in case 2 is still inside the loop (she judges ambiguity), but the nurse in case 3 is outside it (she's just the human face on an automated system). Verdict: I kind of buy this, but then...

Case 5: A composer uses AI to generate every other measure, then hand-crafts only the transitions between them. He says the meaning lives in the transitions. But if the AI eventually handles transitions too, does the meaning vanish, or does it migrate to the meta-choice of when to invoke the AI? Verdict: I genuinly don't know, and it feels like it should line up with case 1 or case 2 but I can't tell which.

So is there a coherent principle — maybe something like the custodial loop — that saves all five verdicts? Or do I have to drop one? And what does it predict about someone who trains the AI that eventually replaces thier own job — is that inside the loop, outside it, or something weirder?
