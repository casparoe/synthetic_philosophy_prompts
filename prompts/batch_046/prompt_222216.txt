Design a near-future, realistic scenario—no exotic physics—featuring an AI system (call it System K) that participates in a repeated social resource-allocation game with human partners. Engineer its architecture precisely: System K maintains an internal representational vector tracking partner deception, but this vector updates solely through gradient descent on counterfactual rollouts of partner behavior, with no homeostatic drive, no survival-linked reward signal, and no sensory transduction analogous to biological perception. Its observed behavior—systematically withholding resources from one partner—is optimally predicted only by attributing to it the belief that that partner is deceptive, paired with an instrumental preference for exclusion.

Then demonstrate that your case satisfies these specifications:
(i) Under Dennett's intentional stance, attributing that specific belief-desire pair is structurally mandatory for predicting System K's behavior across a range of counterfactual interventions, not merely heuristically convenient.
(ii) Under a mechanist or eliminativist account that requires biological embodiment or first-person phenomenological anchoring for genuine belief, the attribution must fail—specifically because the representational state lacks causal-functional coupling to homeostasis or affect.
(iii) There is exactly one isolatable architectural feature generating the disagreement: the combination of purely counterfactual gradient updating with zero homeostatic or reward coupling.

Present the scenario in one paragraph. Then show the reasoning for (i) and (ii), identify the feature that does the work, and explain whether removing it—for instance, by adding a survival-linked reward—collapses the case back into standard intentional-stance agreement. Finally, take a committed position: state definitively whether System K genuinely believes the partner is deceptive, give your reasons, and specify exactly how a defender of the opposing view you reject would attack your verdict.
