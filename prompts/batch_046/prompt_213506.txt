Compare Hofstadter’s Le Ton beau de Marot principle—that a translator should preserve rhyme, meter, and phonetic texture even when literal meaning must bend—with the opposing view that formal constraints are ornamental and that translation’s core duty is to preserve the exact inferential structure of the source, especially when the text carries philosophical argument. Apply both to a concrete case: imagine a short didactic poem in rhymed couplets that lays out a contingency-version cosmological argument, running from the premise of contingency through an inference to a necessary being to a final conclusion. Would forcing that argument into rhymed English couplets preserve its reasoning or distort the logical steps? Which approach gives the reader more of what actually matters? Defend your answer, be explicit about what each side gets right, and write in American English.
