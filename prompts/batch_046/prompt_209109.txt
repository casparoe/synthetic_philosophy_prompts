I serve on the funding review board for the university Center for Subjective Well-Being. We must decide whether to approve a pilot program that adopts a strictly tranquilist metric for allocating mental-health resources: well-being is measured solely by the absence of craving and suffering, with zero weight on positive affect, purpose, or flourishing. The proposal distinguishes first-order tranquility (no present distress) from second-order tranquility (no craving even for future states of calm, including no reflexive attachment to tranquility itself). Under this framework, highly functional students with strong ambition but low suffering might be deprioritized, while students in passive, low-arousal, low-desire states could be classified as maximally well. Draft a formal memo to the board recommending whether to approve the pilot, with an actionable bottom line, concrete evaluation criteria, and an honest treatment of the strongest argument against your position. Write the memo in American English.
