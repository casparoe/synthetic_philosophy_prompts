I am presenting a workshop paper defending the view that Nozick's tracking conditions, when applied consistently to indexical and self-locating beliefs, generate not merely the familiar isolated closure failures but a structurally ordered pattern of closure failure across nearby modal possibilities that share an identical evidential profile. Specifically, if a subject S tracks that p in world w via method M, and p deductively entails an indexical claim q about S's own location in logical space, then in worlds where p is false but M produces the same internal evidential state, S fails to track q—even though q is entailed by p in w—because satisfying the sensitivity condition for q requires responsiveness to modal neighbors that are evidential twins but not logical duplicates. I treat this as a feature of tracking theory: it forces us to reject unrestricted epistemic closure specifically for self-locating knowledge, rather than rescuing closure by imposing ad hoc restrictions on the modal domain of sensitivity.

The audience is a specialized philosophy-of-knowledge workshop of about fifteen people. The majority are closure-friendly epistemologists—mostly reliabilists and some internalists—who regard closure as holding for ordinary deduction and treat Nozick-style counterexamples as aberrations to be quarantined rather than systematized. Two are modal logicians who understand the formal structure of tracking conditions precisely; they are inclined to preserve closure by restricting the range of worlds over which sensitivity must hold. One is a philosopher of language specializing in indexicals, who suspects that self-locating belief operates under distinct epistemic norms and may therefore resist being subsumed under standard tracking conditions at all.
