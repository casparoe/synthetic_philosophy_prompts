(2) These same conditions hold within a single agent across time. I have common knowledge that my future self will be rational, and I know exactly when my planning horizon ends—whether at retirement, at the expiration of a contract, or at death.

(3) So backward induction should apply diachronically: if at the final stage N I will rationally take the immediate payoff, then at stage N-1 I should anticipate that and take it now; repeating this collapses every multi-period plan to immediate defection at stage 1.

(5) Therefore a fully rational agent should never initiate such plans; she should always defect immediately.

(7) Hence either backward induction is invalid, or rationality is incompatible with all intertemporal cooperation—which is absurd.
