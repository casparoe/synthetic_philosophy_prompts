I sit on the investment ethics board for our university's pilot worker-ownership program, and we have to vote Thursday on whether to approve a low-interest loan that would convert the campus dining contract into a worker cooperative. The proposal includes a strange clause—each worker gets a rotating 48-hour veto over hiring or pricing, but only once per quarter—to block managerial overreach without giving anyone permanent authority, which the applicants claim is in line with Mill's concern about foreman tyranny in associationist setups.

Half the board says Mill's late socialism supports exactly this kind of voluntary cooperation with built-in checks, and that property rights are only instrumental to liberty so the fiduciary risk is overstated. The thier half says it's a fiduciery nightmare: Mill also insisted that cooperative management needs disciplined incentives, not procedural gimmicks, and our endowment shouldn't fund governance experiments that destroy accountability. I need to circulate a memo by Wednesday recommending approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions—with a concrete timeline and performance metrics if we go ahead. It should take the accountability objection seriously rather than dismissing it as capitalist reflex, and please don't quote Mill verbatim or rely on whether he wrote those essays before or after Harriet Taylor died, since I'm not sure about the editions or chronology.
