I'm writing a feature on whether Daniel Dennett's framework of the physical, design, and intentional stances remains conceptually stable when applied to systems that blur these categories—specifically, emergent predictive models in climate science and interpretive practices around large language models. Rather than asking for a general assessment, I want a prioritized agenda: which open conceptual questions or research directions in this space actually deserve attention from philosophers and conceptual researchers, and in what order?

I'm weighing at least three lines. First, whether "real patterns" are stance-independent or only exist relative to the predictive goals that make them detectable—an upstream question because if patterns are stance-relative, the framework may be more pragmatic ontology than explanatory taxonomy. Second, whether applying the intentional stance to artificial systems generates what I will call "stance debt": explanatory obligations that cannot be discharged at the design or physical level without circular reasoning, which would show the stances are not genuinely independent. Third, whether the design stance is intermediate in a robust sense, or collapses into physical reduction on one side and intentional projection on the other whenever functional organization is emergent rather than engineered.

Of these, and any others you judge more central, which should come first? Defend the ordering with reference to tractability for non-empirical researchers, dependency—how many other questions turn on this one—and what kind of conceptual result in the near term would move a question up or down the list. I also want an honest assessment of which problems in this neighborhood are overrated: perhaps because they are empirically rather than conceptually settled, or because resolving them would not change how the framework is applied. The deliverable is not a literature survey but a defended agenda, calibrated for someone deciding where limited conceptual resources should go.
