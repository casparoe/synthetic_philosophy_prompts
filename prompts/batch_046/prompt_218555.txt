I've been toying with a specifc version of origin essentialism: not just that you couldn't have come from different parents, but that your essential origin is the actual fertilisation event involving that specific sperm and egg, and that this holds even if we imagine the embryo splitting or being cloned later on. So the claim is that being originated from that pairing is necessary for you, regardless of downstream causal divergence. What would actually count against this? I want the defeaters sorted by how damaging they'd be — maybe cases where psychological continuity clearly survives a change in biological origin, like brain-transplant scenarios, or formal results about identity over time that don't privilege origins. Which of these would a defender of this view honestly concede as a real problem, and which can they just absorb by saying 'well, that's a different kind of persistence'? Also, has this position been qualified into unfalsifiability — if every counterexample gets dismissed as 'not the right kind of origin change' — and is that a bug or just how essentialist claims work? Be honest about whether it's well-insulated, and if so whether that's embarassing for the view.
