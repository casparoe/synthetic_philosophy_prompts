Compare the Buddhist claim that cetana (intention) is the real heart of kamma with a made-up view I’ll call mental-state consequentialism: it keeps the Buddhist focus on reducing dukkha for all beings but judges every action purely by its actual causal results, not by the agent’s inner state. So it’s like consequentialism that’s stolen Buddhism’s obsession with mental purity but thrown away the idea that a clean mind makes an act good regardless of outcome. Which of these two actualy gives a better reason why a bodhisattva should refuse to lie to a tyrant even when the lie saves thousands?

Then run both through this edge case I’ve been chewing on: a bodhisattva knows that lying to a dictator stops a massacre, but the lie plants a subtle kilesa that delays her awakening, which means she can’t save as many beings over the long haul. Use upaya, sila, and the lokiya / lokuttara distinction — actually use them, don’t just list them — to show whether Buddhism can praise the lie without turning into pure consequentialism, or condemn it without turning into pure rule-following. I’m especialy interested in whether Buddhism’s long-range karmic calculus makes it closer to consequentialism than people admit, just with extra steps and more guilt.
