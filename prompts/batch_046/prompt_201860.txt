Consider a small interdisciplinary research group—philosophy, legal theory, and cognitive science—funded for three years with modest resources, tasked not with exegesis but with advancing the conceptual architecture of Strawson’s reactive-attitudes framework and its implications for moral responsibility. I want you to advise them on which open questions and lines of work deserve their limited effort, defended by explicit criteria rather than presented as a survey.

Focus your prioritization on three live problem clusters that extend beyond standard textbook debates. First, the collective-agency problem: can reactive attitudes such as resentment or moral anger be genuinely directed at institutional agents—states, corporations, bureaucracies—without being reducible to attitudes about their individual members, and if so, what does this require of Strawson’s distinction between the participant and objective stances? Second, the attenuation problem: does structural distance—bureaucratic layering, temporal delay, causal opacity—weaken reactive attitudes in a graded, continuous fashion that Strawson’s binary framework cannot accommodate, and would accommodating such gradation force an abandonment of his naturalness thesis about the inescapability of the participant perspective? Third, the criminal-justice implication problem: if one takes seriously Strawson’s argument against libertarian free will, does this genuinely undermine retributivist justifications for punishment in practice, or does it merely shift their ground, and which specific practices—blame attribution, sentencing severity, restorative protocols—would have to change?

For each cluster, rank them in terms of what should come first, second, and third for this specific group. Defend the ordering by specifying which questions are upstream of others, which are tractable through conceptual analysis and limited interdisciplinary collaboration within three years, and what empirical or philosophical resolution in one cluster would most alter the urgency or direction of the remaining two. Make your criteria explicit—conceptual importance to the framework, tractability, and downstream dependency—and show the trade-offs between them rather than treating them as aligned. Identify one cluster you would actively deprioritize, explaining precisely why its resolution, however interesting, would not change much for either the theory or its practical application. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; treat Strawson’s core framework—reactive attitudes, the participant and objective stances, and the critique of libertarian moral responsibility—as common knowledge, but treat the collective-agency extension and the attenuation thesis as underspecified problems that need conceptual construction rather than textual interpretation.
