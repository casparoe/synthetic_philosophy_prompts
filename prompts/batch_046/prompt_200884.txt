I've been trying to settle what actually counts as a biological function, testing my intuitions against concrete cases rather than abstract definitions. Here are my verdicts.

The human heart's circulation of blood is clearly a function. The heart's audible sounds, which physicians exploit for diagnosis, are not a biological function; they are only a causally useful byproduct. The mammalian malleus and incus now transmit sound vibrations, and I think auditory transmission is their present function, even though they evolved from jaw bones used for chewing. Bird feathers were originally selected for insulation and now primarily serve flight and display; I think flight and display are functions, but insulation is no longer one, despite feathers still retaining heat. The human appendix occasionally participates in gut immune interactions, yet I judge it to have no biological function—it is vestigial, and those interactions are mere causal effects. In a synthetic-biology system, researchers inserted a heterologous mevalonate pathway into yeast so that the cells produce a specific terpene; the pathway is metabolically integrated and was deliberately selected for that output. I think producing the terpene is a genuine function, even though the pathway has no evolutionary history outside the laboratory. In a long-term laboratory-evolution experiment, a non-coding RNA that began as transcriptional noise was repeatedly selected over hundreds of generations because it stabilizes a heat-shock mRNA; it is now essential for survival at high temperature, though no wild-type organism ever possessed it. Here I am genuinely torn: part of me wants to grant it a regulatory function because selection fixed it, but part of me resists because the selection was artificial and the trait never existed in nature.

Is there a single principle—etiological, causal-role, or a disciplined hybrid—that delivers all seven verdicts? If not, which verdict should be overturned in the name of simplicity: my denial of function to the appendix's immune interactions, my acceptance of function for the synthetic pathway, or my hesitation about the lab-evolved RNA? Please also predict what your preferred principle says about a case I have not listed: a gene-duplicated pseudogene in a wild plant that regulates a drought-response gene via antisense transcription, thereby boosting fitness, but was never directly selected for that regulatory role and has no engineered origin.

Take a definite position. State the principle precisely, map each clause to the case that motivates it, declare which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate, and defend that trade-off explicitly rather than laying out options and leaving the choice to me.
