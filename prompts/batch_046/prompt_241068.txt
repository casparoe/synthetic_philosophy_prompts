Thomson's lamp gets flipped on and off in intervals that halve each time, so by t=2 minutes it's done infinitely many toggles. Is it on? Off? People have been stuck on this since the 50s or whatever and nobody moves. One side says there's no definate answer because there's no last toggle, so parity never settles; the other says asking about the state "at" t=2 is malformed since the limit point isn't part of the sequence, meaning the lamp just lacks a state there rather than being undefined.

My hunch is the stall isn't about infinity per se but about whether physical properties need continuity at limit points. Are they treating the lamp's state as a mathematical limit of discrete steps, or as a physical property that needs a causal story running right up to the endpoint? If that's right, maybe it's partly verbal—"state at time t" means different things—and partly a metaphysical disagreement about how time and causation work at boundaries.

But I can't tell if there's a hidden formal question in there too. Is this just bedrock intuitions about completeness, or could a distinction—say between limit-behavior and endpoint-properties—or even a result about continuous dynamics actually break it?
