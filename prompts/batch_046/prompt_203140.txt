We were wondering if a poem could actually perform that nesting instead of just describing it. So could you write me a sestina that pretends to be a museum wall-label for an artwork that doesn't exist? The frame should be the label's physical presence in a gallery, the outer message should be the label describing a fictional painting about someone reading a wall-label, and the inner message should be about how meaning is always displaced one layer back. Choose six end-words — two that feel like physical frame stuff, two that feel like literal description, two that feel like hidden interpretation — and rotate them through the stanzas so that by the middle the same word is doing double or triple duty. The envoi needs to collapse all three levels into a single line where you can't tell if it's talking about glass, about reading, or about seeing through something. Make it sound like actual institutional wall-text, but accurate enough that someone who remembers the frame/outer/inner distinction can map exactly which stanza is operating at which level.
