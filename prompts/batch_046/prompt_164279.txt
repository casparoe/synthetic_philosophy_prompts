She maintains that “the same mechanism” ought not to be identified with shared weights, architectural homology, or even isomorphic causal graphs, but rather with membership in an “intervention-equivalence class”: two mechanisms count as identical just in case every surgical intervention on their internal nodes yields isomorphic changes in the output distribution, regardless of divergence in static representations or underlying architecture. She is very adept at deflecting objections by insisting that computational intractability is merely an engineering limitation, and that any apparently behaviourist consequence dissolves once one distinguishes intervention equivalence from mere input–output equivalence.

Write me a formal sequence of questions for this colloquium that makes those deflections progressively harder to use. Please write in polished British English, avoid generic biographical questions, and do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
