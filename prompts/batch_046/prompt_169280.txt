I am writing in connection with my epistemology seminar, where we have spent the past two weeks on Robert Nozick's tracking conditions for knowledge and the familiar tension with epistemic closure. Our instructor walked us through the standard brain-in-a-vat illustration: I can apparently track that I have hands, since in the closest worlds where that belief is false—say, worlds in which I have lost a hand in an accident—I would not continue to hold it; yet I apparently fail to track that I am not a brain in a vat, because in the nearest worlds where that denial is false I still sincerely believe I am not envatted. From this, Nozick concludes that knowledge does not transmit across known entailment.

What confuses me is whether this rejection of closure genuinely follows from the tracking conditions, or whether it instead rests on an unstated asymmetry in how modal proximity is evaluated across different propositions. If p strictly entails q, then every world in which q is false is also a world in which p is false, so the set of nearest worlds where q is false should be a subset of the nearest worlds where p is false—at least if we apply a uniform similarity metric. Given that subset relation, if I do not believe p in those nearby false-worlds, then I should not believe q in an even closer subset of them either, which suggests that tracking ought to preserve closure automatically. A classmate suggested that this reasoning commits what she informally called a modal proximity fallacy: she argues that the counterfactual evaluation for q can recruit a different weighting of similarities—perhaps giving greater weight to mental-state preservation than to physical constitution—so that BIV worlds count as closer for assessing "I am not a BIV" than hand-loss worlds do for assessing "I have hands." On her view, the subset relation collapses once the metric itself shifts with the content of the belief being tracked. Is she right about this? More generally, does Nozick's failure of closure depend on this kind of metric variability between propositions, or is there a deeper logical flaw in my subset argument—perhaps involving the difference between tracking a single proposition and tracking the inferential relation that connects two propositions? I want to understand whether closure breaks for tracking because of how worlds are ordered, or for some other reason entirely.
