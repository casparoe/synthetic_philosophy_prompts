I was re-reading Hofstadter's Ant Fugue dialogue — the one with Aunt Hillary and the colony-level consciousness question — and it got me wondering about a specific edge case that the dialogue doesn't really address. In the story, the ants seem to act like one unified mind through pheromone networks, but I'm noticing a pattern in real collective decision-making that doesn't fit that neat superorganism picture. Here's the thing: in some ant colonies you get what I'd call a 'stuck chorus', where a small subset of foragers keeps reinforcing a depleted food trail not because the food is still thier, but because the trail pheromone is being maintained by ants that are themselves responding to the trail rather than to the food. The colony keeps sending workers to an empty site, and if you remove just those maintaining ants, the rest of the colony immediately redirects. It looks like the collective 'belief' about the food source is being held in place by a self-referential loop among a minority.

Does this kind of stuck chorus have a specific name in the collective cognition literature? I know there's quorum sensing and information cascade models, but those usually assume either correct aggregation or uniform error spread. This feels different: it's a localized feedback lock that creates a false collective representation, and it only takes a small group to maintain it. If there's no established term, I'd want one that distinguishes it from ordinary cascade failure — maybe emphasizing the self-maintaining minority aspect. But also, I might be forcing the 'mind' metaphor too hard here: maybe calling it a 'false belief' of the colony is just anthropomorphism, and I should stick to describing pheromone dynamics without the mental vocabulary. So push back plainly if the premise is confused — I'm not asking for a bibliography or citations, just whether there's a conceptual distinction I'm missing, and if not, what you'd call this phenomnon.
