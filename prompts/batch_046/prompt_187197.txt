Here's an argument I cooked up for a seminar that feels fishy but i can't pin down the leak. (1) Prioritarianism avoids the leveling-down objection because it only weights welfare gains; it never requires destroying welfare just for equality's sake. (2) But lots of goods are positionally rivalrous — think professional credentials, cultural literacy, or being well-informed in a labor market. (3) If A is badly off in education and B is well-educated, B's advantage partly depends on A's relative lack; educating A erodes B's positional value, not just adds to A. (4) So whenever prioritarianism tells us to help A in these domains, it necessarily lowers B's well-being. (5) Since almost every social good has some positional component, prioritizing the worst-off is structurally identical to leveling down the best-off. (6) Therefore prioritarianism doesn't escape the objection; it just smuggles it in through redistribution. (7) If that's right, we should reject prioritarianism or admit it requires constant harm to the well-off.

I want to keep (1) and maybe (5) if possible, but i'm suspicious that (3) is doing dishonest work — maybe positional goods are the exception, or maybe prioritarianism can restrict itself to non-positional welfare. Where does this actually go wrong?
