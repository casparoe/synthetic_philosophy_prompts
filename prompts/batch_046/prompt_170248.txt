I have been thinking about constitutional precommitment—the idea that a society can bind its future self, like Ulysses at the mast, to prevent rash decisions in crisis. I hold that such precommitment is only genuinely rational if it contains what I call a reflexive sunset clause: the constraint must embed within itself a precisely defined suspension procedure, activable only by a supermajority of future decision-makers, and this procedure must itself be immune to ordinary amendment. Without that reflexive structure, I think constitutional binding collapses either into tyranny or into mere advice. What would count against this view? I want the observations, historical cases, or conceptual arguments that ought to move me—whether functioning precommitment devices exist without this feature, whether the reflexive clause generates an infinite regress or paralysis, and whether my position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that is a defect for a view of this kind.
