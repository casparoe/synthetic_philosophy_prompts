If anekāntavāda genuinely maintains that reality possesses many irreducible sides, then it seems we must be able to assert contradictory properties of one and the same thing without qualification: for instance, that a particular object is simultaneously and in the identical respect exactly spherical and exactly cubic. I have been trying to formalize this in a framework I am calling binding-free aspectualism, where every predicate applies to a substance with no mediating parameter—no "from this angle," no "under this description"—so that if two observers report incompatible shapes, both reports are literally true of the bare object. Under that formalization, anekāntavāda looks indistinguishable from a direct violation of the law of non-contradiction. Is the error in my formalization—does the doctrine necessarily embed every predication inside a qualifying condition—or is there a version of many-sided truth that genuinely abandons non-contradiction for metaphysical gain? And if the qualifiers are indispensable, does the view still differ in substance from a mundane contextualism about measurement frames?
