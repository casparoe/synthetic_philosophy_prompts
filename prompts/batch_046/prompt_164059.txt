3. A living human being whose cells are entirely replaced over seven years: at each stage, the present cells do compose that person.
4. Two identical twins raised in complete isolation with no contact, whose lives happen to run in perfect parallel with identical choices and experiences: they do not compose one entity.
5. A distributed network of one thousand robots with no central processor, spread across a continent, running interdependent algorithms that collectively maintain homeostasis, navigate, and make unified decisions about resource allocation: they do compose one cognitive system.
6. A human brain split into hemispheres, each transplanted into a separate body with its own consciousness and agency: the two hemispheres no longer compose one person; there are two.

In both, original parts are separated or replaced, yet in 3 the person survives and the parts still compose them, while in 6 the separation destroys the unity.
