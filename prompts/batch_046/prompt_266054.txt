Design a classification tree for interpreting Thomas Szasz's thesis that mental illness is a myth, intended for use in a graduate-level philosophy of psychiatry workshop. Do not organize the tree around named commentators or historical schools; construct it from logical commitments alone. The root question should ask whether the speaker accepts that severe psychological suffering is real but denies that it is best captured by medical-model predicates. From there, branch on three successive distinctions: first, whether the denial targets the ontology of mental disorders, their epistemic accessibility, or their moral-political function; second, whether psychiatric diagnosis is characterized as an ineliminably value-laden social practice or as a failed attempt at value-neutral taxonomy; third, whether involuntary psychiatric intervention can be justified if the condition in question lacks the status of a literal biological disease. Each leaf must present a distinct interpretive position — labeled with a standard name where one exists, left unnamed otherwise — together with the single most awkward entailment that defenders of that position typically avoid stating explicitly. Include at least one branch combination that is currently unoccupied in the literature; diagnose that empty node as incoherent, practically self-defeating, or merely unexplored, and supply it with a provisional descriptive label if no standard name applies. In addition, flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate because they agree on every branch answer but differ only in rhetorical emphasis or normative tone. Phrase every node as a concrete claim that a student could evaluate without knowing which interpretation it leads to. The tree should contain between six and eight leaves, and at least one leaf should represent a non-obvious combination of answers, such as a view that treats diagnostic categories as purely performative speech acts with no descriptive content whatsoever yet retains them for therapeutic efficacy. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
