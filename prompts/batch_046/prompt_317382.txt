The two camps are these. Camp One holds that heuristic reasoning is essentially approximate Bayesian inference operating under a defeasible default: when no causal or correlational link is known, rational belief assigns independence between features, and in a directed acyclic graph this corresponds to placing uniform or diffuse priors on potential edges rather than asserting zero covariance; formalizing this presumption of independence therefore captures the rational core of analogical, representativeness, and availability-based reasoning. Camp Two holds that heuristic independence is not a statistical prior but a normative principle of cognitive economy—an instruction to withhold commitment about relations rather than to quantify their absence; representing it as conditional independence in a joint distribution collapses epistemic neutrality into quantitative absence of correlation, and thereby erases the distinct weight of argument that separates heuristic judgment from probabilistic calculation.
