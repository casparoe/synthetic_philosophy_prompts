Compare Hobsbawm's account of invented tradition with Anderson's concept of imagined communities, applying both to a hypothetical institution: retroactive civic membership, in which full citizenship is granted exclusively to descendants of participants in a 19th-century state-invented public ceremony, with no requirement of present residence, shared language, or ethnicity. How would each framework explain the political function of anchoring modern rights to this ceremonial lineage? Identify the precise point of divergence—whether the practice is best read as an instrumental fabrication of historical continuity or as the emotional architecture of an anonymous, simultaneous community—and explain how the engineered feature of retroactive membership forces that divergence. Work from the internal logic of the two theories alone; do not supply bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
