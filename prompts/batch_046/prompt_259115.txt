I sit on a hospital clinical ethics committee that regularly reviews contested determinations of death, usually with clinicians, legal advisers, and family representatives present and with decisions required within half an hour. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in the conceptual distinction between organismal death—the irreversible loss of integrated functioning of the organism as a whole, sometimes characterised in terms of critical systems failure rather than mere cessation of heartbeat—and circulatory death—the irreversible cessation of cardiac and respiratory function—that can be operated by people without philosophical training. The procedure should be an ordered sequence of questions: for each, state what counts as a sufficient answer, indicate whether an answer of yes or no terminates the procedure with a determination or sends it forward, and mark the precise points at which the question is one of values rather than medical fact and must therefore be handed back to the committee for a judgement call. Be explicit about which steps are unavoidable judgement calls disguised as procedural steps, and identify the conditions under which the procedure is likely to misfire or produce an answer that conflicts with intuitive moral expectations. Apply the procedure to two cases. First, a patient who shows complete brainstem areflexia but retains spontaneous respiration and hypothalamic temperature regulation, raising the question of whether brain-death criteria are met when some integrative functions clearly persist. Second, a patient immediately following withdrawal of extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, in whom circulation has stopped but isolated cellular metabolism and regional organ perfusion continue for several minutes, blurring the line between circulatory death and sustained somatic integration. In one of these applications, the procedure ought to yield a determination that the committee is likely to find ethically uncomfortable, and you should explain what that discomfort discloses about the limits of the framework. Please present everything in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a letter or email, and please write in British English.
