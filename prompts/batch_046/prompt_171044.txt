I am a high school student trying to understand how abstract philosophical ideas are embedded in real-world institutions rather than just debated in textbooks. I have been studying the procreation asymmetry—the view that there is a strong moral reason not to bring into existence a person whose life would contain severe suffering, but no corresponding reason to bring into existence a person whose life would be exceptionally good. What confuses me is that this asymmetry seems to be written into the design of reproductive medicine, yet I cannot tell whether the institutions genuinely depend on it or merely happen to line up with it.

Consider the following institutional pattern. In most national health systems, prenatal genetic screening for serious hereditary conditions is publicly funded, and genetic counseling protocols present the avoidance of severely affected births as a legitimate medical and ethical objective. At the same time, there is no parallel institutional practice: no counseling framework that encourages conception specifically on the grounds that the expected child would have an extraordinarily high quality of life, and public justifications for fertility-treatment subsidies appeal almost exclusively to the treatment of infertility as a medical condition rather than to the maximization of future welfare. 

What philosophical commitments about the moral status of merely potential persons, the nature of harm versus benefit, and the proper aims of reproductive medicine does this pattern presuppose? Does it strictly require accepting the procreation asymmetry, or is it compatible with symmetric views—those that treat the failure to create flourishing people as an equally significant loss—provided those views are supplemented by principles about state neutrality, tractability, or medical scope? If one rejected the asymmetry entirely, what concrete changes would have to occur in genetic counseling norms and in the rationale for reproductive funding? I want to reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely fits it well, and understand whether the institutional arrangement is philosophically underdetermined.
