Write a proposal for a one-semester independent research project (approximately 4,000–5,000 words) that reconstructs Sen’s liberal paradox in an epistemic rather than preference-based setting. The standard paradox demonstrates that minimal liberal rights—giving each individual decisive authority over at least one pair of outcomes within a personal sphere—can conflict with the Pareto principle when social choice aggregates preference orderings over actions or states of the world. Existing responses generally restrict liberal rights, weaken Pareto optimality, or accept the tension as a feature of collective decision-making, but they all assume the social choice object is an outcome or action-bundle. Frame a precise question: does an analogous conflict arise if the “personal sphere” is reconceived as authority over epistemic norms—specifically, over which evidential standards an individual applies when revising beliefs in a designated domain (e.g., moral, religious, or aesthetic conviction)—and the collective problem becomes “which belief-revision procedure should we endorse?” rather than “which world should we bring about?” Explain why the standard resolution strategies do not apply cleanly: belief-revision lacks the direct outcome-comparability that drives the original argument, yet collective inquiry may still impose coherence constraints that function like Pareto improvements. Outline your method: formalize minimal liberal conditions and a Pareto-like coherence principle for agents equipped with belief-revision rules rather than preference orderings; then construct conceptual test cases (pluralistic moral education versus unified scientific inquiry) to locate where the tension emerges. Specify what success looks like—either a proof that the paradox is structurally unavoidable even in epistemic aggregation, or a rigorous demonstration that it dissolves because authority over evidential standards is incommensurable across agents—together with what failure looks like (the reconstruction collapses back into the preference-based version, or the epistemic analog is too underdefined to evaluate). Give an honest assessment of scope: this is a conceptual reconstruction with formal modeling, no empirical data collection, feasible within one semester if the formal apparatus is kept minimal. Do not include a bibliography or literature review; references can be added separately.
