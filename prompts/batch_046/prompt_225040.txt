I've been sitting in on these foundation-year reviews at the art school across the quad and I'm genuinely puzzled about what view of taste they're running on. The program forces every student through identical drills — observational drawing, color theory, compositional grids — and grades those on rubrics that treat accuracy and craft as basically measurable. But then the final review board can fail someone with perfect technical scores because they "lack visual sensitivity," and they can advance someone with messy execution if the board agrees there's an authentic "critical vision" present. The faculty call this a balance between craft literacy and calibrated perception, and they even have this internal protocol they call a sensory calibration matrix that tries to align different professors' gut reactions so the board isn't just voting on personal preference.

What theory of aesthetic value does that whole apparatus preseuppose? Does the split between objective craft and intuitive vision map onto Hume's distinction between rules of art and the refined sentiment of the true critic? Or is it closer to Kant, where technical skill is determinative judgment but artistic merit requires reflective judgment that can't be codified? Reconstruct the implicit theory: is the program committed to beauty being partly objective (the craft layer) and partly dependent on expert sensibility (the vision layer)? And can you run the same institutional machinery — the drills, the rubrics, the calibration matrix, the board's veto — if you're a full-blown subjectivist, or if you think aesthetic judgments are just cultural constructions with no intersubjective standard?

Also, where is this philosophically underdetermined? The practice seems to need that some people literally perceive aesthetic qualities others miss, which sounds Humean, but it also lets a small committee override measurable achievement based on an inarticulable feeling, which sounds like pure authority. Could a relativist or an anti-realist about taste operate this exact same program, just describing the board's intuition as shared enculturation rather than calibrated perception? I want to know what the practice strictly requires versus what it merely fits with, and whether Hume's standard of taste actually supports giving a committee that kind of power, or whether his emphasis on broad intersubjective comparison undermines the closed-door board model.
