I'm serving on the Charter Revision Commission for the municipality of Meridian, and we must deliver a binding recommendation next month on whether to restructure our twelve-member City Council according to the "Hybrid Deliberative Model" proposed by a local coalition. Under this plan, five seats would be filled by random sortition—citizens drawn from a stratified pool serving single eighteen-month terms with no possibility of re-selection—while the remaining seven seats would remain elective but be subject to an absolute lifetime cap of two terms, regardless of office. The proponents argue that this combination breaks the "professionalization loop," in which career politicians develop insider networks that systematically dilute descriptive representation and create a permanent governing class distinct from the citizenry, while the opponents—several of whom are former council members—contend that stripping institutional memory from nearly half the chamber will accelerate bureaucratic capture by permanent staff, and that strict term limits shorten legislative horizons in ways that empower lobbyists rather than citizens.

I need you to draft a formal policy memo for the Commission that recommends either adoption or rejection of the Hybrid Model, with explicit evaluative criteria the body can apply, a clear reasoning path that connects the structural design to democratic legitimacy rather than to administrative efficiency alone, and an honest, substantive treatment of the strongest argument on the side you reject. The memo should be written as if it will be entered into the public record, and it should turn on conceptual distinctions—such as whether random selection generates a form of representational authority that election cannot replicate, whether the presence of career politicians constitutes a corruption of civic equality or a necessary condition for competent deliberation, and how term limits alter the temporal incentives of elected representatives—rather than on empirical predictions that require specialized data we do not possess. Please frame the recommendation so that it addresses both the sortition component and the term-limit component as an integrated package, explaining whether they reinforce each other or create internal tensions that undermine the proposal's coherence.
