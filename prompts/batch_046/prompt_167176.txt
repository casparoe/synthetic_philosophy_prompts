I've been working through a hybrid position on Plato's account of drunkenness and want a consistency check before presenting it. Here is my current set:

(1) I read Plato's Laws as treating controlled intoxication under a symposiarch not as social license but as an epistemic stress-test: lowering the guard of logos lets an observer see whether thumos and eros remain subordinate, thereby revealing the soul's actual ordering.

(2) I take this revelation thesis to be broadly correct: behavior under alcohol does not manifest an alien self but exposes the dominant motivational hierarchy that sober discipline ordinarily suppresses.

(3) At the same time, I hold the Republic's normative view that the true self is the rationally ordered soul, and that a well-integrated character should not depend on disinhibition to display its virtue.

(4) So I maintain that if someone is consistently virtuous when sober but vicious when drunk, the sober virtue is the genuine moral achievement, while the drunken behavior reveals only an unintegrated appetitive residue, not a deeper or more authentic character.

(5) I invoke modern alcohol myopia—the narrowing of cognition to dominant concerns—to support (2), arguing that intoxication reveals motivational hierarchy rather than some hidden essence.

(6) From this I want to defend voluntary, low-risk symposiarchic assessment in institutional selection (e.g., leadership vetting), provided consent is explicit and results are weighted lightly.

My worry is that (1)-(2) with (5) treat the revealed hierarchy as authoritative, while (3)-(4) deny that authority for moral evaluation, and (6) seems to treat the revealed behavior as institutionally relevant despite (4)'s dismissal of it. Audit this set: identify every pair in real conflict, rank them by severity, and for each specify the cheapest revision and what it costs. Also flag any tensions that dissolve on inspection—particularly whether (2) and (4) are actually incompatible once alcohol myopia is understood properly, and whether (6) can survive without giving up (4). Then tell me definitively: which single commitment, if revised or dropped, cleans up the most conflict at the least cost? Give your reasons and take a clear stand; do not lay out options and leave me to choose. Be self-critical about where my reasoning might be smuggling in a premise it does not own.
