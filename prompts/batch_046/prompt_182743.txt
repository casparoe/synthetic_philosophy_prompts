Consider Aquinas's treatment of tyrannicide in the Summa Theologiae and in De Regno. In particular, he distinguishes a ruler who usurps power from one who was lawfully instituted but abuses that office. He also insists that private individuals may not take matters into thier own hands, and that some form of public authority must be involved if removal is to be legitimate. Apply this framework to the following case.

The city-state of Maren is governed by a council of seven hereditary magistrates. One magistrate, Lucan, seized sole power three years ago and rules by decree. Another, Tiro, was lawfully elected but now imposes taxes without council consent and suppresses dissent through private guards. A group of citizens, led by a former magistrate named Varo, assassinates Lucan but spares Tiro, arguing that usurpation is a graver violation of the common good than abuse of office, and that Varo's prior office gives him sufficient public authority.

Evaluate the citizens' reasoning. Is either killing justified on Aquinas's view? Be specific about the conditions that must be met.

and here's the student answer they turned in:

Aquinas clearly says that killing a tyrant is only okay if there's public authority behind it and if there's no other way. In this case Varo used to be a magistrate so he counts as public authority, even if he's not on the council now. That covers the authority condition. Next, Lucan is a usurper because he never had legitimate power, while Tiro was lawfully elected. Aquinas thinks usurpation is worse than abuse of office, so killing Lucan is more justified than killing Tiro, which is why they spared Tiro — they got the hierarchy right. Also, since Lucan's been ruling for three years there's no higher authority to appeal to, so private action is allowed here under the emergency exception. The double effect applies too: they intended to restore the council, not just to kill Lucan. So the reasoning holds up, though they should have tried to depose Tiro through legal channels first since he's the legitimate ruler. I'd say the killing of Lucan is permissible, but Tiro should be corrected, not killed.
