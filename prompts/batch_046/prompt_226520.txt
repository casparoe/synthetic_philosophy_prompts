As a retired biologist who has recently moved into philosophy of medicine, I will defend the following position to an interdisciplinary seminar: within current DSM and ICD frameworks, Ian Hacking’s looping effects are largely blocked in psychiatric classification not because mental disorders are natural kinds, but because of what I term diagnostic closure—an institutional separation between taxonomic committees and the lived experience of patients that prevents self-modification from feeding back into criteria revision. I grant that this closure is punctured only where patient advocacy achieves category co-authorship, which I take to have occurred in the restructuring of autism spectrum criteria and in the removal of homosexuality from diagnostic manuals, but I deny that looping is the default dynamic for conditions such as major depressive disorder or generalized anxiety disorder in present practice. The audience comprises clinical psychiatrists who will defend clinical utility, philosophers of science familiar with Hacking’s work on human kinds, and data scientists building predictive diagnostic models who will ask whether algorithmic screening breaks the closure by creating automated feedback loops that bypass human self-interpretation. Prepare me for the objections this audience will actually raise: order them by likelihood and damage, phrase each in the vocabulary of the subgroup that will voice it, indicate what I should concede up front, specify where my reply must be firm for the argument to survive, and identify the single question I hope is not asked—namely, whether my distinction between closure and co-authorship applies to algorithmically mediated diagnostic categories I have not examined. Please take a committed position: rank the objections definitively, state which concessions are strategically necessary without collapsing my core claim, defend your own judgment about my weakest point rather than presenting it as one option among many, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
