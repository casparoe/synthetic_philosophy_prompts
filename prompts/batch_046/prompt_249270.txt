I'm on the grant review panel for the Midtown Public Philosophy Fund, and we've got this proposal for an installation called 'The Conceivability Lab.' The idea is visitors try to mentally model scenarios that are supposed to be impossible — like a round square or water that's not H2O — and then report whether they feel a kind of 'phenomenal contradiction' or whether the image holds steady. The team (a modal epistemologist and a cognitive psychologist) say they've got a protocol that distinguishes thin conceivability — just stringing words together — from thick conceivability, where you're actually running a mental model. Thier claim is that thick conceivability without contradiction is a decent guide to metaphysical possibility, and they want the exhibit to let people 'test' famous impossible scenarios live. Our panel is split: some members say this smuggles a disputed philosophical thesis into a science museum as if it were empirically settled, others say it's brilliant public engagement with modal reasoning. I need a memo reccomend approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with actual criterai, a fair nod to the strongest objection from the losing side, and some analysis of whether the thin/thick distinction actually does the work they claim.
