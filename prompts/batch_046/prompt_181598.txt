I want a decision tree—phrased for a technically minded but philosophically untrained reader—that sorts philosophical positions on whether the original person survives this migration. The tree should start with the split between those who think spatiotemporal continuity of the original biological substrate is necessary and those who think psychological and functional continuity are sufficient. From there, branch on three concrete commitments about the migration itself: whether the six-month parallel-run period creates a fatal branching or duplication problem; whether the fact that every intermediate hybrid state remains fully conscious undermines any worry about discontinuity; and whether substrate-independent functionalism is true or false. The leaves must include psychological-continuity theory, animalism, a narrative-identity view, a deflationary no-self position, and any logically possible but apparently unoccupied combinations—each of those labeled unnamed, with a one-sentence diagnosis of why that combination might be incoherent or unattractive. Every node should be a direct yes-or-no or this-or-that question about the parallel-replacement case that a reader can answer without already knowing the philosophical labels, and the paths should lead unambiguously to a single leaf. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific philosophers by name in the node questions; focus solely on the logical structure of the positions as they apply to this gradual, parallel-run replacement process.
