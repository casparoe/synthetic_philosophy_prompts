Construct a thought experiment engineered to isolate whether Leibniz’s claim that this is the best of all possible worlds is best interpreted as an axiological optimization (maximizing aggregate good or welfare) or as a metaphysical thesis about harmony, simplicity, and compossibility among essences.

The scenario must meet these specifications:
- Realistic, one paragraph only: no science-fiction, no visible divine agency, no artificial trolley-style framing. Describe an ordinary situation in which one person’s serious but non-fatal suffering is permanently averted, but this local improvement can occur only if a causally distant, physically unrelated region experiences an infinitesimal shift in a fundamental constant. This shift affects no other sentient being, produces no further welfare change, but breaks the perfect spatial uniformity of natural law.
- After describing the case, provide a step-by-step demonstration:
  1. The verdict of an axiological interpreter: is the modified world better? Why?
  2. The verdict of a strict Leibnizian metaphysician appealing to compossibility, the principle of sufficient reason, and the balance of simplicity and variety: is the modified world genuinely possible, and if so, is it better?
  3. The isolating feature: identify exactly which element of the scenario (the decoupling of local welfare gain from global structural uniformity) forces the two interpretations apart, and state what happens to the disagreement if that feature is removed—for instance, if the constant-shift also prevents some distant harm.
  4. The defensive counterattack: present the strongest objection that a defender of the interpretation that comes out badly (under your own final judgment) could raise against the case, without straw-manning it.
- Finish with a committed, reasoned judgment: does the case show that Leibniz’s “best world” thesis is essentially metaphysical rather than axiological? Take a definite position, defend it with reasons, and do not merely present both options neutrally.

Keep the reasoning explicit, avoid appeals to bibliographies or citations, and do not quote texts verbatim.
