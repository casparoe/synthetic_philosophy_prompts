I am a high school student examining Stoicism's modern revival for an independent study project. I have been studying Epictetus's classical dichotomy of control—the division between what lies within our power and what does not—but I have also encountered a more recent proposal, found in contemporary Stoic writing, that replaces the binary with a trichotomy: things fully in our control, things partly in our control, and things entirely outside it.

Please compare these two frameworks. Does the trichotomy resolve a real ambiguity in the original dichotomy, especially in cases like preparing for a debate or taking an exam graded partly on subjective judgment, where our effort clearly influences but does not determine the result? Or does it merely rename the boundary without changing the practical guidance?

Consider the concrete case of a student who studies thoroughly for a presentation, yet receives a grade that depends both on the clarity of her delivery and on her teacher's unstated preferences. Under the dichotomy, should she regard the grade as entirely outside her control? Under the trichotomy, does placing the grade in the middle category actually help her calibrate her emotional investment? Also, examine whether that middle category risks collapsing back into the dichotomy once we ask what "partly in control" means—whether it denotes probabilistic influence, shared agency, or direct causal contribution.

State which framework offers the more coherent account of emotional resilience and moral responsibility, and indicate whether a student applying these ideas to social anxiety should treat peer approval as falling in the middle category or as fully external.
