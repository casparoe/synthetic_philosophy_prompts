Write a short story about Mara, a branch-weight auditor in a society where Everettian mechanics is official public policy. Every law gets enacted across all branching outcomes, but budgets and resources are still distributed according to Born-rule weights. Mara's job is to check that observed outcome frequencies in policy trials match the squared-amplitude predictions.

Recently she's been experiencing what she privately calls cross-branch mnemonic contamination: her own memories of "failed" outcomes feel emotionally real, not like fantasies or dreams. Its a hypothetical psychological effect where near-identical observer-states in adjacent worlds share faint memory traces, especially under high-stress measurement conditions. She starts to suspect this undermines the self-locating uncertainty that the government uses to justify applying the Born rule to human lives.

Show this through her ordinary routine — she has a cat, she commutes on a train that runs on quantum-random scheduling, she argues with her partner about wether their relationship "counts" in worlds where they never met. Include a scene where she tries to run the standard decision-theoretic reasoning citizens are taught — bet on the high-weight branch, treat yourself as randomly located — and it collapses because she cant uniquely locate herself anymore.

She has to decide whether to file a report knowing it breaks the framework that makes democratic resource allocation coherent. End with her taking an action that only makes sense if probability is neither subjective belief nor objective branch counting.

Keep the physics implicit in her choices, not explained like a textbook. Aim for about 400-600 words.

Finish with a one-sentence summary of the story's core conceptual punch on its own line at the very end.
