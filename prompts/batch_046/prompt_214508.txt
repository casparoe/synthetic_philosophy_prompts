I've been studying Wittgenstein's claim that concepts like "game" are held together only by family resemblance, lacking any essential feature common to every instance. Applying this consistently, it seems there can be no universal structure of "rule-following" shared across different language-games—chess rules, promise-making rules, and counting rules appear to be only analogically related rather than governed by a single normative essence. Yet Wittgenstein insists that language is essentially rule-governed and uses this very point to block the possibility of a private language. Doesn't this commit him to the exact kind of essentialism about rules that his family-resemblance argument was meant to eliminate? If there is genuinely no common normative core across all language-games, on what basis can he claim that private rule-following is impossible, rather than treating it as just another family-related linguistic practice? Please identify the specific misunderstanding in this line of reasoning, and answer in American English.
