After three decades in experimental physics, I have recently begun working through problems in the philosophy of perception, and I am drafting a short essay on the color-phi phenomenon and the so-called Orwellian versus Stalinesque accounts of experiential revision. To set the stage: when a red dot and then a green dot are flashed in rapid succession at neighboring positions, observers report seeing a single dot move smoothly through an intermediate location, apparently colored yellow, despite the fact that no yellow stimulus was ever presented. The Orwellian interpretation holds that the visual system writes the intermediate experience into consciousness prospectively, fabricating a false past before the second flash is fully registered. The Stalinesque interpretation holds that both discrete flashes are initially registered veridically and that the system only afterward airbrushes the record, substituting a coherent motion narrative for the raw snapshots.

In my draft I propose a framing borrowed from signal reconstruction rather than historiography. I suggest that we model the phenomenon not as either preemptive fabrication or post-hoc censorship, but as temporal smoothing within a bounded neural integration window. On this view, the first signal is held in a revisable buffer; when the second signal arrives within the window, the system updates the entire interval in a single distributed computation, so that there is never a discrete "first draft" of experience that gets either predicted in advance or erased after the fact. The intermediate yellow is not inserted into an archived record, nor is it anticipated; it emerges only as the window closes and the smoothed trajectory is settled. This seems to dissolve the false dichotomy, but I am concerned that I may be illegitimately importing mechanistic vocabulary from measurement physics into phenomenology, and that the "integration window" may be nothing more than a renamed Stalinesque revision with a temporal parameter attached.

I would welcome critical comments on the coherence of this proposal. Specifically: does the integration-window model genuinely avoid commitment to both Orwellian and Stalinesque revision, or does it collapse into one upon closer inspection? Is the analogy to predictive smoothing in noisy physical measurements illuminating here, or does it obscure the specifically philosophical problem about the chronology of conscious content? I am also interested in whether this framing undermines the very question that motivates the debate—namely, whether there is a determinate answer to "when, exactly, did the subject experience the intermediate yellow?"—and whether treating that question as a category mistake is a defensible philosophical move or merely an evasion. Please treat this as commentary on an argument in progress, not as a request for bibliographic references or a survey of the literature.
