Consider a hypothetical dynamical system called a careenium, composed of entities—simmballs—each of which carries both a physical state vector and a locally instantiated symbol-token configuration. The symbolic layer across the careenium encodes representations that map onto the collective dynamics of the substrate. I hold the position that in such a system the symbols genuinely push the substrate around: altering the symbol-token pattern across simmballs, without changing any underlying physical parameters or energy conditions, can systematically shift the trajectory of the physical dynamics, so that the symbolic level possesses downward causal efficacy rather than being merely descriptive or epiphenomenal. What would have to be true for this to be wrong? I want the map of its exposure: which observed behaviors of a careenium would decisively undermine downward symbol-causation, which conceptual arguments (say, about causal closure of the physical layer or the distinction between correlation and causation in multi-level systems) would count against it, and whether analogies from computation or neuroscience weaken or strengthen the case. Rank these defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, including whether the position can be qualified—by redefining what counts as a physical parameter or by retreating to a weaker correlation claim—into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that insulation is a problem for a thesis of this kind. I also want to know which concessions defenders of symbol-driven substrate dynamics would themselves accept as genuinely damaging, not only what critics say, and whether there is any result in the study of persistence or emergence that the view stands or falls with.
