I'm presenting my paper on the Sleeping Beauty problem at a phil of science workshop next thursday. I defend a thirder view, but my twist is that self-locating beliefs about "today" shouldn't update via standard conditioning because the centered world lacks stable identity conditions across awakenings—so Beauty's prior over Monday vs Tuesday is structurally fixed by the protocol symmetry, not by a chancy belief about a coin. The audience: mostly philosophers of physics who think the debate is a toy problem, plus two formal epistemologists who co-wrote a reply to Elga and will hit me with the principal principle and decision-theoretic derivations. There's also a grad student in quantum foundations who might push the Everettian self-location analogy. I need objections in the order they'll actually come, phrased in thier voices, with what to concede, what to hold, a two-minute reply, and where each reply is soft. Especially include the one I'm hoping they skip: that my view collapses into halfing once you allow centered worlds with temporal parts. And flag the question I'm praying nobody asks—probably whether this kills all Bayesian updating for indexicals. Keep it to practical prep, not a full defense.
