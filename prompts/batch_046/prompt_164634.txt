hey — I'm drafting a feature for my science-and-ideas column on that familiar argument where people say one vote or one burger can't change anything, so why bother? I want to embed a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts readers into actual philosophical positions on the inefficacy objection, using only concrete cases they can answer without knowing the jargon first.

Start with branches about voting in a 50-million-person election, buying beef in a supply chain that won't adjust, and a climate case where your personal carbon is literally zero-sum against a corporate emitter. Each split should be a claim like "does moral wrongness require that your act makes a causal difference?" or "is symbolic participation enough?" I need leaves for the standard camps — straight consequentialist aggregation, the pure causal-inefficacy excuse, some Kantian-style universalization — but also for weirder positions, like a "threshold deontology" view that says individual acts only become wrong once collective harm is near a tipping point, and the "structual complicity" view that denies individual causation but assigns a symbolic duty not to endorse the system. Every leaf should carry the position name and one sentence describing its most awkward commitment. No bibliographys, no reading lists, just the tree with about 8-10 nodes, phrased so someone can navigate it without already knowing which camp they're in.
