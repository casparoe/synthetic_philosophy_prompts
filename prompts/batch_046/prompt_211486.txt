I run a small editorial board for a philosophy of language journal and we're getting more and more submissions claiming to have discovered 'radically untranslatable' conceptual schemes — in lost dialects, AI outputs, whatever. We need a reusable review procedre based on Davidson's idea of radical interpetation and the principle of charity: the ordered questions we ask a submission, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where we stop and reject the incommensurability claim, and where the decision is really about values not interpretation. Fix the role: we see maybe three of these a month, have two weeks, and the board has to sign off unanimously. Include the hard case where charity forces us to attribute beliefs the authors clearly reject. Then run the procedure on two submissions: one about a dialect that seems to lack any linear ordering of events, and one about an AI system that consistently outputs sentences that look self-contradictory about personal identity. Make one of those come out in a way the submitter will hate. End by saying exactly where the procedre breaks down and can't decide.
