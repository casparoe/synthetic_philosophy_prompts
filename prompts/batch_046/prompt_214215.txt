I have recently begun re-examining my dietary choices after encountering arguments that arable farming harms wild animals through harvesting machinery and habitat conversion, and I find myself uncertain whether conventional ethical rankings of meat still hold. Write an essay on the controversy over whether replacing beef with chicken reduces net animal suffering when both direct farmed suffering and indirect wild-animal mortality — from feed-crop harvest for poultry and pasture conversion for cattle — are weighed symmetrically. Examine whether chicken’s greater feed efficiency per kilogram of meat makes it preferable despite its heavier reliance on crop acreage, or whether beef’s larger land footprint per calorie produces greater cumulative ecosystem displacement. Reach and defend a clear verdict on which dietary shift — beef to chicken, both to plant-based alternatives, or maintaining current consumption — is morally best, and justify your conclusion. Please write in British English, say how confident you are in your verdict, and single out the point about which you are least sure.
