Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of practical reasoning must meet to accommodate the integrity objection—the failure mode in which moral deliberation inserts an alienating consideration between an agent and her ground projects—without reducing integrity to preference satisfaction or dissolving moral demands into project-relative convenience. Identify the single desideratum that eliminates the largest share of popular theories, and state whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable—specifically, whether full agent-neutral justifiability and integrity-preservation can coexist. Briefly score Kantian universalizability, consequentialist aggregation, and virtue-ethical coherence of character against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria. Finally, inject and defend an adjacency constraint: the theory must represent an agent’s ground projects not as fungible entries on a scale but as nodes in a motivational topology, so that requiring her to trade one off against an external moral claim by a common metric is itself the failure mode; say whether this constraint is derivable from the others or is an independent desideratum. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
