I hold that Mill's late writings on socialism—specifically the revisions in later editions of his economic treatise and his posthumous essays on cooperative production—should be read as advancing a position I will call conditional associative socialism. On this view, Mill does not endorse state ownership or revolutionary transformation, but rather holds that socialist organization of production is permissible and perhaps desirable only if it can emerge through voluntary, competitive trial rather than coercive restructuring, and only if it preserves individual initiative by embedding democratic workplace governance within market-tested cooperative associations. The view is meant to sit between orthodox liberalism and state socialism, retaining Mill's commitment to progressive human development and liberty as non-interference while allowing that property relations might be reformed if they fail a practical, comparative test.

What would count against this interpretation? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a list of objections it can easily absorb. Specifically: which passages in Mill's corpus, which biographical or contextual facts about his political engagements in the 1870s, and which tensions with his broader commitments—to utility, to representative government, to the liberty principle—would genuinely undermine the claim that this is his settled view rather than a provisional speculation? Which defeaters would a defender of conditional associative socialism concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss as misreading? Rank the defeaters from fatal (the view collapses into either orthodox liberalism or full socialism) to merely awkward. Include in your assessment whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability—if every apparent contradiction can be explained away as part of the conditional or trial clause—and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for an interpretive thesis of this kind. Finally, consider a specific hypothetical: if it could be shown that Mill regarded the cooperative-trial framework as structurally incoherent because any socialist transition requires preemptive state coordination that precludes competitive testing, would that defeat the view on its own terms, or could it be accommodated?
