Student answer:
Both reject the law of excluded middle and avoid non-constructive existence proofs. Intuitionistic logic is mainly the logical system—dropping LEM—while Bishop keeps classical logic in the background and just insists every object have an explicit algorithm. Brouwers continuity principle, which says every total function from [0,1] to R is continuous, isnt part of intuitionistic logic itself; its an extra axiom tied to his idea of lawlike choice sequences. Bishop called this mystical and dropped it. In Bishops framework you can actually construct discontinuous functions if you have an explicit decision rule for the jumps, but he requires every real function to come with a modulus of continuity, so in practice discontinuity is blocked globally. Meanwhile intuitionism allows lawless choice sequences that arent computable, and thats why Brouwer can derive continuity for all functions. So the core difference is ontological: intuitionism accepts non-computable mental constructions, Bishop only accepts computable ones.
