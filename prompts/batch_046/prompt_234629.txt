Within the philosophy of chaos and determinism, a familiar distinction separates trajectory determinism—the thesis that each state of a system has a unique successor according to dynamical laws—from epistemic determinism, the claim that knowing those laws and initial conditions permits meaningful prediction or explanation. A less commonly examined position, which I will call structural opacity, holds that in high-dimensional chaotic regimes the causal structure itself becomes non-decomposable: not merely that we lack the precision to trace individual trajectories, but that the very notion of isolating "the cause" of a particular macro-scale event dissolves because perturbations are globally coupled in ways that resist local attribution. If structural opacity is real, it threatens explanatory determinism more deeply than standard Lyapunov divergence does, since it undermines causal decomposition rather than forecasting accuracy.
