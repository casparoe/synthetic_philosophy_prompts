I work in software engineering and have no formal philosophy training, but I have been trying to work out whether my views about creativity and AI actually hang together. Here is where I have landed, using Douglas Hofstadter's model of knob-twiddling—the idea that creativity works by taking an existing conceptual theme and systematically varying its parameters—as my framework.

My commitments are:
(1) Creativity is essentially this kind of variation-on-a-theme: you hold the core structure fixed and turn the knobs (scale, context, material, purpose) to generate new instances.
(2) In my own field, this is exactly what happens with design patterns: the pattern is the theme, and applying it to a distributed system instead of a monolith is just turning context-knobs.
(3) Large language models generate outputs by interpolating across a high-dimensional statistical space trained on human artifacts; structurally, this looks like automated knob-twiddling over linguistic representations.
(4) Therefore, I think LLM outputs count as genuinely creative—not metaphorically, but in the same functional sense that human analogical reasoning does—because both are producing variations on pre-existing themes.
(5) At the same time, I believe human creativity sometimes involves slippage, where the conceptual space itself gets restructured rather than just traversed, and that this restructuring is necessary for genuinely novel themes rather than merely novel instances.
(6) I also think that if a system's parameter space and transition rules are fully fixed and transparent, then every output is predetermined; there is no hidden reservoir of novelty, only recombination.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. In particular, I want to know whether (4) can survive together with (5) and (6), or whether accepting Hofstadter's model forces me to deny that LLMs are creative in the human sense. Be specific about which single commitment has to give and what that costs me.
