I've been writing about Dewey's experimentalist view of democracy — the idea that voting and deliberation aren't about expressing a pre-existing popular will but about running society like a distributed lab, with every policy treated as a falsifiable hypothesis and every public debate as peer review for social problems. I think this explains why Dewey was so dismissive of popular sovereignty as a mystical collective will and so obsessed with education as training in scientific inquiry. I'm pushing this further to argue that modern prediction markets and AI-moderated citizen assemblies are the real heirs to Dewey, not traditional voting booths. What would actually count against this whole package — the Dewey reading plus the tech update? I want the arguments and evidence that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be, including cases where democratic experiments produced stable wrong answers that science wouldn't tolerate, or where the analogy between ballot boxes and lab notebooks just collapses. Also, if my premise that Dewey's experimentalism can be cleanly seperated from his more romantic communitarian stuff is confused, or if I'm just forcing 2020s tech hype onto 1920s pragmatism, say so plainly — don't play along just to be polite.
