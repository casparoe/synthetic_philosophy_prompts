Drawing specifically on Hobsbawm’s criteria for invented tradition (the explicit construction of continuity with a suitable past, the role of ritual and repetition, and the social function served by the invention), his materialist skepticism toward primordial nationalism, and Anderson’s account of print-capitalism as the technological substrate of national imagination, how do you think Hobsbawm would assess an algorithmically aggregated “national memory” that lacks human intentionality, ceremonial performance, and institutional authorship? Would he deny it the status of invented tradition on the grounds that tradition requires active social agents performing repetition, or would he treat statistical canonicalization as a functional equivalent performed by infrastructure rather than people? And extending Anderson’s framework, would Hobsbawm see algorithmically mediated collective identity as continuing the logic of print-capitalism into a new register, or as breaking that logic by removing the human reader-citizen altogether? In answering, please separate what is well established in Hobsbawm’s and Anderson’s respective theories from interpretations that remain genuinely contested among historians and theorists of nationalism, and do not present any disputed reading of their work as the consensus view.
