For a philosophical profile I am assembling on population ethics and pronatalism, here are my commitments. I want an audit of whether they hang together.

First, I am a welfare totalist about lives worth living: other things equal, more such lives increase the value of the world, and I reject average utilitarianism because it makes the value of a population depend perversely on the well-being of its actual members.

Second, I accept the non-identity problem's resolution that bringing a particular person into existence with a life worth living does not wrong that person, even if the conditions of that life fall well below what we would call decent.

Third, I hold a strong reproductive asymmetry: there is no positive moral duty to procreate, but anyone who voluntarily reproduces acquires stringent obligations not to subject the resulting child to serious deprivation or harm.

Fourth, I treat ecological carrying capacity as a deontic boundary rather than a merely practical constraint. Breaching planetary boundaries wrongs existing sentient beings through interference and degradation, and this wrong is not canceled out by welfare gains among any additional lives that the breach might make possible.

Fifth, I think long-range sustainability demands that human populations remain well below current replacement trajectories if civilization is to preserve what I call civilizational option value — the open-ended capacity of future generations to choose among radically different social and technological paths. I take this option value to be lexically prior to near-term welfare aggregation.

Sixth, I endorse a scalar, neurobiologically informed gradualism about phenomenal consciousness: moral weight tracks degrees of sentience or recurrent processing capacity rather than binary species membership, which implies that human lives carry greater weight than comparable animal lives, but the margin is substantially narrower than conventional humanism assumes.

Seventh, I want to block the repugnant conclusion without abandoning totalism, and I think this can be done via a critical-level or steeply diminishing-marginal-value mechanism that still allows more good lives, within limits, to be better.

Go through these commitments and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension rank the severity. For each tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs in terms of the other commitments. Finally, tell me which single revision to my profile would clean up the most tensions overall, and whether there is any hidden tension that only emerges once that revision is made.
