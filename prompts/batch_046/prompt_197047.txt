me and my roommate got into it last night about epiphenomenalism and that paradox about phenomenal judgment — you know, the one where if qualia dont cause anything then we cant really know about them? — and i ended up writing out this argument that feels like it proves way too much but i cant see the leak. heres the chain:

(1) If a belief about X is justified, then X has to be part of the causal story that produces the belief — otherwise its just lucky guesswork.
(2) Epiphenomenalism says phenomenal states (like feeling pain) are causally inert, so they never enter the causal story of any belief.
(3) So on epiphenomenalism, beliefs about our own phenomenal states are unjustified.
(4) But the same applies to beliefs about other peoples minds, about dinosaurs, about quarks — none of those things are literally in the causal chain of the belief, only physical traces or inferences.
(5) So if (1) is right, we should be skeptics about other minds, science, and history.
(6) That global skepticism is clearly wrong.
(7) Therefore (1) is false.
(8) But if (1) falls, then (3) doesnt follow, so the paradox of phenomenal judgment collapses and epiphenomenalism gets off the hook.

i really want to hang onto the idea that theres something specially wrong with saying we have no causal access to our own consciousness, but i also dont want to be a skeptic about literally everything else. which premise should i ditch, and whats the cost? please keep whats settled separate from whats genuinely fought over — dont act like theres a consensus where there isnt.
