Since I recently assumed editorial responsibility for a distributed online publication with largely pseudonymous contributors, I have found myself returning to Kierkegaard's critique of the press and the abstraction he calls the public. Please describe the most important implications of his analysis—particularly his distinction between the crowd, which requires physical co-presence and can be dispersed, and the public, which is a phantom sustained solely by the press and possesses no concrete reality—for understanding whether decentralized, asynchronous publishing environments genuinely restore individual accountability, or whether they merely reproduce the logic of leveling and irresponsible anonymity at a different scale. I am especially interested in how his view that the press generates an audience demanding perpetual novelty without personal commitment might illuminate algorithmically mediated engagement dynamics, but please treat this as a conceptual exercise rather than a request for historical citations, bibliographies, or close textual exegesis of specific works.
