Please argue that Davidson's principle of charity breaks down if you try to do radical interpretation on a system that was trained only on synthetic data and never had embodied causal contact with the world. The standard Davidsonian picture says charity works because the interpreter and the interpreted both causally interact with the same environment, so agreement about obvious truths anchors the interpretation. But with a purely synthetic system, there is no shared world to anchor anything—just internal correlations in a generated distribution. That means charity either collapses into pure coherence (so you could map its 'beliefs' onto anything consistent) or you have to smuggle in an unargued assumption that only embodied agents count as interpretable. Make the case that this isn't just a practical limitation but reveals radical interpretation was always implicitly tied to biological agency, not rational agency in general. You can assume familiarity with Davidson's basic setup, but don't cite specific papers—just build the argument, and feel free to mention an artifical language model as your example case.
