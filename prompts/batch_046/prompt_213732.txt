Please argue that applying Nozick's tracking conditions for knowledge to beliefs formed through extended cognitive processes—where mental states are coupled with reliable external artefacts—undermines the principle of epistemic closure in a manner that standard internalist counterexamples do not reach. Set out a precise scenario in which an agent genuinely knows that p via an environmental scaffold, yet in the nearest worlds where that scaffold is absent or decoupled her internal states remain identical, she still believes p, but p is false. Demonstrate that she therefore knows p without knowing she is not in such a decoupled condition, and defend the view that this is not a technical glitch in tracking theory but exposes a structural incompatibility between modal-tracking knowledge and genuinely externalist accounts of cognition. Keep the argument free of generic sceptical hypotheses and of any request for citations.
