I'm co-chairing the taxonomy committee for a digital archive of state violence in the 20th century. We have to decide tagging rules for cases where a regime targeted a group that doesnt cleanly match the UN Genocide Convention's protected categories (national, ethnical, racial, religious) but fits Lemkin's original broader idea of destroying a group through cultural annihilation plus forced assimilation.

Specifically: we've got records of a campaign where authorities banned a regional language in all schools, destroyed religious manuscripts, relocated children to state-run institutions, and killed several hundred resisters. The victims were mostly defined by a mix of regional dialect and religious practice that wasn't recognized as an ethnicity in census records.

My co-chair wants to tag everything like this 'genocide - cultural/physical' using Lemkin's pre-1948 framework. I think that risks making the tag useless for researchers comparing death-toll scales. But I also think refusing the tag erases the specific harm of destroying a group's collective existance through non-lethal means.

Write me a memo recommending our tagging policy. Give me a decision we can actually apply across the archive, with criteria reviewers can use. Be honest about the strongest point on the other side.

Structure it exactly like this:
1. What's at stake (~80 words)
2. The broad-tag case (~120 words)
3. The narrow-tag case (~120 words)
4. The proposed rule (~150 words)
5. The blind spot we shouldn't miss (~60 words)

Keep it casual. A typo or two is fine.
