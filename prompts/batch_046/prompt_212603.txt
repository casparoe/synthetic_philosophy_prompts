I teach an upper-level democratic theory seminar of about fourteen graduate students, meeting once for ninety minutes. I want them to experience—through rules and roles rather than through a lecture—the structural tension in populist representation between treating the will of the people as a pre-existing aggregate and treating it as a performative claim that retroactively organizes grievances into a unified demand. The conceptual framing I want built into the mechanics is this: for the claim that the people want X to stabilize, it must simultaneously perform an exclusion, designating some subgroup as not part of the real people, where that exclusion is not an accidental mistake but a functional condition for the coherence of the will being claimed.

Design a simulation with assigned roles, hidden preference cards, a representative who must declare the people's will at set intervals, and an initially included subgroup that can be redesignated as outsiders based on that declaration. The rules should reshape available actions or symbolic payoffs after each declaration so that participants feel the pressure to align with the declared will and the pressure to police the boundary. Include: the room layout and group division; materials and hidden-information rules; the payoff or privilege scheme; a timeline with phases; facilitator instructions that avoid defining terms up front; and a debrief that maps specific moments onto (1) the descriptive illusion, (2) the performative construction, (3) the necessary exclusion, and (4) the instability when the excluded group contests the boundary.

Also tell me what will predictably fail the first time—especially participants treating the declaration as merely inaccurate rather than constitutive, or the excluded group refusing the assigned role too easily—and how to handle it without breaking the frame. Be frank about which feature, likely the retroactive restructuring of preferences or the structural rather than contingent nature of the exclusion, the mechanics can only approximate and you as facilitator must supply verbally. Do not ask for citations, a bibliography, or a reading list.
