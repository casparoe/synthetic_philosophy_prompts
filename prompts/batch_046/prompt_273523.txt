I'm giving a seminar defense — followed by a job-market-style Q&A — of the following thesis: the standard superrational resolution to the Platonia dilemma succeeds only by implicitly assuming that reasoning-counterparts share symmetric epistemic access to the mechanism that generates payoffs, and this assumption collapses in the Luring Lottery because the draw introduces an asymmetry in what each counterpart can observe about the payoff-generating process; I propose replacing the symmetry condition with what I call the counterfactual mirror principle, which licenses an agent to identify its reasoning with a counterpart's solely when both possess identical epistemic access to that mechanism. This permits cooperation in Platonia, where access is symmetric, while licensing defection in the Luring Lottery, where the draw breaks symmetry, without appealing to causal decision theory or to circular reasoning about rationality.
