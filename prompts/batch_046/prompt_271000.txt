The audience seemed to treat this as decisive, yet I kept thinking about the dogmatism paradox: if the claim is unconditional, it appears to condemn itself, and if it is qualified, it may lose the force needed to do the work it was being made to do. Could you disambiguate the slogan into its distinct precise formulations—say, as an epistemic norm governing belief revision, a methodological constraint on scientific inquiry, or a rhetorical posture about intellectual humility—indicating for each whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or self-refuting? I would especially like to know which reading, if any, could genuinely justify dismissing a particular controversial hypothesis without collapsing into the paradox it invokes. Please state how confident you are in your taxonomy and single out the specific distinction about which you are least certain.
