In our phil of life group we've been circling Schlick's play vs work idea for months and it's driving me nuts. One camp reads him as saying meaning is purely phenomological — you're in a state of absorption with no external goal. The other camp insists he's giving an objective structural criterion: an activity is meaningful if its ends are internal to the practice, like chess or art, regardless of whether the agent feels anything special. We've all read Problems of Ethics but can't agree if "play" is a feeling or a form. Lately I wonder if we're actually fighting about something hidden: whether Schlick's view is circular in a subtle way, since to identify an activity as "play" in his sense you already have to recognize it as self-justifying, which presupposes the very meaning you're trying to explain. So is this just a verbal split between autotelic experience and autotelic structure? Or is there a deeper disagreement about whether meaning can be entirely first-personal, with the structuralists sneaking in objectivity? And given your read, what would move it — clarifying the concept, finding a case where structure and experience come apart, or is the whole debate ill-posed because Schlick never meant to give necessary conditions?
