As a novelist drafting the final chapter of a philosophical fiction, I require a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains alternating through the rhyme scheme—to serve as the private notebook entry of a dying logician. The logician has reconstructed Pascal's wager not as the familiar binary between belief and disbelief, but as a decision problem under radical uncertainty: the payoff matrix is unknown, and the concept of infinite utility fragments into values that resist cardinal comparison. The poem's two refrains should embody the two positions—faith and its refusal—while their shared rhyme sounds gradually expose that both depend on the unstated premise that salvation can be assigned a quantitative value. In the tercets, build the naive expected-utility picture—probability, reward, calculation—then let the final quatrain collapse the comparison by showing that when the nature of the reward is genuinely opaque, the wager's arithmetic dissolves into incommensurability. The verse should be accurate enough that a philosopher of religion recognizes the move from decision-theoretic reasoning to the problem of value pluralism, yet it must stand as an independent lyric without footnotes, citations, or bibliographic apparatus. Render the argument through image and rhythm alone.
