Consider the color phi phenomenon: a subject is shown a red dot at position A, then shortly afterward a green dot at position B, yet reports seeing a single dot traveling continuously from A to B and shifting color—often to orange—at some intermediate point, even though no physical stimulus ever occupies that midpoint with that color. In the philosophy of temporal consciousness, this case is often used to distinguish Orwellian revisions, in which an intermediate representational state never actually occurs and is instead fabricated retrospectively into the stream of consciousness, from Stalinesque revisions, in which the intermediate state genuinely arises but is subsequently erased or overwritten before it can be reported or consolidated.

To sharpen the conceptual tension, imagine a three-layer architecture for visual experience: Layer 1 consists of transient, pre-attentive sensory registrations lasting only tens of milliseconds; Layer 2 consists of narrative-bound perceptual objects that are immediately available for verbal report; and Layer 3 consists of retrospective coherence mechanisms that stabilize the sequence into a unified autobiographical record. The issue is not simply which layer is modified, but whether the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction applies uniformly across these layers.

Please explain the following conceptual problem: if an orange-colored representation is briefly registered in Layer 1 but is actively suppressed before it reaches Layer 2, should we classify this as a Stalinesque revision of Layer 1 (because a genuine state existed and was destroyed) or as an Orwellian revision of Layer 2 (because the narrative layer never contained the orange token and later constructs the illusion that it did)? Alternatively, if predictive processing mechanisms assign an orange token directly to Layer 2 without any corresponding Layer 1 precursor, is this an Orwellian insertion into the narrative stream, or does the absence of a destroyed precursor place it in a different category of error altogether? In your answer, clarify why resolving this matters for whether we should say the subject literally experienced orange at the midpoint, and for how the precise temporal location of that experience—when, exactly, it is dated within the conscious stream—depends on which layer we treat as authoritative for phenomenal content.
