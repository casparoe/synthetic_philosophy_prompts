Write a dialogue between two equally capable interlocutors, Mara and Elias, on whether open borders can coexist with a robust welfare state, structured around two competing conceptual frames. Mara advocates “institutional reciprocity”: she holds that immigration is fully compatible with universal social provision provided that integration is defined strictly as sustained contribution to and compliance with shared fiscal and legal institutions, with no demand for cultural assimilation or a pre-existing shared normative background. Elias advocates “bounded solidarity”: he holds that redistribution depends on a non-contractible reservoir of mutual trust and shared normative expectations that transactional compliance alone cannot generate, so that treating integration as purely institutional removes the very solidarity that makes the welfare state sustainable. Require that each respond directly to the other’s arguments: Mara should explain whether her framework can generate social trust ex nihilo and address Elias’s worry that reciprocity collapses into a hollow accounting exercise; Elias should defend his “normative background” against the charge of exclusionary essentialism and explain whether citizens have any prior moral claim to inherited institutional surplus, or whether that claim itself needs justification. Instruct them to use only hypothetical or generic illustrations, not specific historical cases or scholarly citations. Keep the response under 600 words, in continuous dialogue form without stage directions, ensuring genuine back-and-forth rather than parallel speeches.
