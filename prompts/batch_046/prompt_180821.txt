I have been thinking about the claim that a person cannot have a language that only they understand, because language seems to require some shared standard of correctness. I do not have formal training in philosophy, but I have been testing my intuition against several imaginary situations. I would like to know whether there is a coherent principle that unifies my judgments, and I ask that you respond in American English.

Here are four cases with my intuitive verdicts:

Case 1: A child invents the word "blip" to name the precise feeling of cold water on her toes. She writes it in her private diary whenever the sensation occurs. No one else knows the word, and she never discusses it. Verdict: This seems acceptable—like giving oneself a nickname for a private experience.

Case 2: A man has an implanted neural device that detects a phantom sensation occurring only in his brain. He records the symbol "Φ" in his notebook each time it happens. He can show the symbol to physicians, but they have no independent access to the sensation and no way to check whether he applies the symbol consistently. Verdict: This feels deficient. "Φ" does not seem to be a genuine word, only a private mark without meaning.

Case 3: A painter experiences colors in her dreams that do not match any waking pigments. Over many years she assigns them stable private names—"glorn" and "velk"—and uses them consistently in her journal. Occasionally she applies "glorn" to a waking shade that feels similar, but there is no other person who can correct her. Verdict: I am uncertain. It feels closer to a real vocabulary than Case 2, yet also unstable because she can never be corrected.

Case 4: A musician invents a system of symbols representing emotional states he feels only while playing a particular instrument. He composes scores using these symbols. Other musicians can perform the scores and report experiencing corresponding emotional responses, even though they do not know the private associations behind each symbol. Verdict: This seems like a legitimate notation system, despite its private origin.

Is there one principle that explains why Case 1 and Case 4 appear legitimate, Case 2 appears hollow, and Case 3 falls in between? Or must I abandon one of these verdicts in order to maintain a simpler account?

If you can formulate such a principle, please state it precisely, indicate which of my four verdicts it cannot accommodate, and apply it to a further situation I have not resolved: a meditator feels a distinctive "time-slowing" sensation every Tuesday. She invents the word "drift" for it and marks her calendar. One Tuesday she misses the session, but on Wednesday she feels something similar and writes "drift" anyway. She notices the discrepancy herself, yet has no external check on whether Tuesday's feeling was genuinely present. Does her use of "drift" constitute a meaningful word?
