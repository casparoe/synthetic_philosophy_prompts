I sit on a local heritage board and we've been pushed to issue a formal town appology for our mills' use of forced labour two centuries back. The tricky bit: our museum's endowment was built from those same proffits, and nobody wants to talk about returning it. One colleague argues that an apology without restitution is just moral theatre — it makes us feel better without costing anything. Another says demanding restitution now is collective punishment on people who never chose to be born here, and that we should keep the money but at least say sorry. I feel caught between thinking the apology is cheap if we keep the cash, and thinking it's wrong to burden present residents for past sins. Is there a way to apologise honestly without either letting ourselves off the hook or punishing the wrong people? Or am I missing a distinction between saying sorry and actually fixing the ledger?
