I'm working on a feature for my magazine about how people actually live with philosophical ideas that sound like cosmic horror premises, and I got stuck trying to map Nietzsche's eternal recurrance without just recycling the same three interpretations everyone lists. I want a taxonamy — basically a decision tree with named leaves — that sorts someone by concrete commitments rather than by which scholar they like. The branches should split on questions like: do they take recurrence as a literal cosmological loop or as a hypothetical stress-test? If it's a stress-test, is the "greatest weight" about affirming every single painful detail of suffering or just the overall arc of one's life? Do they think the cycles require strict numerical identity of the same person, or is qualitative similarity enough? Is there any escape hatch — total death, amnesia, even a tiny variation between rounds — or does the repetition have to be exact and uninterrupted? And do they let the unpublished notebook fragments override the published books, or treat the Nachlass as discarded experiments that shouldn't constrain interpretation? Each leaf should carry a standard name if one exists — cosmological literalist, amor fati normative view, qualitative-cycle existentialist, whatever — or be marked unnamed, plus one sentence spelling out its most awkward commitment, like having to defend infinite identical repetition of every moment of agony with zero divergence. Also flag any logical combination that seems open but is actually empty, and tell me whether that emptiness is incoherence, sheer unattractiveness, or just an unexplored spot nobody's bothered to defend. I don't want a bibliography or citation list — I'm writing for readers who know the phrase from pop culture but haven't dug into the interpretive figths, so keep it precise but don't assume they have the secondary literature memorized. There's probably a typo or two in this ask itself, but that's par for the course.
