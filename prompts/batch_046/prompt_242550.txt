I defend a specific version of indirect normativity: normative reasons for an agent are fixed by what that agent would endorse in an idealized deliberative position defined not merely by full information, logical coherence, and freedom from weakness of will, but by a counterfactual stability condition—only those commitments that persist across nearby possible worlds in which the agent retains their core practical identity but is corrected for ignorance, cognitive bias, and resource scarcity generate genuine reasons. I want an honest assessment of what would genuinely count against this formulation. In particular, please address three lines of pressure, sorted by how decisive they would be. First, cases in which an agent’s practical identity and the reasons that flow from it seem essentially constituted by conditions of ignorance, partiality, or scarcity, so that idealizing those conditions away does not clarify the agent’s reasons but dissolves them—consider, for example, commitments whose normative force depends on epistemic limitation or on ground projects that are valuable precisely because they are local and non-universalizable. Second, whether specifying “core practical identity” independently of the normative conclusions we wish to reach renders the stability condition circular or gerrymandered, and whether this collapses the view into a disguised form of actualism about present commitments. Third, whether findings in social epistemology regarding the division of cognitive labor show that some reasons are only accessible through distributed, non-idealized deliberation across multiple limited agents, making the single-agent idealization not merely inaccurate but normatively misleading. For each objection, distinguish what would be fatal from what would be merely awkward, indicate whether a defender of the view could absorb it through further qualification without losing the view’s substance, and assess whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability—if so, say whether that insulation is a vice for a normative theory of this kind. I am especially moved by objections that show the idealization removes not a defect but a constitutive condition of a reason’s force.
