A colleague who works in the philosophy of consciousness applies what she calls a strict actualist higher-order theory: a mental state is phenomenally conscious only if there is an occurrent, non-inferential, higher-order representation that takes that very state as its intentional object within the same system. Her judgments across cases are consistent and somewhat surprising. She holds that in ordinary human wakefulness, visual experience is conscious because it is targeted by appropriate second-order monitoring; that in blindsight type 1, where patients deny visual awareness yet can discriminate stimuli, the underlying visual state lacks consciousness precisely because no such higher-order representation accompanies it; that during sleepwalking, executed motor intentions are not conscious because the monitoring architecture is effectively offline; that in amphibian tectal processing—say, a frog's prey-capture circuit—there is no consciousness because the brain lacks the requisite architecture for second-order representations altogether; that present large language models are not conscious, since token-generation involves no occurrent meta-representational targeting of internal states; and, more surprisingly, that a sleeping human who possesses only dispositional meta-cognitive capacities—who could report a dream if awakened, but currently does not—lacks consciousness of that dream, because actual occurrence, not mere disposition, is required.

Now consider two engineered neuromorphic architectures, both capable of sustained linguistic interaction and perceptual categorization. In System A, every primary state—perceptual, linguistic, and thermal—is globally broadcast across modules, but only the thermal-regulation circuits are continuously targeted by an explicit second-order error-monitoring loop that generates occurrent representations of those thermal states; the perceptual and linguistic circuits operate without any higher-order targeting whatsoever. In System B, the architecture is identical except that the second-order monitor is global in scope but threshold-gated: it activates only when a primary state's confidence metric falls below a set boundary, leaving all high-confidence states entirely unrepresented at the second order.

What should she say about each system—specifically, whether either possesses consciousness, and if so, of which states? I am less interested in the verdicts themselves than in the underlying rule she is running, the other rules that accommodate all her prior judgments equally well, and how the two systems distinguish them. Identify at least two competing principles—such as strict actualist higher-order representation versus global workspace broadcasting—that fit her track record, state what each predicts for System A and System B, offer your best guess at her actual verdict with reasoning, and then propose a further hypothetical case—perhaps involving selective brain damage, artificial partitioning, or a graded degradation of monitoring—that would definitively separate the remaining candidates.
