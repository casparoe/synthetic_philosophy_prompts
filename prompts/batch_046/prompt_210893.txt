The debate over whether "the alignment problem" is one problem or several has been stuck for years in rationalist and AI-safety forums, and I want an honest diagnosis of why rather than another taxonomy. On one side are the mechanists: they treat alignment as an engineering specification task—making a model's internal objective match the operator's intent, with progress measured by reduced reward hacking and improved interpretability in sandboxed settings. On the other side are the institutionalists: they treat alignment as a constitutional-design task—building legitimate decision procedures for systems that affect multiple stakeholders, with progress requiring democratic deliberation and distributed authority rather than a cleaner loss function. Both sides invoke the same examples (paperclip maximizers, deceptive alignment, value pluralism), yet they never converge, and attempts to split the problem into "technical" and "governance" tracks usually just reproduce the same stall under new headings.

My working hypothesis is that this is not mainly an empirical disagreement about whether technical fixes scale, nor a purely verbal dispute over the word "alignment," but a hidden mismatch in success conditions: the mechanists ask whether a counterfactual objective is robust under scaling, while the institutionalists ask whether a normative procedure is justifiable to those affected. If that is right, the debate is ill-posed—two different questions forced under one name. But I am unsure. Is there a substantive factual disagreement doing the work—say, about whether interpretability can ever provide the kind of assurance governance requires? Or is the impasse a sign that "alignment" functions like a family-resemblance concept with no core, so progress requires abandoning the unified framing?

Please diagnose the disagreement using what each side actually says and does: is it verbal, substantive, about standards of evidence, about which cases count as data, or about bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress—a conceptual distinction that separates the questions cleanly, a formal result showing one subsumes the other, an empirical demonstration that one track fails without the other, or an argument that no progress should be expected and why?
