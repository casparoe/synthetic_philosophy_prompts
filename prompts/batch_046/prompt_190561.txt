Design a thought experiment that isolates a tension in Otto Neurath's defense of a universal pictographic language (ISOTYPE, or Bildersprache) against the charge that visual representation is theory-laden. Scenario: two researchers in a 1930s social-science institute use an identical set of standardized silhouette icons—representing factory outputs, labor inputs, and distribution arrows—to construct competing explanations of an economic downturn. One researcher reads the pictogram as evidence of overproduction (a supply-side thesis); the other reads the exact same layout, without any added annotations or verbal captions, as evidence of underconsumption (a demand-side thesis). Constraints: (a) neither explanation may rely on text outside the icon set; (b) the disagreement must turn on a genuinely philosophical issue about whether pictograms can be theory-neutral, not on a simple misreading of a symbol's conventional meaning; (c) the setting must remain realistic—no futuristic technology, no omniscient observers, and no appeal to quantum mechanics or artificial intelligence. After presenting the scenario in one paragraph, provide the following analysis: (1) reconstruct Neurath's argument that standardized pictograms avoid the ambiguity of verbal language and thereby serve the physicalist unity of science; (2) pinpoint the precise structural feature of the icon layout that permits the theoretical divergence; (3) specify what happens if that feature is removed—does the case collapse into a trivial ambiguity that Neurath could easily resolve?; and (4) state how a defender of Neurath's program would attack the thought experiment, for example by arguing that the divergence stems from an illicit verbal interpretation smuggled into the viewing process rather than from the pictograms themselves. Keep the response to roughly three paragraphs, and do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or requests for them.
