In the neg util literature there's this weird stall about whether pain and pleasure are commensurable once you accept lexical threshold views. One camp says if suffering has lexical priority then comparing it to pleasure is like weighing color against temperature — different dimensions entirely, no trade-off possible. The other camp says that's just imaginative failure, that we can always conjure a pleasure intense enough to outweigh a scratch, so there's clearly one axis. I sat through a reading group where this circled for months — everyone agreed on the neuroscience of reward pathways, everyone agreed suffering is bad, yet zero movement. My hunch is it's not about facts at all but about hidden standards for what counts as a "genuine" comparison, plus maybe bedrock intuitions about asymmetry. But I also wonder if the two sides are smuggling in different questions: one about phenomological structure, one about rational preference agregation, both calling it "commensurability." Is this a verbal disagreement about the word, a substantive one about value ontology, or just two fields talking past each other? And given your read, is there anything — a distinction, a formal result, a reframed question — that'd actually break the deadlock, or is this the kind of impasse where progress shouldn't be expected?
