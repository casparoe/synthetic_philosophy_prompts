I've been writing about philosphy of science for a few years and this came up at a dinner with two grad students, Maya and Jonah, who were arguing over whether Nozick's experience machine actually proves anything about well-being. Maya was pushing this idea she called causal anchoring — she thinks well-being isn't just good feelings or even authenticity, but whether your emotional life is tied to events that actually happen outside your head. She said if you plug in and your best friend dies inside the simulation, your grief is hollow not because you don't feel it, but because there's no real loss behind it, and that hollowness is a deficit even if the phenomology is identical.

Jonah said that's just smuggling in an objectivist intuition and giving it a fancy name. He argued causal anchoring collapses into either a preference for not being deceived, which is about autonomy, or a confused claim about emotional weight that doesn't survive analysis — if the grief feels the same and serves the same functions, then by hedonist or functionalist standards the well-being is intact. He also said Maya's view makes well-being depend on metaphysical luck: if the real world happens to match the simulation by coincidence, your grief suddenly gets heavy even though nothing inside changed.

Maya came back with her best point: autonomy can't explain why someone who knowingly chooses the machine still lacks something. If you fully consent and know it's simulated, there's no deception and no autonomy violation, yet she thinks the grief is still hollow. So it can't just be about autonomy. Jonah replied that then she's just asserting a brute intuition that reality-contact matters, and she hasn't shown why that intuition should override the subject's own report of perfect satisfaction.

They turned to me since I was just listening and asked me to referee. I honestly couldn't call it. Can you go through their exhange step by step and say who's right at each move? Is causal anchoring actually distinct from autonomy or hedonism, or is it just a relabel? And what are both of them missing — maybe something about counterfactual dependancy in well-being theories? Don't need any reading lists, just the logic laid out plain.
