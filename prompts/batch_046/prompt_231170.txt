Write the desiderata that a theory of Davidsonian radical interpretation must meet to count as philosophically adequate—not as an exegesis of Davidson's published arguments, but as a live account of how an interpreter assigns beliefs and meanings to a speaker with whom she shares no prior conceptual scheme or translation manual. Your specification should cover at least the following five requirements: (i) how truth-conditions are assigned to the speaker's sentences in the absence of bilingual dictionaries or shared logical forms; (ii) how to distinguish genuine disagreement over belief content from mere semantic divergence, without presupposing a neutral metalanguage in which both parties' claims can be expressed; (iii) how to accommodate systematic, localized error in the speaker's beliefs without collapsing into global skepticism about the interpretive project; (iv) the problem of "asymmetric predicate export"—cases in which the interpreter's richer ontological vocabulary permits her to attribute beliefs using predicates that have no direct analogue in the speaker's language, and whether doing so necessarily distorts the speaker's actual commitments or is an unavoidable feature of charitable interpretation; and (v) whether the principle of charity functions as a normative constraint on rational belief attribution, as a predictive heuristic for behavioral explanation, or as an ontological claim about meaning constitution. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual tension that makes it a genuine constraint rather than a partisan preference, flag any pair or subset you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable, and identify which single desideratum does the most damage to standard rival approaches—such as descriptivist theories of reference, strong Quinean indeterminacy of translation, or causal-historical accounts of content. Then, briefly and without numerical scoring, assess how Davidson's own charity-based view, Quine's radical translation thesis, and a descriptivist alternative fare against your list, noting the specific failure point each reveals. Conclude with a judgment: is radical interpretation best understood as an epistemological method, an ontological thesis about how meaning is fixed, or a normative ethics of belief attribution? Write in polished, formal American English.
