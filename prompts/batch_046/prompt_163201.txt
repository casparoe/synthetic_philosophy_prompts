I'm taking a graduate seminar on concept induction, and we recently worked through Mikhail Bongard's classic twelve-box puzzles, in which six examples on the left obey one visual rule and six on the right obey its inverse, with the task being to induce the separating concept from only these few instances. After class, two members of my study group got into a sustained disagreement about what actually makes these puzzles cognitively demanding and why they remain hard for modern learning systems. Alex defended a "positive-template" account: he argued that the left-hand boxes supply a family of positive instances from which the solver extracts invariant structural relations—say, specific configurations of convexity, orientation, or cardinality—while the right-hand boxes function mainly as a rejection set that prunes false positives. On this view, the central bottleneck is feature extraction; contemporary deep-learning failures on Bongard-style tasks occur because the networks lack the appropriate geometric or relational primitives needed to construct the positive template in the first place.

Blake countered with what he called a "complement-space co-definition" view. He maintained that the right-hand boxes are not merely passive noise but actively construct the concept by specifying its boundary conditions, so that solving the puzzle requires maintaining two coupled representations—the concept and its complement—simultaneously. The difficulty for artificial systems, on this account, is not missing primitives but an inability to perform "negative-space restructuring," whereby encountering a near-miss negative example forces a wholesale revision of the feature hierarchy rather than a simple elimination of a candidate rule. Blake further proposed a more specific mechanism he termed "asymmetric analogical transfer": he claimed that human solvers map structural relations from the left set to the right far more aggressively than in the reverse direction, which explains why people tend to succeed when the positive set is heterogeneous but the negative set is uniform, yet struggle with the reverse configuration.
