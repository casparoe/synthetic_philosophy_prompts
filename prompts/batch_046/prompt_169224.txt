I write for a magazine covering science and mental health, and I need a reusable decision procedure—something I can run in about fifteen minutes before filing—to determine how I should frame any psychiatric diagnosis in a story, specifically through the lens of Ian Hacking's looping effects for interactive human kinds. The underlying principle is that being classified can change the people classified, which in turn changes the classification itself, but this dynamic is routinely misused either to dismiss a diagnosis as "mere social construction" or to ignore it entirely in favor of a purely biological framing. Translate that principle into an ordered checklist: the exact questions to ask about a category's clinical history, its uptake by the people it classifies, and the feedback between self-understanding and diagnostic criteria; what counts as a sufficient answer at each step; where the procedure stops with a clear framing directive (for instance, that the category should be reported as an interactive kind rather than an indifferent natural kind); where it must halt and return the decision to me because the remaining issue is normative or editorial rather than descriptive; and a small set of draft headlines or ledes I can apply as test sentences to catch whether my framing has slipped into either dismissal or biological reductionism. The constraints are practical: I have no research library access at deadline, my editor must sign off, and I apply this to two or three stories per month. Then run the procedure on three cases: prolonged grief disorder, added in the DSM-5-TR revision; autism spectrum disorder, given the extensive self-identification and advocacy movements that feed back into criteria and prevalence; and major depressive disorder, where I want the procedure to reach a conclusion I will actively resist—most likely that looping effects are genuinely present but do not license my usual shorthand framing of depression as straightforward brain-chemistry dysfunction with no interactive dimension—thereby forcing me to report an ontologically ambiguous status. Finally, state precisely what the procedure cannot settle, such as whether the presence of looping effects makes a psychiatric category illegitimate, and flag every point where the journalist's values, not the procedure, must choose the frame.
