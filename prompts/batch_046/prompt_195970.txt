For the past year I've used an open-source neurofeedback system that translates my EEG and eye-tracking data into a real-time "user profile"—an avatar that reports my attention, emotional valence, and stated intentions. I've noticed a disquieting asymmetry. During deep absorption in work, the avatar blanks out and reports "no observer present," yet my experience feels richest then. During anxious rumination, by contrast, the underlying signals are scattered high-frequency noise, but the interface smooths them into a coherent "worrying agent" with clear goals. This leads me to a practical dilemma: I'm considering letting the avatar guide a major career decision by displaying my "true priorities," but I now suspect that the continuous "me" it depicts may just be a user-friendly rendering of discontinuous processes, not something it tracks. Is there a real inner self being misrepresented by this interface, or is the interface itself constructing the only self I have? And if the latter, should I still defer to its reports about what "I" want?
