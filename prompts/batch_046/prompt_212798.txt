I've been playing around with a pretty strong Davidsonian claim, maybe stronger than Davidson himself intended. The view is that radical interpretation doesn't just constrain our attributions—its actually constitutive of belief content in a way that rules out deep conceptual incommensurability. Specifically, if you have two communities that organize spatial reasoning differently—say one uses absolute geographic coordinates embedded in grammar and the other uses egocentric left-right-front-back—Davidson's principle of charity forces us to translate both into our own scheme rather than recognizing genuinely alien conceptual structures. So the position is that apparent cases of incommensurability are always superficial, never deep, and this applies even to artificial systems trained on non-egocentric data.

But I want to know what would genuinely count against this. Not just the usual Kuhn-style objections, but specific things: maybe empirical findings in linguistic anthropology showing some spatial frameworks are genuinely untranslatable without loss, or arguments that charity applies differently to perceptual beliefs versus theoretical ones, or the possibility that an AI could develop beliefs we can't charitably map onto ours. Which escape routes are live and which are just logical possibilities?
