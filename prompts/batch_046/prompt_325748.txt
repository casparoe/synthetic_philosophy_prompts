I'm planning a single ninety-minute seminar for final-year political philosophy students on Amartya Sen's claim that famine is typically a failure of entitlements rather than a natural scarcity event, taking in the Bengal famine of 1943, the Irish famine of the 1840s, and the Great Leap Forward. The students will arrive treating famine as essentially an agricultural collapse — a natural disaster with political complications at most — and I want them to leave grasping that starvation can occur amid adequate aggregate supply, with the causal and moral structure reversed. In particular, I want to contrast the direct entitlement collapse seen in Bengal — wage-earners priced out of rice markets — with what might be termed 'bureaucratic entitlement extraction under statistical illusion' in the Great Leap Forward, where distorted local harvest reports allowed state procurement to outstrip actual surplus. Plan the explanatory arc: which conceptual distinction must be secured before any case can do work — food availability decline versus exchange entitlement failure, direct versus trade-based entitlement, or democratic protection versus market mechanism — and in what order should the three cases appear so that students see the mechanism shift without conflating colonial extraction, market failure, and command-economy information breakdown? Identify the single carrying example: which case should carry the core idea, and what precise features must it possess (and lack) so that students do not reduce it to a narrative of British cruelty alone? Map the misconceptions they will form in sequence — I expect first that Sen denies all harvest failure, then that democratic accountability is sufficient rather than necessary, then that command economies fail for identical market reasons — and specify the exact point in the arc where each should be intercepted. Provide a check for understanding that reveals whether they have grasped the entitlement mechanism itself, rather than merely repeating that politics caused the famine. Finally, state explicitly what should be left out at this level — for instance, whether formal entitlement-set modelling should appear — and justify that omission. Write this as a teaching plan, not as the seminar content.
