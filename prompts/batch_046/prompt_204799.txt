Consider the following argument, which I have been turning over, about inside-view reasoning, outside-view base rates, and epistemic modesty.

(1) Rational forecasting requires both an inside view — the causal mechanisms particular to the case — and an outside view — the statistical base rate for an appropriate reference class.

(2) Epistemic modesty demands that when these two views conflict, we should defer to the outside view, because inside-view narratives are uniquely prone to overfitting and motivated reasoning.

(3) Yet selecting which reference class applies — deciding which similarities are causally relevant and which are noise — is itself an exercise of inside-view judgement.

(4) Therefore every application of the outside view presupposes a prior inside-view classification decision.

(5) If modesty requires us to suppress our inside view after classification, then we may always choose a classification that yields the base rate we prefer, rendering the outside view a rubber stamp.

(6) If, instead, modesty requires us to suppress our inside view even during classification, then we lack the judgement needed to apply the outside view at all.

(7) So epistemic modesty either collapses into arbitrariness or prohibits the very reasoning it requires.

(8) Consequently, the principle that we ought to combine inside and outside views is either empty or self-defeating.

The conclusion feels suspicious: it seems to show that a well-established forecasting norm undermines itself.
