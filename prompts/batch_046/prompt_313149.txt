Ryle's distinction, set out in his 1945 presidential address Knowing How and Knowing That, separates propositional knowledge (knowing that p) from practical competence (knowing how to φ). The intellectualist legend, which Ryle rejects, holds that every intelligent performance is preceded by an internal theoretical operation in which one contemplates a set of propositions and then executes the action.
