Write a one-page proposal for a one-year dissertation fellowship in philosophy. The project addresses what makes consent valid, with particular attention to deception, coercion, and incompetence. Your guiding but half-formed idea is that philosophical and legal treatments typically classify these as three independent vitiating conditions—each invalidating consent through a distinct mechanism—yet they may share a deeper structural failure that the taxonomy obscures. You want to investigate whether they are better understood as manifestations of a single breakdown in what you term reflexive authorization: the consenter’s capacity to endorse a specific outcome as genuinely her own in a way that cannot be performed by a proxy or substitute decision-maker who possesses identical information. The proposal must include the following: (1) a precise statement of the research question—does reflexive authorization provide a unified foundation for the three conditions, or do deception, coercion, and incompetence remain irreducibly distinct in ways this framework misses?; (2) an explanation of why existing accounts leave the question open, noting that they tend to classify by surface causal mechanism rather than by the authority relation disrupted; (3) a concrete method describing what you will actually do month by month over the year, including comparative analysis of clinical consent protocols and contractual adhesion cases to test whether the reflexive-authorization framework distinguishes valid from invalid consent more finely than the standard triad; (4) a clear account of what success looks like—a structural account that preserves distinctions among the three conditions while explaining their common root—and an honest description of failure, such as the framework collapsing them into one without explanatory gain, or failing to accommodate standard counterexamples; and (5) a realistic scope assessment stating which parts of the argument can be completed in twelve months and which must remain for subsequent work. Do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or reading list; refer to existing positions and arguments by descriptive summary only, and indicate where references will be added later. Maintain a formal tone appropriate to a fellowship committee, and frame the project so that its philosophical contribution does not depend on empirical data collection beyond what is available through conceptual analysis of documented practices.
