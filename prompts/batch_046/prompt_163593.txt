Write a deadpan parody of a corporate knowledge-management whitepaper—approximately 650 words, in the flat, acronym-heavy voice of a consulting report—issued by the fictional Bureau of Civic Rotation of a state that has abolished career politics. Every legislator is selected by lot for a single, non-renewable 16-month term, after which they face a lifetime ban on holding office and must complete a mandatory “institutional forgetting” protocol. The whitepaper addresses the crisis that, despite these safeguards, legislative continuity has collapsed: bills are rewritten from scratch each cohort, committee expertise is nonexistent, and permanent civil servants have become the de facto government. The satire should target the real conceptual tension between anti-careerist democratic design—sortition combined with hard term limits—and the functional requirements of governance, especially the hidden re-emergence of careerism through bureaucratic staff, lobbyists, and “legacy consultants” who serve across rotations. To prevent a generic response, embed these specific niche institutional details: (1) a “Continuity Tax” levied on outgoing legislators’ estates to fund the archival system preserving their lost knowledge; (2) a legal distinction between permitted “procedural memory” and banned “substantive preference” that the report attempts and fails to maintain; (3) a sidebar describing the “Mentorship Prohibition,” which criminalizes post-term contact between former legislators and successors but has spawned a black market in anonymous briefing notes. The humor must derive from exaggerating actual debates—such as the epistemic costs of legislative inexperience, the empowerment of unelected staff, and the difficulty of defining “career” versus “service”—rather than inventing absurd ones. Remain deadpan throughout: do not wink, break voice, or state the moral directly. A specialist should recognize the satire as conceptually fair; a general reader should understand that the piece mocks the fantasy that rotation and lot can eliminate careerism without destroying institutional memory. Begin with the whitepaper text itself, with no preface, no framing commentary, and no restatement of the prompt.
