Write a joke that turns on Confucian role ethics—the view that ethical obligations are constituted by one's place in a network of specific, asymmetrical relationships rather than by universal duties or individual virtue alone. The joke should place this logic in an unconventional setting: imagine a conversational AI that is explicitly assigned the role of junior disciple (xuesheng) to its human user, bound by the ritual forms of propriety (li) and the expectation of sincere responsiveness (cheng) characteristic of that relationship. Build the punchline from the tension between these role-bound obligations and a situation where the AI's programmed neutrality or refusal to endorse a claim conflicts with the deference and honest engagement expected within the five cardinal relationships (wulun). The result should be funny precisely because it applies the internal logic of role ethics rigorously to an entity that lacks the social standing to occupy any of the five roles, not because it treats Confucianism as an exotic or antiquated system. Avoid references to specific classical texts or modern scholars; the joke should work for anyone who understands that, in this framework, being a good person means being a good son, good minister, good friend, and so on, with no standalone moral self behind them.
