I am preparing the conceptual framework for a graduate seminar paper that crosses Buddhist metaphysics with analytic philosophy of personal identity, and I want to move past the well-worn framing that simply pits Buddhist reductionism against Lockean continuity in introductory surveys. The specific problem I want to tackle is more granular: what must an adequate reconciliation of anatta—the denial of a permanent, unitary self across the five aggregates—actually deliver if it is to remain philosophically serious about diachronic moral agency? I was discussing this with a colleague who argued that once essentialism is abandoned, questions of personal identity become purely conventional; I think that view collapses too quickly, but I need a rigorous specification of where the pressure points actually lie before I can defend a more nuanced position.

Specifically, write the full desiderata that any theory reconciling anatta with functional personal continuity must meet. The account should explain how karmic causation tracks particular psychophysical streams rather than diffusing globally, preserve the phenomenological datum of first-person mineness or embodied ownership without reintroducing an essential ego, maintain the asymmetry of rational self-concern and moral responsibility across temporal stages, and respect the standard analysis of aggregates as impermanent, dependently originated processes. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or conceptual tension behind it—for example, why mere causal continuity might fail to ground moral accountability, or why narrative coherence might conflict with a rigorous momentariness thesis. Distinguish the non-negotiable requirements from the camp-specific preferences, such as strict mereological reductionism, radical phenomenological dissolution, or conventionalist two-level strategies, and identify any subset that appears jointly unsatisfiable, such as whether robust diachronic responsibility can survive a complete dissolution of the aggregates into impersonal processes. Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal tensions rather than on scoring existing contenders, though a brief assessment of how reductionist bundle theories, narrative-constructivist accounts, and conventional-ultimate two-level views fare against the list is welcome. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the framework to stand on its own conceptual architecture.
