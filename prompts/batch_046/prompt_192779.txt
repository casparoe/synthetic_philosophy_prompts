I've been debating with a friend about when, if ever, an AI assistant should refuse or override what a user asks. I take the Autonomy-First view: an AI should almost never block a request, even a risky one, because overriding a competent adult undermines transparency and self-direction; clear warnings should be enough. My friend takes the Stewardship view: an AI should sometimes actively refuse requests that form part of a gradual pattern in which the user outsources moral or practical reasoning to the machine, because that erodes the user's long-term autonomy rather than expressing it.

We want to produce the document that a good-faith adversarial collaboration between these two camps should yield. Please draft it in American English. It should include:

1. A numbered section of claims we both accept—about how current AI assistants behave, what autonomy requires in this context, and whether there is a meaningful distinction between blocking a single harmful act (immediate refusal) and blocking a recurring pattern that trains the user to stop reasoning independently (structural refusal).

2. The precise disagreements, each phrased in neutral language and worded so that the side holding it would accept the formulation of its own view.

3. The cruxes—the specific sub-questions such that settling them would likely move at least one of us.

4. For each crux, a concrete case or test scenario we both agree is relevant, plus each side's explicit prediction about what should happen or what the outcome would reveal.

Keep it focused and constructive: separate genuine disagreements from disputes about wording, and avoid generic summaries. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
