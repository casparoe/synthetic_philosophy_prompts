Write an essay on psychiatric classification and Hacking's looping effects. Specifically, imagine clinicians propose a new category called "chronic anticipatory fatigue" for people exhausted by constantly predicting algorithmic outputs. Argue whether this kind of diagnosis should be designed to resist looping—where the label changes the behavior it describes—or whether looping is actually a feature that helps patients make sense of their distress and access care. Weigh the danger that we're manufacturing suffering aginst the practical value of giving people a shared framework. Reach a real conclusion: should diagnostic manuals aim for loop-resistant categories, or should they accept that some feedback between label and experience is unavoidable and maybe even good?
