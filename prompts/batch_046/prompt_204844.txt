I am developing a conceptual distinction between what I will call prosperity legitimacy — the claim that sustained economic growth and rising living standards can substitute for democratic authorization — and order legitimacy, which holds that the prevention of chaos and the maintenance of predictable social coordination can perform the same normative work. I am applying this distinction to regimes such as Singapore and post-reform China not to defend either, but to test whether these represent two genuinely different normative arguments or merely rhetorical variations on output-based justification.

Within this framework, I see four open conceptual lines. First, whether performance legitimacy of either kind requires a counterfactual comparison to democratic alternatives, or whether it can be evaluated in isolation from what democratic procedures would have produced. Second, whether there is a coherent temporal structure to such claims — specifically, whether early-generation beneficiaries can bind later generations, and whether prosperity and order differ in how they transmit across generations. Third, whether the framework collapses into a variant of tacit consent once we specify what counts as acceptance of outcomes, and if so, whether the collapse affects both species equally. Fourth, whether there is a meaningful normative difference between performance measured as aggregate welfare gain versus performance measured as catastrophe avoidance.

If you were advising someone with roughly two years of conceptual philosophical work — not empirical social science, but architecture of arguments — which of these should be treated as upstream of the others? I want a defended ordering rather than a survey: which resolution would most reshape the remaining debates, which is most tractable with conceptual tools alone, and which, if settled unfavorably, would render the prosperity-order distinction useless for normative evaluation. Please state the criteria you are using — dependency, tractability, or stakes for practice — and indicate what kind of result over that period would cause you to revise the ranking.
