I've been arguing with my roommate about infinity stuff—Hilbert's hotel, Cantor's different sizes, whether actual infinities exist out in physical space. There's this pattern I keep seeing and I want to know if philosophers have actually named it, or if I'm just inventing a category.

Here's what happens: people are totally fine with shifting every hotel guest up one room to free up space. They accept you can pair every natural number with an even number, and they don't blink at both sets being the same size even though one's inside the other. So they're clearly not pure finitists—they're okay with completed infinite sets in the abstract. But then you ask about the reals being uncountable, or about an actually infinite spatial universe, and they suddenly push back. What's weird is the objection usually isn't "infinity is impossible." Instead they say something like "with the hotel there's a clear rule telling you exactly where everyone goes, but with the reals there's no step-by-step procedure that assigns every one a place in a list." Or with physics: "you can't have infinite space because there's no boundary or center telling you where you stand in it."

So the pattern is that they accept an infinite structure only when there's an explicit, uniform, generative rule—like n goes to n plus one, or n goes to 2n—that tells you where every member sits. But if the totality is "given all at once" without that procedural transparency, they reject it, even though in both cases you're dealing with a completed infinity. It's like they have a hidden filter: infinity is okay if it's mechanically transparent, suspicious if it's structurally opaque.

Does this have a name? Is it a recognized variant of finitism, or closer to some kind of constructivism? And what's the exact boundary—does it concern what we can know (epistemic access to all the members) or what can exist (metaphysical completeness without a generating rule)? I'm not just after a label; I want to know if this is a coherent position with its own literature, or if it's just an inconsistent intuition that collapses once you press it. If there's no established term, how would you draw the line around it so it doesn't just cover every case of "I don't like this infinity"?
