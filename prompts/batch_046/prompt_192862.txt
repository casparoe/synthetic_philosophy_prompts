I run a workshop for about 18 undergrads, 90 minutes. I want them to exeperience why Athenian democracy's failures—Mytilene, Sicily, Socrates—aren't separate accidents but one structural bug: direct democracy treats defeat and dissent as pollution, uses rapid vote reversal like a ritual purge, and lets emotional contagion override long-term strategy. Design a simulation/game with assigned roles (assembly members, generals, a dissenter, a herald who can call for instant re-vote), rules, and a payoff scheme without real money. They face three linked rounds: a rebellious ally, an overseas expedition, and whether to silence the dissenter. The rules should force them to feel time pressure, the ease of reversing thier decisions, and how rhetorical framing shifts payoffs. Include a timeline, a debrief that maps each round onto the actual history, an honest note on which feature of the idea can't be reproduced in a classroom (probably the real religious/purification context or imperial economic stakes), and what will predictbly go wrong the first run. Keep it loose.
