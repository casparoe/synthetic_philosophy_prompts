Please explain the conceptual tension that arises when a community deliberately sustains a collective narrative about its past that conflicts with the documented historical record—not from ignorance of the documents, but as an intentional practice of remembrance that operates outside the authority of the archive. Specifically, address whether this form of remembrance should be interpreted as advancing a competing factual claim about what occurred, or as performing a normative, identity-constituting function for which factual correction is conceptually irrelevant. In addition, examine whether separating these descriptive and performative dimensions is genuinely coherent, or whether that separation already privileges an archival epistemology and thereby dissolves the autonomy that collective memory claims for itself.
