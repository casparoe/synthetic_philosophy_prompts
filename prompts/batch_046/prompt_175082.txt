ok so here's roughly where i've landed on dreams and experience, and i want to check if it hangs together or if i'm helping myself to something:

(1) i buy Malcolm's argument that dream reports don't have the error-conditions of waking reports — there's no independant check that could prove i'm wrong about what i dreamed.

(2) but i reject Dennett's cassette theory, not because i think dreams are full-blown experiences during sleep, but because i think there's an "ambient presence" in sleep — not enough to be an experience with content, but enough that calling it a total phenomenological blank is false.

(3) i think first-person authority requires live monitoring capacity, which is offline during sleep, so an immediate post-waking report is more like editing a rough draft than direct introspection.

(4) i apply the same idea to general anesthesia: no experience, but also not a "gap" in a stream — just a suspension of both synchronic and diachronic phenomenology.

(5) and i still think sleep structures selfhood over time, not through experienced content, but through the diachronic pattern of being awake versus being suspended.

my worry is that (1) actually pushes me toward Dennett — if there's no live check, maybe the content is just constructed on waking. and (3) sounds like i'm just describing the cassette theory i said i reject in (2). also (4) and (5) feel like they clash — if sleep suspends diachronic phenomenology, how can it structure identity? but maybe that last one's only apparent?
