Consider the family-state analogy that figures prominently in debates between nationalism and cosmopolitanism: just as parents bear special obligations to their own children that they do not bear to strangers’ children, citizens bear special obligations to their compatriots that they do not bear to foreigners. Defenders of compatriot priority invoke this mapping to resist cosmopolitan demands for global distributive equality—if familial partiality is not merely permitted but morally required, then national partiality is analogously justified.
