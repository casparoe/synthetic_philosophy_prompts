Question: Does Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge preserve epistemic closure? Discuss specifically whether knowing you have hands lets you know you're not a brain in a vat, and explain how Nozick's counterfactual conditions apply to both beliefs.

Student answer:

Nozick says knowledge needs tracking: p must be true, I must believe p, and if p were false I wouldn't believe p, plus if p is true in nearby worlds I still believe it. So for "I have hands," I track it because in worlds where I don't have hands I wouldn't think I do. But for "I'm not a BIV," I don't track it since in BIV worlds I still think I'm not. Now, closure says if I know p and p entails q then I know q. Since having hands entails not being a BIV, closure would force me to know I'm not a BIV, which I don't. Nozick solves this by borrowing Dretske's relevant-alternatives idea: he restricts the modal space to nearby worlds, so closure only works inside that space, creating a kind of non-monotonic tracking space. That's why he actually rejects closure altogether—if you restrict the worlds, you can't transfer knowledge across entailments that reach outside the neighborhood. The adherence condition makes sure beliefs stay stable inside the neighborhood, but once an entailment points to a distant skeptical world, tracking breaks and closure fails with it.
