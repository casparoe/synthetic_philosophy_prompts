I run a grad-level philosophy workshop, about 8 students, 90 minutes. I want an activity that makes them feel the transparency trap — this idea that the moment you introspect to verify your motive is morally pure, you've already smuggled in a second-order desire (to feel good about your purity) that contaminates the first-order act. Don't lecture on Kant or cite the overjustification literature; just build a game or structured role-play with real small stakes, like hidden "motive cards" and point-trades where particpants have to choose whether to help someone at a cost. The rules should be arranged so that the people who work hardest to monitor and report their own motives end up with worse outcomes or at least feel the contradiction physically. Include a timeline, the card/rule mechanics, and then a debrief that maps each stage onto the trap, says what will predictably go wrong (probably people gaming the honesty cards), and is frank about which feature — long-term character formation? actual self-deception? — the 90-minute format can't reproduce and I have to supply verbally. Keep it runnable with just paper and pens.
