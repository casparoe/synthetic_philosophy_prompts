Draft a formal policy memo for the university’s Curriculum and Character Education Oversight Committee, which must vote on a proposal to replace the existing Virtue and Leadership certificate with a new program titled Situational Architecture for Ethical Behavior. The proposal argues that situationist findings in social psychology show weak cross-situational consistency in moral behavior, and therefore moral education should abandon the language of cultivating stable internal virtues—courage, temperance, justice as personal dispositions—in favor of training students in meta-situational design: the practice of selecting, modifying, and avoiding social contexts so that prosocial conduct becomes probable without relying on robust character traits. The proposal concedes that individuals differ in their willingness to practice such design, but frames this difference as a skill in environmental engineering rather than as a moral virtue. Proponents claim this is pragmatically responsible rather than anti-ethical; opponents, including several committee members, contend it concedes too much to situationism, erases the normative distinction between being a just person and merely inhabiting just circumstances, and violates the university’s mission to form persons rather than only manage contexts.

Your memo should recommend approval, rejection, or conditional approval with specific amendments; present the reasoning that makes the recommendation correct for the committee to act on; and include an honest, substantive treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side—specifically addressing whether a reconstructed virtue ethics centered on meta-situational capacities can preserve what is genuinely normative about character, or whether that reconstruction collapses virtue ethics into instrumental self-management. The document should be actionable for a vote: state a clear bottom line, provide criteria reviewers can apply to the proposal’s curricular claims, and avoid any call for bibliographies, further literature reviews, or external citations.
