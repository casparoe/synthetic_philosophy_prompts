Please argue that Zhuangzi's butterfly dream should be read not as a conventional epistemic skepticism about whether one is currently dreaming, but as an argument for the ontological parity of waking and dreaming states grounded in the instability of self-identification across transformative experiences. Defend the claim that if the boundary between "Zhuangzi dreaming he is a butterfly" and "a butterfly dreaming it is Zhuangzi" cannot be resolved from within either state, then the Cartesian strategy of using the cogito as an Archimedean point collapses: the "I" that attempts to ground certainty through introspective self-certainty is itself subject to the same transformative indeterminacy, so appealing to that "I" to privilege waking consciousness over dream consciousness begs the question. Work only from the conceptual structure of the parable and the logic of the cogito; do not invoke modern neuroscience, psychological studies, or named secondary literature. Your argument should show why the parable undermines not merely our knowledge of which state we inhabit, but the very assumption that there is a stable subject capable of adjudicating between them.
