Write a reusable decision procedure for a municipal zoning-reform coordinator who must approve, conditionally approve, or reject proposed land-use amendments within ten business days. Ground the procedure in the principle of a safe Pareto improvement, defined here as a change that is permissible only if (i) at least one affected party is made strictly better off by a credible welfare metric, (ii) no affected party is made strictly worse off by any credible metric, and (iii) the change introduces no hidden or tail-risk externalities that could, under plausible scenarios, convert an apparent net gain into a net loss for anyone. The coordinator may consult one staff environmental engineer and one appointed community representative; the planning director must sign off on any rejection or conditional approval. Provide an ordered checklist with explicit tests for identifying hidden externalities, resolving conflicts between welfare metrics, and flagging points where professional judgment cannot be eliminated. Apply the procedure to three cases: (a) converting a disused rail yard into a public park with no adjacent residential displacement; (b) upzoning a low-density residential block for mixed-use development that promises tax revenue but carries indirect displacement risk through rent pressure and unmodeled stormwater load; and (c) a proposal that benefits every currently registered voter in the district but excludes recent undocumented residents from the welfare calculus entirely. Include one case in which the procedure produces an answer the coordinator will find unwelcome. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure cannot settle and who must bear that residual risk.
