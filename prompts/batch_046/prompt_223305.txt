Write a villanelle (nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, two repeating refrains) on Nietzsche's eternal recurrence that treats the idea not merely as an ethical test but as a self-referential loop: the very moment of recognizing recurrence is itself destined to recur, so affirming the loop means affirming the burden of its own disclosure. Let one refrain convey the demon's hypothetical announcement that this life, with every detail intact, will return eternally and unchanged; let the other refrain express the cosmological premise that finite states distributed across infinite time must eventually repeat. Structure the poem so the refrains take turns through the stanzas and finally converge in the closing couplet, using that formal meeting to dramatize the tension between the existential demand for amor fati and the physical inevitability of repetition—showing that the two interpretations keep passing each other rather than collapsing into one. The speaker should end by realizing that to embrace recurrence is also to embrace the weight of knowing it. The poem should be philosophically precise enough that a reader acquainted with the cosmological and existential interpretations recognizes both, and compelling enough to stand alone for someone encountering the idea for the first time.
