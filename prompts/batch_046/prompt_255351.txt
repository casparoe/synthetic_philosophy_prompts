The scholar counters that Schrödinger intended only an epistemological monism—the absence of a metaphysical boundary between knower and known—which leaves individual observers numerically distinct and actually makes the measurement problem more acute, not less.

For each reading, indicate whether it is genuinely present in Schrödinger’s known engagement with Vedanta, whether it requires denying the numerical distinctness of successive selves or twins, and whether it actually supports the physicist’s consolation about death or his argument about measurement—or whether it undermines one or both.
