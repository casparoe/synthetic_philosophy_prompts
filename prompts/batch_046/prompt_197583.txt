I hold the view that a consumer-based teleosemantics—of the sort Millikan develops, where representational content is fixed by the proper function of the mechanisms that consume a representation rather than by the conditions that produce it—succeeds in naturalizing mental content in a way that causal-covariance accounts (Dretske, Fodor) do not. Specifically, I think it solves the disjunction problem without ad hoc restrictions, explains misrepresentation as straightforward malfunction rather than statistical deviation, and can accommodate empty representations (unicorns, false predictions) by appealing to the normal explanatory conditions for the consumer mechanism rather than to actual causal correlations. I also think the standard swampman objection misses the mark because historical selection is not an additional property but constitutive of the functional kind in question, and that the charge of circularity—using normative notions of proper function to explain normative mental content—is blunted once we see evolutionary selection as a purely descriptive, historical filter rather than a prescriptive rule.

What would count against this? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a rehearsed list of objections. Which specific results or arguments—perhaps about the indeterminacy of biological function, about cases where consumer mechanisms serve multiple incompatible representational roles (functional pluralism), about the inability to individuate consumer mechanisms without already presupposing representational content, or about evolutionary history being too coarse-grained to assign determinate contents—would genuinely threaten the view? Which of these would a committed teleosemanticist concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss as resting on a misunderstanding of how proper functions are individuated? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and identify where the reasoning is most likely to fail—whether in the move from evolutionary history to specific content, in the claim that malfunction explains error without circularity, or in the treatment of empty representations.
