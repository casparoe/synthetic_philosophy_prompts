I chair the certification board for a historical-science data archive. A research consortium proposes to re-certify a foundational 20th-century atmospheric-measurement dataset by applying a statistical correction that treats the original instrument-calibration logs as systematically rather than randomly erroneous. Our charter mandates evaluation of methodological soundness, but the board is split along lines central to hinge epistemology: one faction argues that treating the calibration framework itself as fallible undermines the very conditions under which soundness can be assessed—akin to doubting the ruler rather than the measurement—while the other faction insists that no archival hinge should be insulated from empirical revision. Draft the board's decision memo recommending whether to approve, reject, or conditionally approve the proposed review. The memo should propose concrete evaluative criteria that allow reviewers to distinguish legitimate dataset correction from framework-collapse; it should treat the strongest hinge-protection argument fairly rather than dismissing it as mere conservatism; and it should specify a timeline together with one procedural safeguard—such as an independent calibration audit—that addresses both concerns without requiring either side to concede that the other is incoherent.
