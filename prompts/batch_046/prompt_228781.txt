I've been noticing this wierd pattern in conversations about climate, meat, voting — anywhere people talk about tiny personal actions that add up to big harm. Someone says "me not buying beef once doesn't change the suply chain, my flight won't melt the ice," using the individual scale to dode blame. Then the same person says "but if everyone did it we'd be fine," using the collective scale to justify outrage or a boycott. It's not hypocrisy exactly; it's sliding between two pictures of causation depending on whether they need comfort or motivation. Is there a name for that slide? I know about collective action problems and the free rider thing, but this feels different — it's not about cheating, it's about which frame you reach for. Is it related to the fallacy of division, or is that not it? And if philosophers don't have a box for it, what would you call it — scale-hopping? Where do its edges sit, like does it work for voting where your ballot literally never tips?
