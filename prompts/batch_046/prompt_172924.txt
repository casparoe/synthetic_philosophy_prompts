I have been trying to read al-Ghazālī's occasionalism not as a skeptical episode but as a positive metaphysics: in his critique of the Peripatetics he denies that fire contains any intrinsic power to burn, insisting instead that God creates each particular burning directly at the moment of contact. Modern dispositional essentialists hold that causal powers are essential to properties—flammability is part of what fire is. It therefore seems to me that occasionalism is essentially the universal denial of dispositional essentialism applied to all created things, with God functioning as the single substance in which all causal powers inhere. So here is my question: if we formalize occasionalism as the thesis that every dispositional property is really a property of God rather than of the object we call its bearer, do we not collapse Ash'arite theology into a kind of panentheistic essentialism far closer to Spinoza than to al-Ghazālī? And if that formalization is wrong, what in his actual account specifically blocks the inference from "created objects lack powers" to "God possesses them as essential attributes"?
