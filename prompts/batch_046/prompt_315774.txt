I am working through the relationship between global workspace architectures and phenomenal consciousness for a chapter in my dissertation, and I want to make sure I am not collapsing distinctions that should remain separate. I understand Baars-style broadcasting as explaining access consciousness: representations become globally available for report, reasoning, and behavioral control. Block's distinction then seems designed to show that such global broadcasting is not sufficient for phenomenal consciousness, since we can conceive of a system—functionally identical in its broadcasting dynamics—lacking any subjective qualitative character. Here is where I am stuck: in actual human cognition, the representations that enter the workspace are not merely broadcast, but are also indexed by a recursive self-model that marks them as belonging to the subject. This makes it look as though phenomenal consciousness is not an independent property at all, but rather globally available information that carries a self-referential tag. If that is correct, then global workspace theory, supplemented with self-modeling, would seem to account for phenomenality rather than merely for access. Is the error in assuming that self-modeling transforms the ontological status of broadcast representations, or is there a deeper reason why Block's distinction survives even when we embed the workspace inside a self-tracking architecture?
