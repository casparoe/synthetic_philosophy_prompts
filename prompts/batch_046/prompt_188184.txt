You're giving the opening remarks at a workshop called Mind in Matter — about half condensed-matter physicists who think consciousness talk is soft, about half philosophy grads who haven't solved a diffrential equation in years. The organizer wants the framing to actually do work: introduce the idea that if panpsychism is taken literally — not as woo, but as the claim that basal physical properties carry some proto-experiential quality — then suffering isn't just a property of brains but an aggregation problem across scales. That breaks our usual moral arithmetic, where harm is counted per subject. Use that tension to tell the room what kind of disagreement to expect in the sessions, not just mention it in passing. Don't pretend there's experimental proof either way, don't cite anyone, and don't use "qualia" or "Hamiltonian" as in-group passwords. Keep it around two minutes, and make sure it sounds like you're speaking to people who don't already agree with you. Oh and don't make it sound like rocks are literally sad — it's the aggregation of micro-level harm that's the issue, not cartoon sentience, and don't let the occurence of that framing turn into decoration.
