Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of cognition must satisfy if analogy-making is its core mechanism—not a peripheral heuristic but the primary engine of concept formation, reasoning, and cross-domain understanding. Specify the phenomena it must explain, including analogical success, analogical failure, conceptual blending, and the persistence of analogical residue (durable conceptual structures left behind when a scientific analogy, such as caloric fluid, is abandoned). State the distinctions it must preserve: between structural alignment and superficial resemblance, between generative analogies that create new categories and decorative ones that merely illustrate, and between intra-domain and ontologically distant cross-domain mappings. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or puzzle behind it. Identify the constraints it must respect: it must avoid circularity in explaining how similarity is recognized before the relevant categories exist; it must accommodate pre-linguistic analogical reasoning without reducing it to language-mediated comparison; and it must remain consistent with empirical findings on analogical development. Flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—especially any tension between accounting for radical conceptual innovation through analogy and maintaining that analogy requires pre-existing shared structure. Conclude with a brief, non-bibliographic assessment of how Hofstadter and Sander's analogy-centered framework performs against these criteria. Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal tensions rather than on the scorecard.
