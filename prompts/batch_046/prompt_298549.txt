I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I've been reading discussions about whether modern physics—particularly the relational view of spacetime in general relativity and information-theoretic accounts of quantum gravity—revives Isaac Newton's theological picture. In these threads, people repeatedly invoke a line summarized as "space is the sensorium of God" to argue that physical reality is not merely governed by God but is literally a mode of divine cognition or presence. They treat Newton's authority as settling that consciousness or mind is ontologically prior to matter.

Before I accept that move, I want to understand exactly what claim is being made. The phrase "space is God's sensorium" seems to hide several very different theses. Could you lay them out as a precise menu? For each reading, I want to know: whether it is a trivial metaphor, a substantive metaphysical claim, or something Newton himself would have rejected given his documented anti-Trinitarian (Arian) theology and his insistence that God is incorporeal; whether the reading requires Newton's own absolute-space container view or can survive in a relational or block-universe framework; and whether it actually supports the conclusion that modern physics makes divine idealism or panpsychism more plausible.

In particular, distinguish at least these possibilities:
1. A poetic analogy: space functions for God like a retina functions for a perceiver, with no commitment to divine embodiment.
2. An epistemological claim: the geometry of space reveals God's rational design, but space itself is inert and non-mental.
3. An ontological claim: space is literally a part or property of God's being—the "sensorium" as an actual organ of divine perception.
4. A causal or instrumental claim: God uses space as a distributed medium through which to perceive and act, analogous to how one might use a cloud-based sensor network—this is the reading I keep seeing in tech forums, but I'm unsure whether it implies God is spatially extended or merely that space is His instrument.
5. A metaphysical claim about omnipresence: God's presence is co-extensive with space, not because space is His body, but because absolute space is the necessary condition for being everywhere.

For each, say which are defensible in light of Newton's own writings, which are false or anachronistic (for example, attributing relational spacetime to Newton), and which one the people invoking the slogan in physics discussions actually need for their argument to work. Also address whether Newton's anti-Trinitarianism and his rejection of corporeal divinity make reading (3) implausible as his own view, even if it is the most radical interpretation available.

Please do not provide a bibliography or reading list; I'm asking for conceptual disambiguation, not scholarship. Write this as a list I can bring back to the discussion.
