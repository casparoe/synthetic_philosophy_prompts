Question: In Appearance and Reality Bradley argues that relations threaten the unity of the proposition. Explain the regress he identifies and why he concludes relations arent genuinely real.

Student answer:
Bradley starts with a proposition like "a is R to b". For this to be one unified fact, R has to actually connect a and b. But if R is internal—part of a and part of b—then R gets split up and loses its own unity. So R must be external, distinct from both terms. But then R is just sitting there unconnected, so we need another relation R1 to link R to a, and R2 for b, and this goes on forever. Because the chain never completes, Bradley says relations are contradictory and belong only to appearance. The real world is the Absolute, where all distinctions vanish into one experience. He also rejects Russell's view that relations can just be primitive and external, since that doesnt explain how a and b are actually unified into one proposition. So relations are unreal.
