Exam question:
Explain Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument and show how it poses a challenge to non-reductive physicalism. In your answer, clearly distinguish the principle of causal closure of the physical domain from Kim's exclusion principle, and identify one response that a non-reductive physicalist might genuinely offer.

Student answer:
Kim's causal exclusion argument targets non-reductive physicalism, the view that mental properties are real, distinct from physical properties, and depend on them through supervenience. The argument starts with causal closure: every physical event has a sufficient physical cause. From closure alone it follows that if a physical base B causes a physical effect P, then any mental property M that depends on B cannot also cause P, since the causal chain is already fixed. This is Kim's exclusion principle.

Consider mental property M causing physical behavior P. By supervenience, M is realized by B. By closure, B causes P, and therefore M is excluded. So mental properties are epiphenomenal. This challenges non-reductive physicalism because that view wants mental properties to do real causal work. In fact, Kim shows that supervenience entails identity—if mental properties depend entirely on physical ones and contribute nothing extra, they just are those physical properties—so the non-reductive position is incoherent. One response available to the non-reductive physicalist is to accept that mental properties are purely phenomenal and do not cause physical behavior, which avoids the conflict entirely.
