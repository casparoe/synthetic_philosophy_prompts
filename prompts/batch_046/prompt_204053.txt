Write a final-exam essay question for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in philosophy of science, together with a model answer outline and a detailed marking scheme. The course level is final-year BA; the examination conditions are 60 minutes and approximately 1,000 words; the question carries 50 marks.

The topic should be the demarcation problem applied specifically to pseudohistory. To prevent answers that merely recite standard summaries of well-known authors, embed within the question itself a novel, invented pseudohistorical claim—label it the “Lithic Resonance Thesis.” According to this thesis, megalithic monuments on four unconnected continents share an identical, precisely measured acoustic tuning ratio that statistical analysis shows could not have arisen by independent cultural evolution; proponents therefore infer a single pre-Holocene maritime civilisation with advanced acoustic engineering. The only “evidence” offered is post-hoc selection of monuments that fit the ratio, combined with an immunising auxiliary hypothesis that the civilisation possessed an “archival technology” which decayed without leaving any other material trace.

The exam question should ask students to evaluate whether the Lithic Resonance Thesis is scientific, pseudoscientific, or non-scientific, requiring them to apply at least two demarcation frameworks studied in the course (for example, Popper’s falsifiability criterion and Lakatos’s distinction between progressive and degenerating research programmes). The question must be designed so that a student who only memorises labels receives a low mark, whereas a student who identifies the specific methodological flaws—particularly the post-hoc measurement selection and the unfalsifiable auxiliary hypothesis—receives a high mark.

Provide:
1. The exam question itself, worded formally and suitable for a 60-minute, 1,000-word response.
2. A model answer outline that lists, in order, the specific intellectual moves a first-class answer must make: (a) accurate but concise exposition of the chosen demarcation criteria; (b) direct application to the Lithic Resonance Thesis; (c) explicit identification of the immunising strategy and the problem of post-hoc accommodation; (d) a comparative judgement that explains which framework yields what verdict and why; (e) a nuanced conclusion that avoids simple labelling.
3. A marking scheme that allocates the 50 marks precisely across these moves (suggested distribution: 10 marks for exposition of criteria; 15 marks for rigorous application; 10 marks for diagnosis of immunisation and post-hoc reasoning; 10 marks for comparative evaluation across frameworks; 5 marks for structure, coherence, and use of British English). For each mark band (first-class 70% and above, upper-second 60–69%, lower-second 50–59%, third 40–49%, fail below 40%), specify the characteristic errors that should reduce the score—for example, conflating falsifiability with verificationism, treating Lakatos’s “hard core” and Popper’s “basic statements” as equivalent, ignoring the role of the auxiliary hypothesis, or reaching a conclusion without comparing frameworks.

Please present the entire response in British English (e.g., “programme,” “behaviour,” “analyse”).
