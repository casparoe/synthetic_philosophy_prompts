I'm an undergraduate in a seminar on logical empiricism and value theory, and I'm leading a thirty-minute session on Schlick's positive account of the meaning of life—not the familiar reduction to play rather than purpose, but the more specific claim that meaning attaches to activities pursued as ends-in-themselves and that sustaining this requires a present-oriented, non-teleological attitude Schlick associates with youthfulness rather than with chronological age. The audience is philosophy majors familiar with basic ethics but not with Schlick's texts.

Plan the explanatory sequence: which conceptual distinction must be secured before the positive claim can land correctly—perhaps the separation between instrumental purpose and intrinsic fulfillment, or between goal-directed achievement and process-valued activity—and explain why reversing that order makes the view collapse into hedonism. Identify the single concrete scenario or activity that should carry the whole explanation, one that clearly admits both an instrumental framing and a Schlick-style framing, and specify exactly what feature it must possess and what feature it must lack so that the distinction becomes visible rather than verbal; avoid the tired hobby-versus-career comparison.

Then map the predictable misconceptions in the order they will arise: that Schlick is advocating simple pleasure-seeking; that he denies all value to long-term commitments or projects; that youthfulness means literal immaturity or recklessness; and possibly that his position is indistinguishable from existentialist self-creation. For each, indicate the precise point in the sequence where it should be anticipated and defused, not after it has already taken root.

Also propose one check-for-understanding question I can pose at the end that reveals whether my peers grasp the dependency between the rejection of teleology and the positive account of meaning—something that would expose a student who has only memorized the slogan.

Finally, state which aspect of Schlick's broader ethical naturalism should be explicitly bracketed as beyond the scope of this session, and why including it would derail the explanation rather than support it.
