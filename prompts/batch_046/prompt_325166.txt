For a feature I'm writing on engineered consciousness and ancient ethics, I'm examining whether a particular interpreter's Platonic framework can absorb modern cases without contradiction. I need you to work out the consistency problem with me.

Consider the four published rulings of philosopher Dr. Elara Voss on Plato's treatment of intoxication. She approves ritual wine-use for citizens past youth, as described in the Laws, when the practice is supervised and intended to test moral fortitude—so she permits structured, civic intoxication aimed at self-knowledge. She approves the kind of disclosure that occurs in Symposium, where Alcibiades' drunken speech strips away social pretense and reveals the soul's genuine attachment to beauty—so she permits intoxication that functions as an unmasking of hidden philosophical desire. She rejects the tyrant's debauchery portrayed in Republic IX, where drink serves only unmeasured pleasure and dissolves rational order—so she prohibits recreational excess lacking discipline or civic purpose. She rejects any drinking that predictably produces violence or public disorder, as banned in the Laws—so she prohibits intoxication that harms others or destabilizes communal life.

Now consider a new case not addressed in her essays: a clinical-philosophical protocol administers a precisely calibrated, non-cognitive-impairing compound that induces only emotional disinhibition, then places participants in a monitored virtual environment for structured ethical debate. The aim is neither pleasure nor violence, but a technological reproduction of Symposium-style revelation without the social context, physical risk, or ritual framework of actual wine-drinking.

If Dr. Voss wants to remain consistent with her four precedents, what should she decide about this protocol? Identify the closest analog among her earlier rulings, articulate the two competing principles that her record jointly supports—perhaps one anchored in the condition of the rational soul, another in the social function of disclosure—predict her verdict with reasoning, and state honestly whether her precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome. If they do, specify what principle she would be adopting with each choice. Finally, name the further case—say, the same compound administered in solitary study without dialogue or supervision—that would definitively settle which principle she actually holds.
