ok so my philosophy reading group wants each of us to write a profile on psychiatric classification so they can poke holes. here's mine, fresh off reading some Hacking after retiring from a biology lab.

i think psychiatric categories are interactive kinds — being classified as bipolar actually changes how people behave, not just how doctors see them. i also still think schizophrenia is close to a natural kind, with real genetic and neural mechanisms, like an element in chemistry. the looping effect is much weaker for schizophrenia than for personality disorders, since psychosis doesn't reshape itself around self-labeling. but i also think that being an interactive kind automatically rules out being a natural kind, because the feedback loop kills mind-independance.

clinically, i think predictive validity is the only criterion that matters — does the label tell us treatment response and prognosis? whether it carves nature at its joints is metaphysics, not medicine. and i think looping usually improves predictive validity over time, since people adapt to the diagnosis and the category gets more homogenous. except autism spectrum disorder is the clear counterexample: looping made it broader, fuzier, and less predictive, especially with self-diagnosis online.

go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in real tension, ordered by severity. for each one say how bad it is and what the cheapest revision would be. also tell me which single change fixes the most problems. and flag any pairs that only look tense but dissolve when you inspect them closer — i don't want false alarms. don't explain Hacking to me, just audit the set.
