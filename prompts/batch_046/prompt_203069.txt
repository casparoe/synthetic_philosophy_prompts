I serve as evaluation chair for a national climate-adaptation investment board. We review multi-decade infrastructure commitments—coastal defenses, agricultural transitions, grid redesigns—under conditions where standard probabilistic risk assessment fails because the underlying distributions are unknown, non-stationary, or structurally undefined. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in Keynes's account of probability and uncertainty—specifically his risk-versus-uncertainty distinction, his treatment of probability as a logical relation rather than necessarily a numerical frequency, his concept of the weight of an argument, and his view that long-run expectations depend on convention and animal spirits rather than on calculable forecasts—that my committee can execute in a single ninety-minute session. The procedure should be presented as an ordered protocol with explicit questions, stopping rules that return a definitive no-proceed or proceed-only-with-conditions, hand-off points where the framework requires a value judgment rather than an epistemic one, and criteria for what counts as an adequate answer at each stage, assuming members have policy expertise but no advanced statistical training.

Apply the procedure to three proposals: (1) reinforcing an existing levee system where historical flood-frequency records exist but climate non-stationarity undermines their reliability across the design lifetime; (2) funding a first-of-its-kind direct-air-capture installation with no operational track record and no defensible basis for assigning failure probabilities; and (3) relocating a low-income industrial district to higher ground, a high-social-cost proposal that the procedure—if applied consistently—will recommend advancing despite deep uncertainty about future storm patterns, a result several committee members will reject on equity grounds.

In your answer, flag precisely where Keynes's framework offers no algorithmic resolution—for example, in weighing present costs against benefits that accrue to future generations, or in selecting among competing conventional judgments about the future—and state explicitly what the procedure can and cannot settle. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citation references; the deliverable must be usable by someone who knows Keynes's ideas only through their practical translation here.
