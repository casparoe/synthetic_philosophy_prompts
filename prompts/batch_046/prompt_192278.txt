In rationalist-adjacent discussions of universal iconography and AI interface design, I keep encountering the slogan that with ISOTYPE Neurath showed that pictures speak a universal language—invoked to claim that visual symbols bypass linguistic ambiguity entirely. I am skeptical that this does any argumentative work without being precisified. Could you lay out the menu of precise theses that slogan might express? I want at least: (1) the weak empirical claim that well-designed pictograms reduce cross-linguistic misunderstanding; (2) the semantic claim that images possess fixed, context-independent meanings in a way sentences do not; (3) the epistemological claim that a fully pictorial system could serve as a protocol language for recording observations in empirical science; and (4) any intermediate positions between these. For each reading, indicate whether Neurath’s actual writings on picture education and physicalism support it, whether it is consistent with his rejection of private protocol sentences, and—crucially—which version a defender of icon-heavy AI interfaces actually needs for their argument to go through. Do not settle on one interpretation; map the landscape with verdicts on defensibility and fidelity to Neurath.
