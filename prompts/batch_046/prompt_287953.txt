Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains that alternate and finally appear together—on whether white lies are wrong, using Dutch directness and Japanese indirectness not as cultural ornament but as two rival accounts of what conversation is for. Assign the Dutch position the refrain “Speak the fact, and pay the cost in air,” and the Japanese position the refrain “Speak around it, and let the shape hold.” Between them, develop the niche conceptual claim that a white lie should be analyzed not as a false proposition about the world but as an asymmetrical “credence loan”: the speaker temporarily borrows the listener’s epistemic trust to preserve social harmony, but the debt accrues interest in reduced transparency, and the moral wrongness—if there is one—lies in the risk asymmetry of who must eventually settle the account. Let the poem’s repetitions enact the circularity of this debt: directness settling immediately at high social interest, indirectness deferring payment with compound ambiguity. In the final quatrain, bring both refrains together without synthesizing them, to expose the shared hidden assumption that conversation is primarily an information exchange rather than a negotiation of face. Keep the rhyme scheme tight and the language formal and polished, accurate enough that a reader familiar with cross-cultural pragmatics and epistemic utility theory recognizes the argument embedded in the form. Do not resolve the tension; end with the debt still outstanding, suitable for closing a seminar in which the discussion has genuinely stalled.
