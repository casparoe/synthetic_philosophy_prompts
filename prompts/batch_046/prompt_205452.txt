I keep seeing a pattern in arguments and art-making that feels related to costly signaling but doesn't quite fit. Here are two instances. First: a writer spends years on a manuscript using a method that actively makes the text harder to read — like composing entirely in palindromes — and when people say it's unreadable, the writer defends it by pointing to the enormous effort and the risk of total failure, as if the difficulty itself proves authenticty. Second: a community advocates for a reform that includes a deliberatly painful, neccessary step; they don't claim the step improves outcomes, only that enduring it shows real commitment. Becuase if you ask why the step is needed, they treat the question as evidence you lack dedication. In both cases there's a costly display, but the cost doesn't make the signal more honest about quality — it actually degrades the result — and yet people consume the degradation as the credential. I know costly signaling and the handicap principle, but those usually involve costs that honestly reveal fitness or quality. Here the waste seems detached, almost like the suffering is being displayed for its own consumption. Does this configuration have an established name? How exactly does it differ from ordinary costly signaling, sunk-cost reasoning, or martyrdom as rhetoric?
