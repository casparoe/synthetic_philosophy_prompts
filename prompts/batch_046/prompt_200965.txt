I'm a referee for this interdisciplinary journal that gets alot of submissions trying to mash quantum mechanics with decision theory, and lately half the papers invoke Everett or many-worlds and then just talk about 'the probability of this branch occurring' like its totally settled. I need a reusable checklist — something I can run in about 20 minutes while reviewing on my laptop — for spotting whether an author is implicitly smuggling in the Born rule, trying to derive it through symmetry or betting arguments, or just ignoring the weighting problem entirely. The procedure should ask concrete questions of the manuscript: do they distinguish deterministic branching from probabilistic weights? do they justify a specific measure over branches? is their decision-theoretic step circular? And it should flag which answers count as acceptable, which are hand-waving, and where I have to make a judgment call because philosophy hasn't settled it.

I want worked applications to three hypothetical papers: one that uses an equal-decoherence measure with unit weights per branch and then applies a frequency argument across copies; one that appeals to self-locating uncertainty but never fixes the reference class for 'I'; and one that technically passes every check but still feels hollow to me because the weights are just postulated. For each, show me exactly where the checklist lands, include the case where the answer bugs me, and be explicit about what the procedure cannot do. Also, I don't have database access when I'm reviewing on the train, so build this from first principles — no reading lists, no citations I need to chase down.
