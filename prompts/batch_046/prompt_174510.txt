My background is in software engineering, not philosophy, and I recently encountered a summary of dialetheism and paraconsistent logic in a popular article. The author characterises the view roughly as follows. Dialetheism is the metaphysical claim that some contradictions are literally true — not merely unsolved puzzles, but genuine cases in which both a statement and its negation hold. It was developed chiefly by Graham Priest and colleagues, largely in response to self-referential paradoxes such as the liar. According to the summary, dialetheists reject the law of non-contradiction outright, and they adopt a paraconsistent logic — specifically a system associated with Priest known as LP — in which the principle of explosion fails, so that from A and not-A one cannot derive an arbitrary conclusion B. The author then applies this to formal verification: if a system specification contained a direct contradiction — for instance, an invariant requiring that the system both crashes and never crashes — then on dialetheist logic this would not automatically render the specification useless, because the contradiction would not entail every possible failure mode. From this, the author concludes that dialetheism is essentially a logical curiosity with no practical bearing on engineering, since no working engineer treats an inconsistent specification as salvageable, and the need to abandon explosion reveals the view to be an artificial technical fix rather than a serious metaphysics.

I suspect this account mixes accurate exposition with subtle distortion, but I do not have the philosophical training to identify precisely where. Which parts would a careful defender of dialetheism accept? Where does the summary conflate dialetheism with other positions, overstate its commitments, or misapply it to formal methods? In particular, is the inference from "explosion fails in LP" to "an inconsistent engineering specification remains useful" valid, and does the conclusion that dialetheism is merely an academic trick follow from the corrected parts? I should welcome an audit that separates the faithful characterisation from the misrepresentations, and that clarifies whether the author's dismissal survives once those errors are repaired.
