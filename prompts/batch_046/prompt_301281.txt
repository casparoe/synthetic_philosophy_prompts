Since I retired from the lab (developmental biology, thirty-odd years) I've been dipping into moral philosophy, and I keep seeing this violinist analogy everywhere — you're kidnapped and hooked up to some musician for nine months so he doesn't die. People use it to argue that abortion is basically just unplugging yourself, so of course it's permitted. But coming from biology, the mapping feels shaky to me, and I want to know exactly where it cracks.

Can you give me the correspondance table first? What's the violinist, what's the dialysis machine, what's the nine-month period, what's the act of walking away? Then I want the disanalogies, especially the ones I think matter because of what I know about gestation. For one thing, the placenta isn't just plumbing — it's a semi-allograft with active imune suppression at the interface. There's no equivalent in the violinist case; you're not re-engineering your own imune system to keep him alive. For another, 'unplugging' in late pregnancy isn't detaching a tube, it's... well, you know what I mean. So which of these breaks are morally relevant, and which are just noise?

Then tell me whether the 'right to disconnect' conclusion survives once the biology is on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the whole argument. Is there a better analogy — maybe symbiosis, or parasitism — that captures the dependency without treating the mother like a passive container? That doesn't match her biological behaviour at all.

Also, people talk about the 'future like ours' argument: killing a fetus is wrong because it robs it of a future like ours, whilst the violinist framing treats the problem as one of dependency. How do these two sit together? If the violinist is young and brilliant, does his having a valuable future change whether you can still unplug? Or does the dependency argument win regardless?

Please don't give me a reading list — I can't chase down papers without a university login. Just work through the logic, and say if there's a principle that gets both the unplugging intuition and the future-like-ours worry without contradiction.
