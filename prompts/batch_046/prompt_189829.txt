I've come around to the view that Goethe's political quietism — his reluctance about the French Revolution and his preference for slow administrative reform in Weimar — wasn't just conservative temperment but came straight out of his morphological thinking. The idea is that he saw states as organisms with an internal form or Gestalt, and that violent restructuring kills that form just like dissecting a plant kills it's organizing principle. I'm convinced but want to stress-test it. What would actually count against this morphological interpretation of his politics? I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to awkward: biographical cases where he clearly backed rapid change, scientific writings where he treats social bodies mechanistically rather than organically, historical outcomes in Weimar that clash with the gradual-development narrative, and the worry that this view is unfalsifiable since any stance he takes can be read as protecting organic form. Which ones would a defender of the morphological reading concede as real problems, and which can they dismiss? Be honest about whether the view risks being insulated from evidence.
