I am drafting the normative framework chapter for my dissertation in institutional epistemology, and before I evaluate any particular defense of prediction-market governance I want the evaluation criteria fixed. Write the list of requirements that any adequate normative theory of futarchy—or of governance by prediction markets more generally—must satisfy to count as legitimate and conceptually coherent, not merely technically efficient. For each desideratum, state the concrete case or institutional scenario that motivates it, the conceptual distinction it forces the theory to respect, and whether it is non-negotiable or a negotiable preference of a particular camp (for example, libertarian, deliberative-democratic, or purely epistemic).

The list should include at least the following, with the reasoning behind each made explicit:

- How the theory aggregates dispersed knowledge without collapsing into a plutocracy of belief-capital, where wealth determines which outcomes are predicted and therefore which policies are adopted.
- Its handling of values that resist probabilistic resolution or monetization—the problem of axiological flattening—where the betting currency forces incommensurable goods into a single metric.
- Its robustness against meta-manipulation, specifically epistemic rent-seeking: actors who bet not to reveal private beliefs but to manufacture the resolution conditions or policy outcomes they prefer, and whether the theory can distinguish belief revelation from preference expression.
- Its account of democratic sovereignty when market-derived recommendations conflict with explicit popular mandates or constitutional constraints, and whether legitimacy derives solely from accuracy, solely from authorization, or from some hybrid that must be specified.
- Its treatment of open-ended normative commitments that lack well-defined empirical settlement conditions—the null-contract problem—since governance involves obligations that may never resolve into a clear true-or-false state suitable for a prediction contract.
- Its intergenerational consistency, given that prediction markets aggregate the beliefs and preferences of current participants, but policies affect the identity and welfare of future people who cannot place bets.

Then, indicate whether any subset of these desiderata—say, full democratic sovereignty together with full epistemic accuracy together with complete resistance to meta-manipulation—is jointly unsatisfiable given standard strategic reasoning about prediction markets, and if so, which requirement does the most work in excluding candidates. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on an exhaustive scorecard of authors. If you briefly indicate how an original futarchy-style mechanism, a deliberative-market hybrid, and a non-governing purely epistemic market model fare against the list, do so only after the criteria are established, and flag where each fails a non-negotiable condition versus a negotiable preference.
