I'm a novelist, so I notice architecture and public spaces the way other people notice weather, and I was walking through Berlin last winter thinking about those enormous stone installations for the Holocaust, which are of course necessary and right, but then I started wondering about that genocide in Namibia and how there's basically nothing comparable in the center of the capital. And its not just Germany. In Japan you have those beautiful peace parks in Hiroshima that draw millions, but the museums about the Nanjing massacre or the comfort women system are tucked away or fought over like embarrassing secrets. Turkey builds grand commemorative spaces for various tragedies but keeps the Armenian genocide locked out of official remembrance, and meanwhile the American South puts up these gorgeous civil rights museams that talk about segregation like its a finished story, while the economic machinery that came out of slavery just keeps running quietly in the background.

So here's the pattern I'm seeing, and I don't know if there's already a precise word for it. A society takes the moral energy that should go toward facing its own specific crime, and redirects it into a very public, very expensive remembrance of either a different crime it also committed but frames differently, or a suffering it experienced itself, or even someone else's suffering that it can safely mourn from a distance. The result is that outsiders look at all that marble and glass and say, look how seriously they take history, and the locals feel they've done the hard work of remembrance without ever having to look at the particular thing that still shapes their present. Its not exactly denial, because the monuments are real and the grief is real. Its not exactly whataboutism either, because nobody is saying you do it too — they're just doing the remembering in the wrong direction, like shining a flashlight away from the body. I keep wanting to call it lateral mourning or displaced commemoration, but I have no idea if historians or philosophers of memory, especially people who study Vergangenheitsbewältigung in comparison with Japan or Turkey or the American South, have already carved this out with exact edges. Does this configuration have an established name, and if not, could you help me define where its boundaries lie? I want to know exactly how it differs from ordinary hypocrisy, from strategic forgetting, and from cases where a country genuinely doesn't know its own past yet. Please write back in one long continuous letter, no bullet points or bold headings or tables, just straight prose like you'd send a friend, and don't worry about giving me a bibliography — I'm just after the concept itself.
