I work in ML infrastructure at a firm that recently installed a hidden safeguard within our deployment pipeline: should an engineer attempt to release a model that breaches our fairness thresholds, an automated layer silently rolls back the change, yet the engineer is never informed that the safeguard is armed. Last week I delayed a rollout because I was uneasy about bias present in the training data; only afterwards did I learn that the safeguard had been active, and that it would have blocked me had I tried to proceed. A colleague maintains that this is irrelevant, since I acted from my own judgement. Nevertheless, I feel unsettled: if the safe outcome was assured regardless of my will, was my caution genuinely free, or merely the only path left open? How ought I to assess whether I can properly claim credit for having made the right call?
