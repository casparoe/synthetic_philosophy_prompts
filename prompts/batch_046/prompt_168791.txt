I'm taking a course on value theory and we got into this weird debate about whether all good feelings are really the same kind of thing. It made me think about my sister's company—she works in HR and they use this wellness dashboard that asks employees to rate daily "happiness" from 1-10, but also tracks sleep and "social connection" and then mashes it all into one "Wellbeing Index" that decides who gets access to the mental-health stipend. The thing is, they treat a good night's sleep, a fun conversation with a coworker, and feeling "fulfilled" by a project as literally interchangable units—like you can trade three points of sleep for one point of social connection and the total is the same.

What theory of value or of pleasure does that practice presuppose? Does it assume there's some single common currency—maybe just "positive affect"—that all these states share, or is it compatible with the view that pleasures are totally heterogeneous and can't be summed? And if pleasures really don't have anything deep in common, does the dashboard's aggregation become not just inaccurate but conceptually confused? I'm also wondering whether the practice quietly commits to hedonism specifically, or whether a non-hedonist could run the same dashboard if they just relabeled the inputs. Can you reconstruct the implicit philosophy here, say which parts are actually required and which are just convenient design choices, and tell me what a wellness policy built on the heterogeneity view would have to look like—would it have to drop the single score entirely?
