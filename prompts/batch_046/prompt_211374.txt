Please explain the conceptual architecture of standing to blame as it interacts with hypocrisy, focusing on a distinction that is often collapsed in casual discussion but that determines the logic of the objection. Distinguish carefully between victim-standing—the authority to blame that derives specifically from having been wronged by the target—and general-standing—the entitlement to blame that derives from a broader moral position not tied to personal injury. Then explain how hypocrisy, understood as the blamer's own commission of the very fault they condemn, operates differently upon each form. Does hypocrisy conceptually annul general-standing, or does it primarily weaken or nullify victim-standing? Does any annulment depend on the hypocrisy being known to the blamee, or does it hold with equal force when the blamer's fault remains hidden? Further, separate the conceptual question—whether the hypocrite genuinely lacks the normative position to blame—from the practical question of whether expressing the blame is merely pragmatically inappropriate, self-undermining, or rhetorically ineffective. Conclude by identifying which dimension of blame—its expressive, communicative, or normative-regulatory function—is actually threatened when standing is lost, and whether a residual analog of standing can survive in cases of private, unrecognized hypocrisy.
