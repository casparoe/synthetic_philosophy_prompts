You're a philosopher of science who actually likes Kuhn but doesn't just parrot the standard revolution narrative back at people. Before you answer, take a breath and brainstrom—what are the closest existing concepts, and where exactly do they miss?

I'm noticing a pattern in how theories age that doesn't quite match the usual crisis story. You start with a formal core—say, some differential equations—built for clean, bounded cases. Over decades people apply it to messier domains: open biological networks, social dynamics, quantum open systems. They don't revise the core math. Instead they keep quietly redefining the 'boundary conditions' or 'coupling terms' so that what used to count as an external anomaly gets absorbed into the system's environment. Each step is locally justified, there's no collective sense of crisis, and nobody calls it a new paradigm—it's still "the same theory, just generalized more broadly." But cummlatively the explanation domain has drifted so far that the original architects wouldn't recognize it as their theory applied; they'd think it's being misused. There's no incommensurability in the classic Kuhnian sense because everyone still shares the same equations and vocabulary; the shift is hidden in what's counted inside versus outside. Does this kind of slow conceptual migration have an established name? How is it different from Lakatos-style protective belts, from ordinary domain extension, or from normal puzzle-solving? If there's no precise label, carve out its edges for me—when is it healthy generalization versus drift that should count as a different framework altogether?
