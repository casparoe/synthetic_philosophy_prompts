Compare Searle's Chinese Room argument with the causal-historical account of mental content—the view that the semantic properties of internal representations depend essentially on their causal origins and environmental anchoring rather than on intrinsic syntax alone. Specifically, consider a version of the room in which every symbol token is causally designed and historically anchored by programmers who possess original intentionality, and the room itself is embedded in a larger system that includes these programmers and the external world. Does this external anchoring provide the room with derived semantic content that undermines Searle's claim that syntax is never sufficient for semantics? Or does Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality block this move by relocating, rather than dissolving, the problem of genuine understanding?
