Both camps seem to accept that subjective fulfillment alone is insufficient (the experience-machine intuition is shared) and that purely objective achievement without subjective uptake is also deficient. Yet nobody shifts. I want a structural diagnosis rather than another taxonomy: is this a substantive disagreement masked by ambiguous language, a hidden empirical dispute about narrative identity, or a clash of bedrock intuitions about whether cosmic indifference undermines the normative force of local value?

I have a working hypothesis that the fracture tracks a hidden temporal ontology—absurdists treating meaning as diachronically narrative-dependent, where coherence must survive the awareness of death and the absence of cosmic purpose, while objectivists treat it as synchronically state-dependent, where value-realization is complete in the present moment regardless of narrative closure. But I also wonder whether there's a deeper divergence about recursive self-modeling versus terminal valuation—whether meaning requires an agent to represent her own life as meaningful in a loop that survives reflection, or whether it can be assigned externally like a readerly interpretation of a text. Given your diagnosis, is there any conceptual distinction—perhaps between authorial and readerly meaning, or between diachronic coherence and synchronic realization—that could actually resolve or dissolve the impasse? Or is this a permanent structural divergence in our evaluative frameworks, and if so, what, if anything, should count as progress?
