I've been thinking on my own about panpsychism and the combination problem—how micro-level experiences might add up to the single stream of consciousness I have now. Here are my judgments across several cases, and I can't see the principle that holds them together. In a normal human brain, I think the micro-subjects do combine into one unified macro-experience; that feels like the clear success case. In a rock or a wooden table, I think the same kind of micro-subjects are present at the base level, yet nothing combines into a rock-mind or table-mind; the result is just dead matter with no inner life. If the corpus callosum is cut and the hemispheres are separated, I think one consciousness genuinely splits into two, which makes me think continuous physical integration is necessary for combination. But if surgeons linked two strangers' brains with the exact same density and pattern of white-matter connections, I still think they would remain two separate people rather than merging into one subject—so physical integration alone can't be sufficient. I also think that if every neuron in my brain were replaced by an artificial chip that perfectly copied its causal and functional role but had no intrinsic experience of its own, my consciousness would gradually fade rather than stay intact; this suggests that combination requires actual micro-experiential parts, not just functional duplicates. Finally, I think that if two people achieved perfect emotional and cognitive synchronization through technology—shared perception, shared memory access, instantaneous mutual response—but without any direct neural wiring, they would still be two subjects; synchronization without structural binding doesn't seem to combine them. Is there one coherent account of combination that delivers all six verdicts? If not, which verdicts should I drop, what principle best captures the rest, and what does it predict for a case I haven't mentioned—say, a large hive of insects with tightly coupled collective behavior but no centralized nervous system? Please state the principle precisely, map each clause back to the case that motivates it, and be explicit about which of my judgments it cannot accommodate.
