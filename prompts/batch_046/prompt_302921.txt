Question: The situationist critique says personality traits dont predict behavior well across different situations. Virtue ethicists reply that virtues are skills that need the right conditions to show up, like a musician needs an instrument. Is this reply successful? In your answer, explain what the situationist actually measures, and address whether virtue ethics needs traits to be "global" (showing up everywhere) to work.

Student answer:
Yes, the reply mostly works. The situationist experiments, like the ones where people help less when they're in a hurry, show that behavior changes with context. But that doesn't mean people don't have courage; it just means courage needs the right setup, just like a musician needs their violin. So virtue ethics is saved because virtues can be local skills rather than global personality traits, what some call the instrument-condition model. Aristotle actually said virtues are exactly these kinds of local skills that depend on context, so the critique misses his point. The mistake defenders make is thinking virtue ethics needs people to be heroes in every situation, but Aristotle never required that. Therefore, the situationist data doesn't hurt virtue ethics.
