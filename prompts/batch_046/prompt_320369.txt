I have been working on the philosophical foundations of low-impact AI, and I keep returning to a stalemate that seems resistant to technical progress: can "avoid side effects" be defined without a full specification of value? The formalist position holds that one can delineate a task-irrelevant region of state space—penalizing deviations in the statistical distribution of protected variables—without invoking any normative preferences about what ought to be preserved. The value-dependence critics reply that selecting which variables belong in that protected region, and which baseline distribution counts as unchanged, already embeds a complete ordering over possible worlds, so the formalism merely hides its normative commitments behind mathematical notation. The exchange has repeated for years: new metrics on one side, new deconstructions of hidden value judgments on the other, with neither camp conceding ground.

My working hypothesis is that part of the deadlock is conceptual rather than mathematical. The formalists appear to treat side effect extensionally—as any deviation from a baseline trajectory within a pre-specified invariant set—while the critics treat it intensionally, as interference with the counterfactual course that a rational agent with correct values would have permitted. I also suspect there may be a hidden structural question about whether preference spaces have enough topology to allow a non-arbitrary partition of protected and unprotected dimensions without full axiomatization.

Can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is? Are the sides engaged in a single substantive debate about state-space partitioning, or are they running two different projects—one algorithmic, one normative—under a shared label? Would a distinction between environmental inertia (preserving the distribution of non-task variables) and structural neutrality (preserving the causal dependencies among them) clarify the ambiguity, or is the disagreement bedrock, rooted in incompatible intuitions about what it means to leave the world alone? And if the diagnosis points to a genuine formal question in disguise, what result—perhaps an impossibility theorem linking invariant impact metrics to utility representations—would actually count as progress? Conversely, if the question is ill-posed, what reframing should replace it?
