My roommate Maya and I got into it over coffee about Nietzshe's idea of eternal recurrance—she treats it like an ethical stress-test, asking whether you'd actually want your exact life on loop forever, not whether the cosmos physically repeats. She's given verdicts on four hypotheticals and I can't tell if she's running on amor fati, on some integrity condition, or on whether you've creatively overcome suffering. Her yes cases: a musician in poverty who turns starvation into symphonies and says she wouldn't change a hungry day; and a former fraudster who betrayed a friend, then spent years integrating the guilt into helping others, claiming even the betrayal belongs in her loop. Her no cases: a rich hedonist living in comfort but secretly envying suffering artists and producing nothing; and a trauma nurse who endures brutal shifts, suppresses her bitterness, and just soldiers on without ever affirming the repetition.

The new case is a tech founder living in a carbon-offset compound, fully aware her supply chain uses forced labor, but she's convinced her charitable donations cancel it out—she feels total peace, has never faced real personal suffering, and genuinely wants this loop forever. She hasn't hidden the knowledge from herself; she's rationalized it away. What should Maya say if she's being consistent? Which of the earlier cases is the closest analog, and if her track record genuinely underdetermines this one, what principle would she be adopting with each choice? Also, name one further hypothetical that would actually tell her rules apart.
