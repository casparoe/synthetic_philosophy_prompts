I run a two-hour workshop for about eight adults with no formal philosophy background. We've been arguing on our own about whether being a good person is mainly a stable inner character—being genuinely honest or brave across the board—or mainly a reaction to whatever situation you happen to be in. I want an activity, not a lecture, that makes us feel why the second view might be right. Design a structured game or role-play with hidden rules or paired scenarios: we should first build firm judgments about strangers' character from brief descriptions, then discover that the descriptions are actually the same individuals in subtly different contexts, and notice how our explanations flip. The rules should make us construct the illusion ourselves before anyone names it. Include a debrief that maps our specific mistakes onto the debate between virtue ethics and the situationist critique, predicts what will go wrong when people defend their initial judgments, and is frank about which part of real moral development—say, the slow formation of habit—this activity necessarily cannot reproduce. No bibliography or citation list is needed.
