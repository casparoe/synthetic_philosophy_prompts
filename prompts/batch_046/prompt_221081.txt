In debates about fiscal policy I often encounter the slogan “taxation is theft” used to dismiss any defense of progressive income taxation without further argument. I want to understand what precise claims could be hiding inside that sentence. Please lay out the distinct theses it might express: for example, whether it asserts that compulsory taxation is morally identical to burglary; whether it claims that democratic authorization cannot legitimate coercive extraction; whether it applies only to redistributive taxes or to all state revenue; whether it depends on a pre-political conception of property rights that treats the tax-funded legal system itself as illegitimate; and whether it requires that the taxed party neither consents to nor benefits from the extraction. For each reading, indicate whether it is a trivial restatement, a substantive and defensible position, or false, and specify which version is actually doing the work when someone invokes the line to reject progressive taxation. Also address whether the slogan survives if property rights are partly constituted by the enforcement institutions that taxes fund. Please write in American English.
