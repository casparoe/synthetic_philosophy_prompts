I have been putting together my position for a graduate seminar on ontological monism and found myself defending what I take to be a rather precise package of commitments. My commitments are these. (1) I accept the singleton hypothesis in the form I call saturated monism: there is exactly one concrete particular, and all apparent multiplicity arises from perspectival partitioning of its internal structure, not from numerically distinct objects. (2) I take the best argument for this to be the saturation-index argument: once the universal fusion reaches saturation point S, any putative additional object is numerically identical to that fusion, since there is no mereological outside left to host it. (3) I reject modal realism; modality is not accounted for by other concrete worlds, but by the singleton’s own modal profile—its capacity to instantiate different internal partitions. (4) I hold that evolutionary debunking undermines our folk-pluralist intuitions about objecthood and distinctness, yet I do not think it touches formal mereological reasoning about saturation and universal fusion. (5) Ontological parsimony should be read as minimising fundamental entities, not as minimising internal complexity or relational richness within the fundamental entity; on this reading the singleton is maximally parsimonious despite its elaborate internal architecture. (6) Where metaphysical theories are empirically equivalent, we ought to prefer the one that avoids positing brute necessary connections between numerically distinct existents—precisely the situation the singleton avoids. The package feels coherent, but I worry that (2) and (3) pull in opposite directions: the saturation-index argument seems to treat parts as genuinely summed within the fusion, yet if modality is only internal partitioning, then the parts appealed to in other possible partitions lack actuality, which makes the uniqueness claim in (2) look circular rather than substantive. At the same time, (4) looks suspiciously selective—why should debunking spare mereological intuition?—though I think the distinction between adaptive folk-psychology and structural reasoning dissolves that worry. Meanwhile (5) and (6) seem aligned, yet if the singleton’s internal richness requires unexplained necessary connections among its own partitions, I may be smuggling back in the very explanatory burden I sought to eliminate.
