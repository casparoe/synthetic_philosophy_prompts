As a retired physicist recently turned to philosophy, I have followed the long deadlock over Galen Strawson's Basic Argument with growing puzzlement. Strawson holds that genuine moral responsibility requires being the ultimate originative cause of one's own mental nature—causa sui—which generates either a vicious infinite regress or circularity, so true responsibility is impossible. The standard compatibilist response is that this demands an incoherent or irrelevant standard: responsibility needs only appropriate causal integration with one's reasons-responsive mechanisms and character, not metaphysical self-origination. For decades the exchange repeats—Strawson charges that compatibilists have redefined the subject, while compatibilists charge that he mistakes a practical, normative concept for a metaphysical one—without either side conceding ground.

My working hypothesis, drawn from watching scientific disputes stall when underlying measurement frameworks diverge, is that part of the impasse is verbal: the two sides may treat moral responsibility differently, as either a binary, essential kind requiring full originative authorship or a scalar, functional kind admitting of degrees within normal human agency. But I also suspect they may be answering distinct questions—one about the metaphysical conditions for ultimate authorship, the other about the functional conditions for justified praise and blame—while a hidden disagreement about whether personal-level causation is causally closed or open to external determination does unnoticed work.

Please diagnose this stall concretely, engaging with what Strawson actually says about originative causation and the regress, and with what compatibilists actually say about reasons-responsiveness and psychological integration. Is the disagreement verbal, substantive, or a misalignment of questions? Are there incompatible starting intuitions that neither side can argue for, or is an empirical or formal issue concealed beneath the conceptual surface? And given your diagnosis, what would genuinely count as progress here—a precise distinction, a conceptual experiment, a reframed question, or an argument that no further progress should be expected?
