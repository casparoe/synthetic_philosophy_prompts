1. karma isn't cosmic scorekeeping; it's a structural claim that wrong actions generate moral debts with a kind of causal inertia — they have to be resolved by the world eventually, maybe across lives.

2. i'm a compatabilist about free will, so i don't think desert needs libertarian choice. formed character and effort within deterministic constraints can still genuinely merit outcomes.

3. for the books to actually balance, i think there has to be rebirth or transmigration, since one lifespan is usually too short.

4. but i reject strong psychological continuity across lives. personal identity is more like a causal-narative thread than a soul carrying memories.

5. moral desert is fully objective and mind-independant, like a law of nature.

6. the practical takeaway should be redistributive justice now, not waiting around for cosmic payback — but i still think karma handles the long-run settlement.

i feel like 2 and 5 might be in tension with the usual line that compatabilism kills objective desert, but maybe that's just a popular confusion i'm overestimating. the thing that actually worries me is 3 and 4 together: if there's no robust "me" that carries over, who exactly receives the desert in the next round? and does 1's "causal inertia" idea even need a persisting debtor, or is it just free-floating?
