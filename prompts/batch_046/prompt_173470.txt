I cover philosophy of mind and the cognitive sciences for a general-interest magazine. I am developing an interactive feature on intuition pumps—thought experiments that persuade not through explicit argument but by embedding hidden framing assumptions, emotional anchors, and modal slides. For the participatory component, I need a self-contained, ninety-minute seminar exercise for twelve graduate students in philosophy, with no required pre-reading, that lets them experience the mechanism from the inside rather than hearing it named in advance.

The exercise should center on a constructed case I am calling the Reflexive Anchoring scenario: a hypothetical neural-symbolic system updates its representations not via gradient descent but through a feedback loop in which prior outputs become hard constraints on subsequent inputs—its designers term this reflexive anchoring. The system produces fluent, context-sensitive responses to ethical dilemmas. The intuition pump asks observers whether they should attribute genuine moral understanding to it, then quietly shifts the framing from an analysis of causal processes to an appeal about what one would feel upon discovering the absence of an inner life—thereby smuggling an experiential criterion into the judgment without argument.

Design a runnable activity—structured deliberation, role-play, or game with explicit rules and small stakes—plus a debrief. Divide the twelve participants into roles such as Evaluators, Architects, and a Panel. They should construct evaluation criteria for the system, apply them to a brief transcript of its responses that you outline in your design, and reach a collective verdict. Crucially, arrange the rules or payoffs so the framing shift happens structurally—perhaps through a hidden scoring rubric, a time-pressure mechanism, or a role constraint that rewards experiential language and penalizes process descriptions—without any facilitator announcing the bias. The participants should arrive at a confident, apparently reasoned conclusion before anyone names the pump.

In the debrief, map each phase onto parts of the intuition-pump structure: the hidden premise, the emotional anchor, and the modal slide. Predict exactly where resistance or confusion will arise and how to manage it without breaking the immersive experience. Be frank about which feature of real-world scientific or philosophical intuition pumps—such as the irreversibility of framing once embedded in published discourse—this classroom simulation cannot reproduce and that the facilitator must supply directly.

Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Keep the design fully self-contained, relying only on concepts and the invented scenario described above.
