I'm the lead on a small municipal heritage team and we keep getting pressure to tear out old pedestrian railings, one-way chicanes, and these weird concrete bollards downtown. Nobody remembers why half of them are there -- records are patchy, some were put up after incidents that never made it into official minutes. I need a practical decision procedue my team can actually run before we ok any removal, grounded in Chesterton's fence and Burkean conservative reasoning about accumulated institutional knowledge. Make it step-by-step: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each, where we stop with a hard no, and where we have to pass it to a person because its a values call not facts. Handle the case where the reason is genuinely lost versus hidden in unofficial archives, and flag where the procedure will misfire or just dress up judgement. Then run it on three proposals: tearing out a rusty river railing, removing chicanes that block emergency access, and demolishing bollards locals say went up after an unreported truck near-miss in the 70s. One of those should come out as keep-it even though the team wants it gone. Also say clearly what this can't do.
