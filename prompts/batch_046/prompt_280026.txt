My gut verdicts on whether a pattern is "real" in Dennett's three stances, and I want to know if there's one principle behind them or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Physical stance: Predicting Neptune from Uranus perturbations — totally real. The luminiferous aether made predictions too, but I say it wasn't real, since it eventually got replaced by a leaner physical story. Semiconductor "holes" behave exactly like positively charged particles in every equation; I call that real, even though it's technically just an absence of an electron.

Design stance: The heart's four-chamber layout for pumping blood — real pattern. An ant gene "for" altruism that correlates with behavior but misfires in hybrid colonies — still real, I think. But my laptop fan that spins up exactly when the CPU hits 80°C: if I say "it wants to keep the chip cool," that predicts behavior well, yet I'm unsure it's more than a metaphor. Meanwhile saying a chess engine "wants" to control the center feels clearly fake, even though it also predicts moves.

Intensional stance: Attributing beliefs and desires to my dog — real pattern, I predict her well. Attributing "belief that users are satisfied" to a chatbot feels fake, even when it helps guess outputs. But a thermostat that "believes" it's cold and "wants" to turn on — I'm genuinely torn, the predictions are so clean. Human cases like "he voted for X because he believed it lowers taxes" are obviously real.

So I split artificial systems three ways (thermostat maybe yes, fan unsure, chess and chatbot no), reject aether despite predictive success, and accept holes and gene patterns despite being "just" absences or correlations. Is there a coherent account — maybe counterfactual robustness plus ontological parsimony? — that delivers all this? State it precisely, say which clause motivates which case, predict whether describing a virus as "wanting to spread" is a real design-pattern, and be honest: which of my verdicts does your best principle have to throw out?
