hey can you build a decision-tree taxanomy for a seminar on moral worth? not the encyclopedia version — i want something that sorts people by what they actually believe about "close but wrong" reasons, not by what label they wear.

frame it from logical space first. axis one: does moral worth require that the agent's reason match the reason that makes the act right (a de re match), or is any good-enough motivation okay? axis two: if there's a bad sub-motive mixed in — say you save a drowning kid mainly to impress bystandars but also genuinely see they matter — does it contaminate the act totally, partly, or not at all?

make each branch a concrete case-node that someone can answer without knowing the jargon. the tree should have about five leaves: (1) strict kantian (only pure duty counts), (2) pure consequentialist (only outcomes matter), (3) aristotelean virtue (stable good character is sufficient), (4) smith-style right-reasons view (must act for the right reason, whatever it is), and (5) a "motivational echo" position where the original moral reason is gone but left a habit that still tracks rightness — include this even though its probably unoccupied or incoherent, and briefly diagnose why.

also include one genuinely empty combination (maybe "pure duty is required but contamination never matters") and say what's wrong with it. flag any pair of leaves your tree cant actually separate — i suspect virtue and echo might blur. keep it to 5-6 questions max, audience is undergrads whove read some kant and maybe smith. dont ask for citations or a bibliography, and dont rely on niche terms in the nodes themselves.
