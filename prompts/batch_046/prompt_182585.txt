I am advising a three-year interdisciplinary programme — philosophers of science, synthetic biologists, and legal scholars — that must allocate limited resources among unresolved conceptual problems in the philosophy of biology. The work must directly inform two practical domains: regulatory criteria for synthetic minimal organisms, and detection protocols for ambiguous biosignatures in astrobiology. We need a defended agenda, not a survey.

The programme is divided over four clusters of open questions:

A. Whether organisational closure and metabolic exchange are necessary conditions for life, or whether information-theoretic replication — observed in some viral entities and in self-replicating computational architectures lacking biochemical substrate — can be sufficient;

B. How to classify borderline cases (prions, viroids, synthetic minimal cells, and substrate-independent replicators) without begging the question in favour of either functionalist or essentialist accounts;

C. Whether 'life' names a natural kind with determinate boundaries, a family-resemblance concept, or a pragmatic kind whose extension shifts with disciplinary purpose (chemistry, clinical medicine, space law);

D. Which moral and legal implications actually follow from extending or restricting the category — in particular, whether synthetic organisms with minimal genomes acquire welfare interests, and whether ambiguous astrobiological entities would trigger protective obligations.

Please rank clusters A–D in order of priority for this specific programme (three years, interdisciplinary, policy-facing). Defend the ordering with explicit criteria: upstream/downstream dependencies, tractability for philosophers working alongside scientists rather than in isolation, and weight relative to the two practical contexts. For each cluster, specify:
- which other clusters it enables or blocks;
- its tractability within the programme's constraints;
- what near-term empirical or technical development would cause you to revise its rank.

Present the ranking in a table with these columns: Cluster | Rank | Priority Justification | Dependency Relations (enables/blocks) | Tractability Note | Revision Trigger.

Then provide a brief synthesis in JSON format with exactly these fields: overall_crux (string describing the single conceptual hinge on which the others depend), top_priority_cluster (string, e.g. "A"), key_trade_off (string describing the main tension in the ordering), practical_contexts_insufficient (boolean indicating whether regulation and astrobiology alone can settle the crux).

The synthesis should not exceed 250 words. It should identify the one crux on which the other three clusters most depend, explain why neither regulatory science nor astrobiology can resolve that crux without prior philosophical work, and justify why the ranking best serves a three-year, mixed-discipline team with limited funding.
