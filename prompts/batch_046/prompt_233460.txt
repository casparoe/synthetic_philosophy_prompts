hey — i'm a phd student working on buddhist ethics and consequentialism, and i'm trying to formalize my own intuitions about when suffering-reduction should override traditional precept-based constraints. i keep running into a pattern that feels consistent to me but i can't quite state the principle, and there's at least one tension that makes me think i might have to drop a verdict.

here are four cases with my gut reactions:

1. a bodhisattva knows that telling a single lie will stop a tyrant from killing a hundred people. i think she should lie. the suffering averted is enormous and the false speech is isolated and protective.

2. a monk can secretly redirect a wealthy donor's gift from building an ornate monastery (merit but no direct lives saved) to a malaria bed-net program that saves roughly five hundred lives. the donor will never know, though if he found out he might lose faith entirely. i still think the monk should redirect it — the scale of suffering reduction just dominates.

3. there's a politically viable path to end factory farming globally within fifteen years, but only through a campaign that deliberately uses fabricated images and false statistics to manipulate public emotion. it would save billions of animals, but it would also generate widespread cynicism, anger, and distrust — basically cultivating the root defilments at scale. i think this is wrong, even though the suffering reduction is orders of magnitude larger than in case 2.

4. a meditation teacher has a technique that dramatically reduces suffering for about ninety percent of students, but causes severe psychological destabilization — sometimes psychosis-level — in about ten percent. if she teaches it blindly with no screening, i think that's wrong. but if she uses careful screening and support for the vulnerable minority, i think she should teach it widely.

so my pattern seems like: direct deception or risk is okay when it's localized and doesn't systematically cultivate greed, hatred, or ignorance in others, but it's not okay when the mechanism itself is the mass production of mental poison, even for huge gains. but then case 2 involves deception that could destroy faith (a kind of mental poison) if discovered, yet i approve, while case 3 involves deception that definitely creates poison and i reject. is the difference just proximity? or am i smuggling in a distinction between physical and mental harm?

i'd like a precise principle — or maybe two competing ones — that delivers verdicts on cases 1-4, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it. also please say which of my four verdicts each principle has to overturn, if any. and predict what the principle says about a case i haven't listed: a government uses non-deceptive nudges (default menu changes, framing) to cut meat consumption by thirty percent, reducing autonomy slightly but generating no felt deception or anger. does my pattern approve?

please be honest about whether some of my intuitions are just inconsistent — i don't want a principle that saves every verdict at the price of being baroque.
