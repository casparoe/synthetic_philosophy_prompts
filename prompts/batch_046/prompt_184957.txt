The Meno problem—why knowledge is better than true belief if both guide action correctly—has produced decades of proposed answers, from stability accounts to achievement-theoretic fixes, yet the debate remains stalled without convergence. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not about knowledge itself, but about two incompatible notions of value being projected onto the same question: one side asks for an instrumentally durable feature (the belief that resists loss or perturbation), the other asks for a constitutively valuable feature (something that makes knowing good regardless of practical payoff). I want a diagnosis rather than another solution: is this stall a verbal dispute about "better," a substantive split over whether epistemic value is pragmatic or architectural, two distinct questions collapsed under one heading, or an ill-posed problem whose persistence signals that no single feature answers it?
