Mayr's BSC defines a species as populations that can interbreed and are reproductively isolated from others. Its main flaw is asexual organisms—bacteria, some plants—since they dont reproduce sexually, so BSC cant apply. Van Valen's ESC fixes this by saying a species is a lineage in its own adaptive zone, so interbreeding is irrelevant.
