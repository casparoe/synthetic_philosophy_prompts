i've been writing about Malthus for a feature and keep noticing this pattern i cant find a clean label for. a theory predicts some long-run squeeze — resources, population, whatever — with no exact clock attached. for decades the data looks fine, critics say it's dead. then eventually the pressure does appear, just in a different form or much later, and defenders say "he was right, just early." but what's interesting is the middle part: the theory wasn't merely delayed, it was actively contradicted by the intermediate years, yet that contradiction gets rewritten as a timing mistake rather than a real falsification. is there an established name for that specific move — treating short-run disconfirmation of a long-horizon mechanism as just being "too soon" — and how exactly is it different from a normal auxiliary fix or from the usual ways theories protect themselves from refutation? i dont want a bibliography, just whether the carving exists and where its edges are.
