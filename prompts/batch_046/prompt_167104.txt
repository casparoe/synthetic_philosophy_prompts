I am preparing materials for a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of consciousness, and in discussions with colleagues I have become convinced that debates surrounding integrated information theory too often dissolve into verbal disagreements rather than substantive conflicts about mechanism. To address this, I want you to draft the joint statement that would result from a rigorous adversarial collaboration between two camps that share more formal machinery than is typically acknowledged.

The first party—unitary exclusionists—holds that within any physical substrate consciousness corresponds exclusively to the single complex that globally maximizes integrated information (phi), and that all subsidiary local maxima are necessarily unconscious sub-mechanisms serving the global complex. The second party—plural integrationists—accepts the mathematical calculus of phi, the postulates of existence and information, and the claim that integration is necessary for consciousness, but rejects the exclusion postulate; they maintain that multiple non-overlapping or nested complexes within one substrate can each realize non-derivative phenomenal experience, provided each achieves a local phi-maximum that is not informationally reducible to the global maximum.

Please structure the document as follows: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can sign, covering the formal properties of phi, the explanatory targets (the unity of the subject, the binding problem, the structure of experience), and the precise scope of existing no-go results regarding classical computation; (2) a disagreements table with columns for the issue, the exclusionist formulation in wording that camp accepts, the pluralist formulation in wording they accept, the underlying crux, and the agreed concrete case or argument that bears on it. For the shared test case, employ the following hypothetical construct: a synthetic cortical lattice of modular columns in a grid, where each column sustains an intra-column phi-max through local connectivity, but the global lattice achieves a higher global phi-max only via a selective, disruptable phase-locked coupling rhythm across columns. Record each side's prediction about this lattice—specifically, whether decoupling the global rhythm eliminates all consciousness in the substrate, or eliminates only a global conscious field while leaving column-level experiences intact. For each crux, also specify what empirical finding or theoretical proof would cause each camp to revise its view, such as simultaneous reportable states from decoupled modules, or a demonstration that local maxima are necessarily redundant with respect to the global maximum. Write formally and without bibliographies, citation lists, or page-specific references to published works, since this is a conceptual exercise. Finally, provide a brief epistemic self-assessment: identify the point in your reasoning that is most vulnerable—whether in characterizing the pluralist position, in designing the lattice scenario, or in assuming shared formal premises—and state precisely what argument or discovery would change your mind about the structure of the agreement.
