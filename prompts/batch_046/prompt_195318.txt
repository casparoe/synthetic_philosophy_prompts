(b) Scientific explanation succeeds because it tracks real, mind-independent modal structures in nature—counterfactual-supporting regularities or law-like patterns—not merely constant conjunctions.

(c) Al-Ghazālī's appeal to the divine custom (al-ʿāda) justifies inductive reasoning: the stability of nature is rationally expectable not because created things possess causal powers, but because God consistently acts according to a stable pattern that can be interrupted only through miracle.

(d) Moral responsibility requires that human agents be genuine originators of their actions, not merely acquirers (muktasibūn) of actions directly created by God; consequently, Al-Ghazālī's doctrine of acquisition (kasb) does not fully repair the threat that occasionalism poses to accountability.

(e) The hard problem of consciousness is dissolved rather than solved by extending occasionalism to mental-physical correlations: on each occasion God creates both a brain-state and its corresponding phenomenal state, with no metaphysical necessity linking them, so there is no explanatory gap that demands a naturalistic bridge—only a divinely maintained conjunction.
