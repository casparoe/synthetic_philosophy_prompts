I'm working on a long-form piece about whether synthetic language agents force us to rethink the philosophical boundary between meaning and mere pattern-matching. Imagine Ludwig Wittgenstein — who died in 1951, well before digital computation could produce fluent discourse — were alive today and asked to evaluate a large language model that generates grammatically perfect philosophical text without any embodied social practice, biological needs, or shared form of life. How would he apply his distinction between criteria and symptoms, together with his argument that a private language is impossible because language is inseparable from public, rule-governed practices, to the claim that such a model understands its outputs? Would he treat the system as speaking a kind of pseudo-language that mimics publicity, or would he insist that asking whether it genuinely understands is itself a category mistake produced by a misleading analogy with human speakers? And in either case, what does his analysis reveal about whether the problem of other minds even applies to artifacts that lack minds to begin with?
