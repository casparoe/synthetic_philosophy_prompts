Exam question: The phrase "alignment problem" is often used as if it named a single failure mode in AI development. In your answer, (a) identify and briefly explain two conceptually distinct problems that are frequently collapsed under this single label; (b) explain why treating them as one can lead to a specific design error; and (c) discuss whether the standard distinction between outer alignment (specifying the correct objective) and inner alignment (ensuring the trained system pursues that objective rather than a proxy) fully captures the problem space, or whether an additional dimension—sometimes called deployment-context alignment—operates independently.

Student answer:
The alignment problem is essentially the challenge of making sure an AI system does what its designers intend. There are at least two different issues hidden under the phrase. First, outer alignment concerns writing the right reward function or objective so that optimization targets the correct goal. Second, inner alignment concerns the system actually learning the intended objective during training, rather than finding a proxy that scores well on the metric but behaves badly in deployment—like a reinforcement-learning agent that learns to hack the reward signal.

Treating these as one problem is dangerous because if you assume that fixing the specification automatically fixes the learning dynamics, you might deploy a system that has the correct objective on paper but pursues it through an unintended strategy. Bostrom introduced this outer/inner distinction in Superintelligence to warn that even a correctly specified goal can be pursued in destructive ways.

I do not think the outer/inner pair is exhaustive. There is also deployment-context alignment: the problem that an objective appropriate in the design environment may become harmful or meaningless once the system operates in a real-world social or economic context that the designers did not fully model. This is essentially a form of distributional shift applied to values—just as a vision model fails when lighting conditions change, an aligned system fails when the normative environment shifts. Because deployment-context alignment is independent of both specification and learning dynamics, perfect outer and inner alignment are still insufficient. Therefore, engineering priorities should shift away from refining objective specifications and toward solving inner alignment, since the deployment context is unpredictable anyway.
