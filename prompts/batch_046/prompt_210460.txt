I am a doctoral candidate in metaphysics drafting a three-year research agenda on the problem of universals. Rather than rehearsing the familiar realism versus nominalism debate, I wish to examine whether ontological dependence relations—specifically grounding and essential dependence—can perform the explanatory functions traditionally assigned to universals without treating them as sui generis entities; I tentatively label this orientation dependence-first universalism, without claiming any established pedigree for the term. Given my background in logic rather than formal mathematics, which of the following open lines of inquiry should I treat as upstream and therefore prioritise: (a) whether grounding relations can be universalised without circularity; (b) how such an approach bears on the problem of mathematical applicability; and (c) whether resolving this genuinely reshapes downstream debates about natural kinds in philosophy of science? Please rank them, defend the ordering by reference to tractability, dependency relations among the questions, and the magnitude of downstream change a result would produce, and specify what conceptual development in the next two years would cause you to revise the ranking. Please respond in British English.
