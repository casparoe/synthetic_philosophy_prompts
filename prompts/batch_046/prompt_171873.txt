I work as a software engineer without formal training in philosophy, and I have been trying to determine whether a structural pattern I repeatedly observe in technology stacks corresponds to any established philosophical concept. The pattern feels adjacent to path dependence and technological lock-in, but the mechanism I see does not match the standard economic accounts I have encountered.

Consider a concrete instance. In 2015, a development team adopts an intermediate data representation largely because a particular vendor's tooling made it convenient at the time; there is no deep technical justification for the choice itself. By 2028, that representation has accumulated an ecosystem of internal libraries, documentation conventions, hiring filters, and design patterns. What is notable is not merely that migration is expensive—there are always switching costs—but that the representation has gradually reshaped the conceptual vocabulary of the team. Engineers now formulate problems in terms native to that representation, so that proposing a cleaner underlying model sounds not like a costly upgrade but like a category mistake, comparable to asking why gravity does not apply on Tuesdays. The original contingent decision has become invisible, and the trajectory now feels overdetermined in hindsight: anyone reviewing the history sees a straight line of necessity rather than a fork that was taken.

In standard discussions of path dependence, the locking mechanisms are usually inertia, network effects, or accumulated investment. Here, however, the path operates through conceptual restructuring: it does not only constrain future options but progressively redefines the space of intelligible alternatives, so that retrospective rationalization becomes part of the mechanism itself.

Does philosophy of history or philosophy of technology have an established term for this specific variant—where path dependence works through the colonization of the conceptual framework required to evaluate alternatives—rather than through cost or network externalities? If the nearest established concepts are ordinary path dependence and Kuhnian paradigm entrenchment, how precisely do they differ from this case, and what are the boundary conditions? If no precise term exists, what name and definition would best capture the phenomenon, clearly distinguishing it from economic lock-in and from ordinary conceptual inertia?
