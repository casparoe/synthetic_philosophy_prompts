I'm working through my position on practical reason for a graduate seminar on normativity, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present. Here is roughly where I've landed: (1) I accept a broadly internalist account of reasons for action, holding that a consideration counts as a reason for an agent only if there is some connection—actual or counterfactual—to that agent's motivational set, though I allow that this counterfactual connection can be mediated by sound practical reasoning rather than brute psychological continuity. (2) I nonetheless maintain that moral obligations are objective in the sense that they apply to agents regardless of their contingent desires; in particular, I think the amoralist genuinely has a reason to refrain from harming others even if sound deliberation would not move them. (3) I subscribe to a form of motivational internalism about moral judgment: if an agent sincerely judges that she morally ought to φ, then she necessarily has at least some motivation to φ, though that motivation may be weak or overridden. (4) I take practical rationality to be fundamentally a matter of coherence and means-end efficiency within an agent's existing framework of ends, not a matter of tracking independent normative truths. (5) I believe that reflective equilibrium, applied to our considered normative judgments together with our best psychological theories, can justify objective moral principles even though that justification does not depend on each agent's pre-existing motivations.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what the least costly revision would be for each real tension. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. Be specific about whether (1) can survive (2) given my allowance for counterfactual deliberation in (1), and about whether (3) ends up helping or hurting my defense of (2). I am not signaling which commitment I would drop; I want the audit to trace exactly what each view commits me to, including whether (4) undermines (5) or whether they can be separated by distinguishing justification from rational motivation.
