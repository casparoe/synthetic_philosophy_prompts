I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I am trying to work through a position I have been calling tranquilism for a discussion group: the claim that the only things with moral or existential weight are the absence of craving and the absence of suffering. On this view, positive states—joy, achievement, love, aesthetic experience—carry no intrinsic value; they are worth pursuing only instrumentally, to the extent that they suppress negative ones. I want to test this idea against an existing narrative rather than a thought experiment, and the film Groundhog Day seems to function almost like a controlled laboratory for it. The protagonist, Phil Connors, is trapped in an infinite repetition of February 2nd in Punxsutawney. He cycles through hedonism, despair, suicide attempts, mastery of skills, and finally a quiet rhythm of service to others. By the end he appears genuinely at peace, and the repetition breaks.

Read the film through the lens of tranquilism. Does Phil's arc show that every positive good he acquires—romance with Rita, musical skill, kindness to strangers—is merely an instrumental mechanism for reaching a zero-craving equilibrium? Or does the film resist that reading, implying that the loop only becomes bearable once Phil stops treating each day as a problem to be optimized away? I am particularly interested in the structural feature of the loop itself: if time has no forward progression and every morning resets the world, is suffering multiplied by repetition, or is it effectively canceled once craving is eliminated?

Focus on the late sequence in which Phil stops trying to change events and instead lives through the day helping strangers and playing music. Is this the tranquilist endpoint—a system with zero unfulfilled requests—or is it a disguised form of craving, perhaps for moral approval or for the affection of Rita? I want a reading that is faithful in both directions: do not reduce the film to an illustration of the idea, and do not distort the idea to fit the plot. Where does tranquilism illuminate a specific scene or character choice, and where does the film complicate the engineer's intuition that an optimized system is one with no errors and no pending requests? What does Groundhog Day contribute to the philosophical debate that pure conceptual argument cannot?
