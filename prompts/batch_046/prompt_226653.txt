So there's this weird mix in democratic reform talk: people push for sortition — like randomly picking citizens for climate assemblies — but they leave career polticians in charge of the budget, and they put term-limits on elected officials while leaving both the bureaucrats and the lottery-selected panels free of them. What philosophical view of representation or democratic legitimacy does that combo preseuppose? Does it assume random citizens have a special kind of unbiased wisdom, but only for "values" questions, while careerists own the "technical" ones? And is there a hidden theory of accountability — term-limits to stop corruption, sortition to stop partisanship, yet career staff stay precisely because we want institutional memory? I wonder if there's one coherent view behind all three, or if it's just an inconsistancy cobbled together from different ideas about fairness and knowledge. If we actually replaced career polticians everywhere with rotating sortition panels, which parts of the current setup would become impossible to justify?
