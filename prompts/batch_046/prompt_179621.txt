Write an essay addressing the following philosophical controversy. Malthus argued that human populations tend to grow geometrically while the means of subsistence grow only arithmetically, producing inevitable positive checks unless restrained. For roughly two centuries this prediction seemed empirically defeated: sustained per-capita economic growth, the demographic transition, and rapid agricultural innovation appeared to break the Malthusian mechanism. Some contemporary interpreters, however, maintain that the theory was never falsified—only delayed—claiming it remains structurally sound as a long-run constraint that technological progress temporarily masked.

Weigh carefully whether a predictive framework of this kind can be legitimately preserved after prolonged apparent failure by appealing to a deferred timeline, without collapsing into an unfalsifiable schema. Your essay should specifically examine three issues: first, whether extending the predicted crisis into an indefinite future functions as an ad hoc immunization strategy of the kind philosophers of science have criticized, or whether such temporal extension can be independently motivated by the theory's own structural logic; second, whether observed demographic and agricultural changes constitute genuine falsifying evidence, or merely unspecified boundary conditions that leave the core mechanism intact; and third, whether there exists a principled philosophical distinction between a theory being correct but premature and a theory being empirically empty.

Reach and defend a definite conclusion: either that the Malthusian framework can be rehabilitated as a valid long-run predictive structure without committing a fallacy of predictive rescue, or that any such rehabilitation necessarily strips the theory of empirical content. Your reasoning should proceed from conceptual analysis of falsification, predictive scope, and theoretical rescue rather than from appeals to specific scholarly authorities or bibliographic references.
