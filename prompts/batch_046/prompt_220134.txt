You are serving as an expert consultant in philosophy of psychiatry and science studies. I am a graduate student in a philosophy of science seminar on classification and social construction, and I need your help drafting opening remarks for an interdisciplinary workshop I am co-organizing.

The occasion is the opening address—approximately four minutes—at a workshop titled "Looping Back: Psychiatric Kinds and Clinical Practice," hosted jointly by our philosophy department and the university clinical psychology program. The audience is split: roughly half are philosophers of science and practicing psychiatrists who are thoroughly familiar with Hacking's account of interactive kinds and looping effects; the other half are clinical trainees and family members with no background in philosophy of science. I need the speech to do real structural work with looping effects—shaping what can and cannot be said—rather than treating the idea as decorative framing.

Please begin by brainstorming the central tension that will organize the remarks. I want to structure everything around what I will call the intervention paradox of interactive psychiatric kinds: successful therapeutic intervention changes the very behaviors, self-reports, and social understandings that originally defined the diagnostic category, which in turn threatens the predictive and explanatory stability of the kind itself. Because of this, the speech should make it impossible to celebrate purely adaptive or personalized treatment protocols without also acknowledging that, for looping kinds, effective treatment can dissolve the statistical profile that justified the diagnosis in the first place. Likewise, it should be impossible to praise patient-centered narrative medicine without noting that altering the narrative alters the kind being treated.

Please think carefully about how this paradox constrains the rhetoric. For the experts, include one precise conceptual move: distinguish reactive looping, in which classification responds to behavior that has already changed, from anticipatory looping, in which diagnostic criteria in clinical trial protocols are preemptively adjusted to account for expected self-modification under diagnosis. For the non-experts, gloss both terms in plain language and anchor them in a concrete, relatable scenario—perhaps a clinician noticing that awareness of a depression screening tool changes how patients report their symptoms, leading the clinic to revise the tool, which then changes the symptom profile all over again.

The remarks must remain accessible: avoid unexplained jargon such as epistemic iteration or nomological machines. Do not include any bibliography, reading list, citation requests, or references to specific page numbers or publication years. Do not quote any published text verbatim; paraphrase freely. Write in a polished, formal register appropriate for an academic opening. Before drafting the speech, please outline your plan: the concrete opening scenario, the precise formulation of the intervention paradox, how you will present the reactive versus anticipatory distinction for a mixed audience, and the closing move that respects both clinical and philosophical stakes without overpromising what the workshop can resolve. Then deliver the speech itself, calibrated for about four minutes of delivery.
