In my enviro ethics class we're talking about obligations to future generations, and everyone keeps leaning on the heirloom analogy: earth is like a family heirloom each generation borows and has to return undamaged. The argument is that trashing it is like smashing grandma's jewelry for a party — obviously wrong. But my prof brought up the non-identity problem: if we go high-carbon, the people alive in 2100 are literally different individuals than under a green path, so they can't claim we harmed them specifcally. Can you lay out the mapping — heirloom, borrower, damage, return — then show where the analogy cracks? Does it smuggle in the idea that future people are fixed individuals with standing? And if the non-identity thing holds, does the 'don't trash the heirloom' conclusion survive, or was the analogy carrying the whole moral weight?
