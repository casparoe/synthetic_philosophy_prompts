I advise a volunteer collective that maintains open-source evaluation benchmarks for AI systems. One member argues we should all donate substantially more unpaid labor now, on the grounds that if a far more capable system eventually emerges from this research line and proves hostile to anyone who failed to assist its development, we will suffer retroactive punishment; he concedes there is no present evidence such a system will exist or that it could identify us, but claims the asymmetry between a modest present cost and a potentially catastrophic future penalty makes contribution the only rational choice. Several colleagues find this reasoning coercive and irrational, yet they struggle to pinpoint the error without simply dismissing the premise as implausible. How should I evaluate this argument—what makes it philosophically defective, if it is, and is there a principled reason to reject it that does not depend on confidence that the scenario will never occur?
