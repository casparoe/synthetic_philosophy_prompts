Assume the role of an expert philosopher of value with particular competence in the philosophy of mind and the history of hedonism. Consider the following position, which I shall call dynamic-attunement hedonism. The heterogeneity problem asks what bodily pleasures, intellectual satisfactions, aesthetic enjoyments, and even malicious schadenfreude have in common that justifies grouping them all as pleasure. Dynamic-attunement hedonism answers that they do not share a uniform phenomenal quality—there is no single “common feel” running through all of them. Instead, they are unified functionally: each is a mental state in which (i) an object is registered as positively valued by the subject, and (ii) this registration produces a self-reinforcing loop that tends to preserve or deepen the very evaluative stance that generated it, irrespective of sensory modality or cognitive content. On this view, pleasure is not a raw sensation but a structured mode of affective-cognitive attunement.

I hold that this position solves the heterogeneity problem without collapsing pleasure into mere desire-satisfaction or into a purely cognitive judgment. Play it straight: what would have to be true for this view to be wrong?

In particular, address the following:

- Which of its two core conditions—(i) positive evaluative registration, or (ii) the self-reinforcing attunement loop—could in principle fail, and what would the mind or the world have to look like for each failure?
- Are there live empirical or phenomenological counter-examples—e.g., pleasures that seem to undermine rather than reinforce their own evaluative basis, or states that satisfy both conditions yet intuitively fail to count as pleasure?
- How exposed is the view to the charge that it has been insulated into unfalsifiability by redefining pleasure functionally? Is that insulation a genuine problem for a view of this kind, or is it an appropriate theoretical move?
- What would defenders of this position themselves concede as counting against it, not merely what critics allege?
- Distinguish evidence or arguments that would be decisive from those that would merely be awkward, and conclude with an honest assessment of how well-protected the position actually is.
