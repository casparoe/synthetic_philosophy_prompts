Construct a decision tree for a philosophy of economics reading group that sorts positions on whether diachronic money pumps establish irrationality. The root commitment is this: an agent who is locally rational at each choice point can nonetheless be structurally guaranteed to suffer a net loss across a predetermined sequence of pairwise exchanges. First-level branches must distinguish whether the agent models her future self as a strategic opponent requiring subgame-perfect planning, as a delegated trustee whose authority can be revoked, or as a non-strategic temporal continuation. Second-level branches must distinguish policies on preference reversal: permitted only under evidential update, permitted under any psychologically coherent change, or categorically forbidden regardless of cause. Third-level branches must address whether the agent can deploy internal commitment mechanisms without external enforcement, and whether such mechanisms survive retrospective dissolution—specifically including the niche position of prospective binding with retrospective amnesty, in which an agent installs a future constraint but permits her later self to annul it costlessly. Each leaf should name the corresponding view, or be marked unnamed, and state in one sentence its most awkward commitment regarding resource loss. Phrase every internal node as a concrete decision scenario—for example, You know you will face bet A then bet B, and you anticipate preferring B-to-A at the second stage—so that traversal requires no prior knowledge of the philosophical labels. Flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate, and briefly diagnose whether each unoccupied logical combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
