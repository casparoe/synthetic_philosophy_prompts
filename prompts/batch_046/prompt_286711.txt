Specify the desiderata that an adequate theory of rational choice under perfect indifference—the situation classically called Buridan’s ass—must meet. For each requirement, give the motivating case or distinction: why the theory must allow action without prior preference, how it should treat the possibility that an agent’s choice retroactively creates rather than discovers a reason, and why it must preserve the difference between causal and evidential structures rather than collapsing them into one mechanism. Identify which desiderata appear jointly unsatisfiable—particularly any tension between demanding that the choice be fully reason-determined in advance and that it remain genuinely open. Then briefly evaluate randomization strategies, arbitrary cutoff rules, and acts of volitional commitment against the list, flagging which ones fall to the bootstrapping objection.
