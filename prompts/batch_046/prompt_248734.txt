I'm currently enrolled in an advanced seminar on Enlightenment moral philosophy, and we've been working through Benjamin Franklin's project of moral perfection as presented in the Autobiography. Our discussions have centered on his list of thirteen virtues, the cyclical weekly practice he used to monitor his failures with black marks in a personal ledger, and the notable fact that he appended Humility to the original twelve only after a Quaker acquaintance rebuked him for habitual pride. Drawing on our professor's framing, I have come to understand Franklin's ordering—beginning with Temperance and Silence and ending with Humility—not as an arbitrary checklist but as a kind of sequential architecture in which each virtue operates as a necessary precondition for the one that follows, with Humility serving as a capstone that retroactively validates the entire structure. This interpretation, however, has left me with a logical confusion that I cannot resolve on my own.

Franklin openly admits that he never achieved perfection, especially regarding Humility, observing that the very effort to cultivate modesty often left him feeling secretly proud of his own self-awareness. If we take the sequential architecture seriously, his failure at the final stage seems to entail that the preceding twelve virtues were never genuinely secured either, since the capstone remains missing. Yet Franklin clearly believed he had made durable progress in areas like Order and Industry, and he never suggests that those earlier gains were illusory. So my question concerns the internal logic of his model: does Franklin intend these virtues to be cultivated in a strictly linear fashion, where incomplete mastery of one undermines all subsequent ones, or is the weekly rotation better understood as a spiral of iterative, partial improvements that do not depend on a final completion? And if the latter is correct, why does Franklin present Humility as a belated addition rather than an original component—does this imply that the democratic civic virtue he later argued was essential to republican survival must remain perpetually incomplete, or have I misunderstood the dependency relation embedded in his list?
