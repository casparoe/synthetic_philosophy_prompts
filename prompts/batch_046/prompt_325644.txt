I'm taking a philosophy of mind seminar and my friend and I got into it over whether introspection is actually trustworthy, not just occassionally wrong. I tried to formalize our disagreement and now the argument feels like it eats it's own tail. Here it is: (1) Introspection is supposed to be authoritative about my occurrent beliefs — if I sincerely report that I believe P, that's strong evidence I believe P. (2) But beliefs aren't actually discrete mental objects sitting in some "belief box"; they're spread across lots of smaller sub-personal processes, more like a weather pattern made of air currents than a single sentence in a file. (3) So when I introspect "I believe it's raining," I'm either tracking a fictional unified object or I'm tracking the whole distributed pattern. (4) If I'm tracking the fiction, then introspection is systematically misleading about my actual mental economy. (5) If I'm tracking the actual pattern, then I should be able to report on its sub-components — which smaller processes are active, how they're weighted against each other, etc. (6) Obviously I can't do that; my report is always coarse-grained and silent on the mechanics. (7) So introspection must be tracking the fiction. (8) Therefore it's unreliable about what I actually believe. (9) But then my introspection in step (8) — that introspection is unreliable — is itself about a fictional object, so I have no reason to accept it. (10) So either I accept global skepticism about my own mind, or I reject (2) and go back to treating beliefs as discrete items, which feels like denying basic cognitive science. The second option feels like giving up on science, but the first feels like it proves way too much. Which premise or step should I actually reject — (2), (3), or (7) — and what does giving each one up cost me? I really want to hang onto (1) and (6) if I can, but tell me if that's just wishful thinking. Also please push back hard if I'm mixing up levels of description or if the whole task is confused: I know the distributed-cognition premise is doing a lot of work and it might be a category mistake.
