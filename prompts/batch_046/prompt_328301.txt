I'm on the IRB for a university med school, and every other month we get study protocal's claiming to measure raw phenomenal quality in patients — after-images, psychedelic states, anesthesia awareness. The investigators always say they're isolating qualia: something intrinsic, privately known, and yet they want subjects to report it on a 1-7 scale. I think there's a deep incoherence in that package, along the Dennett quining-qualia line and the Chase-Sanborn worries about contradictory attribution conditions. So I need a reusable audit procedure — like a flowchart in words — that I can run in about 15 minutes on a measurment section. It should ask an ordered set of questions about whether the study attributes to its target all three of: (1) epistemic privacy, (2) no functional or behavioral role, and (3) public reportability. Where all three land together, flag incoherence and stop with reject. But build in escape hatches: dropping one saves the study but changes the object, and ambiguous cases where I just have to call it. Be honest about misfires — maybe it kills legitimate phenomenology or lets through disguised behaviorism.

Run it on three proposals:
A. A study asking post-op patients to rate the pure intrinsic redness of a visual after-image on a Likert scale.
B. A study using only reaction-time discrimination between tones, zero first-person report, claiming this captures what it is like to hear them.
C. A study using structured first-person interviews about ineffable qualitative shifts during ketamine infusion, where investigators explicitly deny the reports have any functional role.

Then after the procedure, give me a brief JSON summary with fields: "procedure_name", "steps" (array of strings), "hard_cases" (array of strings), "case_results" (object with keys A, B, C each mapping to "flag/reject/escape/judgment"), and "known_misfires" (array of strings). Write it casual, like you're talking to a tired committee member who needs this done before lunch.
