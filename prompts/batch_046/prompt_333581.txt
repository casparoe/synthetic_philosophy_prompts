I have been working through the standard philosophical distinction between finkish dispositions and masked—sometimes called antidoted—dispositions, and I have devised a scenario that seems to collapse the two categories. Consider a ceramic vessel that is genuinely fragile, disposed to shatter upon sharp impact, but which is enclosed in an adaptive reactive shell. When struck, the shell does not merely absorb the blow, which would constitute an ordinary mask; instead, it detects the impact and instantaneously triggers an internal chemical restructuring that transforms the ceramic into a flexible polymer, thereby destroying the fragility at precisely the moment the triggering condition is satisfied. After the impact, the vessel remains inside the same shell, now composed of an entirely different material. My difficulty is this: the shell simultaneously prevents the expected manifestation (the shattering) and eliminates the underlying disposition (the fragility), with no temporal gap between these effects. Does this show that the fink/mask taxonomy is not genuinely categorical but depends instead on an arbitrary choice about whether to treat the shell and its contents as a single compound bearer of dispositions or as separate entities? And more generally, if any masked disposition can be redescribed as a fink operating on a broader system that includes the interfering factor, does the distinction retain any independent explanatory value for evaluating conditional analyses of dispositional ascriptions?
