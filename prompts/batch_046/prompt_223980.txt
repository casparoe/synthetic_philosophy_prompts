I chair our university’s Curriculum Integrity Board, and since my own department’s enrollment crisis last fall I have become personally convinced that we need a rigorous, binding decision about our legacy general-education sequence. The sequence originated in 1968 from one professor’s idiosyncratic reading list and grew by institutional accretion rather than by design; we must vote by March whether to preserve it intact, replace it with a modular skills-based core, or phase it out over three years. The central philosophical tension is path dependence: several colleagues argue that the curriculum is genuinely “locked in,” meaning that any removal of a pillar collapses an irreplaceable architecture shaped by contingent history, while others contend that the same critical-reasoning outcomes would have converged regardless of that 1968 origin, so the current form is functionally replaceable without loss.

To structure my draft opinion, I want the board to work through a conceptual distinction I have been developing—call it the “gravity-well versus canal-branch” framework—without treating it as settled: a gravity-well model holds that pedagogical institutions are pulled toward functional optima and would reach similar outcomes from many different starting points, whereas a canal-branch model holds that an initial contingent cut, like that 1968 syllabus, determines every downstream feature, so that reform now is not improvement but the destruction of an irreplaceable trajectory. The memo must recommend one concrete course of action with a clear timeline, treat the strongest canal-branch argument with genuine intellectual respect rather than dismissal, and avoid any empirical claim that I could not verify without access to a research library.

Please present your response as exactly five numbered items, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introductory or concluding text outside the numbering; taken together, these should form the memo’s bottom-line recommendation, the reasoning supporting it, the honest acknowledgment of the opposing view’s best point, the proposed implementation timeline, and a brief note on the chief risk of the recommended path. Within these five items, please indicate your confidence in each claim you make, and in the final item explicitly identify the one point about which you are least certain, explaining in a single sentence why that particular judgment is the weakest link in your reasoning.
