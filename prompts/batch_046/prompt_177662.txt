If Kurt Gödel were alive today and encountered the proposal that consciousness might survive death not through an immaterial soul but through continuous instantiation as a computation within the space of all consistent formal systems — treating objective mathematical reality itself as the substrate for posthumous existence — how do you think he would respond? Given his own conviction that there is an afterlife in which the mind comes to understand all mathematical truths, would he regard this computational-platonist account as a natural extension of his belief that mathematics exists independently of human minds, or would he reject it for substituting an abstract algorithm in place of a genuine soul?
