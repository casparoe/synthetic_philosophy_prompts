I had a long and rather exhausting argument with a friend about speciesism and the grounds of moral status for non-human animals, and I have tried to crystallize our deadlock in the outline of a short essay. The central claim is this: if anti-speciesism is correct that moral status tracks sentience rather than species membership, then deliberately engineering farm animals to possess drastically reduced sentience—so that they experience little or no suffering while remaining biologically productive—ought to count as a profound moral improvement, perhaps even an obligation, since it minimizes harm without sacrificing benefit. Yet there is a powerful contrary intuition that creating stunted beings specifically in order to exploit them is not a moral victory but something closer to an atrocity. This suggests that our objection to factory farming is not fully captured by the reduction of suffering, but involves a kind-based or integrity-based constraint: there is something wrong with reducing a being to a mere instrument, even if that being lacks the capacity to suffer. The essay asks whether anti-speciesism can consistently accommodate such a constraint without collapsing into the very speciesism it rejects, or whether it must instead bite the bullet and endorse engineered diminution.

Could you comment on the coherence of this framing? In particular, I am unsure whether I have constructed a false dilemma: might the anti-speciesist reply that the wrongness lies not in the diminished sentience itself, but in the instrumentalization, which remains independently objectionable? Or is there a hidden contradiction in holding both that reducing suffering is good and that reducing the capacity to suffer for the sake of exploitation is bad? I would also welcome your assessment of whether analogies to other biotechnological interventions—such as breeding anencephalic animals for organ harvest—strengthen or weaken the point, and whether there is a principled distinction between permissible welfare improvements and impermissible moral-status reductions. I am not asking for citations, bibliographies, or a reading list; I want to know whether the conceptual architecture holds together and what, if anything, both sides in my original disagreement were missing.
