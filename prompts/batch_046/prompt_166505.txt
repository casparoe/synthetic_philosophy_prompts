Consider the problem of gradated moral status, but with a specific framing intended to break the usual scalar intuition: instead of arranging entities on a single continuum from thermostats through insects to humans, treat moral status as a vector with at least three independent dimensions—(i) phenomenal consciousness or suffering-capacity; (ii) preference-autonomy or goal-directedness; and (iii) relational or ecological embeddedness that generates derivative interests. Because these dimensions do not align across entities—an insect may score non-trivially on (i) and (iii) but near-zero on (ii), while an advanced thermostat or language model might score on (ii) without (i)—the result is not a ranking problem but a problem of status conflicts and potential incommensurability.
