I encountered a philosophy-of-mind handout that characterizes Daniel Dennett's view of real patterns and the three predictive stances—physical, design, and intentional—in the following terms. The physical stance explains a system's behavior through its underlying physics and chemistry; the design stance explains it by treating the system as optimized for a function, whether through engineering or natural selection; and the intentional stance explains it by attributing beliefs, desires, and rational decision-making. According to the handout, Dennett holds that a pattern is genuinely real only if it is projectible from the physical stance, which entails that patterns detected at the design or intentional levels are merely pragmatic heuristics lacking independent ontological weight. The handout therefore claims that Dennett regards intentional-state attributions as literally false but instrumentally indispensable: they have no truth conditions, and a complete neuroscientific account would reveal only neural dynamics, with nothing corresponding to beliefs or desires in the brain. The handout also asserts that Dennett restricts the design stance to evolved biological organisms, explicitly denying that artifacts, economic markets, or linguistic conventions genuinely invite design-level explanation. From all of this it concludes that Dennett is ultimately a reductive physicalist about the mind—someone who tolerates mental talk only as a temporary predictive convenience that a mature physics would eventually render obsolete.

How fair is this summary? Please examine its main claims one by one: which ones a careful reader of Dennett would accept, which ones subtly misrepresent his position and in what direction, and which ones are outright mistakes. I am especially interested in whether the handout's final conclusion—that Dennett is a physicalist eliminativist—follows from the accurate portions of the summary, or whether it relies on the errors. If the summary confuses Dennett's actual view with a stricter instrumentalism or with eliminative materialism, please identify the distinction and explain what Dennett really says about the reality of intentional patterns.
