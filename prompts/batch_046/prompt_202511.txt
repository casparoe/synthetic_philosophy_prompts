Write a satirical, deadpan internal memorandum—approximately 600 words, presented as continuous prose only with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if composed in a single unbroken letter—from the "Office of Evolutionary Governance" announcing a university-wide restructuring of all biological research and teaching according to a newly adopted "Unit-First Administrative Protocol." The memo should declare that, effective immediately, all organisms have been administratively dissolved into their constituent replicators, vehicles, and interactors, each assigned separate budgets, tenure lines, and conflict-of-interest policies; that altruistic behavior will henceforth be audited through inclusive-fitness ledgers rather than evaluated by moral philosophy; and that evolutionary transitions in individuality—such as the origin of multicellularity or eusocial colonies—will be treated as mergers subject to antitrust review and transitional-compliance oversight. The satire should arise from treating genuinely contested conceptual distinctions—replicator versus vehicle versus interactor, the conditions under which multilevel selection operates versus whether kin selection and group selection are mathematically equivalent descriptions, what counts as an individual during an evolutionary transition, and whether the extended phenotype should be taxed as outsourced labor—as if they were settled bureaucratic categories with clear reporting structures. Include at least one invented but conceptually coherent administrative mechanism, such as a "Transitional Individuality Compliance Office" or a "Phenotypic Outsourcing Tax," so that the piece requires original conceptual construction rather than repetition of standard textbook summaries. The tone must remain deadpan throughout: no explicit winking, no direct statement of objections, no named living scholars, and no request for citations or bibliographies. A specialist should recognize that the memo grasps why the gene-centered view, the individual-centered view, and multilevel selection actually disagree about what counts as adaptation, altruism, and exploitation, while a non-specialist should understand that something is profoundly wrong with reducing living systems to accounting units. The piece must be fully intelligible to a model without web access.
