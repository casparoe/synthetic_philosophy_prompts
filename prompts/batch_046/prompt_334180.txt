I coordinate maintenance for a neighborhood tool cooperative that recently acquired a precision CNC mill requiring a certified operator. I earned the certification, but I am relocating in four months, and the cooperative needs a fully trained replacement before then. Despite recognizing that the apprenticeship should begin immediately, I have deferred scheduling the training sessions for eight consecutive weeks — each time telling myself that the mill is not in heavy use, that I am too exhausted after my paid work, or that I will certainly start next Monday. At the same time, I am drafting a binding agreement that would require me to return for one weekend per month during the six months after my move, specifically to supervise the remaining handover. I am inclined to sign it, reasoning that this future obligation will force me to complete the preparation now; yet I also suspect I may be manufacturing a costly future commitment primarily to justify present delay. Without asking for generic productivity advice, can you assess whether my pattern of deferral and my proposed contract represent a coherent response to my own preferences over time, or whether the contract is better understood as a rational precommitment mechanism against present bias?
