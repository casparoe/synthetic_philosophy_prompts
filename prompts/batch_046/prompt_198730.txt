Please write an exam question with a model answer and marking scheme for a final-year undergraduate philosophy exam: 90 minutes, an essay of approximately 800 words, 30 marks, on what exactly Descartes's cogito proves. The question must not be answerable by a standard recitation of the claim that I think therefore I am establishes that I exist as a thinking substance; instead, it should force students to confront whether the argument proves only the occurrence of a present mental event, the existence of a particular thinker, or the substantial nature of that thinker, and where it risks circularity. To keep the prompt away from textbook summaries, inject the following niche framing: present a hypothetical objector who argues that the move from there is thinking occurring to I am a thinking thing illegitimately assumes that every mental event must belong to a persisting substance with identity conditions that Descartes has not yet secured, and ask students to evaluate whether Descartes can block this without appealing to premises that depend on the very substantiality he aims to establish. The question should also require students to address whether temporal parts of a thinker pose a specific problem for the scope of the conclusion.

Please provide:
1. The exam essay question itself, clearly worded and self-contained, with the niche framing built into the prompt.
2. A model answer of roughly 600-700 words.
3. A marking scheme that distinguishes a first-class answer (A range, 70 per cent and above) from a strong upper-second answer (B range, 60-69 per cent) by naming the specific philosophical moves each must contain, and that identifies at least two specific misconceptions or errors that should cost marks.
4. Write everything in British English (for example, behaviour, centre, programme).
5. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
6. Do not use verbatim quotations inside quotation marks unless you have fetched the exact wording from a source; otherwise paraphrase without quotation marks. Do not make the answer depend on specific publication dates or titles that have not been verified.
