For a reading group I have been asked to articulate my philosophical profile on language acquisition and the poverty of the stimulus, so that the others can identify inconsistencies. Here is my position, stated precisely: (1) I accept that the poverty-of-the-stimulus argument succeeds specifically for core syntax—namely, the acquisition of unbounded hierarchical embedding—while failing for phonology, lexical semantics, and pragmatic inference; (2) I reject any language-specific innate module in the style of Fodor or Chomsky, but I accept that human infants possess an innate, domain-general bias toward hierarchical grouping in sequential input, structurally analogous to spatial navigation; (3) I maintain that pure statistical learning over surface-form distributions, absent architectural biases, cannot account for the rapid acquisition of recursive syntax by age three, yet I also hold that connectionist architectures equipped solely with domain-general working-memory constraints and hierarchical attention mechanisms can approximate recursive grammars given data volumes comparable to a child's actual multimodal exposure; (4) I believe the broader explanatory gap in language acquisition is largely dissolved once we acknowledge that linguistic input is far richer than standard text-corpus analyses suggest—through prosodic bootstrapping, cross-modal statistical correlations, and social scaffolding—without invoking innate syntactic representations; and (5) I take it as definitional that if a learning mechanism requires innate representational primitives specific to a domain, then it is not genuinely learning but merely triggering those primitives, which is why I deny that there are innate syntactic primitives of any kind. Please audit these commitments. For each pair that pulls in genuinely incompatible directions, indicate the severity of the tension, whether it demands abandoning a core claim or only qualifying it, and identify the cheapest revision that restores consistency. Also identify any pair that only appears to conflict but dissolves on closer inspection. Order the real tensions by severity. Finally, if I could make only a single revision to preserve my anti-nativist and anti-representationalist instincts while maximizing coherence, which commitment should I adjust and how?
