I'm putting together a taxonomy tree for a philosophy blog post—comparing Norway's Halden prison to American mass incarceration, but deeper than the usual rehab-vs-punishment framing. I need four branching claims that actually split positions, not just label them.

The splits should be:
1. Is the core failure quantitative (we lock up far too many people) or qualitative (the conditions inside are wrong regardless of census)?
2. When rehabilitation works, is it because the prisoner becomes a different moral agent, or just gains skills and social capital?
3. Is the harm of prison mainly excessive suffering, or mainly a specific degradation—being treated like inventory rather than as a person?
4. Does the physical layout and daily rhythm carry moral meaning that programming alone cant fix?

Arrange these into a tree where each path leads to a leaf. Every leaf should name a position (or write 'unnamed'), summarize it in one sentence, and state its most awkward commitment. Note any logically possible but empty combinations and diagnose them—incoherent, ugly, or just unexplored. Flag positions that answer identically across branches so the tree cant separate them.

Dont just map options—take a committed stance on which branches survive scrutiny and which ones collapse. Start right with the tree itself, no preface or commentary. Audience is readers who know Halden is fancy but havent thought about prison architecture as moral expression.
