I recently concluded an extended debate with a friend concerning origin essentialism—the view that a particular person necessarily originates from the specific sperm and egg from which she in fact developed. My friend assigns this thesis a credence of roughly 0.85; I am closer to 0.35. To locate our disagreement precisely, I ask for your own credence in the following carefully circumscribed claim: necessarily, I could not have developed from a different sperm-egg pair, even if the counterfactual zygote possessed my exact genome, developed within a uterine environment functionally identical to my actual one, and yielded an organism with full psychological continuity with me. Decompose your reasoning by assigning a rough probability, together with one line of justification, to each of the following components: first, that the essential properties of a biological individual are fixed by metaphysical necessity tied to actual causal origins rather than to structural, genetic, or functional continuity; second, that the individuating origin is the particular gametes themselves, rather than the specific genetic sequence produced or the spatiotemporal location of the fusion event; third, that our modal intuitions about alternative origins survive rigorous control for genomic and environmental identity, and that these intuitions genuinely track metaphysical possibility rather than merely epistemic accessibility or narrative coherence; fourth, that standard arguments for origin essentialism avoid conflating necessary identity conditions with tautological descriptions of actual history. Combine these into an overall credence. Then indicate which component your final number is most sensitive to, and specify which component is the likeliest source of divergence between my friend at approximately 0.85 and me at approximately 0.35. Lastly, describe what form of evidence or hypothetical scenario—such as synthetic embryogenesis using reconstructed gametes that replicate an existing genome—would most substantially revise your credence.
