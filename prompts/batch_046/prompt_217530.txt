Write a dialogue between two AI safety researchers—Dr. A, defending an approval-directed architecture, and Dr. B, defending a goal-directed architecture—debating the design of an emergency-resource allocator for a regional hospital network. Dr. A’s system uses a counterfactual approval oracle that estimates the ratings human supervisors would have assigned to alternative allocations, but the oracle was trained on historical data in which supervisors systematically overweighted visible patient gratitude relative to clinical outcomes. Dr. B’s system optimizes an explicit utility function over survival and distributive equity, with weights fixed by a deliberative civic panel, yet Dr. B acknowledges that these weights risk “value lock-in” if community norms shift. Each should respond directly to the other’s central worry: Dr. A should argue that adversarial auditing of the oracle can correct for gratitude bias without abandoning corrigibility, while Dr. B should argue that explicit weights, despite their rigidity, prevent hidden optimization loops that approval-seeking might mask. The exchange should remain balanced—neither speaker reduced to a strawman—and should end without either conceding, leaving the tension between adaptability and transparency genuinely unresolved.
