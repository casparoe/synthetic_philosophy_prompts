ive been trying to systematize my gut feelings about whether beauty is in the object or in us, especially after re-reading Hume's essay on taste and finding it kind of unsatisfying. Here are my verdicts on specific cases, and I can't tell if there's one principle behind them or if I'm just inconsistnet.

Case 1: There's a landscape painting that gives me an immediate, strong feeling of beauty. Then I learn the painter traced it from a photo and lied about the location. My feeling doesnt change, but I now think it's not truly beautiful, just manipulative. So authenticity seems to matter even when my response stays the same.

Case 2: There's atonal music I hated, then studied for weeks and now find profound. My friend with equal training still hates it. I think I'm right about its structural coherence, but Hume says ideal critics should converge. So maybe convergence isnt required?

Case 3: A museum shows a perfect hologram of a destroyed sculpture - visual experience identical to the original. I think the hologram isnt beautiful at all, just a ghost. But in another room there's a restored statue where 40% is modern plaster, and I think that one IS beautiful, maybe more so because we can see the form. So reconstruction is okay here but not there?

Case 4: Two unbiased experienced critics disagree about a poem's ambiguity - one calls it beautiful, the other ugly for failing to resolve. I feel like both could be right, which makes beauty seem totally subjective.

Is there a coherent principle? Maybe something about whether aesthetic properties are "grounded in the object in the right way"? Or do I have to drop one of these verdicts? Which ones are actually incompatible?
