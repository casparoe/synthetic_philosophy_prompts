Two of my friends, Jake and Mara, got into it over beers about what Wittgenstein actually meant about religious belief. (1) Jake said Wittgensteins point is that religious language runs its own language-game, so asking for scientific proof of God is like asking for the weight of the number three -- category error. That makes belief rationally insulated from science. (2) Mara said that cant be right, because Wittgenstein also wrote that religious belief is a kind of passion that directs your whole life, not just a grammer. If its just grammer then refusing chemo for prayer is just misusing vocabulary, but Wittgenstein clearly thought such belief was serious and world-engaging. (3) Jake replied that directing your life doesnt mean making empirical predictions. Chess directs your actions but doesnt claim anything about tumor growth. Religious belief guides ethics and ritual without competing with oncology. (4) Mara said thats too tidy. When someone literally prays for a tumor to shrink and skips treatment, theyre not playing chess -- theyre treating God as a causal agent in physics. Wittgensteins insulation works for God is love but collapses for miracle claims. Without a principled line between grammatical belief and empirical expectation, Jakes view just protects religion from criticism whenever its convenient.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. What, if anything, are both missing? Is there a consistent Wittgensteinian position that handles miracle-expectation without just reducing belief to science?
