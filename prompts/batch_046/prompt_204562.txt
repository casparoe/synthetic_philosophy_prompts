I recently became engaged to my partner of eight years, and as we plan a state-recognized ceremony that carries no religious authority, I keep facing a sincere question from friends and family that I cannot yet answer with philosophical precision: if marriage is not essentially for raising children together, what is it for, and why should the state care? I want to construct a rigorous argument—not a defensive appeal to equality, but a constructive account of the institution's purpose—that treats same-sex marriage not as an extension granted to an originally reproductive institution, but as the case that reveals marriage's actual core. Please argue for the view that marriage is fundamentally an institution for the public, legally backed transformation of private dependency into durable structures of mutual obligation, and that this "dependency-stabilization" framing makes same-sex marriage paradigmatic rather than exceptional. In developing the argument, please do the following in order: first, distinguish the procreative paradigm from the dependency-stabilization view with enough precision that the difference is conceptual, not merely rhetorical; second, explain why same-sex couples, lacking biological default pathways to care, must deliberately construct every element of their dependency network, and why that deliberate construction exposes the institution's true function rather than weakening their claim to it; third, address the objection that without reproductive potential the state has no legitimate interest in recognizing the union; fourth, defuse the concern that reducing marriage to mutual obligation collapses it into a private contract that lawyers could draft, and show why the ceremonial and status dimensions resist that reduction; and finally, draw out one implication for marriage policy that applies generally, not only to same-sex couples. Keep the reasoning philosophical rather than legal or sociological, avoid naming specific court cases or scholars, and do not provide a bibliography or reading list. I am writing this for thoughtful non-specialists, so the concepts should be exact but not technical, and I need the argument to stand on its own reasoning without relying on citations I could not verify.
