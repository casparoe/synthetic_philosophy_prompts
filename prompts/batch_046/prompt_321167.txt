For a graduate philosophy-of-mind workshop, design a sorting taxonomy—not a prose comparison—for positions on the relationship between sphexish mechanical rigidity and free will, treating behavioral flexibility not as a single property but as decomposing into at least three dimensions: (i) variability in response to genuinely identical stimuli, (ii) recoverability from interrupted sub-routines without a full loop-restart, and (iii) representational re-description of the goal-state during execution. The taxonomy should take a respondent through concrete, label-free diagnostic questions—e.g., whether an agent that repeats its nest-check exactly ten times regardless of interruption exhibits a failure of freedom specifically in dimension (ii), and whether a deterministic but representationally flexible system differs in kind from a non-representational but behaviorally flexible one—before assigning them to a leaf. Required leaves include: (a) a view that treats all three dimensions as jointly necessary and sufficient for agency; (b) a view that treats only representational flexibility as relevant and dismisses behavioral variability as epiphenomenal; (c) a view that denies any of the dimensions matter for free will but accepts them as marks of intelligence; (d) a view that accepts sphexish rigidity in non-human animals but insists human free will requires a categorical break in at least one dimension that is not mechanistically reducible. You must also include one deliberately unoccupied logical combination—e.g., a position that requires behavioral variability but denies that representational flexibility is ever present in free agents—and explain briefly why it is likely vacant (incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored). Wherever the taxonomy cannot separate two positions—such as two forms of compatibilism that agree on all three dimensions but differ only in their metaphysics of causation—flag the ambiguity explicitly rather than forcing a false separation. Phrase every branching question so that someone can answer it without knowing which philosophical label it corresponds to, and build the tree first from the logical space of answer-combinations, then locate actual positions within them. Do not request citations, bibliographies, named texts, or reading lists; the instrument should be usable by someone reasoning from first principles.
