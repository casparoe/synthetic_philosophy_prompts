so i've been trying to pin down a failure mode that keeps popping up in talk about competetive dynamics where everybody loses. here's the pattern: you've got a bunch of actors—companies, countries, whatever—locked into some harmful practice. the standard story is that no one can stop first or they get crushed. but the version i'm seeing is weirder. each actor privately hates the practice and would prefer everyone stop, but they also believe that *most other people genuinely like it* and aren't just trapped. so they keep performing enthusiasm, and this performance convinces everyone else that thier reluctance is unique. it sustains the race not through real disagreement, but through a misaligned second-order belief about values.

i've noticed it in talk about AI races—labs saying they want safety but assuming rivals are all-in—and in academia, where everyone complains about metrics but assumes colleagues actually care. is there an established name for this specific setup, where a collective action problem is fueled by pluralistic ignorance about enthusiasm rather than about harm? or is it just pluralistic ignorance with extra steps? and if it's its own thing, what are the boundary conditions—like, does it need that exit is technically possible but socially punished, or can it occur even when leaving is physically impossible? i don't need a bibliography, just want to know if the carving already exists and where its edges are.
