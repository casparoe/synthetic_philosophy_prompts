Write a two-page dissertation-fellowship proposal on the debate between Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett about whether dreams are experiences. Rather than rehashing whether first-person dream reports are authoritative, frame a precise, narrower question that both standard readings leave open: can either Malcolm’s criterion-based denial or Dennett’s confabulation account accommodate the emerging sleep-science picture of partial arousal, micro-awakenings, and discontinuous narrative fragments during NREM sleep without collapsing into the other? Argue that both thinkers share an unstated binary—either there is a continuous experiencing subject during sleep or there is none at all—and propose replacing it with a distributed, fragmentary model of nocturnal cognition in which “experience” is assembled from discontinuous traces rather than occurring as a unified stream or being wholly invented upon waking. In your proposal, state the exact question, explain why existing commentary overlooks it, outline a realistic one-year method (conceptual analysis of how “experience” is operationalized in sleep-science literature; reconstruction of Malcolm’s argument that dream reports lack independent criteria; evaluation of Dennett’s awakening-construction model against fragmentary-arousal data), describe what a successful outcome and a clear failure would each look like, and keep the scope appropriate for a single year. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; refer to philosophers and positions by name only. At the end, say what would change your mind: identify the specific argument or empirical finding that would cause you to abandon the fragmentary model and revert to either Malcolm’s or Dennett’s binary, and point to where your reasoning is most vulnerable.
