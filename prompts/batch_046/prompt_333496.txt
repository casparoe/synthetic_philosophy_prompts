I've been working through the fine-tuning argument on my own — the one about fundamental constants being improbably right for life — and I tried to push it one step further into what I think of as lineal, or narrative, tuning. Now the reasoning seems to explode into something absurd, and I can't locate the exact crack.

(1) If a specific configuration of conditions is necessary for an observed outcome, and that configuration is wildly improbable given random chance alone, then chance alone is not a sufficient explanation for the outcome.
(2) The values of the fundamental constants — gravity, the nuclear forces, the cosmological constant — are necessary for complex life, and they sit in an extraordinarily narrow band within the space of physically possible values.
(3) Therefore complex life is not sufficiently explained by chance; some further account — design, a multiverse, selection effects — is required.
(4) But my own existence is not explained merely by those constants being right; it also requires a specific evolutionary branch, the particular meeting of my parents, and the uninterrupted absence of fatal accidents on particular days across decades.
(5) For each of these historical links, there existed a vast space of alternatives that would have broken the chain and prevented me from existing; only one narrow trajectory led here.
(6) Applying (1) to each link: every specific contingent event in that chain — that meeting, that safe Tuesday in 1987, that evolutionary fork — is wildly improbable in its particularity, and each is necessary for the observed outcome (me being here now).
(7) So each link demands its own further explanation beyond chance, generating an infinite cascade of required explanations, one for every contingent event in every person's history.
(8) That result is absurd. We do not believe that every Tuesday that passes without an asteroid strike requires its own cosmic explanation.
(9) Therefore (1) must be false, and with it the original argument in (3) collapses.

What I can't see is the precise escape route. Is the error in (5), where I treat a specific historical path as if it were a parameter space to be tuned? Or is it in (6), where I confuse the improbability of this particular path with the improbability of some path being taken? I also wonder whether there's a hidden shift between explaining why life is possible at all and explaining why this exact life occurred. If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (1) and (3) intact and pay the price elsewhere, but I want to know exactly what I'm buying — and whether abandoning (5) forces me to deny that my parents' meeting was genuinely "unlikely" in any meaningful sense. Where does the chain actually break?
