Write a dialogue between two philosophers—Mara and Linde—debating whether ontology can be fixed in domains where direct information is permanently inaccessible, or whether such domains require a suspension of ontological commitment. Mara should defend a view of structural identification: she holds that when an entity’s causal and relational structure is fully mapped, its ontology is sufficiently determined even if its intrinsic nature remains hidden, and she should apply this specifically to synthetic cognitive architectures whose internal phenomenology is, by design, third-person inaccessible yet functionally isomorphic to biological cognition. Linde should defend ontological deferral: he holds that without access to the intrinsic properties that ground identity conditions, structural correspondence licenses only instrumental prediction, not ontological identification, and he should press Mara on whether treating functional analogues as ontologically equivalent commits a category mistake. The exchange must contain real back-and-forth—Mara should respond to Linde’s charge by distinguishing identity conditions from explanatory access, and Linde should reply by questioning whether structural isomorphism can ever be verified without at least some baseline of direct access. Neither side should be a strawman. Inject the following somewhat unconventional framing: they should distinguish between “natural” inaccessibility (for example, the interior of a black hole) and “designed” inaccessibility (for example, encrypted or algorithmically opaque states), and they should ask whether this distinction undermines Mara’s uniform structuralism or instead gives Linde a reason to be selectively permissive. Write in polished, formal American English.
