I've been trying to systematize my reading of Foucault for a philosophy discussion and want to check whether my interpretation actually coheres. Here are the commitments I've settled on: (1) power is fundamentally productive rather than repressive—disciplinary mechanisms generate capacities, knowledges, and subject-positions rather than merely constraining pre-existing ones; (2) knowledge and power are co-constitutive, which means there is no neutral Archimedean standpoint outside the regimes of truth that make inquiry possible; (3) the modern subject is constituted through normalization and internalized surveillance, not an autonomous agent that pre-exists and then encounters power; (4) genealogical analysis can nevertheless expose the historical contingency of present norms and genuinely open space for transformation; (5) resistance is always immanent to power relations, never arriving from some pure outside; and (6) practices of ethical self-formation in Foucault's later writings permit transformation without requiring a return to an essential human nature.

I'm particularly worried about three interactions. First, (2) and (4) seem to pull in opposite directions: if every critical discourse is already embedded in a power/knowledge nexus, then genealogy risks being just another regime of truth rather than a lever for change. Second, (3) and (5) look like they conflict—if subjects are entirely effects of power, there appears to be no locus from which resistance could emerge—though I suspect this dissolves once resistance is not treated as requiring a pre-social agent. Third, (3) and (6) sit uneasily together, because ethical self-formation sounds like it requires a kind of reflexive agency that (3) denies.

Could you audit this package? Go through the pairs, distinguish the real tensions from the merely apparent ones, and for each genuine conflict rate its severity and identify the cheapest fix—whether that's weakening (2), reconceiving genealogy, dropping (6), or something else. Then tell me which single revision or reconception would resolve the most tension at once, and what part of my interpretive project I'd have to sacrifice to make it.
