Write an essay addressing the following controversy in urban ethics. Philosophers of space sometimes distinguish between a residence right—the claim of current occupants to remain in a neighborhood without displacement by market forces or policy—and a development right—the claim of owners or municipalities to alter density and land use. NIMBY objections to upzoning are typically defended by appealing to the residence right: long-term residents have formed legitimate expectations, social ties, and participatory stakes that generate a special obligation to protect them from disruptive change. Critics reply that restrictive zoning is itself a collective mechanism that artificially constrains housing supply, thereby inflating property values and benefiting incumbent homeowners at the expense of potential newcomers who have no voice in local decisions. The deeper controversy is whether recognizing this structural asymmetry undermines the moral standing of NIMBY objections altogether, or whether residence rights retain independent moral weight even when zoning is acknowledged as coercive and exclusionary.

Weigh the strongest arguments on both sides. Consider, in particular, whether the democratic deficit of zoning—where outsiders affected by exclusion are not represented—negates any special claim residents have; and consider, conversely, whether the value of continuity, place-attachment, and community self-determination can justify exclusionary rules even if they produce scarcity. Reach and defend a clear conclusion: either that the asymmetry defeats the moral legitimacy of NIMBYism (perhaps with limited exceptions), or that residence rights survive it because they rest on non-instrumental goods that scarcity-effects do not cancel. Structure the essay with an explicit thesis, reasoned premises, and a direct defense of your verdict. Do not treat the question as merely empirical or legal; address it as a normative philosophical problem.
