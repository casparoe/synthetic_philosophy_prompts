Compare Leopold von Ranke's aspiration to represent the past exactly as it occurred with the scepticism about historical objectivity advanced by E.H. Carr and Hayden White. Carr contends that historical facts are never pure but are always mediated by the historian's selection and interpretation, while White argues that historical narratives are irreducibly structured by pre-figurative tropes—metaphor, metonymy, synecdoche, and irony. Now introduce a distinction between epistemic objectivity, which concerns the intersubjective verification of discrete archival claims, and narrative objectivity, which concerns the coherence conditions governing how those claims are emplotted into a story. Do Carr's interpretive mediation and White's tropological analysis together undermine Ranke's ideal only if we conflate these two senses of objectivity? Or do they demonstrate that even a fully accurate factual record necessarily involves an arbitrary narrative structure? Be specific about whether any meaningful sense of historical objectivity survives beyond mere factual correctness once both mediation and tropological emplotment are granted.
