In my reading group people keep comparing the arc of a civilization to the metabolic life-cycle of a big mammal -- rapid growth, peak energy burn, then senescence and inevitable collapse -- and they use it to argue that decline is biologically hardwired, so intervention is basically geriatric care. Spenglers culture-organism idea gets cited, and Ibn Khaldun's asabiyyah curve looks like a metabolic spike if you squint. Meanwhile linear-progress folks reject the framing entirely, and Polybius saw cycles as constitutional, not biological. Can you audit this analogy properly? Give me the correspodence table -- what plays the role of calories, hormones, cellular aging, death -- then the breaks: can civilizations transplant institutions? do they have DNA? Then tell me whether the fatalist conclusion survives or if the mammal picture was carrying the whole argument. And if the analogy collapses, what's a mechanical or physical analogy for cyclical history that doesn't smuggle in biological inevitability?
