I am seeking a consistency audit of my position on collective memory and the ethics of forgetting. I hold that (1) societies bear an institutional duty to preserve accurate remembrance of injustices they perpetrated or permitted; (2) individuals responsible for minor offenses long past have a moral right to be forgotten that can override public interest in remembrance; (3) Margalit's distinction between thick and thin relations explains why remembrance is obligatory within some communities and not within others; (4) Rieff's critique of therapeutic culture implies that public commemorations should avoid turning historical grief into emotional spectacle; and (5) because remembering is a collective duty, it ought to be embedded in education and monuments rather than left to private choice. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? In particular, does (4) conflict with (1) if institutional remembrance necessarily involves emotional engagement, and does (3) actually support (2) or undermine it?
