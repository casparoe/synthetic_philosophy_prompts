I've been trying to work out whether accuracy-first epistemology can survive contact with real cognitive limits. Here is the argument I keep returning to, and it seems to prove that accuracy-first thinking makes rational belief impossible for anyone with a finite mind.

(1) On the accuracy-first view, the only thing that makes one belief state better than another is that it is closer to the truth overall.

(2) Epistemic utility theory then says that, given your evidence, you should adopt the credences that maximize expected accuracy.

(3) Now suppose you face more propositions than you can precisely evaluate—thousands of hypotheses about distant events, other people's mental states, and future contingencies. Call this the triage problem.

(4) One rational response is epistemic triage: you deliberately hold coarse or suspended judgments on low-stakes propositions so you can allocate limited reasoning resources to the ones that matter for action.

(5) But any triage state assigns some propositions a less accurate credence than your evidence technically supports, which lowers the total expected accuracy summed across all propositions.

(6) So by (1) and (2), triage is always epistemically worse than distributing your best estimates everywhere, even if that leaves you with no cognitive bandwidth for practical reasoning.

(7) Since (7) is absurd—no finite agent can avoid triage—the accuracy-first view must be false, or else rational belief is impossible for finite minds.

What I cannot see is where the slip is. Is it in (5), assuming that total summed accuracy is the right metric? Or does accuracy-first epistemology already allow weighting accuracy by importance, and I am smuggling in an unweighted sum? Or is the real cost that if I reject (1), I am committed to intrinsic epistemic values other than truth—like coherence, understanding, or explanatory power—and I want to know what giving those up costs?

Please tell me which step to reject and what rejecting it costs. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Write in American English.
