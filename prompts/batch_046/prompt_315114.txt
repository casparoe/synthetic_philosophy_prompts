I'm drafing this weird novel where inanimate things carry a kind of background hum of awareness—not full human minds, just present in a way that feels almost like panpsychism if you take it seriously. I got stuck on that combination problem everyones talks about: how do you get one big consciousness out of a million tiny ones? Last week I re-read The Little Prince and it suddenly seemed like Saint-Exupéry was playing with exactly this tension, maybe without knowing it. Could you read the book through that lens?

I'm especially curious about the prince and his rose—is their love something that builds up from all the small actions like watering and sheltering, or is it one macro-experience that can't be split into parts? And the fox's lesson about taming, where he talks about becoming responsible for whatever you've tamed—does that describe a binding of two micro-subjects into something larger? I also wonder if the prince himself works like a macro-subject absorbing the strange viewpoints of the king, the businessman, and the lamp-lighter, or if he stays one unified consciousness the whole time. I need this to feel rigorous but not like I'm turning the story into a philosophy textbook. Where does the analogy actually hold, and where does a honest critic have to admit it breaks down?
