As a software engineer without formal philosophy training, I have been working through an argument about statistical machine translation in the spirit of Hofstadter's old concern that such systems merely shuffle symbols without grasping meaning. I formalized it as a chain, but it collapses into a contradiction I cannot resolve.

(1) Genuine linguistic understanding requires more than correct input-output mapping; a system must possess internal representations whose content is fixed by conditions of satisfaction or causal-functional roles, not merely by statistical correlation among symbols.
(2) Modern neural translation architectures — including transformer-based models — implement only high-dimensional statistical correlations over token distributions; they have no verified internal representations with conditions of satisfaction.
(3) Therefore these architectures do not genuinely understand language.
(4) Human infants, however, initially acquire vocabulary through statistical distributional learning in speech streams, long before they possess explicit conceptual models or direct causal access to every referent.
(5) So if (1) is correct, then an infant's early correlation-based representations do not constitute understanding.
(6) Yet infants clearly understand at least some expressions — for instance, they deploy refusal terms and request terms appropriately — despite lacking full conceptual models.
(7) Therefore (1) must be false: correct statistical correlation structure is sufficient for minimal understanding.
(8) But if statistical correlation alone is sufficient, then any system implementing the correct correlations understands, including a static lookup table pairing every input sentence with its correct translation, or a surface-level Markov chain.
(9) Such lookup tables and Markov chains do not understand language.
(10) So (7) is false, which restores (1), which forces us back to (5) — contradicting (6).

The pressure is that I seem forced either to deny that infants understand language, or to accept that lookup tables understand it. Both outcomes feel unacceptable. Where exactly does the reasoning slip? If I must preserve (1) and (3) at all costs, what price does that exact, and is there a less damaging repair — for example, a hidden distinction between embodied human correlation structures and disembodied artificial ones, or a scope restriction on (1) that saves infant understanding without rescuing statistical translation?
