Restate Foucault's account of disciplinary power, the knowledge-power nexus, and normalization—together with the panopticon and the examination as mechanisms—in the vocabulary of second-order cybernetics and homeostatic control theory, with which I am fluent. Treat norms as regulatory set-points, surveillance as feedback measurement, self-correction as homeostatic adjustment, and the productivity of power as the system's capacity to generate behavior that maintains the loop. For each element, indicate what translates cleanly, what requires distortion (for instance, whether diffuse, capillary power can be mapped onto a centralized controller without loss), and what lacks any expression in this vocabulary. Then assess whether the untranslatable residue points to a genuinely non-cybernetic dimension in Foucault's concept of power, or to an ambiguity in the original analysis that the framework exposes. In doing so, separate what is broadly settled in Foucault scholarship from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present any disputed interpretation as the consensus view.
