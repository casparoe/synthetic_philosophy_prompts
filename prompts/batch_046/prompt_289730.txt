While studying the relation between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, I have come to treat Ned Block’s distinction as essentially epistemic: access consciousness is phenomenal consciousness considered from an observer’s or reporter’s point of view, with no separate ontological status. On that reading, global workspace theory—which explains consciousness through widespread information broadcasting—should automatically cover phenomenal consciousness as well, since broadcasting is just the mechanism that makes phenomenal states accessible. To examine this, I have been imagining a hypothetical dual-broadcast architecture in which one global workspace integrates propositional content for reasoning and verbal report, while a second, functionally isolated workspace integrates affective valence without allowing cross-activation or cognitive access; in this system, information in the second workspace is globally broadcast within its own domain yet entirely unavailable to reasoning or report. Given that setup, I wonder: if Block’s distinction were truly epistemic, wouldn’t the existence of this isolated affective workspace dissolve the hard problem, because there would be no further fact about “what it is like” beyond the broadcasting itself? Is the error in my interpretation of Block—does he intend the distinction as a substantive metaphysical division rather than a methodological one?
