Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on what makes consent valid, with particular attention to deception, coercion, and competence.

Camp A ("Propositionalists"): consent is valid only when directed at an accurately described specific act; any material deception or omission about the nature of what is consented to voids consent, because the object of authorization was never actually presented, regardless of whether the outcome aligns with the consenter's deeper values.

Camp B ("Dispositionalists"): consent authorizes an agent's practical judgment within a domain rather than binding them to a fixed description; consent survives descriptive inaccuracies or omissions if the agent acts within the authorized scope, no direct coercion is present, and the consenter possesses adequate domain-specific competence, even if they lack full propositional knowledge.

The deliverable should contain:
1. A numbered section of agreed claims — formal conditions both accept (e.g., that direct coercion undermines consent; that some minimal competence is necessary; that consent is withdrawable), plus shared acknowledgments about the formal structure of their disagreement.
2. A disagreements table with columns for Issue, Propositionalist Formulation, and Dispositionalist Formulation. The issues should cover: whether consent targets a proposition or a practical authority; whether competence is global or domain-specific; and whether non-coercive deception always invalidates consent or only when it exceeds the scope of authorization. Each formulation must be worded so its own side would sign it.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I take likely cruxes to be: whether a consenter can authorize an agent to fill in unspecified details; whether anxiety-driven information avoidance undermines competence specifically; and whether omissions of low-salience but high-impact facts count as deceptive voiding.
4. For each crux, present the concrete case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use this shared case: a generally competent patient with an anxiety disorder consents to "surgery to relieve chronic back pain," authorizing the surgeon to select the appropriate technique. The surgeon chooses an experimental implant with a 5% paralysis risk and 90% efficacy, but withholds both the experimental nature and the paralysis risk, believing disclosure would trigger refusal contrary to the patient's values. For each crux, state exactly what each camp predicts about the validity of consent in this scenario, and specify what each side would count as decisive evidence capable of changing its view.

Format the output as: (I) Agreed Claims; (II) Disagreements Table; (III) Cruxes and Predictions, with each crux followed by the shared case and the two predictions. Keep the prompt formal, concise, and free of requests for citations, bibliographies, or named historical sources.
