Camp One, which I'll call the Linguistic-Entrenchment side: they hold that inductive validity depends on the entrenchment history of predicates in our actual inferential practices. "Grue" is inductively useless not because nature rejects it, but because it lacks the cross-contextual stability that earns a predicate inductive rights. For them, induction is partly a normative feature of how we've organized our reasoning framework, though they do not deny that the world cooperates.

Camp Two, the Real-Kind Structuralists: they hold that induction succeeds when it tracks objective modal structures—laws, essences, or kind-bound properties. "Grue" fails not mainly because of language habits, but because it does not correspond to any genuine kind or law. They may grant that entrenchment is a useful heuristic, but only because it tracks these deeper structures, not because it constitutes them.
