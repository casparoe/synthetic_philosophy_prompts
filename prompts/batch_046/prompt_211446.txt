Please explain the conceptual core of Popper's argument in The Poverty of Historicism that there can be no unconditional laws of historical development, because any social prediction that becomes known can alter the behaviour of the agents being predicted. I am particularly interested in whether the reflexivity objection genuinely distinguishes historical from natural-scientific explanation, or whether it collapses once we recognise analogous feedback loops in quantum measurement and in climate modelling, where the observer or model is coupled to the system observed. Focus on Popper's distinction between forecasting a specific historical event and identifying long-term trends, and examine whether that distinction remains stable if we allow that social agents can strategically respond to probabilistic, non-deterministic forecasts without those forecasts being causally efficacious in a physical sense. Is there a coherent conceptual space for a 'weak historicism' that accepts Popper's critique of deterministic prophecy but still holds that bounded, statistically robust patterns in history are law-like enough to underpin genuine explanation, or does any admission of historical regularity necessarily commit one to the unconditional predictability Popper rejects?
