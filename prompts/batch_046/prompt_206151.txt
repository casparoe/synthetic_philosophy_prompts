I am writing as a high school student preparing an independent study presentation on Wittgenstein's later philosophy. Rather than asking for a general summary, I want to apply his idea of family resemblance directly to a specific set of practices, including one I have constructed myself for the purpose of comparison.

Please rank the following four practices on a scale of 1 to 5 according to how strongly their internal unity is constituted by family resemblance rather than by an essential defining feature. Here, 1 indicates that the practice is almost certainly unified by one necessary and sufficient condition, and 5 indicates that it is unified only by a network of overlapping similarities with no common core.

The four practices are:
A. The practice of playing chess according to its standard rules;
B. The practice of democratic voting in large modern nation-states;
C. The practice of 'glorping,' which I define as a cooperative interaction in which each participant must simultaneously hold the belief that the other participant is being deceptive, with success determined not by any single rule but by a shifting cluster of contextual conditions such as timing, shared history, and mutual recognition;
D. The practice of constructing formal proofs using standard mathematical notation.

For each practice, state the numerical rating and provide a concise justification. Then, identify which two practices are structurally closest to one another in terms of how their unity is constituted, and explain your reasoning in a single paragraph. I do not need bibliographic references; I want your reasoning applied directly to these cases.
