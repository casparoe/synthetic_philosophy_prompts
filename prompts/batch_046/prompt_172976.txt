Please describe the most important implications of Goodman's grue paradox—where emeralds observed before some future time are equally well-described as green or as grue—for the design of feature-selection criteria in predictive modeling systems that learn from temporal sequences. Specifically: does the paradox demonstrate that any inductive bias toward temporal stability is ultimately arbitrary, or only that such biases must be explicitly justified? What would a feature-selection protocol have to look like if it genuinely refused all prior ordering of predicates? And if complete refusal is impossible, what minimal commitments about projectibility must even a supposedly neutral learning algorithm implicitly smuggle in?
