Please describe the most important implications of Schopenhauer's doctrine that music is not a representation of Platonic Ideas but an immediate objectification of the Will—particularly for two issues we have been debating in my advanced aesthetics seminar: first, whether this allows for genuinely non-representational emotional cognition, and second, how it bears on the aesthetic status of algorithmically generated soundscapes that possess formal coherence without any intentional compositional origin. In addressing these, I would like you to consider whether, on Schopenhauer's account, the absence of a composer's will means such soundscapes cannot express the Will at all, or whether the listener's own will becomes the relevant site of objectification, and what that shift implies for the ontology of musical meaning. Please keep the analysis conceptually rigorous and suited to developing into a seminar paper; I am not looking for bibliographies or citation lists, but for a precise unpacking of the philosophical consequences.
