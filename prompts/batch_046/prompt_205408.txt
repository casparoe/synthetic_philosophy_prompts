I was argueing with two friends—Maya and Jin—after a philosophy of mind discussion about pain asymbolia, where people feel pain but don't seem to mind it at all. Maya said this proves the badness of pain isn't in the sensation itself but in a seperate suffering-layer that usually accompanies it. She said if you can have full nociception with zero motivation to stop it, then pain is basically just a bodily signal like temperature, and only becomes bad when the affective reaction gets added on top.

Jin pushed back. He said asymbolics still pull away from flames, still seek medical care eventually, and still call the feeling 'painful' rather than 'warm.' He argued that shows the representational content—your body being damaged—is still carrying negative evaluation, just without the emotional panic. He said Maya is confusing motivational punch with normative content, and that asymbolia looks more like evaluative blindness than proof that normal people are adding something extra.

Maya came back with the mirror case: people who suffer intensely from stimuli that don't damage tissue at all, like in some neuropathic conditions. She said if suffering can float free from actual bodily harm, and bodily harm signals can float free from suffering, then they must be two independent things, and only the suffering part is actually bad. She also pointed out that asymbolics genuinely wouldn't pay anything to avoid the feeling, which means no harm is being done to them, so there's no hidden badness Jin is trying to save. Jin said that mirror case actually undermines her point: if suffering without damage is still bad, that just confirms suffering carries badness, but it doesn't show that pain-with-damage isn't also bad for a different reason—namely the damage representation. He said Maya assumes there's only one kind of badness in play. He also noted that asymbolics still protect themselves, which is awkward for her view: if pain were truly neutral they should have no reason to avoid damage, yet they do, suggesting the negative evaluation is still operating below conscious affect.

Can you go through their exchange round by round and say who's right about which specific point, and what if anything both parties are missing? Lead with the bottom line—who's right at each step and what's missing overall—then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question.
