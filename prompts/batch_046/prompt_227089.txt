I spent most of last night in a heated but genuinely confused argument with a friend about whether anyone actually experiences anything while asleep, or whether the whole phenomenon of dreaming is just a narrative we construct upon waking. Rather than continuing the circular debate, I want you to channel its tension into a sustained piece of satire. Write a deadpan parody of a terms-of-service agreement — roughly 700 words, formatted in numbered clauses — for a fictional subscription service named Oneiric Continuity Partners, which offers clients "phenomenological verification" and "continuity coverage" for their sleep states.

The target is the philosophical dispute between those who deny that dreams are experiences, in the tradition of Norman Malcolm’s skepticism about internal observation during sleep, and those who defend them, in the tradition of Daniel Dennett’s arguments including his cassette-style account of how dream narratives might be assembled at awakening. Your contract should exaggerate the real commitments of both positions rather than inventing straw men: include a clause that voids all coverage for any "experience claim" lacking contemporaneous waking testimony; a "Delayed Insertion Rider" that treats reported dream content as possibly loaded into consciousness at the moment of awakening rather than lived through; and a "No-Branching Continuity Clause" that quietly raises the problem of whether the waking self is numerically identical to the sleeper who allegedly underwent the experience, treating split-identity scenarios as coverage exclusions. The tone must remain strictly legalistic and deadpan — never winking — so that a non-philosopher reads it as an absurd but coherent bureaucratic document, while a specialist recognizes that every provision tracks an actual move in the debate. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or bibliographic references; the prompt should be fully answerable without web access. Keep the irony legible, the metaphysical machinery precise, and the internal consistency strict.
