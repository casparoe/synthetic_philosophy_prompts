I am preparing the interview questions for next week's colloquium podcast on self-locating belief, and my guest will be an unnamed philosopher who defends a position she calls centered coherence regarding the Sleeping Beauty problem. According to her view, Beauty ought upon awakening to assign a credence of one-third to the coin having landed heads, but she explicitly rejects the standard self-locating indifference principle as the source of that value; instead she derives it from what she terms a diachronic coherence constraint, which requires that an agent's credences over all centered worlds accessible within a single epistemic trajectory must stand in a specific weighted relation to her prior probabilities over the corresponding uncentered worlds, with the weights fixed by the structure of the awakening protocol itself. She maintains that this is a genuine update rule rather than a redescription of prior beliefs, and she extends the same constraint to the Absent-Minded Driver problem, arguing that the driver can preserve rational consistency across his centered perspectives without invoking causal decision theory, provided only that his probabilities match the coherence weights dictated by the logical structure of the scenario rather than by any physical continuity of memory.
