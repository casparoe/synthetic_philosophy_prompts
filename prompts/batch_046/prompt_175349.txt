I am taking a course on comparative defence policy and recently found myself in a rather confused discussion with friends about Sweden’s decision to suspend universal conscription in 2010 and then reactivate it in 2017, with the first new cohorts called up in 2018. Rather than treating this as a simple matter of military efficiency, I would like to analyse the episode through the lens of what I have been calling the embedded neutrality thesis: the claim that armed neutrality is credible only when defence obligations are distributed across the citizen body rather than concentrated in a professional caste, so that abandoning neutrality or entering foreign conflicts becomes politically costly because the population is directly implicated in the defence structure.

Please treat Sweden’s 2010 suspension and 2017 reinstatement as the case. First, map the episode onto the thesis: identify the relevant actors (the Riksdag, the professional officer corps, the conscript pool, and the neutral public), set out what the actual options and payoffs looked like at each decision point, and say what the thesis predicts should happen to Sweden’s neutrality credibility when conscription is paused versus reactivated. Then tell me honestly where the mapping breaks. The 2017 decision was explicitly justified by reference to Baltic security threats rather than to neutrality credibility; the 2018 system is highly selective—only about four per cent of each age cohort is enlisted—and gender-neutral, which differs sharply from the Cold War universal male model. Does the embedded neutrality thesis have anything to say about selective, gender-inclusive conscription, or does it implicitly depend on near-universal male service? And does the episode ultimately confirm the thesis—showing that Sweden felt compelled to re-embed defence obligations once the security environment shifted—or does it reveal that the thesis was never really about neutrality credibility but about managing threat perception?

I am particularly interested in whether there is a meaningful distinction between structural conscription (the legal obligation existing on paper) and performative conscription (the ritual of widespread service that binds civil society to the state), and whether Sweden’s post-2018 arrangement counts as the latter or has become merely a selective reserve mechanism. If the theory cannot speak to selective, gender-neutral systems, say so plainly rather than forcing it.
