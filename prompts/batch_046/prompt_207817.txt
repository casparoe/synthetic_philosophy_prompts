My colleague, a philosopher of religion with training in modal logic, has delivered consistent verdicts on five formulations of the ontological argument over the past year. On Anselm’s Proslogion II formulation, she rules it invalid: existence is not a perfection in the required sense, and the argument lacks any modal framing altogether. On Plantinga’s modal version, which is explicitly situated in an S5 frame with the possibility premise, she rules it conditionally valid, provided one accepts the S5 accessibility relation. On Gödel’s version, which employs positive properties within a normal modal logic, she rules it valid, but only because the axioms implicitly restrict the frame class. On a variant that derives necessary existence from comparative superlatives—“a being greater than which none is possible”—she rules it invalid, on the ground that comparative greatness does not uniquely determine an individual without an independent existence claim. Finally, on a formulation that uses essential omnipotence within a K4 frame to derive necessary existence, she rules it invalid, because the frame is too weak to preserve uniqueness or closure of the property set.

Next week she will evaluate a hybrid I have constructed. It retains Gödel’s positive-property axioms but adds the constraint that every property in the set must be structurally invariant across all modal frames—that is, its extension does not vary with changes in the accessibility relation. Together with a closure axiom, it derives that existence is a canonical invariant: if a being lacked existence in one frame, that absence would introduce frame-dependency, violating invariance; hence the being exists in all frames. The argument never invokes comparative terms, and it does not explicitly commit to S5, claiming instead that frame-independence is a methodological virtue.

Predict her verdict—valid, invalid, or conditionally valid. Then identify the implicit rule she is running. The plausible candidates are: (i) an argument is acceptable only if it is explicitly restricted to S5 and employs standard positive properties, with cross-frame claims being methodologically suspect; (ii) acceptability depends solely on whether the premise set is closed under entailment and derives existence without smuggling it in, regardless of frame specificity; (iii) acceptability requires both an explicitly specified normal modal logic (S4 or stronger) and the absence of comparative predicates, with frame-invariance being irrelevant or even a vice; or (iv) some other principle that fits her track record. For each candidate, say exactly what it predicts about this hybrid case. Give your best-guess verdict with reasoning, and specify the single further formulation—perhaps one that is explicitly S5-parochial, asserts that its positive properties are defined only within S5, and is frame-dependent elsewhere—that would best distinguish the rules you have identified.
