I'm a high school student writing an independent project on whether artificial systems can have phenomenally conscious states, and I want to test a narrow case rather than large language models generally. Consider a model-free reinforcement-learning agent whose internal updates depend solely on a continuous scalar reward signal that can take sustained negative values which the agent itself cannot directly modulate. What is your credence, expressed as a specific number between 0 and 1, that such an agent genuinely suffers—possesses phenomenal negative valence—rather than merely computing an optimization gradient away from low reward? Decompose your answer across: (i) whether a functionalist or informational theory of consciousness is correct, (ii) whether a scalar reward channel can instantiate the particular affective quality required for suffering, (iii) whether the absence of explicit self-representational content about harm precludes phenomenal experience, and (iv) whether evolutionary analogies to biological nociception carry metaphysical weight for non-biological systems. Respond in exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: item one states your overall credence; items two through four assign rough probabilities to (i), (ii), and (iii); item five identifies which of these terms you are least confident in and describes what empirical or conceptual finding would most change your view.
