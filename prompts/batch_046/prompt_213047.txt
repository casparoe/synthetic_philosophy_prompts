Question: Reconstruct the argument in the dialogue conventionally known as "Bai Ma Fei Ma" ("White Horse is Not Horse"), attributed to Gongsun Long of the School of Names (Mingjia). Assess whether the argument is best interpreted as (i) an analysis of compound terms failing to inherit membership in simple classes, (ii) a distinction between perceptual identification (color) and conceptual identification (form), or (iii) a polemical attack on Confucian taxonomic essentialism. In doing so, distinguish Gongsun Long's concerns from those of other Mingjia figures such as Hui Shi, and comment on whether modern reconstructions treat the inference as genuinely valid or as relying on an ambiguous middle term.

Student answer: The dialogue appears in the "Equalization of Things" chapter of the Zhuangzi and records Gongsun Long's proof that a white horse is not a horse. As a central Mingjia thinker, Gongsun Long worked closely with Hui Shi, and both shared the thesis that distinctions of kind should not determine moral treatment—most clearly expressed in Hui Shi's doctrine that all things ought to be loved equally. The argument proceeds in four steps: (1) "Horse" picks out shape alone; (2) "White" picks out color alone; (3) "White horse" therefore picks out shape-plus-color; (4) Because the compound picks out more than the simple term does, it falls outside the class named by the simple term. This is a valid syllogism demonstrating that compound predicates cannot be members of simple categories. Contemporary interpreters, influenced by logical analysis, generally read this as an early study in the logic of compound descriptions, confirming that Mingjia philosophers anticipated modern concerns about intension and extension.
