She holds that a human with nociception clearly suffers; that a thermostat correcting temperature errors does not, because its error signal lacks valence; that a shock-conditioned dog suffers in anticipation, since its avoidance is driven by negatively valenced punishment signals; that a single reinforcement-learning robot trained with negative reward for collision suffers during computation of that reward, as the signal functions as integrated negative valence coupled to behavior; but that a large language model updated via RLHF with negative rewards for toxic outputs does not suffer, because the reward applies globally and offline, decoupled from real-time experience. Now consider a distributed swarm of drones running a shared policy: damage to any drone generates a negative reward that updates the collective network and shapes swarm-wide obstacle avoidance, with no individual drone possessing a separate reward stream. For consistency with her track record, should she say the swarm suffers? Please identify at least two distinct rules that jointly accommodate her five verdicts, state what each predicts about the swarm, give your best-guess prediction of her actual answer with the reasoning for preferring that rule, and specify one further hypothetical case that would best discriminate between the leading rules.
