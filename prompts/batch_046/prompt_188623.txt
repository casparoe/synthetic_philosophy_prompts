I have been thinking about Rousseau’s critique of theatrical spectacle—particularly his conviction that representation corrupts natural sentiment, that the stage fosters vanity and social inequality, and that artificial display displaces genuine civic interaction—while discussing with friends whether any of these objections survive in an age of digital and algorithmic performance. I would like you to predict how Rousseau would evaluate four specific modern or near-future forms of spectacle that did not exist in his lifetime, but I want the answer anchored in his actual philosophical commitments rather than in generic cultural conservatism.

First, immersive virtual-reality theater in which every spectator wears an individualized headset, so that no two audience members share the same visual or narrative experience and collective response becomes impossible. Second, real-time algorithmic drama generated by predictive models that adjust plot, dialogue, and emotional intensity based on continuous biometric feedback from viewers, making the spectacle maximally engaging and never fixed in advance. Third, what I will call “parasocial streaming theater”: performers broadcasting unscripted, intimate interactions to geographically dispersed audiences who experience an illusion of direct personal friendship despite never sharing physical space. Fourth, a hypothetical technology I am calling “empathic projection,” in which neural interfaces allow spectators to directly feel simulated emotional states transmitted from performers, dissolving the boundary between observer and observed that Rousseau seemed to treat as essential for moral judgment.

For each form, please state whether Rousseau would regard it as a straightforward extension of the corruption he diagnosed, a qualitatively worse moral danger, or—perhaps surprisingly—something he might tolerate or even value, and identify the specific feature of his philosophy that drives each verdict. Then tell me what principle, if any, unifies these four judgments, and apply that principle to one case you have not discussed: a publicly funded community theater in which local citizens perform unscripted narratives drawn from their actual lives for neighbors in a shared physical space. I also ask that you say what would change your mind—whether a different interpretation of his distinction between natural and artificial sentiment, or empirical findings about digital media’s effects on social trust and equality, would cause you to revise one or more predictions, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
