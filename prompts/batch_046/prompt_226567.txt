Two people in my study group got stuck arguing about Gödel's modal ontological argument yesterday. Maya started by saying that Gödel's definition of a God-like being -- having every positive property -- hides a circularity, because the property 'having all positive properties' is itself either positive or not, and Gödel never gives an independent rule for sorting them, so the base set is just whatever we intuitively like. Jordan said that's wrong, Gödel treats positive as primitive, like Moore's good, and circularity only happens if you try to derive the base set inside the system, which he doesn't do. Maya then said the closure axiom is still trouble: if positive properties are closed under entailment, and necessary existence is positive, then being God-like entails necessary existence, which is okay, but uniqueness might also get entailed unless Gödel explicitly blocks it, and he never shows that block. Jordan replied that uniqueness isn't part of being God-like; two beings could both have all positive properties if exclusivity isn't included, so there's no hidden uniqueness claim. Maya came back with the modal collapse worry: in S5, if it's possible that some being has all positive properties, and those include being maximally great, then every world accesible from ours contains one, which is basically saying God exists in every possible world. Jordan said she's misreading accessibility: Gödel's axiom only says there's at least one world where the properties are instatiated, not that every world can reach it, so it doesn't spread everywhere. They turned to me and asked who was right step by step, and also said I should point out what they're both missing about how Gödel's version actually differs from Plantinga's modal argument, since they kept talking past each other on that. Can you adjudicate move by move and say what's missing?
