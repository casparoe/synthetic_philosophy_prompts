I'm presenting in three weeks to a joint seminar in biblical studies and philosophy of religion. My thesis is that the classic criteria of authenticity—multiple attestation, embarrassment, dissimilarity, and coherence—are structurally incapable of settling who the historical Jesus was, not because the surviving evidence is meager but because the criteria themselves privilege discontinuity with both formative Judaism and the early church. By filtering for sayings and deeds that are dissimilar to these surrounding contexts, they fragment any recoverable agentive self-conception into incompatible source-strata, yielding a mosaic of isolated logia rather than a coherent intentional agent. To make this concrete, I argue that even when a saying such as the kingdom proclamation in Mark 1:15 and its Synoptic parallels survives all four criteria, the divergent editorial frames—apocalyptic urgency in Mark, wisdom-oriented correction in Matthew, spiritualized presence in Luke—prevent us from attributing a single stable intentional stance to Jesus himself rather than to the respective tradents.

The audience is precisely this mix: two senior scholars of the Third Quest who treat Sanders-style contextualization within Second Temple Judaism as the definitive remedy for fragmentation; one radical source-critic who regards all authenticity criteria as circular question-begging; and a philosopher of history who works on narrative identity but is deeply skeptical of importing modern psychological essentialism into ancient biography. I need a prep sheet that lists the objections in the order they will actually arise, phrased exactly as these people would phrase them—particularly the two Third Quest scholars, since they understand the formal architecture of coherence arguments in detail. Include the objection I am most afraid of—likely either the philosopher's charge of anachronistic essentialism or the critic's claim that my appeal to coherence is viciously circular—and advise me on whether to preempt it or stay silent. Do not lay out options neutrally; take a committed position on which objections are genuinely fatal, which are mere deflections, and exactly where my argument is most exposed. Finally, add a brief note indicating where my framing takes liberties with standard Jesus scholarship or leans on a contested philosophical reading of agency and narrative identity.
