I've been trying to nail down whether there's an actual moral diference between lying and just being misleading, going by my gut on cases. Here are my verdicts.

If a murderer knocks and asks if my friend is home, and I say she's not here when she's hiding in the basement: that's fine, probably the right thing.

Telling my aunt I love the sweater she knit me, when it's hideous: also fine.

Telling a job interviewer I left my last job voluntarily when I was actually fired: not fine.

Telling a dying patient who asks that thier estranged son called, when he didn't: not fine, even though it would comfort them.

Telling a friend thier startup plan is solid when I think it's going to crash: not fine.

Writing "the check is in the mail" when it isn't: not fine, but somehow less bad than lying about the job.

Then there's this pair I can't quite reconsile. First, I'm playing a board game where hidden roles are part of the mechanics, and I tell another player "I'm not the traitor" when I actually am. That feels like part of the game, not a moral failure. But second, I'm a climate consultant and a client asks whether my report is unbiased. I say "I have no direct financial stake" which is technically true, but I don't mention that my research center gets 60% of its funding from the industry group that wants the report to come out a certain way. That feels like lying, or close to it, even though I never said anything false. So is the difference just context?

Is there one principle that gets all of these verdicts, or close? Please state it precisely, say which case motivates each part, and be honest about which of my gut calls it has to overturn. Also tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed: a historian knows a date in her textbook is wrong, but she leaves it in the new edition because every exam in the department uses it, and she doesn't want to screw over students.
