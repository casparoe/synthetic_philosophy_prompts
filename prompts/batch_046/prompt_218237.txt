As a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mind, I am trying to grasp the dispute over whether perceptual experience is corrected after the fact—an Orwellian revision of memory—or staged in real time through a Stalinesque construction of the present, with the color phi phenomenon serving as the canonical example. Write a short story about a technician calibrating an analog display device that renders her own visual consciousness in real time, including a color-phi sequence in which a moving point appears to change hue at a location it has not yet reached. Present the narrative in exactly five scenes, each rendered in one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion outside the sequence itself. In the third scene, introduce a previously unreported anomaly: a brief interval in which the technician's subjective duration collapses to zero, leaving a gap that memory later fills with continuous motion while present perception registers only discontinuity—so that the story asks, without answering, whether this gap is erased by an Orwellian rewrite or never experienced because of a Stalinesque omission.
