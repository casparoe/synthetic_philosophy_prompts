I'm in a phil/history diss and I'm trying to map my own gut reactions about judging dead figures, especially around that tired 'man of his time' line. Here are my verdicts, and they feel like they should cohere but maybe don't.

Jefferson writing equality while owning slaves: I think he's blameworthy for the slavery in a direct, personal way, but I don't want to say his political philosophy is just rotten root and branch. So condemn the practice, don't erase the ideas.

Churchill: I feel like 'man of his time' partly excuses the casual imperial racism - lots of people said worse and meant it - but it doesn't excuse the Bengal famine decisions, which seemed avoidable even with 1940s knowledge. So partial excuse, partial not.

Columbus: the 'man of his time' defence totally collapses for me because Las Casas was literally right there condemning the enslavement. At the same time I resist reducing him to pure villainy because the contact and exchange dimension complicates things, though maybe that's just me being soft.

Some days I think he should be in textbooks with full context; other days I think naming buildings after him is wrong no matter the medical payoff.

Is there a coherent principle behind all this - maybe something like 'blame tracks available moral alternatives weighted by institutional power and direct causation'? Or is that too neat? I want to know which verdict I have to drop to get coherence, especially Columbus vs Churchill, and what the principle predicts for someone who held progressive racial views for their era but still ran a factory using child labor because it was standard.
