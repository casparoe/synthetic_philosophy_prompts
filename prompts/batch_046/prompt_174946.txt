hey, i'm a retired biologist who got into philosophy late, and there's a disagreement in my study group i need help sorting out. two people got stuck on stoic control stuff. one guy, ex-engineer like me, said the modern stoic revival completely botches Epictetus's dichotomy because it treats control like a psychological dial you turn, when for Epictetus it was metaphysical: your prohairesis (the faculty of choice) is free but everything else is woven into fate by logos. he said without the determinist backdrop the whole distinction collapses into worry about what you can change, which is just common sense with greek branding.

the other woman, she studies ancient phil part-time, said he's being a purist. she pointed out that Epictetus lists body, property, opinions of others as outside our power, and modern therapy uses exactly that boundary to build resilience. she said you don't need to believe in cosmic determinism to draw the line between your judgments and external outcomes.

then he hit back: but in the ancient view even your judgments aren't controlled in the modern sense - they're expressions of your character which is shaped by prior causes. so the control in the dichotomy isn't agency like we mean it today, it's more like this is the only domain where moral evaluation applies. she said that sounds like hair-splitting.

he then said if you drop the metaphysics you also drop the reason externals are indiferent - it's not that they don't affect happiness, it's that they can't corrupt virtue because virtue is defined as correct use of impressions, which is exactly the free part. without that structure, telling someone don't worry about money is just toxic positivity.

she said no, the structure survives as functional psychology: your reaction is your domain, the event isn't. she added that revivalists now explicitly reject determinism but keep the practical core, which proves the metaphysics was scafolding, not load-bearing.

he said that's like saying you can keep a bridge after removing the pillars. then he added something more specific: modern CBT teaches you to reframe the initial impression itself, but for Epictetus the impression (phantasia) isn't up to you at all - only your assent (synkatathesis) is. so modern therapy puts control in the wrong place: it tries to control the stimulus interpretation rather than accepting the impression and controlling the assent.

she said that's too subtle for clinical practice and the functional boundary still holds.

they asked me who was right. i'm just a retired biologist so i don't want to take sides blindly. can you adjudicate point by point? who gets the dichotomy right, where are they both missing something, and does the modern version actually depend on hidden metaphysical commitments they think they've dropped?
