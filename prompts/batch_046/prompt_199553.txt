Jeffrey conditionalization is standardly introduced as the update rule for cases in which an agent receives uncertain evidence structured over a fixed partition: probabilities shift within the partition according to the evidential weights, and probabilities elsewhere are redistributed by conditioning. This standard presentation, however, presupposes that the partition is stable and that the agent's logical and conceptual framework is not itself in dispute.

Consider a more demanding family of cases in which the evidence is not only noisy in degree but ambiguous in structure — for example, when it bears on whether two previously distinct outcomes should be collapsed, whether a previously unrecognized event-type belongs in the space, or whether the underlying logic governing the domain is classical or otherwise. The relevant open questions then include: whether Jeffrey-style rules can be coherently extended to situations of partition-instability or framework-uncertainty (where the very algebra of events may shift); how such extensions interact with coherence constraints of the Dutch-book variety when the betting framework itself is not fully specified; and whether imprecise-probability versions of Jeffrey updating offer normative advantages over precise-probability versions when structural ambiguity is present.
