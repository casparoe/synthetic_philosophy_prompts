I have recently begun cataloguing how protest slogans shift as they move between social platforms, and this work has made the Darwinian theory of culture—memes as replicators subject to variation, selection, and retention—feel personally urgent rather than merely abstract. I am drafting an opening for a public lecture aimed at listeners who understand Dawkins's basic framework but have not studied memetics in depth, and I want to begin with a joke that actually engages the conceptual machinery rather than trading on the tired "ideas are viruses" trope.

The specific tension I have in mind is that cultural replicators, unlike genes, are routinely and intentionally modified by their hosts before transmission: a person can deliberately weaken a meme's contagiousness—say, by replacing a punchy falsehood with a nuanced qualification—thereby inheriting an acquired trait that reduces rather than enhances replication fitness, something biological replicators do not experience. Could you write a joke built around this contrast? Ideally, the punchline should come from the meme's own perspective—perhaps complaining to a gene about being ethically edited into obscurity—so that the humor depends on recognizing both the Lamarckian character of cultural transmission and the paradox of a replicator being deliberately made less adaptive by the very phenotype carrying it. Please keep the joke self-contained, without references to specific authors, texts, or technical jargon beyond the core replicator idea, so that it works for an audience with no specialized background beyond general familiarity with evolutionary metaphors.
