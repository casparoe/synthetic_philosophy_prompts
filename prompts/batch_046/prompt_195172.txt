I belong to a small research collective that pools grant funding and distributes it as monthly living stipends scaled to each member's need. This year we received an unexpected windfall. One colleague proposes that rather than increasing anyone's stipend, we should temporarily reduce the two best-funded members—who are just barely able to cover their dependents' costs—down to the collective baseline. He argues that our distribution principle should weight each person's welfare by how badly off they are, and that reducing disparity among the better-funded members increases the moral weight of their situation relative to the worst-off, even though the worst-off receive no additional resources and the reduced members are made absolutely worse off. A second colleague objects that this harms people without benefiting anyone, and that a genuine commitment to helping the worst off should either direct resources downward or leave the better-funded members untouched. I feel there is something philosophically confused in the first proposal, but I cannot say whether it misapplies a priority-based view or secretly relies on equality having independent value. Is his argument defensible? Please distinguish between what is settled in the relevant philosophical literature and what remains genuinely disputed among informed practitioners, and avoid presenting any contested interpretation as the consensus.
