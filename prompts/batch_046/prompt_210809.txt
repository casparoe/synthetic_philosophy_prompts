tbh I've been stuck on a twist of the ship of Theseus ever since my metaphyics module, and I'm not sure if it's got a proper name or if I'm just overthinking. Picture a university college: over two hundred years every building, tutor, and statute gets replaced, even the original charter is rewritten clause by clause. People still call it the same college, but not because of spatiotemporal continuity — there's none — and not because it serves the same purpose, since it went from training clergy to doing AI research. They say it survives because its 'external relations' never snapped: the same voting rights in the university senate, the same ceremonial obligations to the other colleges, the same legal debts passed on. Now compare a rock band where every member is replaced but they keep the setlist: usually we say that's a different band using the old name. So there's this pattern where identity hangs on continiuos relational ties outside the object, not on internal structure or function, but we apply it really inconsistently. Is there an established term for this kind of 'persistence through external relations'? How's it different from functionalism about institutions or from just saying 'it's socially constructed'? And if there's no name, what should we call it — and where does it collapse, like when those external relations get replaced one by one too? I'd love to know if I'm describing a real distinction or just muddying the waters.
