I'm writing this after a long row with my friend Dave in the pub last night about time-travel paradoxes. We were arguing about the classic grandfather setup, but we kept fixating on a specific varient that I can't find a clean name for. Normally you either vanish in a contradiction, or the universe stops you via one of those consistency-loop arguments, or you create a bootstrap loop where information has no origin. But we were picturing the case where you genuinely succeed: you stop your parents meeting, yet instead of popping out of existance you just remain there in 1955, fully intact, with all your memories and your time machine, except now there's literally no causal chain in that timeline that produced you. Dave insisted this is just the bootstrap paradox run backwards, but that feels wrong to me - in a bootstrap loop something is its own cause, whereas here you're not self-caused, you're just uncaused.

So is there an established term for this exact configuration? I'm not after the grandfather paradox itself, nor the consistency-loop case, nor the information-loop scenario. I mean specifically the persistence of an entity after its own causal origin has been erased, where the problem isn't that the universe can't allow it but that we now have a person sitting in the timeline with no generative history. Does philosophy of time have a name for that - maybe something like a 'causal orphan' or 'ontological drift'? And if not, what distinguishes it properly from those other three cases I mentioned? Is the contradiction meant to be logical, or is it more like an ontological weirdness that doesn't technically violate consistency?
