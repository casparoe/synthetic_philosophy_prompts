As a journalist covering science and philosophy, I am drafting a feature on whether panpsychism—if taken seriously—creates ethical obligations toward fundamental physical entities. Before I interview philosophers of physics and ethicists, I need a rigorous specification of what an ethically adequate theory of “pan-sentience” must deliver, not a survey of the literature. Write the desiderata: the phenomena, boundary conditions, and conceptual distinctions any such theory must accommodate to be taken seriously in public discourse.

Specifically, the list must include:

1. The separation of sentience (subjective experience) from suffering (negative valence) at the fundamental level—explain the physical or conceptual argument for why one might exist without the other, and why collapsing them undermines the ethical project.
2. An aggregation rule for macroscopic suffering from microscopic sentient units that avoids a fallacy of composition—state the rule and defend its non-arbitrariness.
3. Consistency with the empirical absence of detectable consciousness in simple physical systems (e.g., isolated particles, vacuum states)—clarify what “non-functional” or “hidden” sentience means here, and whether this move renders the claim unfalsifiable.
4. A principled criterion for moral consideration that avoids both full-blown pan-moralism (equal status for all entities) and anthropocentric exclusion—identify the criterion (integration, causal power, complexity gradient?) and explain why it is not ad hoc.
5. A response to the scaling problem: if every quantum interaction involves minute suffering, does this aggregate into a cosmically catastrophic problem of evil? State the mathematical or conceptual reply required.
6. Respect for physical constraints from quantum field theory and thermodynamics on how information or experience could be instantiated at the fundamental level—name the specific constraint and explain why violating it breaks physical plausibility.

For each desideratum, provide: the motivating case or consideration behind it; whether it is non-negotiable or camp-specific; and any conflict with another desideratum (particularly 3 versus 5, or 2 versus 4).

Then inject a speculative, niche framing: “thermal suffering gradients,” the idea that temperature differentials in quantum fields might map onto differential valence states. Treat this as an unestablished hypothesis and specify what additional desiderata it introduces—for example, whether high-entropy regions must be judged morally worse than low-entropy ones, and how that interacts with the second law of thermodynamics.

Next, briefly score four existing positions—micropsychism, cosmopsychism, Russellian monism paired with neutral monism, and integrated-information panpsychism—against the six desiderata, noting which each sacrifices and the key trade-off involved.

Finally, identify whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable given current physics or logic, and flag which single requirement excludes the most candidates.

Deliver the response in this structured form:

- First, the desiderata as a JSON object with named fields: “desideratum_number”, “requirement”, “motivation”, “status” (non-negotiable / camp-specific), “conflict_notes”, “thermal_gradient_implications”.
- Second, a comparison table with columns: “Position”, “Satisfies”, “Sacrifices”, “Key_Tradeoff”.
- Third, a 150-word journalist-style synthesis paragraph arguing why the list itself is a result—i.e., which requirements cannot be jointly satisfied and what that implies for the feature’s framing.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating conflicts rather than on exhaustive exposition of the four positions.
