I have been thinking about pain asymbolia, the condition in which people report that they feel pain yet show no distress and make no attempt to escape whatever is causing it. If the very same burning or stabbing quality can be present without any urge to withdraw or any sense that something is wrong, then it seems to me that the unpleasantness of pain must be a separate emotional overlay—perhaps just an evolved alarm reaction—rather than part of the sensation itself. From this, I have come to think that when philosophers claim pain is intrinsically bad, they are really only describing our instinctive aversion to it, as if we confuse the raw feeling with a kind of evolutionary panic. But I am unsure whether I am reading the condition correctly. Does pain asymbolia actually show that the badness of pain is purely instrumental—a warning signal we happen to dislike—or have I mistaken the absence of suffering for the absence of harm? And if the affective and behavioural layers can be removed by a lesion without altering the sensory experience, what does that tell us about whether pain is bad in itself, rather than merely bad as a signal?
