After a long, exhausting argument with a close friend about whether taking the simulation argument seriously should weaken our commitment to far-future longtermism, I want us to stop talking past each other. I represent the Dampening camp: I think that if Bostrom-style reasoning is sound, then indexical uncertainty about our position in a possible nesting of simulations collapses the expected causal reach of present actions, which should reduce the rational urgency of cosmological-scale intervention relative to nearer-term moral obligations. My friend represents the Preservation camp: she thinks the same formal reasoning leaves longtermist priorities intact, because moral weight is indexed to the causal structure we actually inhabit, and because the possibility of upward propagation through simulation layers actually amplifies the stakes of avoiding catastrophic outcomes.

Please draft the adversarial-collaboration document that the Dampening camp and the Preservation camp could jointly sign. Structure it as follows.

First, a numbered agreed-claims section. Include statements about the logical structure of the simulation disjunction, about what longtermism requires (for instance, that expected value can depend on causal reach and on the scale of affected populations), about the formal possibility of nested simulations with defined propagation rules, and about which empirical or conceptual results both sides accept as settled. State any decision-theoretic or probabilistic results in precise but non-bibliographic language.

Second, a disagreements table. Each row should name a residual dispute, state the Dampeners' position in wording they would accept, state the Preservationists' position in wording they would accept, and indicate whether the dispute is substantive or partly verbal.

Third, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would move at least one side. I suspect central cruxes include whether moral obligation is indexed to the base-reality causal graph or to the local simulation layer, and whether exponential suppression of influence through nesting layers is a feature of the formal model or an artifact of an unjustified prior. Include any others you discover.

Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete thought experiment, formal model, or piece of reasoning that both camps agree would bear on it. Then record, in advance, what each camp predicts that case or model will show. For example, if the crux concerns nested influence, present a simplified multi-layer simulation model with defined propagation rules, and state whether the Dampeners predict that the present-value of a far-future intervention asymptotes toward zero or remains bounded away from it, and what the Preservationists predict in the same terms.

The document should be formal and polished. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. Do not invoke specific scholars beyond the minimal framing above. Do not use verbatim quotations from any text unless you have fetched the exact passage from a source; otherwise paraphrase without quotation marks. If you lean on a specific formal result, state it in your own words.
