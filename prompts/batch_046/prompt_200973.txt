Exam prompt: Explain the sorites paradox for the predicate 'bald,' and evaluate whether supervaluationism or epistemicism offers the more satisfactory resolution. In your answer, clarify exactly how each view treats the principle of tolerance—that is, the claim that removing a single hair cannot turn a bald person into a non-bald one.

Student response: The sorites paradox arises because we accept that removing one hair from someone who is bald does not make them non-bald, yet iterating this reasoning eventually forces us to call a person with thousands of hairs bald. This reveals that 'bald' lacks sharp boundaries. Supervaluationists address this by denying that there is a single correct precisification: on different admissible sharpenings the cutoff falls in different places, so for borderline cases sentences like 'John is bald' are neither true nor false. They therefore reject bivalence only in these indeterminate zones. Epistemicists, by contrast, maintain that there is an exact hair count that marks the boundary; our problem is purely cognitive—we cannot know where the line is. Consequently, the epistemicist rejects the tolerance principle outright, since there is indeed a point where one hair makes the difference. The supervaluationist, however, preserves tolerance by ensuring that no admissible sharpening contains a sharp jump between adjacent hair counts. Overall, supervaluationism is preferable because it treats vagueness as a linguistic phenomenon rather than an epistemic limitation hiding a precise physical fact.
