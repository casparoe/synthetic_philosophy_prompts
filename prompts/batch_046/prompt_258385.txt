I hold the strong quining view that qualia are not merely unexplained or hidden but conceptually incoherent — a mental analog of the round square. The core argument, in the tradition of Dennett’s “Quining Qualia” and sharpened by Chase and Sanborn, is that qualia are defined by four jointly unsatisfiable conditions: they are intrinsic (non-relational), private (first-person exclusive), ineffable (not capturable by public language or functional description), and directly known with incorrigible certainty. Yet every actual or conceivable act of identifying, comparing, remembering, or reporting a qualitative state employs relational and functional criteria — behavioral discrimination, neural correlation, intersubjective calibration, or structural mapping across modalities. So the conditions that allow us to think about a quale are precisely the conditions that deny its defining properties. I extend this with two further claims. First, apparent “phenomenal contrast” cases — Mary’s room, inverted spectra, or intermodal synesthetic translation — do not rescue qualia, because they presuppose that qualitative character can be held constant across structural or informational changes; but that constancy can only be specified relationally (by cognitive role or structural isomorphism), not by an intrinsic nature. Second, appeals to “phenomenal indeterminacy” or vagueness fail, because physical properties have precise determinacy conditions, and attributing a sui generis vagueness to qualia merely adds an unexplained exception without specifying its content. The upshot is that there is nothing answering to the description of “intrinsic qualitative character” — only functional-dispositional states misdescribed as bearing an intrinsic glow.

What would genuinely count against this position? I want the map of its exposure, sorted by how decisive each item would be: decisive defeaters (observations, arguments, formal results, or conceptual distinctions that would force abandonment of the incoherence claim), merely awkward accommodations (things defenders could absorb by qualification), and evidence that is ambiguous or already disputed. Specifically: Is there a demonstration that qualitative states can be systematically tracked, translated, or interpolated across cognitive architectures without reduction to shared functional roles — for instance, a case where intermodal phenomenal mapping depends solely on geometric structure rather than on behavioral or neural convergence? Would a rigorous formal result showing that any system exhibiting the relevant discrimination patterns must instantiate non-functional, intrinsic properties undermine the relational-dependence argument? What about arguments from phenomenal sorites or boundary indeterminacy — do they strengthen incoherence by showing qualia lack determinacy conditions, or do they provide a positive model of how intrinsic properties can be vague? I also want an honest assessment of whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability — e.g., by treating every apparent qualitative report as a misdescription of functional state — and whether that insulation is a theoretical virtue or a defect for a view of this kind. Include what defenders of the incoherence thesis would themselves concede as counting against it, not only what critics say, and say whether you think the position risks anything real or has been qualified into safety. If you conclude it is well insulated, say whether that is a problem.
