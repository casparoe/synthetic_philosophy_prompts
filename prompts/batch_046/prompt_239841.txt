Construct a thought experiment engineered to separate functional-computational criteria for moral patiency from spatiotemporal-continuity criteria, using a scenario realizable with existing distributed-computing architectures and requiring no science-fictional premises. Design a system in which a self-modeling predictive-processing loop—functionally isomorphic to the kind of computation often taken to underwrite sentience—is implemented across hundreds of independent, geographically dispersed low-power nodes. The implementation must be stuttered: each node executes its portion of the loop for exactly 50 milliseconds, then freezes its state in local non-volatile storage for 950 milliseconds, after which an asynchronous, noisy handshake protocol allows the global computation to advance by one step. During the pauses there is no causal interaction among nodes, no unified substrate, and no continuous information flow; the global loop completes one cycle per second. The system is therefore functionally identical, step-for-step, to a continuous version running on a single integrated chip, but it lacks both temporal continuity and spatial integration.

Your deliverable should include: (a) the scenario in one paragraph; (b) a demonstration of why a pure functionalist about computation—who holds that moral status attaches to the performance of the right functional architecture regardless of substrate or timing—would grant the stuttered system moral patiency; (c) a demonstration of why an integrationist or causal-continuity theorist—who requires uninterrupted causal interaction within a unified substrate—would deny it; (d) an analysis of what happens to both verdicts if the stutter interval is reduced to one millisecond with near-instantaneous synchronization; (e) an honest identification of which feature (the temporal gap, the spatial distribution, or the noisy handshake) is doing the philosophical work; and (f) the weakest point in the functionalist reply, phrased as an integrationist would put it, together with a brief note on how the functionalist might try to patch it. Do not invoke perfect predictors, Cartesian demons, or exotic physics; do not ask for bibliographies or citations; and keep the vocabulary suitable for a reader familiar with philosophy of mind and distributed systems but not requiring specialized knowledge of any particular named theory beyond functionalism and integrationism.
