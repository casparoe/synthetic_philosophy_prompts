I am particularly interested in whether this mechanism can produce a durable equilibrium rather than a mere temporary truce.

Please state your actual credence, expressed as a precise number between 0 and 1, that a stable, non-coercive compromise can emerge between two fully incommensurable value systems—specifically, a deontological framework that treats personal dignity as lexically prior and non-fungible, and a consequentialist framework that permits dignity-tradeoffs for aggregate welfare—solely through reciprocal asymmetrical concessions, where each side yields ground on commitments of lower internal salience in exchange for protection of core commitments. Your answer should be decomposed into explicit sub-probabilities: (i) the prior that the incommensurability is metaphysically or structurally real rather than merely epistemic; (ii) the likelihood that asymmetrical concession can generate mutual gains without requiring a shared normative currency; (iii) the probability that the resulting equilibrium remains stable over time against strategic defection or interpretive drift by either party; and (iv) a residual term accounting for ambiguity in what counts as "stability." Provide one line of justification for each component, show how they combine into the final number, and then identify which sub-question is the weakest link in your reasoning. Finally, describe what kind of evidence—drawn perhaps from institutional design, treaty history, or formal modeling—would most substantially shift your overall credence.
