Write a villanelle on Zhuangzi's butterfly dream that uses the form's two refrains to enact the skeptical symmetry between Zhuang Zhou and the butterfly. One refrain should evoke the waking self who remembers dreaming; the other should evoke the dreamed creature who might now be dreaming the self. Rather than treating the scene as Cartesian doubt about deceptive senses, build the poem around the Zhuangzian idea of wuhua — the continuous transformation of things — so that the skepticism concerns the absence of any metaphysical boundary between dreamer and dreamed, self and other. Let the octave and tercets render both states with equal sensory detail, giving neither the status of a privileged frame. Place the volta near the start of the quatrain, where the distinction collapses instead of resolving. In the final couplet, bring the two refrains together without indicating which belongs to wakefulness, so the rhyme and repetition do the philosophical work of leaving ownership unsettled. Keep the line count to nineteen, maintain the villanelle's rhyme scheme, and make the concept accurate enough that a reader of the Zhuangzi recognizes the structure rather than just the anecdote.
