I am preparing a postgraduate seminar on the philosophy of biology and wish to examine a consistent line of reasoning. Consider the record of verdicts published by the philosopher Dr. Marcus Whitfield across four boundary cases.

First, he classified two riparian bird populations separated by an unbroken mountain range but capable of interbreeding in captivity as a single species, judging the barrier geographical rather than reproductive.

Second, he divided two morphologically identical beetle populations into distinct species upon demonstrating that their mating rituals were temporally incompatible, despite their occupation of adjacent habitats.

Third, he retained one species designation for a circumpolar ring of gull populations in which the terminal forms are reproductively isolated, maintaining that continuous interbreeding through intermediate populations preserves genealogical unity.

Fourth, he denied species status to a long-persisting clonal lineage of aquatic plants that had ecologically diverged and could no longer hybridise with its sexual progenitor, describing it as a stabilised clone lacking evolutionary independence.

Now consider this further case: a deep-sea microbial community that reproduces solely asexually, in which heritable metabolic variation is introduced vertically through bacteriophage transduction of pathway-encoding genes originally acquired horizontally from unrelated lineages; the community occupies a stable hydrothermal niche, exhibits no gene flow with surface ancestors, and has maintained this integrated viral-metabolic inheritance system for approximately two million years.

If one wishes to remain consistent with Whitfield's precedents, how should he rule on this community? Identify the closest analogue among the four cases, and determine whether his track record genuinely underdetermines the verdict. Should it prove underdetermined, set out the competing principles that yield opposing outcomes, specify the further case that would adjudicate between them, and state precisely what principle Whitfield would be endorsing with each possible decision.
