Write a one-page research proposal (approximately 800 words) for a philosophy workshop or small interdisciplinary grant on the ethics of space colonization. Focus on the specific case of terraforming a genuinely lifeless planetary body — for instance, a Mars-like world with no extant biosphere and no reasonable prospect of hidden life — and frame the central question as follows: does the ethical evaluation of altering such a world depend on whether we can coherently attribute to it a status of "potentiality-value," meaning a non-instrumental moral status derived not from present living inhabitants or ecosystems but from the body's capacity to become life-supporting? In other words, can potentiality-value occupy a stable middle ground between the preservationist view (that extraterrestrial environments should not be altered, by analogy to wilderness preservation) and the expansionist view (that settlement and transformation are justified by human survival or flourishing interests), or does this category inevitably collapse into one of the two?

State the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer: for example, whether recognizing potentiality-value generates an obligation to terraform (as fulfillment of a latent good), generates only a permission, or instead introduces novel constraints on how terraforming may proceed (such as preserving certain geological features that embody the potential). Explain why the existing preservationist and expansionist literatures do not settle this, noting that both typically assume the presence or absence of actual life rather than analyzing the moral significance of life-capable but life-absent systems. Propose a conceptual rather than empirical approach: analyze the conditions under which a non-living system can be said to have a "potential" that generates moral reasons without being reducible either to human preferences or to the rights of future inhabitants. Clarify that this is not a scientific proposal about planetary engineering feasibility, but a philosophical project about the ontology of value and the logic of moral reasons across temporal stages of a system.

Describe what progress would look like: a decision framework that classifies planetary bodies not merely by present biological content but by their potentiality-status, with corresponding ethical obligations, permissions, and constraints. Say what would count as failure — for instance, if the analysis shows that "potentiality-value" is either incoherent or fully reducible to instrumental or intrinsic value — and be honest about scope: this is a six-month conceptual project suitable for a philosophy fellowship or workshop, not a resolution of the full ethics of space colonization, and it will not produce empirical predictions but should yield a distinction testable in policy reasoning. Include a brief argument for why funding philosophical work on this framing is worthwhile for bodies actually deciding on exploration priorities, rather than on the more familiar question of whether humans have a right to colonize. Do not include a bibliography or citation list; refer to positions and arguments generically, and note that specific references will be filled in separately.
