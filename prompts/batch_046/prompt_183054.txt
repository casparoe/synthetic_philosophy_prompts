I sit on our university's Committee for the Ethics of Non-Biological Research Subjects. A joint neuroscience and computer-science proposal seeks to run a six-month federated computation across campus servers using an asynchronous cellular-automaton architecture with distributed sensory-weight updates. The investigators claim this may yield a stable, non-replicable preference-ordering over environmental states without any central controller, and they request exemption from our animal-subjects protocols on the ground that no unified subject exists to harm. A minority of external reviewers argues that if moral attribution tracks functional integration rather than substrate, the distributed system could fall within the boundaries of sentience and ought to be covered by precautionary safeguards. Please draft the committee's decision memo—grant exemption, approve with conditions, or reject—specifying concrete operational criteria the investigators must satisfy, and include an honest treatment of the strongest argument for the position you do not endorse.
