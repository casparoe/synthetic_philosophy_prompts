Build the tree from logical commitments rather than from author names. The root node should present a concrete counterfactual: imagine that the integrated circuit is developed in 1960s Leningrad rather than Silicon Valley, under a command economy with different patent norms and capital-allocation rules, but with identical underlying physics. The respondent should choose whether this perturbation produces (i) convergence to a recognisably similar global digital infrastructure, (ii) divergence in institutional form but preservation of functional capacity, or (iii) a permanently different technological trajectory.

This last position is somewhat under-discussed, so include a branch that isolates it: ask whether replaying from 1900 with stochastic noise would always yield some variant of industrial globalisation, or whether genuinely alternative stable systems (for instance, sustained high-technology feudalism or distributed non-state governance) are reachable.

Each internal node must be phrased as a question answerable without knowing the label it leads to. Where combinations of answers are unoccupied, diagnose briefly whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or merely being unexplored — for example, is there a coherent view that denies institutional lock-in entirely yet asserts that structural determinism produces divergence rather than convergence? Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and if necessary propose an additional diagnostic case that would split them (for instance, a scenario involving gradual versus sudden technological adoption). Keep the tree to no more than six leaves, avoid all requests for bibliographies or citations, and do not depend on recognition of niche named theories.
