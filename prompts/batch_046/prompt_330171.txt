Write a deadpan promotional brochure—approximately 650 words, with section headings and a sidebar—for a fictional consultancy, “Strawson & Partners,” that trains corporate leadership to operationalize the distinction between reactive attitudes and the objective attitude within human-resources governance. The brochure should maintain an earnest sales-pitch voice throughout, presenting a three-tier taxonomy of employees: “Full Participants” (toward whom managers deploy calibrated resentment, gratitude, and moral indignation); “Partial Participants” (receiving attenuated reactive attitudes); and “Objective Attitude Targets” (those subject to clinical-management protocols in which all reactive attitudes are suspended). The satire should turn on an accurate rendering of the underlying philosophy—specifically, that the objective attitude was conceived as an exceptional stance toward those outside the moral community, not as a bureaucratic instrument for excluding inconvenient staff—without ever stating this critique explicitly. Include an invented case study of a manager who replaced resentment toward a negligent supervisor with “strategic objective attitude,” inadvertently triggering a firm-wide reactive-attitude deficit, and a pricing sidebar for workshops entitled “Calibrating Your Resentment” and “The Ethics of Exemption.” A specialist should recognize that the distortion being satirized is a genuine feature of applying the framework instrumentally, and should find the reductio fair.
