There's this weird split in how research institutions handle human kinds. In genetics labs they talk about 'ancestry components' and continental clusters like they're just statistical patterns, but on the ethics-board paperwork and grant reports they still bucket people into old census race boxes—Black, Asian, Hispanic, etc.—as if those are the natural groups the science tracks. What view of race or ancestry does that setup presuppose? Is it assuming social race tracks biological ancestry well enough to be a proxy, or that the two are separate domains filed together for convenience? Reconstruct the implicit ontology, say whether the practice stays consistent if race is purely socially constructed, and tell me what'd have to change in the bureacracy if you replaced census boxes with pure genetic cluster labels everywhere.
