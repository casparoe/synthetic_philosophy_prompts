In a discussion of Hegel's master–slave dialectic, someone keeps repeating the line that the master depends on the slave, using it to claim that hierarchical institutions—like university grading or corporate management—are inherently self-undermining because the authority of the top depends on recognition from those below. Unpack the precise theses that sentence could express: causal interdependence, conceptual or normative dependence on subordinate recognition, a phenomenological claim about the master's self-certainty being mediated by the bondsman, or a historical claim about the development of freedom. For each, say whether it is a truism about social life, a substantive Hegelian thesis requiring the specific structure of desire and struggle, or false when mapped onto modern bureaucratic roles. Then identify which reading actually supports the argument about grading and management, and which ones are being silently imported to make the slogan sound like a knockdown objection.
