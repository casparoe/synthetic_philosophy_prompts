I am giving a seminar presentation next Thursday to our philosophy of economics workshop, and I want a Q&A prep sheet tailored to the people who will actually be in the room rather than a generic list of objections lifted from the decision-theory literature. Specifically, I claim that an agent at any given node is rationally required to take into account not only her current preferences but also the fact that her future reasoning dispositions are not yet fixed, because those very dispositions are being constituted by the reasoning she performs now; this breaks the induction step that requires treating future rationality as already determined. Please draft the eight or so objections I will actually receive, ordered from the most damaging to the least damaging, each phrased in the voice and vocabulary of the audience that would raise it.
