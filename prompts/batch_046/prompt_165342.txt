exam question: define narrow-sense heritability (h²) precisely and explain why a high heritability coefficient for working-memory capacity in a highly standardized twin study does not license the claim that environmental interventions will be ineffective in the general population. be explicit about population-level vs individual-level interpretations, and address what happens to the coefficient when environmental variance changes across settings.

student answer:
Narrow-sense heritability h² equals V_A divided by V_P, but since V_P is just V_A plus V_E, it tells us the share of spread in a population that comes from additive genes. its not about individuals—if h² is 0.75 you cant say 75% of my working memory is genetic. in the twin study they standardize the environment, so V_E is suppressed and h² looks big. in teh real world nutrition and schooling vary a lot, so V_E goes up and h² should drop proportionally, meaning genes explain less of the spread. but theres also gene-environment interaction: some genotypes only show their potential in enriched settings, so improving the environment can actually unlock genetic variance. this is consistent with Fishers 1930 fundamental theorem, which proves additive genetic variance is invariant across environments, so V_A stays constant and only the denominator changes. therefore exporting teh lab heritability number straight into policy is a category mistake, and environmental interventions still matter.
