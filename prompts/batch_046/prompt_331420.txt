I was discussing Dennett’s taxonomy of minds with a friend who is taking a graduate seminar on evolutionary epistemology, and she summarized the framework from her professor’s lecture notes in a way that struck me as too neat. According to her account, Darwinian creatures possess hardwired behavioral repertoires fixed by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures can reshape behavior through trial-and-error reinforcement, but only at actual bodily risk; Popperian creatures carry internal models that allow them to rehearse actions mentally, thereby avoiding fatal mistakes without ever leaving the armchair; and Gregorian creatures, equipped with language and cultural tools, can inherit fully formed cognitive solutions from others rather than generating them individually. She then insisted that Dennett presents these four categories as strict evolutionary stages—so that every Gregorian mind necessarily evolved through being a Popperian one first—and that the decisive feature of Gregorian cognition is something like direct access to the contents of other minds, functioning almost as a shared mental database. From this she concluded that the taxonomy effectively dissolves the frame problem for artificial intelligence: once a system is built to Gregorian specifications, it need not search through a combinatorial space of possibilities because it can simply download pre-vetted answers from the cultural repository.

I have a nagging feeling that this summary conflates design-space distinctions with a linear phylogenetic ladder, and that the characterization of language as a kind of mental telepathy or direct-content-sharing mechanism misrepresents what Dennett actually means by cultural transmission. I also doubt that Dennett believes Gregorian architecture eliminates internal search altogether, since that sounds more like a caricature of cultural learning than a solution to the frame problem. Could you audit this summary for me? Specifically, I would like to know which parts are faithful to Dennett’s actual view, which are common pedagogical simplifications, and which are subtle misrepresentations—particularly regarding whether the four kinds are historical stages versus alternative design strategies, what Gregorian cognition genuinely adds beyond Popperian simulation, whether language provides anything like “direct access” to others’ mental contents, and whether the taxonomy is meant to solve the frame problem in AI in the way my friend describes. If parts of her conclusion depend on the errors, please say so.
