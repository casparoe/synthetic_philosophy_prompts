In A Literary Review Kierkegaard contends that the modern press invents “the public”—a phantom audience that holds opinions without anyone being personally responsible for them—thereby replacing individual commitment with an anonymous, leveling abstraction. Given this diagnosis, there seems to be a tension with his own authorship: by writing through constructed personae—Johannes de Silentio, Anti-Climacus, and others—he deliberately withholds a single authoritative voice, multiplying perspectives rather than owning them. If the fundamental harm of the press is that it dissolves the speaker into collective noise, does Kierkegaard’s pseudonymous practice not reproduce that very dissolution within one consciousness, turning the author into a private press that generates his own phantom public? Or is there a structural feature of indirect communication—something about how these voices are meant to be appropriated individually—that distinguishes this multiplication from the leveling anonymity he condemns?
