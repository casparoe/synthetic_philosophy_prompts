From the perspective of a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, I have been trying to work out a coherent view of consciousness and agency by analogy to distributed systems architecture, and I would like an audit of whether my commitments actually cohere.

First, I reject the Cartesian theater: I do not think there is a central observer or single internal screen where experience is presented to a homunculus. Instead, I find the multiple drafts model compelling—conscious processing as parallel, distributed streams with no canonical version until one is selected or reported.

Second, I think the self should be understood as an emergent property of this architecture, comparable to how an event-sourced system reconstructs global state from an immutable distributed log rather than storing it in a single authoritative database. There is no central controller service, yet the mesh as a whole initiates actions and maintains continuity over time.

Third, I believe introspection gives me privileged, though fallible, first-person access to at least some mental states—more direct than any external observation could achieve—because being inside the distributed process provides access patterns unavailable to an outside monitor.

Fourth, I accept that if multiple drafts exist in parallel and only become determinate when probed by attention or linguistic report, then there may be no fact of the matter about what I consciously experienced prior to that probing event; the report may be constructing rather than revealing a pre-existing state.

Fifth, I want to maintain that such a distributed, emergent system can still bear moral accountability for its outputs, in roughly the way an organization without a single decision-maker can still be held responsible for system-level outcomes.

Please examine these five commitments for joint tenability. I am particularly concerned about tension between the first and third—if there is no central observer, who is the subject of privileged introspective access?—and between the second and fifth—if the self is purely emergent with no central controller, what grounds the kind of unified agency required for moral responsibility? I also suspect tension between the third and fourth: if introspection is just another probing mechanism that selects one draft, then it may not be revealing a pre-existing mental state at all, which threatens the idea that it gives genuine first-person access rather than manufacturing the state it reports. For each genuinely tense pair, rank the severity, identify the cheapest revision that would resolve it, and say what that revision costs me. Also identify any pairs that only appear tense and dissolve on inspection. Finally, tell me which single commitment I should abandon if I want to preserve the core distributed-systems intuition behind the first two while cleaning up the greatest number of inconsistencies across the whole set.
