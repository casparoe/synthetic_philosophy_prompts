As part of my coursework in jurisprudence and the philosophy of law, I have been studying the Hart–Devlin exchange on legal moralism. I would like you to draft the joint statement that a rigorous, good-faith adversarial collaboration between two camps might produce: (1) liberal anti-legal-moralists in the tradition of H. L. A. Hart, who maintain that the criminal law should not enforce private morality in the absence of tangible harm to others; and (2) social-cohesion legal moralists in the tradition of Patrick Devlin, who maintain that a community is entitled to use law to defend its shared moral fabric against acts it regards as immoral, even when those acts are victimless.

The document should be structured as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims—covering at least the distinction between harm and offense, the conditions under which a society might experience moral disintegration, the empirical limits of state competence in shaping private conduct, and what kinds of evidence could in principle distinguish social collapse from ordinary moral change. Second, a table or clearly delineated section stating the precise residual disagreements, with each position phrased in wording that its own camp would accept; in particular, address whether “immorality” functions as an independent legislative category and whether the preservation of shared norms is constitutive of social identity or merely instrumentally valuable. Third, identify the cruxes—those sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I take the central cruxes to be (a) whether shared morality is constitutive of a society’s identity rather than only instrumentally useful to it, and (b) whether legal prohibition of victimless immoral conduct has demonstrable causal effects on long-run social cohesion. For each crux, specify one concrete case, empirical pattern, or institutional example that both camps would agree bears on it, and record each side’s explicit prediction about what that evidence shows.

In addition, please inject and address the following framing, which is not part of the standard textbook presentation: replace the concept of “society” with that of a “moral ecology,” treating moral norms as functioning analogously to ecological niches and legal intervention as either stabilizing or disrupting these niches in ways comparable to ecological management. Both camps should state, in neutral wording, whether this framing is genuinely substantive—adding explanatory or normative content—or merely metaphorical, and whether adopting it changes the structure of their disagreement.

Finally, please indicate how confident you are in the drafting of this joint statement, and single out the specific point about which you are least sure—particularly your assessment of whether the “moral ecology” framing genuinely advances the debate or instead obscures it. Write in a formal, polished style appropriate for a seminar submission. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
