(1) When I think that Socrates is mortal, there's one unified thing I'm thinking — not just two mental files floating about separately.

(3) But if that glue is itself part of what I'm thinking, then I need a further glue to connect the first glue to Socrates, and then another, ad infinitum. So Bradley's regress starts.

(10) If there's no binding, then propositions aren't unified complexes; they're just unordered sets of mental files.
(11) But then false belief is impossible (there's no specific combination that fails to match reality) and logical inference is impossible (there's no structure to preserve across steps).
(12) So the very idea that propositions are unified wholes seems to destroy structured thought. Where do I bail?
