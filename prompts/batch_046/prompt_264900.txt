Write a double-sestina — two interwoven sestinas, one voiced like a brittle 1950s digital machine and one like von Neumanns brain — on the claim that neural noise isnt a bug but the actual language of computation. Machine end-words: gate, pulse, zero, one, wire, clock. Brain end-words: noise, drift, threshold, fire, synapse, heat. Each stanza should interleave one rotated line from each sestina so the forms literally clash. The envoi needs a single shared line that pairs one word from each set, showing von Neumanns real point: not that brains are computers, but that both are strange hybrids that fail in opposite directions — digital parts shattering when a gate misfires, analog tissue computing precisely through drift and heat. Be specific about the philosophy: statistical logic, unreliable components tolerated in bulk, a hybrid language neither fully discrete nor continuous. Someone familiar with his brain-computer writings should recognise it; everyone else should just get a good weird poem. No bibliography, just the verses.
