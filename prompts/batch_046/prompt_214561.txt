I am refining the framework for a graduate seminar on belief under adversarial epistemic conditions, and I want the specification for an adequate epistemology of conspiracy theories before we turn to evaluating particular accounts. Rather than a comparative scorecard, please provide the full list of desiderata—the conditions a theory must satisfy to count as adequate for adjudicating conspiracy beliefs in contexts where opacity is sometimes manufactured, sometimes incidental, and sometimes strategic.

For each requirement, state the motivating case or conceptual distinction that makes it necessary, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. The theory must handle the following phenomena and distinctions without collapsing them: documented institutional conspiracies versus unverified intentional-cabal theories; structural or institutional-drift explanations that yield conspiracy-like outcomes without centralized intent; and what I will term engineered pseudo-conspiracies—situations in which actors deliberately construct the appearance of a hidden plot, complete with fabricated evidence and false whistleblowers, in order to discredit legitimate institutional suspicion or capture epistemic markets, where the conspiracy is real as a social artifact but false as an explanatory target. The theory must also preserve the distinction between having some conspiratorial explanatory weight and having decisive weight, and between epistemic vice (e.g., motivated reasoning, hermeneutical closure) and rational belief revision under conditions of genuine secrecy.

In addition, specify which general results from social epistemology—such as the defeasibility but non-arbitrary dismissibility of expert testimony, or the social distribution of evidential access—the theory must remain consistent with. Then indicate whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given standard constraints on rational belief (for instance, whether requiring both maximal openness to hidden-agency explanations and strict parsimony is incoherent). Finally, identify the single desideratum that, in your judgment, eliminates the largest share of currently prominent theories, and explain briefly why. A table is acceptable, but keep the emphasis on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on exhaustive scoring of named views.
