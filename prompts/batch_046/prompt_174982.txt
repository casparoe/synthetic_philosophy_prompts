For each requirement, provide the motivating conceptual distinction or case (for instance, the separation between deliberative sovereignty and mere choice sovereignty, the need to accommodate reverse nudging in which agents pre-commit to counter-architectures, consistency with behavioral-economics findings on cognitive fluency without contradiction, and the difference between restructuring first-order preferences versus reshaping second-order endorsement). Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect camp-specific preferences, such as welfare-maximization versus autonomy-preservation priorities. Then determine whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable in light of standard anti-paternalist objections—including the knowledge problem and the value of autonomous error-making—and identify the single requirement that most sharply excludes the conventional libertarian-paternalism framework.
