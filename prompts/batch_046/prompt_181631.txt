When a subject's verbal report conflicts with third-person measurements—for instance, a person claims vivid awareness of a stimulus that produces no corresponding neural signature—the discrepancy is resolved by reinterpreting the report rather than questioning the measurement. Is it compatible with the view that suffering is a real mental state, or does it implicitly reduce suffering reports to narrative constructions? Give a committed answer that takes a definite position on which commitments are essential and which are only incidental, and say whether the practice could survive if those essential commitments were replaced by rival ones.
