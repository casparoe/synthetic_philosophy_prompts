For a workshop on social ontology, I want a consistency check before presenting. My commitments are: (1) generational labels are not natural kinds—there is no biological essence to being a Millennial or Gen Z member; (2) cohort effects are real causal patterns produced by shared exposure to technological and economic conditions during a formative window; (3) generational identity is constituted by what I call “indexical cohort membership”: being assigned to a birth-range category and socially recognized within it, with no deeper metaphysical essence; (4) despite this thin ontology, generational categories can ground claims in intergenerational justice, such as climate debt, because the cohort functions as a collective bearer of differential responsibility; (5) “Boomer,” “Millennial,” and “Gen Z” are genuine predictive kinds in sociology, not arbitrary tags, because those shared formative conditions yield stable dispositional patterns. Do these hang together? In particular, does (3) undermine the substantial explanatory or moral weight I assign in (4) and (5)? And does (2) implicitly commit me to a “formative-window essentialism” that conflicts with (1) and (3)? Please audit the tensions, rank them by severity, identify which pairs are only apparently in conflict, and specify the cheapest revision that preserves the core of (2) and (4).
