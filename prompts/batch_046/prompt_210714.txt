Question: In Frankfurt's classic counterexample to the principle of alternative possibilities, Jones decides to do X and Black is standing by ready to force Jones if he decides otherwise, but Black never actually intervenes because Jones decides on his own. Explain Frankfurt's argument that Jones is still morally responsible despite lacking alternative possibilities, and then discuss one major objection.

Student answer:
Frankfurt shows that Jones is responsible because Black never actually steps in, so there's no real coercion in the actual timeline. The principle of alternative possibilities says you need to be able to do otherwise to be responsible, but Frankfurt argues that what's important is that the decision came from Jones's own reasoning. Even if he couldn't have done otherwise, he's still the source of the action.

One big objection is the flicker of freedom. Critics say Jones still had an alternative possibility in the form of showing a prior sign that Black was watching, so PAP isn't really violated. Frankfurt answers this by saying that even that flicker is enough to ground responsibility, and he ties this to his hierachical model where your second-order volitions line up with your first-order choices. So PAP survives in a weaker form but Frankfurt's main point about determinism still holds.
