Is there a name for this pattern? Someone stops eating meat and first says they just don't like the texture or that their roommate went vegan. A year later they're laying out a pretty tight suffering-capacity argument, talking like that logic was always the engine behind the choice. Or someone returns a lost wallet and first admits they were scared of security cameras, but months later frame it as respect for property rights they always held. The philosophical reason isn't fake—it's genuinely defensible—but it clearly got bolted on after the behavior was fixed, and now they treat that bolt-on as the load-baring wall. It's not quite rationalization since the reason isn't invented, and it's not simple hypocrisy because if you press them they might admit the timeline. Is there an established term for this kind of retroactive moral grounding, especially in debates about whether acting for the right reasons actually matters? If not, can you coin one and draw its edges—what separates it from ordinary self-deception and from the standard philosophers' story about people who start with bad motives and gradually adopt good ones?
