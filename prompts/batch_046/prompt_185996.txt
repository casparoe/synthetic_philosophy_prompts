I'm moderating a colloquium interview with a philosopher who defends a revision of the principle of alternative possibilities she terms "deliberative modal openness." She grants that Frankfurt-style cases defeat the classical formulation—moral responsibility does not require a physically open alternate action—but contends that they leave intact a narrower condition: responsible agency requires that the agent's actual practical-reasoning process be modally sensitive to at least one genuinely open deliberative pathway, even when the behavioral outcome is causally determined. She maintains that locating openness in reasoning architecture, rather than in available actions, evades the flicker-of-freedom objection, because the relevant alternative is a live deliberative option rather than an unexercised physical movement. She further applies this framework to artificial decision systems, arguing that deterministic architectures lacking internal deliberative openness merit only derivative, functional responsibility, whereas systems with the relevant reasoning-structure openness could bear non-derivative responsibility regardless of substrate.

Please draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that press this position at its joints. I want at least one question probing how her reasoning-structure condition differs in substance from a sophisticated flicker defense; one asking what conceptual or empirical considerations could distinguish her modal-sensitivity requirement from mere counterfactual robustness in an agent's belief-formation processes; one addressing whether her view risks over-generating responsibility in deterministic systems that successfully simulate deliberative architecture; and one exploring whether her distinction between derivative and non-derivative responsibility for artificial agents can be sustained without tacitly importing a metaphysical commitment to consciousness, original sourcehood, or biological substrate. For each question, provide a brief sketch of the answer she is most likely to give and a sharper follow-up tailored to that reply.

In framing these questions, separate what is broadly settled in the literature—such as the logical structure of Frankfurt's original counterexample and the standard formulation of PAP—from what remains genuinely contested, including whether reasoning-level openness can bear the normative weight she assigns it and whether substrate-independent deliberative architecture is sufficient for non-derivative agency. Do not present her "deliberative modal openness" revision as if it represented the consensus position on Frankfurt cases.
