Write a short dialogue between two philosophers of action, Dr. Aris and Dr. Bede, debating whether sphexishness—the mechanical rigidity exhibited by the sphex wasp, which repeats an invariant behavioral sequence even when circumstances have changed—undermines free will or instead clarifies its preconditions.

Aris should argue that genuine flexibility requires the capacity to interrupt and restructure one's own behavioral algorithms, and that sphexishness reveals apparently flexible action as merely nested determinism: the wasp lacks freedom, and human behavior differs only in the complexity of the nesting, not in kind.

Bede should argue that sphexishness marks a real ontological boundary—not between determinism and freedom, but between rigidity and adaptability—and that free will should be understood not as an escape from causation but as a specific mode of causal openness, one the wasp lacks precisely because it cannot modulate its own rigidity parameters in response to feedback.

Have them respond directly to each other's points rather than talk past one another. To push the debate into less familiar territory, introduce a hypothetical "meta-sphex" organism: its first-order behaviors are rigidly sequenced, but it possesses a second-order mechanism that can adjust the rigidity thresholds of those sequences—though only within bounds that are themselves fixed and non-negotiable. They should dispute whether this second-order modulation counts as genuine flexibility or merely higher-order sphexishness, and whether phenomenological awareness of the modulation changes the metaphysical or normative assessment.

The dialogue should be balanced, with neither character a strawman, and should conclude without forced resolution, clarifying whether the remaining disagreement is substantive—about causation, responsibility, or the nature of adaptability—or largely terminological.
