I'm a career counselor at a small college center and I keep seeing grad students who say they feel empty even though they're crushing every milestone—good grades, internships, the works. I read Moritz Schlick's essay on the meaning of life, where he splits activities into ones done for some outside purpose and ones done playfully, just for their own sake, and claims only the second kind actually carry meaning. I want to turn that into a practical intake procedue I can run in a 45-minute session with a burnt-out student who lists their weekly activities.

Here's the twist: I also want the protocol to catch two hybrid cases I think Schlick misses. First, "borrowed play," where something looks playful—like playing in a jazz band—but is really just resume-building for music school. Second, "stolen play," where an activity that used to be playful got forced into a purpose-frame by parents or bosses, so the student now experiences it as labor even though it should be in the play column. I need a step-by-step flowchart with specific test questions at each gate, worked examples of both hidden-meaning and confirmed-hollowness cases (including one where the student won't like the result), and clear flags for where my judgment as counselor can't be replaced by the checklist. Don't tell me to read more Schlick—just build the reusable procedure.
