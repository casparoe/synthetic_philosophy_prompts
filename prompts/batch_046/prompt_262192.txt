The mediators need a reusable procedue they can actually run in about fifteen minutes, grounded in whatever actually makes promises binding rather than just hurt feelings or social pressure.

I want a step-by-step checklist that asks the right questions in order — for instance, whether the promise was scaffolded by an institutional role or a pre-commitment device that made reliance reasonable, whether the promisee actually changed their position in a way that creates moral residue, and whether honoring the promise now would require treating someone as a mere instrument — with clear stops for "no obligation" and "yes, still bound," plus explicit flags for where it's a values judgment and not a fact. Then run it on three cases: (1) a student who promised to co-sign a lease and then got a much better housing offer; (2) a lab mentor who promised shared authorship on a paper but the research turned out to involve fabricated data, so they want out; and (3) a case where the procedue says the promise is still binding but the mediator is going to really hate telling someone they have to follow through. Please flag where the procedue can't settle things, keep everything in American English, and don't include citations or reading lists — I just need the tool, not a bibliography. Thanks!
