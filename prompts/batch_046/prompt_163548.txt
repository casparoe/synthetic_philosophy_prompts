Following a recent discovery that undermined a long-held belief about my own ancestry—one I had never questioned and upon which I had organized significant personal and ethical commitments—I have been trying to understand Wittgenstein’s hinge epistemology and determine whether that disrupted belief was genuinely a hinge or merely a strong conviction. In working through the implications, however, I constructed an argument that seems to show the view collapses into an untenable relativism, and I cannot locate the precise error.

1. A hinge proposition is not justified by evidence but stands fast as part of the framework within which justification operates.
2. Whether a proposition stands fast is determined not by its content but by its functional role: it is embedded in my form of life and organizes my practical and linguistic commitments.
3. Therefore, if I systematically organize my inquiries, actions, and interpretations around some proposition P, then P acquires the functional role of a hinge for me.
4. Once P is a hinge, counter-evidence against P can only be assessed within the framework that already presupposes P; hence no evidence could rationally dislodge it.
5. Consequently, for any proposition whatsoever—no matter how arbitrary—if I commit to organizing my life around it, it becomes rationally immune to revision.
6. This implies that someone who organizes all inquiry around a manifestly false claim, such as that the Earth is flat, has made that claim a hinge and is therefore rationally justified in dismissing contradictory satellite data.
7. But it is plainly false that flat-earthers are rationally justified in dismissing such evidence.
8. So hinge epistemology must either deny that arbitrary propositions can become hinges, or admit that rational justification is purely internal to one’s chosen framework.
9. The first option requires an objective, theory-independent criterion for legitimate hinges, which Wittgenstein explicitly rejects; the second option collapses into a radical epistemic relativism that makes rational critique impossible.
10. Therefore, either hinge epistemology licenses dogmatism or it abandons its own anti-theoretical commitments.

Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost—especially for my attempt to understand whether my disrupted ancestral belief was a hinge rather than a revisable conviction? I am particularly concerned not to misrepresent the role of form of life in step 2, yet I also do not see how to block the move to step 5 without introducing the very kind of external rational standard that the view aims to avoid.
