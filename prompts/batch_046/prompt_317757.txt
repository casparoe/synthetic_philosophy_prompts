I will defend at next week's metaphysics seminar a restricted answer to the special composition question: concrete parts compose a whole if and only if they are bound into a single self-regulating causal loop that maintains a stable functional profile across perturbation. On this view, living organisms and some engineered artifacts compose, but arbitrary mereological sums—such as a scattered collection comprising the moon and my left shoe—do not. I reject both unrestricted universalism and mereological nihilism, and I claim that this middle position is ontologically parsimonious without collapsing into either extreme.

The audience is a metaphysics workshop containing one committed mereological nihilist who accepts only simples, one unrestricted universalist, two philosophers of science skeptical of any composition relation that is not reducible to physics, one Kantian who regards special composition as a transcendental illusion of reason, and several graduate students recently convinced by the familiar material-beings argument against unrestricted composition. They value scientific respectability, logical precision, and parsimony, and they are quick to charge that restricted views smuggle in mysterious relations.
