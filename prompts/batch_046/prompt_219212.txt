Rank the following four frameworks for assigning biological functions to exapted traits—structures originally selected for one adaptive role that are later co-opted for another—in terms of how plausibly they determine the current function of avian flight feathers, given that feathers initially evolved for thermoregulation. The frameworks are: (A) a strict etiological account that fixes function exclusively by original selection history; (B) a pure causal-role account that fixes function exclusively by present contribution to fitness; (C) Millikan's proper-function account, which requires a reproductive history of the trait-type for the relevant effect; and (D) a hybrid 'developmental-entrenchment' account, which assigns the new function only if the co-opted role becomes developmentally canalized while the ancestral role is fully redundant, leaving the trait with an ambiguous or dual designation during transitional phases. Assign ranks 1 (most plausible) through 4 (least plausible), and justify your ordering. In particular, explain whether framework D genuinely resolves the ambiguity of transitional stages or merely postpones it.
