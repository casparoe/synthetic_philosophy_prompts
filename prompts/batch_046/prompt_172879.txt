Write a deadpan bureaucratic FAQ of about 600 words in the voice of a government administrative manual for the fictional Office of Pairwise Moral Accounting, which enforces Scanlonian contractualism as literal regulatory law. The office operates by “moral pixelation”: every harm or benefit must be registered one-to-one between a specific individual and a specific principle, and may never be summed, averaged, or aggregated across populations to justify overriding even one reasonable objection. Exaggerate the real feature of Scanlon’s view—that justification depends on whether each affected person could reasonably reject a principle—while pushing it to a deadpan reductio on the aggregation problem: collective catastrophes become administratively invisible because no pairwise entry registers the total, and beneficial collective action is paralyzed because any single objection blocks the principle. Include at least three FAQ entries, for example on evaluating a principle that saves thousands but burdens one person, on defining moral pixelation, and on whether class-action objections are permitted. Stay fully in character: never wink, never state the critique outright, never slip into consequentialist reasoning, and treat moral pixelation as a real doctrine within the world. A reader familiar with contractualism should recognize the logic being tested and feel the tension between individual standing and large-scale collective action. After the FAQ, state how confident you are in its philosophical accuracy and identify the single point you are least sure about. End with a one-sentence summary as the very last line.
