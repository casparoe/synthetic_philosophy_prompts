A derives that action X is optimal and wants to hold the self-trust belief "if i believe X is optimal then X is optimal". ask them to show why this doesn't just collapse into standard Bayesian updating about checker error -- specifically, why the modal fixed-point structure of Löb's theorem still bites even with bounded omniscience, and where exactly the rescue fails.

the model answer should hit five seperate things: (1) the distinction between probabilistic credence and modal belief in self-trust, (2) why epsilon-error doesn't break the fixed-point circularity, (3) the precise step where bounded omniscience fails to save the agent, (4) what this means for actual AI architectures using approximate proof verification, and (5) the common mistake of treating this like an induction or empirical self-correction problem. about 600 words.

marking scheme: 20 marks total, 4 each, with a one-line note on the typical failure mode for every point and how candidates usually recieve partial credit. keep it closed-book -- no citation requests, no reading lists -- since i want to see reasoning not lookup. write it from the perspective of a long-time rationalist reader who knows the difference between parroting Yudkowsky and actually understanding the modal logic.
