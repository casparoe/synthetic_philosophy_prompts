I am trying to understand a passage in Kant’s Metaphysics of Morals, in the Doctrine of Virtue, where he discusses obligations regarding animals. He denies that we have direct duties to non-rational creatures, arguing that only beings capable of rational autonomy are ends in themselves. Yet he also maintains that cruelty to animals is wrong, not because it violates any right of the animal, but because it hardens the human agent and thereby undermines the disposition to fulfill duties toward other people. He treats animals as lacking the kind of self-consciousness that would ground intrinsic moral status.

What confuses me is how to interpret the logical structure of this position when mapped onto distinctions that are standard in contemporary moral philosophy but foreign to Kant’s own vocabulary: the separation of moral patienthood from moral agency, and within that, the difference between threshold and scalar accounts of moral status. If Kant holds a strict threshold view—where rational autonomy is a binary gate for direct moral concern—then animals lie entirely outside that gate, and the prohibition on cruelty looks purely instrumental, valued only as a causal safeguard for human moral character. But if we read his argument as allowing a scalar or relational form of value, then the claim that cruelty to animals hardens us might presuppose that animal suffering already carries some non-instrumental weight; otherwise the psychological link to human harm would not by itself generate a moral reason.

Could you help me parse the internal logic of the passage? Specifically, does the indirect-duty framework consistently uphold a binary threshold, or does it implicitly require that animals possess a minimal, mediated value that Kant officially denies? Is the hardening claim best taken as an empirical psychological observation, or as a structural feature of a relational value theory in which our obligations to rational beings are partly constituted by how we treat non-rational ones? I want to understand whether the tension is genuine or dissolves once we distinguish carefully between purely instrumental value and value that is mediated through duties to humanity.
