For an upper-level seminar on transitional justice, draft a 60-minute, 25-mark final exam essay question—together with a model answer and a marking scheme—centered on a hypothetical mechanism called the “silent archive protocol.” Under this protocol, a post-conflict state grants perpetrators individual legal immunity on the condition that they permanently destroy all private documentation of their crimes and are permanently prohibited by law from speaking about them in any forum; victims receive private, sealed state acknowledgment and compensation, but there is no public testimony, no published record of individual culpability, and no institutional naming of perpetrators. The question should ask students to evaluate whether this protocol can be justified as transitional justice, requiring them to compare its moral and practical trade-offs with the actual designs of South Africa’s Truth and Reconciliation Commission (individual amnesty contingent on full public disclosure) and Argentina’s CONADEP / Nunca Más process (truth-gathering without amnesty, followed by criminal trials). It must do more than elicit summary: students should identify the normative goods transitional justice aims to deliver—such as public acknowledgment, victim dignity, institutional reform, deterrence, and social trust—argue whether the protocol secures, undermines, or transforms each good, and defend a clear overall position against at least one serious objection. Provide a model answer that executes this argument. Then provide a marking scheme built around five must-hit analytical moves—accurate characterization of the South African and Argentine models; identification of the goods at stake; analysis of the protocol’s impact on each good; engagement with a serious objection; and overall argumentative coherence—allocating specific marks across these for a total of 25, with a one-line note on how candidates typically fail on each move. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
