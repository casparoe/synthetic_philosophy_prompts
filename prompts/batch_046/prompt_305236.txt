I'm a citizen of a country that, generations before my birth, forcibly assimilated an indigenous population and seized lands that now underpin public infrastructure I use every day. The state recently introduced an optional registry in which citizens may formally acknowledge an inherited institutional link to that harm—not as a personal apology, but as recognition that present citizenship structurally benefits from past wrongs. I was born long after the policy ended, and my parents were immigrants who arrived even later, so neither I nor my family ever took part. Some friends argue that signing expresses a legitimate responsibility tied to the continuity of the state, while others insist it imposes a guilt I cannot rationally bear, since I never chose my citizenship and caused no injury myself. I want to understand whether a meaningful distinction exists between being personally blameworthy and being structurally tied to a collective past, and whether registering such a statement can be justified without accepting personal moral guilt.
