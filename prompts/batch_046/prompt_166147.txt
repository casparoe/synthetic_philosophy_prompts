I'm prepping a sessoin for my philosophy of mind grad seminar — about 13 people, one 55-minute block. We've read some Dennett on sphexishness and the usual free-will debates, and I want an in-class game that lets them feel the rigidity from the inside, but I'm tired of the standard "drag the cricket" retelling. Instead I want the activity to spotlight what I'm calling the verification prison: the idea that the wasp isn't just stuck in a motion loop, it's stuck in a rational-seeming check loop. The check feels like good reasoning — "let me verify the burrow is clear" — but that very verification step is what prevents adapting to the moved cricket. So the game should have an explicit verification ritual that feels necessary and smart, and then the environment should shift in a way that makes that ritual counterproductive, but the rules or social setup should make it hard to drop.

Design the full thing for me: group size and roles (maybe split them into teams of 3-4?), the physical materials (cards, token's, a board?), the rules and payoff scheme using no real money — maybe points that determine who picks the reading for next week, or just candy — arranged so that people actually perform the loop themselves before anyone names sphexishness. The debrief should map specific moments in the game onto the inspection, carry, and failed-adaptation phases, with special attention to how the verification step locked them in. Also say what will predictably go wrong the first run — like someone skipping the check and breaking the illusion, or the group adapting too fast — and how to handle it. Finally, be frank about which part of real sphexishness — probably the total absence of representational flexibility — this classroom simulation can't capture, and what you'll have to supply verbally in the debrief.
