I am a high school student organizing an interview series for our school's philosophy journal, and next week I will speak with a philosopher who defends a particular theory of collective cognition she calls the ant-fugue model. On her view, an ant colony is not just a distributed system but can possess a genuinely integrated mental life structured analogously to a musical fugue: individual ants act like voices that enter at staggered temporal intervals, and colony-level cognition depends on the precise phase-locking of chemical signaling trails rather than on any shared representational content held by the members. She also employs a thought experiment involving an external agent she calls Aunt Hillary, who gradually replaces living ants with robotic surrogates that replicate local behavior exactly but lack the specific temporal delay properties of natural pheromone transmission; she argues that this substitution dissolves the colony mind even when every observable functional output remains intact.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking her to restate the view. I want at least one question pressing her on how the ant-fugue model differs in kind from standard distributed-cognition accounts applied to human groups, one question challenging whether the Aunt Hillary substitution case demonstrates the genuine absence of a colony mind or merely a change in physical substrate, and one question about whether her commitment to temporal-phase integration forces her toward panpsychism or allows her to restrict collective minds to specific biological architectures. For each question, include a brief follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—especially the reply that functional equivalence misses the relational-temporal essence of the fugue.
