I'm writing a feature on the limits of semantic externalism, and I'm struck by how rarely anyone presses the Twin Earth scenario past natural-kind terms like water or gold into the territory of mental-state vocabulary—belief, desire, intention. Most treatments stop at the chemistry. So I'd like a decomposed credence, not a survey: what's your actual probability, on a scale of 0 to 1, that the externalist conclusion genuinely carries over to mental-state terms in the same structural way? Decompose it into at least three parts: first, your confidence that the original Twin Earth argument succeeds for natural kinds; second, your confidence that mental-state terms share whatever semantic feature makes that success possible—whether that's a causal-historical chain, a social division of linguistic labor, or something else; third, your confidence that an internalist correction for mental states can be consistently maintained without collapsing back into some form of externalism. Give each term a rough number with a one-sentence justification, then combine them into an overall figure. Which of these three terms are you least sure about, and what kind of conceptual result—not a citation, but a finding—would move it by at least 0.2? I want to know where the argument actually lives, not which books cover it.
