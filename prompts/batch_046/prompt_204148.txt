The exam question was: "Explain Nozick's tracking conditions for knowledge, and discuss whether they are compatible with the principle that knowledge is closed under known logical entailment. Use the example of knowing that one has hands versus knowing that one is not a brain in a vat."

Here is the student's answer, reproduced in full:

"Nozick's tracking theory replaces the justified-true-belief analysis with two modal conditions. First, the sensitivity condition: if p were false, S would not believe p. Second, the adherence condition: if p were true in nearby possible worlds, S would still believe p. Together with the truth of p and S's actual belief, these are meant to capture knowledge.

Regarding epistemic closure, Nozick famously denies that knowing p and knowing that p entails q is sufficient for knowing q. The standard case is hands and BIV: I know I have hands, and I know that having hands entails not being a handless BIV, yet I do not know I am not a BIV. The reason is that in the closest world where I am a BIV, I still believe I have hands, so my belief that I am not a BIV fails sensitivity. Thus tracking blocks closure for this entailment.

However, Nozick's account still preserves closure at a higher level because he accepts the KK principle: one knows p only if one knows that one knows p. This means that although first-order knowledge doesn't transmit, second-order tracking can be maintained. Also, Nozick's conditions are essentially a form of reliabilism, since they require that the belief-forming process be generally reliable across worlds where p holds or fails."
