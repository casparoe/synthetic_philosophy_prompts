Consider a municipal system introduced in the 2020s that replaces traditional social-work case files with continuous, automated "well-being optimization" for residents of public housing. Non-visual sensors in apartments track air-quality fluctuations, foot-traffic patterns, and appliance-usage rhythms; machine-learning models translate these streams into real-time "household stability scores." These scores do not trigger police action directly, but they determine priority for preventive counseling, maintenance schedules, and, in borderline cases, lease-renewal reviews. Residents are told the system is opt-out, yet opting out requires submitting to a manual assessment that is recorded in a separate administrative track. The architects describe the platform as neutral infrastructure—there is no visible guard, no explicit norm enforced by a sovereign, only "data-driven care" calibrated to each household's patterns.

If Michel Foucault were presented with this arrangement, drawing on his analyses of panopticism, biopolitics, and the power/knowledge coupling he developed in his work on discipline and governmentality, how would he characterize the specific mode of power operating here? Would he see it as a direct extension of the disciplinary normalization he analyzed—where individuals internalize a norm through perpetual visibility—or rather as a mutation of biopolitical management that operates on populations through predictive risk rather than corrective training? Furthermore, how would he assess the architects' claim that decentralization, automation, and the absence of an explicit prohibitory law render this form of power non-coercive and therefore outside the scope of his critique? In particular, address whether the production of "household stability" as a statistical object—generated by data scientists whose categories become self-fulfilling—exemplifies the knowledge-power nexus he described, and whether his framework can accommodate a system where the "watcher" is an algorithmic process with no human subject behind it, or whether this forces a revision to his account.
