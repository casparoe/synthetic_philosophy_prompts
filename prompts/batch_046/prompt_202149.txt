I've come to think the dollar auction doesn't really demonstrate irrational escalation—it's rational deterrence under exit-cost asymettry. Quitting doesn't just stop your losses, it gives your rivel the prize plus everything you've sunk, so each new bid is buying a credible threat, not digging deeper. The sunk-cost fallacy is basically a mislabel. What would count against this? I don't want generic "people are emotional" answers—give me the structural defeaters. Which equilibrium results in auction theory fall apart if you treat sunk bids as deterrence capital? Would the view survive silent-bidding variants, or experiments with hard round caps? And what about escalation in solo contexts—no rival, just your own project—where there's no one to deter? Rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a defender of this view would actually concede versus dismiss.
