Write a joke that operates inside the logic of rational herding and information cascades, rather than treating them as mere synonyms for groupthink. The humor should hinge on the specific structural feature of the classic cascade model: that each agent updates in a fully Bayesian manner on both their private signal and the observed actions of predecessors, which means the collective error is not caused by any individual irrationality, ignorance, or social pressure, but is an emergent property of the aggregation rule itself. Set the scenario in a context familiar to longtime rationalist readers—perhaps a prediction market pricing an AI risk, a peer-review cascade in a niche philosophy-of-science journal, or a Bayesian forum thread evaluating a novel argument—where every participant is explicitly reasoning about common knowledge and yet the thread converges to an obviously wrong conclusion. The punchline should land precisely on the moment when someone realizes that no individual in the chain can be corrected without an exogenous shock to the information structure, because each person's private belief is already consistent with the public record. Do not rely on generic stereotypes about nerds, tech culture, or academic politics; the joke should be legible mainly to someone who understands why rational herding is a failure mode for aggregation mechanisms rather than for individual psychology.
