Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed conciliationist—who holds that recognized disagreement with an epistemic peer provides higher-order evidence that rationally requires revision of one's doxastic attitude toward that of the peer or toward suspension—and a committed steadfastist—who holds that a rational agent may maintain her original belief through peer disagreement provided she retains independent access to the reasoning chain that generated it. The collaboration should address both the canonical static case of symmetric peer disagreement over a first-order claim and a constructed scenario in which the peers disagree about how to calibrate the weight of testimonial evidence from a shared source, where each peer possesses differential meta-cognitive transparency into her own prior reasoning—call this the asymmetric-transparency case. Present the deliverable in two parts: first, a numbered list of agreed claims about the conditions for genuine epistemic peerhood, the distinction between first-order and higher-order evidence, the formal structure of conciliationist revision rules, the steadfastist conditions for legitimate belief retention, and the criteria for distinguishing substantive disagreement from verbal dispute; second, a disagreements table that states each camp's precise position in wording its own proponents would accept, identifying the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp—including specifically whether asymmetric meta-cognitive access undermines the symmetry presupposed by conciliation, whether steadfastness can be maintained without collapsing into dogmatism when transparency is only partial, and whether the dispute over calibration weights in the testimonial case is substantive or terminological. For each crux, record the argument, hypothetical case, or conceptual test both camps agree bears on it, with each side's predicted outcome noted in advance. Conclude by specifying which portions of the dispute, if any, are verbal rather than substantive, and state what kind of empirical finding or conceptual clarification would cause each camp to revise its core commitment.
