I am trying to determine whether karma, construed narrowly as a theory of moral desert rather than as a general causal metaphysics, can be formulated consistently with my own judgments about particular cases. Here are my verdicts. Case one: a person who lives with sustained cruelty and exploitation dies peacefully in old age, surrounded by comfort; I feel that in some deep sense he has not received his moral due, even though I do not think anyone should retroactively inflict suffering upon him. Case two: a surgeon whose unavoidable error kills a patient despite her best care; I do not think she has accumulated negative desert, yet I also do not think the cosmos owes her compensation for her subsequent anguish. Case three: a society that continues to enjoy institutional advantages built on historical violence; I feel there is a lingering collective moral debt embedded in the structure, but I do not think every individual beneficiary is personally blameworthy. Case four: someone who sincerely intends to help a stranger but accidentally causes serious harm, compared with someone who intends harm and fails entirely; I think the accidental harm-doer bears a heavier burden, yet I also feel that if karma tracks actual suffering caused, she should carry more negative weight than the failed malefactor, which conflicts with my intuition that intent matters more than outcome. Case five: an individual who lives with consistent compassion but endures relentless undeserved suffering; I want to deny that this reflects hidden past-life debts, but if karma genuinely distributes well-being according to desert, it is difficult to see why virtue should not be matched with flourishing. Is there a single coherent principle of karmic moral desert that accommodates all five verdicts? If not, which one must be abandoned, and why? Furthermore, what does the surviving principle imply about whether karma can be genuinely collective, whether it requires conscious awareness of one's desert, and whether it permits moral luck in the allocation of suffering?
