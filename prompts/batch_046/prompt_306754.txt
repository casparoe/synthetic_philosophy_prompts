Hey, quick excercise for you. I was trying to mash Al-Ghazali's occasionalism together with Woodward-style interventionist causation and now I've got an argument that seems to prove literally universal acausation. I can't spot the leak.

(1) Al-Ghazali holds that fire never burns cotton—God is the direct, immediate cause of every particular burning.
(2) Interventionism says X causes Y iff there's a possible intervention on X that changes Y, holding other direct causes fixed.
(3) So put out the fire—intervene on X. In occasionalism God could still burn the cotton, or not; the presence of fire doesn't constrain him.
(4) So there's no intervention on fire that reliably changes burning. Fire fails the causal test, occasionalism wins.
(5) Now apply (2) to God's will: is there an intervention on God's will that changes burning? Obviously we can't manipulate God.
(6) So God's will also fails the interventionist test.
(7) By (2), God doesn't cause burning either.
(8) Result: burning happens, but nothing—neither fire nor God—causes it. Universal acausation.
(9) That's absurd, so one step is rotten.

I think the rot might be in (3)—maybe Al-Ghazali's sunna means God correlates burning with fire-contact, so removing fire actually does change the outcome via God's habit, not via fire's power. But then (4) collapses and occasionalism doesn't win the test; it just gets weird. Or maybe (5) is a category mistake: interventionism was built for empirical variables, not for the ground of being. If I drop (5) I save causation but have to explain why occasionalism gets special treatment. What's the least costly fix? And does fixing it leave occasionalism smuggling causation back in through the back door of the sunna?
