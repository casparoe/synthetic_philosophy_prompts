I'm serving on a state constitutional convention's Subcommittee on Intergenerational Fiscal Obligations. We are considering an amendment that would entrench a climate-adaptation trust fund: once capitalized, future legislatures could not reduce its annual contribution below a formula tied to state GDP, except through a constitutional amendment requiring a sixty-percent supermajority and a twenty-four-month waiting period. Proponents describe this as a constitutional Ulysses contract—binding a future legislature that might otherwise "wirehead" the budget by diverting the fund during a revenue crisis. Critics object that it is an anti-democratic dead hand, treating future voters as irrational agents who must be tied to the mast, and that it illegitimately transfers the logic of individual self-binding onto collective decision-making across different democratic majorities.

Draft the subcommittee's formal recommendation memo for the full convention. Recommend adoption, rejection, or adoption with specific modifications, and provide reasoning that the opposing side could actually respond to. The memo should distinguish precisely between two forms of precommitment: binding one's own future preferences (the classic Ulysses case) and binding a future collective that does not share the present collective's identity or preferences, and it should explain whether the proposed mechanism achieves the first, the second, or neither. It should also take the wireheading analogy seriously: is diverting a trust fund during fiscal stress genuinely analogous to an agent hacking its own reward signal, or does that analogy collapse once we recognize that future legislatures are not continuous with the present agent? Conclude with an actionable recommendation that includes one concrete procedural safeguard—such as a sunset clause, an emergency override subject to judicial review, or a mandatory independent audit—rather than leaving the issue abstract. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific scholars or texts.
