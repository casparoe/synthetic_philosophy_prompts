Design a thought experiment built to the following specifications. The subject is Moore’s paradox—specifically, the absurdity of sincerely asserting that some proposition P holds while simultaneously asserting that one does not believe P—but applied not to a unified mental state, rather to compartmentalized doxastic fragments that a single agent maintains under divided attention in an ordinary social setting. Constraints on the scenario: it must be realistic (no science fiction, no demons, no perfect predictors, no technology beyond everyday use), fit in one paragraph, and involve a proposition P about an immediate practical matter such as a schedule change, a food-allergy warning, or a meeting cancellation. The agent must sincerely utter a Moore-paradoxical sentence, with the first clause indexed to one fragment formed via direct perceptual or testimonial evidence and the second clause indexed to another fragment formed via a conflicting but context-segregated source, without the agent recognizing the conflict. After presenting the scenario, show the verdict each of three theoretical frameworks delivers: (1) the transparency or normative account, on which belief aims at truth and assertion expresses belief; (2) a compartmentalized mental-file account, on which belief is stored in context-bound files with limited cross-access; and (3) an expressivist account that treats the expression of belief in P as a social commitment-marker rather than an inner-state report. For each framework, state whether the paradox is preserved, dissolved, or relocated, and explain why. Then identify the single structural feature of the case—namely, the contextual indexing of belief fragments—that causes the frameworks to diverge, describe what happens if that feature is removed (that is, if the fragments are merged into a unified doxastic state), and sketch how a defender of the framework that comes out worst would attack the case without denying its internal coherence. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
