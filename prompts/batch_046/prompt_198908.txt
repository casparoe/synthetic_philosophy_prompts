I am drafting a classification exercise for my school's philosophy seminar on the paradox of deontology—the tension that arises when agent-centered prohibitions against harming, applied consistently across multiple agents, seem to license outcomes far worse than a single permitted violation would have prevented. Rather than asking for named philosophers or texts, I want a decision tree that maps the logical space of responses by how they answer concrete cases.

Please construct a branching taxonomy with exactly four levels. At each node, pose one diagnostic question or scenario that a respondent can answer without knowing any philosophical label. The root split should ask whether the paradox reveals a genuine inconsistency within agent-relative morality, or whether it is only an illusion created by treating separate agents as a single collective decision-maker. The second level should split on what I will call the partition problem: if the harm necessary to save five lives is divided among five agents so that no individual performs a lethal act, does the deontological prohibition still bind each contributor? The third level should split on the mirror-allowance case: if I am permitted to violate the constraint solely because doing so prevents another agent from committing an identical violation elsewhere, does that generate a real exception or merely a post-hoc rationalization?

Every leaf must carry either a recognized position name (for example, moral absolutism, threshold deontology, or agent-neutral revisionism) or the label unnamed, followed by exactly one sentence describing its most awkward commitment—particularly what it says about the partition scenario. Please flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate in practice, and identify any branch combination that appears logically incoherent or is simply unoccupied in the debate. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works; the purpose is to sort beliefs by what they imply about cases, not by which sources defend them.
