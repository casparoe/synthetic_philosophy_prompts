sam said thats way too brain-centric. she pointed out that in an orchestra or a multiplayer raid the players have totally different private thoughts — the second violinist thinks hit my notes, the healer thinks save the tank — but the sheet music and game rules bind them into one action. she called this institutional scaffolding and said the jointness lives in the interlocking roles, not inside everyones head.

jordan came back saying sam is just describing coordination, not true jointness. he said ants also interlock behaviors but nobody thinks we are building. he argued if you let institutional scaffolding count, then a factory line is joint action even when workers hate eachother and never share a goal, which seems wrong.

sam replied that ants dont represent the collective goal at all, but orchestra members and gamers do use we talk in voice chat or edit logs. she introduced this idea she called proxy intentionality — the institution or rule-system holds the shared content so individuals dont need direct mind-access to eachother. she said jordans standard makes almost all human cooperation impossible since we can never literally peek inside other brains.
