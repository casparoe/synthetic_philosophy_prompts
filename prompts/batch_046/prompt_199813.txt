I am currently enrolled in a seminar on Madhyamaka philosophy and comparative logic, and I have been asked to assist a small municipal arbitration panel that reviews contested land-use proposals. The board members have no philosophical training, yet they repeatedly encounter applications that frame conflicts as rigid binaries—development versus preservation, private rights versus public goods—without examining whether the terms themselves are coherently defined or whether they possess the independent, inherent existence (svabhāva) that the framing assumes. I need you to translate Nāgārjuna's tetralemma—the four-cornered examination of affirmation, negation, both, and neither—together with the Madhyamaka doctrine of emptiness into a reusable audit procedure that this panel can run without consulting texts.

The deliverable should be an operational checklist or flowchart in words: the ordered questions to ask of any proposal, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the procedure stops with a recommendation to reject the conceptual framing, where it must hand the decision back to human judgment because the question is one of values rather than logical coherence, and where it is likely to misfire or produce false negatives. Please apply the procedure to two worked cases: first, a riverfront plan that presents the choice as either commercial construction or untouched ecological preservation; second, a high-density housing initiative that pits "neighborhood character" against "housing rights" as if these were mutually exclusive inherent properties of the site. Include at least one case where the procedure dissolves the binary but leaves the board without a clear policy path—an outcome they will find uncomfortable. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the panel must be able to run this from the procedure alone. Be explicit about which steps are genuinely procedural and which are judgment calls dressed up as logic.
