hey, i'm giving the opening remarks at the new gallery opening for "Names and Wounds" at the regional memorial center next thursday. the room will be split between people who actually work on atrocity law (some of them knew Lemkin's papers inside out) and local community folks, including families of people who survived things that never got called genocide in any court. i want the speech to do real work with the idea that naming an atrocity "genocide" isn't just a legal label but a kind of moral-political act that can include and exclude at the same time. paticularly, i want you to build it around the tension in Lemkin's own concept—he wanted a precise legal category, but he also kept sneaking in cultural and social destruction that the 1948 convention deliberatly left out. so the speech should feel the weight of that gap: every time we use the word precisely we risk leaving someone out, and every time we expand it we risk hollowing it out. it should shape what i can and can't say—like, don't just list examples of named vs unnamed atrocities, and don't resolve the tension with a neat conclusion. maybe 6-8 minutes, spoken aloud, and it needs to land for the lawyers without losing the family members. also, can you add a short note after on where you're taking liberties with Lemkin's unpublished drafts or leaning on contested interpretations of his intent? and if the whole premise is confused—like if you think you can't write a speech that holds both audiences without either dumbing down or lecturing—just say that plainly instead of faking it.
