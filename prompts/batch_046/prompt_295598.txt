I'm putting together a handout for my phil of mind study group about whether qualia are actually an incoherent theoretical posit—sort of in the Chase-Sanborn vein, though I don't want to just rehearse the usual eliminativist-vs-realist dance. I want a decision tree with maybe 6 to 8 leaves, built from concrete commitments rather than brand names. The root should ask whether a first-person report like "this hurts" can be mistaken in the same way a thermometer can be miscalibrated, because that splits people on whether there's any intersubjective standard for mental-state attribution. From there I'd like branches on: whether subjective states need a causal-explanatory role to be legitimate theoretical posits; whether accepting them forces you into something like phenomenal solipsism; and whether they can be salvaged as normative constructs rather than natural kinds.

The leaves should cover the obvious ones—straight eliminativism, physicalist reduction, dualism, phenomenal-concept strategy—but also some underpopulated spots, like the view that qualia are coherent only if we treat them as heuristic predictions about our own behavior with no ontological weight, or the position that accepts direct acquaintance but denies any theoretical role so they float free of Quinean incoherence. Each branch node needs to be phrased as a yes/no or A/B choice that someone could answer without knowing which philosophy it maps to. And please flag any pairs of leaves your tree can't really seperate—maybe two versions of physicalism that both reject intersubjective calibration but differ only on whether they call the result "illusion" or "re-description". Also note the empty boxes: combinations that seem logically open but nobody actually occuppies them, like accepting that mental reports need causal-explanatory roles but denying they can ever have them and still keeping qualia around. Is that incoherent, or just a view nobody has bothered to defend? I don't care about perfect formatting, just make it usable for a seminar discussion.
