I have recently been appointed healthcare proxy for my aunt, who executed a detailed advance directive five years ago refusing all artificial nutrition and hydration should she ever develop severe dementia. The directive's language is unambiguous, but honoring it now requires withdrawing nourishment from someone who seems to want it, which has forced me to confront whether the authority of an earlier self can survive what I have come to think of as axiological discontinuity—a deep, stable shift in values that is not merely cognitive error but a transformation in what matters to her.
