As a retired physicist who has only recently turned to philosophy of science, I have been trying to understand whether Ludwig Boltzmann—who died in 1906, well before quantum mechanics reached its modern form—would have recognized anything of his own statistical-mechanical program in the present debate over the Born rule within Everettian quantum mechanics. Boltzmann treated macroscopic probabilities as objective features of large ensembles, yet he also resisted reducing them to subjective ignorance. If we imagine him confronted with the many-worlds interpretation, in which unitary evolution produces deterministic branching and the only apparent problem is recovering the squared-modulus weights, I want to know how he would judge the two dominant strategies for doing so: the decision-theoretic derivation advanced by Deutsch and Wallace, which treats rational preference over bets in branching worlds as grounding the weights, and the self-locating-uncertainty approach, which holds that an observer should assign credences proportional to the measure of branches containing her, absent any further physical postulate. Would Boltzmann have accepted either as a legitimate statistical-mechanical explanation, or would he have insisted—consistent with his resistance to subjective interpretations of entropy—that both illegitimately import epistemic reasoning into ontology? And might he instead have sketched a third option, perhaps treating the branches as statistical ensembles suppressed by an as-yet-unknown dynamical mechanism that restores objective frequencies without collapsing the wavefunction? I am asking not for a biographical reconstruction but for an analysis of which of these three positions best aligns with Boltzmann's actual commitments to ergodic theory and to the objectivity of statistical laws, together with the specific feature of his philosophy—say, his view that entropy increase is a property of macro-state descriptions rather than of individual micro-trajectories—that would force a rejection of the self-locating-uncertainty view regardless of its formal coherence. Finally, if his commitments genuinely underdetermine the choice, what further conceptual development—perhaps a precise account of how branch weights could be measured experimentally—would be required to settle it?
