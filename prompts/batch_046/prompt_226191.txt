Below are five normative rationales for making unemployment benefits conditional on work-search or labor-market participation—rationales that figure, in various forms, in debates about workfare, benefit fraud, and which claimants are treated as undeserving. Please rank them from the most defensible to the least defensible within a liberal framework that accepts needs-based redistribution but requires that conditionality respect equal moral status.

The five rationales are:
(A) The moral-hazard argument: unconditional support weakens the incentive to seek employment, harming both the recipient and the broader economy.
(B) The reciprocity argument: drawing on collective resources creates a duty to contribute labor in return, so conditions enforce fair exchange.
(C) The social-contract argument: citizenship in a welfare state involves mutual obligations—society guarantees a floor, and recipients must demonstrate active efforts to remain self-sustaining.
(D) The character-formation argument: mandatory work-related activities build practical skills, social connections, and self-respect that passive receipt does not provide.
(E) The temporal-reciprocity-weighting argument: the strictness of conditions should scale with a recipient’s projected lifetime fiscal contribution; young, high-potential workers should face tighter conditions because preserving their labor-market attachment yields greater long-term collective return, whereas older or chronically displaced workers should face looser ones.

For each rationale, assign it a rank from 1 (strongest) to 5 (weakest) and provide one or two sentences explaining its placement, with particular attention to whether it treats the recipient as an equal moral agent or merely as a unit of economic optimization. After completing the ranking, state how confident you are in the ordering overall, and identify the specific adjacent pair about which you are least certain.
