I am a PhD student in philosophy of art working on race, aesthetics, and the politics of expression. In a recent graduate seminar on Black aesthetics, a fellow student dismissed my presentation on non-representational abstract painting by members of the Black avant-garde. She invoked the maxim that all art is propaganda, arguing that since these paintings carry no explicit racial-political message, they fail Du Bois's standard for valuable art and therefore do not merit serious aesthetic attention. I want to bring a precise disambiguation back to the seminar rather than a general history of Du Bois's aesthetics.

Briefly describe where I encountered the claim and exactly what argumentative move it was being used to perform—namely, dismissing abstract, non-representational work as aesthetically and politically deficient because it lacks an explicit message.

2. The menu of precise readings (~400 words). Lay out the distinct theses that the claim all art is propaganda could be expressing, arranged from the most trivial to the most radical. Include at least these variants: (i) a communicative triviality that all art expresses some viewpoint or social location; (ii) a sociological thesis that all art serves some collective social interest; (iii) an intentionalist thesis that all art is deliberately engineered to persuade audiences of specific doctrines; (iv) an instrumentalist thesis associated with Du Bois's aesthetic arguments—namely, that Black art ought to be deployed in service of racial justice and uplift; and (v) an ontological thesis that denies any autonomous aesthetic value outside propagandistic function. I am especially interested in whether a meaningful distinction can be drawn between propositional propaganda, in which art conveys explicit beliefs or doctrines, and dispositional propaganda, in which art shapes affective orientations and perceptual habits without propositional content—treat this distinction as an axis that cuts across the five readings.

For each reading listed above, say whether it actually supports my colleague's move—i.e., whether it entails that non-representational abstract painting lacking an explicit message is deficient or fails a Du Boisian standard. Be explicit about which readings are being invoked, even implicitly, when the slogan is used to end an argument about whether a painting counts as politically serious art, and identify any equivocations between the propositional and dispositional axes.

4. Verdict on Du Bois's likely position (~150 words). Without quoting specific texts from memory and without providing a bibliography or reading list, state which of these readings best captures the view Du Bois actually advanced in his writings on art, propaganda, and racial uplift. Justify your choice by reference to the general structure of his aesthetic arguments—particularly his insistence that Black artists bear a special responsibility to represent and uplift—rather than by citation of particular passages or publication dates.
