I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of science working at the intersection of evolutionary biology and metaphysics of explanation. I am developing a framework to evaluate explanatory strategies in evolutionary developmental biology according to whether they function as cranes—incremental, bottom-up scaffolds—or as skyhooks—unexplained, top-down design principles—while also assessing their vulnerability to greedy reductionism, understood as the collapse of higher-level phenomena into their lowest-level constituents without residue. To test the coherence of this framework, please evaluate the following five explanatory moves, which I have formulated to capture distinct positions in recent debates about morphological novelty: (A) the incremental co-option of cis-regulatory modules as stepwise scaffolds for new form; (B) the appeal to physical self-organization in tissues as an emergent constraint on genetic change; (C) the postulation of evolutionary capacitance—stored genetic potential that remains silent under selection until a sudden environmental shift releases it; (D) the invocation of an autonomous "developmental logic" that structures organismal form prior to and independently of selective filtering; and (E) the claim that all morphological innovation can be exhaustively explained by point mutations in deeply conserved toolkit genes. First, rank these five moves from the most clearly crane-like (0) to the most clearly skyhook-like (10), assigning each a precise numerical score and providing a brief conceptual justification. Second, rate each move's structural susceptibility to greedy reductionism on a scale of 1 (structurally protected against reduction) to 5 (highly vulnerable to being greedily collapsed), with a sentence explaining the score. I am not seeking bibliographic references, reading lists, or citations; I need to know whether this specific conceptual architecture produces stable, non-arbitrary distinctions when applied to these moves together.
