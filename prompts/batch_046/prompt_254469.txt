Suppose you are advising a small research group with three years and two junior scholars in conceptual history and feminist philosophy, working without archival resources. They intend to concentrate on Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's defence of women's education, specifically the tension between her negative argument that ignorance is not a virtue and therefore women possess a right to learn, and her positive argument that study perfects the soul for union with God but ought to remain private rather than seek public authority. Which open conceptual questions arising from this tension should they prioritise, and in what order? For each priority, explain its upstream status: for example, does settling whether her right to learn rests on natural law or on theological vocation determine how we should read her apparent silence on institutional reform? Assess tractability using only conceptual analysis of her known writings and comparison with other early modern defenders of women's study. Identify which lines of inquiry are overrated — for instance, tracing direct historical influence on later feminist movements — and which neglected but feasible questions deserve attention instead. Rank three priorities, defend the criteria you use (downstream dependence, philosophical payoff, feasibility for junior scholars), and specify what argument or textual interpretation over the next two years would cause you to reorder the list.
