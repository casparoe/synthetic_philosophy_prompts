I am preparing an essay for my formal epistemology course on belief revision under uncertain evidence. Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises within Jeffrey conditionalization when the uncertain evidence bears not on an ordinary state of the world, but on the reliability of the very partition over which the conditioning is performed—so that the probabilities assigned to the cells depend on a calibration judgment that is itself subject to revision by the rule being applied. The example should be concrete: describe a specific kind of belief state or evidential situation that generates the tension, and explain why this is not merely a technical circularity in computation or a standard case of prior uncertainty, but rather raises a substantive question about whether the framework presupposes a stability in its evidential structure that the updating process can undermine. I am interested in the conceptual shape of the problem, not in a mathematical derivation or in references to particular authors.
