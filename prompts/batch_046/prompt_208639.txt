My co-founder and I are restructuring our firm's decision-making after a failed product launch. She argues that our risk-averse engineering culture is shaped by our CTO's Calvinist upbringing: she treats Weber's Protestant ethic not as a macro-historical pattern but as a direct psychological mechanism, claiming that Weber's idea of elective affinity means religious values can causally determine workplace norms like exhaustive documentation and failure-avoidance. I object that Weber was explaining the origins of capitalist rationalization, not individual office dynamics, and that comparative economic studies of Catholic and Protestant regions have essentially shown culture to be epiphenomenal to material and institutional incentives—so treating a CTO's theological background as an independent causal variable is a category mistake. Yet I worry I am over-correcting: if values are never genuinely causal but only reflections of underlying conditions, why do sociologists still invoke Weber at all? Is my co-founder wrong to apply the thesis at the organizational level, wrong to treat elective affinity as direct causation rather than mutual reinforcement, or am I wrong to assume that comparative economic history has eliminated culture from causal explanation altogether?
