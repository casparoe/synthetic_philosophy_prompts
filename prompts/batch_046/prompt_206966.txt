I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm serving as an outside grader for an undergraduate philosophy-of-science seminar. The assignment asked students to evaluate whether cross-cultural variation in moral foundations undermines moral realism. The prompt required three things: (i) a careful summary of the empirical WEIRD findings without overstating their scope; (ii) a clear distinction between descriptive disagreement about what people value and normative disagreement about what should be the case; and (iii) an assessment of the baseline-convergence reply, which treats near-universal prohibitions on extreme cruelty and incest as fixed calibration points from which culturally specific divergences can be evaluated for coherence with those anchors rather than treated as direct falsifications of a universal moral code.

Here is one student submission. Please assign a grade on a 100-point scale and provide specific, point-by-point comments that justify the score. Evaluate separately how the answer handles the empirical summary in part (i), the conceptual distinction in part (ii), and the assessment of the baseline-convergence reply in part (iii). Identify any subtle mischaracterizations, conflations, misattributions, or inferential steps that do not follow, even if they are not glaring errors. Do not open with a general impression; begin with the grade and then address each part in turn. I am grading anonymously and do not want my own view signaled, so present the assessment as if you were the examiner.

Student answer:

Henrich, Heine, and Norenzayan's 2010 analysis showed that WEIRD populations are outliers in analytic reasoning and individualism. Building on this, Haidt's moral foundations research across dozens of societies found that Western subjects treat harm and fairness as autonomous domains, whereas non-WEIRD subjects integrate loyalty, authority, and purity into a single unified framework. This reveals deep disagreement: different cultures rank moral priorities differently. Once we see that describing a foundation as central is already a normative commitment, descriptive disagreement collapses into normative disagreement, leaving no neutral arbitration procedure. The baseline-convergence reply, associated with some evolutionary ethicists, points to near-universal prohibitions on extreme cruelty and incest. From these fixed points we can evaluate other beliefs for internal coherence. But evolutionary psychology explains both prohibitions—incest via the Westermarck effect and cruelty aversion via kin-selection logic—so convergence tracks adaptive utility, not moral truth. Therefore the WEIRD data show not local variation within one universal framework, but that moral codes are equally valid constructions, with the Western harm-and-fairness emphasis lacking any objective priority.
