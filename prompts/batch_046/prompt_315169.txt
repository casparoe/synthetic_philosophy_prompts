Write the full specification—the desiderata—that any adequate normative taxonomy of the varieties of free will worth wanting must satisfy. I am not asking for a defense of one view; I want the checklist against which competing taxonomies—compatibilist, libertarian, revisionist, hierarchical, source-based, and reason-responsive—should be judged.

For each requirement, state the motivating case or distinction: for example, the need to separate local choice from global character-formation, or to distinguish freedom under external coercion from freedom under internal compulsion or manipulation. Specify which phenomena the taxonomy must classify—moral responsibility, creative agency, autonomy, the sense that one could have acted otherwise, and self-formation over time—and which empirical constraints from psychology or neuroscience it must respect rather than ignore.

Then address the tensions: identify at least two pairs of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable, explaining whether the conflict is real or only apparent. For instance, does requiring ultimate sourcehood necessarily conflict with requiring sensitivity to reasons, or can a single capacity satisfy both? Does demanding immunity from manipulation collapse into an unmeetable standard of causal isolation?

Next, say which single desideratum does the most work in ruling out rival taxonomies—whether it is hierarchical self-endorsement, reason-responsiveness, or immunity from covert control—and explain why insisting on it eliminates the largest number of competitors.

Keep the conceptual weight on the criteria and the cases behind them rather than on scoring every named position in detail. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or author citations. If you refer to named positions, describe them precisely enough that the requirement is clear without relying on the reader to look them up.
