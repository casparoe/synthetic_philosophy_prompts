Following the standard rationalist framing of inside versus outside view reasoning, I have begun applying base-rate analysis to my philosophical commitments. In domains where epistemic peers have maintained persistent disagreement for generations—on questions like the scope of epistemic modesty itself, or on how to weight intuitive judgments against formal models—the observed convergence rate is effectively zero. This suggests that my inside-view reasoning in these areas is mostly noise. But when I apply the same outside-view logic to my belief that the outside view should dominate philosophical reasoning, I seem to hit a self-undermining regress: philosophers disagree just as sharply about meta-epistemology and the proper boundaries of reference-class reasoning, so I should apparently discount my confidence in epistemic modesty itself. How should this recursion be halted? Is there a principled distinction—perhaps treating meta-level beliefs about reasoning methods as belonging to a separate reference class with different base rates—that allows the outside view to constrain object-level philosophy without dissolving all confidence at the meta-level? Or does consistent application of the outside view ultimately collapse into a global skepticism that undermines even the tools used to reach it?
