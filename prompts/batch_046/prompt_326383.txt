Write a dialogue between Dr. Elena Voss and Dr. Marcus Yen, both philosophers of science with expertise in conceptual analysis but holding opposing views on conceptual slippage within tightly bounded practices. Voss maintains that when a macro-level concept is copied into a specialized microdomain and gradually modified, those modifications are not corruptions but adaptive refinements that grant the microdomain genuine epistemic autonomy. Yen holds the contrary view: that without strict fidelity to the definitional conditions of the source domain, a microdomain cannot reliably distinguish substantive conceptual application from metaphorical extension, and therefore its knowledge-claims lack objective purchase.

Their exchange should focus on a concrete trajectory they both know well: the concept of failure as it is copied from structural engineering into the microdomain of fermentation taxonomy in traditional Korean jang production, and then further imported into computational models designed to predict fermentation outcomes. Voss should argue that within the jang microdomain, failure acquires a precise normative dimension—indicating not physical collapse but a deviation from an intended microbial-flavor profile—which makes the concept more discriminating, not less. Yen should respond that this normative loading introduces a dangerous ambiguity: when the computational model imports the jang-modified concept of failure, it treats flavor-deviation data as if they were structural-stress measurements, generating predictions that conflate physical breakdown with aesthetic judgment. Each interlocutor must engage the other’s actual point rather than talk past it—Voss needs to explain why the model’s conflation is an implementation error rather than a conceptual one, while Yen needs to explain why adaptive refinement can be halted at any non-arbitrary boundary without collapsing into relativism. Do not reduce either speaker to a strawman; both should acknowledge the real force of the opposing position. Conclude without either conceding, but with the precise locus of disagreement—whether conceptual autonomy requires definitional fidelity or can be sustained through controlled slippage—clearly articulated.
