For an upper-level philosophy of social science exam, draft one 75-minute essay question with a target answer of about 800 words on Weber’s Protestant Ethic thesis and the problem of explaining with values. The question should present a specific constructed scenario—imagine a society in which Calvinist predestination beliefs are widespread but institutional inheritance rules block capital accumulation, and capitalism still fails to emerge—to force examinees to adjudicate whether Weber treats culture as an efficient cause, a selective cause through elective affinity, or an epiphenomenon, and whether this saves the thesis from unfalsifiability. Instruct the examinee to take a committed position: can culture cause social change without being reduced to material conditions, and does Weber’s ideal of value-freedom (Wertfreiheit) survive if values do causal work? Provide a model answer of roughly 700 words that argues for one of these reconstructions with explicit reasoning rather than surveying both sides. Include a marking scheme out of 25 points built around four must-hit conceptual moves—distinguishing efficient from selective causation, addressing the falsifiability objection via the scenario, evaluating whether value-freedom is compromised, and applying the elective-affinity mechanism precisely—with marks assigned to each and a one-line note on how candidates typically miss it. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; rely only on core Weberian concepts.
