A fellow student in my philosophy of language seminar summarised Douglas Hofstadter's essay A Person Paper on Purity in Language as follows. Hofstadter contends that English pronouns and gendered nouns are irredeemably sexist because they treat the masculine form as the unmarked default, and he proves this by systematically substituting racial terms—white and black—for gendered ones in ordinary sentences, thereby showing that we would never tolerate an analogous racial hierarchy. He therefore concludes that anyone who resists gender-neutral language reform is implicitly endorsing structural sexism, and that logical consistency requires the complete abolition of gendered pronouns in favour of invented neuter forms. The student went on to claim that this makes the essay an anticipation of current debates about bias in large language models, since LLMs trained on historical corpora reproduce precisely these masculine-default patterns in pronoun resolution. I feel the summary captures the essay's rhetorical strategy but suspect it misrepresents its satirical framing and overstates its normative commitments. Please assess which parts are accurate to Hofstadter's argument and method, which are subtle misrepresentations—especially concerning the function of the racial analogy and the essay's stance on mandatory reform—and whether the connection to algorithmic pronoun bias genuinely follows from his reasoning or imports anachronistic assumptions about statistical language modelling.
