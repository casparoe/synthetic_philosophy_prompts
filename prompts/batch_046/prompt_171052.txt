In my metaphysics seminar we are comparing counterfactual and interventionist accounts of causation, and I am trying to apply them to a case that seems to strain both frameworks. Write a dialogue between a counterfactualist and an interventionist debating whether omissions can cause a system failure in the following scenario: a distributed sensor network collapses not because any node emits a false signal, but because two separate nodes both fail to transmit a required heartbeat message—each omission is redundant, neither is individually necessary or sufficient for the collapse, and there is no single manipulable variable that could restore the system. The counterfactualist should argue that both omissions remain genuine causes through a refined dependency relation that survives redundancy, while the interventionist should maintain that causation requires a well-defined intervention target and that these omissions are at best explanatory gaps rather than causes. Have them address each other’s points directly—especially whether redundancy undermines counterfactual dependence and whether structural omissions can ever satisfy interventionist criteria—without either becoming a strawman. Keep the exchange rigorous and concise, as if both speakers are graduate students defending their positions in a seminar room.
