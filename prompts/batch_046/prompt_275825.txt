I'm a retired biologist who picked up philosphy of mind in the last few years, and there's this argument that keeps looping in my study group. Two friends—Mara and Leo—got stuck on whether gut bacteria can have original intentionality or only derived, using Dennett's two-bitser against Searle. I want you to go through it move by move and say who's right, where both miss something, and who comes out ahead overall.

(1) Mara said take Searle's point about the Chinese Room: the bacteria just push chemicals around, syntax with no semantics. The "meaning" of those signals—like "there's stress" or "we need more serotonin"—only shows up when the host brain interprets them. So the bacteria have at best derived intentionality, and the brain is the only place original intentionality lives.

(2) Leo came back with Dennett's two-bitser idea: there's no magic threshold where interpretation suddenly becomes intrinsic. Stack interpreters—bacteria interpreted by gut tissue, interpreted by brain, interpreted by social environment—and you never hit bottom. The original/derived line is just a functional-complexity gradient, so either the whole chain has some original intentionality or none of it does.

(3) Mara said that's reductio ad absurdum: if Leo's right, then every enzyme and thermostat gets original intentionality too, and Searle's whole distinction collapses into "whatever is complex enough for me to like." She said biological brains have intrinsic causal powers that produce aboutness, not just interpretation layered on interpretation.

(4) Leo replied that "intrinsic causal power" is just a label we stick on the most complex node. The bacteria genuinely regulate neurotransmitters in response to environmental conditions—food scarcity, toxins—which makes their states about those conditions. That's enough for original intentionality at their level; consciousness isn't required, only the right functional aboutness.

Please adjudicate each of the four moves—rate them from clearly mistaken to clearly right, briefly justify each, and say what both are missing and who is more right overall. Don't just pick a winner; tell me where each one's reasoning actually holds up.
