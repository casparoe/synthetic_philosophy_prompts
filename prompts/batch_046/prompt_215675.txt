I am a high school student working on a philosophy essay, and I have formed the following views about Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities, but I am unsure whether they form a consistent package. (1) I think Frankfurt's original counterexample works: Jones is morally responsible for deciding to kill Smith even though Black's hidden mechanism makes it impossible for Jones to refrain, so I reject the principle of alternative possibilities as a necessary condition for moral responsibility. (2) I also believe that if determinism is true, then no human action involves the kind of robust, libertarian alternative possibilities that free-will defenders usually demand, yet I do not think determinism eliminates moral responsibility. (3) At the same time, I find the flicker-of-freedom defense unconvincing, because any residual mental twitch or prior signal that could count as an alternative is causally disconnected from the agent's actual choice. (4) Finally, I suspect that Frankfurt cases persuade us mainly through a psychological illusion: they make us confuse the absence of viable options with the absence of authorship, even though authorship can remain fully intact. Please tell me whether these four commitments hang together. In particular, does my dismissal of flickers in (3) create tension with my determinism claim in (2), and does my psychological explanation in (4) weaken the logical force I attribute to Frankfurt's case in (1)? Please answer in American English, and please do not include a bibliography or citations.
