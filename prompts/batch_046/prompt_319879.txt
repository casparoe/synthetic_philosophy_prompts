Please construct a rigorous argument that genuinely joint action requires the kind of plural-subject commitment Margaret Gilbert identifies—where participants jointly commit to acting as a body—rather than merely interlocking individual intentions. Apply this to the case of a decentralized autonomous organization (DAO) governed by smart-contract voting: participants each intend to vote on treasury allocations, share common knowledge of the protocol rules, and produce a coordinated collective outcome, yet they never acknowledge one another as co-agents in a shared obligation. Argue that this absence of mutual commitment means the DAO's actions are aggregated individual actions rather than joint action, and respond to the Bratman-inspired objection that shared cooperative activity only needs interlocking intentions plus common knowledge of a joint plan, which the smart-contract architecture apparently supplies. In particular, explain whether the normative binding force that distinguishes joint action—such that each party owes the others performance—can be replicated by algorithmic enforcement, or whether it necessarily requires an explicit "we intend" attitude that code cannot instantiate. Conclude by assessing whether adding an on-chain governance token that binds voters to the collective decision alters the analysis, or whether the underlying absence of interpersonal acknowledgment remains decisive.
