People who defend philosophical intuition often say running a thought experiment is basically like running a lab experiment in your head — you isolate one variable, hold the rest constant, and read off the result. The idea is that Gettier-style cases or trolley scenarios are just mental controls, so intuition counts as data rather than bias, and we can build theories straight from it. I want to analyse that analogy properly: what corresponds to apparatus, replication, blinding, peer review? Where does it break — no shared instrument, framing effects everywhere, no way to rerun with the same subject? And theres a newer comparison people are making, that intuition pumps work more like adversarial examples in ML: tiny engineered inputs meant to push a cognitive system into a specific misclassification. Does that framing hold up better? What does each analogy smuggle in about whether intuition tells us about reality or just about our own bugs, and can either actually support the conclusion that thought experiments are trustworthy evidence?
