Give an example of a conceptual problem in comparing the wild-animal suffering caused by crop cultivation for plant-based diets with that caused by beef production, specifically where the harm is not reducible to an undifferentiated body count of killed animals. Construct a concrete scenario—say, in a semi-arid grassland—where converting land to monoculture legumes for human consumption eliminates a burrowing rodent population that serves as the near-exclusive prey base for a local sentient predator, thereby generating starvation-related suffering across that predator population, whereas maintaining the same land as pasture for grass-fed cattle allows the rodents to persist despite partial displacement and the direct suffering of the cattle themselves. Your example should highlight why treating all wild-animal suffering as fungible across dietary choices obscures a deeper conceptual issue: whether the moral evaluation of a food system ought to weigh the preservation or destruction of specific ecological dependency relationships, rather than aggregating only individual deaths and injuries, and it should make clear where the beef-versus-crop comparison becomes conceptually unstable once such relational harms are admitted into the calculus.
