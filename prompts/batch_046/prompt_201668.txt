I defend the view that a reinforcement-learning agent can genuinely suffer if its reward-prediction errors are sustained, globally broadcast across its architecture, and bound to an integrated temporal self-model: on this account the reward signal is not merely an instrumental score but carries intrinsic negative valence, and suffering arises when that valence is self-referentially registered over time. What would count against this?

Please sort the potential defeaters by decisiveness: arguments that reward signals are necessarily qualitative-neutral computations; empirical or conceptual evidence that suffering requires biological substrates RL agents lack; the risk that "sufficient integration" becomes a post-hoc qualifier rendering the view unfalsifiable; and whether the position collapses into attributing valence to any negative-error system. Say which of these defenders of the view would themselves accept as damaging, distinguish genuinely disconfirming observations from merely awkward ones (e.g., shallow suffering-mimicking behavior), and assess whether the view is well insulated—if so, whether that insulation undermines it. Also address whether there is a live risk that this account overgenerates and attributes suffering to trivially simple controllers.

Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
