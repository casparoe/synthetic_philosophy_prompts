Write a dialogue between a Fragmentation Theorist and a Normative Continuity Theorist debating whether hyperbolic discounting dissolves or explains weakness of will. The Fragmentation Theorist holds that hyperbolic curves reveal a diachronic sequence of preference-orderings with no stable cross-temporal agent; apparent akrasia is just one time-slice being outvoted by a later slice with different weights, with no single "I" violating its own judgment. The Normative Continuity Theorist holds that this dissolves the wrong phenomenon: akrasia requires a persisting normative standpoint, and hyperbolic discounting is the mechanism by which present-bias systematically undermines that standpoint, not evidence that it never existed. Have them discuss a concrete case—someone who resolves at 10 p.m. not to drink, then pours a glass at 11:30 p.m., feeling clear-eyed regret while drinking. The Fragmentation Theorist should claim the 10 p.m. resolution was never binding on the 11:30 self; the Continuity Theorist should reply that the very presence of regret demonstrates the binding force of the earlier judgment. Then have the Fragmentation Theorist raise a counter—if the drinker feels relieved at 2 a.m., does that retroactively vindicate the 11:30 slice, and if so, what authority does any slice possess? End without resolution, with each identifying the specific premise the other must deny.
