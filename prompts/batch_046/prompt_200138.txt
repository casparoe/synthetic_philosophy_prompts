I am a doctoral student in historiography trying to determine whether presentism is genuinely avoidable. I have built an argument that appears valid but yields an absurd conclusion—namely, that the methodological injunction against presentism is a category mistake and should be abandoned altogether. The steps are as follows:

(1) To avoid presentism, a historian must apply only the normative standards that past agents themselves recognized.

(2) Identifying which standards past agents recognized requires selecting evidence and interpreting texts through conceptual categories—e.g., "religion," "political authority," "economic obligation"—that belong to the historian's own present framework.

(3) Therefore any recovery of past normative standards necessarily imports present conceptual schemes; descriptive interpretation and normative evaluation are inseparable.

(4) So presentism is not a contingent failure of method but a structural condition of all historical understanding.

(5) If presentism is structurally unavoidable, then the methodological rule "avoid presentism" demands the impossible.

(6) A rule demanding the impossible is not a regulative ideal but a category mistake: it treats an unavoidable structural feature as if it were a correctable error.

(7) Hence historians should stop treating presentism as a vice and abandon the distinction between presentist and non-presentist history altogether.

This strikes me as wrong—historians do meaningfully distinguish, for instance, judging feudal obligations by democratic norms from understanding them within their own conceptual world. I am inclined to preserve the idea that presentism is unavoidable in some structural sense, yet I do not want to collapse the normative distinction. Which step should I reject? Is there an equivocation between importing present conceptual categories (descriptive) and importing present normative standards (evaluative) between (2) and (3), or a modal slide between (4) and (5)? And if I reject (1) by reconceiving the avoidance of presentism as reflexive awareness of anachronism rather than its elimination, what does that cost my account of historical objectivity?
