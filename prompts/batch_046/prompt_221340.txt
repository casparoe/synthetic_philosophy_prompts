hey, i'm drafting a novel about an archivist in a city where nothing ever gets deleted — every conversation, every failure, every old hero's mistake is indexted and searchable forever. i was trying to build a philosphical argument for why this world would actually destroy the kind of history that helps people live, borrowing loosely from nietzsche's essay on monumental, antiquarian, and critical history. but now the argument feels like it proves too much, and i'm worried i smuggeld in something that doesn't belong.

(1) monumental history needs singular, towering examples that stand apart from ordinary life.
(2) antiquarian history needs a bounded past that can be revered without swallowing the present.
(3) critical history needs the power to condemn and then forget, to clear space for the new.
(4) in a world of perfect digital recall, every past moment is equally present and searchable.
(5) so monumental figures can't tower — they're just data points in an infinite list.
(6) antiquarian reverence turns into total preservation of every trivial detail, so the past isn't bounded at all.
(7) critical judgment collapses because you can't selectively condemn what is permanently archived; omission is technically impossible.
(8) therefore all three uses of history for life are destroyed at once.
(9) but these three are the only ways history serves life.
(10) so perfect recall doesn't just harm history — it annihilates its value for living entirely.
(11) yet perfect recall is just antiquarian history taken to its logical extreme.
(12) so antiquarian history, pursued consistently, undermines itself and the other two modes.
(13) therefore any culture that wants history to serve life must actively practice forgetting and selective ignorance.

it feels like there's a slip somewhere between (7) and (8), or maybe between (11) and (12). i really want to keep (3) — that critical history requires forgetting — because it's central to my character's arc. but i'm not sure if i'm just forcing nietzsche into a digital metaphor he wouldn't recognise. which step do you think i should reject, and what does giving it up cost me?
