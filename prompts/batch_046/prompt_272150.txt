I'm in a 40-person co-housing group rewriting our shared-use rules for the kitchen and workshop. Half want hard rules with automatic fines—leave a tool out, pay $20, no excuses. The other half want loose guidelines like 'keep it tidy' and a council that decides case by case. One member proposed a meta-rule: whatever strictness level we pick for the kitchen, we apply the exct opposite in the workshop—tight kitchen means loose workshop, and vice versa. He calls it balancing our 'normative temperature.' Something feels off about treating rule-following like a thermostat you flip between rooms, but I can't pin it down. Is his compromise conceptually coherent, or does it miss something deeper about why societies demand rules in the first place?
