Design a 90-minute workshop game for about 16 grad students that makes the moral equality of combatants in just war theory feel viscerally weird instead of textbook-abstract. Split them into two armies of 8; each gets a sealed envelope with a just-cause card (just or unjust) that they must not share. Give them tokens for soldiers and civilians and a payoff sheet: killing an enemy soldier gives +2 strategy but costs -3 morality if that soldier was actually fighting justly; killing civilians is -10 flat. They negotiate rules of engagement across three 10-minute rounds, then all envelopes open. The debrief should map their round-one confidence that they could 'just tell' who was just onto traditionalist thinking, connect the post-reveal panic to the revisionist claim that unjust soldiers aren't liable, and predict the failure mode where people peek or invent fake indicators of justice. Be frank that this can't reproduce the actual fear of facing an armed enemy who sees you as liable. Keep it casual, one typo is fine.
