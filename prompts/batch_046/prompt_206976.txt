I'd like a short poem, about sixteen lines with no fixed meter, structured so that its form does the philosophical work: each stanza should present a binary decision point for a term in the sequence, and the poem should branch without ever converging on a single definite path; the final stanza should place both continuations side by side without resolving between them. The poem should take seriously the distinction between weak negation — showing that assuming a predetermined outcome leads to contradiction — and strong negation — actually constructing the specific counterexample — and should leave that logical tension open rather than resolving it into either a sermon about openness or a shrug about indeterminacy. It needs to be accurate enough that someone familiar with choice sequences and Heyting's formalization would recognize the specific failure of excluded middle being staged, but it should also work as poetry for a reader who has never encountered constructivism. Please avoid citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts; the point is to render the idea precisely through the poem's structure.
