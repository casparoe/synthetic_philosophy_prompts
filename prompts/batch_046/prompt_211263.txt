Write a dialogue between two philosophers of law—Juno and Marek—debating whether a constitution can contain a "mirror amendment," meaning a clause that says any change to it must be ratified by an assembly constituted exactly as the clause originally describes. Juno argues this sets up a vicious reflexive loop that destroys legal authority unless there's an unamendable ground somewhere; Marek argues it's just a procedural knot that courts untie by applying the most recent valid enactment, with no need for metaphysical closure. They should actually respond to each other's points—no talking past one another, and neither should be a strawman. Make sure the occurence of reflexive self-reference is treated as a live problem for Juno but a harmless feature for Marek. Start with the dialogue itself, no preface or commentary. Write it in British English. Keep the tone casual—a typo or two won't hurt.
