Consider the engineering practice governing the construction of what its practitioners call a careenium: a layered architecture in which discrete symbolic structures are arranged as a formal control layer over an array of physical substrate units they term simmballs. In this practice, operators program the careenium with rules and then observe the simmballs reconfiguring in patterns that mirror the symbolic instructions. They consistently describe the relation not as correlation, implementation, or interpretation, but as the symbols literally pushing the substrate around—attributing downward causal efficacy to the careenium itself, independent of the local physical dynamics among the simmballs. This description is maintained even where a purely mechanistic account of the substrate interactions would fully predict the same behavior, and it is explicitly contrasted with views on which the symbolic layer is causally inert or merely descriptive. At the same time, the engineers deny that the careenium possesses consciousness or intentionality; they treat it as an abstract pattern that nonetheless exerts physical force.

What philosophical commitments about causation, physicalism, and the relation between symbolic-level and substrate-level descriptions does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory of downward causation or symbolic efficacy that would render their description coherent rather than metaphorical. Determine whether the practice strictly requires rejecting reductive physicalism, or whether it can be reconciled with a non-reductive but still physicalist framework, and identify which aspects of the practice are genuinely philosophically underdetermined—compatible with several rival metaphysical views. Finally, specify what would have to change in their design methodology and explanatory vocabulary if we replaced their implicit theory with a view on which symbols never push substrate, but are only interpreted post hoc by observers.
