Hey, I'm a phil prof advising a tiny press that puts out bilingual editions of 16th-c. french verse — Marot, Ronsard, that crowd — and we're trying to operationalize that 'le ton beau de marot' idea into an actual refereeing procedre. The core principle is that translation should chase the beautiful tone of the original, keeping form and emotional punch intact even when literal meaning has to bend; I'm also injecting a specific formal-resonance constaint, namely that the phonetic texture — density of nasals, sibilance, etc. — should be mirrored, not just rhyme and meter. I need a reusable cheklist, basically ordered tests in words, that a referee can run in about 25 minutes per submission. They see roughly four a month; stakes are accept/revise/reject, and the editor-in-chief has to sign off. The referees know english poetry cold but don't all read old french, so nothing can depend on source-language fluency. The checklist needs to handle hard cases: rhyme scheme intact but emotional register flattened; emotional match perfect but stanza form broken; and a version that preserves the sound profile so precisely it sounds like parody. Apply it to three hypotheticals: (1) perfect ABBA rhyme with dead feeling — this is the one I'd personally want to accept but the procedre should reject it; (2) broken form but alive spirit; (3) phonetic-texture match that becomes ridiculous. Mark every point where judgment can't be eliminated, be honest about misfires, and say flat-out what it can't do. Also please separate what's settled about this view from what's genuinely contested among people who study translation — don't present the framing behind that phrase, or my added sound-texture rule, as consensus.
