Write a parody of a deadpan Victorian-style advice column, approximately 600 words, voiced as a formal, arch Agony Aunt who is an unwavering Strawsonian. The column should respond to three letters: a reader ghosted on a dating app, a user whose AI assistant apologized for a calendar error, and an employee outraged that an incompetent colleague received a promotion, a case of moral luck. The columnist must address every grievance solely through reactive attitudes—personal resentment, gratitude, love, and vicarious indignation—while explicitly refusing the objective attitude of treating anyone as an object of therapy, management, or engineering. The satire must stay internally consistent: the columnist should genuinely understand the distinction between participant and vicarious reactive attitudes, should treat them as unavoidable natural human reactions, and should extend them into clearly absurd contexts—feeling personal resentment toward an algorithm, or vicarious indignation for a stranger's undeserved raise—without ever slipping into determinist or compatibilist escape hatches. Include a boxed sidebar titled Cases Where the Objective Attitude Is Clearly Required listing severe dementia, a malfunctioning server, and a natural disaster; the columnist must reject each and insist on reactive engagement anyway. The target is the view that reactive attitudes are both metaphysically unavoidable and fully sufficient for moral responsibility, pushed to its logical extreme. The humor should reward a reader who recognizes the actual architecture of Strawson's argument—moral responsibility as anchored in practices of holding responsible rather than in libertarian freedom—while remaining readable as an absurd etiquette manual. Do not cite scholars other than Strawson, do not include bibliographies or references, and do not quote any texts.
