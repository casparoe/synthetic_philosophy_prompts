Consider the following argument about the consistency of Karl Popper's philosophy of science with his own demarcation criterion.

(1) By Popper's criterion of demarcation, a theory counts as scientific only if it is empirically falsifiable in principle—there must exist some possible observation that could contradict it.

(2) Darwinian evolutionary theory, taken as a universal explanatory framework, is not falsifiable in this sense. For any observed biological trait, absence, or distribution, an explanation can always be constructed post hoc by appealing to natural selection, genetic drift, developmental constraint, or historical contingency.

(3) Therefore Popper classifies Darwinism not as an empirical science but as a metaphysical research programme: heuristically valuable, but not subject to empirical refutation as a whole.

(4) Popper's evolutionary epistemology—developed in works such as Objective Knowledge—proposes that the growth of scientific knowledge follows a structurally Darwinian logic: theories are conjectured (variation), subjected to critical testing and empirical checks (selection), and the survivors are retained provisionally.

(5) If this mechanism is genuinely Darwinian, then evolutionary epistemology inherits the same unfalsifiability. Consider a historical episode in which a well-corroborated theory is abandoned not because of empirical failure but because of a shift in aesthetic or metaphysical preference—say, the rejection of deterministic mechanics despite its predictive success. The evolutionary model can accommodate this only by relabeling the shift as a change in selection environment, which makes the framework immune to refutation by any actual history of science.

(6) Yet Popper treats evolutionary epistemology not as a metaphysical programme but as part of the philosophy of science, expecting it to guide and justify scientific practice, and he places its objects—theories—in World 3, where they are subject to logical rather than biological selection.

(7) So either evolutionary epistemology is falsifiable in a way that Darwinism is not—which requires identifying a specific structural disanalogy that saves it, likely tied to the logical nature of World 3 selection—or Popper applies his demarcation criterion inconsistently, allowing his own framework the exemption he denies biology.

The argument seems to show that Popper's philosophy of science undermines itself. Where exactly does it slip?
