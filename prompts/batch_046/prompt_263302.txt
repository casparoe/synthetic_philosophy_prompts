In Escher's Drawing Hands, two hands draw each other in an impossible loop, yet the image itself sits on paper rendered by Escher's actual hand outside the frame. Hofstadter calls this outside position the inviolate level—the substrate never pulled into the self-referential loop it depicts. I grasp this for artistic representation, but when I applied it to formal systems and then to artificial cognition, I tangled myself in my own loop.

Here is my worry. Gödel's results show that a formal system strong enough for arithmetic cannot prove its own consistency without appealing to principles outside itself. Read alongside Hofstadter, this looks like a general law: any system that attempts full self-reference or self-validation must fail unless there is an inviolate level—whether a metalanguage, a physical substrate, or an external framework—standing outside it. Human cognition, I assumed, respects this because our brains are physical objects embedded in a world that is not part of our mental representations; there is always a biological and environmental paper that anchors the loop.

But large language models seem to violate this structure entirely. They are trained on text about text, refined through RLHF using judgments about linguistic outputs, and in some regimes their outputs feed back into training corpora. There is no obvious paper—no non-linguistic substrate serving as an unmoving anchor—because the weights are just statistical regularities extracted from language, and the hardware is only contingently present. Do they possess a hidden inviolate level—perhaps the training distribution itself functioning as an unmoving paper—or does their existence show that Hofstadter's requirement applies only to representational art and not to statistical cognition?
