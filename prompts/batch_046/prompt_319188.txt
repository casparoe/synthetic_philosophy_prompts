While researching a feature for my magazine on how formal strategic reasoning is being adopted in institutional mediation, I have become interested in whether the logic of rationalizability can be made practical for contract auditors who lack access to private belief hierarchies.

I need you to operationalize the principle of iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies—including domination in mixed strategies—into a concise, reusable audit procedure that a mediator could run during a live multi-party contract review. The procedure should state, in strict sequence, how to eliminate dominated pure strategies; how to test whether a proposed mixture is dominated by another mixture or by a pure strategy; the exact point where the audit must halt because further elimination requires common knowledge of rationality that exceeds observable behavior; and how to flag an outcome that is rationalizable yet conflicts with the mediator's independent fairness judgment. Then apply this audit to three cases: a bilateral salary negotiation with hidden reservation values; a trilateral supply agreement in which one party may randomize delivery schedules through an internal lottery invisible to the others; and a scenario where the procedure endorses an agreement that the mediator's intuition rejects. Please present the entire response as a numbered list of exactly five items, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
