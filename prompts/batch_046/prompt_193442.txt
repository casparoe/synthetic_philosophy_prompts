I've been working through Dennett's critique of the Cartesian theater and his multiple drafts model of consciousness in a graduate seminar that pairs philosophy of mind with clinical neuropsychology, and I'm struck by how little the core disagreement has shifted despite decades of empirical work on parallel processing and temporal binding. The defenders of a unified 'theater' — by which I mean the idea that conscious experience requires a singular, integrated presentation to a central observer — and the proponents of distributed, multi-track processing both seem to concede that there is no unique anatomical audience for all mental content. Yet they draw radically different philosophical conclusions from that concession. Before I commit to writing a long-form essay on this, I want to understand the structure of the impasse itself: is it primarily a verbal disagreement, in which one side uses 'unity' to mean phenomenological coherence and the other uses it to mean functional integration for action and report? Or is it a substantive ontological dispute about whether consciousness is best modeled as a property of a system at an instant, versus an emergent narrative constructed retrospectively? And might there be a hidden empirical disagreement — perhaps about the precise temporal dynamics of interhemispheric binding, or about how anosognosia and split-brain phenomena should be interpreted — that the philosophical vocabulary has obscured?

Please offer a diagnosis supported by what each side actually commits to, and then tell me, given that diagnosis, what kind of progress is actually possible. Should we prioritize a conceptual distinction that dissolves the conflict, a formal framework for distributed representation without a central bottleneck, or targeted empirical studies of narrative self-modeling in clinical populations? Defend your prioritization by explaining what criteria you're using — tractability, downstream consequences for theories of personal identity, or the likelihood of actually resolving the disagreement rather than supplying new ammunition for both sides — and specify what result, if it arrived, would genuinely shift the debate rather than being assimilated into it.
