Since leaving the lab I've gotten obsessed with historical materialism and now I've argued myself into what feels like a trap. Here's the chain: (1) The economic base determines the superstructure, including science and philosphy. (2) So Marxism, being part of the superstructure, is determined by 19th-century capitalist relations. (3) That makes it an ideological reflex, not an objective science. (4) The Marxist reply is that Marxism grasps real historical movement, so it's scientific despite its origin. (5) But then they need a criteron to separate true science from ideology, and that criteron is itself part of the superstructure and determined by the base. (6) So either Marxism is false ideology, or it's true but applies to itself and destroys its own claim to objectivity. (7) I know Engels admitted relative autonomy in his letters, and Althusser said the base is only determinate "in the last instance," but that sounds like saying gravity explains bird flight in the last instance even though aerodynamics does all the actual work. Where does my chain break? Is step (3) wrong because determination doesn't equal falsification? Or is (5) the weak link, assuming the superstructure is one uniform block? I'd like to hold onto the idea that theories can feedback and change material conditions—my old career convinced me of that—but I don't want to slide into idealism.
