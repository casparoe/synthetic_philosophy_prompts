Write a proposal for a one-semester philosophical research project—approximately 5,000 words—on a deliberately narrow variant of the paradox of tragedy. Restrict the domain to non-representational and non-narrative works: specifically, instrumental music in minor modes and abstract visual art that audiences consistently describe as sorrowful or tragic, despite the absence of any identifiable character, plot, or situation that could serve as an object of pity. State the precise research question: if the standard paradox depends on explaining why we enjoy negative emotions directed at fictional sufferings, does the absence of such a sufferer dissolve the paradox, or does it relocate it as a puzzle about why we value the subjective experience of sorrow or existential weight in itself? Survey the standard explanatory families—cathartic release, emotional conversion or transfiguration, compensatory control, and meta-response theories—arguing that each implicitly presupposes a representational target and therefore leaves this restricted domain unsettled. Propose a conceptual method rather than an empirical one: develop a fine-grained taxonomy that distinguishes object-directed pity from non-object-directed melancholy, awe-weighted sorrow, and somatic emotional resonance, then test whether any reconstructed version of the standard mechanisms can apply once the representational object is removed. Specify what a useful result would look like—perhaps a principled demarcation between artworks for which the paradox genuinely arises and those for which it does not—along with an explicit failure condition, such as discovering that all apparently non-object-directed responses can be reduced to displaced object-directed pity after all. Assess the realistic scope for a single semester, explain why further conceptual clarification rather than audience data is the appropriate tool here, and avoid requesting any bibliography, citation list, or literature review by named source. The deliverable should be a self-contained argument that a funding committee or advisor could evaluate without consulting external references.
