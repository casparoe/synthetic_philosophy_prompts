Write a deadpan parody of an institutional review-board memo, about 600 words, with numbered bureaucratic sections and a sidebar table. The memo comes from a fictional university “Committee on Distributed Cognition” and evaluates whether the ant colony known as “Aunt Hillary”—the collective organism in the classic ant-fugue thought experiment—should be granted moral-patient status and research-subject protections. The satire should exaggerate the real philosophical tension: we apply individual-centered tests (mirror recognition, unified reportability, integrated-information metrics) to entities whose cognition is genuinely distributed across thousands of agents with no central controller. Invent and describe a specific evaluation protocol called the “Fugue Coherence Test,” in which the colony’s pheromone-mediated response to contrapuntal structure is scored for global-workspace integration, and include a sidebar table assigning “partial personhood credits” (e.g., 0.0003 per ant). Keep the voice entirely deadpan—take both the functionalist intuition that the colony might think and the bureaucratic impulse to quantify consciousness seriously, without ever breaking character or stating the objections outright. The piece should work for a reader familiar with collective-mind debates and for one who isn’t, and should contain no bibliographic references, citations, or appeals to external sources.
