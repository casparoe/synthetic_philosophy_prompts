I have been thinking about those thought experiments where someone is responsible for an act even though they could not have done otherwise — the ones involving a hidden controller who never actually steps in. As I understand it, the idea is that if you make a choice for your own reasons, it does not matter that some external force was standing by to make you do it anyway; since that force never acted, your responsibility remains intact.

Now I have been turning over a different scenario in my head, and I am not sure whether it fits this pattern or breaks it. Imagine a person — let us call her Elena — who has what I would describe as a kind of built-in moral autopilot. It is not a conscious rule she follows, but a deep, automatic habit of mind that quietly reshapes her reasoning whenever she is about to choose selfishly. It never takes over in an obvious way; it simply ensures that, by the time she reaches a decision, the generous option feels like her own. Because this autopilot is part of her, not an outsider like the hidden controller, it is always running. Had she started to drift towards cruelty, the autopilot would have redirected her attention before she could even form the intention.

My confusion is this. If the hidden-controller case shows that responsibility survives when a backup mechanism is present but unused, then Elena's autopilot seems to be a backup mechanism that is permanently present — only it is inside her. So does the same reasoning apply? Does it mean Elena is not truly responsible for her kindness, because her choice was guaranteed by this internal safeguard? Or is there a hidden distinction — perhaps between something that is part of you and something that interferes with you — that saves her responsibility, even though the original thought experiment never mentions such a distinction?

I worry I have conflated two different things, but I cannot see where the line should be drawn. Is Frankfurt's point only about external interference that never happens, or does it also speak to cases where our own psychology makes certain choices practically inevitable?
