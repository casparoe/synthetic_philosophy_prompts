You are a professional metaphysician specializing in material ontology and conceptual translation across disciplinary vocabularies. Before you begin translating, take a moment to brainstorm the core commitments of both the statue-and-clay debate and relational database architecture, identifying where the mappings are likely to be strained. Think carefully and proceed step by step.

I am fluent in relational database normalization, entity-relationship modeling, schema evolution, and temporal or versioned tables. I want you to translate the four main positions in the material-constitution debate about a statue made of clay — specifically (i) the strict-identity view that there is only one object, best characterized as clay; (ii) the constitution view that the statue and the clay are distinct objects that coincide spatially; (iii) the artifact-eliminativist view that only the clay exists and the statue is a non-fundamental projection or practical fiction; and (iv) the four-dimensionalist or perdurantist view that treats the statue and the clay as different four-dimensional spatiotemporal worms or as entities with different temporal parts — into the vocabulary of database entities, tuples, schemas, foreign keys, referential integrity, normalization forms, and versioned history tables.

For each position, provide the translation: which database concepts correspond to the statue, the clay, their relationship, and their persistence conditions? Then report the residue: which metaphysical claims translate cleanly into database operations, which require distortion or the introduction of non-standard database constructs (for example, treating one tuple as simultaneously two entities with different primary keys), and which have no expression at all within standard relational theory?

Crucially, inject the following specific scenario that is not the standard classroom example: suppose the statue is melted down into an unformed ingot, the material is stored for years with no statue-shaped form, and then the exact same clay is recast into an identical statue. In database terms, frame this as a schema migration involving a soft delete, a history-table entry, a foreign-key reference to the original material batch, and eventually a new tuple with the same descriptive attributes but a different surrogate primary key. Ask whether the metaphysical positions can distinguish this case from continuous existence, and whether the database vocabulary forces a decision — for instance, that referential integrity demands either one persistent record or two — that the metaphysical debate treats as open.

Finally, evaluate whether the untranslatable residue points to a genuine limitation of the database framework in capturing material constitution, or whether it instead reveals that one or more of the metaphysical positions was relying on an implicit assumption that becomes visible only when forced into a vocabulary where identity is strictly tied to primary-key persistence and referential constraints.
