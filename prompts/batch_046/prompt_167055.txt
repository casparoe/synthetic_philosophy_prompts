So I want you to write the full desiderata for a theory that explains the logical structure of this tradition—the cluster of thinkers around Hui Shi and Gongsun Long and their puzzles about naming, sameness, and difference. List the requirements: the textual cases any interpretation must handle (the white-horse argument, the separation of compound properties such as hard-and-white, and the infinite-divisibility paradoxes about measurement), the logical distinctions it must preserve without collapsing (for instance, class membership versus property attribution, sameness versus qualitative similarity), the comparative-logic constraints it must respect without anachronistically importing Aristotelian or modern formal categories, and the interpretive hazards it must avoid. Crucially, inject one new constraint: call it the "semantic autonomy condition," which holds that in this tradition the act of naming does not merely label a pre-given reality but actively establishes the boundary conditions within which predication is valid, so that any adequate theory must explain how a name can exclude instances without denying their existence. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or failure mode behind it, and flag which two requirements you suspect are in tension with each other. Then identify the single requirement that does the most damage to the standard universalist-versus-particularist framing, and briefly score three interpretive approaches—the metaphysical universalist reading, the nominalist logical-puzzle reading, and a "predicative boundary" view that treats the paradoxes as rules for semantic exclusion—against your list. The deliverable should be precise enough that someone without specialization in pre-Qin philosophy could use it to adjudicate interpretations, and it should state explicitly what the criteria cannot do (for example, whether they can settle the historical-psychological question of what Gongsun Long actually intended).
