(4) here's the twist: what about a claim that's unfalsifiable not in principle, but only because testing it is computationaly intractable (think multiverse predictions or deep architecture hypotheses that'd take centuries to check)? Should that count the same as something contradicted in principle?

Build it with about 6-7 leaves, each split labeled by the claim that divides it, not by philosopher names. For each leaf, name the position (or write "unnamed" if nobody defends it) and give one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Flag any branches that collapse into each other — i.e., answer combinations that always travel together and can't be separated. Make the diagnostic questions concrete enough that someone can answer without knowing the jargon. Don't ask for citations, bibliographies, or further reading — just the tree and its reading instructions.
