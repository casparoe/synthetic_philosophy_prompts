Given that Gödel's 1949 rotating-universe solution demonstrates that closed timelike curves intersect every worldline whenever the vorticity vector is non-vanishing, and given that Gödel took the corresponding failure of global temporal congruence to show that objective time is an illusion projected from local frames, I want to press a tension that seems overlooked. If there is genuinely no globally consistent ordering of 'before' and 'after' in such a spacetime, then a causal theory of temporal direction — which defines 'earlier' by local causal connectibility — must either tolerate circular causation along those curves or restrict its domain to causally well-behaved sub-regions. So why do philosophers of physics still discuss causal theories of time as candidates for the fundamental structure of spacetime, rather than conceding that Gödel's metric proves causation is irreducibly parochial and therefore inapplicable at cosmological scales? And does the chronological protection conjecture, which aims to rule out such curves on physical grounds, miss the deeper point that even the logical consistency of their presence undermines the universality required by any causal account of time?
