Analyze this episode through the lens of Avicenna's argument. I do not want a historical chronicle or biographical detail about the investigators; I want a disciplined conceptual mapping. Identify precisely which sensory modalities the tank eliminated and which—interoception, proprioception, vestibular feedback—the subjects still retained, and determine whether Avicenna's argument requires the absence of all bodily input or only the absence of the five external senses. Then, drawing on the documented outcomes—disorientation, visual and auditory hallucinations, and in many cases a dissolution rather than a sharpening of self-boundary—evaluate whether these results falsify Avicenna's claim that the soul necessarily knows itself without bodily mediation, or whether they merely demonstrate that the experiment never achieved the true conditions of the hypothetical. Finally, consider what the episode reveals about the argument's scope: was Avicenna advancing an empirical prediction about consciousness under sensory removal, or was he advancing a logical point about the conditions of self-reference that no laboratory arrangement could confirm or undermine? Be explicit about where the analogy holds, where it collapses, and whether the experimental failure constitutes a genuine philosophical objection or only a category mistake.
