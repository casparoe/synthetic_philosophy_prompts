I have been reflecting on whether my younger self should still control my older self, and I have reached four judgments that I cannot reconcile. One: if at thirty I sign a directive refusing artificial life support should I ever be permanently unconscious, and at seventy I am in that state with no awareness whatsoever, doctors ought to honor the document. Two: if at thirty I instruct that I be withdrawn from care should I develop dementia that erases my recognition of family, but at eighty I have that dementia yet clearly enjoy eating and music, the instruction ought to be ignored. Three: if at twenty-five I legally pledge my future assets to a religious cause and refuse treatment for leaving it, then at forty-five I have left and need both the assets and treatment, I should be freed from the pledge. Four: if at thirty I direct that should I ever suffer a severe mental illness that makes me terrified and begging for release, I must still be kept in safe institutional care, and at fifty-five I am indeed begging, the directive ought to be enforced. Is there a single principle about the authority of an earlier self that accommodates all four, or does consistency require abandoning one? Please also say what this principle implies if every one of my beliefs and values is slowly replaced over two decades by a gradual neural intervention. Deliver your answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
