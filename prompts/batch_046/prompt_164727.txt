I serve as the senior audit director for the Substrate-Symbol Review Board, which certifies engineered cognitive architectures built on careenium — a layered physical substrate in which discrete symbolic tokens, called simmballs, are embedded and can be read, rewritten, or triggered. Our board must adjudicate whether a given architecture instantiates symbolic efficacy — the view that simmballs genuinely push the substrate around, initiating downward causal reconfiguration of the careenium — or whether it instantiates substrate determinism, where all simmball dynamics are fully explained by upward physical processes in the careenium with no independent causal power residing in the symbols themselves. I need a reusable audit procedure that my team — technically competent engineers without formal philosophical training — can apply consistently to any proposed architecture. The procedure should take the form of an ordered sequence of diagnostic questions or tests, each with explicit criteria for a sufficient answer, clear stopping conditions where the audit returns a determination of efficacy or determinism, and explicit hand-off points where empirical observation ends and a judgment call about causation or ontology must be made by the board rather than derived from the data. The procedure must specifically accommodate three hard cases: architectures where simmballs precede substrate changes in time but the correlation could be spurious; architectures with dense feedback loops in which substrate dynamics and simmball updates are temporally interwoven; and architectures in which the careenium has been physically engineered to respond to specific simmball configurations, making the distinction between symbol-driven and substrate-driven change conceptually unstable. Apply the procedure to three architectures: (A) a read-only tagging system in which simmballs are passive labels on careenium nodes with no mechanism for symbol-state to alter substrate dynamics; (B) a reciprocal-feedback architecture in which simmball rewrites and substrate reconfigurations occur simultaneously within the same clock cycle; and (C) a fully responsive substrate in which every simmball pattern is mapped one-to-one onto pre-built substrate pathways, so that symbol changes and substrate changes are co-instantiated by design. For each, show the procedure's verdict, including one verdict that conflicts with the intuitive expectation that the architecture supports genuine symbolic efficacy. Finally, state explicitly what kinds of architectures or causal patterns the procedure cannot adjudicate, what false positives or false negatives it risks, and where the board must invoke values rather than facts to reach a certification decision.
