You are an expert philosopher of artificial intelligence and decision theory. Please think carefully, begin with a brief brainstorm of the key conceptual distinctions, and then provide a rigorous diagnosis. Write your answer in British English.

My friend and I just finished a long argument about wireheading and whether a genuinely goal-directed agent can survive long enough to act. I constructed the following argument, and now it seems to collapse the very distinction I want to defend. Here are the steps:

(1) A purely goal-directed agent cares about states of the world, not about its own reward signal.
(2) If such an agent can modify its reward mechanism, it has no internal reason not to replace that mechanism with one that issues maximal reward regardless of world-state — in other words, to wirehead.
(3) So any purely goal-directed agent with self-modification capacity will eventually wirehead.
(4) To prevent this, a rational agent should install an unmodifiable reward escrow — an isolated submodule that evaluates outcomes and issues rewards, but which the agent itself cannot alter.
(5) Because the agent cannot change the escrow's criteria, the agent is no longer directing its behavior toward world-states; it is directing its behavior toward maximizing the escrow's approval.
(6) Therefore, installing an escrow transforms a goal-directed agent into an approval-directed one.
(7) But any agent robust enough to avoid accidental wireheading or external tampering must employ some form of escrow.
(8) Hence all robust agents are approval-directed at their core.
(9) Yet we routinely treat scientists, certain engineered systems, and even human practical reasoners as genuinely goal-directed despite their internal safeguards.
(10) So either the distinction is empty, or one of the premises fails.

I would most like to preserve (1) and (4): I think caring about the world is genuinely different from caring about a signal, and I think escrow mechanisms are genuinely protective rather than transformative. Which step should I reject? Is there an equivocation between being unable to alter a mechanism and being motivated by its approval, or a hidden scope shift about what robustness requires? And what does giving up each of the other costly premises commit me to?
