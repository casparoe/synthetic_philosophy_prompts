ok so im mapping out my views on self-trust and the Löbian obstacle for a small reading group. heres the set:

1. rational agents ought to be able to reflect on their own reasoning and correct errors, including meta-level mistakes about how they reason.
2. there's a genuine Löbian obstacle: a consistent formal system cant contain a universal self-trust predicate over all its own outputs without collapsing.
3. in practice we do trust our reasoning, and i think that trust is rational, not just a heuristic.
4. the right response is domain- or time-restricted self-trust — i trust my logic today but dont automatically extend that to whatever i derive next tuesday.
5. i want to model this with indexed operators T_t, where each only applies to beliefs formed before time t, rather than one global trust predicate.
6. total absence of self-trust is self-undermining: you couldnt justify revising the belief that you lack self-trust.
7. reflective equilibrium is the right method in philosophy, so our own considered judgments should carry real evidential weight.

im especially worried about (2) vs (3) being in conflict, and whether (7) clashes with (4) or (5). but maybe some tensions are only apparent. can you audit the set? rank the real tensions by severity, say for each pair what has to give and what the cheapest fix costs. also flag any pair that only looks tense but dissolves once you distinguish the right levels. dont lecture me on the theorem — just check consistency.
