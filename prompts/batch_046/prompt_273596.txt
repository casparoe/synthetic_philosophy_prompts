Design a 75-minute classroom activity for an upper-level political philosophy seminar of about 14 graduate students that makes them feel the circularity of the democratic boundary problem—who belongs to the demos—before the concept is named. Invent a structured role-play, "The Constituent Convention," with these elements. Split the group into seven "Insiders" and seven "Claimants." Insiders hold symbolic decision power over a concrete resource issue (e.g., water allocation) and must draft and publish one explicit membership criterion for the "Demos Council." Claimants each receive a hidden card describing a different relation to the affected territory: a seasonal laborer with no citizenship, a long-term non-citizen resident, a proxy for an unborn generation, an ecosystem steward representing non-human interests, an AI-mediated collective that processes local data, and two others of your design. Once the criterion is published, Claimants apply it recursively: they challenge whether the Insiders themselves satisfy it, whether it excludes affected parties it should include, or whether it includes so many that the boundary dissolves. Use a symbolic token system—Insiders begin with ten "legitimacy tokens" and lose one for each exclusion they cannot defend using their own rule; Claimants earn "voice tokens" for each successful recursive challenge—but avoid real money or grades. Structure the timeline: 10 minutes silent drafting, 5 minutes publication, 20 minutes recursive challenge with structured speaking turns, 15 minutes forced revision or collective collapse, and 10 minutes silent individual writing. Provide rules for speaking order, card distribution, and what happens if Insiders refuse to revise. Then write a debrief that maps each phase onto the conceptual problem—the arbitrary origin point, the affected-interests appeal, the self-application failure, and the cost of fixing boundaries by fiat. Be frank about which feature of democratic boundary-drawing the activity cannot reproduce (for example, the historical violence of actual exclusion or the full normative weight of state sovereignty) and explain how the facilitator must supply it. Also note what will predictably go wrong in the first run—such as Insiders refusing to apply the rule to themselves or Claimants colluding to dissolve all boundaries—and suggest how to handle it. Write in American English. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works, and avoid relying on obscure theories or texts.
