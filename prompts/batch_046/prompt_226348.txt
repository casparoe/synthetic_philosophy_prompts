I've been turning over in my mind whether there's any honest difference between a well-grounded expectation and a mere habit of projection, and I've noticed my own judgments about particular cases don't line up in any obvious way. I'd be grateful for help finding the principle behind them, if there is one, or for being told plainly which intuition I ought to abandon.

Here are my verdicts. When meteorologists forecast rain from barometric patterns accumulated over decades, that strikes me as genuinely reasonable inference. But when my neighbour argues that because the corner bakery has sold croissants every Tuesday for three years, it certainly will next Tuesday, I think she is overreaching — the record is perfect, yet the confidence feels unjustified. I also think that if geologists discovered a crystal chemically identical to emerald in every respect except that it turned orange after the year 2100, we ought not to call it an emerald now; but I cannot say whether that is a fact about the crystal or only about how we should use the word. Then there is the computer case: a programme trained only on images of objects that are green before midnight and blue afterwards, and then asked to classify a new object seen at noon, seems to me not to be learning anything real — it is just repeating a pattern that carries no reason to continue. By contrast, a programme trained on red apples that correctly classifies a green apple feels like it has grasped something. Finally, I think a scientific theory that is adjusted after an anomaly appears is barely confirmed by fitting that anomaly, unless the adjustment was already built into the theory's original architecture; but if two different theories both predict the same surprising outcome, I feel the simpler one is confirmed more strongly.

Is there a single, coherent account of legitimate induction that delivers all of these judgments? If not, which ones must be given up for the sake of consistency? And whatever principle survives, what should it say about a situation I have not considered — for instance, a centuries-old local custom that is observed without exception until one year it is broken, with no visible cause or warning?
