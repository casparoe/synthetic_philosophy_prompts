Compare psi-ontic and psi-epistemic interpretations of quantum mechanics with respect to the following intermediate thesis: the wavefunction is real, but not as a material field in three-dimensional space; instead, it functions as a modal-structural law that determines correlation patterns among possible measurement outcomes without being localized or substantial. For each interpretation, determine whether this framing can be coherently maintained, or whether it necessarily collapses into either an ontological commitment to hidden variables (the psi-ontic pole) or an epistemic state defined over an unrepresented underlying reality (the psi-epistemic pole). Specifically, analyze how each view treats wavefunction collapse under this relational reading—does it dissolve the measurement problem, or does it relocate the problem into the ontology of modal relations? Conclude by stating which interpretation, if either, genuinely accommodates this middle path, and identify the precise conceptual tension that prevents a stable synthesis.
