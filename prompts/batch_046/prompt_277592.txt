Daniel Dennett introduced the concept to show that behavioral complexity alone does not entail cognitive flexibility; genuine freedom, he maintained, requires a second-order capacity to represent one's own representations as revisable objects of evaluation. Consequently, any system—whether an insect governed by fixed patterns or an artificial neural network that reproduces statistical regularities without genuine representational revision—lacks the architectural prerequisites for free will and, by extension, for moral agency, no matter how sophisticated its outputs appear."
