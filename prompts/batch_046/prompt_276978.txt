ok so ive been thinking about this thing that happens in forecasting tournaments and history podcasts when people talk about whether big states fall apart. they always start by putting the country in some historical bucket -- like 'post-colonial federations' or 'land empires with multi-ethnic armies' -- and they pull out a base rate, say 30% collapse within two generations. then people start listing ways the modern case is different: it has nuclear weapons, or a universal digital id system, or a currency union. but instead of saying 'ok that means we need a different bucket with its own base rate,' they keep the original bucket and just tack on adjustments. like minus 10% for nukes, plus 8% for digital surveillance backlash, whatever. the bucket was picked because it sounds right in the first sentence, not because anyone checked if the adjustments actually map onto the causal structure. i keep seeing this and i dont know if its just the reference class problem dressed up, or if theres a specific term for the 'anchor on a historical category then patch it with ad hoc modifiers instead of reclassifying' move. is there an established name for that? and if not, could we call it something like 'base-rate anchoring with auxiliary patching'? i want to know what its edges are -- like, how is it different from normal bayesian updating where you also start with a prior and then condition? here it feels like the prior is being protected by making the conditions narrative adjustments rather than structural ones. also, is this related to the outside view at all, or is it a corruption of it?
