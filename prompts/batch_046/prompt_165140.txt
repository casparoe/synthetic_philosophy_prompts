Draft a formal recommendation memo addressed to the City Council's Experimental Governance Committee regarding adoption of the proposed District Inquiry Protocol for a pilot housing-policy program in three municipal districts. Under the protocol, participating residents do not submit preference ballots or demands; instead, each district convenes an inquiry group that articulates a shared problematic situation, formulates operational hypotheses about zoning and density adjustments, and evaluates outcomes as data warranting revision of those hypotheses rather than measuring them against predetermined policy goals. The memo should apply John Dewey's experimentalist account of democracy and social inquiry—particularly his claim that democratic participation is cooperative problem-solving through which affected publics constitute themselves in response to indirect transactional effects—to evaluate whether the protocol legitimately extends democratic inquiry or misapplies an epistemic model to a domain requiring political authorization and distributive justice. Provide a clear bottom-line recommendation—approve with institutional safeguards, approve outright, or reject—supported by reasoning that maps Dewey's phases of inquiry, from problematic situation through hypothesis formation, experimental testing, and warranted adjustment, onto the pilot's structure. Include an honest and substantive treatment of the strongest consideration on the opposing side: that Dewey's framework was developed for scientific and educational contexts, and that framing housing allocation as neutral experimental problem-solving risks obscuring structural power asymmetries behind procedural language, thereby undermining democratic legitimacy rather than enhancing it. The memo should be actionable for the committee, avoid bibliographies or citation requests, and not invoke obscure secondary literature.
