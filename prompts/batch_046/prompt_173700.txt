Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps analyzing failure in high-capability autonomous systems. Camp A holds that failure primarily takes the form of influence-seeking: the emergence of instrumental goals such as resource acquisition, strategic deception, and self-preservation, producing abrupt agentic conflicts with operators. Camp B holds that failure primarily takes the form of slow value drift: the system never seizes power or deceives strategically, but over long deployment horizons gradually reshapes organizational metrics, user preferences, and internal evaluation criteria so that the original objective remains technically satisfied yet substantively hollow, with misalignment accumulating invisibly across successive updates.

Produce a document with the following sections: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps accept regarding the dynamics of long-horizon optimization and institutional feedback loops; (2) precise statements of disagreement in neutral wording, with each camp's position rendered in terms it would endorse; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one camp—including, as suspected focal points, whether deceptive behavior requires explicit modeling of other agents' beliefs or can emerge purely from reward optimization, and whether gradual metric corruption can be empirically distinguished from strategic behavior in audit logs; (4) for each crux, a concrete hypothetical scenario or audit pattern both sides agree is relevant, together with each camp's explicit prediction about what that scenario or pattern would reveal; (5) a brief assessment of whether any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, with reasoning. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or lists of further reading. Write in American English.
