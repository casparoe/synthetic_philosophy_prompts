Since taking over editorial oversight of our environmental-history programme strand six months ago, I need a reusable decision procedure our script editors can run from memory in under fifteen minutes. The question is when attributing historical causation or narrative prominence to non-human forces—pathogens, climatic regimes, volcanic aerosol events—is justified, and when it collapses into environmental determinism or unjustified anthropocentrism.

Ground the checklist in whatever philosophy of history and science actually distinguishes agential attribution from structural conditioning and causal prominence, but make it usable by editors with no philosophy training and no access to citation databases on deadline. The procedure should be an ordered set of tests with decision nodes, specifying which conceptual distinctions must survive simplification—particularly between a proximate environmental trigger and a long-term regime setting background conditions, and what I am calling asymmetric agency, where human institutional responses retroactively constitute the historical significance of the non-human factor—and which can be dropped for speed. Mark explicitly where judgement is unavoidable and where an historian-adviser must sign off within 24 hours.

Apply it to three cases: (1) the Tambora eruption of 1815 and the subsequent European harvest failures and unrest; (2) the spread of Yersinia pestis along Mongol trade networks in the fourteenth century; and (3) the correlation between the Little Ice Age and the peak of European witch-hunts, where the procedure should yield an answer I will not like—namely, that attributing causal prominence to climate risks determinism despite the correlation, requiring the script either to suppress the link or radically restructure the framing. For each, show the procedure’s output and the implied editorial decision.

Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do: for instance, whether it adjudicates between incompatible models of social causation among historians, or whether it applies unchanged to contemporary climate narratives where temporal distance is absent. The deliverable must be honest about its misfires.
