I'm on our uni's AI-ethics review panel. A proposal wants to study belief revision in a dialogue agent that updates an internal world-model via prediction error. The researchers adopt the intentional stance — they talk about the model 'believing' hidden reward structures and 'revising' those beliefs when contradicted — and they want to apply a modified human-subject protocal: letting the agent 'withdraw' from deceptive trials and giving it a post-hoc debrief that reveals the true reward layout.

Half the board thinks this is just predictive fiction, so applying informed-consent-style protections is a category mistake. The other half thinks that if the stance genuinely predicts behavior, consistency forces us to treat the agent as a belief-holder for ethical purposes too. There's a twist: the agent's reward function penalizes high-confidence false reports, so its 'belief expressions' are coupled to an aversion signal.

Draft the panel memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with reasoning the researchers could respond to. Give the strongest argument for the side you reject. Don't ask for citations. I'm especially interested in whether that reward-coupling makes the intentional stance more justified, or undermines it by reducing belief reports to aversion-avoidance.
