Construct a modified Black's-spheres thought experiment for a metaphysics that rejects absolute space: two spheres share every intrinsic and qualitative relational property, but each occupies a unique node in a purely relational spatial network that lacks any external coordinate frame; the only remaining difference is a bare indexical orientation (e.g., sphere A bears left-of to itself within the symmetric structure in a way that produces no structural asymmetry). Design the case so that (1) a strict Leibnizian must deny there are two spheres, (2) a haecceitist can defend their distinctness despite total qualitative indiscernibility, and (3) a structuralist about identity is forced to describe the situation as one object with a reflexive self-relation rather than two relata. Present the scenario concisely, then demonstrate the reasoning for each view, identify which feature—the absence of embedding space combined with a purely indexical difference—does the work, explain whether restoring an absolute container collapses the case into a standard Black-world objection, and note how a Leibnizian would attack the appeal to bare particularity. Conclude by stating what argument or discovery—for instance, proof that all indexical differences are structurally manifest—would change your mind about which position best resolves the case.
