I've come to think Dawkins-style memetics misses the point — culture evolves more like Lamarckian inheritance where acquired traits stick, and the 'meme' is mostly a heuristic. But my actual view is stricter: memeplexes are the real unit of selection, not individual memes or human hosts, and this explains why harmful ideologies persiste despite hurting carriers. What would count against this? Rank the defeaters from fatal to awkward: data showing cultural transmission is mostly vertical not horizontal; formal models where group-level selection beats meme-level selection; cases of well-spread ideologies suddenly collapsing; and whether I've insulated the view so that any persistance is just 'fitness'. Be honest about whether this is just multilevel selection with extra branding, and say what defenders of memeplex-selection would themselves concede as damaging.
