The working title is “Moral Provenance and Structural Resonance: A Third Path Between Autonomism and Moralism.” The standard debate asks whether knowledge of an artist’s immoral conduct should affect our aesthetic judgment of the work. Both sides tend to treat the moral fact as external to the work’s aesthetic properties—either causally irrelevant (autonomism) or contaminating (moralism). I want to propose that for narrative and autobiographical art, moral failures in the artist can become structurally resonant within the work itself, not because the work expresses those failures, but because the work’s formal organization presupposes a moral framework that the artist’s conduct systematically violates.

Consider a concrete hypothetical: a novelist constructs a non-linear narrative about forgiveness and moral repair, using a deliberately fragmented chronology that mirrors the slow, non-teleological process of atonement. The form’s refusal of easy redemption arcs is aesthetically successful precisely because it respects the difficulty of forgiveness. It is later revealed that the novelist refused to acknowledge harm done to former collaborators and used royalties to fund legal defenses against them. The essay asks whether the aesthetic value of the novel’s formal structure is undermined not by a causal taint but by structural hypocrisy: the form demands a moral posture—patience, non-instrumentality—that the conditions of its production explicitly reject.

I distinguish three responses. First, autonomist denial: the formal properties remain intact and biography is irrelevant. Second, strong moralist contamination: the revelation alters emotional or cognitive uptake, making the form feel hollow. Third, structural resonance: the revelation does not change internal formal coherence, but it changes the work’s moral provenance—the institutional relationship between the work’s internal moral logic and the conditions of its production. This is not a causal claim but a categorical one, analogous in structure (not in ontology) to art forgery: the object remains perceptually identical yet enters a different evaluative category.

I tentatively argue that structural resonance applies only where there is minimal semantic or narrative content; pure formal works—say, abstract geometric painting—block the pathway, because there is no internal moral logic to resonate against.
