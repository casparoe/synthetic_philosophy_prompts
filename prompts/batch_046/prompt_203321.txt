Please describe the most important implications of applying Hofstadter's strange-loop conception of the self to a sustained, bidirectional coupling between two distinct brains that share a single high-level symbolic pattern constituting an "I". Address specifically: (1) whether the coupled system should be understood as one person, two persons with a shared core, or a novel plural subject; (2) how moral responsibility ought to be allocated when one biological substrate executes an action driven by intentions that arose within the shared loop; and (3) whether the destruction of one brain eliminates the self, merely truncates its physical extension, or leaves it fully intact in the remaining substrate. Separate what follows with reasonable analytic clarity—such as the conditions under which a symbolic pattern can persist across separate cortical substrates—from what is genuinely contested among philosophers of personal identity and philosophy of mind, and do not present any contested interpretation, including substrate-independence claims about the loop, as the established consensus.
