everyone talks about "the german character" or "the japanese temperament" like a nation is just one big person with personality traits -- disciplined, melancholic, innovative, whatever. it's used both to defend sweeping generalizations about whole peoples and to push back ("you can't reduce 80 million people to one psyche"). audit the nation-as-individual analogy: lay out the correspondance table -- what plays personality, upbringing, trauma, genes, free will? then the breaks: do nations have unified inner lives, can they "change their mind," does the analogy smuggle in essentialism? and tell me wether it actually licenses either conclusion -- that national-character talk is coherent and acceptable, or that it's incoherent and never okay to generalize -- or if it's just carrying rhetorical weight it doesn't earn. separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who actually think about this, and don't present a contested view as the consensus.
