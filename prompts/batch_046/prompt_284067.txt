i sit on the oversight board for our city's direct-vote pilot on capital spending. if a project costs over $4M residents can vote direct, and there's a 48-hour reversal trigger if 12% sign a petition. last quarter a $10M waterfront plan was nearly killed after a viral video of cracked pavement, then flipped back 36 hours later — feels like the Mytilene reversal. then the same assembly voted to suppress a dissenting engineer's report calling the plan redundant, which is basically the Socrates problem: the crowd silences the expert it finds annoying. council now asks whether to expand this citywide. draft the memo for tuesday's meeting: reccomend approve-with-conditions, reject, or sunset the pilot, with concrete safeguards if you go with conditions. take seriously the pro-democracy argument that modern checks — mandatory cooling-off windows and sortition review panels — fix the old Athenian failure modes. but also engage the strongest counter-argument that those are just band-aids on the Sicily-style overreach problem, letting amateurs greenlight complex infrastructure they don't understand. make it concrete enough that council can actually vote on it, not just a philosophy lecture.
