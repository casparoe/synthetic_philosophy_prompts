Analyze the 1989 Velvet Revolution in Czechoslovakia — specifically the rapid collapse of Communist Party authority following the 17 November student demonstration, the formation of Civic Forum, the nationwide general strike, and the subsequent election of Václav Havel to the presidency — through the three interpretive frameworks developed for 1789: social-class determinism (revolution as the expression of underlying structural economic contradictions), ideology-driven explanation (revolution as the practical unfolding of a prior conceptual framework that remakes political reality), and contingency (revolution as the product of local, unrepeatable accidents and individual decisions). For each framework, specify who counts as the revolutionary children — whether the reform communists of 1968, the dissident intellectuals of Charter 77, or the party apparatus that attempted to negotiate — and whether the revolution consumed or expelled them in any analog to 1793–94; then say what the near-total absence of terror, class warfare, and ideological fulfillment reveals about that framework's explanatory scope. Next, run the analysis in reverse: do the Velvet Revolution's dependence on Gorbachev's decision not to intervene, its explicit rejection rather than fulfillment of Marxist-Leninist doctrine, and the absence of both a mobilized bourgeoisie and a peasant insurgency, support, undermine, or remain neutral toward each interpretation of 1789? Be disciplined about the mapping — identify the relevant actors, their available options, and the evidence each framework must treat as central — and state explicitly where a framework is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
