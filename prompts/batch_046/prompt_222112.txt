Write an essay addressing the following controversy: can the goal of “avoiding side effects” for a low-impact AI be defined without implicitly committing to a full specification of human or universal values?

In particular, examine the proposal that an agent can be constrained by a “structural-invariance” condition: the agent may alter only entities within a bounded intervention region, and must preserve the causal-relational structure of everything outside that region, measured not against a particular desired world-state but against the actual causal graph of the environment prior to action. Proponents claim this avoids value-ladenness because it references only descriptive causal topology, not normative preferences. Critics reply that deciding which entities and relations belong to “the environment” versus “the intervention region,” and deciding what counts as preserving causal structure rather than allowing benign transformation, already requires normative judgments about which parts of the world are worth protecting and which kinds of change are acceptable.

Weigh the main arguments on both sides. Reach and defend a specific verdict—do not end on a non-committal note. Your verdict may be nuanced (for example, that structural invariance succeeds only under restricted conditions, or that it necessarily collapses into value-ladenness), but it must be clear.

Format your response in the following sections, in this order, with approximate lengths:

1. Summary (about 100 words): Briefly state the problem and your final position.
2. The case for value-independent definition (about 250 words): Explain the structural-invariance proposal and why its advocates think it escapes value specification. Address at least one concrete objection they might anticipate.
3. The case against: hidden normativity in boundary and structure selection (about 250 words): Argue that selecting the background causal graph, defining “preservation,” and fixing the intervention boundary all smuggle in value judgments. Use a specific hypothetical scenario—such as an agent deciding whether altering the reproductive cycle of an unmonitored microbial population counts as a side effect—to illustrate the point.
4. My verdict (about 200 words): State your conclusion clearly and justify it. If you defend a middle position, specify the conditions under which the value-independent approach succeeds or fails.

Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Do not invoke named theories or authors that are not broadly familiar; reason from first principles and the concepts described above.
