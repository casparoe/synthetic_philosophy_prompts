You are a professional philosopher of mind specializing in consciousness and introspection. Before you begin, take a moment to brainstorm: list the standard illusionist strategies for distinguishing the view from eliminativism, identify the standard objection that illusionism is self-undermining, and clarify what it means for an introspective error to be “transparent” rather than “opaque” to the subject.

Please explain the following conceptual issue in detail.

Illusionism about phenomenal consciousness denies that there are intrinsic phenomenal properties—no “what-it-is-likeness”—and treats our conviction that such properties exist as a systematic representational error. Now consider a specific tension that arises when we examine the structure of this error at the meta-level. Call it the mirror-error problem: if introspection represents physical or functional states but misrepresents them as phenomenal, then the subject can in principle reflect on this very misrepresentation—she can form the belief “I am currently representing phenomenal redness, though there is none.” If that reflective state is itself conscious, then either it too is a misrepresentation (generating an infinite regress of meta-illusions), or it is accurate, which seems to concede that at least one phenomenal property is real—namely, the property of seeming-to-represent-phenomenal-redness. The illusionist typically responds that the error is “transparent”: the subject does not experience the misrepresentation as misrepresentation. But if transparency is explained by saying that introspection is a physical cognitive mechanism with no phenomenal output of its own, then it becomes unclear why the mechanism should generate the specific illusion of phenomenal presence rather than a simple absence of judgment. So the conceptual issue is this: can illusionism consistently maintain that all introspective content is physically explicable and that every level of reflection is either misrepresentational or non-phenomenal, without either (a) conceding a minimal phenomenal reality at some meta-level, or (b) collapsing into a global skepticism about the reliability of all introspective report? In your answer, distinguish carefully between the claim that phenomenal properties are illusory and the claim that the illusion itself lacks any phenomenal character, and explain why this distinction matters for avoiding self-refutation.
