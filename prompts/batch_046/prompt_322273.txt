Write the full list of desiderata that any adequate interpretation of Nāgārjuna's catuskoti (the four-cornered negation) and Madhyamaka emptiness must satisfy to count as philosophically and textually responsible—not merely a summary of familiar readings, but the specification of what the interpretation has to deliver. For each desideratum, state the concrete case or conceptual pressure behind it: (i) how it handles the rejection of svabhāva (inherent existence) across the four positions without collapsing them into a single denial or into logical contradiction; (ii) how it preserves the conventional/ultimate (samvṛti/paramārtha) distinction without either reifying emptiness as an absolute or reducing it to nihilism; (iii) how it dissolves the self-refutation charge—if all views are empty, is the emptiness-view itself empty?—rather than dismissing it; (iv) how it treats what I will call the modal-conditional tetralemma, where the four corners are applied not to static entities but to conditions of arising (from self, from other, from both, from neither), and whether these are four distinct ontological commitments being rejected or four modes of conceptual proliferation (prapañca) being blocked, without conflating the two readings; (v) how it accommodates the claim that emptiness is itself empty (śūnyatāśūnyatā) without smuggling in a hidden essence; and (vi) how it respects the soteriological function of the device—eliminating attachment to views (dṛṣṭi)—without reducing the tetralemma to pure rhetoric that carries no cognitive content. Flag any pair of these desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied—especially whether preserving the logical structure of the four corners conflicts with a purely therapeutic or pragmatic interpretation, and whether accommodating the modal-conditional cases forces a metaphysical reading that undermines the claim that all four positions share one root error (reification). Then, briefly, assess how the standard interpretive families—dialetheist/logical, semantic/non-propositional, therapeutic/pragmatic, and ontological—fare against your list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their tensions rather than on exhaustive scoring. Please state explicitly how confident you are in your assessment and single out the one point about which you are least sure—particularly regarding whether any interpretation can jointly satisfy both the logical-form and the anti-reification desiderata in the modal-conditional cases.
