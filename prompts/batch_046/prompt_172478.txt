(2) But relations don't always need both relata to exist — I'm taller than a hypothetical 12-foot giant, and that comparison holds just fine. (3) So normative relations can hold even when S is missing: there are reasons right now for future people, fictional detectives, or agents in worlds that already died out. (4) But if Sherlock Holmes has reasons to solve cases, that's absured; a reason with no possible chooser is just a floating ought with nowhere to land. (5) So either normative relations are special and always require real agents (giving up 2), or reasons aren't relations at all (giving up 1). (6) Either way, free-floating rationales collapse into meaningless prescriptions. I feel like something sneaky happens betweeen (3) and (4) but I can't name it. Which premise do I actually need to drop, and what's the price of loosing it?
