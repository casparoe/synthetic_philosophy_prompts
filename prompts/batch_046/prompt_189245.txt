Assume a philanthropic funder has committed substantial resources over four years to research groups working exclusively on "prosaic AI alignment"—improving the safety and steerability of systems built from current methods (large-scale pretraining, RLHF, retrieval-augmented generation, layered human oversight, and incremental filtering) without relying on theoretical breakthroughs in interpretability, consciousness studies, or formal verification. The funder asks you to advise on an agenda of genuinely open conceptual and practical questions, not routine engineering tasks that additional compute would resolve.

Your task is to defend a ranked agenda of the three to five open questions or lines of work that should receive the largest share of attention and funding. For each entry, specify: (i) what makes it conceptually open rather than technically routine within the prosaic paradigm; (ii) why progress on it is upstream of other alignment efforts that also avoid new architectures; (iii) how tractable it is for interdisciplinary teams—philosophers, organizational sociologists, legal scholars, and ML practitioners—working without access to future model families; and (iv) what specific institutional, empirical, or regulatory evidence over the next two to four years would cause you to promote or demote it.

Crucially, address the tension between "institutional scaffolding" (designing audit regimes, liability frameworks, and human oversight structures that treat the underlying model as a persistent black box) and "technical patchwork" (incremental improvements to constitutional AI, retrieval filtering, or reward modeling that operate inside current architectures). Which of these two families contains the more neglected but consequential open questions? And does your answer depend on whether regulatory environments remain fragmented versus become harmonized across jurisdictions?

Deliver a defended agenda rather than a survey. State explicitly the criteria governing your ranking—such as upstreamness for other work, tractability without theoretical breakthroughs, robustness to architectural stagnation, and scalability across legal contexts—identify where these criteria conflict, and explain how you resolve the conflicts. What should change the ordering if, within the funding window, current scaling laws flatten or if major jurisdictions impose incompatible oversight mandates?
