After three evenings of circular debate with my closest friend about mind uploading and gradual neuronal replacement, we have resolved to produce an adversarial-collaboration statement rather than continue arguing past each other. I hold the Continuity Thesis: if biological neurons are replaced incrementally by synthetic functional equivalents that preserve causal and informational architecture, the original conscious subject persists without interruption, and there is no hidden metaphysical cutoff at which they perish and are replaced by a psychological duplicate. My friend holds the Substrate-Discontinuity Thesis: there exists a genuine, non-arbitrary boundary—tied to the dissolution of biological causal integration—after which the pre-upload subject is no longer present, even if a functionally continuous successor exists.

We need the joint document that both of us could sign. Structure it as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: about the physical procedure of gradual replacement, about established findings on neural plasticity and split-brain dynamics, about which analogies (the Ship of Theseus, teletransportation scenarios) are structurally relevant and which are misleading, and about what both sides agree would not resolve the dispute. Second, a disagreements table presenting the Continuity Thesis and the Substrate-Discontinuity Thesis in precise, neutral wording—each formulation acceptable to its respective advocate, with no straw-manning—followed by explicit identification of any disagreement that is merely verbal (for instance, if survival is being used with different success conditions). Third, the cruxes: the specific sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party. I take the central cruxes to be (a) whether phenomenal consciousness requires token-level biological substrate continuity or is fully sustained by functional isomorphism at each time-slice, and (b) whether personal identity here is a further fact beyond psychological and causal continuity. For each crux, specify a concrete test, empirical consideration, or hypothetical scenario that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts. In particular, analyze the staggered bilateral replacement protocol—complete synthetic replacement of the left hemisphere over a decade while the right hemisphere remains biological, followed by replacement of the right—addressing whether the intermediate split-brain dynamics undermine phenomenal unity, whether the subject should report experiential discontinuity upon full replacement, and whether the intermediate state contains one subject or two. Finally, for each crux, state what kind of evidence or argument each side regards as genuinely capable of overturning its own position, and conclude by distinguishing the genuinely empirical or conceptual disagreements from those that dissolve upon clarifying terms.
