I have been thinking about Libet's experiments, in which brain activity—specifically the readiness potential—begins roughly 350 milliseconds before a person reports the conscious urge to move a finger. Libet himself suggested a brief veto window, yet that still leaves the initial neural preparation occurring without any conscious input. So it strikes me that if free will is genuinely real, my conscious choice must be the cause of that early brain activity; otherwise the physical process has already determined the outcome before awareness even enters. But causation runs forward in time, so consciousness cannot reach backward to initiate the readiness potential. Given this, wouldn't any theory that saves free will have to deny that the brain physically initiates voluntary action? I do not understand why philosophers propose compatibilist accounts—where conscious deliberation operates within neural processes—rather than treating Libet's result as proof that mental causation is impossible.
