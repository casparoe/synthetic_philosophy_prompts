We just got a grant proposal called Project Bind that wants to train a model to generate pre-conceptual phenomenal descriptions of synthetic stimuli and then compare them to human reports of raw sensory givenness. The PI argues that if the model outputs match human reports within some statistical bound, that supports qualia being a coherent, measurable property.

Half the board thinks the whole framework is conceptually broken — they keep mentioning this Quining-qualia idea, that private ineffable mental content contradicts itself the moment you try to report it, bind it to language, or feed it into a model. The other half says incoherence is too strong, that we just dont have good measurement tools yet, and rejecting this would be like rejecting neuroscience in 1900 because we didnt understand action potentials.

I need to draft the board's decision memo for next tuesday's meeting. Recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions. But also treat the other side seriously: maybe the study could reveal that reports of raw givenness are systematically unstable or concept-dependent, which would still be a meaningful result even if qualia are incoherent.
