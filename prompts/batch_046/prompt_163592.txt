I'm preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher defending a specific, somewhat unconventional reading of Benjamin Franklin's moral perfection project in his Autobiography. On this reading, Franklin's well-known admission that he could never reach perfection is not Stoic humility or Christian acknowledgment of fallenness, but a proto-utilitarian recognition that the marginal cost of further self-tracking exceeds its welfare benefit, especially once the audit becomes self-referential. The guest further holds that Franklin's specific precepts—framing Industry, for instance, as cutting off unnecessary action rather than as mere labor—reveal a particularist, context-sensitive conception of virtue that sits uneasily with universalist Stoic maxims.
