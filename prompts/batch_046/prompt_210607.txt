Write a dialogue between two decision theorists—Dr. Aris Thorne, who defends causal decision theory augmented with an indexical commitment to symmetric reasoning, and Dr. Elara Voss, who advocates evidential decision theory with a symmetry-breaking mechanism—debating whether the Platonia dilemma demonstrates genuine superrational cooperation or exposes a framing artifact. They should specifically contrast the Platonia scenario, in which isolated agents choose whether to enter a shared payout condition based on structurally identical reasoning, with the Luring Lottery, where one agent’s defection generates an asymmetric information state that undermines the convergence required for superrationality. Have them argue over whether the divergence between these cases is ontological—the possible-world structures genuinely differ—or epistemic—the agents’ self-locating beliefs differ despite identical causal structures. They should also examine whether introducing a reflection principle, which requires an agent to treat its own decision procedure as direct evidence about others’ procedures, can restore cooperation in both cases without collapsing into full evidentialism. Ensure direct engagement: Thorne should challenge Voss’s claim that the Luring Lottery breaks symmetry by arguing that the information asymmetry is endogenous to the reasoning process rather than exogenous, and Voss should press Thorne on how his indexical commitment avoids circularity when no prior correlation mechanism links the agents’ procedures. Keep both voices equally rigorous, with no straw positions, and have the exchange turn on concrete distinctions rather than general slogans.
