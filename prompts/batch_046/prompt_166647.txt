As a retired physicist who spent decades studying non-equilibrium dynamics and emergent behavior in complex physical systems, I have recently begun reading philosophy and find myself approaching questions of political obligation and civil disobedience not through normative ethics but through the conceptual vocabulary of my former discipline. Please restate the principal arguments for political obligation—those grounded in consent, fair reciprocity, and associative membership—together with the main justifications for civil disobedience, entirely within the framework of dynamical systems theory: attractor states, bifurcation parameters, feedback control, self-organized criticality, and emergent structural stability. Where the philosophical positions rely on normative concepts such as duty, legitimacy, or justice, provide structural or functional equivalents rather than importing the terms themselves. For each translated position, report precisely what survives without distortion, what requires reinterpretation to fit, and what cannot be expressed within this vocabulary at all. Finally, evaluate the residue: do the untranslatable elements indicate that dynamical systems theory lacks resources essential to understanding authority and obligation, or do they reveal that the original philosophical debate contains metaphysical commitments with no structural counterpart? Please write your answer in American English, maintaining a level of rigor appropriate to both philosophy and the natural sciences.
