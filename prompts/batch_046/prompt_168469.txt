Here is where I have landed on consciousness, and I want a consistency check before presenting it. I hold: (1) Consciousness is a user illusion in a specific, non-Cartesian sense: the brain constructs a virtual self-model through predictive processing, but because of a systematic failure of meta-representational tagging it treats its own generative models as unmediated qualitative presence; there is no inner theater, only a misrepresented computation. (2) Phenomenal concepts are not metaphysically special; they are indexical, first-personal modes of presentation for physical-functional states, analogous to here or now. (3) The hard problem is not ontological but structurally epistemic: any physical explanation must be grasped by the very self-model it describes, so the mechanism always collapses into its content from the inside, producing an unavoidable sense of explanatory incompleteness. (4) Despite this, subjective experience is genuinely present, but only as a relational property—having an integrated self-model that represents the world from a particular point of view—not as an intrinsic, non-physical quality. (5) Because the illusion depends on this specific tagging failure, no amount of third-person neuroscience can fully dissolve the first-person feeling of direct acquaintance; the gap is not contingent ignorance but an architectural loop. (6) The boundary of consciousness is sharp, not gradual: a system either implements the full self-model architecture with the relevant transparency structure, or it lacks consciousness entirely; there are no borderline cases.
