I came across a summary of Wittgenstein’s hinge epistemology in a philosophy blog post, and I would like to know how much to trust it. The author writes: “Wittgenstein argues that there is a special class of propositions—such as that the Earth has existed for many years, or that I have never traveled to the Moon—that function as fixed hinges for all reasoning and inquiry. These hinge propositions are not supported by evidence, but they are not arbitrary either; they form the bedrock at which justification comes to an end. Because they lie entirely outside the scope of doubt, Wittgenstein treats them as a distinctive form of knowledge—what he calls ‘hinge knowledge’—that is rational without requiring evidential support. From this it follows that hinge epistemology is really just a variant of foundationalism: it isolates a privileged set of beliefs that are self-justifying and serve as the ultimate ground for all other knowledge.” I am inclined to think the blog is wrong to call this foundationalism, but I am unsure exactly where the distortion lies.
