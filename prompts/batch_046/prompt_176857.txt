Let the first refrain be a statement of local preference at one choice point (for instance, preferring option A to B in the present moment), and let the second refrain be the agent's willingness to pay a small premium to avoid reversing that preference at a later point. Arrange the stanzas so that each tercet represents one transaction in the pump sequence: a bet is offered, the agent accepts according to the local preference expressed by the first refrain, then a subsequent bet exploits the reversal implied by the second. The refrains should take turns as the form demands, so that by the final couplet they appear together—A1 and A2 side by side—making visible the intransitive cycle (A preferred to B, B to C, C to A) that permits the net extraction of value. Do not resolve the tension: leave open whether rationality requires a global coherence constraint that forbids the cycle, or whether the agent's local autonomy should be preserved despite the predictable loss. Use precise language—reference preference reversal, sequential bets, and the closing of the loop—without naming specific philosophers, and ensure that someone familiar with diachronic rationality can recognize the structure of the argument in the rhyme and refrain pattern, while a reader unfamiliar with it still encounters a coherent dramatic monologue about being slowly emptied by one's own consistent choices.
