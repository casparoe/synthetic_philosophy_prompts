Write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—one defending a Burkean, Chesterton-fence approach to institutional preservation and one advocating explicit rational reconstruction—debating the proposed abolition of an intermediate appellate review layer within a specialized regulatory tribunal. Originally established to filter frivolous technical appeals, this layer has over time become an unintended coordination device: litigants and agencies now rely on its procedural delays to negotiate informal settlement frameworks that stabilize cross-jurisdictional compliance norms. The rationalist contends that once this hidden function is mapped, it can be replaced by a direct, transparent settlement protocol, making the appellate layer redundant. The Burkean responds that identifying a function ex post is not equivalent to engineering its stability conditions, and that removing the layer may collapse tacit norms that depend on its presence as a focal point. Have them respond to each other’s actual points: the rationalist should challenge whether the Burkean’s view is unfalsifiable—if every post-reform failure is attributed to an undiscovered hidden function, Chesterton’s fence risks becoming an argument from ignorance rather than a principle of epistemic humility. The Burkean should answer by proposing a precise criterion for legitimate reform—namely, that removal is permissible only when supported by a predictive model of the hidden coordination mechanism that demonstrates the replacement will preserve the equilibrium—while maintaining that such models are rarely available for institutions shaped by evolutionary selection. They should also examine whether the fence applies differently to deliberately designed versus organically evolved institutions, and whether the settlement dynamics represent a genuine “reason the fence was put up” or merely an accidental byproduct that rational design ought to supersede. Conclude with a clear, unresolved tension that leaves the status of the fence principle genuinely in question.
