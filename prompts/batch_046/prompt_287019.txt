I moderate a high-volume rationalist forum where users routinely embed claims inside hypotheticals, analogies, and meta-linguistic frames, and I need a reusable decision procedure—ordered tests with explicit stop conditions—for classifying any given post according to Hofstadter’s frame-message, outer-message, and inner-message taxonomy (from Gödel, Escher, Bach), but stripped to what a non-philosopher can run in under five minutes without outside references. The procedure must distinguish the frame (the instructions for how to interpret what follows), the outer message (the literal propositional content), and the inner message (the claim that resolves only once the frame is applied), and it must survive three hard structural cases: adversarial framing, where the frame explicitly contradicts the outer message; nested framing, where an embedded analogy or hypothetical carries its own independent framing instructions; and reflexive framing, where accepting the inner message undermines the frame that generated it. For each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure halts and defers to human judgment because the question is normative rather than classificatory, and which nuances of the original theory must be preserved versus which can be dropped for usability. Then apply the procedure to three worked examples: first, a post that opens with the framing sentence “Consider a purely fictional AI system” and then describes an architecture identical to one currently under development, concluding with a normative claim about that architecture; second, a debate reply that begins “Assuming for argument’s sake that consequentialism is true” and proceeds with a detailed consequentialist case without ever explicitly dropping the framing assumption; and third, a case where the procedure forces an obvious moderation decision by the taxonomy that is nevertheless wrong from a community-health perspective, with an explicit diagnosis of where and why the misfire occurs. Fix the operational context precisely: dozens of these judgments per week, misclassification either suppressing legitimate argument or allowing bad-faith framing to spread, no philosophical expertise required to run the checklist, the moderator alone signs off with an appeal path to a senior editor, and the answer must flag every judgment call dressed up as procedure and state clearly what the taxonomy cannot do.
