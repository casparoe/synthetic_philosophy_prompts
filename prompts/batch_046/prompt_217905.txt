I recently read a popular psychology blog summarizing the cross-cultural WEIRD morality literature. The author writes something like this: "Western, educated, industrialized, rich, and democratic populations are outliers not only in cognition but in moral psychology. On standard moral-foundations measures they score very high on harm and fairness, but essentially at floor for loyalty, authority, and purity — capacities that are robust and universal elsewhere. The cross-population variation is largely heritable rather than cultural, which means WEIRD individuals do not merely weight binding values differently; they largely lack the corresponding moral instincts that most of humanity possesses. Consequently, moral philosophers who treat harm-reduction and fairness as the core of ethics are not uncovering universal truths but describing a local biological anomaly. And if moral cognition is biologically heterogeneous across human populations, then moral objectivism — the claim that there is a single correct moral framework — collapses, because there is no unified human nature for morality to be true to."

The blogger uses this to argue that any non-relativist ethics is just parochial WEIRD bias. I am not sure how much to trust the summary. Could you audit it claim by claim? For each element, say whether it is accurate to the descriptive literature, a defensible simplification, a subtle conflation, or a straightforward error. In particular, distinguish what the data actually say about cultural versus biological sources of variation, about whether low scores indicate absence rather than differential weighting, and about whether WEIRD populations truly sit near floor on binding foundations. Then assess the normative move: does the empirical heterogeneity, even if fully granted, entail the failure of moral objectivism, or does that conclusion depend on the misrepresentations? Finally, if a moral objectivist wanted to accept all the cross-cultural findings without accepting relativism, what conceptual distinctions — for example, between moral psychology and moral epistemology, or between variation in intuition and variation in correct norms — would they need to invoke?
