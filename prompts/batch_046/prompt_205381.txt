Please design a diagnostic decision tree — intended for a philosophy-of-religion seminar — that sorts commitments on the cosmological argument, treating kalam (temporal-finitude) and contingency (modal-dependence) versions as branches of one logical space rather than as separate debates. Build the tree from five concrete diagnostic questions arranged in logical order, each phrased so a student can answer yes or no without knowing technical labels. The splits must cover: (1) whether the principle of sufficient reason applies collectively to the universe as a whole or only distributively to its parts; (2) whether an infinite regress — temporal or modal — is vicious or benign; (3) whether any terminating explanation must be temporally prior, as kalam demands, or only ontologically prior, as contingency allows; (4) whether that terminator must be a necessary being or could itself be contingent; and (5) whether it must be personal or may be impersonal. Present the result as a numbered list of exactly five items (1–5), each item being one or two sentences: state the question, name the two local positions it separates with an informal label (e.g. 'hard naturalist', 'cyclical cosmologist'), and in the same item note the most awkward commitment of each. In the item where the logical space allows a contingent, impersonal, temporally prior creator with no further explanation, include that empty cell and diagnose in one clause whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and avoid referencing obscure named theories that depend on specialised scholarship. Write in British English, with no introduction and no conclusion.
