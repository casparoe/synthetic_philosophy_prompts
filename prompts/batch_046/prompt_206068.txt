I am preparing the final examination for an upper-level seminar in philosophy of psychiatry, enrolling both advanced undergraduates and first-year graduate students. The exam is a single 90-minute essay, worth 20 marks, and must be answerable without access to outside sources. I want the question to center on Ian Hacking's account of looping effects and interactive kinds, but it should be impossible to answer well by merely summarizing that framework. To achieve this, please embed within the question itself a novel, hypothetical diagnostic category—call it predictive social withdrawal (PSW)—defined not by subjective symptoms but by an algorithmic risk score derived from adolescents' digital communication patterns. The prompt should describe how, once assigned, these adolescents are offered a targeted behavioral intervention, and how ethnographic reports indicate that many subsequently reinterpret ordinary solitude as pathological and form peer groups organized around the label, despite initially feeling no distress.

The essay question should ask students to determine whether Hacking's looping effect genuinely applies to PSW, and if so, whether it operates in the standard interactive fashion or in some inverted or destabilized form. Crucially, require the answer to take a definite position—arguing, for instance, that the loop is asymmetrical because institutional labeling precedes self-awareness, or that the statistical origin of the category severs the feedback loop altogether—and to defend that stance with explicit reasoning about what conditions Hacking requires for an interactive kind. The model answer should run approximately 800 words and must address five specific moves: first, distinguishing interactive kinds from indifferent kinds in the context of PSW; second, analyzing whether self-awareness of the label alters behavior in a manner that feeds back into the classification criteria; third, evaluating how institutional power, specifically the algorithm and the intervention, shapes or disrupts the loop; fourth, confronting one serious objection, such as the claim that because the initial assignment is pre-subjective there is no human kind available to loop with; and fifth, delivering a concluding judgment on whether PSW ought to be treated as an interactive kind for purposes of clinical policy and resource allocation.

Please provide the exam question itself, the model answer, and a marking scheme built around those five must-hit points, allocating marks that sum to 20 and including a one-line note on how candidates typically miss each point. The marking scheme should also distinguish an A-range response—which takes a clear, reasoned stance, anticipates the objection, and integrates the institutional-analysis point into the argument—from a B-range response, which describes the scenario accurately but treats Hacking's framework as a checklist or remains non-committal by presenting both interpretations without adjudicating between them. Finally, please ensure the model answer is committed rather than balanced: it should choose one interpretation of the loop and argue for it, giving its reasons, rather than laying out options and leaving the evaluation to the reader.
