I'm on my town's parks board and lately we keep recieving these wild proposals for "green tech" experiments in our public spaces — like synthetic moss walls that supposedly filter air, or releasing engineered soil bacteria to break down trash faster. I need a real decision procedure I can run in about 20 minutes with no science PhD, grounded in the precautionary principle but not just "better safe than sorry." Specifically, I want it to seperate the strong version (block anything with serious uncertain harm unless it's absolutely necessary) from the weak version (uncertainty alone can't justify inaction), show me exactly where they split on a concrete case, and build in a check for whether the harm would be reversible or permanent — since I think that changes things but I don't know how to weight it. Give me the ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure automatically says no, where it stops being technical and becomes a values call, and flag the spots where I'm just dressing up judgment as rules. Then apply it to both the moss wall and the bacteria release, with one of those coming back with an answer I'll probably hate.
