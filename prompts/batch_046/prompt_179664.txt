The walls become transparent; the furniture dissolves into water; the tide acquires speech and memory. Yet the characters continue to treat the comparison as a single continuous idea, and I intend the reader to feel rather than analyze the shift. The emotional resonance survives; the logical mapping does not. This strikes me as conceptual slippage within a tightly bounded microdomain—akin to the drift of analogies in certain cognitive architectures, where stylistic coherence outlasts structural fidelity—but I am unsure whether this specific failure mode in sustained literary metaphor has been formally distinguished from simple inconsistency, from intentional surrealism, or from a character's own cognitive confusion.
