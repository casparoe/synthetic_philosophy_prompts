I am writing a presentation for my philosophy of language seminar, in which we have been examining Stalnaker's unified account of indicative and counterfactual conditionals. Having considered the standard treatments—belief-revision norms for indicatives and world-similarity selection for counterfactuals—I have formed the view that the two belong to entirely separate logical modules: one epistemic, governing rational belief change, the other metaphysical, governing relations among possible worlds. This has led me to conclude that the evaluation conditions for counterfactuals should exert no normative constraint on how one updates beliefs upon encountering indicative conditionals in empirical science. Yet scientific practice seems to violate this isolation, since researchers often calibrate predictive forecasts against retrospective counterfactual models. Is the isolation thesis a misreading of Stalnaker's framework? Specifically, does a unified conditional semantics entail some form of cross-modal coherence between counterfactual evaluation and indicative belief revision, and if so, what exactly does that coherence require?
