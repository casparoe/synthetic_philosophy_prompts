I have been trying to work through a personal decision about parenthood without leaning on philosophy texts I barely understand. If I conceive a child now, she will inherit a specific, manageable but lifelong genetic condition; if I wait for a therapy that may arrive too late, any child I eventually have will be a different person altogether, with a different genome and a different life. The thought that she cannot be harmed—because she owes her very existence to my not waiting—feels both compelling and like an evasion. I want a villanelle of nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a quatrain, using the classic A1 b A2 / a b A1 pattern so that two refrains keep returning and finally stand together in the closing couplet. One refrain should carry the idea that this particular child would never have existed had I delayed; the other should carry the feeling that I have still placed her on a harder path than the one a different child might have walked. Let the rhyme and repetition make the two lines circle each other without meeting, so that the form itself shows why they keep passing without resolution. The tone should be intimate and unsettled rather than didactic; it should be precise enough that someone familiar with the underlying puzzle recognizes its logic, but it should avoid technical terms and should leave the moral question hanging open rather than answering it. I need this for a private conversation, not for a classroom.
