I serve as the senior justification auditor for a legislative drafting office in a pluralistic constitutional democracy. Every proposed bill that imposes a coercive burden must carry an official “public-reason statement,” and my team must vet it before publication. I need a reusable audit procedure—ordered tests, stopping rules, explicit judgment-call markers, and a time budget of about thirty minutes—that translates the idea of public reason into practical steps without requiring philosophical expertise. The procedure must distinguish the policy’s motivating values from its official justification; identify the specific liberty being restricted; test whether the justification depends on metaphysical, theological, or otherwise sectarian premises that not all reasonable citizens share; and separate cases where an argument is merely intelligible to outsiders from cases where it is genuinely independent of any comprehensive doctrine. It should include a specific filter for overlapping arguments—such as appeals to human dignity, potential life, or collective welfare—that sound secular but may embed contested metaphysical commitments. Please design the checklist so it stops with a clear “fail,” a “pass with conditions,” or a deferral to democratic judgment when the issue is irreducibly one of values rather than procedure. Then apply it to three proposals: first, a law restricting commercial activity on one designated day per week, justified primarily by worker rest and family cohesion; second, a law banning a specific medical procedure, justified partly by secular claims about biological potentiality and partly by theological claims about ensoulment; and third, a public-health mandate that I personally support but whose official justification leans heavily on a comprehensive ethical framework about collective flourishing that a significant minority rejects. For each case, show exactly where the audit stops, where it forces a failure or conditional pass, and where it hands the decision back because the disagreement is about values, not justification form. Include a brief “limits of this procedure” section—specifically, whether it can resolve deep disagreement about what counts as a “political” versus “comprehensive” value in the first place, and whether it risks smuggling secular metaphysics through apparently neutral language. At the end, please state how confident you are in the procedure’s overall design and single out the specific step or distinction—particularly the line between intelligibility and genuine independence—that you are least sure has been correctly translated from theory into practice.
