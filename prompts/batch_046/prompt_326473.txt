I've been mulling over pain asymbolia lately — you know, that condition where people report feeling pain but don't act like it's bad, don't avoid the source, sometimes even laugh about it. I was trying to build an argument for a discusssion with my friends about whether pain is intrinsically bad or only bad because of how we represent it, and I think I accidentally proved that suffering doesn't exist for anyone without a very specific kind of metacognitive loop. Here it is, numbered so you can see where the gears grind.

(1) In asymbolia, the nociceptive signal is intact but the affective "this is awful" layer is missing or disconnected. (2) So badness isn't in the signal itself; it's in that second-layer appraisal. (3) But if badness is just appraisal, then any state with that appraisal is bad, and any state without it isn't — which means you could in principle have a "reverse asymbolia" where you feel intense dread/appraisal with no underlying sensation, and that dread should be just as bad as pain. (4) That actually sounds right to me, but here's where it gets weird: (5) infants and many animals seem to lack the full metacognitive appraisal architecture, yet we still think they suffer. (6) So either they don't suffer (which is absurd), or the appraisal layer isn't necessary after all, which contradicts (2). (7) To save (2), you might say infants have a simpler, non-cognitive appraisal mechanism — but then asymbolics should have that too, since their brains aren't missing the limbic hardware entirely, just the connection to behavior. (8) So it looks like either pain is bad independently of appraisal (which means asymbolics should suffer, but they don't report it), or suffering requires appraisal and infants don't suffer. Both feel wrong. (9) Therefore, the distinction between sensation and appraisal can't be doing the work we want.

Where did I slip? I want to keep the idea that badness involves some kind of representation — maybe even that "reverse asymbolia" thing — but I don't want to deny that babies feel pain. Is there a hidden equivocation between "appraisal" and "affective integraion"? Or did I conflate the absence of behavioral avoidance with the absence of badness? If you had to choose which premise to drop, which one costs the least?
