His friend said that's a false choice because of what she called "reflection causality" — the idea that abstract structures don't act on us, but our brains evolved physical patterns that necessarily reflect them, so the causal chain runs through the brain's physical instantiation of the pattern rather than through the abstract object itself.

She answered evolutionary tuning — like eyes tracking photons — and he said that fails because photons are physical and numbers aren't, so tuning to physical patterns doesn't explain knowledge of non-physical ones. Then she brought up computer-verified proofs, saying if a machine checks a theorem by running code it's causally interacting with representations of the structure, giving indirect access. He countered that's just symbol-shuffling, not access to numbers, and if symbol-access counts Benacerraf's problem collapses everywhere. They turned to me but I was lost. Can you walk through their exhange step by step and rate who's right at each move?
