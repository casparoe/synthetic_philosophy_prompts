For my philosophy of science seminar I have to write an essay on whether Darwin's explanation of adaptive design and Turing's model of computation represent genuinely parallel inversions of reasoning—cases in which competence emerges without comprehension—or whether that analogy collapses under closer inspection. The controversy turns on whether evolutionary competence, being historical, embodied, and coupled to environmental feedback, is structurally different from the substrate-independent, syntactic rule-following of computation, or whether both are equally valid demonstrations that intelligent outcomes need not be produced by intelligent causes. I need the essay to defend a specific verdict: either uphold the parallel and draw out its consequences for whether artificial systems can possess understanding, or reject it by arguing that evolutionary adaptation involves a world-coupled responsiveness that computation cannot replicate even in principle. Please weigh the main arguments on both sides, but do not end on a non-committal note—reach a justified conclusion.
