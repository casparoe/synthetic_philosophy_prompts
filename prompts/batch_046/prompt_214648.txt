Next week I'm intervewing a philosopher who defends care ethics but with a twist: she thinks the literature has been too focused on "emotional labor" and not enough on what she calls "attentional scarcity." Her view is that real caring requires sustained, non-divisible attention — not just feeling the right things — and that modern institutions (like welfare offices or hospitals) don't just devalue care, they structurally fragment people's attention so that care becomes literally impossible even when people want to do it. She also thinks this explains why care ethics keeps getting pushed aside by justice frameworks: justice can be done in quick, procedural bites, but care can't.

I want 8 to 10 questions that go way beyond "what is care ethics." I need:
- at least one on why she thinks emotional-labor talk misses the point and what attentional scarcity adds,
- one on the elitism objection (if care needs lots of uninterrupted attention, doesn't that make it a luxury for the wealthy?),
- one on how her view handles the classic care-vs-justice tension — does justice have to ration attention too?
- and one on what institutional redesign would actually look like if we took attentional scarcity seriously.

For each of those four, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. The rest can press other weak spots — maybe on whether AI or bureaucratic algorithms can "care" if they have no attention to fragment, or on whether her view accidentally essentializes women as attention-givers. Make them sound like actual podcast questions, not essay prompts. Thanks!
