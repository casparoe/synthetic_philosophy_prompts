In the dollar auction, once committed expenditure exceeds the prize value, further bidding is usually diagnosed as sunk-cost escalation. But if the rules and rationality are common knowledge, shouldn't game theory identify a precise escalation ceiling—calculated from comparing the incremental bid to the second-place penalty one avoids by continuing—below which quitting is strictly dominant? Empirical bids disperse well past any such calculated point rather than clustering near it. Does this dispersion indicate a failure of strategic reasoning in subjects, or is the deeper mistake the assumption that an open-ended auction with a continuously shifting payoff structure admits any well-defined dominant-strategy cutoff at all?
