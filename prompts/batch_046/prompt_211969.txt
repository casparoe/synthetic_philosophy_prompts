I am defending a thesis in a philosophy-of-physics seminar next week. The audience consists mainly of substantivalists about spacetime who treat Einstein’s point-coincidence argument as having dissolved the hole argument, leaving behind only a benign gauge redundancy that poses no threat to the ontology of manifold points. My position is that if one reconstructs general covariance in sheaf-theoretic terms—representing spacetime not as a manifold of pre-given points but as a sheaf of local metric fields defined over a topological site—then the hole argument does not vanish but transforms structurally: the diffeomorphism freedom becomes a functorial automorphism of the sheaf that fixes no objects in the underlying site, which means that appealing to “the same points” to break the symmetry is not an ontological resolution but a category mistake, since points are not in the domain over which the metric sheaf is evaluated. Prepare me for the question period: what objections will they actually raise, phrased in their vocabulary; which objections have genuine force and which are deflections; what should I concede up front to maintain credibility; which single reply must land for the proposal to survive; and what is the one objection I am hoping nobody asks? Order the objections by likelihood and damage, provide a concise reply for each with a note on where that reply is weakest, and keep the format as a speaker’s prep sheet rather than a full defense.
