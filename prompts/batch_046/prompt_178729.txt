I sit on a regional planning board, and every other meeting some consultant rolls out an economic model with obviously false premises -- infinite capital, rational robots, no neighborhood attachment -- then waves off the objection by citing Friedmans F-twist: bad assumptions are fine if predictions work. I need a 30-minute review procedure my board can actually run without a philosophy degree. Lay out the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where we stop with a rejection, and where the answer is just our values talking. Make sure it respects the real distinction in Friedmans argument between predicting an aggregate and licensing an intervention that reshapes behavior, and build in my own check: predictive insulation versus causal leakage. Apply it to two models we're actually seeing: a housing supply forecast that assumes developers never face credit constraints, and a congestion pricing study that models commuters as identical, asocial optimizers. One should come out the way the board wants but the procedure forbids, and flag exactly where human judgment has to take over.
