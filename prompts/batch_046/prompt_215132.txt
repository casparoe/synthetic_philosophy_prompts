Been reading up on Kelly betting and geometric rationality for a side project. There's a pattern I keep noticing and I dont have a word for it. Someone works out the growth-optimal leverage, then scales it way back "for safety." When pressed, they dont cite different utility — they point to a counterfactual near-ruin: "if I had bet just 20% more, that one crash year wipes me out." They treat that hypothetical wipeout as a real strike against the larger bet. But they never mention the mirror counterfactual: betting 20% less and missing the rebound, leaving them permanently behind. So they seem to be counting counterfactual ruin events near the zero boundary but ignoring symetric missed-growth events. Does this one-sided counterfactual accounting near ruin have a name in decision theory? Or is it basically loss aversion with extra steps?
