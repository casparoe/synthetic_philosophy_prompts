I spent the better part of yesterday in a long argument with a close friend about whether metaphysics retains any substantive content once quantifier variance is taken seriously, and I want to verify that the position I ended up defending is genuinely coherent rather than merely defensible in conversation. Here is the package of commitments I now hold:

(1) Standard ontological disputes—whether composite objects exist over and above their parts, whether abstract numbers exist, whether possible worlds are concrete entities—are largely verbal disputes arising from different semantic choices for the existential quantifier or different underlying frameworks, not disagreements about a single shared reality.

(2) Nevertheless, there remains a substantive, non-verbal domain of metaphysical inquiry: questions of grounding, ontological dependence, and structural determination are real and are not dissolved by quantifier variance, because they concern determination relations that hold within any acceptable framework rather than which entities are admitted into the domain.

(3) Deflationism applies selectively. It dissolves debates framed in purely extensional vocabulary, but it does not apply to debates involving modal or normative operators, on the grounds that quantifier variance does not extend in the same way to intensional or normative contexts, which are evaluated relative to fixed accessibility or normative structures rather than to variable domains.

(4) I resist extending deflationism to grounding relations themselves. Even though grounding claims are expressed with an existential quantifier—there exists an x such that y depends on x—the claim that mental properties depend on physical ones is not a verbal dispute about quantifier choice, but a substantive claim about determination relations within a shared framework.

(5) Finally, I maintain that if ontology is indeed verbal, metaphysics still has serious work in what I call cross-framework dependency mapping: constructing directed graphs that represent how entities in one quantificational scheme depend on entities in another, without requiring any meta-framework that quantifies over both schemes simultaneously. This task is metaphysically substantive even though it carries no ontological commitment to a uniquely correct scheme.

My friend pressed me on the transition from (1) to (2): if quantifier variance undermines existence claims, why should it not also undermine grounding claims that have the same logical form? I also worry that (3) introduces an unstable boundary—if variance stops at modal operators, is that a principled limitation or an ad hoc rescue? Meanwhile, (5) strikes me as potentially suspicious: it may be nothing more than a relabeling of (2) that allows me to keep metaphysics alive by changing its subject matter.

Please audit this set. I want to know which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are merely apparent, and what the least costly revision is in each case. Order the tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify exactly which commitment must be weakened or abandoned and what that costs the overall view. Also indicate whether there is a single revision—dropping or substantially modifying one of these five commitments—that would resolve the majority of tensions with minimal damage. I am genuinely unsure which commitment I should be most willing to sacrifice, so please do not treat any of them as fixed.
