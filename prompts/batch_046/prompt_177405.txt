Consider the following position, which I will call access-limited ontological commitment (ALOC). Standard Quinean ontological commitment holds that a theory is committed to those entities over which it quantifies. ALOC introduces a further constraint: ontological commitment to an entity is warranted only when the properties that individuate that entity are informationally accessible in principle to users of the theory. If the defining properties of an entity lie behind a structural barrier—meaning that no extension of observational, computational, or causal capacities could ever reveal them—then quantification over that entity should be treated as a formal convenience rather than a genuine ontological commitment.

Within this framework, one must sharply distinguish epistemic limitation from structural inaccessibility. Epistemic limitation concerns what we do not yet know but could come to know; structural inaccessibility concerns information that is closed off by the architecture of the system under study. For instance, if a physical theory posits specific internal configurations within a black hole that are not merely hidden behind an event horizon but are such that no causal pathway could ever carry information about them to an external observer, then ALOC counsels ontological agnosticism about those particular interior states, even if the theory quantifies over them for mathematical consistency. The theory retains its predictive utility—yielding correct values for mass, charge, and angular momentum—but its ontology is trimmed to include only the externally accessible properties, while the interior ontology is suspended rather than affirmed.
