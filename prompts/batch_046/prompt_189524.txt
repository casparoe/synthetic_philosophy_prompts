You serve as the ethics advisor to a regional health authority that must repeatedly choose between funding widespread programs reducing mild, diffuse suffering—such as chronic ergonomic discomfort—and targeted programs preventing a smaller number of cases of extreme, localized pain, such as severe neuropathic episodes. The authority operates under a version of negative utilitarianism with a lexical threshold: below a specific severity boundary, suffering and pleasure are incommensurable, so no aggregate of mild relief can outweigh a case above the boundary, but once the boundary is crossed the usual quantitative comparison of suffering applies. Write a reusable decision procedure the board can run: the ordered questions to ask, the evidential test for whether a case crosses the lexical boundary, how to aggregate affected populations after that determination, the precise points where quantitative reasoning must yield to an explicit value judgment rather than computation, and the specific misfire that arises when mild suffering is numerically aggregated near the boundary. Apply the procedure to both funding options, including the case in which it recommends the severe-pain program despite affecting far fewer people and despite the board’s prior intuition that widespread mild relief should take priority. End by stating exactly what the procedure cannot settle about the moral status of pleasure.
