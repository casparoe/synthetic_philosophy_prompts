I've encountered an argument that extends Gödel's first incompleteness theorem directly into theoretical physics. The claim is that any sufficiently expressive formalization of a fundamental theory—including one cast in a higher-categorical or homotopy-type framework—must contain physically meaningful propositions that are true yet permanently unprovable within the system's own derivation rules. From this, it is concluded that a complete "theory of everything" is not merely practically unattainable but logically impossible in principle. I can see that physical theories are interpreted empirically rather than treated as bare syntactic objects, and I suspect that the error involves conflating syntactic unprovability with predictive failure, but I am unsure whether higher-categorical structures genuinely affect Gödel's conditions or whether the mistake is solely in applying an arithmetic result to an empirical domain. Can you disentangle precisely where this extension fails and why it does not establish a logical ceiling on physical derivability?
