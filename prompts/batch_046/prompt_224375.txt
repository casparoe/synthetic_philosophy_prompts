hey, trying to build this dataset and i keep coming back to an argument my study partner threw at me about workfare and the undeserving poor. it feels like it proves way too much but i can't find the exit.

(1) The state can deny welfare only if the recipient violated a reciprocal duty - they could have contributed but chose not to.

(2) Whether someone "could contribute" depends on counterfactual labor-market access, not just personal will. in a region with 25% unemployment, the same failure to find work is unavoidable, not blameworthy.

(3) So in depressed regions the "undeserving" category literally empties out - nobody locally meets the condition for denial.

(4) If a policy category is empty in some places and full in others, applying it nationally means treating the exact same behvaior differently based only on postcode.

(5) But formal equality requires that identical actions recieve identical state treatment, regardless of geography.

(6) So to preserve equality, the state must either drop the deserving/undeserving distinction everywhere, or apply it everywhere.

(7) Applying it everywhere means labeling people in high-unemployment areas "undeserving" despite (2), which is false.

(8) Therefore the distinction must be dropped nationally, moving us toward unconditional support.

(9) But unconditional support kills the work incentive that makes welfare sustainable, so (8) is politically and economically impossible.

(10) Hence the concept of the undeserving poor is incoherent - it forces geographic discrimination or an unaffordable universal grant.
