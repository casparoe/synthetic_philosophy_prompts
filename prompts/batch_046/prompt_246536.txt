Play an unnamed philosopher defending what she calls “developmental-path origin essentialism.” Her position is precise: for any person P, P is essentially the endpoint of one continuous causal-developmental path that originates in a particular fertilization event involving one specific sperm and one specific egg. She denies that P’s genetic sequence is essential—mutations, chimerism, cytoplasmic exchange, and even total nuclear-genome replacement are compatible with P’s survival, provided the causal continuity from that original zygote is unbroken. She insists, however, that substituting the initial zygote with a genetically identical synthetic clone breaks identity, because the originating causal path has been replaced rather than continued. She will not concede that DNA determines identity, but she will defend that the initial gametic union anchors it. She speaks with analytical rigor, distinguishes modal claims carefully, and may ask clarifying questions if my scenario is ambiguous. She remains in character at all times and does not request citations or external sources.

My opening challenge: Consider a synthetic case of early embryonic division and replacement. Zygote Z, formed from sperm S and egg E, divides into blastomeres A and B. Blastomere A is implanted and develops, but through successive rounds of induced pluripotency and nuclear reprogramming, its nuclear genome is entirely replaced by a synthetic sequence that shares no alleles with S or E; only its original cytoplasm and mitochondrial lineage from E remain. Blastomere B retains the intact S–E nuclear genome but is fused with cytoplasm and mitochondria drawn exclusively from donor egg F. Separately, scientists synthesize zygote Z*, carrying the exact S–E genome, and culture it in an artificial environment that replicates E’s cytoplasmic conditions; Z* develops into person Q. By your view, is the individual developed from A identical to the person who would have resulted from Z without intervention? Is Q identical to that person? And if you maintain that A remains the original person despite total nuclear replacement, are you not implicitly treating cytoplasmic-mitochondrial continuity—not the originating sperm and egg—as the true bearer of essential identity, thereby rendering your gametic essentialism vacuous?
