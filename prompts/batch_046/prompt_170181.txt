I'm putting together a seminar handout on normative reasons in worlds without agents — the problem of what philosophers occasionally call free-floating rationales. Rather than a literature review, I need a conceptual taxonomy in the form of a branching tree that sorts positions by the answers they give to successive diagnostic questions. The root should ask whether normative force is essentially relational: must there always be some agent for whom a reason is a reason? From there, branch on whether reasons require the actual existence of such an agent or only its modal possibility; on whether reasons must be action-guiding to qualify as normative rather than merely evaluative; and on whether non-mental properties (such as suffering or disorder) can ground reasons even when no mind is present to register them. Each leaf should be labeled either with the standard name of the position — relational agent-dependence, modal potentialism, non-directive moral realism, eliminativism about agentless reasons — or marked "unnamed" if the combination lacks a recognized defender, followed by a single sentence stating its most awkward commitment (for example, that it must either deny that there is anything wrong with suffering in a permanently empty universe, or else explain that wrongness without any possible bearer of the reason).

Please construct the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of authors, phrase every node as a question a student could answer without knowing which interpretation it leads to, and flag any pair of leaves that the questions cannot genuinely separate. For any empty branch, diagnose briefly whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or simple neglect. The audience is graduate students in ethics who need to see where they stand, not by picking a label they recognize, but by answering concrete questions about empty worlds, guidingness, and the ontology of value; do not ask for bibliographies, author lists, or further reading, since the point is to map the conceptual terrain from the inside.
