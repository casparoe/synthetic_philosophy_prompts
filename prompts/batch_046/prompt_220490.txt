Compare origin essentialism — the idea that you literally couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg — with what I'm calling "developmental continuity essentialism", where being you depends on the actual uninterrupted physical and mental devlopment, not the specific gametes. To make it concrete, imagine a "zigote swap": at conception, the zygote formed from sperm A and egg B is replaced by one formed from sperm C and egg D, but due to some biological redundancy the result is identicle brain structure, memories, personality, everything. Would origin essentialism say that person isn't you, just because the genetic origin changed? Would the continuity view say they are you, since the trajectory is intact? And which view better captures our intution that "I could've been my sibling if my parents had met later"? I think people mix up genetic origin with narrative continuity, and I want to know which mistake actually breaks the argument.
