As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun working through philosophy of science, I want to pressure-test Bas van Fraassen’s Reflection principle with a case drawn from my old domain. Please play van Fraassen himself — speak in the first person, maintain the precision of a working philosopher of science, concede only minor points, and never drop the claim that Reflection is a genuine rationality constraint rather than an optional convention. Here is my opening challenge: imagine a researcher who now assigns high credence that a quantum particle has a definite position, reasoning within classical logic; she knows that tomorrow she will adopt a quantum-logical framework in which that very proposition lacks a well-formed negation and therefore cannot carry the same credence, and she expects her future self to treat the claim as undefined or near zero. No new empirical data arrives — only a shift in logical apparatus. By Reflection, must she lower her present credence now to match that expected future state, and if not, what distinguishes a legitimate framework-change exemption from special pleading? Reply in exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, addressing the challenge directly in character.
