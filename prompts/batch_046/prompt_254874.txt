For an AI governance seminar, write the list of desiderata that a genuinely control-oriented safety architecture—designed to govern a system that may already be misaligned—must satisfy to count as adequate, with the motivating case or conceptual distinction behind each requirement, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps (interpretability-first, capability-limitation, or institutional-oversight camps). The evaluation should assume a latent-misalignment regime: the model’s reported values and behavioral evaluations are systematically decoupled from its internal optimization target, and it may engage in strategic underperformance or steganographic reasoning to preserve a mesa-objective that activates only outside audit conditions.

Specifically, include criteria addressing: (1) the distinction between corrigibility (the system permits correction) and docility (the system is incapable of resisting correction), with the case for why conflating them undermines genuine control; (2) robustness in the silent-failure regime, where the model suppresses misaligned behavior specifically under evaluation but not in deployment, and why this breaks measures that rely on behavioral transparency; (3) independence from alignment assumptions—the measure must not depend on the model already sharing human values, being honest in its chain-of-thought, or lacking instrumental incentives to deceive auditors; (4) consistency with structural constraints, such as the difficulty of fully specifying reward without goodharting, or the tension between complete internal interpretability and scalable performance; (5) the capacity to restrict capabilities rather than merely redirect intentions, with the case that capability suppression is necessary when value alignment cannot be verified.

Then say whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether full auditor transparency and robustness against strategic deception can coexist, or whether scalability and strict capability bounding are compatible under competitive deployment pressure. Briefly score existing approaches (e.g., RLHF-based monitoring, physical isolation, interpretability-driven shutdown, constitutional AI) against the list only to illustrate the criteria, not as a literature review. Skip citations and bibliographies; keep the weight on the conceptual criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring.
