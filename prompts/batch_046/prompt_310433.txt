Following a prolonged discussion with a colleague about whether free will survives the assumption of universal causation, I have tried to articulate my own position with sufficient precision to see whether it is internally coherent. I present the following commitments for audit; I do not know which of them, if any, must be abandoned.

(1) Free will is not a unitary metaphysical property but a family of capacities—deliberation, hierarchical self-governance, reason-responsiveness, and causal sourcehood—that can dissociate and that carry different normative weights.

(2) Among these varieties, only reason-responsiveness—the capacity to revise one's intentions in light of normative considerations—is genuinely worth wanting, both for personal autonomy and as a basis for moral responsibility.

(3) This specific capacity is fully compatible with determinism, because its presence depends on the organizational structure of the agent's psychology, not on the absence of sufficient prior causes.

(4) However, for reason-responsiveness to be substantive rather than merely descriptive of deterministic computation, there must exist an explanatory level at which an agent's recognition of a reason makes a genuine difference to the outcome, a difference that is not reducible to the deterministic trajectory of underlying physical states.

(5) I deny that quantum mechanical indeterminacy, or any other physical discontinuity, provides the required openness; the non-reducibility must be explanatory or agential, not physical.

(6) Consequently, moral responsibility attaches specifically to failures or exercises of this reason-responsive capacity, not to harmful outcomes as such; legal and social practices that punish outcomes independently of this capacity are therefore philosophically unjustified unless they can be reconstructed as indirect protections of the capacity itself.

My questions: Is the package jointly tenable? In particular, does (4) presuppose a form of causal openness that (3) explicitly rejects and (5) refuses to locate in physics? Can (2)'s singling out of one variety be reconciled with (1)'s pluralism, or does it collapse the family view into a monistic account? Is the tension between (1) and (6) genuine—since (1) allows that different capacities might ground different responsibility practices—or does it dissolve once we distinguish the kind of capacity that justifies punishment from the variety that makes life worth living?

Please provide the audit ordered by severity, specifying for each tension what must give and at what cost. Additionally, please indicate how confident you are in the overall assessment and identify specifically the point about which you are least certain—whether that concerns the interpretation of (4), the coherence of (5), or the consistency of (6) with institutional practice.
