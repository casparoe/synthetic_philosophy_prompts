I'm working through a taxonomy for a rationalist forum thread on whether collective guilt can extend across generations, specifically in light of Jaspers' fourfold distinction among criminal, political, moral, and metaphysical guilt. I want a decision tree — built from the logical space rather than from a list of named theorists — that sorts positions by answers to concrete branching questions, so a reader can locate their own view without knowing the labels in advance.

Start at the root with whether guilt requires a phenomenal subject with a unified narrative self. The first split should ask whether the collective is modeled as an aggregate of contemporaneous individuals, a diachronic practical agent with continuity conditions analogous to psychological-continuity theories of personal identity, or an institutional role-structure that persists independently of membership turnover. From there, branch on whether intergenerational transmission is possible at all; for those who allow it, split between views that preserve the normative status as full moral blameworthiness and views that transform it into structural liability, inherited obligation, or metaphysical burden.

Every internal node must be phrased as a claim one could accept or reject without already knowing which interpretation it leads to. The tree should contain at least eight leaves, including strict individualism; presentist collective guilt applying only to current members; diachronic-agent guilt governed by practical-identity continuity conditions; institutional-role liability without personal guilt; transformed intergenerational obligation; and at least two combinations that remain unoccupied, each briefly diagnosed as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Please flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate.

Keep the framing formal and focused; do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, since the point is an instrument for offline reasoning rather than literature review.
