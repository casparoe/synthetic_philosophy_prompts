Compose a villanelle in which the speaker is a community's shared remembrance confronting an archival discovery that undermines the narrative by which the community understands its own continuity. Fix two refrains: "What binds us is the story we keep" and "The documents say it happened differently." Let the poem's structure enact the conceptual deadlock: the alternating lines should represent the competing authorities of mnemonic solidarity and historical correction, and the final couplet—where both refrains must appear together—should leave them locked in mutual dependence without synthesis. Develop the specific stakes: to accept the archive is to risk dissolving the present social bonds constituted by the remembered past, while to reject it is to maintain identity at the cost of deliberate epistemic refusal. Do not resolve which should give way; the form's circular repetition should show that each claim defines itself against the other, ending not with victory but with co-existence. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with the philosophy of collective memory and the ethics of historical revision would recognize the tension, and complete enough that the form itself does the argumentative work.
