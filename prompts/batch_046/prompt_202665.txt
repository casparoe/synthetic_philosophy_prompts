I have been working through an institutional-design problem that seems to sit adjacent to Sen's liberal paradox, but I am unsure whether it is genuinely distinct or merely the familiar conflict between minimal liberalism and the Pareto principle rendered in a more complex form. Here is the configuration. There are three agents, each holding a minimal liberal right over a separate domain: Agent A over her own information environment, Agent B over the pedagogical canon in his school, and Agent C over the risk-threshold protocol in her workplace. The social planner must select a policy that fixes values for all three domains simultaneously. The domains are not preference-independent: the information environment shapes the student body that B teaches, B's curriculum trains the safety officers whose judgment C's protocol governs, and C's risk culture feeds back into the sources that A considers credible. Each agent's preference ordering is defined solely over her own domain, and we apply a weak Pareto condition—if every agent strictly prefers one policy to another on the dimensions they care about, the latter is excluded.

The result is not simply that liberal rights conflict with Pareto efficiency, but that they generate a specific cyclic delegation loop. For any two policies, one can construct a third that respects two agents' rights and Pareto-dominates the second with respect to the third agent's domain, yet violates the third agent's minimal right; traversing these possibilities yields a closed cycle in which protecting A forces a global state that undermines B, protecting B forces one that undermines C, and protecting C loops back to undermine A, with no fixed point that honors all three rights together with Pareto. The agents' preference orderings over their own domains never change; what shifts is the global consequence of exercising those rights.

Does this loop have an established name—perhaps as an interdependent or multi-dimensional extension of Sen's paradox—or is it essentially the original result with extra agents and causal linkages? I want a committed answer, not a survey of options: state clearly whether you regard this as a named variant or a separate phenomenon, specify exactly what distinguishes it from Sen's two-agent framing (the dimensional interdependence, the cyclic structure, or something else), and if you think it is unnamed, propose a precise term with a definition and clear boundary conditions indicating which institutional configurations it covers and which fall back into standard Sen-style impossibility.
