Okonkwo rejects this move and advances a modified variant of Black's thought experiment: she asks us to imagine two macroscopic crystalline structures, grown in separate vacuum chambers at causally disconnected extremes of an otherwise empty observable universe, with identical internal quantum states maintained through perfect isolation and with no shared frame of reference possible between them. She claims that in this extended case relational properties collapse into mere descriptions of each sphere's local environment, leaving us with two entities that share every qualitative and every genuinely relational feature yet remain numerically distinct.

They should specifically work through whether spatiotemporal position can serve as a non-circular distinguishing feature, whether accepting primitive haecceity is a philosophically acceptable cost, and whether Okonkwo's isolated-crystal variant actually alters the logical structure of Black's original argument or merely magnifies its scale without changing its force. Keep the tone rigorous but conversational, and do not include any request for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, since the audience will be reasoning through the argument live.
