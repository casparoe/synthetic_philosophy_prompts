For a postgraduate philosophy seminar of about ten students with ninety minutes available, design a runnable deliberation activity that makes the discursive dilemma in judgment aggregation felt rather than merely described. The scenario should be a mock institutional review board that must reach a collective verdict on whether to approve a research proposal, evaluated across three binary premises—scientific merit, ethical safeguard, and institutional fit—where the group is split into two subgroups using incompatible aggregation rules: one subgroup applies premise-based logical derivation, the other applies direct conclusion-based voting, with neither knowing the other follows a different rule. Provide explicit roles, a phased timeline (individual assessment, structured deliberation, revelation of the hidden rule divergence, forced resolution attempt), rules that create genuine coordination tension without allowing easy compromise, a debrief that maps each crisis point onto the tension between allowing all possible input combinations, treating members equally, and deriving conclusions consistently from premises, and an honest note on which structural feature—specifically the impossibility of satisfying all three conditions simultaneously—cannot be reproduced in the room and must be supplied by the facilitator. Ensure the idea emerges from the rules and payoffs rather than from post-hoc explanation, and do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
