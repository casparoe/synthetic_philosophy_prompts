Read Mary Shelley's Frankenstein through the lens of responsability gaps for autonomous systems. Victor builds the creature and then abandons it; the creature acts with kind of autonomy Victor never fully controls. Focus on the scene where creature asks for a companion and Victor refuses — some readers say this is moment he could have closed gap, others say it was already unbridgeable. Does Shelley dramatize a real gap, or does she show Victor using "autonomy" as excuse for negligence? Apply the distinction between design-time control (what you bake in before it runs) and run-time control (what you can steer once its going). Where does the novel resist that framing? Don't reduce the book to illustration, and say what story adds that pure philosophy misses.
