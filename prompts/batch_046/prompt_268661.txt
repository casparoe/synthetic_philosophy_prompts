In an undergraduate seminar on political obligation I am helping design, we have been working through an instructor's implicit taxonomy of resistance rather than relying on standard textbook classifications. Her verdicts so far have been as follows: open tax refusal directed at an unjust war, with explicit willingness to accept imprisonment, counts as justified civil disobedience; a masked blockade of a supply route that imposes economic harm on uninvolved parties is unjustified and does not qualify as civil disobedience; a whistleblower who anonymously leaks classified evidence of illegality and then flees jurisdiction is not engaging in civil disobedience at all, though she treats it as a separate, excusable form of moral resistance; a peaceful sit-in at a government office conducted openly with acceptance of arrest, but undertaken without any prior attempt at legal remedy, is unjustified as civil disobedience though distinguishable from mere criminality; a legal boycott operating entirely within statutory bounds is not civil disobedience; and a researcher who deliberately corrupts a government dataset to expose systemic fraud, then reveals the corruption and accepts professional ruin, falls outside civil disobedience into a different moral category altogether, despite her expressed sympathy for the act.

Next semester we plan to present a new case for evaluation. A group of public-school teachers has spent two years pursuing failed legal challenges against a state-mandated curriculum they consider pedagogically and ethically harmful. They now continue teaching all required subjects but openly omit the contested curricular modules, accept likely termination, and frame their refusal as both a professional duty and a political protest directed at legislative reform. Given her track record, what verdict should we expect? Identify the rule or rules she seems to be running—whether something like a modified Rawlsian framework with an unusually strict exhaustion-of-legal-remedies condition, or a distinction based on communicative openness versus destructive or deceptive method, or another logic entirely—and specify which feature of the teachers' case is doing the decisive work. Finally, propose a further hypothetical case that would best distinguish between the leading interpretations of her position.
