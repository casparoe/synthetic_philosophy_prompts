I have recently found myself in recurring arguments with friends about whether the last twelve millennia of human societies exhibit a coherent directional arc—toward larger scale, deeper hierarchy, and state formation—or whether that appearance is largely a projection of modern state-thinking onto a far messier past. Having encountered Harari’s account of cognitive revolutions and large-scale fictional cooperation, Diamond’s thesis about geographic endowments and agricultural diffusion, and Graeber and Wengrow’s counter-narrative emphasizing deliberate political choice and seasonal flexibility, I want to locate my own commitments precisely. Rather than an essay comparing authors, I need a diagnostic taxonomy built from concrete claims: a decision tree or branching classification for an intellectually engaged but non-specialist discussion group, designed so that someone can answer each node without already knowing which interpretation it leads to.

Please construct a tree that begins with a root commitment about whether there is a unidirectional trajectory from small-scale egalitarian bands to complex states that demands a single dominant explanation. From there, branch on specific answerable divisions: whether agriculture constituted an irreversible social trap or could be adopted and abandoned within seasonal cycles; whether large-scale cooperation depends primarily on shared fictional narratives that scale beyond face-to-face interaction, or on material conditions alone; whether geographic and biological endowments determine political form irrespective of local agency; and whether societies possess what might be called seasonal dual morphology—the capacity to oscillate deliberately between hierarchical and egalitarian structures within the same population across the year. The leaves should include Harari-style cognitive determinism; Diamond-style geographic determinism; the Graeber-Wengrow view of conscious political flexibility; a strong anti-grand-narrative position that treats the arc itself as an anachronistic historiographical fiction; and at least one genuinely unnamed or unoccupied combination, with a brief diagnosis of whether its emptiness stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Every node must be phrased as a concrete case or claim, never asking “are you a Harari-style thinker?” The tree should explicitly flag any pair of leaves it cannot genuinely separate—for instance, if a purely energetic-technological determinism and Diamond’s geographic package make identical predictions about Eurasian state formation within your framework, say so openly. Keep the instrument to six or seven leaves.

Finally, please say how confident you are in the logical separation of the branches—particularly in your placement of the seasonal-flexibility and anti-narrative positions relative to one another—and single out the specific unoccupied combination or inseparable pair about which you are least certain.
