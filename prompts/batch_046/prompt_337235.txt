My verdicts on biological function attribution, and the principle—if any—that unifies them. A heart transplanted into a recipient pumps blood properly, so it has that function, despite belonging to a different evolutionary lineage. The human appendix, now serving mainly immune storage rather than digestion, has immune storage as its real function, not merely a vestigial digestive one. A synthetic enzyme designed de novo, with no evolutionary precursors, that stably catalyzes a reaction inside a living cell, has only an engineered purpose and no biological function. A gene selected ancestrally for temperature sensing that now regulates circadian rhythms retains temperature sensing as its true function, with circadian regulation being a spandrel. A viral gene that lyses host cells functions properly relative to the virus but malfunctions relative to the host, and both judgments stand. A kidney kept alive ex vivo in a perfusion machine, filtering waste that never returns to an organism, performs its causal role but lacks biological function altogether. Can an etiological account, a causal-role account, or a hybrid capture all six? If hybrid, state precisely when evolutionary history governs and when present contribution or organismal embedding governs, and identify which verdict a simpler principle must reject.
