I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of mind, currently preparing a chapter on comparative cognition. In a recent seminar we discussed Dennett's taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures, and I found myself advancing a rather stark position: that phenomenal consciousness is not distributed gradually across the animal kingdom, but is instead an all-or-nothing property that emerges only at the Gregorian stage, when an organism can construct and evaluate explicit, culturally transmitted representations of its own mental states. On this view, while cephalopods and corvids may be highly sophisticated Popperian problem-solvers, they nevertheless lack the recursive representational architecture required for subjective experience to be genuinely present in them; and attempts to attribute consciousness to Skinnerian or Darwinian systems—including most invertebrates and certainly all present artificial systems—commit a category error by conflating adaptive flexibility with subjective experience.

But I want to be genuinely challenged, without being directed towards a reading list or bibliography. What would actually count against this position? I want the specific observations, conceptual arguments, or formal considerations that ought to move me—including what defenders of gradualist or panpsychist alternatives would themselves concede as damaging if true, and including whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability and whether that insulation is a problem for a claim of this kind. In particular, I am interested in whether there is a decisive distinction between Popperian and Gregorian cognition that does not already smuggle in the conclusion, whether evolutionary continuity arguments genuinely undermine the all-or-nothing claim, and whether the position makes predictions about artificial or synthetic minds that differ from rival accounts in a testable way. Please distinguish evidence that would be merely awkward—say, complex tool use in an apparently Skinnerian creature—from evidence that would be genuinely decisive—say, demonstrating that a Popperian creature can report on its own qualitative states without Gregorian representational resources. I also want an honest assessment of whether this framing collapses into a form of linguistic exceptionalism that is independently implausible.
