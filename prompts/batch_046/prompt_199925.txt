I spent three decades in experimental condensed-matter physics and have only recently begun studying philosophy systematically. Like many scientists, I find Weinberg’s, Hawking’s, and Feynman’s dismissals of philosophy intuitively compelling—philosophy rarely changed anything in my laboratory—yet I also recognize that conceptual clarification is not useless in principle. Could you explain, at a somewhat technical level, the conceptual distinction between philosophy as a heuristic generator of empirically testable hypotheses and philosophy as an interpretive framework that assigns semantic or ontological content to formalisms that are already empirically adequate? Specifically, I want to understand whether debates over quantum-mechanical interpretations—such as Everettian, Bohmian, or QBist frameworks—represent a genuine case of scientific underdetermination that demands philosophical adjudication, or whether they constitute an ornamental superstructure that leaves predictive content entirely untouched. Please avoid broad surveys and citation lists; instead, dissect the logical claim that interpretive commitments are epistemically inert, and clarify whether and how such commitments could ever feed back into the design of empirical research without collapsing into ordinary methodological choice.
