Write a short dialogue between two philosophers, R and S, arguing over whether Williamsons luminosity argument really defeats the KK principle. Dont just replay the usual warming-up case. Instead, have them zero in on a mental state they nickname "epistemic twilight" — youre genuinely convinced of p, but the state itself carries a built-in pull away from commitment, a kind of self-obscurity that isnt just vagueness or margin-for-error. R should defend KK by saying that even in twilight you can know youre in it, and that second-order knowledge just tracks the first-order state with noise; S should attack by saying the self-obscurity is internal to the state, so you can be in twilight without being in a position to know it, which breaks luminosity without relying on boundary vagueness. Make them respond to each other directly — R might accuse S of sneaking in a hidden indexical, S might reply that R is redefining knowledge to save the principle. Keep both sides sharp, no strawmen, and dont have them cite papers or ask for reading lists.
