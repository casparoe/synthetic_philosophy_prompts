I am a retired biologist who has recently begun studying moral philosophy in earnest. At present I am working through Jeremy Bentham's manuscript essays on sexual morality, particularly the sections in which he treats practices he describes as involving a non-natural use of the sexual faculty. There is one stretch of argument that I find conceptually slippery, and I should be grateful for help in disentangling it.

In the passage in question, Bentham appears to claim that widespread moral disgust toward certain sexual practices is analogous to culturally acquired gastronomic aversions—say, the revulsion some societies feel toward eating particular animals—rather than to a direct perception of physical harm. From this he seems to infer that, because the aversion is not grounded in tangible injury to any party, it ought not to enter the legislative calculus at all. Yet elsewhere in the same manuscript he allows that moral pain—the sympathetic distress caused by witnessing others violate social norms—counts as a real harm in the felicific calculus, provided it is not merely the projection of an irrational prejudice.

What puzzles me is whether these two commitments can be held together without tension. If the disgust felt by the majority produces genuine moral pain in them when they observe the practice, does Bentham have any principled reason for excluding that pain from the sum, other than his prior conviction that the disgust itself is groundless? And if he does exclude it, on what basis does he distinguish between a groundless sympathetic pain that should be discounted and a well-founded one that should be weighed? I should particularly like to understand whether his comparison with food preferences holds up under scrutiny: after all, gastronomic aversion rarely produces sustained sympathetic suffering in observers, whereas sexual norms seem to generate precisely that sustained, collective moral distress.

Could you help me see whether there is a coherent reading of Bentham on this point, or whether the tension I have identified is real? I am not asking for a bibliography or a reading list; I simply want to understand the conceptual architecture of the argument as it stands.
