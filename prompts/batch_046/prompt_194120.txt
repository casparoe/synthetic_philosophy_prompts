Plan the teaching sequence: what conceptual distinction must be secured first (for instance, between judgments and external events, or between CBT's locus of control and Stoic prohairesis) so that the dichotomy is not misread; the single concrete scenario that should carry the entire session, with explicit criteria for what it must include and exclude, and why standard career or athletic examples fail; the misconceptions each audience subgroup will likely form—such as treating indifference to outcomes as emotional suppression, or interpreting partial influence as full control—and the exact stage at which each should be intercepted; a final diagnostic question that reveals whether participants have understood the philosophical claim rather than adopted a therapeutic framing; and which contemporary practice, such as the trichotomy of control proposed by some modern Stoics or the exercise of premeditatio malorum, should be deliberately excluded at this level and named as out of scope.
