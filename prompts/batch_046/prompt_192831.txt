I'm trying to isolate what distinguishes a genuine handicap signal from other forms of costly display, and my judgments across cases are not fully consistent. A peacock's tail that slows flight and attracts predators is clearly a handicap. A founder who publicly destroys a working prototype to demonstrate she has nothing to fear from competition feels like costly signaling, but the harm is staged and temporary rather than an enduring impairment of her capabilities. A religious order that requires novices to sleep outside in winter: if hypothermia is genuinely risked, this counts; but if the abbots secretly provide hidden warming blankets unknown to the novices, does the signal remain honest, and does my answer change if the novices eventually discover them? A job candidate who declines a substantially higher salary elsewhere without negotiation, accepting the lower-paid role to signal loyalty: I am inclined to treat this as handicap-like, yet the cost is purely counterfactual—she never actually bears the lost income—rather than a physical burden. A corporation that maintains an ornate, persistently loss-making flagship store that serves no logistical purpose looks like status display, but since the cost is borne by shareholders rather than by the executives who design it, I judge that it is not a handicap signal at all. By contrast, a species that evolves vivid coloration in an environment where predators are color-blind seems to involve no survival cost; is this still signaling, or merely a neutral trait?

Is there a coherent principle—perhaps one that requires the cost to be intrinsic to the signaler, unavoidable, and not transferable or concealed—that captures all of these verdicts? If not, which of my judgments should be dropped? Please also state what your principle predicts for a case I have not considered: a surgeon who routinely refuses AI-assisted diagnostics in straightforward procedures, accepting an occasional missed diagnosis she could have prevented, in order to demonstrate that her unaided clinical judgment remains superior.
