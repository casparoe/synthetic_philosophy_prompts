So I turned 40 last month and my dad died suddenly right after, which sounds like a cliché but now I'm actually lying awake wondering what the hell makes anything worth doing. Not in a depressed way exactly, just... I can't unsee the arbitrariness. Anyway I want to figure out what I actually think about meaning, not just repeat what I've read.

Here are my gut verdicts, and they're kind of all over the place:

1. Getting up every night for my kid who has nightmares - exhuasting, repetitive, no "achievement" to point at. But it feels genuinely meaningful, like this is exactly what I'm supposed to be doing.

2. Finishing my PhD dissertation after six years - everyone said it should be the peak. I defended, got the degree, and felt... nothing. Maybe relief, but not meaning. Now I wonder if I was just chasing a credential.

3. Helping a stranger whose car broke down in a snowstorm last winter - pulled over, got them running, drove away. For like two days I felt like "okay, that's the good stuff." Then it faded completely. But the kid-nightmares thing hasn't faded, even though it's been years.

4. Deciding to embrace the absurd like Camus talks about - I tried imagining Sisyphus happy, living in the present, not asking for cosmic justification. It worked intellectually for about a week, then started feeling like a trick I'm playing on myself. Not meaningful, just distracted.

5. Volunteering at a food bank every saturday - should be meaningful, right? But after three months it felt like maintenance, like I was just delaying entropy. Meanwhile, fixing my neighbor's broken fence after a storm (one time, no commitment) felt more real, even though it was smaller.

6. Writing this list right now - trying to be honest about what I feel - actually feels somewhat meaningful, even though it's just navel-gazing. That bothers me because it shouldn't be enough.

So what's going on? Is their a principle that gets all of these, or close? I feel like meaning has to involve some kind of connection to something outside myself (the kid, the stranger) but also has to feel real from the inside, not just like I'm performing virtue. But then why does the food bank feel hollow while the fence feels real? And why does the dissertation - which is "my" achievement - feel empty?

Also, what should I think about the case I keep waffling on: I have this idea of quitting my job and moving to a small town to just garden and read. Part of me thinks that sounds like the good life, part of me thinks it's just avoiding the hard parts of being connected to people. Would your principle say that's meaningful or a cop-out?

Please tell me if there's one coherent account behind my verdicts, or if I have to drop some. And be honest about which ones don't fit - especially the dissertation one vs the kid one, since they both involve long-term commitment but feel totally seperate. If you can, also say what your principle predicts for one case I haven't mentioned: taking care of an elderly parent with dementia for years, where there's no gratitude or recognition and the work is mostly cleaning and waiting. Does that stay meaningful or fade like the food bank?
