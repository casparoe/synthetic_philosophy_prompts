I am preparing a graduate seminar on social epistemology and want a diagnostic instrument that maps where students genuinely stand on the reductionism debate about testimony, rather than which label they favour. To prevent answers that merely rehearse textbook summaries of face-to-face conversation, I want the tree constructed around a somewhat artificial but concrete chain: a climate scientist publishes a quantitative finding that she herself never directly observed, having relied entirely on an automated satellite-data programme that applies corrections she cannot manually verify; a policy adviser then cites this finding in governmental testimony.

Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy that takes a respondent from an initial commitment—"In this chain, the adviser can acquire knowledge if and only if the data pipeline is reliable"—through a sequence of branching questions on which reductionist and anti-reductionist accounts actually diverge. Each internal node should pose a concrete question about this pipeline-to-policy chain: for example, whether the adviser needs independent positive reasons to trust the scientist personally; whether understanding the satellite corrections is necessary for testimonial entitlement; whether the scientist's lack of direct observation severs the testimonial link; or whether meta-competence about one's instruments is required for speaker credibility. Every leaf should be a named stance in the debate—such as strong global reductionism, local reductionism with a reliability condition, pure anti-reductionism, or hybrid views that permit testimonial entitlement only when the speaker possesses appropriate second-order awareness of her source.

Phrase every node so that a respondent could answer it without already knowing which interpretation the answer leads to. Where combinations of answers leave a logical space unoccupied, note the gap briefly and diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored in the literature. Finally, flag any pair of named positions that your tree cannot cleanly separate given these particular branch conditions, and explain why their divergence must lie elsewhere—perhaps in commitments about the nature of understanding, the scope of social cognition, or the role of institutional trust rather than in this specific automated-testimony case. Use British spelling and avoid bibliographic requests or citation lists.
