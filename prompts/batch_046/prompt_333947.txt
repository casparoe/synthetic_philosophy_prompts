I'm on the internal review board for our university's autonomous reasoning institute, and we just recieved a proposal for something they're calling a "reflective consistency protocol." The basic idea is that their agent can derive predictions about its own future beliefs and then feed those derivations back in as premises for its current planning. One of the senior reviewers—he's done work in modal logic—said this runs straight into the Löbian obstacle, where if an agent trusts its own reasoning framework enough to apply it reflexively, it either collapses into overacceptance or ends up distrusting itself entirely, with no stable middle ground. The research team isn't denying the theorem, but they want to deploy anyway with a "trust-decay" parameter that weakens the feedback loop after two iterations, claiming that's philosophically different from full self-trust and avoids the collapse.

We need to draft the board's written opinion for Thursday's meeting: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with reasoning the researchers can actually respond to. The memo should take seriously whether the Löbian problem is a hard rationality constraint or just an engineering glitch you can patch with decay rules, and it should address the worry that an agent that can't fully endorse its own reasoning isn't genuinely autonomous in the sense the institute promises in its charter. I'm genuinely unsure whether self-trust has to be all-or-nothing or if partial, decaying trust is rationally coherent—there's something about the agent's authority over itself that I can't quite pin down. Can you write the opinion? Make it formal enough to circulate, include a clear bottom line with a timeframe if we're conditioning approval, and don't include citations or a reading list since half the board works remotely without database access. Also, please deal honestly with the strongest argument on the losing side rather than just dismissing it.
