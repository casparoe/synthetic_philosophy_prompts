I'm a high school student in an ethics seminar where we've been studying Confucian role ethics, particularly how filial obligations (xiao) interact with other relational duties when the conditions of presence and ritual are altered by technology. Our instructor has given consistent rulings across four hypothetical cases, but my study group is divided over what she would say about a fifth situation we have constructed, and I would like help reasoning through it in a way that coheres with her track record rather than simply appealing to intuition.

Her rulings so far are these. First, she approved of a daughter who relocated overseas for work but sustained weekly video rituals with her parents and sent regular material support, judging that physical absence does not dissolve xiao when relational presence is actively maintained by the child. Second, she rejected a son's decision to live nearby but delegate all elder care to paid staff while he pursued leisure, ruling that outsourcing the labor of care without personal emotional engagement violates the relational core of the child-parent bond. Third, she conditionally approved of a student who missed an important examination to attend to her father's serious illness, but only on the condition that she formally suspended rather than abandoned her academic obligations, indicating that competing role duties should be reconciled through structural adjustment rather than simple neglect. Fourth, she rejected a proposal that an adult child could satisfy ancestral obligations purely through automated digital memorials lacking any personal emotional investment, stating that ritual without interior participation reduces form to emptiness.

The new case involves a teenager whose parents are secular and neglect traditional ancestral rites. She trains a large language model on her deceased grandmother's letters and conversations, then uses this system to guide her through seasonal rituals—offerings, narrative remembrance, and moral consultation—that her parents refuse to perform. She treats the AI as a relational mediator that restores intergenerational continuity, does not deceive anyone about its artificial nature, and reports genuine emotional engagement and moral growth through the practice; her parents know of it and remain indifferent. Would our instructor approve, reject, or approve with conditions? Which of her four precedents is the closest analog, and if her record genuinely underdetermines the outcome, what principle would she be adopting with each choice? In particular, does the presence of sincere emotional investment and structural integration outweigh the synthetic mediation of the relational partner, placing this closer to the approved overseas case or the conditionally approved exam case, or does the delegation of the intergenerational bond to an artificial agent align it with the rejected cases of outsourced care and empty digital form?
