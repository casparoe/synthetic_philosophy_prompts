I'm preparing an essay for a seminar in metaphysics that examines the standard taxonomy of disposition-blockage, particularly the distinction between finks and masks. In the usual framing, a fink destroys or removes the categorical basis of a disposition exactly when the manifestation conditions are met—consider the poison that eliminates fragility at the moment of impact—whereas a mask preserves the categorical basis but interposes an independent blocker that prevents manifestation, as when a fragile vase is wrapped in protective foam. These categories are typically presented as exhaustive, yet I think they miss a structurally intermediate case that I want to explore.

I propose a class of cases I call coupled masks: the blocker is not continuously present and independent of the stimulus, but is activated precisely by the conditions that would trigger the disposition, without altering the underlying categorical basis. For a mechanical example, consider a live wire connected to a circuit breaker calibrated to open exactly at the current threshold that would cause electrocution; the wire's conductive properties remain intact, but its dangerous manifestation is coupled to its own suppression mechanism. For a psychological parallel, consider a disposition to social anxiety that is suppressed only by a coping mechanism that engages specifically in large-group settings. In both instances, removing the blocker allows standard manifestation, yet the blocker is causally tied to the trigger conditions rather than being an extrinsic obstacle. I tentatively argue that recognizing coupled masks requires adding a structural dimension—perhaps the temporal or causal profile of the blocker relative to the stimulus—to our taxonomy, though I worry this may collapse into a temporary fink if one holds that relational context partly constitutes the categorical basis.
