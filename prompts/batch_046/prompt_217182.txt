Call the guiding idea causal-functional integrationism: a plurality of entities compose a single object if and only if they form a network in which the state of each member is non-trivially constrained by the states of every other, yielding a unified functional profile that none exhibits alone. Apply it to a research team linked by shared cloud repositories and real-time collaborative tools—each researcher's mental states are constrained by the digital infrastructure—so the team plus its technology literally composes one distributed cognitive object. Extend the network through citation links, supply chains, and communication grids, and by transitivity the integration sweeps outward, eventually absorbing every causally linked entity into a single vast "noosphere-object." The conclusion is ontologically catastrophic—ordinary chairs, bodies, and laptops dissolve into one undifferentiated whole—yet each premise feels defensible. Which step should I reject, and at what cost? I want to hold onto the intuition that functional integration matters for composition, but I also want to preserve the reality of discrete ordinary objects.
