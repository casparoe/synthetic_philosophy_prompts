I was arguing with my roommate about whether marxism can actually explain culture without just saying economy did it, and I realized my own view is kind of a mess. So heres where I land: (1) I think the economic base — production relations plus productive forces — is the primary driver of legal and political superstructures in the long run, but (2) I also think IP law and patent regimes can loop back and reshape the forces of production within a generation, not just reflect them. Then theres (3) my belief that religious revival movements are mostly epiphenomenal to material contradictions, not independent causal forces, except that (4) I want to say the 19th-century abolitionist push was genuinely led by moral-philosophical ideas that cant be reduced to economic interest, and that this is a feature of the theory, not an exception I have to apologize for. Oh and (5) I reject technological determinism but I do think energy regimes — coal versus solar — constrain political possibility in ways class relations alone dont capture.

Do these five actually hang together? Which pairs are in real tension and which just look like it? I especially need to know if holding (2) and (3) together is just handwaving, and whether (4) forces me to ditch (1) or only to complicate it. Rank the tensions by how bad they are, and tell me the least costly revision if I have to drop something.
