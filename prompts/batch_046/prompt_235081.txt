I work as a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I have been trying to apply Nietzsche's tripartite distinction—monumental, antiquarian, and critical history, drawn from his early essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life—to the way engineering teams relate to legacy codebases and technical debt. My working position is that these three modes are jointly exhaustive in this applied domain: monumental history inspires ambitious rewrites that glorify a future architecture, antiquarian history preserves legacy systems primarily to maintain organizational continuity and identity, and critical history justifies refactoring or deletion in the name of present health. I want to defend the stronger claim that no genuinely distinct fourth mode exists—specifically, I reject what we might call "generative history," in which historical patterns (past architectures, deprecated design patterns, version-control genealogies) are synthesized into novel structures that neither glorify the past, preserve it intact, nor destroy it, but instead treat it as a probabilistic training set for future construction.

What would have to be true for this Nietzschean-exhaustiveness claim to be wrong? I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they would be: which conceptual distinctions, which empirical patterns in how teams actually use archives and commit histories, and which analogies to other domains (for instance, evolutionary biology or legal precedent) would genuinely break the tripartite schema rather than merely stretching one category. I also want to know what defenders of Nietzsche's taxonomy would themselves concede as counting against it—perhaps cases where critical history collapses back into antiquarian preservation, or where monumental aspiration becomes indistinguishable from willful ignorance of the past. Finally, I want an honest assessment of whether the framework risks being qualified into unfalsifiability, such that any apparently novel historiographical practice can be redescribed as a mixture of the three, and whether that insulation is a virtue for a conceptual taxonomy of this kind or a vice that renders it useless for evaluation.
