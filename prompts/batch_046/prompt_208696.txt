Exam question: Confucian role ethics rejects the liberal idea of an autonomous, rights-bearing individual in favor of a self constituted through relational networks. Drawing on the distinction between ordered relational patterns (lun) and authoritative personhood (ren), analyze whether the introduction of an artificial mediator—specifically, a predictive algorithm that advises adult children on eldercare decisions—creates a genuinely new role within the five cardinal relationships (wulun) or merely extends existing filial obligations (xiao) through instrumental means. In your answer, address Roger Ames's claim that roles are achieved through interactive practice rather than ascribed by birth or status, and consider whether algorithmic mediation can participate in the mutual cultivation that defines relational personhood.

Student answer:
Confucian role ethics holds that moral identity is formed through the wulun, the five cardinal relationships, rather than through intrinsic individual properties. The term lun refers to these ordered interaction patterns, while ren—often rendered as benevolence or authoritative personhood—names the quality cultivated through correct participation in them. Roger Ames argues that roles are achieved through sustained performative engagement, not assigned by birth; this extends Mencius's view from the four sprouts that human nature tends toward goodness and requires only the right relational conditions to develop fully.

Applying this framework, an algorithmic eldercare advisor lacks consciousness and therefore cannot possess ren. Because every position in the wulun requires reciprocal humanization, the AI cannot occupy a relational role analogous to parent, child, or minister. Nevertheless, since roles are achieved rather than fixed, the adult child can construct a new "algorithmic filial" obligation by treating the system's outputs as binding directives, much as a minister follows the ruler's commands within the bond of loyalty (zhong). In this respect, the mediator functions like a ritual specialist who stands outside the family yet guides its moral performance. Thus, even though no mutual obligation exists with the machine itself, the child's achieved practice creates a genuine extension of xiao through instrumental dependence.
