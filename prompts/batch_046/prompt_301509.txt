But I think there's something more structural going on that connects to this thing called the dollar auction, where people bid for a dollar but the loser also pays, so you keep going just to avoid losing more. In Melville's novel, the crew isn't bidding cash exactly, but they're locked into a collective escalation: every mile they sail without the whale makes turning back feel like throwing away all the sufferng they've already paid for. It's like a distributed version of that auction, where no one person holds the full cost, so nobody can pull the plug alone.

Could you read Moby-Dick through that lens — specifically this distributed-collective twist on escalation of commitment?
