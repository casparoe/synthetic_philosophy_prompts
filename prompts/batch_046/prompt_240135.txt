okay so i've been trying to map out why frankfurt cases don't just kill PAP for actions but also seem to eat epistimic responsibility, and i think i've talked myself into a corner. here's the chain:

(1) in the classic frankfurt setup, Jones kills Smith and is responsible even though Black's device makes it impossible for him to refrain — the blocker only kicks in if he tries to stop.

(2) the usual defense is that the blocker never actually interferes; Jones's own mental process runs untouched, so the actual sequence carries the moral weight.

(3) now imagine a belief blocker: if you don't come to believe p on your own, a device implants p. but you do come to believe p naturally. by the same logic, you're responsible for believing p, even though you couldn't have failed to believe it.

(4) but epistimic responsibility seems to require that you could have withheld assent — otherwise justified belief collapses into caused belief.

(5) so if frankfurt reasoning applies here, nobody can ever be blamed for a false belief they couldn't avoid, which sounds wrong.

(6) yet if we block the reasoning for belief, we need a principled reason why mental causation is different from physical causation, and i can't see one that doesn't also break the original frankfurt case.

(7) therefore either frankfurt doesn't actually refute PAP, or it refutes PAP everywhere and destroys epistemic agency too.

where does this slip? is there a hidden premise between (2) and (3), or is the cost just accepting (7)? i kind of want to keep the intuition that Jones is still responsible, but i don't want to lose the idea that belief-formation can be rationally assessed. which step should i reject and what does it cost me?
