In philosophy of mind, functionalism is often defended with the analogy that mental states are like computer programs: just as the same software can run on a laptop, a server, or a phone, so the same psychological role can be realized in neurons, silicon, or whatever substrate gets the causal job done. This is used by defenders of liberal functionalism and artificial-mind arguments to license the conclusion that cognition and even consciousness are not biologically proprietary — that multiple realizability is not a remote possibility but a structural feature of the view, and that an artificial system with the right functional architecture literally instantiates the mental states we do.
