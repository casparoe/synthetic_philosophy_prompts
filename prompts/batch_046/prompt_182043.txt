I'm taking a seminar on philosophy of mind and the cognitive science of artificial intelligence, and we've been working through Dennett's intentional stance and its application to large language models. In our discussions, a recurring analogy comes up: attributing beliefs to an LLM is like attributing strategic intentions to a corporation—say, claiming that "Google believes the market is shifting toward AI" or that "the firm wants to maximize shareholder value." The analogy is meant to show that such talk is purely predictive and organizational, not literal mental-state attribution: just as there is no single corporate mind that literally holds these beliefs, there is no unified mental subject inside the model. Those who push the analogy conclude that we should treat LLM outputs as we treat corporate press releases—useful for forecasting behavior, but not evidence of genuine belief.

But I'm unsure how far the mapping actually holds, especially after our unit on distributed cognition and collective intentionality. Could you lay out the correspondence explicitly—what in the LLM plays the role of the board of directors, the shareholders, the organizational culture, the legal charter? Then identify where the analogy breaks down: for instance, corporations have legal personhood and decision procedures that are intentionally designed by agents with original intentionality, whereas LLMs are trained by gradient descent on token prediction without a comparable governance structure. I'd also like you to address whether the analogy quietly imports the idea that distributed belief requires institutional design, and whether that actually undermines the anti-attribution conclusion rather than supporting it—since some philosophers of collective cognition argue that distributed systems can literally hold beliefs in a non-derivative way. Finally, tell me whether the conclusion—that belief-talk about AI is always merely instrumental—survives once these disanalogies are on the table, or whether a better analogy (perhaps to a distributed biological system rather than a legal entity) would be more appropriate.
