I work for a mid-size city planning dept and we keep getting pitched these "adaptive infrastructure" projects — localized albedo pilots, modular seawalls that shift tidal flow, carbon-capture bioreactors in public parks. None are obviously toxic, but they all share this creep feature: once built they reshape future options, risks spread across decades and neighborhoods, and there's no single catastrophic threshhold, just a slow drift into a worse baseline. We have to review them in about 45 minutes, we have no philosphy staff, and council wants a yes/no with a one-page justification.

Can you turn the precautionary principle into an actual decision procedure my team can run? I need steps in order, what counts as passing each one, where it forces a "no" or "delay," and where it has to hand off to council because it's values not risk. It should handle the hard cases: science is genuinely uncertain but the proposal is cheap and technically reversible, and cases where harm isn't a bang but a slow bleed. Apply it to three proposals: painting a downtown garage roof with high-reflectivity coating; installing a pilot direct-air-capture unit in a residential park with a 20-year contract; and building a temporary tidal gate that protects one waterfront district but accelerates erosion in the adjacent one. Make sure one comes out with an answer my team won't like but the procedure demands anyway. Also be honest about what this can't do — where it misfires or just dresses politics up as analysis. No bibliographies, just the checklist and the worked cases.
