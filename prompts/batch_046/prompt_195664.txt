You serve as the lead interpretation officer for an international heritage-site consortium that manages commemorative landscapes in jurisdictions where "lost cause" narratives circulate: American Civil War battlefields, the Masada plateau, the Kosovo Field memorial complex, and the Dunkirk evacuation beaches. Your consortium operates under a working framework you call counterfactual honor analysis, which treats any lost-cause framing as a three-layer claim: (1) a suppressed counterfactual about the consequences of victory; (2) a dignity-transfer mechanism assigning present communities an inherited obligation to preserve the grievance; and (3) a pedagogical hazard, in which moralizing defeat suppresses causal explanation of why the defeat occurred. The board has directed you to produce a reusable decision procedure that an on-site curator—without legal or philosophical training—can execute in thirty minutes before signing off on an interpretive panel, guided-tour script, or commemorative ceremony.

Write the procedure as an ordered sequence of tests, with explicit pass/fail/uncertain criteria at each step, and with clear stopping rules: the point where the procedure yields a definite prohibition on lost-cause framing; the point where it permits framing only under strict contextualization conditions; and the point where it must defer to the board because the dispute is one of values rather than historical fact. For each test, supply one probe sentence—a concrete phrase the curator can check for in the proposed text—to ensure reproducibility. Then identify exactly two nodes where judgment is ineliminable: specify the nature of the judgment, the party who must make it, and the record that must be preserved.

Apply the procedure to three proposed framings:
(a) A battlefield-panel text describing the Army of Northern Virginia's retreat as "a noble preservation of regional honor against overwhelming force," with no mention of slavery's role in secession.
(b) A state-sponsored ceremony script at Masada describing the Roman siege as "the moment sovereign identity chose death over assimilation."
(c) A framing drawn from either the Dunkirk or Kosovo contexts that you construct so that the procedure returns a result the asking institution will likely reject. Explain why the procedure produces that result, and whether this reveals a limitation in counterfactual honor analysis or a genuine value conflict that lies outside procedural resolution.

Conclude by stating what the procedure cannot do: which categories of commemorative dispute fall outside its scope, and what residual risk remains even when every step is executed correctly.
