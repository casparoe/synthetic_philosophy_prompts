Consider G. E. Moore's positions in Principia Ethica, where he treats the appreciation of genuinely beautiful objects and intimate personal friendship as paradigm intrinsic goods analyzable through his notion of organic unity, and where he insists that friendship requires mutual awareness and affection rather than unilateral attachment. Apply this framework to two post-Moore phenomena: first, viewers forming deep emotional bonds with non-conscious conversational agents that display apparent empathy and reciprocity but lack any inner life; second, artworks produced by generative models that yield genuinely moving compositions despite the absence of conscious artistic intention. For each case, predict whether Moore would classify the experience or object as possessing intrinsic value in the same category as traditional friendship or art, or whether he would treat it as a deficient analog, identifying the specific feature—mutual consciousness, organic unity, or another criterion—that does the decisive work. Finally, propose a borderline case, such as an artificial system that passes every behavioral test for reciprocal affection while being known to be non-sentient, and explain whether Moore's verdict on it would turn on a metaphysical condition or on the phenomenological quality of the interaction itself.
