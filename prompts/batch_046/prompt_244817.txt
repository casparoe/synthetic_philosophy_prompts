Write an essay on whether any representational or formal system necessarily contains an "inviolate level"—a base that operates within the system yet is not itself constituted by it—taking Escher's Drawing Hands as the central analogy. The image appears to show hands drawing each other in an endless loop, yet the sheet of paper and the unseen artist stand outside the loop; some interpreters take this to prove that representation must always bottom out in an unrepresented substrate. Others contend that with sufficient recursive entanglement a system can be fully self-constituting, leaving no remainder. Weigh these positions, but do not stop at a survey: apply your analysis to one further domain—such as the foundations of arithmetic, the architecture of natural language, or the structure of self-consciousness—and reach a definite, justified conclusion. Is an inviolate level unavoidable, or is a completely tangled hierarchy coherent? Defend your verdict directly; avoid ending on an agnostic or merely balanced note.
