As a novelist serving on the Acquisition Ethics Board of the Meridian Archive of Living Voices, I am preparing the formal opinion for circulation before Thursday’s session. We must render a decision on the Mvezo Bequest: approximately four hundred hours of recorded testimony gathered under a community covenant informed by Ubuntu, in which the speakers are understood not as discrete, self-owning individuals but as nodes within a relational field of personhood constituted by reciprocal recognition. The covenant’s conditions are precise and, from the donors’ standpoint, non-negotiable—display must suppress individual proper names in favor of collective relational designations; research access must be mediated by a custodial council that includes community representatives holding interpretive veto; and any derivative publication must attribute the material to the relational network rather than to named informants. Our charter, however, is built on premises of Western individual authorship: named provenance, discrete informed consent, and assignable copyright, without which our counsel warns we cannot secure institutional liability coverage, maintain accreditation, or defend against future legal challenge.

Half the board regards accommodation as an admirable but institutionally destructive gesture: it dissolves archival responsibility into ambiguity, they argue, and imposes upon our legal framework a metaphysics of the person that our procedures are not designed to recognize. The opposing half regards refusal as procedural neutrality masking a form of extraction, noting that our current framework already enforces an alien ontology of individual possession upon material that was never offered as private property.

Please draft the memo I will circulate. It should recommend a concrete course—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—with a specific implementation framework and timeline if applicable. The reasoning must engage the relational conception of personhood seriously on its own philosophical terms, not reduce it to cultural preference, and must treat the strongest argument from the opposing side with intellectual honesty rather than dismissal. Given my work in fiction concerned with collective narrative voice and the ethics of attribution, the memo should reflect a novelist’s sensitivity to how framing determines whose story is told, without substituting literary impression for institutional reasoning. Do not include bibliographies, requests for citations, appeals to authorities that would require verification, or reading lists.
