I am a doctoral student in philosophy of science organizing a workshop on theories of scientific progress, and I need you to draft the joint statement for an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the debate over Karl Popper’s problem of verisimilitude.

The two parties are:
(1) Restricted-measure defenders, who hold that Miller-type impossibility results apply only to unrestricted classical content-measures, and that formally restricted metrics—evaluated over shared observational fragments, structural isomorphisms, or propositional subsets—preserve a non-trivial, comparative ranking of false theories by proximity to truth.
(2) Benchmark-rejectionists, who hold that any verisimilitude metric requires a canonical, fully specified true theory as its benchmark, that such a benchmark is metaphysically unavailable or operationally inert, and that scientific progress should therefore be captured by structural preservation, problem-solving capacity, or predictive novelty rather than by distance-to-truth.

Please produce the document they should have signed together, structured as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: about the formal structure of Popper’s original proposal; about what Miller’s argument demonstrates and where its scope ends; about what an adequate account of scientific progress must explain (e.g., why some false theories seem to yield deeper explanations or broader predictions than others); and about what both sides regard as inadequate (e.g., equating progress with mere predictive success absent explanatory structure). Second, a clearly demarcated disagreements table stating the precise residual differences in wording each camp would accept for its own position—specifically, whether Camp (1) can dispense with a complete true-theory benchmark by substituting a family of partial models, and whether Camp (2) denies that false theories can stand in any comparative relation of being closer to truth in any restricted domain, or only denies that such relations are scientifically meaningful. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. I expect one crux concerns whether replacing the benchmark with a family of partial models collapses the metric into triviality or leaves it empirically inert; another concerns whether structural-preservation accounts can distinguish between two false theories that preserve prior structure equally well but differ in empirical content or predictive risk. Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides agree is relevant—both should treat the transition from Newtonian gravitation to general relativity as a live test case—and record in advance what each camp predicts about it: whether Camp (1) predicts that general relativity is measurably closer to the true gravitational theory under some restricted metric, and whether Camp (2) predicts that no distance judgment is warranted even in principle, with only structural-preservation or problem-solving gains applying.

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests. Do not invoke highly specialized formal results that depend on notation unfamiliar outside specialist journals; keep the discussion grounded in conceptual distinctions both camps actually defend. Where part of the dispute turns out to be verbal—for example, over whether “truth-likeness” functions as a technical measure or as an honorific label—identify that part explicitly and explain why it is verbal rather than substantive. Write in a formal register appropriate for a workshop document that both parties could put their names to.
