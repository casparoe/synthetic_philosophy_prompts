ok so in my town if you want to replace a single-family house with a duplex or triplex you have to go through discretionary review and neighbors can block it for 'neighborhood character,' but building a bigger mansion on the same lot is by-right, its automatic approval. meanwhile the city just declared a housing emergency and adopted a plan saying we need more units. what theory of property or urban justice does this asymmetry actually presuppose? is it treating ownership like a social license where the community holds a veto over change but not over the status quo? and can that be squared with the usual liberal idea that zoning is just an exercise of police power for public welfare? it seems like the practice pulls in two directions - using private-property rhetoric to defend exclusion, and public-interest rhetoric to defend the zoning code itself. is that just hypocrisy, or is there a coherent philosophical view that licenses both?
