Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between the Architectural-Rigidity camp and the Functional-Flexibility camp would produce on sphexishness—the invariant, loop-bound behavioral pattern typified by a digger wasp’s stereotyped burrow-check-seal sequence when its prey is displaced, extended here to any system that repeats an identical motor subroutine without revising its overall goal-structure. The document must contain four parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: include shared descriptions of the loop’s structure (initiation, execution, interruption response, repetition); agreement on the distinction between representational updating and mere sensorimotor feedback; agreement that flexibility can be graded rather than binary; and agreement on the minimal conditions for saying a system has “revised its plan” rather than “restarted its program.” Second, a disagreements table with two columns—one for each camp’s formulation of its own view, written in wording each side would accept—covering: (a) whether sphexishness demonstrates the absence of free will or only the absence of a particular kind of cognitive architecture; (b) whether a system that acquires a context-sensitive halt-module (activating only after repeated loop failures) thereby escapes sphexishness or merely acquires a layered rigidity. Third, identify the cruxes: specifically, whether executive flexibility without representational flexibility is sufficient to defeat the sphexish diagnosis, and whether sphexishness is metaphysically or normatively relevant to free will only in systems that already possess meta-cognitive self-monitoring. Fourth, for each crux, specify one concrete hypothetical case or experimental design both camps agree is genuinely probative, and record in advance what each camp predicts the outcome or analysis of that case would show. Keep the statement formal, do not invoke specific bibliographic citations or reading lists, and treat the wasp example as an illustrative conceptual pattern rather than as a claim about any particular historical observation.
