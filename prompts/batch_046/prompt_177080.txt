Write a short dialogue between two people — maybe a museum curator and a logician — arguing about where meaning lives in this weird museum case. There's a vase thats been misclassifed for fifty years as a ritual object but its actually part of a self-referential art project about misclassification itself. The logician uses Hofstadter's frame/outer/inner message idea from GEB to say the real meaning is in the inner logical loop: the markings on the vase literally encode instructions that reference the database error, so understanding requires decoding that hidden layer. The curator says no, the meaning is in the frame — fifty years of wrong institutional context IS the work, and the "inner" markings are just decorative noise that only looks intentional after the fact. They should actually respond to eachother's points, not talk past. Deal with the problem that if the frame makes meaning then the artist never really controlled thier message, but if the inner message controls it then the museum's long mistake was just irrelevant noise rather than part of the art. Don't have them ask for citations, a bibliography, or a reading list. Make it in the weeds so its not just "Hofstadter said X" — apply the frame/outer/inner distinction to this specific vase and let both sides have a real point that doesn't collapse easily. Keep both characters equally smart.
