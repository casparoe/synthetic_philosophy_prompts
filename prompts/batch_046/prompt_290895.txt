A colleague has been working through Frege’s puzzle about identity and has built what she calls the “pathway overgeneration” argument; she is convinced it collapses Fregean semantics into either relativism or psychologism, but she cannot locate the precise misstep. She uses the notion of an epistemic pathway—the specific chain of perceptual, linguistic, or inferential steps through which a thinker first fixes reference to an object—as a precise rendering of Fregean sense. Here is her reasoning, numbered exactly as she presents it.

(1) In Frege’s puzzle, “a = a” is trivial and “a = b” can be genuinely informative because the two expressions present the same object via different epistemic pathways.
(2) If two expressions share an epistemic pathway, they share sense; if they differ in pathway, they differ in sense.
(3) Consider a thinker who fixes Venus via its evening appearance (pathway E) and later fixes the very same celestial body via its morning appearance (pathway M).
(4) By (2), the thinker associates two distinct senses with Venus.
(5) If identity is genuinely objective, then for “the object reached via E is identical to the object reached via M” to be true, the two pathways must converge not merely on one reference but on one mode of presentation; otherwise the thinker is entitled to withhold the identity judgment without error.
(6) But withholding “Hesperus is Phosphorus” is precisely the confusion Frege’s puzzle diagnoses: it is a mistake about the object, not a legitimate difference in pathway-based identity conditions.
(7) So (5) must be false: sameness of reference is sufficient for identity, regardless of pathway.
(8) If sameness of reference is sufficient, then “a = b” and “a = a” express exactly the same relational fact whenever a and b corefer.
(9) Therefore the only remaining difference between them lies in the thinker’s psychological states, not in the objective content of the identity statement.
(10) From (9) it follows that there are no informative identity statements in language as such; all cognitive value is relocated into subjective psychology.
(11) That eliminates any semantic role for sense and collapses the Fregean distinction between sense and reference.
(12) Hence accepting Frege’s solution together with the pathway analysis of sense yields a reductio: either we allow pathway-relative identity (which makes astronomical discovery impossible) or we reduce sense to psychology (which destroys Fregean semantics).

She wishes to preserve the idea that epistemic pathways explain cognitive value, so she is reluctant to abandon (1) or (2); and she wishes to preserve the objectivity of discovery, so she is reluctant to abandon (7). She asks which step is the real culprit, why that step looks plausible on first inspection, and where exactly the “pathway slide” occurs—whether in an equivocation between semantic and metaphysical identity, a scope shift over quantifiers, or a modal confusion about what sense determines. She also wants to know what each of the standard positions—the pure Fregean, the direct-reference or Millian view, and the hidden-indexical account—would have to pay in order to reject that step, and whether any of them can preserve both her commitments without buying the reductio. Where does the argument go wrong, and what does repairing it cost?
