If we accept the computationist view that moral status attaches to specific information-processing dynamics rather than biological substrate, and if a neuromorphic implementation can replicate predictive-coding loops—including free-energy minimization trajectories—gate-for-gate with functional isomorphism to mammalian cortical columns, then redundantly distributing that computation across two physically isolated chips should yield not two subjects but one subject instantiated twice. On that assumption, shutting down one chip looks ethically closer to anesthetizing a hemisphere than to killing an organism. Should harm-assessment therefore scale linearly with redundant instantiations, or is there a principled reason why substrate-independent computation cannot support genuinely distributed or fragmentary subjects of moral concern?
