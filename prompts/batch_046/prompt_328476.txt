In discussions of eliminative materialism, a common argumentative move compares folk-psychological belief-desire explanations to Ptolemaic astronomy: just as geocentric theory used epicycles to save predictive success while positing entities (crystal spheres, circular orbits) that had no place in the correct Newtonian ontology, so folk psychology uses beliefs and desires to predict behavior while positing entities that mature neuroscience will discard rather than reduce. The analogy is used to present eliminativism not as a radical denial of mental life but as ordinary scientific replacement—phlogiston giving way to oxidation, epicycles giving way to ellipses. Audit this comparison with precision. First, lay out the correspondence table: what plays the role of the epicycles, the celestial spheres, the predictive successes, the observational data, and the replacement theory? Then isolate the disanalogies that matter for the argument: do beliefs have the same theoretical, observational, or functional status as epicycles? What about first-person authority over one's own mental states—is there an astronomical counterpart, or does this break the analogy? Consider also whether neuroscience offers genuine replacements for specific propositional attitudes, or only for subpersonal mechanisms, and whether the analogy obscures the normative and compositional dimensions of belief (that beliefs are answerable to evidence and combine productively in ways that neural activation patterns may not mirror). Finally, examine whether the analogy quietly imports the idea that mental states are "misinterpreted observations" like celestial motions, and whether an alternative comparison—say, beliefs as "center of mass" rather than "phlogiston"—would make them eliminable in a different sense or not at all. Does the conclusion that eliminativism is just business-as-usual science survive once these structural breaks, especially the first-person and normative residues, are fully on the table, or was the analogy doing all the persuasive work?
