Consider Hofstadter’s claim that the self is a strange loop sustained by the symbol “I,” which refers back to the neural patterns that generate it. Now imagine that self-reference is implemented not as a localized token but as a distributed token-cloud: a population of interlinked representations spread across prefrontal and parietal circuits, none of which individually denotes the self, yet whose collective dynamics are globally self-monitoring. If the strange loop strictly requires a unitary symbol capable of pointing back to its own generator, then this distributed architecture appears to lack the requisite singular referent and should fail to close the loop. Does this entail that distributed-coding models of self-representation are incompatible with Hofstadter’s framework, or can the loop emerge at the population level without being localized in any particular symbol?
