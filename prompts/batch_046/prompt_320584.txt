Okay so for a seminar I'm writing down my actual commitments on whether history loops or marches forward, and I want people to poke holes. Here goes, don't laugh.

- I'm convinced history moves in a spiral: same political pathologies come back, but they hit different material conditions each time, so it's not pure repetition.
- I take Polybius's anacyclosis seriously as a mechanism for constitutional decay and renewal, and I think it applies even to modern bureaucratic states if you reinterpret monarchy and democracy as administrative centralization and populist decentralization.
- I think Ibn Khaldun's asabiyya is the right engine for those cycles, and I believe it still operates today through class or ideological solidarity rather than just tribal kinship.
- At the same time, I think modern pluralism fragments that solidarity so severely that you don't get the sharp dynastic collapses Khaldun described — instead it's slow erosion.
- I reject Spengler's determinism about cultures being organisms with fixed lifespans, though I still borrow his culture/civilization split as a descriptive heuristic.
- I think Toynbee's challenge-response framework explains why some societies adapt out of a cycle and others don't, but he totally missed what I'd call institutional sedimentation — every cycle leaves legal and technical residues with a half-life longer than the political cycle, so later generations face the same politics with heavier bureaucratic baggage.
- Given that sedimentation, I think modern revolutions can't actually reset the constitutional clock; they just pile new rules on old ones, which makes the spiral tighter and more rigid over time.

So my question: do these actually hang together? I'm especially worried that applying Polybius to modern states clashes with my claim that sedimentation prevents clean dissolution, and whether using asabiyya in modern contexts is compatible with saying pluralism kills sharp collapses. Also, is there an obvious contradiction I'm missing where one of these tensions is just fake? Go through the pairs, tell me which ones are in real tension and how bad, which ones only look bad, what the cheapest fix for each real tension is and what it costs me, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems.
