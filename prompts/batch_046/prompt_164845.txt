Two people in my reading group fought about Kuhn and incommensurability, specifically whether psychology's shift from behaviorism to cognitivism counts. (1) Maya said it's a textbook paradigm shift: behaviorists talk about stimulus-response pairs, cognitivists talk about mental representations, and you can't translate 'belief' into behaviorist language without losing the concept entirely, so the taxonomies are incommensurable. (2) Jake said that's wrong - cognitivism literally grew out of behaviorism's failures, they share the same experimental methods and subjects, and 'mental representation' was introduced precisely to explain the same behavioral data behaviorists couldn't, so there's continuity, not incommensurability. (3) Maya answered that shared methods don't matter for Kuhn; what matters is whether the terms have the same meaning across paradigms, and 'learning' means conditioning for one and information-processing for the other, so they're talking past eachother. (4) Jake said if that's the standard then every theoretical revision in science is incommensurable, which makes Kuhn's claim trivial and useless. (5) Maya said Jake is missing Kuhn's point about local incommensurability - only certain clusters of terms shift, not the whole language, so it's not trivial, just precise.

Adjudicate move by move: rate each from clearly wrong to clearly right and briefly justify. Also say what both miss about how Kuhn applied incommensurability to special sciences versus physics, and who has the stronger case overall. No need for citations, just work it out.
