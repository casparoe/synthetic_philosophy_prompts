I'm currently taking a seminar on Enlightenment philosophies of happiness that has focused on Émilie du Châtelet's view that genuine well-being requires intellectual autonomy, liberation from socially imposed illusion, and the active cultivation of judgment rather than passive consumption of pleasure. Our discussion recently turned to contemporary well-being engineering—specifically, algorithmic mood-tracking applications, AI companions marketed as emotional-optimization tools, and the biometric quantification of happiness through continuous data collection. If du Châtelet were alive today, how do you think she would assess this industry? Would she see it as an extension of her rationalist ideal, in which reason is used to master one's emotional life, or rather as a sophisticated form of the very dependency and external authority she warned against? I am particularly interested in whether she might argue that an AI therapist—however effective—cannot deliver the specific kind of freedom from prejudice she valued, precisely because its reasoning is opaque to the user and therefore replaces self-cultivation with calibrated submission to an unseen mechanism.
