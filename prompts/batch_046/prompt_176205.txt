ive been thinking on my own about whether pain is actually bad in itself or just feels awful, and i keep going round in circles. theres this condition called pain asymbolia where people feel the sensation and know theyre hurt but dont suffer or care about it at all - my mates cousin apparently has it, he burned his hand on the hob and just said 'oh thats hot' and kept cooking, no panic. for him it seems like pain is just a neutral warning, not bad in itself.

but then i think about people who seek out pain for fun, like ice swimmers or people covered in tattoos. they feel the hurt and even fancy it, but i still reckon its damaging their bodies, so maybe the badness is in the harm rather than the feeling?

then theres a baby stubbing its toe and screaming. the physical damage is tiny but the suffering is proper real, and i think thats definately bad - but mainly because the babys upset, not because of the injury.

the case i keep flip-flopping on is this pill i imagined that turns all pain into a tickle. youd still know youre injured, but you wouldnt mind it. part of me thinks its obvious everyone should take it - why suffer needlessly? but then youd keep playing on a broken leg and ruin yourself, so maybe losing the 'bad feeling' is dangerous after all.

so is there a principle that captures my gut feelings? like pain is bad only when it involves suffering, or only when it signals real harm, or both? and do i have to drop one of my verdicts - especially that pill one, or the one about the tattoo people? also what would the principle say about someone who literally cant feel pain at all and walks around on broken bones without knowing?
