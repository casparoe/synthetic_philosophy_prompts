I recently completed service on a municipal citizens' assembly convened by sortition to advise on charter reform, and I have been asked to present my own philosophical commitments about democratic design at a public forum next month. Before doing so, I want an internal consistency audit of the package I have arrived at. Here are my commitments: (1) At least one legislative chamber should be filled by sortition rather than election, because election systematically filters for ambition, rhetorical fluency, and access to capital, thereby producing a descriptively unrepresentative class of career politicians. (2) Strict term limits—no more than two consecutive terms—are essential for every elected office, since careerism is the central pathology that undermines representative accountability. (3) I also hold that the loss of institutional memory and substantive legislative competence caused by rapid turnover is a serious harm, not a negligible one; experienced officeholders develop procedural knowledge and domain expertise that are genuinely difficult to replace. (4) Democratic legitimacy is fundamentally procedural: it derives from citizens' consent to the institutional framework, not from the epistemic quality, descriptive accuracy, or welfare outcomes of particular decisions. (5) Consequently, I support a hybrid design in which an elected chamber operates under strict term limits, while a parallel chamber is filled by lot with members serving single, long (twelve-year), non-renewable terms—allowing expertise to accumulate without creating reelection incentives. (6) I reject defending sortition on epistemic grounds—e.g., by claiming that randomly selected bodies produce collectively wiser judgments—because that would tether legitimacy to outcome quality in a way that conflicts with (4). Please examine this set for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision for each real tension? Rank the tensions by severity, specify what each revision costs, and tell me whether there is a single change that would resolve the most inconsistencies without undermining the core motivation for the hybrid model. Please respond in American English.
