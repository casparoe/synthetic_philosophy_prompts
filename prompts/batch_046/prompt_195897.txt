Write a short story related to prison rehabilitation versus punishment, using Norway's Halden-style normality principle but twisted into a fictional pilot programme. The setting is Solhaug, a made-up prison in southern Norway modelled on Halden, where inmates vote on the guards shift patterns and meal rosters even though staff keep all legal authority. Your main characters are Marcus, an American exchange prisoner in for non-violent drug offences back home who thinks the whole thing is just soft power disguised as democracy, and Ingrid, a Norwegian officer convinced that this "symetrical accountability" is the only honest end-point of rehabilitation — if you treat people as citizens, you can't lock them up without letting them shape the institution that holds them. Have them clash over whether punishment can ever be rehabilitative when the power to punish is never actually shared. Then let something small go wrong — a broken lock, a burnt meal, whatever — so both have to sit with the tension rather than resolving it neatly. Keep it casual and grounded, not preachy, and don't turn Marcus into a strawman.
