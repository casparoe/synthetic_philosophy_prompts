The speaker’s thesis is that the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents can be circumvented not by weakening reflective endorsement altogether, but by adopting a two-tier architecture: a ground tier in which the agent asserts consistency and reliability for its base reasoning procedures, and a reflective tier in which it asserts only conditional reliability, separated by an explicit syntactic “Löbian gap” marker that blocks the provability predicate from applying across tiers. The talk is forty minutes, with a twenty-minute question period; the speaker needs objections in the order they are likely to arise, ranked by damage to the proposal, each phrased as that subgroup would phrase it. Include, clearly marked, the one objection the speaker most hopes is not raised — the technical challenge that, if pursued rigorously, collapses the distinction between the two tiers by showing that the gap marker itself can be internalised within the ground tier’s proof apparatus. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations; the speaker will supply references if needed, but the model should construct the objections and replies from the logical and conceptual structure alone.
