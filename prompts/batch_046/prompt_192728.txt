Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between Camp A—methodological anti-essentialists who hold that 'national character' is incoherent because within-group variance always exceeds between-group variance and that generalizations about peoples are ethically permissible only as explicit, non-reifying statistical claims—and Camp B—qualified structuralists who defend modal, probabilistic generalizations about culturally transmitted dispositions anchored in institutional history rather than innate essence. The statement must first list shared commitments: the empirical reality of cross-population clustering in values and practices, the moral hazard of reification, and the logical point that statistical generalization does not entail universal attribution. Next, it must state each camp's irreducible disagreement in wording that camp accepts—particularly whether controlling for subnational region, class, and generational cohort eliminates all meaningful national-level clustering, and whether any surviving clustering justifies practical use in diplomacy or education. Then it must identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side—and specify what both sides agree would count as decisive evidence, including cross-cultural psychological data and historical case studies of rapid value shift. Finally, it must judge which parts of the dispute are verbal, specifically whether the disagreement over 'character' collapses once both sides restrict themselves to modal rather than essential claims. Present the statement in exactly five numbered sections, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
