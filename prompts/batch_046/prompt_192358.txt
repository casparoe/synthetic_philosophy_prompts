The participants have basic probability theory and modal logic but no background in decision theory. Do not explain the assumption to me; instead, design the teaching plan. Specify the exact order in which the components must be introduced and what logical dependency governs each step—whether the probability-space framing must precede the distinction between disagreement over facts and disagreement over priors, or vice versa, and why. Map the predictable misconceptions—especially the conflation of a shared prior over world-states with a shared prior over measurement-error structures, and the belief that common knowledge of rationality alone guarantees convergence regardless of the prior space—and identify the exact point in the sequence where each misconception should be intercepted. Include one closing check question that would reveal whether the group grasps that common knowledge of posteriors forces agreement only under the further condition that the prior space itself is common across agents. Finally, state explicitly which standard formal machinery—say, the full Aumann agreement framework—should be deliberately omitted at this level and named as left out. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
