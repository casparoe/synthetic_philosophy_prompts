I am trying to understand Kurt Gödel's philosophical position on the reality of time by tracking how he seems to have assessed different cosmological models in his 1949 rotating-universe essay and in the unpublished notes that followed. From what I can reconstruct, he rendered a consistent set of judgments across several cases. In a static Einstein universe with closed spatial sections and no cosmic rotation, he apparently treated temporal succession as a purely subjective ordering, since there is no physical feature to distinguish past from future globally. In an expanding Friedman model without rotation, he seemed to grant that the cosmic expansion supplies an objective arrow, yet he still denied that time is a substantial entity, treating it instead as a relational feature anchored to matter distribution. In his own rotating Gödel universe, where closed timelike curves exist, he took the possibility of returning to one's own past as direct evidence that temporal passage is not an intrinsic feature of reality. Finally, in a hypothetical cyclically contracting model in which successive epochs are causally isolated, he appeared uncertain, suggesting that the very concept of a global present becomes incoherent, though he did not clearly commit to whether local temporal relations survive.

Given this pattern, I want to know how he would have assessed, or should have assessed for consistency, a model he never examined: a non-expanding, globally rotating universe in which a slight anisotropic shear eliminates all closed timelike curves, yet preserves an absolute rotational frame that every local observer can in principle detect without reference to distant matter. Would he have said that the absence of closed timelike curves restores an objective temporal order, or that the persistence of global rotation undermines it in a different way? I am not asking for a simple yes or no; I want the plausible rules he seems to be running—whether he ties the unreality of time specifically to the logical possibility of past-directed travel, or more broadly to any global rotational structure that makes temporal ordering frame-dependent—along with what each rule predicts for this new case, your best guess at which rule captures his actual commitments and why, and what further cosmological scenario, such as one involving a non-rotating mechanism that nevertheless produces closed timelike curves, would best distinguish these interpretations.
