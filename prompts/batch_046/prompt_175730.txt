I'm looking for the established term, if there is one, for the following configuration in the logic of temporal loops. Imagine a system that sends a design, formula, or instruction set backward in time, where an earlier version receives it, incorporates it, and then sends it back again—but each transmission applies a deterministic transformation rather than an exact copy: perhaps compression, error correction, or adaptation to local conditions. Over iterations the content converges to a stable fixed point that was never authored outside the loop. Because the transformation rule is state-dependent, that fixed point is not arbitrary; it is the unique attractor of the dynamics. There is no causal contradiction of the kind found in the grandfather paradox, since nothing prevents its own origin, and there is no static, unchanging object that lacks a source of the kind discussed in the bootstrap or ontological paradox. Instead there is an infinite convergent cascade that is self-justifying without an external ground, leaving what feels like an explanatory gap about why that particular fixed point rather than another. I have seen informal analogies to self-modifying code archived and then recovered in altered form, or to evolutionary traits whose present shape results from iterated ancestral feedback rather than a single origin event. The pattern seems to sit near both familiar paradoxes but not to coincide with either, though I am unsure whether philosophers or logicians have already named it, formalized it, or shown that it collapses into one of the standard two upon closer analysis. Does this configuration have a specific name? If not, can it be precisely defined—especially its boundary conditions, such as whether the convergence must be deterministic or whether probabilistic convergence also qualifies, and whether the loop requires a physical transmission mechanism or can occur in purely informational or normative domains? I will not be satisfied with a bare label; I want a careful carving of what exactly distinguishes this from nearby paradoxes, where its edges blur, and whether it represents a genuine third category or merely a dynamic variant of an existing one. Please also say what would change your assessment—whether a specific logical result showing that all such loops reduce to bootstrap cases, or an argument that the apparent explanatory gap dissolves once the dynamics are fully specified.
