Write an essay on whether Hume's defence of suicide in his suppressed essay is genuinely consistent with the sceptical empiricism he pushes elsewhere, or whether it smuggles in a hidden theological premise he pretends to reject. There's a real tension: he argues life belongs to us, not to God, but that sounds like he's answering a theist on their own turf rather than dismissing the turf altogether. Meanwhile his bundle-theory of the self makes it hard to see who this "owner" of the life actually is.

Weigh both sides propperly. One line says he's just being dialectical — granting theist premises for the sake of argument to show suicide still isn't sinful. The other line says he needs a robust notion of self-ownership that his empiricism can't supply, so there's a hidden commitment lurking behind the rhetoric.

Reach a verdict and defend it — dont end on a fence-sitting note.

Structure:
1. The tension set up clearly (~150 words)
2. The dialectical-consistency defence (~150 words)
3. The hidden-commitment objection (~150 words)
4. Your verdict with reasoning (~100 words)

Write in British English. Keep it tight — no bibliography, no further-reading lists, and avoid long verbatim quotes from memory.
