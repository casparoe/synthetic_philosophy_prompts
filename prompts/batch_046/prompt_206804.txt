I am designing a diagnostic decision tree for an advanced metaphysics seminar on modality and identity, and I need it to sort students by their substantive commitments about cross-world persistence rather than by the philosophers they happen to name. The tree should begin with a single starting commitment that sounds intuitive: “There is a genuine, non-metaphorical sense in which I—this very individual—might have chosen a different career path in another possible world.” From that node, each branch should be labeled by a concrete metaphysical question that divides the logical space, phrased so that a student can answer it without already knowing which interpretation it leads to. The first split should ask whether the subject in the counterfactual scenario is numerically identical to the actual subject or merely a highly similar substitute; the next should ask whether any cross-world identity requires an individual essence (a haecceity) that is independent of all qualitative and relational properties; then whether one permits branching, so that the very same actual individual can be identical to two distinct, non-identical individuals in different worlds; and—this is the specific angle I want included—whether temporal parts can be shared across worlds, so that an enduring object in world W₁ and its “counterpart” in W₂ literally overlap in a four-dimensional region, or whether only instantaneous stages, not whole objects, can be co-located between worlds. The leaves must include the standard named positions—Lewis-style counterpart theory governed by purely qualitative similarity relations, Kripke-style transworld identity anchored in essential origins, and the eliminativist view that rejects cross-world reference altogether—but they should also map out unnamed combinations, especially any view that combines strict transworld identity with a “modal stage” ontology in which only instantaneous stages, not enduring objects, are shared between worlds. For every unnamed leaf, please provide a one-sentence diagnosis of why the combination is unoccupied: whether it is incoherent, unstable under closer analysis, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of named positions that this particular branching structure cannot genuinely separate, and specify what further distinction—perhaps about the uniqueness of counterparts or the role of causal continuity—would be required to pull them apart. The intended audience is graduate students who have encountered the standard debate but have not necessarily worked through its interaction with four-dimensionalist part-ontology.
