I've been in this political philosophy seminar and my friends Priya and Tom got into it over whether compatriots actually have stronger moral claims on you than strangers do. Priya said there's this thing she called institutional reciprocity -- maybe it's a real term, maybe she made it up -- where citizens share a scheme of cooperation that produces goods like legal protection and infrastructure, and since you're actively maintaining it through taxes and compliance, you have special reciprocal duties to the other people keeping it running. Tom said thats just describing why living together is convenient, not why those people get a stronger moral claim on your wallet than someone starving across the border. Priya pushed back that it's not just convenience, it's that you're embedded in specific relationships of mutual reliance, and if you abandon that for pure cosmopolitanism you undermine the very institutions that eventually help outsiders too. Tom said that sounds like a consequentialist argument for nationalism, not a genuine special obligation, and he asked if Priya would still prioritize citizens if the government was totally corrupt and not cooperating with anyone. She said probably not, but then Tom pointed out that means the special claim depends on the government being decent, not on citizenship itself, which kinda collapses her view into "help good institutions wherever they are." Then Priya brought up this edge case: imagine a country where citizens are randomly assigned at birth but can opt out at eighteen, and those who stay get special healthcare funded by taxes. Tom said if opting out is real then staying creates voluntary obligations, but Priya said the opt-out is so costly that it's not genuinely voluntary, and besides nobody chose to be born there in the first place. They kept circling and asked me to referee. Can you go through their exchange step by step and say who's right about which point, who is more right overall, and whether they're both missing something about whether voluntary association is even necessary for special obligations?
