If Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz were alive today and observed the dominant model of women's education—decentralized, algorithmically-curated micro-learning platforms that grant modular credentials without sustained institutional discipline, communal life, or long-form apprenticeship—would she regard it as the fulfillment of her defense of women's access to knowledge, or as the dissolution of the very protected, rigorous space she argued women needed? Take a definite position: identify which of her commitments (uninterrupted access to books, freedom from domestic labor, structured intellectual community, or the framing of study within religious vocation) you think she would treat as non-negotiable, and argue precisely whether this modern model serves or betrays them.
