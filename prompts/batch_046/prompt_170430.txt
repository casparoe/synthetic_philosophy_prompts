Consider the institutional practice in high-energy physics funding and peer review: review panels and funding agencies routinely renew and expand support for string-theoretic and multiverse cosmological research despite the acknowledged absence of distinctive empirical predictions testable with present or foreseeable technology, and despite the well-known landscape problem that undermines the framework's unique predictive power. Concurrently, these same bodies frequently reject or severely restrict funding for other theoretical programs—such as certain modified-gravity or emergent-spacetime proposals—on the explicit ground that they are empirically untethered or unfalsifiable. The standard defense for sustaining the former is not empirical success but a cluster of non-empirical virtues: mathematical consistency, structural uniqueness (the claim that string theory is the only known framework consistently unifying gravity and quantum mechanics), and inevitability (the claim that multiverse structures are unavoidable consequences of well-confirmed inflationary dynamics). What implicit philosophy of scientific confirmation and rational theory choice does this differential treatment presuppose? Reconstruct the theory—whether it is a form of structural realism, a uniqueness-based non-empirical confirmation principle, or something else—that renders the pattern coherent rather than arbitrary. Distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely accommodates it: could a strict empiricist, a Bayesian who weights predictive novelty and empirical risk heavily, or an explanatory pluralist endorse the same funding pattern? Assess whether the practice applies its non-empirical criteria consistently or selectively smuggles in empirical expectations. Finally, specify what would have to change in peer-review norms, grant evaluation criteria, and institutional risk-assessment if the implicit philosophy were replaced by one that treated sustained empirical risk and predictive distinctiveness as necessary conditions for long-term theoretical investment.
