I've been trying to figure out why the Strawson-style debate about reactive attitudes and moral responsability is so stuck. The usual loop is familiar: compatibilists say resentment tracks quality of will, not some spooky libertarian freedom; incompatibilists say if determinism is true then holding someone responsible is like resenting a rockslide. But I've started thinking the real stall might come from something like attitude modularity — the fact that we can suspend reactive attitudes locally (towards someone with severe dementia, or maybe even an automated system) without tearing down our whole practice of blame and prase. Compatibilists read modularity as proof that these attitudes are practical tools we calibrate; incompatibilists read it as proof we're inconsistently applying a metaphysically loaded standard that demands real alternate possibilities. So is the disagreement actually about free will, or is it a hidden disagreement about whether moral responsibility is a unified practice versus a patchwrok of local suspension protocols? Is it verbal — one side talking about emotional responses, the other about normative standings? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move things: a result showing reactive attitudes are neurologically all-or-nothing, a distinction between suspension and revision, or just accepting that the question is ill-posed? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not another replay of the standard exchange.
