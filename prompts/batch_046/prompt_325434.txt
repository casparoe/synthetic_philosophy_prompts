I run a small mediation service for families in our diaspora community, mostly second-gen folks whose parents still think in terms of traditional obligations but who live in a world of contracts and job mobility. Lately we keep hitting the same wall: people come in with conflicts that aren't really about money, they're about whether someone failed a role — daughter, elder brother, employer who should act like a father figure, etc. I want a decision procedure my mediators can actually use in a 90-minute session, grounded in Confucian role ethics but without asking anyone to read Mencius first.

The procedure should run like a checklist: first, identify the roles actually occupied (not just family ones — job roles, civic ones, whatever is in play). Second, check whether there's a rectification-of-names problem, meaning the role is being named wrong (calling an employer a "father" when the contract doesn't support it). Third, determine if there's a direct conflict between obligations or just a tension that can be reordered. Fourth, flag where the procedure has to hand off to the parties' own values because role ethics doesn't have a single answer — especially when modern nested roles (like being both manager and elder sibling) create obligations that point opposite directions. Make it concrete: include the exact questions a mediator asks, what counts as a sufficient answer, and where you stop with "this is a values call, not a role failure." Then run it on two cases: one where a daughter signed an employment contract requiring relocation and her father claims she violated her filial role, and another where a factory supervisor is also the elder brother of an underperforming worker, and firing him might violate brother-obligation but keeping him violates managerial role-obligation. Include one case where the procedure gives an answer the family probably won't like. Also be honest about where this breaks: if the roles themselves aren't recognized by both parties, or if someone is invoking "tradition" selectively to win an argument. Don't give me bibliographies — none of my mediators have time for that — but if you reference specific concepts like zhengming or the five relationships, explain them in one sentence so the mediator knows what they mean without leaving the room.
