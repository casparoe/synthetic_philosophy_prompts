I'm a PhD student in philosphy of mind, currently trying to finish a thesis chapter on value theory, and I'm genuinely stuck on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism. I keep wondering: if the pleasure of listening to a good album and the pleasure of solving a hard proof feel so totally different, what could they possibly share that makes them both pleasures? I was discussing this with a friend over coffee who works in neurophilosophy, and she suggested that the whole puzzle only arises if you assume pleasure has to be a uniform qualitative feel. If you switch to a dispositional account — where pleasure is essentially just the tendency to seek repetition of a mental state — then the commonality is functional, not phenomenological, and the heterogeneity worry just dissolves.

But then I started reading Mill more carefully and I got the impression that he had already dissolved it in a different way, by saying that qualitative differences are really just quantitative ones scaled by a kind of depth coefficient. And now, with all the fMRI studies showing nucleus accumbens activation across completely different rewarding activities, it seems like neuroscience gives us a single neural substrate that underwrites both accounts. So my actual confusion is this: if Mill bridged quality and quantity with his coefficient idea, and modern brain imaging confirms there's just one reward pathway, hasn't the heterogeneity problem been solved twice over? Or am I conflating Mill's distinction between higher and lower pleasures with the actual philosophical question about what makes diverse experiences all count as pleasure?
