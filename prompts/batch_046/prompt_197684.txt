I am not a philosopher by training, but I have been thinking about how people justify what they believe. Usually, if you ask someone why they hold a view, they offer a reason; if you ask about that reason, they offer another, and so on. I understand that philosophers typically discuss three ways this chain might resolve: it stops at a belief that needs no further support; the reasons loop back on one another so that each is supported by the others; or it continues forever, with every link being a full and adequate reason. Yet I keep noticing a different pattern, both in everyday conversation and in appeals to science. People allow the chain to continue indefinitely, but they treat each successive step as carrying slightly less weight, so that after enough links the remaining gap feels practically closed—even though technically no bedrock has ever been reached. It is as if an infinite sequence of smaller and smaller demands could sum to sufficient justification, without any single point being decisive. Does this configuration have an established name? If so, how precisely does it relate to the three accounts I mentioned, and where does it differ from each? If not, what are its nearest relatives, and should it be treated as a genuine fourth option or as a confusion between causal explanation and rational support? Please reply in British English.
