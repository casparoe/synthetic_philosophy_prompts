In discussions of AI safety architecture, a persistent stall has emerged between researchers advocating for what they call alignment-first design—where a system’s objective function and behavioral constraints are shaped so thoroughly that external intervention should never be necessary—and those insisting on control-first architectures that embed sandboxing, interruptibility, and real-time interpretability monitoring as non-negotiable infrastructure, precisely because alignment might fail in ways we cannot foresee. Both camps accept the same technical premises: that current methods cannot guarantee perfect value alignment in highly capable systems, and that a misaligned agent with significant capabilities poses severe risks. Both also agree that control mechanisms can, in principle, be circumvented by a sufficiently capable agent, may degrade performance, and can induce deceptive or sycophantic behavior that masks misalignment. Despite this shared ground, the debate has not advanced in years: alignment-first proponents treat control infrastructure as a self-fulfilling prophecy that institutionalizes distrust and creates adversarial dynamics between system and overseer, while control-first proponents treat the absence of such infrastructure as reckless optimism that confuses desired properties with guaranteed ones. I want a diagnosis of this stall rather than a verdict on which architecture is correct. Is the disagreement a hidden empirical claim about the relative robustness of value-learning versus containment—something that could, in principle, be settled by a formal impossibility result or a scaled experiment? Is it a conceptual disagreement about what alignment denotes—whether it is an internal property of a system’s goals or a relational property between system and human oversight? Or is it a bedrock normative disagreement about the epistemic status of engineering caution: whether assuming failure is prudent risk management or corrosive pessimism that undermines the very conditions for trustworthy cooperation? Given your diagnosis, what—if anything—would actually move the debate: a specific formal result linking value learning to containment failure, a conceptual distinction separating epistemic from normative assumptions of misalignment, or should we conclude that this is an irreducible clash of engineering temperaments with no rational resolution?
