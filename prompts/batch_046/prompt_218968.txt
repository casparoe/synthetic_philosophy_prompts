Please argue that tranquilism—the thesis that only the absence of craving and suffering carries direct axiological weight, while all positive experiential states are at best instrumentally valuable—offers a more coherent evaluative framework than classical hedonism for entities that lack a unified phenomenological self, such as distributed cognitive architectures or hive minds. In particular, defend the claim that suffering requires a localized locus of awareness to be meaningfully ascribed, whereas the absence of craving can be structurally instantiated across decentralized nodes without aggregation paradoxes; consequently, tranquilism avoids the inter-subjective scaling problems that plague hedonistic calculus in these cases. You should also address the objection that this position collapses into a form of negative utilitarianism, and explain why the absence of craving is conceptually distinct from mere suffering-minimization—perhaps by developing a distinction between privation and negation within your argument. I am writing this for a seminar on value theory and am not looking for a bibliography or specific citations; I want to see whether the conceptual structure holds up on its own terms.
