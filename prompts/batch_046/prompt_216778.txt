I have come to accept a particular reading of the split-brain data: not merely that attention is divided, but that the left and right hemispheres can sustain genuinely distinct streams of conscious awareness, with the left hemisphere's interpreter generating a false narrative of unity. From this I conclude that in such patients there are two subjects of experience rather than one subject with divided cognition, and that the philosophical doctrine of the unity of consciousness is an artifact of normal brain integration rather than a metaphysical necessity. What would have to be true for this position to be wrong? I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they are: specific empirical results about interhemispheric coordination or residual integration that I might be neglecting; conceptual arguments about why behavioral coherence still supports one subject; philosophical objections showing that my inference from modular processing to dual subjects commits a category mistake; and an honest assessment of whether defenders of this view have qualified it so heavily—say, by claiming the right hemisphere is only partially conscious—that it has become unfalsifiable, and if so, whether that insulation is a virtue or a defect. Please include what a committed proponent of this view would themselves concede as damaging, not only what critics say.
