I run a philosophy seminar for grad students, about 10 people, 90 minutes. Design an acitivity that makes them feel Popper's point that history can't have laws—because predicting a trajectory changes the system itself. Use a card game with a fictional society (call it Meridia): groups study round-one data, propose a "law" of development, then apply it in round two. But there's a hidden rule—once a law is announced it enters Meridia's "institutional memory" and deterministically but unpredictably warps round-three data. They should hit the contradiction themselves before anyone names Popper. Give concrete rules, index-card materials, a token payoff scheme with no real money, and a debrief mapping each collapsed law onto his prophecy-vs-prediction distinction. Be frank about what this can't reproduce—probably the open/closed society distinction. Also say what will predictably go wrong the first time. I want a committed take: pick one specific hidden-rule mechanic and defend it, don't lay out options for me to choose.
