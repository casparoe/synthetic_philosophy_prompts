The disagreement must not stem from hidden world-evidence or different world-priors; instead, inject this structural feature: each engineer applies a distinct, internally rational calibration protocol to the same shared monitoring stream, and while each knows the other uses a different protocol, the choice of protocol is governed by a separate meta-belief structure that is not part of their common prior. After the scenario, provide: (1) the verdict the strict common-prior view gives—that shared priors plus common knowledge of rationality should eliminate disagreement—and why it seems to apply here; (2) the verdict of the model-relativist view you have defined, which treats the calibration protocol as part of the belief state, and why it permits disagreement to stand; (3) the exact feature doing the work, namely the separation between world-priors and protocol-priors; (4) whether removing that separation collapses the case back into standard agreement; and (5) how a defender of the strict view would attack the case, likely by arguing that fully rational agents should include their protocol-priors in the common prior as well. Keep the case self-contained, use no citations or named papers, and do not ask for bibliographies or further reading.
