Write a two-page research proposal for a six-month independent study. The central problem is this: Simone Weil maintained that political parties act as machines for manufacturing collective passion and that allegiance to them corrupts individual judgment, yet she also conceded that under conditions of severe economic inequality some organizational vehicle is unavoidable if the disadvantaged are to have any representation at all. The standard interpretation treats her well-known essay on the abolition of all parties as a flat rejection of every form of party organization, leaving the functional concession unexplained. Your proposal should investigate whether Weil’s critique applies differentially. Specifically, does it target only parties organized around doctrinal coherence and formal collective identity, or does it extend to what might be called affinity groups—informal, non-membership-based clusters that generate analogous patterns of collective passion without formal structures, platforms, or enrollment? State the precise question so that it admits a determinate answer: if the distinction holds, can Weil’s abolitionist conclusion be restricted to doctrinal parties alone, thereby preserving her functional acknowledgment that representation requires organization? Or does her analysis of collective psychology collapse the distinction, forcing us either to extend the abolitionist conclusion to affinity groups as well or to abandon the functional concession? Explain the approach: reconstruct her account of collective passion and judgment-corruption from her published essays and notebooks without relying on secondary literature, then apply the reconstructed framework to the conceptual contrast between formal doctrinal parties and informal affinity clusters. Describe the method month by month—first, reconstruction of the psychological mechanism; second, analysis of whether formal membership and doctrinal coherence are essential to that mechanism or merely contingent vehicles; third, evaluation of whether affinity groups avoid the corruption Weil describes or reproduce it in a different register. Say what a successful result would look like: perhaps a restricted abolitionism that applies only to organizations with formal doctrine and membership, leaving room for unstructured representation, or perhaps the discovery that any collective passion-formation triggers the same corruption, which would force a revision of her functional concession. Say also what would count as failure—specifically, if the conceptual distinction between party-types dissolves under her own analysis, leaving the project with no stable axis of differentiation. Give an honest assessment of scope: this is solo conceptual work without archival access, so it must remain an internal reconstruction and conceptual application rather than a historical or empirical study of actual party structures. Present the entire proposal in continuous prose only—no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables—written as one would compose a formal letter. Do not request or include a bibliography, reading list, or specific citations; refer to positions and arguments in general terms and supply references yourself.
