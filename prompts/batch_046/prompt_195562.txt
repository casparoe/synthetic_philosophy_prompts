people keep comparing Arendts idea of the banality of evil to a factory assembly line -- each worker only sees one part, no one sees the whole product, and thats how ordinary people do horrible things. they use it to argue that modern bureaucracy makes moral responsibility diffuse and basically unavoidable. can you lay out the correspondence table? what plays the role of the factory floor, the foreman, the defective part, and the end customer? then tell me where it breaks: does Arendt actually think Eichmann was just a cog, or is there a specific kind of thoughtlessness that the factory image hides? and if the analogy collapses, does the conclusion about diffused responsibility still stand, or was the assembly-line picture doing all the rhetorical work?
