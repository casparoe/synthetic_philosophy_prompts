Next month I present at our interdisciplinary philosophy-of-biology workshop a thesis developed in my recent work on taxonomic practice: that the so-called "species problem" is mischaracterized when treated as an ontological crisis about whether species exist as natural kinds. Rather, I argue, it is a pragmatic misalignment between two distinct explanatory targets—lineage-tracking for evolutionary theory and interaction-tracking for ecological modeling—and I defend a "dual-track" framework in which evolutionary biologists and ecologists are licensed to employ different grouping criteria without the pretension that these must converge on a single ontology of "the" species. The audience will include Dr. Chen, a systematist who studies cryptic beetle lineages and treats the phylogenetic species concept as the only rigorous standard; Dr. Alvarez, a philosopher committed to reproductive isolation as a real causal process and therefore to the Biological Species Concept as identifying genuine natural divisions; several ecology graduate students who rely on morphological species for rapid field surveys and are suspicious of philosophical frameworks that complicate practical taxonomy; and a philosopher of science who has previously argued that pluralism about species is merely an evasion of the realist question.

Give me the objections I should expect, in roughly the order they will arise, phrased as each of these interlocutors would actually phrase them in the Q&A. For each objection, indicate what I should concede, what I must hold, and where my reply will be weakest, keeping in mind that I have only limited time for rebuttal in a thirty-minute session. I also want you to include the objection I am secretly hoping nobody raises—namely, the challenge that my dual-track framework collapses into a problematic instrumentalism that cannot distinguish between a genuinely explanatory grouping and a merely useful fiction, and that without a unified ontology I lack grounds for saying one track is wrong about a particular case rather than merely inapplicable. Be prepared to address the charge that by treating ecological interaction-tracking as autonomous from evolutionary lineage-tracking, I am ignoring empirical work showing that reproductive isolation and ecological niche divergence are causally coupled during speciation. I do not need bibliographic recommendations or reading lists; I need strategic preparation for defending the position in the room.
