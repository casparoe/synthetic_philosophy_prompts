I was arguing with my study group about whether its okay to believe stuff without proof, like religious things or just trusting your own memory about yesterday. We tried to extend Clifford's shipowner worry into this idea we called "belief debt" — that if you believe one thing, you're secretly on the hook for all the hidden claims it pulls in, even ones you never think about. Then we built this argument that James's will-to-believe idea actually makes the problem worse instead of fixing it, but now I'm not sure if we're cheating somewhere. Here are the steps we wrote down:

(1) Clifford is basically right that believing without enough evidence is wrong, because false beliefs can hurt people who depend on you.
(2) James says we can sometimes choose to believe when evidence is missing, as long as the choice really matters, can't be avoided, and feels genuinely open.
(3) But every belief carries belief debt: hidden commitments you don't realize you're signing up for. If I decide God exists, I'm also implicitly committed to a bunch of historical and moral claims that usually come with that package, even if I never think about them.
(4) So if I use James's rule to license the first belief without checking, I'm also licensing all those hidden commitments without evidence.
(5) That means James doesn't reduce moral risk; he multiplies it. Clifford's worry about one false belief applies to this whole hidden tree of beliefs.
(6) So either we stick with Clifford and never believe without full evidence for the belief and everything it logically pulls in, or we admit belief isn't morally regulated at all.
(7) But tracing every hidden entailment is impossible — we'd never form any beliefs.
(8) Therefore the ethics of belief collaps: we can't follow Clifford, but James makes the danger bigger.

This seems to prove there's no middle ground, which feels too neat. Which step should I reject? Explain why it looked okay but isn't, and then tell me what Clifford, James, and someone who thinks belief isn't voluntary (maybe a permissivist?) each have to pay if we drop that step. Also please say how confident you are in this diagnosis and single out the point you're least sure about — probably whether belief debt is a real problem or just a confusion we made up.
