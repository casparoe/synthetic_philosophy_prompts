I'm working as an election administrator for a semi-autonomous region, and every time we run a local referendum we end up in court over who belongs on the voter rolls. Our legal team says we should base the rolls on democratic boundary theory — the idea that anyone affected by the decision should be part of the demos — but that doesn't tell my staff what to do on Monday morning. Could you turn that principle into a reusable decision procedure? I need the questions asked in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer for each one, where the procedure stops with a clear "no," and where it has to hand the decision to the commission because it's a value judgment not a fact. It should also be honest about which steps are really just judgement calls wearing a procedural hat. There's also this niche constraint in our charter — the "non-fragmentation clause" — which says we shouldn't seperate households across different rolls if they share a residence, even if members have different citizenship status; I have no idea if that's in the philosophy literature but we have to build it in. Then run the procedure on two proposals: long-term residents who hold citizenship in a neighboring state but have lived here for fifteen years, and temporary workers who hold local citizenship but have only been present for six months. One of these should come out in a way that I'll probably dislike — flag that, and say what the procedure can't settle.
