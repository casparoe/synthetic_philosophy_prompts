Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a strict Popperian falsificationist and a predictive-risk practitioner on whether falsificationism solves the demarcation problem. The falsificationist holds that empirical falsifiability in principle is necessary and sufficient for science, and that pseudoscience is defined by systematic immunization against refutation. The practitioner accepts in-principle falsifiability as necessary but denies it is sufficient: because of the Duhem-Quine problem and conventionalist stratagems, logical form alone cannot demarcate; instead, science is distinguished by whether practitioners actually expose specific, quantitative core predictions to severe tests that, if failed, would genuinely damage the program, regardless of available auxiliary adjustments.

Format the document as: (i) a numbered list of agreed claims—covering the logical structure of falsifiability, the reality of immunizing moves in historical cases, the core/auxiliary distinction, and agreed descriptions of cases such as astrology and psychoanalysis; (ii) a disagreements table stating each camp's precise position in wording that camp would accept, identifying whether any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive; (iii) the cruxes, including whether the Duhem-Quine problem renders logical demarcation permanently indeterminate, and whether there is an intersubjectively verifiable practical criterion for when an auxiliary revision becomes an ad hoc rescue rather than legitimate refinement; and (iv) for each crux, a concrete historical or hypothetical case that both sides agree is relevant, with each side's explicit prediction recorded in advance of what that case demonstrates about their view. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; avoid appeals to highly technical literature outside standard philosophical training; and keep the prompt self-contained for a model without web access.
