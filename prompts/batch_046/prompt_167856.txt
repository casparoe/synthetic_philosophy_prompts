ok so im trying to get my views on political freedom straight before a reading group and i want a consistency check. heres roughly where ive landed, with maybe a typo or two in my notes:

1. i accept berlin's negative liberty - freedom is absence of interference, full stop, and thats the frame we should use for state coercion.
2. but i also think republican non-domination is genuinely distinct: the benevolent slave with the kind master is still unfree, and negative liberty alone cant capture that.
3. i think if you formalize domination as expected-interference weighted by power asymmetry, republican freedom actually collapses into negative liberty, so its not a real third concept.
4. i think democratic participation is constitutive of freedom, not just instrumentally good - being governed by laws you never had a hand in is itself a form of domination.
5. i reject positive liberty (autonomy / self-mastery) as conceptually muddled and dangerous because it licenses paternalism.

so do these hang together? im especially worried that 3 undermines 2, and that 4 is smuggling in the positive-liberty idea i reject in 5. but maybe some pairings only look bad on the surface? rank the tensions by severity, say for each what the cheapest revision is and what it costs me, and tell me which single change would clean up the most mess. please dont just flag every pair - discriminate the real clashes from the ones that dissolve once you look closer.
