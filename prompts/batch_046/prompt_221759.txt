Look at how german federalism actually works: the Länder run schools and police, but the federal goverment shapes policy through joint financng and framework deals, and in the Bundesrat state ministers vote as blocks taking orders from their cabinets. Meanwhile the EU preaches subsidiarity but regulates vacuum cleaner wattage. What theory of political authority do these habits presuppose? Is sovereignty divisible like property, or is it relational? And if you take the US anti-commandering principle seriously—Congress cant order states around—doesnt that also kill conditional spending, or is there a hidden idea that authority is transactional not territorial? Reconstruct the implicit picture, check if its consistent across the US, german and EU cases, and say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined—maybe it fits both corporatism and pure instrumentalism.
