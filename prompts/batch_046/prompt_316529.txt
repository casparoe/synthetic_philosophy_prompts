I recently faced a disciplinary review in which my character was treated as something I ought to have engineered differently, and trying to make sense of the proceedings I turned to Galen Strawson’s Basic Argument, which I take to show that true moral responsibility requires being causa sui and therefore collapses into an infinite regress because no one can be the ultimate source of their own nature. The board, however, operates with what they call “recursive character renovation”—the view that through sustained reflective practice one can become the genuine source of one’s revised dispositions across an iterative loop, breaking the regress not at a single origin but over time. My confusion is whether Strawson’s argument implicitly presupposes a static, one-time genesis of the self, such that this dynamic, ongoing model of self-formation might escape it; or whether, conversely, the board’s framework actually concedes Strawson’s point, since one still never authored the very capacity for renovation itself. Should my defense invoke the Basic Argument directly, or does the board’s model expose a hidden presupposition in Strawson’s reasoning—perhaps about the temporal structure of authorship—that I ought to challenge instead?
