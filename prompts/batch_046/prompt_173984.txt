I was arguing with my friends last night about whether voting is ever actually rational, not in some textbook way but just going by my gut on cases, and I realized my intuitions are all over the place. In a big national election I never vote and feel zero guilt—obviously my one vote doesn't change anything. But in my building's co-op board election with maybe thirty people I also skip it and feel wierdly guilty, even though statistcally it's basically the same problem. Then jury duty: if I'm the lone holdout for not guilty and the other eleven want guilty, I think I should absolutely not cave, even though that means a hung jury and thousands in wasted costs—so there my single vote actually decides something huge, but I still feel it's right to stick rather than compromise. At work we did a straw poll on office plants and I voted for the monstera even though I drink tea and don't care about plants, just because the intern who set it up seemed disappointed when people ignored it. And here's the one I keep going back and forth on: imagine a referendum on funding a lunar art archive meant to last ten thousand years, paid for by a tax so hidden I'd never notice it. I'd vote yes, but if the cost showed up on my bill I'd vote no, and I can't tell if that's hypocrisy or just being consistent.

So is there a single principle that captures all of this—maybe something about expressive versus instrumental value, or about whether my participation recognizes other people's standing? Would it say I should vote, or that it's irrational, and does that match intuition?
