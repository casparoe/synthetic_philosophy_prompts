A philosopher of science, Dr. Voss, has assessed whether particular mathematics-science pairings exemplify what Wigner called unreasonable effectiveness. Her verdicts: calculus predicting planetary motion is expected; Fourier analysis describing heat diffusion is expected; Riemannian geometry describing spacetime curvature is surprising but retrospectively justified; complex-number formalism predicting quantum interference is genuinely surprising; and knot-theoretic models describing DNA supercoiling are surprising. Now consider a proposed application: p-adic number fields are being used to model hierarchical cortical processing, on the grounds that the ultrametric tree structure of p-adic spaces mirrors the nested assembly organization of neuronal populations, even though p-adic analysis was developed for purely number-theoretic purposes and neuronal tissue has no obvious arithmetic structure. Predict whether Dr. Voss would classify this as another case of unreasonable effectiveness or as an expected structural analogy; identify the closest analog in her track record; state the two competing rules—one emphasizing the mathematics' independence from the domain, the other emphasizing whether the target entities naturally instantiate the structures—that both accommodate her earlier verdicts; and name one further pairing that would settle which rule she is running.
