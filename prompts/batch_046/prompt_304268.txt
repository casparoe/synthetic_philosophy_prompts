A colleague recently summarized the evolutionary debunking argument in metaethics for me as follows: evolutionary psychology suggests that our core moral judgments—such as the wrongness of harming kin or the obligation to reciprocate—were selected for their contribution to survival and reproductive success, not for their correspondence to mind-independent moral facts. Consequently, if moral realism is true, then our moral beliefs are the product of a causal process that is entirely insensitive to moral truth, which gives us a defeater for those beliefs. The realist can only block this conclusion by demonstrating a necessary, law-like connection between evolutionary fitness and moral truth—say, that cooperative dispositions are adaptive precisely because cooperation is objectively valuable. Absent such a bridge, we are rationally compelled to abandon moral realism in favor of either moral anti-realism or an error theory about moral discourse.

This strikes me as capturing a familiar line of reasoning, but I worry it flattens important distinctions I have seen mentioned in more technical discussions. For example, some defenders distinguish between domain-general debunking, which targets all normative cognition, and domain-specific debunking, which undermines only particular clusters of belief—such as incest-avoidance norms—while leaving more abstract moral principles untouched. The summary above seems to assume that once evolutionary origins are revealed for some beliefs, the defeater generalizes automatically to the entire domain of morality. There is also a further subtlety: the argument appears to presuppose that justification requires a causal connection between belief-forming mechanisms and the facts they concern, yet certain realist positions—particularly those influenced by constitutive or normative accounts of moral knowledge—deny that causal tracking is the relevant standard for moral justification at all. Given these complications, I would like an audit of the summary's fairness. Which elements accurately reflect the standard debunking challenge advanced by figures like Sharon Street or Richard Joyce? Where does it conflate distinct responses—such as the "third-factor" strategy defended by some realists—with the claim that realists must establish a metaphysically necessary fitness-truth link? And if we correct the overgeneralizations and mischaracterizations, does the conclusion that moral realism is rationally undermined still stand, or does it depend precisely on the errors embedded in the summary?
