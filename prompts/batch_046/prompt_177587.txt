Write a two-page research proposal for a small interdisciplinary grant. The question concerns the Knobe effect—the asymmetry in which harmful side effects are judged intentional while helpful ones are not—but approached through an angle that existing experimental-philosophy studies have largely ignored. Specifically: does the effect depend on an implicit assumption that the harmful outcome is causally downstream of the agent's choice? I want to test whether describing the side effect as structurally inevitable (it occurs regardless of what the agent decides) eliminates the asymmetry, even when the outcome remains morally bad. Frame the proposal with a precise statement of this question; explain why the standard literature—which typically holds causal structure fixed and varies only moral valence—cannot settle whether the Knobe effect tracks moral evaluation or tacit causal-dependence inferences; describe the method (vignette-based experiments with explicit counterfactual inevitability conditions, plus a conceptual analysis of how folk psychology distinguishes intended from merely caused outcomes); state what results would support the causal-dependence interpretation versus the standard moral-bias interpretation, and what would count as project failure (for instance, if inevitability framing produces no modulation, or if the philosophical taxonomy collapses into the empirical one); and argue why a philosophical deliverable—refining the conceptual structure of intentionality attributions—matters beyond the psychology lab. Keep the scope realistic for a six-month project. Do not request a bibliography or citations; I will supply those. Finally, include an explicit epistemic note: say what evidence or argument would change your mind about the causal-dependence framing, and identify where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
