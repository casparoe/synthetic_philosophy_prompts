As a recently appointed member of my university's Strategic Research Investment Committee, I have become acutely aware that our funding protocols embed a structural escalation trap. When a research programme receives its second tranche, the principal investigator must personally co-sign a public progress covenant that stakes their professional reputation; should the committee later terminate funding, this covenant is forfeit, contractual penalties with our industrial partner are activated, and the university's future grant credibility rating is downgraded. We face precisely this situation with the Meridian Interdisciplinary Initiative: eighteen months overdue, delivering substantially below contracted metrics, yet now seeking a third extension. Termination by Thursday's meeting would trigger the covenant forfeiture and partner penalties; approval would extend a failing commitment.

Please draft the formal decision memo to the Vice-Chancellor for Research, recommending either immediate termination with a defined winding-down timetable or continuation subject to binding revised conditions. The memo should analyse the case through the lens of escalation of commitment and the dollar-auction structure, but it must distinguish carefully between fallacious sunk-cost reasoning and genuinely rational exit costs — particularly the credibility-covenant mechanism, which renders withdrawal structurally more expensive than in an ordinary funding cycle. Whichever course you recommend, you must address the strongest counter-argument with intellectual honesty: if termination, engage the claim that discontinuation wastes irrecoverable relational capital with the partner and unjustly penalises junior researchers bound by the covenant; if continuation, address the concern that extending rewards strategic over-commitment and erodes the covenant mechanism's disciplinary function.

Present the reasoning in polished formal prose appropriate for institutional circulation, and end with a single line beginning 'Verdict:' followed by the recommendation in no more than fifteen words.
