Write a dialogue between two people at a constitutional design workshop arguing over who should sit in a new transnational climate assembly. One defends a "reciprocal exposure" rule — you belong to the demos only if your daily choices are vunerably interwined with everyone else in the decision loop, so migrant workers and future generations get weighted seats but distant outsiders dont. The other thinks thats just smuggling in a new boundary problem: vulnerability is measured by tech and capital flow, not actual political reciprocity, so the rule quietly excludes rural subsistence communities that dont show up in the data. Have them actually respond to each other — neither should be a strawman — and let them circle around whether "mutual vulnerability" is a real democratic criterion or just a fancy metric for who already has power.
