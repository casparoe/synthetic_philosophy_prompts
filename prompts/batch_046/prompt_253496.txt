I'm on the regional health equity board and we're voting next week on Proposal 47 — the "Functional Parity Cap" — which would restrict access to advanced prosthetic upgrades for patients in the top income quartile, not to redirect the savings (there aren't any; the tech is privately funded) but strictly to lower the district's mobility-score disparity metric. Some board members say this is exactly what prioritarian justice demands: reducing the gap matters even if the worst-off stay exactly where they are. Others say it's the classic leveling-down objection and we should reject it outright. Draft a policy memo for the board, maybe 600 words, recommeding approve or reject with a clear bottom line. Don't just lay out options and leave us hanging — take an actuall position and defend it, but include an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side. There's also a modified version where we cap the rich patients' upgrades and tax the private clinics to fund basic mobility aids in poor districts, except the tax revenue is projected to be negligible and wouldn't change outcomes for the worst-off in any measurable way. Say whether that wrinkle changes your recommendation. Write it like a real memo — no citations, no bibliography, just reasoning we can act on.
