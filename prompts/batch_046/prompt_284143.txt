You are the methodological referee for a clinical-research journal that receives adaptive-trial protocols in which participants or analysts face repeated assessments under partial blinding with amnesia-like information restrictions—scenarios that generate self-locating belief states structurally analogous to the Sleeping Beauty problem. Your job is to decide, within forty-eight hours and without consulting specialists, whether a submitted statistical analysis plan correctly updates beliefs about trial outcomes when the updater may be uncertain about which assessment "day" or track they currently occupy, rather than uncertain only about an uncentered world-state.

Write a reusable audit procedure—structured as an ordered checklist with decision nodes, a small set of test protocol clauses, and explicit sign-off categories—that a referee with standard statistical training but no background in epistemic logic can apply. The procedure must operationalize the philosophical distinction between centered and uncentered propositions: identify when conditioning should proceed by standard Bayes on world-states alone, when it must be modified because the observer's temporal or spatial indexical is part of the evidence, and when the protocol's blinding mechanism makes that distinction collapse.

Include the following: (1) the first three checks in sequence, with the exact question the referee asks of the protocol text at each step; (2) three worked applications—(a) a straightforward adaptive trial where standard conditioning applies and the referee certifies "uncentered—standard Bayes applies," (b) a trial with repeated wake-ups and memory wipes that forces an indexical correction and requires certification of "centered—indexical correction required, see appendix," and (c) a borderline case, such as a protocol where the total number of assessments is unknown to the observer, where the procedure must return "indeterminate—consult specialist" and explain precisely why the referee's judgment cannot be eliminated; (3) for each worked case, the exact language of the referee's report entry; (4) the points where the procedure will misfire or where metaphysical commitment about the nature of self-location is unavoidable; and (5) the sign-off form with the three certification options and a mandatory flag for cases falling outside the procedure's scope. Ground the distinctions in the actual logic of centered worlds and self-locating belief, not in generic statistical caution. Do not request citations, bibliographies, or specialist reading lists.
