Write a villanelle suitable for closing a seminar on consciousness, structured so that the form enacts a specific philosophical tension within global workspace theory. Use two refrains that alternate throughout: one should express the functional success of global broadcast — information made available to reasoning, memory, and verbal report — and the other should express the unexplained phenomenal residue, the absence of any inner illumination despite full access. Arrange the stanzas so that different cognitive modules — perception, working memory, executive control, the report system — each describe receiving and using the broadcast signal, yet none claim or deliver a felt quality. The villanelle's repetitions should show these functional descriptions cycling without ever resolving the second refrain; the final couplet should place the two refrains adjacent but unreconciled, mirroring the claim that access consciousness can be fully mapped without entailing phenomenal consciousness. The poem should be accurate enough that a specialist recognizes the architectural claims about broadcasting and consumption, but it should not ask for citations, bibliographies, or references to specific papers; paraphrase any technical notions without quotation marks.
