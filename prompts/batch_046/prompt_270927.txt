So two people in my study group, Maya and Leo, got stuck in an arguement after watching some old Sperry footage. Maya said split-brain cases are basically proof that two conscious minds can live in one skull. She described the classic task: show a chicken claw to the right visual field (left hemisphere, language) and a snow scene to the left visual field (right hemisphere, no language). The patient picks a shovel with the left hand and a chicken with the right, and then the left hemisphere says something like I picked the shovel because I need to clean up after the chicken. To Maya, the right hemisphere clearly knew about the snow and guided the left hand to the shovel, but the left brain just confabulated a reason. That means there are two separate belief-forming systems with separate intentions.

Leo said she's overreading the behavior. The right hemisphere isn't a full conscious agent; it's more like a smart zombie module that can match images to objects via emotional salience but lacks a global workspace or self-model. The confabulation isn't evidence of a second mind, it's evidence that one mind -- the left one -- is trying to maintain narrative coherence over actions it didn't author. He said if the right hemisphere were truly conscious, it should be able to report its own experiences, not just guide a hand.

Maya came back with the preference studies: when you present options to the left visual field and ask the patient to point with the left hand, the hand consistently picks one thing, and if you ask the left hemisphere why it picked that, it makes up a reason. Even weirder, sometimes the left hand undoes what the right hand just did -- pulling pants up with one hand and down with the other. That looks like two agents with conflicting desires, not one agent with a glitchy limb.

Leo said those conflicts are just disconnection symptoms. The right hemisphere can process emotional valence and drive approach or avoidance, but that's not the same as having a preference in the full-blooded sense. He also accused Maya of being a reverse language-chauvinist: she's assuming any information-processing that guides behavior must be conscious, but we already accept that lots of unconscious processes guide action.

Then Maya threw out this idea she half-remembered from somewhere: maybe consciousness isn't all-or-nothing but comes in degrees of integration density, and the right hemisphere has maybe sixty percent of the integration needed, so it's not a full second person but it's not nothing either -- a kind of silent partner with muted awareness. Leo said that sounds like a cop-out: either there's something it's like to be that hemisphere, or there isn't. Adding a percentage just avoids the hard question.
