As part of an independent study in philosophy of science, I have come to defend a version of pragmatic encroachment: whether a subject genuinely knows that p depends not only on the strength of their evidence but also on the practical stakes of being wrong. I apply this specifically to scientific belief, proposing that two researchers with identical access to the same climate-model dataset might differ in whether they know the model’s conclusion—one writing a classroom report, the other advising binding international regulation—purely because the stakes differ. From this I draw a further institutional claim: peer review and verification standards should be tiered by practical consequence rather than applied uniformly, because the concept of knowledge itself shifts with stakes. What would count against this position? I want more than the familiar bank-case intuitions or the charge that it collapses into contextualism. Specifically, which formal results in decision theory, statistics, or philosophy of science would undermine the idea that stakes affect knowledge itself rather than merely rational belief? Which intuitions about scientific objectivity—say, that replication and verification ought to be context-independent—would be fatal, and which would defenders concede as merely awkward? Could the view be insulated by redefining “evidence” to include stakes-sensitivity, and if so, is that insulation philosophically virtuous or a vice? I also want an honest assessment of whether my tiered-peer-review extension is a natural consequence or an overreach that introduces inconsistencies the core view does not commit to. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a committed pragmatic encroacher would themselves concede, and assess whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability. Finally, please state how confident you are in your assessment overall, and single out the one point—whether about the philosophical literature, the analogy to scientific practice, or the risk of insulation—where you are least sure.
