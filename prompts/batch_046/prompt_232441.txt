Write an essay on the following controversy. Montaigne’s essay on cannibals is frequently interpreted as an early statement of cultural relativism: he appears to praise Tupinambá ritual practices while condemning European cruelty, suggesting that moral judgment is bound to local custom. Yet a closer reading reveals that he judges European torture and religious violence by standards he treats as universal—natural reason and human dignity—while praising the cannibals precisely for living closer to those standards than do civilized Europeans. The tension deepens when we consider his simultaneous commitment to Stoic withdrawal and his apparent admiration for societies without private property or hierarchical command. Some readers take this combination to imply a value pluralism that respects incommensurable ways of life; others take it to show that Montaigne holds a single, austere natural-law criterion and uses foreign cultures only as a rhetorical mirror. Weigh these interpretations carefully. In particular, address whether Montaigne’s praise for the absence of property and authority among the cannibals can be reconciled with his Stoic ideal of self-governance without collapsing into either strict relativism or an unexamined universalism. Defend a clear verdict—either that Montaigne is best read as a cultural relativist, or that he is best read as a universalist using comparative ethnography as moral critique—and justify it through close reasoning rather than appeals to authority. Present your argument entirely in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though composing a single letter.
