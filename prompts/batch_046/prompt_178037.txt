I'm trying to name a specific ambiguity I keep running into in philosophy-of-mind discussions of the color phi phenomenon and retroactive theories of conscious experience. In the illusion, a red dot followed by a green dot elsewhere is perceived as a single dot moving and turning yellow midway. Some revisionists say the brain "rewrites" the first experience after the second arrives; others reply that there is no temporal window for editing. I think both sides repeatedly slide between two different models of revision without flagging the shift. One is Orwellian: the subject's earlier experience of red is replaced in memory, so they never genuinely experienced red at all. The other is Stalinesque: the red is genuinely experienced, but then airbrushed out of the final conscious report, leaving a seamless green-to-green narrative unless the subject is specifically probed. The objections that defeat one do not defeat the other, yet debaters treat them interchangeably. Is there an established term for this particular failure to distinguish retroactive replacement of experiential content itself from retroactive suppression of an already-completed experience? If not, can you coin one and specify exactly how it differs from ordinary equivocation and from the standard Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction itself? Lead with your bottom line: is there a recognized concept covering this ambiguity, and if not, what is the sharpest coinage with its boundary conditions? Keep the response to one paragraph, no more than 160 words.
