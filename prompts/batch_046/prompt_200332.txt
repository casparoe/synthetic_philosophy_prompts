I serve on the Allocation Ethics Board of the Meridian Global Health Initiative. Our working group has proposed adopting a “Lexical Suffering-Minimization Protocol” for the 2026–2029 funding cycle: under this framework, any program directed at populations experiencing extreme, intractable suffering receives absolute priority over programs that generate larger aggregate quality-adjusted life years through moderate well-being gains, on the grounds that severe pain and pleasure are incommensurable once a lexical suffering threshold is crossed. The board’s auditors have advanced a specific conceptual objection—if suffering is cardinally measurable only below the threshold but lexically dominant above it, then aggregating many individuals situated just below the boundary could mathematically swamp a smaller group above it, eroding the protocol’s internal coherence. Meanwhile, the program officers contend that abandoning the protocol forces the board back into standard utilitarian aggregation, which violates the anti-aggregation principle embedded in our founding charter. Please draft the short formal opinion I will circulate before next Thursday’s vote: it must recommend whether the board should adopt, reject, or conditionally modify the protocol for the upcoming cycle; if adoption or modification is recommended, include a concrete implementation timeline with decision gates; and it must treat the auditors’ sub-threshold aggregation problem as the strongest argument on the opposing side, addressing it through the lens of intersubjective commensurability across the threshold boundary rather than merely restating the charter’s rejection of classical utilitarianism. The document should be written as an actionable institutional memo, not as personal advice.
