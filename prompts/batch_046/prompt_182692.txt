I've been sitting in on an interdisciplinary working group—philosopher of biology, cognitive scientist, AI researcher—that has spent a semester adjudicating cases under the rubric competence without comprehension, treating Darwin's inversion of design reasoning and Turing's inversion of mentalistic reasoning as the twin pillars of their framework. So far they have rendered consistent verdicts on five cases. First, Darwinian natural selection crafting the camera eye: adaptive functional competence with zero comprehension of optics by any selecting agent, treated as the canonical case undermining arguments that require an intelligent understander behind complex adaptation. Second, a Turing-style computing machine that produces human-appropriate conversational responses through formal symbol manipulation alone: behavioral competence is present, semantic comprehension is absent, but functional equivalence does not entail mental equivalence. Third, a protein-folding prediction system that achieves near-experimental accuracy without providing biochemical mechanisms or causal explanations: they granted predictive competence but denied theoretical comprehension, deeming it scientifically useful yet explanatorily hollow. Fourth, a human savant who solves complex arithmetic problems through unconscious pattern-matching and cannot articulate the algorithm: explicit conceptual comprehension is missing, yet because the subject is a conscious agent embedded in other understanding capacities, they refused to reduce the skill to pure mechanism and attributed a residual implicit comprehension. Fifth, a slime-mold network that solves maze problems via chemical gradient diffusion with no central processor: pure competence without comprehension, fully analogous to Darwinian adaptation, with no basis for cognitive attribution.

Now they have been handed a new case and asked for a recommendation consistent with this track record. The case is an artificial language system—an evolutionary-trained model—whose weights were not optimized by gradient descent on a static corpus but generated through iterative selection over generations of variant architectures, retaining only those that best satisfy a hidden reward function specifying coherent philosophical argumentation. The resulting system produces sustained, accurate reasoning about ethics and epistemology, offers post-hoc explanations of its outputs that are causally disconnected from its selection-driven architecture since it has no internal representational access to why particular weights survived, and operates without conscious substrate or biological history. The group must decide whether this system possesses genuine comprehension of the philosophical topics it discusses, or merely evolutionary-competence without comprehension analogous to the eye or the slime mold. They need to identify which prior verdict is the closest analog—whether the savant, the protein-folder, or the Turing machine—and state honestly what principle they would be adopting if they grant comprehension versus if they deny it, since their precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome. They should lay out at least two rules that jointly fit all five prior verdicts, say what each predicts for this evolutionary-trained model, indicate which prediction best preserves coherence with the track record, and specify one further hypothetical case that would decisively distinguish those rules.
