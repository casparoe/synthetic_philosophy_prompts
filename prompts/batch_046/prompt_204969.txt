Here is an argument I constructed for a seminar on moral responsibility and now find suspicious because it seems to collapse the debate between Dennett and Caruso into a false dilemma. (1) Dennett holds that determinism is compatible with moral responsibility because an agent can still be the source of her actions in the sense of being responsive to reasons, and punishment can serve its proper social function without requiring metaphysical freedom. (2) Caruso holds that determinism eliminates ultimate sourcehood, so retributive punishment is never justified; at most we may quarantine dangerous agents as we quarantine carriers of disease, with no moral blame attached. (3) Now introduce a deterministic predictor that can identify, before any crime, whether an agent is reason-responsive—she would have refrained given stronger reasons—or reason-rigid—she would have acted regardless of what reasons were presented. (4) Dennett permits punishment, understood as a reason-responsive practice, only for the responsive agents, and permits pure quarantine for the rigid ones. (5) Caruso permits only quarantine for both, since determinism removes ultimate desert from both. (6) But the distinction between responsive and rigid is itself a modal distinction about what the agent would have done in counterfactual circumstances, and in a deterministic world these counterfactuals are fixed by prior states no less than the actual actions. (7) Therefore, if determinism undermines the moral significance of actual causal origins, it should equally undermine the moral significance of modal causal origins—there is no metaphysically privileged form of "responsiveness" that survives determinism intact. (8) So Dennett's reason-responsiveness criterion collapses: it tries to preserve a desert-like boundary using modal properties that determinism renders as causally fixed and morally inert as actual actions. (9) Conversely, if one insists that modal properties do retain moral weight despite being determined, then determinism does not eliminate all desert-relevant distinctions, and Caruso's uniform quarantine model is too sweeping. (10) Hence either Dennett's compatibilism collapses into Caruso's universal quarantine, or Caruso's rejection of desert fails. The conclusion feels like a forced choice. Which step should be rejected? I would prefer to keep the idea that reason-responsiveness marks a genuine moral boundary between punishment and mere containment, but I want to know precisely what that costs—does saving it require abandoning determinism, or can it be defended within a compatibilist framework without smuggling in a hidden libertarian premise about uncaused modal capacities?
