I sit on the funding panel for our university's cross-disciplinary cognition grant programme, and we've recieved a pilot proposal that's a bit of a head-scratcher. The researchers want £40k to build what they call a careenium architecture — basically a processing layer where symbolic tokens, which they call simmballs, don't just label physical states but actually push the substrate around. Their argument is that the symbol's content exerts downward causal pressure: for instance, a simmball representing heat physically biases the silicon dynamics toward heat-seeking behaviour, rather than the dynamics just being interpreted after the fact.

One external reviewer said this is just dualism with a soldering iron, and that symbols never push substrate — it's always substrate dynamics being read as symbols. The PI came back saying that's exactly the symbol-grounding mistake, and without this downward causation you can't explain why the system halts at a wall when it represents danger.

We need to decide by Friday whether to approve the pilot. Can you draft the short memo I'll circulate before the meeting? It should recommend a concrete course — yes, no, or conditional — with reasoning that takes the PI's claim seriously (don't just dismiss it by waving at physical closure) but also doesn't pretend we've solved philosophy of mind with one grant. Make it something the board can actually act on.
