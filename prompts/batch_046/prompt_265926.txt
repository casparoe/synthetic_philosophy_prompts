me and two friends were arguing about Hofstader's idea that we can kind of live on in other people's brains - not literalley, but through patterns and memories they carry. (1) my friend said take it literal for a second: imagine tech where one continuous awareness is distributed across three different brains, like each head runs part of the same loop. the hosts mostly live normal lives, but together they form one subject with way more cognitive range. he said if Hofstadter is right that the self is just a pattern, then this should be possible - its one "I" spread across locations. (2) i said thats not what he meant at all. he was talking about symbolic survival, like your parents living on in you through habits and values. distributing one consciousness across live brains isnt persistence, its just splitting a person into pieces that happen to sit in other heads. (3) he said youre still stuck on the brain-container idea. if the pattern is what matters, three brains hosting one pattern is just one self with three addresses. (4) she jumped in and said even if thats true, welfare counting gets weird. if that distributed self experiences one massive good life, but the three hosts also experience three normal lives, do we count four lives? or one? she said this shows Hofstadter's idea breaks when you apply it to ethics, since you cant tell whose welfare youre measuring. he said thats a feature not a bug - it just means our individualist ethics need fixing. can you go through these four moves and say who's making sense where? specifically, is move 1 actually extending Hofstadter or misreading him, and does move 4 reveal a real problem or just confuse personal identity with welfare accounting?
