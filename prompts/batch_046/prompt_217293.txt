Please argue that the thermodynamic arrow of time is not ultimately explained by low-entropy cosmological boundary conditions coupled with symmetric statistical mechanics, but rather requires an irreducible directional asymmetry built into the dynamics of quantum decoherence itself. Specifically, defend the position that decoherence should be modeled not as a symmetric branching of the wavefunction into equally weighted histories, but as a directed, non-branching correlation collapse that intrinsically distinguishes past-correlated states from future-uncorrelated ones. Maintain that this directional decoherence—not entropy increase—constitutes the fundamental ground of macroscopic irreversibility, and respond to the objection that this merely relocates the arrow from cosmology into quantum dynamics without genuinely explaining it. The argument should not appeal to specific cosmological models or to citation-dependent technical results, but should turn on whether decoherence dynamics can coherently bear this explanatory burden on conceptual grounds alone.
