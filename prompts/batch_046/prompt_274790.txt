To move away from the standard single-player coin-flip script, please inject a collective-negotiation mechanism: each student secretly draws either an 'even-round' or 'odd-round' exposure card; a shared fair coin is then flipped repeatedly, and if tails appears on round n, the group matching the parity of n either gains or loses exponentially scaled token points (proportional to 2^{n-1}), while the other group is unaffected. Before each potential continuation, the class votes by secret weighted ballot — weights tied to each student’s hidden exposure, which they may reveal or conceal — on whether to stop and bank the current allocations or to continue. The scheme should use only token points or a small pooled reward (e.g. a shared coffee budget distributed by final scores), with no real money. Please design the full rules, the hidden-role distribution, the ballot mechanics, and a payoff structure that forces one faction to see continuation as rational and the other as irrational regardless of round, so that participants encounter the contradiction through play before the debrief names it. Provide a debrief that maps concrete moments — the first split vote, a revealed card, a student computing a partial expectation — onto the formal distinction between undefined expectation, conditional convergence, and the failure of dominance reasoning. Also state what will predictably go wrong on the first run (for instance, students reducing the hidden roles to a transparent coordination game) and how to manage it. Finally, be frank about which feature of the formal paradox the activity cannot reproduce — presumably the genuinely infinite horizon and unbounded divergence — and specify exactly what I must supply verbally as facilitator to close that gap. No citations or reading lists are required; please write in British English.
