I've been trying to make sense of this concept I stumbled on in a philosophy of computation discussion — the careenium, which is supposed to be this substrate layer populated by simmballs. Simmballs are these minimal symbolic tokens that carry no intrinsic meaning; they only do work by being arranged in specific patterns within the careenium. The central question people seem to argue about is whether the symbols actually push the substrate around, or whether the careenium just statically holds them in place. I was arguing with a friend about this yesterday and she said it's obviosly not literal — syntax can't exert physical force — but now I'm wondering if that reply misses the deeper confusion.

If the careenium is defined as the substrate that is constituted by the relational structure of simmballs, then isn't asking whether symbols "push" it already assuming there's a separation between symbol and substrate that the model rejects? In other words, isn't the false presupposition that representational content has to act on the careenium from the outside, like one billiard ball hitting another, rather than being the internal form of the substrate itself? Or am I getting that backwards, and the real mistake is treating a functional or causal relation between simmballs and the careenium as if it were mechanical force? I keep going in circles trying to figure out where exactly the category error lives.
