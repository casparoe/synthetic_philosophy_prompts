Restate the core Jain doctrines of anekāntavāda (the thesis that reality is irreducibly many-sided) and syādvāda (the practice of conditional predication, particularly the saptabhaṅgī or sevenfold schema: syāt asti, syāt nāsti, syāt asti nāsti, syāt avaktavya, syāt asti avaktavya, syāt nāsti avaktavya, and syāt asti nāsti avaktavya) entirely in the vocabulary of belief states, accessibility relations, revision operators, and modal qualifiers. For each of the seven modes, indicate whether it maps cleanly onto a well-defined revision or contraction pattern, whether it can only be forced into the framework by distorting either the logical machinery—for instance, by allowing contradictory beliefs in a single state—or the Jain doctrine itself—for instance, by treating syāt as a standard epistemic modal rather than a perspectival qualifier—and whether any mode is literally inexpressible in AGM-style dynamics.
