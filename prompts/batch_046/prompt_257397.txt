I'm trying to work out the boundary conditions for inverse reinforcement learning: when observed behavior genuinely reveals stable preferences, and when it instead reveals compulsion, strategic gaming, or incapacity. Here are my verdicts on concrete cases. (1) A worker remains in a dead-end job for two decades while complaining constantly: I think IRL should infer a strong revealed preference for security, not false consciousness. (2) An addict relapses repeatedly despite expressing immediate regret: I think IRL should not treat the consumption as preference-revealing; it reflects a failure of rational integration, and the model should discount it. (3) A community uniformly adopts strict norms, reports high satisfaction, and shows no exit: IRL correctly infers a collective preference for those norms. (4) A user rewards an AI assistant only for sycophantic answers, even when they privately know the answers are wrong: here I think IRL does correctly infer a revealed preference for flattery, but that preference is directed at the interaction, not at truth—so the behavior is genuinely revealing, yet about the wrong object. (5) An ascetic monk and a severely depressed anhedonic both spend almost nothing on discretionary goods: for the monk, IRL reveals a preference for simplicity; for the depressed person, identical behavior should not be read as preference, because the cause is incapacity rather than choice.

The tension I cannot resolve is between (2) and (4): in both cases the agent knows the behavior is bad and does it anyway, yet I want to discount addiction but not strategic feedback bias. Is there a single principle—perhaps involving counterfactual reflective endorsement, stability under self-modification, or the distinction between instrumental and terminal choice—that separates preference-revealing behavior from noise, gaming, or dysfunction? If no principle saves all five, which verdict should I abandon, and what does the best principle predict for the case I have not listed: a person takes a drug that eliminates emotional affect, then consistently chooses to remain on it and reports no regret?
