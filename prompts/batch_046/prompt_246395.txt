I sit on our university's animal ethics board—philosophy dept representative, actually—and there's this pattern I keep noticing. We have a hard binary gate: mammals, birds, fish, decapods go through full review; drosophila, nematodes, or computer simulations of nociception just check a box and move on. But then inside full review we push the 3Rs constantly, especially replacing 'higher' animals with 'lower' ones—mice with larval zebrafish, zebrafish with invertebrates—explicitly on the grounds that they suffer less. So suffering comes in degrees when we're optimizing, but sentience is an on/off switch when we're deciding who needs protection. Lately people are submitting protocols with cortical organoids and simple reactive robots that have feedback loops, and we're trying to cram them into the same binary form. What philosophical view of moral status does all this actually presuppose? Could it be consistent with taking gradation seriously all the way down—through insects, maybe even to thermostats—or is the binary just bureaucratic lazyness? Seperate what's genuinely settled from what's contested among people who actually work on this, don't present one view as the consensus.
