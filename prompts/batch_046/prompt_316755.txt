Higher-order theories of consciousness hold that a mental state becomes conscious in virtue of being targeted by a suitable higher-order representation, yet the framework faces several unresolved tensions that determine whether it survives as a distinct program or collapses into global workspace theory or first-order representationalism. The open issues I see are these: first, whether genuinely targetless higher-order states can occur and, if they do, whether they generate consciousness without any object-state, thereby undermining the core relational claim; second, whether the misrepresentation problem—where a higher-order representation attributes state X to the subject while the subject is in state Y or in none—can be resolved without ad hoc accuracy conditions that beg the question against rival views; third, whether the denial of animal consciousness is a necessary consequence of the theory or only of an implausibly strong version of the higher-order requirement, and whether weakening that requirement to accommodate animals destroys the theory’s explanatory advantage; fourth, whether higher-order representations must be occurrent or can be merely dispositional, and whether that distinction saves the framework from requiring constant, resource-intensive self-monitoring; fifth, whether the theory can non-circularly distinguish consciousness from self-consciousness, given that the required higher-order state already involves some form of self-reference.

Which two or three of these actually determine whether the research program survives, and which are internal housekeeping issues that could be settled without shifting anyone’s commitment to the framework? Rank them by how much resolving each one would move the broader debate—for example, by neutralizing the strongest first-order objections, by showing the theory applies non-trivially to non-human cognition, or by clarifying whether the view requires an implausible architecture of constant meta-representation. Defend the ordering with explicit criteria: tractability for a researcher working alone, how many other questions depend on this one being settled first, and whether it addresses a live objection rather than a peripheral puzzle. Also identify one line of inquiry within this list that looks important but is actually a dead end, and explain why pursuing it would not change the verdict on the framework’s viability. I want a defended agenda for where effort should go, not a survey of the literature, and I do not want a bibliography or reading list.
