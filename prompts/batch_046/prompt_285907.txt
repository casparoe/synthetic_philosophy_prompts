I'm taking a philosophy of mind course on consciousness and temporal experience, and I'm stuck on whether perceptual revision is Orwellian or Stalinesque in the color phi phenomenon. Specifically, I want to understand whether an initial green flash is genuinely present in phenomenal consciousness and then retroactively overwritten, or whether it is erased and replaced before it ever enters consciousness, with the revision occurring inside some brief phenomenal editing window. Write a dialogue between two equally informed philosophers debating what color phi reveals about the actual timing of experience. One should defend the Orwellian view that the green experience occurs and is then corrected; the other should defend the Stalinesque view that the green experience is never phenomenally present and is substituted by a constructed red-green sequence. Have them respond directly to each other's arguments, not talk past one another, and require them to address a modified case: subjects in a rapid-phi variant report seeing both colors at once with no felt sense of correction or erasure. They should conclude by marking precisely where their disagreement depends on empirical predictions about neural timing versus conceptual commitments about what counts as the time at which an experience genuinely occurs.
