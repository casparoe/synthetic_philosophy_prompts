I'm a novelist drafting a novel about an institutional architect who designs labor and welfare systems, and in my background reading on moral philosophy I've kept encountering a structural pattern I have no word for. The pattern runs as follows: a deontological framework treats a particular relational act—say, compelling one person to work for another's exclusive benefit—as categorically forbidden, on the ground that it treats the compelled party merely as a means. Yet when that same relational structure is fragmented across contracts, intermediaries, and market mechanisms—so that no single agent performs the forbidden act and the burden becomes statistical rather than targeted—the framework no longer registers a violation. Each contractual link is individually permissible, but the aggregate reproduces precisely the asymmetry that was prohibited in the direct, face-to-face case. What interests me is not that consequences are ignored, but that the constraint itself seems resolution-dependent: it binds with full force at the level of visible, individual interaction, yet dissolves or becomes structurally invisible when distributed across complex institutional architecture, without ever being formally overridden or weighed against outcomes.

I've encountered discussions of agent-relativity and of the paradox of deontology, but neither quite captures this specific failure mode—the way an absolute prohibition can be diffused through social complexity rather than defeated by argument. Does this configuration have an established name in the literature? If it does, I want to know exactly how it relates to indirect-effect problems and to the non-identity problem, and where its boundaries lie; if it does not, I want it named and defined with precision—distinguished from ordinary auxiliary hypotheses, from simple consequentialist accommodation, and from plain equivocation—so that I can understand exactly what the phenomenon is and what it is not. Please begin with your bottom line in the first sentence, then develop the reasoning without preamble and without restating my question.
