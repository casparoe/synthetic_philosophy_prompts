Here is my opening move. I present a modified ring of Gyges that grants not physical invisibility but perfect epistemic erasure: after any unjust act, every other person's memory of your involvement is permanently and verifiably destroyed, yet your own consciousness, memories, and affective capacities remain fully intact. Crucially, you possess an innate moral sensibility — a direct, non-inferential distress at causing harm to others — but you also know with certainty that this distress is undetectable, carries no social cost, and will never influence anyone's behavior toward you. Given these conditions, do you still maintain that the rational agent will act unjustly? And if you dismiss that internal moral distress as rationally irrelevant, on what precise grounds do you separate it from the external costs — social punishment, legal risk, reputation — that the original ring removed?
