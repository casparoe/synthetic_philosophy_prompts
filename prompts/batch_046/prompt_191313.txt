Write a dialogue between two equally sharp philosophers debating judgment aggregation and the discursive dilemma. Character A defends premise-based aggregation modified by a "competence-partition" rule: different members of the collective have recognized authority over distinct premises, so premise judgments should be weighted by domain expertise and conclusions derived mechanically. Character B defends conclusion-based aggregation, arguing that collective rationality requires a logically coherent final judgment set and that weighting premises merely relocates the paradox—when expert domains overlap, the meta-aggregation of weights reproduces the same inconsistency. Have them respond directly to each other’s points: A should challenge whether collective coherence is a legitimate normative constraint on heterogeneous groups; B should press A on how her rule handles overlapping expert claims without either collapsing into conclusion-based voting or generating an infinite regress of arbitration levels; A should propose a concrete structural fix, such as a non-voting arbitration layer for overlaps; B should show why that fix either violates procedural fairness or reintroduces the dilemma at the arbitration stage. Keep the discussion conceptually dense—focused on logical structure, normative constraints on collective belief, and the stability of aggregation rules—without requesting citations, bibliographies, or references to named theories.
