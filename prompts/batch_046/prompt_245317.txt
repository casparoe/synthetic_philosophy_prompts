me and some friends have been going in circles about eliminative materialism -- specifically the Churchland-style move where folk psychology gets compared to phlogiston or caloric, something that seemed useful but eventually turned out to refer to nothing real. The anology is everywhere in these debates: beliefs and desires are the "entities" that will vanish once mature neuroscience arrives, just like phlogiston vanished when we got oxygen chemistry. I want to actually audit it instead of just waving it around.

So can you lay out the correspondence explicitly? What maps onto the old theory, the entities, the predictive successes, the replacement framework, and the moment of elimination? Then the breaks: does folk psychology have law-like generalizations comparable to combustion laws, or is it more like a narrative framework? Is there any experimental result that could play the Michelson-Morley role for belief-talk, or is elimination going to be more like a gradual reinterpretation where we still say "she believes it's raining" but mean something totally different? Also, my friend threw in this idea she calls "social scaffolding" -- that belief and desire talk isn't trying to describe brain states at all, but works like traffic rules or money, coordinating behavior without describing anything. If that's right, the phlogiston comparison might be comparing apples to oranges, since no one ever thought traffic rules were "false theories" about physics. Does that disanalogy kill the anology, or can the eliminativist absorb it? And bottom line: once you map the breaks, does the conclusion -- that beliefs and desires literally don't exist -- still stand, or was the analogy doing all the rhetorical work?
