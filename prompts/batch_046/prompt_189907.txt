I have been revisiting Richard Jeffrey's account of conditionalization in a seminar on formal epistemology, specifically his treatment of uncertain evidence—cases where an agent's experience does not fully determine a unique proposition but instead shifts confidence across an entire partition of possibilities. Recently, a colleague suggested what she terms "stratified Jeffrey updating": rather than holding the evidential partition fixed and merely redistributing credence according to the observed probabilities, the agent also allows the granularity and boundaries of the partition itself to be revised by the same evidential input. I am trying to determine whether this stratified variant constitutes a genuine extension of Jeffrey's framework or whether it collapses into standard Jeffrey updating applied to a more finely divided state space. Specifically, I want a comparison that addresses whether permitting the partition to shift solves any philosophical problem—such as cases where the taxonomy of live possibilities is itself in dispute—that Jeffrey's original rigidity conditions were designed to preserve, or whether it instead introduces formal pathologies like non-commutativity, loss of convergence, or violations of the constraint that posterior probabilities must match the evidential probabilities on the observed cells. Please compare the two approaches with attention to where the divergence is technically substantive rather than merely notational, and indicate whether a defender of classical Jeffrey conditionalization should regard stratified updating as a legitimate generalization or as an unnecessary departure from the framework's core motivation.
