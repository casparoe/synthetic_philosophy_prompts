So I'm putting together the final exam for my moral philosphy seminar — upper-level, mostly juniors and seniors who've already done the intro ethics sequence. We spent a lot of time on Glaucon's challenge in Republic Book II and the ring of Gyges, but I don't want students to just recite the standard line that justice is only good for its consequences. I was chatting with a friend about what I'd call a "reverse ring" — not invisibility, but total transparency plus emotional numbness. Specifcally: imagine a ring that makes every unjust act you do immediately visible and correctly attributed to you, yet also strips away all internal feeling about moral choices — no guilt, no satisfaction, no warmth from helping someone. The point is to seperate the "being seen" part from the "feeling good" part and see if justice still collapses.

I want an essay question that asks students to use this reverse-ring scenario to evaluate Glaucon's claim that no one is just willingly. The question should push them to ask whether justice is valuable only because of external rewards and punishments, or whether there's some internal structural feature — maybe consistency of character, or a kind of relational coherence — that survives even when visibility and affect are manipulated seperately. The answer should be doable in about 60 minutes, maybe 700 words max.

Please write the exam question itself, then a model answer of roughly that length, and then a marking scheme. Present the marking scheme as a numbered list with exactly five items, each just one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Each item should name one specific conceptual move the best answers must make and the corresponding mistake that should lose marks. Don't ask for citations or a bibliography — this is a closed-book exam.
