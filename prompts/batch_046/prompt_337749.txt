I'm trying to write up the results of a long-running argument between two friends about the comparative morality of what sociologists call honor, dignity, and victimhood cultures. One of them—call her the Dignity Universalist—holds that dignity culture is morally superior because it grounds status in an intrinsic, non-indexical worth that does not depend on social recognition or comparative rank; she sees victimhood culture as a pathological regression in which moral standing becomes contingent on suffering and third-party validation, effectively reintroducing status competition through the back door. The other—call him the Honor Pluralist—defends honor culture as containing a coherent, relational structure of moral accountability that dignity culture wrongly dissolves; he argues that victimhood culture is not the opposite of honor culture but its bureaucratized continuation, and that dignity's supposed universalism actually conceals an implicit indexical moral status (who counts as fully human is still socially determined, just hidden behind formal rules).

Please draft the joint statement these two should have produced after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The document should open with a numbered list of claims both can sign—about the descriptive features of each culture, about what moral superiority would have to mean in this debate, about the formal similarities between grievance mechanisms in honor and victimhood cultures, and about the empirical conditions under which each culture fails to protect the vulnerable. Then present a disagreements table with exactly these columns: Issue; Formulation the Dignity Universalist accepts for her own view; Formulation the Honor Pluralist accepts for his own view; The crux (the sub-question that, if settled, would move at least one side); A concrete case or institutional design both agree bears on it; The Dignity Universalist's prediction about that case; The Honor Pluralist's prediction about that case. For each crux, be precise: for instance, if one crux concerns whether dignity culture can account for moral injury without smuggling in status distinctions, specify a test case—such as how a dignity-based legal system should treat dignitary harm versus tangible injury—where both sides agree on the institutional details but predict different normative outcomes.

In addition, identify any part of their dispute that turns out to be verbal rather than substantive—say, if universalism is being used in two different senses—and explain which part and why. End with a brief structured summary in JSON format with these named fields: "agreed_claims" (array of strings), "verbal_disputes" (array of objects with "term" and "resolution"), "cruxes" (array of objects with "crux_name", "test_case", "dignity_prediction", "honor_prediction"). Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the argument should stand on conceptual reasoning that does not require looking up specific texts.
