You chair the editorial ethics board of the Meridian Public Affairs Foundation, a nonprofit publisher of long-form reporting on municipal infrastructure and housing policy. At next Thursday's meeting, the board must vote on whether to adopt the proposed Adaptive Engagement Protocol for our donor newsletter. Under the protocol, every fundraising appeal must follow a three-stage architecture: an opening calibrated to the individual subscriber's emotional profile (drawn from an optional intake quiz completed at sign-up), followed by a factually accurate but selectively sequenced narrative of a specific housing-system failure, and concluding with a values-aligned closure that frames donation not merely as financial support but as an act of identity preservation—specifically, that giving protects the kind of city the subscriber said they want to live in.

The communications director contends this is legitimate persuasion: the underlying claims are verifiable, the cause is genuinely consequential, and emotional framing is unavoidable in advocacy writing; suppressing personalized appeals would treat readers as incapable of responding to reasons they themselves endorsed in the intake survey. Two board members object that the protocol amounts to manipulation: it deploys psychographic data to target known emotional vulnerabilities, sequences information to amplify regret aversion, and collapses financial contribution into moral self-definition, thereby treating the subscriber as a behavioral object to be optimized rather than a reasoning partner. They stress that the design goal is precisely to elicit donations that subscribers would withhold if they encountered the same information in a neutral, non-personalized format.

Please draft the short policy memo you will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete decision—approve, reject, or approve subject to enforceable conditions—with an explicit time frame for implementation or review. Your reasoning should treat the strongest counter-argument with intellectual honesty, and should provide practical criteria that staff could apply in future campaigns to distinguish permissible emotional framing from impermissible psychological exploitation. Do not request bibliographies, cite specific scholars by name, or invoke obscure theoretical frameworks; ground your analysis in conceptual distinctions and institutional judgment alone.
