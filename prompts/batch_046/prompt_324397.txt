My friends Maya and Jordan got into it over lunch about desert and meritocracy. They were arguing about this hypothetical bonus system at work where you get points for actual contribution, but there's a weird rule: if your contribution relied on equipment or resources you didn't personally build or earn, you lose half your points. Maya said thats totally unfair, because desert should track your skill and effort, not luck about what tools you had access too. Jordan said no, merit is always comparative within the actual conditions people face, so if one engineer had a custom lab and another had old gear, giving them equal credit ignores that the first one's output was partly bought, not deserved.

Maya pushed back that if you take that far, desert collapses into equality of opportunity, and then nobody deserves anything since birth luck always plays a role. Jordan said that's not collapse, it's just recognizing desert is "within-a-framework" - you deserve according to the rules of the game, not some cosmic scoreboard. Maya said but then meritocracy is circular: we make the rules, then say people deserve what the rules hand out. Jordan said it's not circular, its conventional, like chess - you deserve to win if you checkmate, even though chess rules are made up.

Can you walk through there exchange move by move and say who's right about what? Also, are they both missing something important about how desert actually works?
