I'm writing a position paper on Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor and want a consistency audit before I present it. Here are my commitments: (1) the Inquisitor's diagnosis is largely correct—human beings systematically prefer the security of authority and material satisfaction to the anxiety of autonomous moral choice; (2) freedom, understood specifically as the burden of deciding for oneself, is nevertheless intrinsically valuable and not merely instrumentally so; (3) if a benevolent authority could maximize aggregate human flourishing by eliminating that burden, such a society would be all-things-considered better than a free but miserable one; (4) Dostoevsky intends Ivan's poem to show that Christ's refusal to answer the Inquisitor vindicates freedom even at the price of suffering, which means (3) must be rejected on Dostoevskyan grounds. Which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which only appear so? Rank the tensions by severity, specify the cheapest revision for each, and tell me whether (4) actually resolves the conflict between (1) and (2) or merely relocates it.
