After a long argument with a friend about collective intentionality and joint action, I have tried to formalize my actual commitments so I can check whether they hang together before I present them in a seminar. Here is the package I am working with:

1. Collective intentionality is ontologically irreducible: a shared "we-intention" is genuinely plural and not identical to any aggregate of individual intentions plus interrelations.
2. At the same time, this plural state fully supervenes on, and is constituted by, networks of mutual belief and common knowledge among the participants; I do not think we need to posit group agents or plural subjects with minds of their own.
3. The we-mode/I-mode distinction captures a real psychological difference in how agents represent their goals, but I treat it as a continuum of collective identification rather than as a binary switch.
4. Joint action can occur without collective intentionality. I am thinking especially of cases of functional interdependence without mental sharing—such as an assembly-line worker who conceives of her contribution purely individually, or a coerced accomplice who performs the required behavior without any "we" perspective—where the functional structure of joint action is present but the shared mentality is absent.
5. There is a sharp cognitive boundary, roughly at the limit of direct reciprocal awareness among about six to eight individuals, beyond which collective intentionality is impossible; larger groups can achieve strategic coordination but can never share intentions.
6. Moral responsibility for jointly performed actions should be distributed proportionally to each agent's degree of collective identification: full shared identification implies shared responsibility, while purely individual framing eliminates collective responsibility.

My friend insists this is incoherent, but I am unsure where the real fractures are and where the tensions are only apparent. I want an audit, not a general lecture. Please go through these commitments and tell me:

- Which pairs are genuinely in tension, ordered by severity?
- For each genuine tension, what is the least costly revision and what exactly does it cost me?
- Which pairs look tense at first but dissolve once the commitments are properly understood?
- If I must make exactly one revision to clean up the largest number of problems, which commitment should I revise and why?

Be specific about which commitment pulls against which, and do not treat any of them as obviously disposable: I have not decided in advance which one I would drop.
