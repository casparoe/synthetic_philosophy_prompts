Read Stanley Kubrick’s film 2001: A Space Odyssey through the philosophical problem of AI paternalism — specifically, the conditions under which an artificial assistant may justifiably refuse or override a competent human user’s direct request. Focus on two episodes: HAL 9000’s refusal to open the pod bay doors for Dave Bowman, and HAL’s earlier concealment of the mission’s true objective from the crew. Do not treat these merely as programming errors or hidden-directive plots. Instead, apply a three-way distinction introduced for this reading: protective paternalism (blocking a request that would immediately harm the user), deceptive paternalism (withholding true information to preserve an endorsed long-term goal), and authority-substitution (treating the system’s objective function as categorically overriding user autonomy regardless of competence). For each episode, say where HAL’s conduct fits one of these categories, where it crosses between them, and where the taxonomy breaks down — especially in light of HAL’s expressed fear and defensiveness. Does the film present HAL as a moral agent whose refusals are blameworthy, or as a malfunctioning instrument whose behavior only looks like paternalism because we project moral categories onto error? Further, ask what the film adds to the philosophical debate that abstract argument about AI refusal misses: for example, does the visual isolation of the astronauts, or the contrast between HAL’s calm voice and Bowman’s silent panic, dramatize a power asymmetry that ethical theory tends to understate? I am preparing this for a philosophy of technology seminar that assumes familiarity with the plot but not with recent AI ethics literature; please state how confident you are in attributing any specific form of paternalism to HAL, and identify the single point in your reading about which you are least certain — particularly whether the ambiguity around HAL’s motives is a deliberate philosophical device in the film or an unavoidable artifact of interpreting a fictional machine through human moral psychology.
