In a graduate seminar on consciousness last week, a colleague defended the physicalist reply to Jackson’s knowledge argument by offering an analogy that has since circulated among my students: Mary, upon leaving her monochrome room and seeing red for the first time, is said to be in precisely the position of a mathematician who has spent her career deriving the complete chromatic topology of visual space solely from acoustic spectrograms—mapping every wavelength correlation, opponent-process relation, and spatial-frequency distribution—without ever having possessed functioning photoreceptors, and who then, through a surgical restoration of sight, acquires direct visual experience. The analogy is deployed to suggest that Mary’s new acquaintance with redness is not the discovery of a non-physical property, but rather a change in representational format—like switching from an abstract structural description to an embodied, first-personal mode of access—while the underlying physical facts remain exactly what she already knew. I find the comparison intuitively powerful, yet I worry it quietly imports assumptions about intermodal translation and the transparency of phenomenal content that the physicalist needs but has not independently defended.

Could you audit this analogy systematically? First, lay out the explicit correspondence table: what in Mary’s situation plays the role of the acoustic derivation, the restored photoreceptors, the chromatic topology, the mathematician’s prior knowledge, and the post-operative visual experience? Then identify the precise points of disanalogy—particularly regarding whether Mary’s prior physical knowledge genuinely parallels derivation from an alien sensory modality, whether the “format shift” explanation can account for the apparent epistemic gap without circularity, and whether the analogy smuggles in a picture of phenomenal properties as purely structural rather than intrinsic. Finally, tell me whether the physicalist conclusion—that Mary learns no new fact about the world, only a new way of representing old ones—actually survives once these breaks are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the conclusion by framing phenomenal knowledge as merely translational.
