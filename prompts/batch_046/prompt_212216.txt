I’m working on a scene in my second novel where a burnt-out history professor starts a podcast about American self-mythology, and she keeps trying to sort her callers into real camps instead of letting them just chant slogans. Could you build me a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on American exceptionalism? I want the first split to be whether you treat the claim as descriptive (actual institutional differences), normative (a special moral duty), or ideological (power-laundering in patriotic dress). Then branch on what I’m calling the reflexive problem — if every nation claims it’s the special snowflake, does that falsify the American version or just make it unremarkable?

From there map out leaves for the Tocquevillian comparative thesis, the missionary liberal-democracy view, the critical-theory alibi line, and that cynical “only exceptional in its hypocrisy” stance, plus any unnamed combos that seem empty. Each leaf should carry a one-sentence note on it’s most awkward commitment, and the branch nodes should be concrete questions a listener could answer without knowing academic labels — like “do founding documents create genuinely different political habits, or just louder versions of the same ones?” Flag pairs your tree can’t seperate, and please don’t include a bibliography or ask me to verify scholars, since this is purely for dialogue and my character definately wouldn’t have one. Keep the whole thing under 500 words.
