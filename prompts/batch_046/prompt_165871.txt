I am preparing the metaphysical architecture for a novel in which a sculptor's marble masterwork is gradually reconstructed from synthetic composite, yet is intended to remain the identical artistic object, while the protagonist undergoes complete neural digitization and must determine whether she has survived or been replaced. Before I fix these premises in the narrative logic, I wish to audit the consistency of my underlying commitments.

I hold:
(a) that material constitution is best explained hylomorphically—objects are unitary composites of form and organized matter, not pairs of coincident entities—so a statue and its material substrate are one thing described in two ways, not two things occupying one region;
(b) that human personal identity is essentially biological: the substantial form of a person requires continuous living organic tissue, which means that a digital upload or synthetic bodily replacement preserves only a qualitative duplicate, not the same subject;
(c) that within fiction, a character's persistence should be governed by narrative and psychological continuity rather than by material substrate, which is why I treat my digitized protagonist as genuinely continuing;
(d) that the metaphysical principles applying to real-world entities should extend without discontinuity to fictional ones—if digitization fails to preserve personal identity in reality, then portraying it as preservation in a novel is not imaginative license but metaphysical error;
(e) that an artwork can survive total material replacement provided its formal structure and appropriate causal-historical lineage are maintained, so the reconstructed synthetic statue is genuinely the original masterpiece, and I see no principled reason within hylomorphism why artistic form should tolerate replacement while biological form should not.

Which commitments genuinely conflict, which conflicts are merely apparent, and what is the least costly revision to restore coherence? Please rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each what must be surrendered and what that surrender costs the narrative framework I have outlined.
