I am designing a fifty-minute session for first-year students in an interdisciplinary history-and-science seminar with no prior training in philosophy of history or epidemiology. The session must teach one idea: that microbes, climate shifts, and volcanic eruptions can function as genuine “historical actors”—forces with real causal efficacy in human affairs—without collapsing historical explanation into environmental determinism. The conceptual core is a sharp distinction between intentional agency and networked causal agency, and I want students to see precisely why giving causal weight to a non-human force does not automatically erase human institutional responsibility.

Use the 1815 Tambora eruption and its hypothesized atmospheric cascade—specifically the way disrupted monsoon patterns are thought to have accelerated the 1817 Bengal cholera outbreak—as the single carrying case, because it forces volcanic, climatic, microbial, and colonial administrative failures into one explanatory frame. Plan the teaching arc: the dependency order in which the pieces must be introduced so that the determinism-versus-integration distinction is earned rather than asserted; what properties this Tambora-to-cholera example must possess and what narrative pitfall it must avoid, especially the risk that it becomes a story in which British colonial water-management failures disappear; the misconceptions students will generate in predictable sequence—likely that “actor” implies intentionality, that admitting environmental causation renders human choice irrelevant, and that temporal correlation between eruption and epidemic proves direct mechanistic causation—and the exact point in the sequence where each misconception should be headed off; a concrete check for understanding that demonstrates they have grasped the non-determinist integration rather than merely repeated it; and which element of contemporary philosophy-of-science discussion—whether demarcation criteria or Actor-Network Theory—you will deliberately leave out at this level and why.
