These clusters, K should argue, persist across nearby possible histories where the shock still occurs but its surface features vary, and they exhibit causal homeostasis: they support predictions and interventions (for example, about labor-market entry behavior or risk preferences) in ways that pure age or period effects do not.

L should defend an "interventionist anti-realist" position drawn from causal reasoning: generational ontology fails a separability test. Any effect attributed to "being Gen Z" decomposes into age effects, period effects, and specific technological exposure, with no residual causal mechanism belonging to the cohort itself. The exchange must be genuine back-and-forth: K should directly address L's separability objection, perhaps by arguing that interaction effects between age and period are themselves cohort-specific and non-decomposable rather than noise to be factored out.
