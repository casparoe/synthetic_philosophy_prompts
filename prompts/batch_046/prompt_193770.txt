Consider a moral philosopher who has published the following verdicts on whether a blamer retains standing to blame a wrongdoer when the blamer bears some related moral fault:

- A legislator who regularly accepts corporate donations blames a lobbyist for bribery: she denies standing.
- A recovered alcoholic who drove drunk years ago blames a currently intoxicated driver: she grants standing.
- A factory owner who cut emissions by 40 percent but still emits blames a competitor who emits more and made no cuts: she grants standing.
- A nation that engaged in twentieth-century colonization blames a present-day state for territorial expansion: she denies standing.
- A journalist who misrepresented a single source once blames a colleague who fabricates habitually: she grants standing.

Now consider this new case: A climate scientist who took one unavoidable flight to present research advocating for reduced aviation emissions blames an airline executive for aggressively expanding luxury flight routes purely for profit. Predict whether she grants or denies the scientist standing. Identify at least two competing principles that could explain her pattern across cases 1–5—such as distinctions based on structural identity of the fault, causal contribution to the blamed wrong, institutional versus personal role, or degree of reform—state which one you think she actually employs, justify your choice by citing the closest analog among the earlier cases, and describe one further hypothetical case that would distinguish your preferred principle from its nearest rival. If you believe her precedents genuinely underdetermine the new case, specify what principle each possible verdict would commit her to, and identify which earlier case best supports each commitment.

Return your response as a JSON object with exactly these fields:
- "predicted_verdict": "grants standing" or "denies standing"
- "confidence": "high", "moderate", or "low"
- "closest_analog_case_number": integer 1–5
- "closest_analog_brief_description": brief string
- "candidate_principles": array of at least two strings, each naming and briefly describing a rule
- "preferred_principle": string with brief reasoning tied to the analog
- "distinguishing_case": one-sentence hypothetical that splits the rules
- "underdetermination_analysis": string explaining whether the precedents settle the case; if not, list each possible verdict with the principle it adopts and its supporting analog
