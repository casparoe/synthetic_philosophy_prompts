Compose a poem of exactly five stanzas—each functioning as one or two compressed sentences of philosophical tension rendered as image, with no introduction or conclusion outside the poem—about a virtual sword destroyed in a persistent-world game. Use free verse with a rhyme scheme that tracks an ontological turn: ABAB in the first two stanzas as the speaker describes the sword's weight in the player's hand and its entry in the ownership database; ABBA in the third stanza, where the speaker realizes its reality lies not in pixels or records alone but in the overlap between executable logic and social convention; and rhyming couplets in the final two stanzas that leave open whether the loss is damage to a real object or merely an institutional change. The poem should distinguish, without citing any philosopher, three registers of virtual existence—representational appearance, institutional ownership, and causal code-state—so that a reader familiar with virtual ontology recognizes the conceptual framework, while a general reader still grasps the grief of losing something that is partly made of agreement. Keep the tone formal and polished, and let the structural shift in rhyme mirror the shift from naive realism to an institutional-causal hybrid ontology.
