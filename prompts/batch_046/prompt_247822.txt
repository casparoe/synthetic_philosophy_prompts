ok so i want to check if my view on functionalism hangs together before i present it in seminar. here's where i'm at:

1. i'm a functionalist about propositional attitudes but not about phenomenal feel — beliefs and desires are just causal roles, but there's something it is like to feel pain that goes beyond the role.
2. i buy multiple realizability, but only within a substrate class: different carbon brains can realize the same pain, but silicon can't, because the electrochemical continuity matters for the phenomenal part.
3. i think functional roles have to be anchored to actual environmental interactions, not just internal state-transitions — so a perfect brain-in-vat simulation doesn't have real beliefs, it just mimics the causal profile.
4. still, if we build an embodied robot with the right causal loops interactng with the real world, i'd grant it beliefs even if its chips are silicon, because the role is satisfied.
5. i'm a physicalist overall, and i think zombie worlds are conceivable but that doesn't show they're possible — conceivability is just a guide, not proof.

do these five actually cohere? specifically, does (2) clash with (4) — if silicon can't do phenomenal pain, why can it do beliefs? and does (3)'s externalism undermine the functionalist idea that mental states are just internal roles? also, is (5) compatible with (1) given that i reject phenomenal functionalism? rank the tensions by how bad they are and say which single revision fixes the most. don't just say thier all fine.
