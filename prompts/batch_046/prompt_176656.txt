Hey, I'm a software engineer with zero formal philosophy background, but I've been thinking about the tragedy of the commons in tech and I'm stuck trying to see if there's one principle behind my gut reactions.

Here are my verdicts on cases:

1. A small open-source library used by millions, maintained by one person who never recieve payment. Eventually they burn out and archive the repo. To me that's a clear tragedy of the commons — everyone took, nobody gave back.

2. A company wiki that everyone reads but nobody updates. It gets stale and people complain, but it doesn't actually collapse; people just work around it. I don't feel like that's a tragedy, just annoying.

3. A neighbourhood air-quality scheme where every factory cuts emissions a little, but collectively it's still unhealthy. No single factory is the bad actor. I think this is a tragedy, but it's weird because there's no clear 'collapse' point — it just stays bad forever.

4. A free public Wi-Fi hotspot in a cafe. One customer hogs all the bandwidth, others get slow speeds, but the owner keeps it running because it brings people in. I don't think that's a tragedy at all, even though it's technically a shared resource being depleted.

5. An open-source security tool on GitHub with alot of downloads and zero pull requests. The maintainer quits. But here's the thing — I feel like this is a tragedy only if the tool is actually critical. If it's just a niche script, I don't care. So my verdict seems to depend on how 'essential' the commons is, but I can't formalise that.

6. Wikipedia pages for obscure programming languages: few editors, lots of readers. It never collapses, but it also never gets good. I call this 'stagnation without tragedy' — it's not good, but it's not the commons failing either.

I'm trying to figure out if there's a coherent principle that gets all six, or close. Maybe something about rivalrous depletion plus absence of governance? But case 4 has depletion and no governance, yet I said it's fine. And case 3 has governance (the scheme) but still feels like tragedy. So my principle needs fixing.

Is there an account that captures these, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? Also, what would it predict for a case I haven't listed: a blockchain protocol where validators are rewarded with tokens, but the energy cost is externalised onto the grid? And please, can you answer in British English? (Also, sorry if there's a typo or two — I'm writing this on my phone between builds.)
