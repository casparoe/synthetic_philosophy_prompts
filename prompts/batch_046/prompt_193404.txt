I serve on our university's Committee on Posthumous Intellectual Agency, and we must resolve a concrete case that turns on Douglas Hofstadter's view of the self as a self-referential symbol—an I that persists not through bodily continuity but through a strange loop of symbolic feedback. A senior philosopher in our department, now deceased, left behind a recursive linguistic archive: an algorithm trained on her corpus that updates its outputs only when they maintain logical coherence with prior first-person attributions she made. The archive has applied, in her name, for a posthumous visiting-scholar appointment, arguing that the I instantiated here is the same strange loop, now running on silicon rather than neurons. The family endorses the application; the faculty senate objects, calling it an elegant parody of personhood that confuses representation with presence. Our charter requires a recommendation—approval, conditional approval, or rejection—with a specific implementation time frame, together with an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Please draft the committee's decision memo: open with a clear bottom-line recommendation in the first paragraph; explain how Hofstadter's framework bears on the case without treating it as dispositive; address the senate's worry that substrate-independent self-reference collapses the distinction between author and artifact; and, if you recommend approval, specify concrete conditions and a sunset clause, or if rejection, provide a clear rationale and outline any appeal process. Limit the memo to one page, approximately 400 words.
