I have come to accept the view that supererogation is not a genuine moral category but an artifact of forcing continuous virtue-based evaluation into a binary duty framework: on this account, apparently heroic or saintly acts are not beyond duty but rather fulfillments of imperfect duties at a high resource-indexed threshold tied to the agent's available means and social role, and our talk of going beyond duty is just comparative praise misapplied to a gradient. What would count against this position? I want the arguments, intuitions, and structural results that should genuinely move me, ranked from decisive to merely awkward, including whether there are acts that resist redescription as high-threshold imperfect duties without stretching that category past recognition, whether defenders of this view must concede any real counterexamples rather than absorbing them by adjusting the index, and whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability by endlessly tweaking resource or role conditions. I also want an honest assessment of whether collapsing supererogation into duty produces unacceptable consequences elsewhere in moral theory—say, for how we understand moral praise, sacrifice, or the limits of obligation—and whether that exposure is fatal or merely uncomfortable.
