(1) If a belief-type was fixed by selection purely for reproductive fitness, and fitness is uncorrelated with truth in that domain, then instances of that belief-type lack epistemic justification.
(2) First-order moral beliefs (e.g., harming is wrong) were fixed purely for fitness, with no correlation to moral truth.
(3) So first-order moral beliefs lack justification.
(4) Metaethical beliefs — including the belief that (1)-(3) form a valid debunking argument — are generated by the same domain-general reasoning mechanisms, and there's no seperate "truth-tracking" module for metaethics.
(5) Therefore metaethical beliefs are also fixed purely for fitness, with no correlation to truth.
(6) In particular, the belief in (1) is itself an instance of a belief-type fixed purely for fitness.
(7) So (1) applies to itself: we lack justification for accepting (1).
(8) But if we lack justification for (1), we can't legitimately deploy it to reach (3).
(9) Hence evolutionary debunking either implodes into global skepticism or it's self-undermining.

I want to keep (1) and reject (5) if I can, but I'm not sure there's a principled reason metaethical reasoning gets a free pass. Is the error in (4) — assuming domain-generality — or is there a hidden modal slide between "fixed by selection" and "lacks justification"? Or am I just biting a bullet?
