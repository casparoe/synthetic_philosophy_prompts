If suffering-minimization is the correct framework for evaluating diets, and if one weights all affected sentient lives by the intensity and duration of their welfare regardless of species, then beef requires far more land per calorie than legumes but much of that land is pasture rather than mechanized cropland, poultry kills the largest number of farmed individuals per calorie though each lives briefly, and vegan staples depend directly on harvests that kill wild rodents, insects, and ground-nesting birds. Given these inputs, why doesn't the calculus consistently rank grass-fed beef above both conventional chicken and plant-based staples, since it avoids the direct mass killing of farmed birds and the hidden harvest mortality of grain and legume fields? Is the rejection of this ranking based solely on denying moral status to wild invertebrates, or does it rest on a deeper error in how land-use substitution, feed-crop displacement, and counterfactual replacement are aggregated when comparing pasture, feed agriculture, and direct human-consumption crops?
