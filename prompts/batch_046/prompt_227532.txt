I think part of the problem is that mechanism is being used for two different things: one side means a single predeterminded formal architechture, the other means any computable process including self-modifying, resource-bounded systems that revise their own proof procedures over time. Is the deadlock mostly verbal—both sides actually agreeing that minds outrun static formal systems, but talking past each other on what counts as a mechanism? Or is there a hidden substantive disagreement about whether even open-ended, learning-based architectures are still Gödel-vulnerable in some generalized sense? I want a real diagnosis, not a survey: are they answering different questions (logic vs cognitive architecture), disagreeing about what evidence should look like in philosophy of mind, or is the framing just ill-posed because Gödel's result doesn't rigorously transfer outside arithmetic? And honestly, if it's definitional or cross-purpose, what distinction or concrete example would actually move things—should we just retire the Gödel move in this context, or is there a precise formal result that could settle whether self-modifying systems escape the limitation?
