I think they're smuggling in half a dozen different theses under one catchy phrase, but I'm not sure how to separate them. Can you map out the distinct precise claims that slogan could stand for? I'm thinking of things like: (1) the purely semantic redundancy claim that asserting 'p is true' is equivalent to asserting p (the old Ramsey-style redundancy), (2) Horwich-style minimalism that there is no substantive nature or property called truth, (3) a syntactic prosentential view that 'is true' just operates like a pronoun, (4) a metaphysical claim that truth lacks causal or explanitory power, and maybe (5) an epistemic claim that we never need to invoke truth to explain belief-formation. For each one, tell me if it's basically a platitude, if it's genuinely contentious, and if it's false — and crucially, whether that particular reading actually blocks the realist who wants to say that the predictive success of science is best explained by our theories being approximately true. I also want to know which of these is the reading people usually have in mind when they drop that line to end an argument, and whether any of them are compatible with still accepting that truth plays a role in inductive reasoning. I need this sorted out before I present on deflationism next week, and I'd rather not just parrot the slogan back at them.
