Write an essay on whether historians can escape the standards of their own time. Focus on the distinction between evaluative presentism—judging past agents by contemporary moral norms—and cognitive presentism—classifying past beliefs and practices through conceptual categories available only in the present. Examine the claim that cognitive presentism is unavoidable because explanation necessarily draws on the explainer's conceptual repertoire, and weigh it against the proposal that historians can perform a "register shift": adopting past evaluative and classificatory schemes for explanatory reconstruction without endorsing them. Address the instability objection, which holds that register shift either collapses into genuine adoption (with its attendant moral and epistemic risks) or remains a presentist simulation disguised as recovery. Reach a justified conclusion about whether meaningful escape from present standards is possible, and state what this implies for the objectivity of historiography. Finally, identify the weakest point in your reasoning and specify exactly what argument or evidence would lead you to reverse your verdict.
