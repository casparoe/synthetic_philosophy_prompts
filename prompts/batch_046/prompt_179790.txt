Since I began supporting a close family member who relies on an augmentative and alternative communication device rather than spoken language, I have been reconsidering the boundary between Popperian and Gregorian minds in Dennett's taxonomy. As I understand the distinction, a Popperian creature can generate and evaluate hypotheses internally without executing every behavioral variant, while a Gregorian creature augments its cognition through culturally transmitted tools—particularly language—that allow it to manipulate representations developed by others. What I want to understand is whether Gregorian status requires language to function as an internal representational medium, or whether it can be satisfied functionally by any substrate—technological, gestural, or symbolic—that enables the same kind of cultural transmission and representational offloading. Please compare the conceptual architecture of Popperian and Gregorian cognition with this substrate-independence question in view. Specifically, address whether the distinction collapses when a mind performs Gregorian-style cultural learning through a non-vocal technological scaffold, and what this comparison implies for assessing cognitive equality across communicative modalities that do not involve natural language.
