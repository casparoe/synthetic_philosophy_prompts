I've been covering neuroscience and philosophy for years, and lately I've been stuck on this analogy everyone reaches for with split-brain patients: people say it's like two captains in one ship with the radio line cut. The idea is that the corpus callosum is just a communication cable, so once it's severed you've got two independant minds steering the same body. It's used constantly to argue that consciousness is modular and that personal identity isn't really tied to the whole brain — more like two roommates who share a kitchen but never talk.

Can you audit this? Give me the mapping — captains, ship, radio, cargo, the harbor — and then show me where it collapses. For one thing, is the left hemisphere actually an agent with intentions, or is it closer to a subroutine that lost its API access? Does the analogy quietly assume unity is just information-sharing, and ignore deeper integration? I'm also wondering how it interacts with the access-consciousness versus phenomenal-consciousness split: does the analogy treat both sides as having full inner experience with divided access, or does it conflate the two? Does the 'two people' conclusion survive once these disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument? And if you think it's broken, what's a better analogy — maybe something like a distributed system with a partitioned database?
