Next month I present to an epistemology seminar my view that Clifford's shipowner parable collapses once belief is decomposed into doxastic assent (involuntary) and doxastic investment (the voluntary allocation of inquiry resources under evidential symmetry). The audience includes two Cliffordians who treat belief as voluntary action, a James scholar committed to the forced/living/momentous taxonomy, and philosophers of mind who deny doxastic voluntarism entirely. Give me exactly five anticipated objections in the order they will come, phrased as each faction would phrase them, each paired concisely with what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest point in my reply; present them as a numbered list 1 through 5, each entry only a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
