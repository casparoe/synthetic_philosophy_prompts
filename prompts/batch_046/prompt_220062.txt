A colleague recently summarized the ontological debate about generational cohorts as follows: generations such as Boomers, Millennials, and Gen Z are real kinds in precisely the same sense as biological species; each possesses an essence fixed by its formative technological environment, so membership is binary and explains behavior more reliably than age or socioeconomic class. The Pew Research Center cutoff years therefore discover natural boundaries rather than construct conventions. It follows that social policy should treat inter-generational conflict as a clash of incompatible natures rather than a distributive dispute within a shared culture, and that slogans like OK Boomer express literal kind-predication rather than rhetorical blame. I think this overstates the ontological commitment of serious cohort research, but I am unsure exactly where the missteps lie. Which parts of this summary fairly represent the strongest real-kind defense of generational ontology, which parts illegitimately import essentialism, technological determinism, or a misreading of demographic conventions as natural discoveries, and does the normative conclusion about policy and rhetoric survive once those philosophical errors are corrected?
