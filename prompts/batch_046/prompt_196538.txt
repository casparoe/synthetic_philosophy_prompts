I'm trying to figure out where "I" am in various brain-transplant and copy scenarios, and I want to see if there's a principled story behind my gut reactions. In Dennett's classic brain-transplant case where my brain gets put into a new body, I think I'm clearly the brain-person, not the body left behind. If my brain is split and each half goes into a different body, I think neither is fully me, but I also feel both have some claim — maybe I'm branching? If I'm gradualy replaced neuron-by-neuron with silicon chips but the old neurons are kept alive in a vat and start thinking they're me too, I still feel I'm the silicon-body one, though the vat-neurons seem to have a case too. If I'm scanned and uploaded into a computer that runs perfectly, I think that's probably not me, just a copy, unless the upload happens while my brain is destroyed in a way that preserves causal continiuty. And if two uploads are made and then merged into one consciousness, I don't know whether that's me dying or me becoming larger. Is there one account of personal identity — psychological continuity, bodily continuity, first-person access, or maybe a hybrid — that gets all these? Or do I have to drop one of my verdicts? Also, what does your principle say about a case I haven't mentioned: my brain is replaced with an artificial one that copies my memories but also adds a second layer of consciousness that experiences the world in paralel?
