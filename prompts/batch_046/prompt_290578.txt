In defenses of Gödel's modal ontological argument, proponents often invoke an analogy with modal completeness: the collection of "positive properties" is treated like the axioms of a formal system; consistency of that collection (together with necessary existence) is shown; and then the S5 frame conditions are said to guarantee that any consistent modal description is satisfied in some world, which—because the property includes necessary existence—collapses into actual instantiation. The analogy is used to counter the objection that the argument merely defines God into being, by claiming the inference is as legitimate as deriving model existence from consistency in logic. Give the explicit correspondence table—what corresponds to the axioms, the consistency proof, the model, the accessibility relation, and the actual world—then pinpoint the fractures: whether "positive properties" are stipulated like axioms or discovered like theorems, whether the consistency claim is established without already assuming an instance, whether logical satisfiability carries the same ontological weight as metaphysical instantiation, and whether the S5 collapse from "some world" to "this world" imports modal machinery that does no independent work. Conclude by saying whether the analogy genuinely deflects the "definition into existence" charge, or whether it quietly transfers logical semantics into ontology and thereby carries the conclusion it was meant to support.
