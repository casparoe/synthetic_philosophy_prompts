In a philosophy-of-science seminar, a fellow student summarized Karl Popper's view of evolutionary theory in the following way. Popper began by attacking Darwinism as tautological: the principle usually rendered as survival of the fittest defines fitness through survival itself, so that whatever lives is declared fit by definition, and the theory can never be contradicted by observed extinctions or failures of adaptation. Although Popper later conceded that this particular circularity charge was mistaken—since fitness can be specified independently through structural and physiological traits—he never abandoned the deeper judgment that natural selection is not a genuine scientific theory in the strict falsificationist sense. Instead, he treated it as a metaphysical research program: a broad historical framework that organizes biological knowledge but is itself shielded from empirical risk, because any observed outcome can be accommodated after the fact by constructing a suitable adaptive narrative. On this reading, Popper ultimately assigned Darwinism the same epistemic status he assigned to psychoanalysis and to historicist Marxism, namely that of a pre-scientific worldview that stimulates hypotheses without being falsifiable in its core, and he implied that mature biology should eventually transcend its dependence on selectionist explanation.

I am unsure how much of this captures Popper's actual position and how much overstates his skepticism. Could you audit the summary? Specifically: (1) Does Popper's mature work genuinely preserve the judgment that evolutionary theory is empirically shielded in its core, or did he distinguish a metaphysical framework of natural selection from genuinely testable predictions about adaptation and selection? (2) Is it fair to group Darwinism with psychoanalysis as equally lacking scientific standing, or did Popper assign them different epistemic roles despite applying similar language about metaphysical research programs? (3) Where does the summary conflate Popper's methodological criticisms of particular evolutionary explanations—untestable just-so stories—with a global dismissal of the research program itself? And (4) does the summary's conclusion that Popper regarded Darwinism as pre-scientific speculation follow from his texts, or does it misread his claim that the framework is metaphysical yet scientifically fruitful?
