Design a 75-minute classroom activity for an upper-level philosophy seminar of about 14 students that makes participants experience the tension in Plato's view—especially in Laws I and the Symposium—between regulated intoxication as an epistemic test that strips away rhetorical artifice to reveal true character, and the danger that what is revealed is mere disorder requiring magistrate interpretation rather than endorsement. The deliverable should be a structured simulation, not a lecture or standard debate. Divide students into groups of four or five with assigned roles: citizens proposing a civic policy, a magistrate-observer who records but does not vote, and a physician of the soul who may ask clarifying questions. Use the same policy case across two phases. In the sober phase, restrict communication to formal argument without personal disclosure; the group produces a rational recommendation. In the symposium phase, each citizen draws one pharmakon card that imposes a communicative constraint—such as forbidding abstract nouns, requiring a personal anecdote, or forcing defense of the opposite position—simulating the stripping away of techne. The group then revises or reaffirms its recommendation. After both phases, the full seminar votes collectively—by publicly signing a joint verdict or allocating symbolic city honors—on whether to keep the sober recommendation or adopt the symposium-phase position, without knowing in advance which outcome Plato's magistrate figure should privilege. Include a payoff scheme tied to social commitment rather than money. In the debrief, map the sober phase onto rational techne, the cards onto removal of rhetorical mask, and the magistrate's notes onto Plato's claim that revealed disorder is politically useful only if interpreted. Predict what will go wrong—students treating the symposium phase as pure performance, or the magistrate dominating interpretation—and advise the facilitator to withhold normative judgment until after the vote and to restrict the magistrate to descriptive recording during play. Be explicit that the activity cannot reproduce the metaphysical claim, central to the Laws, about wine literally altering the soul's substance like heated metal, and that you as facilitator must supply that image in the debrief. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or bibliographic references.
