As a retired biologist who has only recently begun working through philosophy of science, I have tried to set down my own commitments on explanation and reduction for a small discussion group, and I want an internal consistency check before I defend them. My profile is as follows: (1) I am a greedy reductionist — every biological phenomenon is in principle fully explicable by chemistry and physics alone, with no ontological remainder. (2) I accept Daniel Dennett's distinction between cranes and skyhooks and hold that all legitimate scientific explanations are cranes: cumulative, bottom-up, with no mysterious downward causation or top-down design injection. (3) I think evolutionary adaptation produces genuinely functional structures — immune defenses, sensory apparatus — whose organizational achievement is not merely heuristic but describes real, nature-built complexity. (4) I also hold that these organizational achievements possess causal powers their chemical parts lack individually, such as self-maintaining homeostasis across environments. (5) I maintain that invoking organizational principles not derivable from the parts alone smuggles in a conceptual skyhook, however naturalistically it is dressed. (6) I believe all teleological language in biology is entirely dispensable in principle, since evolutionary history provides a complete crane-based substitute, though I concede it is practically indispensable. (7) Methodologically, I think we ought always to prefer the lowest-level available explanation, even when a higher-level organizational account is more predictively powerful, because only the lower-level account avoids implicit skyhook commitments.

Please audit these seven commitments: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts dissolve upon inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, state what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then give your bottom-line verdict: which single revision — weakening or abandoning one specific commitment — cleans up the most conflicts? Lead with that verdict in one sentence. Present the audit in order of severity, from worst to least. Keep the response under 350 words; do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
