Here's an argument I sketched for a seminar on the temporal structure of perceptual consciousness, and I'm now suspicious of it because it seems to rule out every available explanation of the color phi phenomenon. The steps are numbered.

(1) In color phi, subjects presented with a red dot at position A followed by a green dot at position B report seeing a single dot change color from red to green as it traverses the intermediate positions.

(2) Because the report occurs only after the green dot at B has been presented, the intermediate experience—greenness, motion, particular spatial locations between A and B—must be either generated before B appears (predictive) or constructed only after B appears (postdictive).

(3) If predictive: the visual system produces specific qualitative content without any corresponding external stimulus being present at that time. By parity, this means that in ordinary apparent-motion perception, and indeed in any case where the brain fills in continuity across discrete stimuli, conscious experience systematically contains determinate qualities that are not causally tied to concurrent stimuli. The result is that hallucination becomes the default mode of perception rather than a deviation from it.

(4) If postdictive: we face the distinction between Orwellian and Stalinesque revisions. Either the intermediate experience genuinely occurred during the interval between A and B and was subsequently overwritten or erased in memory (Orwellian), or it never occurred during that interval and was instead formed at the later time with false temporal indexing, so that it only seems to have been present then (Stalinesque).

(5) The Orwellian option implies that conscious experience is constantly being revised after the fact. What we access in introspection and report is not the original experiential content but a reconstructed replacement, which means first-person reports are systematically unreliable about what was actually experienced.

(6) The Stalinesque option implies that consciousness routinely misattributes its own contents to incorrect temporal locations. The "when" of an experience, as given in awareness, is not a trustworthy indicator of when that experience actually occurred.

(7) Both postdictive options generalize beyond color phi: any perceptual construction—filling in the blind spot, apparent motion, the continuity of awareness across saccades—would involve either erased original contents or false temporal tagging.

(8) Therefore, either prediction is correct and perception is pervasively hallucinatory, or postdiction is correct and consciousness is either constantly revised or systematically mislocated in time.

(9) But we have strong reason to reject pervasive hallucination as the norm, and we treat first-person temporal reports and introspective access as generally trustworthy.

(10) So every available account of color phi—predictive, Orwellian-postdictive, or Stalinesque-postdictive—accepts an absurd consequence. Hence the phenomenon cannot be accommodated within any coherent theory of conscious experience.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? I would prefer to keep (1) through (3) intact, since the predictive-parity point seems genuinely troubling, but I'm open to giving up (7) if the generalization is illegitimate. Is there a hidden equivocation between "generated without concurrent stimulus" and "hallucinatory," or is the mistake in assuming that postdictive construction must apply uniformly across all perceptual filling-in?
