Write a dialogue between Sam and Riley, two equally sharp archaeologists, about whether broken objects in the ground can ever justify claiming a shared belief or institution. Sam pushes a "double redundancy" rule: you only infer mental or institutional content if the same anomalous pattern appears in two independant material tracks—say, pottery and lithics—and neither ecology nor taphonomy can cover it alone. Riley rejects that as circular labeling and argues for "affordance tracking" instead: map what actions objects enabled, stay silent about mental states. Have them actually clash over one concrete hypothetical site at high altitude where grinding stones are buried beneath house floors but never in middens, and where decorative ceramic motifs suddenly vanish. Sam reads it as a ritual discontinuation of domestic space; Riley reads it as seasonal abandonment plus a depleted local clay source. Each needs to answer the other's specific objection—Sam should explain why ecological explanations fail his redundancy check in this case, and Riley should explain why adding "ritual discontinuation" gives no extra predictive leverage over the materialist story. Keep it casual, no strawmen, and let them talk past each other as little as possible. You can misspell "independent" and drop an apostrophe in "each others" if it feels natural.
