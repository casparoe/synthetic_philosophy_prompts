I've been trying to understand the distinction between cranes and skyhooks in explanations — the idea that legitimate explanations build upward from simpler mechanisms without mysterious outside help, while skyhooks invoke something that is not itself accounted for. I keep encountering the phrase that there are no skyhooks, only cranes, usually invoked to defend greedy reductionism. But I have noticed two puzzling cases that seem to blur the boundary. First, evolutionary accounts of human cooperation sometimes feel circular: cooperation evolves because it benefits groups, yet recognizing those benefits seems to require the very cooperative cognition being explained. Second, reductionist explanations of consciousness describe subjective experience in terms of neural dynamics, yet the evidence for those dynamics comes from conscious subjects reporting their states — so the explanation appears to borrow from the phenomenon it aims to reduce. When people use the no-skyhooks slogan to dismiss these circularity worries, what are the different precise claims they could be making? Please lay them out as a menu: the trivial reading (no miraculous interventions), the methodological preference for bottom-up mechanisms, the strong metaphysical claim that no top-down causation is ever real, and any intermediate positions. For each, say whether it actually resolves the two circular worries I described, whether it is defensible on its own, and which version a proponent of greedy reductionism most likely intends. I am putting this together for a non-specialist discussion group, so I want the distinctions sharp enough to bring directly to the table.
