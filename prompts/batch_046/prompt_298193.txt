and heres my classmate's answer (they wrote it fast):

Mill spent like 35 years working for the East India Company, so he knew India well. Even though he wrote that famous essay on liberty and believed in individual freedom, he said Indian society was stuck at a lower stage of civilization and not ready for democracy. He supported what people call benevolent despotism — British rulers should govern firmly but also educate and reform India until the people are ready to rule themselves. But Mill also basically thought Indians could never fully catch up to Europeans, since cultural backwardness was mostly a permanent thing. He got a lot of this from his dad James Mill, who wrote a well-known history of India arguing that Indian culture contributed almost nothing useful to science or goverment, and John Stuart pretty much agreed with that dismissive view. Also, Mill's real reason for supporting colonialism was economic — protecting British trade — and the moral talk about helping India was mostly cover.
