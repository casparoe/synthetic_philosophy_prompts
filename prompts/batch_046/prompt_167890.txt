In our history-of-science seminar we've been stuck for months on whether the Middle Ages, Renaissance, and Scientific Revolution are actual periods or just later inventions that historians projected backwards. Same chronology, same archives, zero progress. My working theory is it's not about evidence but about ontology identification: one side thinks a period is real only if there's a causal cluster that members could recognize, but since we can't acces the mental categories of people in 1500, we can't identify the period that way; the other side says that's relevent, a period exists if it explains patterns we see now, regardless of internal access. So is the stall because we're mixing up a substantive disagreement about historical ontology with a verbal confusion about what "real" means? Or is there a hidden empirical question—like, if we found a 15th-century text where someone calls themselves a "Renaissance man," would that actually settle anything? I want a diagnosis: is this verbal, substantive, about standards of evidence, or bedrock intuition? And what kind of distinction or finding would actually move it, if any?
