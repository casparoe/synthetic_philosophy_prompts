On Shapiro's view, structures exist in re: they are present only in their concrete instantiations, whether in physical arrays or in mental representations. Through these instantiations, we gain causal access to structures, so the instantiation bridge dissolves Benacerraf's challenge rather than merely relocating it. Field's fictionalism, by contrast, denies that mathematical objects exist at all, treating mathematics as a useful fiction. His conservativeness argument shows that any adequate nominalistic physical theory can be extended with mathematics without yielding new physical predictions, which establishes that mathematics is dispensable for science.
