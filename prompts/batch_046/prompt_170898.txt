I am a legislative reform consultant advising a regional federation on redesigning selection for a 120-member assembly. The principle I need operationalized is one I will call the exposure-rotation constraint: democratic legitimacy requires that every citizen have a structurally guaranteed opportunity for direct deliberative exposure through serving in the assembly, while being insulated from career incentives, yet these two goods conflict under both pure election and pure sortition. Turn this into a reusable decision procedure—an ordered checklist with explicit pass/fail tests—that I can run on any proposed selection rule (elected, sortition, hybrid, or term-limited) to assess whether it satisfies the constraint. The procedure must resolve hard cases: random selection that yields insufficient policy expertise; term limits that open a lobbying revolving door; and hybrid systems that risk generating a recurring class of professional citizens. For each step, indicate where judgment is irreducible and cannot be eliminated by the rule itself. Then apply the procedure to three mechanisms: (1) election with eight-year term limits and no post-service lobbying restriction; (2) pure sortition with three-year single terms and fifteen-year ineligibility for reselection; and (3) a hybrid of sixty elected seats with six-year terms and sixty lot-drawn seats with two-year terms, with a bar on serving in both tracks. Include one case where the procedure produces a result the federation will find undesirable, and state clearly what the procedure cannot adjudicate—for instance, whether the constraint should yield to party coherence or to economic efficiency.
