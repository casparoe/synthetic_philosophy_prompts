Student answer:

"(a) Cranes are bottom-up mechanisms that build complex things step by step, like evolution constructing the eye without jumps. Skyhooks are mysterious top-down forces that hang explanations out of nowhere, like vitalism or Cartesian dualism. Greedy reductionism is Dennett's term for the correct kind of reductionism that uses cranes to break higher-level phenomena into their neural parts without leaving explanatory gaps.

(b) Predictive processing explains consciousness through the brain minimizing prediction error across interoceptive and exteroceptive streams. This is a classic crane: self-awareness is built up from basic computational steps with no magical leap. The free-energy principle just formalizes this crane. Some critics say it acts like a skyhook because it seems to presuppose an integrated self in the very mechanism meant to explain it, but that objection confuses the model with what it models — the principle itself is purely mechanistic. Because this is a crane-built explanation, greedy reductionism is exactly the right tool: it lets us eliminate all talk about subjective experience and replace it with talk about precision-weighted prediction errors, since first-person reports are just noisy byproducts of the underlying neural dynamics and have no independent evidential value.
