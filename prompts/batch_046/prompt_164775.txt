I'm in the middle of revising my second novel and I've hit a wall with my editor over the ending. The protagonist, Mara, spends the whole book making what she thinks are independant choices—career, who to marry, whether to forgive her sister—but in the final chapter she finds out her parents had been using this old behavioral-nudge app since she was a kid. Every major decision was gently steered: the app didn't force her, just made some options feel easier and others feel wierd. She still feels like they were her choices, and she doesn't want the app turned off for other families. My editor says this makes her a puppet and the ending is nihilistic, but I think there's a version where she's still genuinely free in some important sense, not just "whatever feels right to you."

Is there actually a philosophically solid middle ground here—where freedom doesn't require being untouched by outside influence, but also isn't just "whatever feels right"? I keep thinking about those old debates about whether free will has to be this deep, soul-level thing, or whether it can be something more practical—like being able to reflect on your reasons and not being coerced. But I'm not sure how to map that onto Mara's situation without making it sound like I'm just making excuses for manipulation. What's the real distinction I should be writing toward, and is there a concrete test I could use to check whether a given version of her choice actually counts as free?
