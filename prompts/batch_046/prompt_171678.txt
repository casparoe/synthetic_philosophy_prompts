Rather than sorting students by author or school, I want a decision tree that begins with the concrete commitment "There is a reason to φ even if no actual or possible being could ever grasp or respond to it," then branches through whether the absence of agents is permanent or merely contingent, whether the ground is objective value, counterfactual agent-responsiveness, or purely structural relations among normative states, and whether an artificial normative architecture that entirely lacks consciousness could still instantiate such a reason. At each leaf, name the position by its logical profile—such as full structural realism, weak potential-agent dependence, strong agent-dependence, or error-theoretic denial—and state its single most awkward commitment in one sentence; also identify any combination of answers that seems unoccupied and briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Please present the taxonomy as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences describing a successive branch, its corresponding leaf commitments, and any unoccupied nodes, with no introduction or conclusion. Be explicit if any two leaves cannot be genuinely separated by the questions asked, and flag any pair your tree cannot distinguish. If the very idea of classifying reasons without reasoners rests on a category mistake—treating normative force as something that can float free of reasoning capacities—say so plainly rather than constructing the tree politely.
