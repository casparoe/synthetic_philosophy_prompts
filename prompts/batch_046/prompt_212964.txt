(3) I think that a personalized, algorithmically adaptive tool—specifically, a language-model interface that has been fine-tuned on my prior patterns of reasoning and that I invoke automatically during deliberation—can become part of my extended cognitive architecture, not merely an instrument I use.

(4) I maintain that generic, publicly available resources—such as an unpersonalized search engine or a standard reference database—can never be part of my cognition, no matter how frequently or automatically I consult them, because they lack the requisite individual-level coupling.

(5) I deny that external storage (for instance, a notebook in which I record beliefs) counts as extended belief or as part of cognition; storage is a passive repository, whereas I accept extension only for active reasoning and decision-making processes.

(6) I reject brain-bound cognition on anti-substrate-chauvinist grounds: biological tissue has no metaphysically privileged status, and the boundary of mind should be fixed by functional dynamics rather than by cranial location.

Order the real tensions by severity. Then tell me which single revision, if I were forced to make only one, would clean up the most inconsistencies across the package.
