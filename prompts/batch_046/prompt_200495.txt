Write a one-page research proposal for a four-month independent philosophical study on Descartes's cogito. State the precise question: does the argument establish the existence of a substantial thinking thing, or does it establish only that a present-tense act of thinking is occurring, with any substantial commitment depending on an implicit and unexamined premise about the identity conditions of mental events? Identify the gap in existing treatments not as the familiar Cartesian-circle objection, but as the neglected indexical reading: the possibility that Descartes uses 'I' as a token-reflexive expression whose reference is fixed by the very occurrence of the thought, which would allow the inference to existence without licensing the further claim that this 'I' is a non-extended substance enduring through time. Explain why a conceptual-reconstruction approach—working only with premises Descartes explicitly licenses in the opening moves of his first-person project—advances the debate more effectively than a broad historical survey. Specify what a successful deliverable looks like: either a reconstructed argument that validates the substantialist reading without smuggling in later Cartesian commitments, or a demonstration that the substantialist conclusion requires premises unavailable at that stage. Define clear failure conditions, such as a reconstruction that must appeal to later doctrines about mind-body distinction. Keep the scope realistic for one semester with no archival or empirical component, and do not request a bibliography, citation list, or further reading; you may refer to general positions, but indicate that specific references will be added separately. Please present the proposal in British English.
