I'm a novelist — not a philosopher, just someone who reads too much — and I've been roped into giving the opening remarks at our local arts centre winter gathering. The room's split: half are writers and editors tearing each other apart over whether to keep a controversial contributor in next year's anthology, the other half are neighbours and funders who came for wine and don't want a lecture. I want the talk to hang on Popper's paradox of tolerance, but not the usual democracy-defence version. It needs to do real structural work — show why our tiny editorial board can't claim neutrality without basically dissolving the reason we exist, and why saying 'we tolerate all views' is itself a choice that excludes the people we're meant to protect. Keep it under five minutes, casual enough I don't sound like I'm reading a thesis, and don't load it with references or a reading list — I won't check them and half the room wouldn't care.
