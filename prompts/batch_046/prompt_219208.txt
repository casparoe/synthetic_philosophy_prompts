(1) the usual rationalist take is that wireheading is bad specifically because it decouples reward from actually making the world better -- you get the dopamine hit without the causal connection to achievement. (2) so a reward is only "legitimate" if it's causally downstream of the right kind of world-state change that the agent brought about. (3) but in a deterministic physicalist picture, my feeling of satisfaction after i finish writing a paper is just neurons firing that were going to fire anyway, given the prior physical state. there's no special "normative causation" that makes this different from an electrode stimulating the same neurons; in both cases the satisfaction is the deterministic result of prior physical processes, not of some irreducible "earning" relation. (4) therefore either determinism makes all satisfaction wireheading-like, or we need some non-physical relation (maybe a normative one) that distinguishes "earned" reward from "hacked" reward. (5) but that sounds like dualism or at least some spooky metaphysics that doesn't fit with a materialist worldview. (6) so it looks like either determinism is false, or wireheading isn't actually bad in the way we think.
