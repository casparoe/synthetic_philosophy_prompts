Write a branching taxonomy — eight leaves, each reached through four binary nodes — that sorts substantive interpretations of Von Neumann’s architectural comparison in The Computer and the Brain (the unfinished work drawn from his 1956 Silliman Lectures) on the question: is the brain fundamentally digital, analog, or hybrid?

Build the tree from logical commitments, not from a list of author names. Each branch node must be phrased as a concrete claim about neural computation or reliability that a reader could answer yes or no without already knowing which interpretation it yields. The four nodes must be:

1. At the functional level, are neural signals all-or-none discrete states or continuously graded?
2. With unreliable components, does the brain achieve reliability through digital error-correction or through statistical noise-absorption? (Inject the non-standard distinction between “noise-correction digitalism” and “noise-absorption analogism” here; both paths must appear in the tree.)
3. Does Von Neumann’s model of self-reproducing automata apply literally to brain development, only metaphorically, or not at all?
4. Is the brain’s “language” reducible to formal mathematical logic, does it require a different logic, or is it non-logical?

The eight leaves must include exactly five named positions that have actually been defended in philosophy of mind or neuroscience (for instance: hard digitalism, continuous analog dynamism, hybrid substrate-functionalism, statistical-reliability analogism, and heuristic-metaphorical comparativism). The remaining three leaves must be unoccupied combinations of answers; for each, state in one sentence whether the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and why.

For every leaf — occupied or not — provide the position name (or “unnamed”) and one sentence describing its most awkward commitment: the claim it must defend that sounds strangest against ordinary intuition. Then flag explicitly any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining in one sentence why the distinction collapses (for example, because both commit to the same answer on all four nodes).

The audience is a graduate seminar in philosophy of mind. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or web-accessible verification. Do not quote Von Neumann verbatim; paraphrase any specific claims about neurons, logic, or self-reproduction. Make sure the taxonomy can be constructed and evaluated from general knowledge of the brain-computer analogy and Von Neumann’s well-known arguments about component unreliability and logical language.
