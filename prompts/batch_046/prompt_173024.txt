A philosophy grad student was suspended for plagiarism—he copied big chunks of a literature review verbatim and doesn't deny it happened. But in his appeal he argues that since he sincerely holds al-Ghazali's occasionalist view—where God is the only true cause and human actions are just 'occasions' (asab)—our integrity policy is built on an implicit Aristotelian theory of human causal agency that he rejects. He wants us to overturn the suspension or, if we don't, issue a formal note saying our rules presuppose a metaphysics of causation we haven't defended. My co-chair says it's an obvious stunt and wants to reject flat, but another member thinks there's a real tension: if occasionalism is true, can you still hold someone 'responsible' in the causal sense that sanctions require? We need a board memo with an actual bottom line—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—plus reasoning that addresses his argument head-on rather than dismissing it, and an honest account of the best reason he might be wrong. I especially want you to think about whether al-Ghazali's own distinction between true causation and customary causation ('ada) saves enough agency to justify institutional rules, or if that distinction collapses once you take occasionalism seriously. Don't just summarize texts; I need something concrete the board can actually vote on, and please treat the strongest point on the losing side fairly.
