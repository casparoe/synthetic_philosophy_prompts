Please explain the conceptual tension between phenomenal bonding and what we might call the reciprocity regress in panpsychist theories of consciousness. Begin with your bottom-line conclusion in one sentence, then develop the reasoning. The standard combination problem asks how discrete micro-subjects of experience combine into a single, unified macro-subject. One proposed resolution is phenomenal bonding: micro-experiences enter relations analogous to chemical bonding, producing a genuinely composite consciousness rather than a mere aggregate. Now consider the reciprocity constraint: for bonding to yield true unity rather than co-location, each micro-subject must have some form of phenomenal access to the others within the composite; yet if that access-relation is itself experiential, it seems to require further subjects to relate the relata, generating an infinite hierarchy of meta-experiences. Conversely, if no such reciprocal access is required, the micro-subjects remain phenomenally isolated and the macro-level is only an aggregation, leaving the original problem unsolved. In no more than 250 words, give an intuitive explanation of why this tension forces phenomenal bonding to collapse either into an infinite regress or into a denial of genuine macro-level unity. Do not cite authors, texts, or page numbers; derive the argument from the conceptual structure alone.
