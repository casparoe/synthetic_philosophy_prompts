You sit on a state sentencing review board overseeing a pilot program for serious juvenile offenses. The program assigns offenders to rehabilitative or custodial tracks using an algorithmic risk-assessment tool that currently includes a "moral culpability weight" derived from neuropsychological evaluation of practical reasoning capacity at the time of the offense. A proposal before Thursday's vote would eliminate that weight entirely, leaving only predictive-risk and treatment-amenability variables.

Proponents argue, in line with Gregg Caruso's skepticism about libertarian free will, that neuroscience undermines the moral desert required for retributive distinctions; they favor a public-health quarantine model in which detention is justified solely by dangerousness and treatability, not by blame. Opponents, drawing on Daniel Dennett's compatibilist naturalism, contend that moral responsibility survives determinism, that stripping desert collapses the normative distinction between punishment and mere social control, and that retaining some culpability-sensitive weighting is essential to the legitimacy of custodial sanctions.

Draft the board's formal decision memo—approve the proposal, reject it, or approve it with specific conditions—for circulation before the vote. Recommend a concrete course of action with an implementation timeline (e.g., phased removal, pilot retention with audit conditions, or immediate adoption with safeguards). The memo must treat the Caruso-style skeptical position seriously rather than simply asserting that offenders "choose" freely, and it must explain whether and why the Dennett-style defense of desert succeeds or fails in this particular institutional context. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; build the reasoning directly from the philosophical positions and the practical design of the assessment tool.
