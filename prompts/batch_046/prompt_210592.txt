Write the opening remarks (about six minutes) for an interdisciplinary workshop titled "Counterfactuals We Can't Ground," held to honor a retiring philosopher of religion. The audience is split: half are specialists in philosophical theology who have argued for decades about Molina's scientia media, counterfactuals of creaturely freedom, and the grounding objection, and they will hear any imprecision in the distinction between natural, middle, and free knowledge; the other half are the honoree's family, law students, and computer scientists working on causal inference who have never encountered the term "middle knowledge" and will tune out if you sound like you're delivering a lecture. The concept of Molinism must do real structural work in the speech: frame the workshop's central tension—knowing what a free agent would do without thereby causing it—as structurally parallel to the problem of predictive modeling that intervenes on its own predictions, but do not reduce the theological problem to a technological one or vice versa. Because of this structure, you must be explicit about what the workshop can and cannot hope to settle: you can map the logical space of counterfactual knowledge, but you cannot, within these proceedings, resolve whether such knowledge is compatible with libertarian freedom. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and do not quote any specific text verbatim. To keep the remarks from being a generic summary, inject the following somewhat niche framing: present Molina's three categories of divine knowledge—natural, middle, and free—as analogues to three epistemic postures an observer can take toward a branching process: observer-independent (what must happen given the laws), observer-sensitive-but-non-interventionist (what would happen if conditions were set but not forced), and observer-constitutive (what happens because the observer wills it). Use this triad to organize the day's panels without claiming it is standard Molinist exegesis. The tone should be affectionate and occasion-specific, landing for the experts without losing everyone else, and it should close by stating the one question everyone in the room—regardless of discipline—should leave holding but not answering.
