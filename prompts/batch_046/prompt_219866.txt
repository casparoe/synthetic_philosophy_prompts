Next thursday I'm presenting my draft chapter to our dept's virtue ethics reading group and I'm kinda nervouse, since two regulars are hardcore situationists who think character traits are basically a myth. My claim is that the standard situationist critique—think Milgram-style obedience or bystander-effect studies—doesn't actually kill virtue ethics if we stop treating virtues as global behavioral dispositions and instead see them as second-order regulatory capacities. So practical wisdom isn't being consistently generous in every single context; it's being consistently good at noticing when generosity is called for and suppressing competing impulses before they take over. 

The audience is three people who know the empirical lit well (they'll mention Doris and Harman), two Kantians who think I'm just smuggling deontology in through the back door, and one grad student writing on moral luck. I need the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased like those people would actually say them, with what I should concede, what I should hold, and especially the weakest point in my own position that I should defend rather than hope they miss. This is only a 20-minute talk with 15 minutes of Q&A, so I need short replies, not essays—what's the objection I'm praying nobody asks?
