I am applying for a six-month conceptual-analysis fellowship and need a polished two-page proposal. My half-formed idea is that the long stalemate between naturalist accounts of health—treating it as statistically typical functional contribution to survival and reproduction—and normativist accounts—treating it as value-laden, goal-relative, or welfare-constituting—rests on a shared but rarely questioned assumption: that "health" denotes a single, scale-invariant property of an organism. I want to develop what I call "scale-relative functional profiling": the claim that health judgments are implicitly indexed to a specific biological scale (molecular, organismic, ecological, or even social), and that once we make the scale explicit, the naturalist/normativist opposition dissolves into a family of related but non-competing concepts rather than one correct theory. At the molecular scale, health looks like homeostatic or predictive-regulatory coherence (naturalist-friendly); at the organismic scale, it involves agent-relative goals and capacities (normativist-friendly); at the ecological scale, it concerns resilience and interdependent function, which is neither purely descriptive nor purely normative. The project would formalize this framing, construct precise conditions for applying each scale-relative concept, show how it resolves standard counterexamples to both Boorse-style naturalism and Nordenfelt-style normativism without inventing an ad hoc hybrid theory, and identify exactly where genuine disagreement survives. Please turn this into a proposal containing: (1) a precise statement of the research question and the central thesis; (2) an explanation of why current positions leave this gap open—particularly their implicit commitment to scale-invariance and their tendency to treat counterexamples as fatal rather than scale-misapplied; (3) the method, broken down roughly month by month for six months, specifying what conceptual distinctions I need to build, what thought experiments or case constructions I will use to test scale-indexing, and why this conceptual-engineering approach is preferable to empirical clinical study or full-scale history of medicine at this stage; (4) what a successful result looks like—ideally a taxonomy of scale-relative health concepts with clear applicability conditions—and what counts as failure, such as if scale-indexing collapses back into one standard camp or proves redundant; and (5) a realistic scope statement acknowledging that there will be no data collection, no complete literature review with citations, and no clinical collaboration, with a note that references will be added later. Write in the voice of an applicant arguing that the work is philosophically significant, avoids false dichotomies, and is genuinely doable alone in six months.
