While filing a dispatch from a workshop on formal epistemology, I overheard two researchers arguing about the status of logical uncertainty inside the logical induction framework. The first claimed that because logical inductors converge exponentially on logical truths, logical uncertainty is not a genuine epistemic state but merely a temporary computational deficit; an agent with infinite resources would eliminate it entirely, which distinguishes it fundamentally from empirical uncertainty, which persists even for omniscient agents about the future. The second objected that convergence describes an algorithmic trajectory rather than the ontology of belief, and that even an ideal reasoner must assign intermediate credences to unproven mathematical conjectures, so logical uncertainty is structurally analogous to empirical uncertainty in requiring probabilistic management rather than binary knowledge. The first then pressed that if the two uncertainties were genuinely analogous, logical inductors ought to satisfy some version of the principal principle linking credence to objective chance, but since logical truths have no underlying stochastic process, the analogy collapses and logical uncertainty must be a distinct, resource-bound phenomenon. The second replied that demanding an objective-chance substrate imposes an unjustified physicalist model on reasoning; logical induction operates through coherence constraints and market mechanisms that serve the same functional role as empirical updating, and the absence of “chance” does not dissolve the epistemic parallel. They asked me to referee. Please adjudicate the exchange in exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: rate the first researcher’s opening claim, rate the second researcher’s reply, rate the principal-principle objection, rate the coherence-mechanism reply, and in the final point identify what both sides overlook about whether convergence properties define epistemic categories and state which position is more defensible overall.
