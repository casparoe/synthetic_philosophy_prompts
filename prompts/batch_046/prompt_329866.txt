Defend the idea that writing Whig history — treating the past as an inevitable march toward our present — isn't just sloppy thinking but does a backwards kind of moral harm we havn't really named. Call it retroactive paternalism: by framing past choices as only meaningful in light of outcomes they couldn't forsee, we treat dead agents like instruments in our origin story. Make the case that this applies even to small-scale histories — how we tell the story of a local craft dying out, or a failed scientific instrument — not just grand political narratives. You need to show why adding "context" doesn't fix the problem, and why genuinely respecting past autonomy means accepting their projects could have suceeded on their own terms, not just served as stepping stones. Also tackle the obvious objection that all history is selective, so some progress-narrative is unavoidable, and say exactly where that misses the point.
