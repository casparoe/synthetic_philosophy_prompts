I serve on the Preservation and Institutional Memory Board of the Museum of Applied Technology. By March 15 we must issue a formal decision on a proposal to deaccession and dismantle the museum’s sole intact 1983 Meridian Routing Core—the last physical node from a proprietary urban-data network adopted by three municipalities and abandoned after a 1987 regulatory pivot—and replace it with an interactive digital reconstruction synthesized from archival records of parallel, surviving networks in other regions. The proponents, a coalition of digital historians, contend that the network’s collapse was path-dependent lock-in triggered by the 1987 pivot, yet also that its intended function—distributed municipal data routing—counterfactually converged globally through later open standards, so preserving this specific failed artifact perpetuates a misleading technological determinism; they ask for full dismantling and installation of the digital substitute by June 30. The opponents, including our staff archivists and an external historian of technology, respond that path dependence is precisely what renders the failure trajectory irreplaceable, that claims of functional convergence smuggle in untestable counterfactual assumptions about what “would have arrived anyway,” and that destroying the only material trace of a non-convergent branch erases rather than interprets history.

Please draft the board’s decision memo recommending a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—including an explicit time frame and any enforceable conditions. The memo should treat the philosophical tension between path-dependent lock-in and counterfactual convergence as a genuine issue for the board, not merely invoke preservation policy. Give a serious, substantive account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. The document should read as an actionable institutional opinion with a clear bottom line, suitable for circulation before the vote, rather than as personal advisory guidance. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
