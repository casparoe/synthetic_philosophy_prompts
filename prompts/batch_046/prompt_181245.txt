Write a short story about a maintenence worker named Elio who lives inside a sealed thermodynamic computing cluster — basically a server farm that runs entirely on recaptured waste heat — and whose job is to act like a human Maxwell's demon. He doesn't delete data; he sorts packets between low-entropy crystal storage and high-entropy thermal pools, and over time he notices that every sorting decision makes his own memory of that decision a little fuzzier, like the cluster is charging him a Landauer-style tax for processing information. The story should sit with the question of whether his growing confusion is just physical fatigue, or whether playing the demon is literally dissipating his cognitive entropy into the system, and it should end without telling us if he ever actually chose anything or was just riding the thermal gradient all along. I want it grounded in the actual physics-of-information idea that erasing a bit carries a thermodynamic cost, but placed in a near-future world where people live inside heat-recycling data sanctuaries and treat entropy management like plumbing. Keep it quiet and intimate rather than flashy — I'm more interested in the dread of realizing your thoughts might be leaving the room as heat. Don't bother explaining the science like it's a textbook or giving me references; just let the concept live inside Elio's experience, maybe with him wondering if his forgotten childhood is now just part of the server's warm air.
