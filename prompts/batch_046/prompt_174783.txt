The Hart-Devlin debate on legal moralism has been going since the sixties and honestly it feels like everyone just restates the same moves: Hart says the law shouldn't enforce private morality unless there's harm, Devlin says a society needs shared moral bonds or it disintergrates, and then people cheer for whichever one matches their politics. But I'm suspicious the stall is structural. Hart seems to treat morality as something individuals critically reflect on, while Devlin treats it like social glue that exists prior to reflection. So are they actually fighting about the ontology of morality -- wether it's essentially critical/individual or collective/pre-reflective -- and only using law as the battlefield? Or is it verbal, since Devlin counts moral disgust and social instability as a kind of harm, and Hart just defines harm away? There's probably also an empirical piece about wether liberal societies actually fall apart, but neither side says what disintergration would look like, so no data can touch it. Give me a diagnosis: substantive disagreement, verbal confusion, two different questions (social ontology vs state coercian), or just unarguable intuitions? And tell me what, if anything, would count as progress -- a sociological result, a sharper distinction, or should we accept the question is ill-posed?
