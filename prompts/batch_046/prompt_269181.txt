In my graduate seminar on the philosophy of language we have been working through Kripkenstein's presentation of Wittgenstein's rule-following considerations, and one of my students introduced a worry about what I have begun to call the "calibration asymmetry" within skeptical solutions. The argument runs as follows. If the skeptical paradox genuinely eliminates all straight facts about whether an individual speaker means addition by "plus," and the skeptical solution replaces these with conditions of warranted assertability within a linguistic community, then the community's correction of deviant usage must itself be governed by some shared practice of applying the correction-criterion. Yet each individual member applies that criterion through her own past usage, which remains subject to the very same skeptical underdetermination. Consequently, it seems the community can correct the individual only if at least some members possess a non-skeptical, privately calibrated grasp of the rule—otherwise we face only an infinite regress of underdetermined correctors. Given this calibration asymmetry, is the skeptical solution not self-defeating? It appears to smuggle back in exactly the kind of fact-of-the-matter about meaning that the paradox was designed to dissolve, now relocated into the collective authority of the group rather than the individual mind. I find the reasoning seductive, but I suspect it imports a factualist conception of normativity into an account that was meant to be thoroughly practice-based.

Please address where, if anywhere, this line of thought missteps. In particular, clarify whether the skeptical solution genuinely requires that correctors enjoy a privileged first-person access to the rule being enforced, or whether the asymmetry between individual corrigibility and collective authority is actually a structural feature rather than a defect of the view. Your answer should be written in American English, should avoid any bibliographic recommendations or lists of further reading, and should explain whether the "calibration asymmetry" I have described represents a genuine internal tension in Kripkenstein's framework or rather a confusion produced by treating communal assent as if it rested on hidden metaphysical grounds.
