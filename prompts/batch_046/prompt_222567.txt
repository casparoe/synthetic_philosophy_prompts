I am drafting an adversarial-collaboration exercise for my philosophy seminar. Please write the joint statement that a defender of a Dennett-inspired compatibilist view—who maintains that deterministic neuroscience does not eliminate the form of agency that supports limited, backward-looking moral desert in punishment—and a defender of Caruso's free-will skepticism—who holds that determinism undermines all retributive desert and supports only a forward-looking quarantine model of social defense—could both endorse after good-faith negotiation.

Organize it as follows:

1. Numbered agreed claims: premises both accept about human agency, the empirical limits of rehabilitation, the dangers of algorithmic bias in risk-assessment tools, and the distinction between expressing moral condemnation and preventing harm.

2. A disagreements table: precise, neutrally worded formulations of where they still differ, each phrased so that its own side would accept it. Include at least (a) whether an agent can author an action in a normatively weighty sense under determinism, and (b) whether assigning non-carceral rehabilitative burdens through predictive risk algorithms can ever be deserved rather than merely prudent.

3. Cruxes: the two or three sub-questions such that resolving them would shift at least one camp. I take likely cruxes to be (i) whether the phenomenology of authorship carries independent normative weight or is only instrumentally valuable, and (ii) whether long-term recidivism data from purely preventive regimes can match the social-stability outcomes that desert-sensitive frameworks claim to secure.

4. For each crux, identify one concrete case or empirical pattern both sides agree is relevant—perhaps involving a proposed non-carceral Moral Repair Board that calibrates rehabilitative obligations via risk scores—and state each camp's explicit prediction about what that case or pattern demonstrates.

Keep the framing concrete with that institutional example, avoid strawmen, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; I need only the conceptual architecture both parties could sign.
