I am organizing the closing session for a graduate philosophy of mind reading group that has spent the semester on consciousness and sleep, and we have focused particularly on whether dreams are experiences. We have worked through Malcolm's verificationist challenge — the claim that because there is no criterion for checking mental occurrences during sleep, reports given upon awakening cannot be taken as descriptions of prior experiences — and through Dennett's response, which treats many dream narratives as reconstructed or confabulated at the moment of waking rather than as direct recordings of events that occurred in sleep. Rather than ending with expository summary, I want an exercise that makes the poem's form enact the conceptual structure of the disagreement itself.

Please write a sestina: six stanzas of six lines each, followed by a three-line envoi, using the standard rotation of these six end-words — verify, sleep, report, wake, construct, experience. The formal rotation should mirror the philosophical rotation between the two positions: in stanzas where verify and sleep are paired near the line-ends, the voice should carry Malcolm's skepticism about unobservable mental states; where construct and wake are paired, the voice should shift to Dennett's view that the dream narrative is assembled upon awakening. The envoi must bring all six words together in a single compressed image that refuses to settle whether the dream was genuinely experienced or merely constructed — leaving the tension structurally open, as the form cycles without reaching a final station. The poem should be precise enough that a specialist can identify which lines belong to Malcolm's verificationism and which to Dennett's narrative-cassette model, but it should not cite texts, offer dates, or provide a bibliography. Avoid resolving into either a defense of unconscious phenomenology or a reductive dismissal of dream reports as pure fiction. Aim for roughly forty lines, readable aloud, with each stanza maintaining grammatical coherence despite the repeating end-words.
