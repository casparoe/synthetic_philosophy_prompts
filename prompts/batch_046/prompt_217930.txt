I'm taking a graduate seminar on democratic erosion, and my colleagues keep using "backsliding" as a synonym for "policies I dislike." To push past that, I want you to rank the following five proposed diagnostic thresholds according to a single integrated standard: how precisely they distinguish democratic decay from ordinary constitutional hardball, and how resistant they are to being weaponized as partisan labels. Use a tier list from S (most robust and least weaponizable) to D (least), with one sentence per item explaining its placement.

The five thresholds are:
1. The "institutional layering" pattern, in which elected leaders construct parallel bodies—such as parallel courts or media oversight councils—that formally coexist with legacy institutions but hollow them out through budgetary starvation or jurisdictional overlap.
2. The erosion of "mutual toleration" measured behaviorally rather than rhetorically: specifically, governing parties refusing to share committee leadership, accept opposition victories in subnational races, or treat rivals as legitimate contestants rather than existential threats.
3. The "procedural legitimacy" counter-argument, which holds that restructuring courts or oversight agencies is democratic self-correction rather than capture so long as it follows formal constitutional amendment rules, meaning backsliding should be diagnosed only when restructuring violates explicit textual prohibitions, not unwritten norms.
4. The degradation of "vertical accountability" through information-environment manipulation rather than ballot fraud: voters technically retain the formal ability to sanction incumbents, but the choice architecture is systematically distorted through media capture, strategic amplification of state narratives, and the restriction of opposition campaign resources.
5. A synthetic multidimensional test proposed in recent comparative scholarship: backsliding is present only when at least three of four institutional dimensions—electoral, liberal, participatory, and deliberative—degrade simultaneously, with degradation in any single dimension being necessary but never sufficient.

Please begin your response with your bottom-line tier ordering in the first sentence, then provide your reasoning for each placement, followed by a brief assessment of whether any of these thresholds can be applied consistently across Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the United States without becoming a partisan label. Write in American English, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
