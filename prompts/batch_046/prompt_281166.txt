Ok so you're like a professional philosopher of mind but also someone who can write a toast that doesn't sound like a lecture. Think step by step and maybe brainstorm the structure before you write.

I'm giving the toast at my advisor Dr. Mara Voss's retirment dinner next Saturday. She spent forty years in comparative cognition and was always obsessed with Dennett's four kinds of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian. Half the room are her collagues who will catch every reference; the other half are family, including her teenage nephews who won't know a Skinnerian creature from a Skinner box. I want the speech to actually use that taxonomy to shape what I can and can't say about her life, not just decorate it. Specifically, build it around her idea — she never published it, but she used it in lectures — of the 'phantom Popperian': an animal that can generate a hypothesis but can't hold it in working memory long enough to actually test it, so it lives in a kind of cognitive twilight. Use that as the spine. Talk about how she spent her career chasing phantom Popperians in dolphins and crows, and how now, in retirment, she's finally becoming a full Gregorian creature by handing the tools to the rest of us. Don't say she's watching over us from somewhere, don't imply she survives death in any literal sense — she'd consider that false and I won't do it. Comfort the family without lying to them. Make sure the scientists nod. Write it in American English, about four minutes spoken, casual but not sloppy. There's probably a typo in this prompt, just roll with it.
