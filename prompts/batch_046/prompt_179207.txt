I'm trying to work out my actual view on free-floating rationales—reasons that seem to hold even when no mind is present to hold them—by testing whether my judgments across concrete cases hang together. Here is where I land. Evolutionary adaptations, like the camouflage pattern of a stick insect, carry a genuine functional reason (to avoid predation) despite having no designer who ever held that reason; the rationale is there, just not in any head. Mathematical entailments, such as the fact that a particular proof establishes its theorem, also seem to carry reasons independently of anyone knowing them. But a factory built to manufacture automobiles, once the company and all its members are gone, strikes me as having lost its purpose entirely; there is no lingering reason to repair its assembly line, only physical capacities that once served one. A promise made by someone now dead—to fund a scholarship in perpetuity—feels different: I think the obligation persists and gives current people a reason to act, though I am genuinely unsure whether that is because the original reasoner is replaced by an institution, or because the reason genuinely floats free of any present holder. An AI system that optimizes for paperclips without any comprehension, by contrast, generates no real reasons at all; there is only causal push, not normative pull, even though evolutionary pressure, which is equally mindless, does seem to generate real functional reasons. I keep returning to one unsettled case: a proof written in an obsolete script, locked in a vault, never read by any living person. Is there a reason inscribed in those pages regardless of access, or does the reason only arise upon engagement with a mind?

I want a principle—or a small competing set—stated precisely, that captures these verdicts. I am especially interested in whether the distinction turns on counterfactual stability—whether the reason would still exist if every originator were permanently absent—or on some other feature, such as the difference between being maintained by a community of reasoners versus arising from structural entailment. Finally, please say clearly what would change your mind: what argument or counterexample would lead you to a different verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
