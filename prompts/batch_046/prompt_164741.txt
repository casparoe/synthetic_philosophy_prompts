I am trying to consolidate my position on the formal and normative status of inductive simplicity before writing it up, and I want a consistency audit rather than a general introduction. My commitments are roughly these:

(1) Solomonoff induction correctly formalizes Occam's razor: for computable hypotheses, prior probabilities should be weighted by 2 raised to the negative Kolmogorov complexity of the hypothesis relative to a universal Turing machine.

(2) Kolmogorov complexity is invariant up to an additive constant across universal machines, so judgments of relative simplicity are not radically language-relative for hypotheses of non-trivial size.

(3) In practice, rational agents never access the true Kolmogorov complexity; they can only deploy resource-bounded approximations such as MDL with specific encoding schemes.

(4) Occam's razor applies not only within a fixed representational framework but also to the choice of framework itself: we ought to select the encoding scheme (and hence the reference universal machine) that minimizes the joint description length of model and data.

(5) Solomonoff induction is normatively binding, not merely descriptively interesting: a fully rational agent's credences ought to converge to the Solomonoff distribution.

(6) There is no objective fact of the matter about which universal Turing machine is the correct reference for computing Kolmogorov complexity; the choice is conventional, and rational disagreement about the reference machine is therefore permissible.

Which of these actually pull against one another, which tensions are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the genuine conflicts by severity, indicate what each revision costs (e.g., giving up normative binding, giving up framework-level simplicity, or accepting a canonical reference machine), and be specific about whether (3) undermines (5) or whether (2) and (6) can be reconciled with (4).
