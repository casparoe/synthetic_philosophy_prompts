I am a researcher in active inference and variational free-energy minimization, fluent in hierarchical generative models, precision-weighted prediction errors, epistemic versus pragmatic value, and policy selection under uncertainty, but with only a passing familiarity with Kant. Restate the transcendental argument of the Second Analogy of Experience—that the objective succession of states in appearance requires a necessary, rule-governed connection in perception, not merely a subjective sequence of representations—using exclusively the vocabulary of active inference: generative models, hidden states, transition dynamics, precision, and the distinction between perceptual and active inference. Map each step of Kant’s argument onto a corresponding element of the architecture with as little distortion as possible. Then provide a residue report: identify which commitments of the transcendental argument (for example, the necessary and universal validity of causal rules, the irreducibility of objective time-determination to subjective association) translate cleanly into constraints on the generative model or its inference dynamics; which ones can only be preserved by distorting either the philosophy or the engineering (for instance, by treating normative necessity as merely high-precision priors); and which ones have no structural counterpart in active inference at all. Conclude by assessing whether the residue indicates a genuine limitation in using predictive architectures to model objective temporal order, or whether it suggests that Kant’s notion of necessary connection was already doing the work of a learned transition model.
