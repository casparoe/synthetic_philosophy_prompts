I serve on the University Committee for Scientific Archives and Interdisciplinary Classification, and we must vote by the 15th of next month on whether to acquire a recently donated set of Kurt Gödel’s private lecture notes from the 1950s. The collection contains detailed mathematical derivations extending his rotating-universe cosmological work—specifically, metrics admitting closed timelike curves—alongside sustained philosophical annotations arguing that temporal succession lacks objectivity because those curves collapse any invariant distinction between earlier and later. One faction urges full acquisition and placement in the physics archive, treating the derivations as primary scientific manuscripts that deepen the cosmological model. The other faction urges rejection, or at minimum placement in the philosophy archive with a scientific disclaimer, on the grounds that Gödel’s metaphysical conclusion—that time is in some sense unreal—derives not from empirical observation but from symmetry properties of the metric, and that housing these claims alongside physics would compromise the archive’s credibility. A further complication is that the notes contain references to specific colleagues and to internal correspondence regarding the Institute for Advanced Study’s review of cosmological research during that period; the donor agreement is silent on whether such institutional history may be published.

Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—acquire and classify in one archive, split the collection, reject it, or approve with conditions—together with a clear implementation timeline. The recommendation must treat the scientific value of the rotating-universe derivations seriously, address Gödel’s argument about the unreality of time without reducing it to mere speculation, and engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side, whether that is the risk to scientific credibility or the risk to philosophical integrity. The memo should be actionable for the committee and should not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
