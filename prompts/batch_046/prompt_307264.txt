I'm moderating a colloquium interview with an unnamed philosopher who recently defended a view she terms narrative discontinuity without epistemic loss within the literature on transformative experience. She grants that choices such as becoming a parent or undergoing deep religious conversion can radically alter subjective values and phenomenal access, but she rejects L.A. Paul's thesis that rational deliberation requires first-person simulation of the post-transformation self. In its place she proposes a coherence-trajectory criterion: the decision-maker evaluates whether the anticipated narrative arc integrates with current second-order commitments about agency and moral continuity, without requiring predictive access to future phenomenal states. She further maintains that this dissolves the unknown-unknowns problem in transformative choice, provided we abandon the assumption that personal identity across transformation is preserved by psychological continuity rather than by diachronic narrative authority.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her to restate the view from scratch. I want at least one question pressing on how the coherence-trajectory criterion avoids collapsing into conservative bias that merely privileges present values; at least one on how her rejection of psychological continuity interacts with standard Lockean or Parfitian accounts of personal identity; and at least one on the ethics of advising others to undergo transformative experiences—for instance, medical students selecting specialties that reshape moral sensibility. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give: specifically, her likely defense that narrative authority is not present-bias in temporal disguise, her likely appeal to a non-reductionist social ontology of the self, and her likely distinction between advisory transparency and advisory substitution. Conclude with a final question asking what empirical or phenomenological finding could genuinely undermine her framework, with the explicit condition that she may not dismiss it as merely a research program rather than an objection.
