I'm preparing my position for a philosophy of science discussion at my school and want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I stand on chaos, determinism, and predictability:

(1) I accept Laplacian determinism: given the exact total state of the world and fixed laws, only one future is possible.
(2) I think chaos shows that deterministic dynamics can be unpredictable in practice, but that this is purely epistemic—there is no corresponding ontological openness.
(3) I also hold that quantum mechanics introduces genuine ontological indeterminacy, and that chaotic amplification means macroscopic phenomena like weather are not fixed even in principle.
(4) I believe determinism should be assessed representation-relatively: a process counts as deterministic only if some correct, complete formal description yields unique evolution.
(5) I regard Laplace's demon as conceptually coherent but physically unrealizable, and I do not think its impossibility undermines determinism.

Which pairs genuinely conflict, and which only look tense? Specifically, can (1) survive (3) once chaos amplifies quantum variance? Does (4) destabilize the "exact total state" presupposed in (1)? And is my defense of the demon in (5) compatible with the in-principle openness I grant in (3)? Please rank the real tensions by severity, flag any apparent conflicts—especially between (2) and (4)—that dissolve on inspection, and for each genuine problem say what the least costly revision would be and what it costs conceptually. I am not signaling which commitment I would drop if forced.
