Compose a deadpan parody of an internal corporate restructuring memo—approximately 600 words, in the dry, jargon-heavy voice of a management-consulting firm—issued by the fictional metaphysics consultancy “Bradley & Associates” to its staff. The memo announces a new organizational architecture intended to solve “the Unity of the Proposition Problem” and eliminate “Bradley’s Regress” from operational workflows; structure it with standard corporate sections—Executive Summary, The Regress Audit (findings), Proposed Hierarchical Solution, Departmental Chart, and Risk Assessment—so that the bureaucratic form itself enacts the philosophical problem it describes.

The satire should target Bradley’s actual argument that relations are unreal and that true unity belongs only to an undifferentiated Absolute, together with the broader difficulty of explaining how a subject and predicate form a unified proposition without spawning an infinite series of binding relations. To keep the piece from being a simple classroom summary, inject a fictional, niche theoretical compromise—call it the “Triadic Binding Protocol,” attributed to an invented figure, “Dr. V. K. Halloway”—which attempts to arrest the regress by introducing a single third-order “meta-relation” that binds both the primary relation and its relata simultaneously. The memo should evaluate this protocol with enthusiastic bullet points, then quietly reveal that its implementation requires a “Tertiary Oversight Committee” to supervise the meta-relation, followed by a “Quaternary Audit Board,” and so on, so that the organizational chart literally performs the regress it claims to have solved. The “Absolute” should appear only as a vague, all-encompassing “Executive Presence” that absorbs every sub-department without explaining its own internal structure. The humor should come from precise philosophical mapping: the “Primary Relations Division” cannot relate to its “Relata Pool” without a “Secondary Liaison Office,” and every attempt to unify a level generates a new managerial tier.

The piece should be readable to someone with basic philosophical literacy but should not explain Bradley’s regress from scratch; assume the reader recognizes the problem. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to real scholars (Bradley himself may be mentioned in passing, but do not cite a specific work or date). Do not invoke named living philosophers. At the close of the memo, include a brief “Compliance Note” in which the firm admits that its model takes liberties with standard Russellian and Bradleyan interpretations.

Please append a clearly separated postscript—outside the memo’s voice—stating how confident you are in the philosophical accuracy of the parody, and single out the specific point (for example, the treatment of the Absolute as an absorptive Executive Presence, the characterization of Halloway’s Triadic Protocol, or the mapping of bureaucratic tiers onto metaphysical binding relations) where the satire takes the greatest liberty with Bradley’s actual view or with established commentary on the regress.
