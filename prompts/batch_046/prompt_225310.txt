Compare Moore's account of aesthetic appreciation with his account of friendship—personal affection—within his broader ethical framework. Both are said to be among the greatest intrinsic goods, yet aesthetic appreciation is directed toward an object lacking consciousness, while friendship is essentially reciprocal and involves mutual awareness. Does Moore's principle of organic unity apply analogously to both: is the value of a beautiful scene partly constituted by the perceiver's conscious state in the same way that the value of friendship is constituted by the relation between the friends? Or does the absence of reciprocity in the aesthetic case make it a limiting instance that strains the claim that intrinsic value always belongs to mental states or to organic wholes built from them? Be direct: if this parallel misreads Moore—say, if he treats aesthetic value as purely internal and friendship as structurally different—say so plainly rather than forcing the comparison. Give your bottom-line verdict in the first sentence, then reasoning, with no preamble and without restating the question.
