I sit on the faculty senate's academic policy committee and I need to draft a memo for Tuesday's vote. The proposal is to replace our three-specialist tenure review with a nine-person random faculty jury — yes/no vote, majority rules — for all candidates. Backers keep citing Condorcet. Skeptics say judgement isn't independant and competence varies wildly across fields; one of our philosophers even argued that if voter competence drops below 1/2 outside one's own discipline, then cross-disciplinary aggregation can drive group accuracy downward rather than upward, especially for interdisciplinary hires. I need you to write the memo I'd circulate: reccomend adoption, rejection, or a conditional one-year pilot, with a concrete timeline and specific conditions. Treat the jury theorem seriously — address independence failure, heterogeneous competence, and that dilution objection head-on — but don't just summarize Condorcet like a textbook. Give the strongest counterargument honestly and say what feature of the case makes it bite. Keep it actionable: the committee should be able to vote on the bottom line.
