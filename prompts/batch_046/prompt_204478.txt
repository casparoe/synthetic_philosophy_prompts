I run a small philosophy-of-science workshop and were pitching a foundation for a three-month project on Jeffrey conditionalization when the partion itself is in doubt. The question we want answered: if your uncertainty isn't just about which cell of a fixed partion is actual, but about what the correct partion even is—say, you're weighing rival ways of carving up the evidence space—can Jeffrey's rule still be applied in a principled way, or does it collapse back into standard Bayes over a bigger state space? Existing treatments mostly take the partion as given (Jeffrey's own setup, the usual Dutch-book defenses, Field-style rigidity constraints), so they don't tell us whether partion-uncertainty breaks the rationale for Jeffrey updating or just makes it messier. Our angle is conceptual: trace exactly where the partion-stability assumption hides inside arguments that Jeffrey is distinct from Bayes, then see if relaxing it produces incoherence or just a more complicated but still valid norm. The delivarable should be a decision framework for epistemologists facing this kind of meta-uncertainty—when to Jeffrey-update, when to Bayes-update over partions, and when to hold back. We'd count it as failure if all three turn out to be existantially equivalent, since then the debate was never really about updating rules. Scope is tight: conceptual mapping, not formal proofs, one working paper, and a short practical guide. If someone asks why a foundation should fund this, we'd say AI alignment and scientific reasoning both deal with evidence that doesn't come pre-sorted, so getting the normative structure right has real downstream value. We'll handle citations ourselves.
