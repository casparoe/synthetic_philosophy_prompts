Draft the joint statement that should result from an adversarial collaboration between a neo-Aristotelian virtue ethicist and an experimental situationist critic of character-based moral psychology. The document must contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can sign, restricted to what is empirically well-established or conceptually uncontroversial about moral behavior, trait measurement stability, and the logic of virtue ascription—explicitly separating settled findings (e.g., that situational factors influence behavior, that trait measures show moderate but not perfect cross-situational correlation) from genuinely contested interpretations, and never presenting a disputed reading of the situationist literature or the demands of virtue theory as consensus; (2) a disagreements table with columns worded so that each camp accepts its own side’s formulation, addressing at minimum: (a) whether virtue attribution conceptually requires ascription universality—i.e., cross-situational consistency across all domains or only within a relevant subset; (b) whether situational variables hold epistemic dominance over trait variables in explaining particular moral behaviors; and (c) whether observed behavioral variance falsifies virtue ethics outright or only a psychologically unrealistic, maximally universalist version of it; (3) for each disagreement in (2), identify the precise crux—the sub-question whose resolution would rationally move at least one party to revise a substantive claim; (4) for each crux, specify one concrete behavioral case or empirical finding (for instance, a classic experimental paradigm or a precisely parameterized hypothetical scenario) that both sides agree bears on the issue, and record in advance each side’s prediction about what that case demonstrates regarding the crux; (5) a brief concluding assessment of whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal—identifying the exact formulation that generates apparent conflict without genuine substantive disagreement, and explaining why. Throughout, maintain a formal register suitable for a philosophical journal’s discussion section, avoid bibliographic requests or citation demands, and ensure that contested claims are flagged as such rather than treated as established doctrine.
