I'm running a philosophy-of-mind workshop with about 12 grad students, 90 min, and I want them to feel the actual punch of Avicenna's flying-man argument—not just summarize it. I want to inject a twist that's missing from every standard handout: instead of one suspended figure, design a collective isolation exercise where each participant gets a 'sensory-null' role (no sight, no sound, no touch, no memory cues) and the group must reach agreement on whether they share one awareness or are separate ones, using only fist-person statements and never referencing bodies, language, or social roles. The rules, roles, and decision points should force them into the gap between knowing-that-I-am and knowing-what-I-am before anyone names Avicenna, mentions the flying man, or brings up knowledge-by-presence. Include a timeline, the debrief that maps their stuck points onto Avicenna's distinction between immediate self-awareness and representational knowledge, an honest note on which metaphysical commitment—say, the soul's independence from the body—this activity can't reproduce and you have to supply, and a heads-up on what's gonna break the first time, like people cheating with eye contact or slipping into third-person talk.
