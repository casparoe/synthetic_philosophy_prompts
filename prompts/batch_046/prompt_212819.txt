I'm working through Borges's essays on time for a discussion group and keep hitting the same wall. He denies temporal succession—basically channelling Berkeley and Hume—saying the present is just a point with no real past or future attached to it. But then he writes these stories where memory seems to do real work, like when a character recognizes that a moment now is identicle to one decades earlier. I'm trying to come up with an example of a conceptual issue that shows exactly where this gets weird, not just in the famous garden-of-forking-paths way but in something more granular. Can you construct a hypothetical scenario—maybe involving someone who "remembers" a conversation that hasn't happened yet, or who experiences two moments as the same without being able to say which came first—where Borges's denial of linear time makes it impossible to describe what's going on without smuggling in the very temporal relations he rejects? I want the example to actually expose the tension, not just illustrate it, and it should feel like something that could happen in one of his fictions but isn't just a direct retelling of one I already know.
