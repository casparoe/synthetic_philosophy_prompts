As a novelist constructing a fictional cosmology in which certain contradictions are locally sustained—say, a being that is genuinely both present and absent in the same respect—without allowing that contradiction to render every other statement in that world simultaneously true and false, I need to understand the conceptual machinery that contains such contradictions. Could you explain, in intuitive terms rather than formal derivation, how dialetheism distinguishes between contradictions that are genuinely true in a domain and the classical principle of explosion that would spread them everywhere? Specifically, how does the rejection of disjunctive syllogism within paraconsistent logic function not as an ad hoc exemption, but as a structural feature of entailment—one that requires relevance between premises and conclusions? Furthermore, is there a recognized conceptual distinction between contradictions that constitute the ontology of a fictional world and those that merely reflect unresolved tensions in its narrative representation; and if so, how does that distinction affect whether we treat the contradiction as world-building or as an epistemic limitation of the characters within it?
