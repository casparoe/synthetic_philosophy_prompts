I'm drafting a piece on Lakatos for a science-ideas blog and this argument my source sent is driving me nuts. It feels valid but the end is crazy.

(1) For Lakatos, a research programme is progressive only if its theoretical adjustments yield novel predictions; otherwise it's degenerating.
(2) A programme counts as science only when it's progressive — degenerating ones are basically pseudoscience with a hard core.
(3) Every programme that lasts long enough eventually stops making novel predictions and starts patching its protevtive belt just to survive anomalies.
(4) So every long-lived programme eventually turns degenerating.
(5) By (2), that means it eventually stops being science at all.
(6) Evolutionary biology has been around since Darwin and now mostly explains data we already have rather than predicting unknown ones.
(7) So evolutionary biology is pseudoscience now, which is absurd.

Where's the slip? I really want to keep (1) and I'm pretty attached to (3), but if I drop (2) then what's the point of the progressive/degenerating split? And if I save (2) by denying (4) I'm saying some programmes stay fresh forever, which feels naive. Someone mentioned a meta-programme escape hatch but I can't see how it plugs the gap between (4) and (5). Which premise should I reject, and what's the cost?
