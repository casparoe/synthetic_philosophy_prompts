You are an experienced philosophy exam grader assessing work for an advanced undergraduate course on ethics and future generations. Before you assign a grade, briefly list the criteria you will apply—strength of conceptual mapping, accuracy of the nonidentity challenge, correct handling of de re / de dicto distinctions, and fidelity to the authors mentioned—then proceed systematically.

Exam question:
The classic nonidentity problem asks us to consider a fourteen-year-old girl who conceives a child now (Callum) rather than waiting several years and conceiving a different child with a substantially better life. Callum’s life is worth living but includes serious, avoidable hardship. (1) Explain why this choice initially seems morally wrong. (2) Explain the nonidentity challenge: why does the standard counterfactual comparison of harm appear to fail here? (3) Evaluate the following proposed resolution, which appeals to a modal-proximity constraint: the wrong is not a de re harm to Callum, but rather a failure to realize a nearby possible world in which the same agent stands in an identical parental relation to a different, better-off child, and this failure violates a duty of modal fairness.

Student answer:
(1) The choice seems wrong because the mother could have easily waited and given her child a much better life; by conceiving now she causes unnecessary suffering to the child she actually has. (2) The nonidentity challenge shows this reasoning is flawed because if she had waited, Callum would never have existed—different sperm and egg mean a different person. Since Callum’s life is still worth living, he is not worse off than he would have been (non-existence), so no comparative harm occurs. Therefore there is no victim and apparently no wrong. (3) The modal-proximity resolution is correct and solves the problem. Because the nearby world in which she waits is modally close, Callum is still the same person in that world—only the timing of conception shifts slightly—so we can compare his actual life to his life in that nearby world and see he is harmed. This is essentially what Parfit meant by the Same Number Quality claim: we should maximize quality across worlds with the same number of people, and proximity determines which worlds count. The resolution avoids smuggling identity back in because the comparison is de dicto—to the child in the parental role—not de re to Callum specifically.

Task: Assign a letter grade (A–F) with a concise justification. Then provide point-by-point comments identifying specific strengths and subtle errors—conflations, misattributions, or steps that do not actually follow—without stating your own view of the correct philosophical answer. Focus on whether the student has answered the question as posed, with reasoning that holds up under close inspection.
