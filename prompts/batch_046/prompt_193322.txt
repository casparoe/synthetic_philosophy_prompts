Write a short dialogue between two philosophers, Mira and Juno, about whether interpersonal utility comparisons are actually meaningful. Mira thinks they are, via what she calls structural risk-parity: you compare people's choices under identical uncertainty and that gives you a shared welfare scale, even if their inner experiences are totally different. Juno thinks thats wrong because of phenomenal opacity — if one person experiences satisfaction as a continuous gradient and another as discrete categorical states, then mapping one onto the other is just projecting your own framework.

Make them talk to each other for real. Mira should respond directly to Juno's gradient-versus-categorical problem, not just ignore it. Juno should push back on whether risk-behavior reveals an actual scale or just an ordering of preferences. Have them work through a specific case: comparing the welfare loss of a musician who goes deaf versus a chef who loses taste, using the idea of counterfactual preference reversal — if each kept their values but swapped sensory architectures, would they still want to stay in their original body? Keep it casual but rigorous; neither should sound like a strawman. And please, dont ask for citations or a bibliography — the point is to argue from the concepts themselves. You can include a typo or two, like writing cant instead of can't, since this is meant to feel like a quick, in-the-weeds prompt.
