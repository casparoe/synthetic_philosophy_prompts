I was talking with a friend who recently read a long-form article on panpsychism, and she tried to explain the combination problem to me. She said panpsychism holds that consciousness is a fundamental feature of reality, present even in basic physical entities like quarks or fields, and that human consciousness is somehow built out of these micro-conscious pieces. The hard part, she said, is the combination problem: explaining how many tiny subjects or tiny feelings could merge into one big subject like me, rather than just leaving me as a crowd of little minds with no unified experience. According to her summary, defenders usually answer this with a principle she called phenomenal transparency — the idea that a macro-subject does not experience its micro-parts as separate, just as I do not experience my individual neurons, so there is no literal adding up of experiences, only a unified macro-field that makes the micro-level phenomenally invisible from the inside. She added that critics think this is just a redescription, not an explanation, and that if transparency really works, then panpsychism collapses into a kind of emergentism that its supporters explicitly reject. She concluded that because transparency either explains nothing or turns panpsychism into the physicalism it was meant to replace, the view is caught in a dilemma it cannot escape.

I do not have a philosophy background, so I am not sure which parts of this are standard descriptions and which are her glosses or misunderstandings. Could you go through her summary carefully? I would like to know which claims about panpsychism and the combination problem are accurate, which are common simplifications, which subtly misrepresent the positions of defenders or critics, and whether her final dilemma actually follows once the misrepresentations are corrected. In particular, please address whether phenomenal transparency is actually the mechanism panpsychists propose, and whether the charge of collapsing into emergentism or physicalism holds up.
