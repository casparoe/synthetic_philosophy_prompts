You are the senior epistemic auditor for a federal regulatory body that certifies whether scientific advisory panels genuinely know that a policy-relevant statistical correlation obtains, and whether that knowledge legitimately closes over proposed mechanistic explanations. Construct a reusable audit procedure—ordered tests, decision nodes, or a flowchart in prose—that applies Nozick's tracking conditions (sensitivity to falsity, adherence across nearby worlds) together with standard epistemic closure principles. The procedure must be executable in thirty minutes by a non-philosopher auditor whose sign-off binds the agency. It must explicitly distinguish: direct tracking of the statistical claim; closure-licensed inference to a causal interpretation; and nested conditionals where closure and tracking conflict. Provide three worked audits: an unambiguous pass, a failure where sensitivity collapses in a second-order conditional, and a case where closure yields a mechanistically implausible conclusion that the panel will reject. At each step, mark which judgments are procedural and which are irreducible calls for the auditor. Conclude by stating what the procedure is structurally unable to settle—particularly where multi-step closure outstrips single-step sensitivity.
