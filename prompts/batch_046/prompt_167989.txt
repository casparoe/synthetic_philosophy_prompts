Consider a community whose collective commemorative practices conflict with the established historical record—what we might term a case of mnemonic divergence—particularly where the moral framing of remembrance (for instance, the veneration of figures whom archives depict as harmful) diverges from documented chronology. Make a formal argument that in such conflicts, collective memory ought to take precedence over the archival record within the specific domain of axiological sedimentation, by which I mean the layered embedding of normative judgments into commemorative rituals, while empirical chronology should remain authoritative for factual reconstruction. Your argument should explicitly distinguish mnemonic sovereignty, understood as a community's normative authority to structure its own remembrance, from epistemic accountability, the obligation to preserve empirical accuracy in historical archives, and should explain how maintaining this boundary prevents the position from collapsing into either unrestrained constructivism or naive archival positivism.
