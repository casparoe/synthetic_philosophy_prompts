People keep saying the self is like a center of gravity — Dennett's idea that it's just the point where your stories and actions converge, not a ghost inside — and they use it to argue there's no Cartesian ego, just an abstract focal point useful for prediction. But how far does the physics actually hold? Give me the correspondence table: what plays the role of mass, trajectory, external frame of refrence, gravitational pull? Then where it breaks: physical centers are calculable from positions alone, but narrative ones seem to need an audience or language, and they can split or merge in ways physical ones can't. Does this smuggle in a false singularity and stability, especially for people with severe disocciative patterns or collective agents that share one narrative? And if the mapping breaks that badly, does the anti-substantialist conclusion still stand on its own, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument?
