I was in this course on Foucault last semester — we covered Discipline and Punish and my prof kept going on about how power and knowledge aren't seperate, they kind of produce each other. I never totally bought it until I started thinking about these smart-city dashboards that track "wellness metrics" in real time. Anyway, can you write a short story about a city planner named Elena who launches an open-data "livability index" for her town? It should track noise, foot traffic, air quality, stuff like that, with color-coded zones. The point isn't that it's a dystopia; Elena genuinely believes she's helping people make better choices. But over time the categories start reshaping behavior — people avoid orange zones, shops move, and eventually a community garden gets reclassified as "low-usage green space" and quietly removed from funding, not because anyone banned it but because the metric made it invisible. I want the story to show how the knowledge (the index, the colors) and the power over citizens are the same thing, not two different forces. Keep it grounded and a little ambigious, maybe end with Elena noticing the garden's gone and feeling satisfied that the data is "cleaner" now. You can call the town Millbrook or leave it nameless.
