Right, so I spent thirty-odd years in molecular biology and now I'm poking around in philosphy for fun. I've come round to thinking virtue ethics gets unfairly battered by the situationist crowd — not because the Milgram-style data is wrong, but because everyone assumes a virtue has to be a static, cross-situational disposition like a gene allele. If instead you model virtues as dynamical attracter basins in a person's state-space — shaped by ecological embedding and sensitive to local parameters but still genuinely explanatory — then the fact that behaviour shifts with framing doesn't kill the view, it just describes the basin's boundary conditions.

So: what would actually count against this? I don't want the usual "people aren't consistent" reheated. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Which empirical patterns — say, in developmental psych, computational modelling of personality, or ecological momentary assessment — would break it? Which formal result about attracter stability, predictive uniqueness, or timescale separation would make it collapse into situationism or narrative identity? What predictions does this dynamical-virtue framing make that its rivals don't, and where does it risk being qualified into unfalsifiability? If it has been, is that a problem for a theory of character?

Also tell me: what would defenders of this view themselves concede counts against them, versus what they'd dismiss as measuring the wrong timescale or wrong ecological level? And which of these defeaters do you think are already lurking in the literature, versus which are still hypothetical?

Please be honest about whether the position is well insulated, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice.
