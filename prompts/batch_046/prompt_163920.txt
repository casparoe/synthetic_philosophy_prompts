ok so here's roughly where i've landed on Zhuangzi and dream scepticism and i'd like to know if it actually hangs together or if i'm talking out of both sides of my mouth.

(1) i read the butterfly passage as mainly about wuhua — transformation of things — and the arbitrariness of privileging one state over another. its not really a sceptical argument like Descartes'; it's more like a nudge that the boundary between 'me' and 'not me', or waking and dreaming, is less stablity than we pretend.

(2) at the same time i think the standard dream argument does fail, but for functional reasons: waking experience has this long-range coherence, practial engagement with tools and other people, and a kind of narrative stablity that dreams usually don't. and i think we can rely on that without begging the question against the sceptic.

(3) i'm also a contextualist about knowledge: in ordinary life you don't need to eliminate every far-fetched possibility, but in disorienting 'high-stakes' contexts — like waking up from general anaesthetic or after taking something strong — 'i might be dreaming/hallucinating' does become a relevant alternative you have to check.

(4) on identity, i lean towards the idea that there's no enduring substrate, just a flow. so asking 'was it really Zhuangzi or really the butterfly?' is a false dichotomy; there's just transformation without a thing that transforms.

(5) but here's the thing: Zhuangzi himself seems genuinely puzzled about which one he is, and he remembers being the butterfly. if there's no substrate, why should that memory matter? and if it doesn't matter, why is he disoriented rather than just indifferent?

(6) finally, i think phenomenal concepts are special in explaining why we feel there's an explanatory gap about consciousness, but i don't think that applies here because the waking/dreaming distinction is behavoural and functional, not phenomenal.

so do these hang together? specifically: does (2) sit ok with (3) given that (2) sounds like a flat denial that dreams threaten knowledge? is (4) compatible with (5) or am i sneaking in a self that i said doesn't exist? and does (1) clash with (2) — if its not scepticism, why am i spending energy refuting the dream argument? tell me which pairs are actually in tension, how bad it is, and what the cheapest fix is for each. also flag anything that only looks tense but isn't.
