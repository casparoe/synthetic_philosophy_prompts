Rank the following five research programmes from the most clearly progressive to the most clearly degenerating under a refined Lakatosian framework: (A) logical positivism in philosophy of science, (B) evolutionary psychology's account of moral intuitions, (C) embodied cognition in cognitive science, (D) the effective-altruism research agenda in normative decision theory, and (E) a hypothetical programme that integrates first-person phenomenological description with predictive modeling of subjective experience. Apply a stricter criterion than Lakatos's standard formulation: treat a programme as degenerating only when adjustments to its protective belt reduce empirical content rather than merely accommodating anomalies, and only when it has failed across at least two successive problem shifts to generate predictions not already entailed by rival programmes. For each of the five, assign it to a tier—progressive, stagnant, or degenerating—and justify the placement in a single sentence that identifies a specific theoretical move, such as an ad hoc auxiliary hypothesis, a hard-core revision, or a genuinely novel prediction. In your reasoning for item E, specify one concrete protective-belt adjustment that this hybrid programme might use to defend its hard core against an anomaly like the predictive failure of introspective reports in altered states.
