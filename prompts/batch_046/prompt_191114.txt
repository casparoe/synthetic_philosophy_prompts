Rather than simply repeating the familiar analogy of the statues of Daedalus, I want a poem that brings the classical problem into contact with a contemporary idea I encountered in our supplementary reading: the virtue-epistemological claim that knowledge involves a kind of cognitive achievement or epistemic credit, whereas true belief can be merely accidental or passively received.

Please write a Petrarchan sonnet with an octave in ABBAABBA rhyme and a sestet in CDECDE, using iambic pentameter. The octave should establish the functional equivalence of belief and knowledge—both guide the traveler, both hit the mark—while the sestet's volta at line nine should pivot to the issue of stability and ownership: is the value in being anchored externally, or in the agent's own intellectual effort? Let the rhyme scheme and word choices mirror this structural shift, pairing terms of motion and guidance in the octave with terms of earning, tying, or holding in the sestet. The philosophical content should be precise enough that a reader familiar with both the Meno and modern credit theories of knowledge recognizes the conceptual moves, but please do not include direct quotations from Plato or any modern source; paraphrase freely. End on an open, unsettled note, reflecting the fact that our seminar concluded that the problem remains genuinely unresolved.
