I've been thinking lately about whether life can still be meaningful when the universe itself seems indifferent, and I've been comparing notes with a friend who has strong intuitions about this even though she's never studied philosophy formally. Here are four judgments she's made.

She says that a composer who spends forty years writing symphonies no one will ever hear, who feels deeply fulfilled by the work itself, is living a genuinely meaningful life. She also says that a caregiver who spends decades working with strangers in need, feeling constantly empty and never personally satisfied, is still living a meaningful life. On the other hand, she says that a wealthy person who spends every day accumulating more money, feeling perfectly content, is living an absurd and meaningless life. And she says that a hermit who lives alone tending a small garden, affecting no one else, feeling peaceful and satisfied, is also living a meaningful life.

Now I'm wondering about a new case. There's a linguist who spends her entire adult life constructing an elaborate artificial language, complete with its own grammar, poetry, and internal philosophy. She knows with complete certainty that no other human being will ever learn or use it. She lives modestly on a small inheritance, is totally absorbed in the project, and describes her existence as profoundly satisfying. She has no audience, no family, and no social impact whatsoever.

Given her earlier judgments, do you think she would call this linguist's life meaningful or absurd? Which of her four earlier cases is the closest analog? I can see at least two different principles that might explain all four of her original verdicts: one that resembles the absurdist idea that meaning is created through a kind of conscious rebellion against indifference, regardless of outside recognition; and another that resembles a contemporary hybrid view, where meaning requires both that a person genuinely cares about something and that what they care about has value independent of mere satisfaction. What does each principle predict about the linguist? Which principle do you think she's actually running, and what further hypothetical case would best distinguish between them?
