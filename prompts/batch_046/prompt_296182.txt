Write the desiderata that a normative theory of the right to the city must meet to adjudicate conflicts over zoning, density, and housing supply—particularly NIMBY opposition and municipal planning authority—among incumbent residents, prospective residents, and the broader urban polity. For each requirement, specify the motivating case or conceptual tension: for example, the distinction between legitimate democratic voice in local planning and exclusionary veto; the intergenerational lock-in problem, where current residents' preferences restrict future populations' access to housing; and the need to preserve genuine collective goods of neighborhood life without reducing them to property-value protection. Identify which desiderata are non-negotiable (e.g., consistency with equal basic liberties, prohibition on explicitly exclusionary criteria) and which reflect the prejudices of particular camps—market libertarianism, communitarian localism, or centralized developmentalism. Flag any pair you judge jointly unsatisfiable, especially whether substantive autonomy for existing residents over local land-use change can coexist with guaranteed adequate affordable supply for non-residents; and state which single requirement eliminates the largest class of competing theories. Keep the conceptual weight on the criteria and their motivating tensions rather than on scoring every existing position.
