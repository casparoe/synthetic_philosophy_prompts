Design an assessment for an upper-level metaphysics seminar final (90 minutes, 25 marks total). The topic is the problem of universals, but frame it through a specific, somewhat technical pressure point rather than a generic "compare realism, nominalism, and trope theory" prompt.

Please provide the following sections in this order:

1. The Exam Question (~250 words). Write one essay prompt that asks students to evaluate a hypothetical "selective structural realism" about universals: the view that only mathematically or formally definable properties are genuinely shared universals, while qualitative properties (colors, tastes) are practical fictions tracking structural similarities below our discrimination threshold. The question should require students to: (a) explain why this view might dissolve both the traditional problem of shared presence and the determinable/determinate redundancy problem; (b) show how an Aristotelian immanent realist and a trope theorist would each argue that the proposal merely relocates the ontological difficulty rather than solving it; and (c) assess whether vagueness—specifically sorites-susceptible qualitative properties—gives the selective realist a decisive advantage, or whether it undermines her by revealing that even structural properties have vague boundaries. Insist that answers go beyond summary and defend a verdict on whether selective realism is a stable compromise or an unstable halfway house.

2. The Model Answer (~800 words). Write a polished essay that takes a clear position (for or against the stability of selective realism), demonstrates command of immanent realism and trope theory, addresses the determinable/determinate relation without treating it as merely taxonomic, and explains precisely where vagueness does or does not shift the burden.

3. The Marking Scheme (~350 words). Structure it around five must-hit points that together total 25 marks, specifying the marks for each. For each point, include a one-line note on how candidates typically fail it (e.g., conflating determinables with determinates, treating vagueness as only an epistemic problem, or summarizing the three positions without adjudicating the selective proposal). Make clear what separates an A-range answer (contains all five moves with precise reasoning) from a B-range answer (accurate summary but missing the crux on determinables or vagueness). Do not include reading lists, citations, or references to specific page numbers.
