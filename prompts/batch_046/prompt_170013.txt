I serve as the junior student delegate on my high school’s Restorative Justice and Policy Review Committee. We must issue a formal recommendation on Case 2024-17: a deaf student, who communicates through American Sign Language interpreters, filed a grievance alleging that a teacher made ableist remarks during class. The administration dismissed her account, noting that her emotional response seemed disproportionate, that interpreter-mediated testimony is less direct than that of hearing witnesses, and that our grievance form contains no category for accessibility-related credibility concerns or for linguistic mediation in testimony. They subsequently placed a disciplinary notation on her record after she protested the dismissal. She now asks the committee to recommend expungement of that notation and revision of the grievance protocol.

Some members argue this instantiates testimonial injustice—her credibility was discounted because of stereotypes linking disability and mediated communication to unreliability—together with hermeneutical injustice, since the school’s framework lacks the conceptual resources to treat interpreter-mediated testimony as fully authoritative and to recognize accessibility barriers as relevant to evidentiary evaluation. Others counter that institutional fairness requires uniform evidentiary standards; creating special procedures for particular identity categories risks undermining procedural neutrality and producing unworkable exemptions.

Please draft the committee’s decision memo for submission to the principal and board. Recommend one of the following: uphold the notation with no policy change; expunge the notation and revise the protocol; or approve with specified conditions. Your reasoning should apply the concepts of testimonial and hermeneutical injustice to the concrete facts of this case, treat the procedural-neutrality objection as the strongest consideration favoring the opposing view, and propose criteria the administration could apply in future grievances without requiring specialized philosophical training. Write in a formal institutional voice with a clear, actionable bottom line.
