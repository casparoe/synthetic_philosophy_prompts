I'm working through some problems in formal epistemology for an independent reading course, and I've been trying to apply a sampling framework to the Sleeping Beauty problem. Here is the setup I'm using: Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, and she is awakened on Monday regardless of the outcome; if the coin lands Tails, she is awakened again on Tuesday with her memory erased, and if it lands Heads she remains asleep. My study partner argues that upon awakening Beauty should set her credence in Heads to one-third, on the grounds that she occupies one of three equiprobable observer-moments—Monday-Heads, Monday-Tails, and Tuesday-Tails. But this reasoning strikes me as conflating rational credence with statistical sampling. If we treat each awakening as an independent draw from the space of all possible awakenings generated by the protocol, then over many repetitions of the experiment the long-run frequency of Tails-awakenings to Heads-awakenings approaches two to one. So shouldn't Beauty's self-locating belief that "I am currently in an awakening" simply be calibrated to that objective base rate, making one-third correct not because of some mysterious de se update but because it tracks the limiting distribution of her own observer-moments? Where I get stuck is this: if we accept that her belief is essentially an empirical frequency estimate over her experienced awakenings, then the halfer position—holding steady at one-half—looks like it's committing a kind of indexical error by refusing to condition on the fact that she is one of the drawn samples. Could you clarify whether there is a principled reason to reject this frequentist reading of her self-locating belief, and whether framing the puzzle as a reference-class problem over awakenings rather than as a question about centered worlds actually dissolves the paradox rather than resolving it?
