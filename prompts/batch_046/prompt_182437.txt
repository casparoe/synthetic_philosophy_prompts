I run a philosophy workshop on political obligation and self-binding, about 9 people, 80 minuts. I want an in-room activty that makes participants feel the force of constitutional precommitment — not just individual Ulysses-style self-binding, but the legitimacy problem that happens when one democratic generation writes rules that bind people who didn't consent yet. To keep it from being too familiar, I'm injecting a twist I'm calling the recursive binding trap: the amendment rules are themselves part of what's locked in, so anyone who wants to escape the contract has to operate the very mechanism that was designed to stop escape.

Design the full session. First half (~25 min): rules for how they collectively draft a 4- or 5-rule mini-constitution for the second half, including an amendment clause they think sounds fair at the time. Second half (~30 min): a crisis scenario hits where the rules clearly hurt the majority, but amending them requires a vote format or veto structure they baked in earlier. I want actual roles — maybe hidden "framer" vs "bound citizen" cards assigned after drafting? — and a payoff scheme using points or privileges (like speaking order or snack access, nothing real). Include the debrief that maps moments in the room onto the legitimacy gap and the recursive trap. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong — I bet they'll just ignore the rules and revolt — and how to contain that without killing the effect. Finally, be honest about which feature of real constitutional theory this 80-minute thing with 9 grad students can't reproduce, so I know what I have to supply in the closing talk.
