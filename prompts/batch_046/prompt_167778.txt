Draft an adversarial-collaboration document between two opposing camps on famine causation, using Amartya Sen's comparative analyses of the Bengal famine of 1943, the Irish famine of the 1840s, and the Great Leap Forward of 1959–1961 as the shared evidentiary base.

Camp A holds the entitlement-collapse view: large-scale famine mortality is primarily a distributive and political failure in which aggregate food availability can remain adequate while access collapses; democratic institutions coupled with an uncensored local press operate as structural preventives rather than mere risk reducers.

Camp B holds the compound-causation view: famine mortality arises from the interaction of ecological or production shock, institutional fragility, and informational failure; to classify famines categorically as political crimes obscures the structural substrate conditions that make entitlement collapse possible, and democratic mechanisms diminish but do not categorically eliminate famine risk.

Structure your response as follows.

First, present a numbered list of descriptive claims both camps can endorse, restricted to the three reference cases: approximate mortality magnitudes; whether aggregate per-capita food availability declined significantly during peak mortality years; whether food exports, requisitions, or redistribution failures continued during those years; and the presence or absence of democratic electoral accountability and press freedom.

Second, present a disagreements table in which each contested claim appears twice—once in wording Camp A accepts for its own position and once in wording Camp B accepts—without collapsing either into neutral vagueness. The table must include: (i) whether a substantial decline in aggregate food availability is necessary for mass famine mortality; (ii) whether the deliberate suppression of harvest and mortality data by governing authorities—term this mechanism information quarantining—is sufficient, given only a moderate production shortfall, to generate mass entitlement collapse; (iii) whether democratic institutions with uncensored local information flows prevent famines categorically or only probabilistically reduce their occurrence.

Third, identify the two or three cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving one in either direction would move at least one camp. For each crux, specify a concrete historical comparison or counterfactual scenario drawn from the three cases that both sides agree is probative, and record in advance what each camp predicts about that scenario.

Fourth, conclude with an assessment of whether any portion of the dispute is purely verbal—specifically, whether disagreement over labeling a famine a political crime concerns only the assignment of moral culpability or also the underlying causal structure—and if a verbal component exists, isolate it and restate the remaining substantive disagreement in causal rather than moral language.

Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke theoretical frameworks or evidentiary claims that fall outside the shared reference cases and the conceptual opposition between entitlement failure and compound causation. Use formal, precise prose throughout.
