(3) To accommodate this without multiplying entities, I treat "statue" as a phase-sort applied to a substance—the marble-substance enters and exits a statue-phase, analogous to a human being entering and exiting a child-phase—so that phases do not add ontological bulk. (4) At the same time, I hold that artifactual kind-membership depends on institutional and intentional contexts; there is no natural-kind "statue-ness" in the world independent of artistic practice, which means the boundary conditions for entering or leaving the statue-phase are conventionally fixed rather than carved at nature's joints. (5) Yet I also want to insist that the difference in modal profile between the statue-phase and the underlying substance is metaphysically robust, not a façon de parler: for instance, if we discovered the "marble" was actually a synthetic composite engineered to look like stone, that might alter the substance present without necessarily dissolving the statue-phase, which suggests the two descriptions track genuinely different properties. (6) Finally, I reject four-dimensionalism for artifacts—I do not think the statue is a spacetime worm with temporal parts, because that seems to solve the problem only by making persistence an arbitrary matter of part-selection rather than explaining why reshaping destroys one thing and not the other.
