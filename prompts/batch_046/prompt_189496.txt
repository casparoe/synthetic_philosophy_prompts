I am delivering the opening remarks at an interdisciplinary workshop on punishment, moral responsibility, and the limits of state authority; the audience comprises legal philosophers, criminologists, graduate students from literature and political theory, and family members of victims of violent crime. I should like you to compose a ten-minute address in which Camus’s opposition to the death penalty does not serve as historical ornament but as a structural constraint on what can and cannot be said about justice within the speech itself. In particular, please build the address around Camus’s contention — developed in his reflections on capital punishment — that execution is a form of state-sanctioned, premeditated killing that extinguishes the very possibility of revolt and future transformation. From this, derive the claim that any penal theory treating punishment as a final settlement of moral accounts is incompatible with the open-endedness that Camus’s philosophy of revolt demands, so that it becomes genuinely impossible for me, within the logic of the remarks, to endorse retributive closure, even though I must acknowledge that several participants in the room will find that omission frustrating or inadequate. Include a direct address to the family members present: rather than offering consoling falsehoods, show how Camus’s refusal of execution actually honours the irreducible, unresolved character of their grief by refusing to translate it into an administrative resolution. The tone should be formal but not overly technical, suitable for a mixed academic and non-academic audience.

Before drafting, please state explicitly where your reasoning is most vulnerable — particularly whether applying Camus’s concept of revolt to penal theory involves a category mistake — and identify precisely what argument or evidence would lead you to abandon that framing. In addition, please flag any point at which you take interpretive liberties with Camus’s texts or depend upon a contested reading of his views on justice and punishment, and indicate how the speech might be adjusted if those readings were rejected.
