You are a senior moral philosopher specializing in comparative ethics and Confucian thought. I am a PhD student in philosophy preparing to interview a scholar—call her Dr. Lin—who defends a reconstructed Confucian role ethics. Before you draft anything, please take a deep breath, recall the standard pressure points in the literature (the liberal demand for critical distance, the problem of role conflict, the charge of conservatism, the comparison with care ethics, and the status of ren relative to particular virtues), and then briefly outline the specific conceptual innovation she introduces: in nested roles, obligations do not aggregate but undergo what she terms “normative filtering,” such that only obligations aligned with the primary relational axis of the immediate context carry full weight, and she applies this to both kinship and institutional relationships like physician-patient or teacher-student roles, treating role-specific virtues such as epistemic humility as constitutive rather than instrumental.

Once you have sketched those pressure points, please draft eight to ten formal, rigorous interview questions suitable for a podcast or colloquium Q&A. The questions must go far beyond asking Dr. Lin to restate her position. For each question, include a follow-up explicitly keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Your sequence should cover the following, with at least one question and follow-up for each:

1. The liberal critique that role ethics lacks the critical distance to condemn oppressive or abusive roles, anticipating her likely appeal to internal role transformation or normative filtering.

6. An institutional application—say, a physician whose familial obligations conflict with professional protocols—asking precisely how normative filtering operates and which axis should prevail, with a follow-up that probes whether this risks reducing institutional ethics to family ethics by another name.

Please present the questions in a numbered list, each followed by its keyed follow-up.
