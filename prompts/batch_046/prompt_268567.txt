me and a colleague have been going back and forth about whether the Anthropocene is a real geological epoch or just a useful historical metaphor with ecological dressing. we want to write an adversarial collaboration doc we can both actually sign and post somewhere. one camp (mine, kinda) is with the formal stratigraphers — AWG-style — who think there should be a single official boundary, probably the Crawford Lake GSSP tied to the plutonium-239 spike around 1952, and that the ICS/IUGS gets to vote on whether it counts. the other camp thinks thats a category mistake: Anthropocene is closer to "the Industrial Revolution" but planetary, so it should have plural, seperate start points — maybe the Orbis spike around 1610, maybe 1784 with Watts patent, maybe 1945 — and authority should sit with interdisciplinary historians and earth-system scientists, not just geologists with formal voting rules.

draft the joint statement. first, a numbered list of claims we both agree on: human activity has measurably altered earth systems; Crutzen and Stoermer popularized the term around 2000; there is a sharp mid-20th-century jump in several stratigraphic markers; local ecological shifts often predate global chemical signals. then a disagreements table in neutral wording — not "youre wrong about" but "Position A holds... Position B holds..." — covering the boundary question and who decides.

then the cruxes. i think the big ones are:
1) whether global synchronicity of a marker is necessary for a period to be "real," or if diachronous regional impacts can be bundled under one name (this is the diachronic visibility problem);
2) whether decision authority belongs to stratigraphic bodies with voting rules, or to interdisciplinary committees;
3) whether maintaining a "pre-human baseline" has normative weight in setting the boundary.

for each crux, include a concrete case or piece of evidence we both accept is relevant, plus what each side predicts. for example, the Svalbard ice core shows plutonium deposition in 1953 with no local coal or nuclear source — Position A says this proves global synchronicity justifies one GSSP; Position B says it only shows one tracer travels globally, while regional soil-erosion or deforestation timelines remain locally specific and shouldnt be forced into a single date.

keep it casual, like a blog post we co-authored. dont ask for citations, bibliographies, or a reading list — we both know the literature well enough. and please, no verdict on who is right; just make the residual disagreement exact.
