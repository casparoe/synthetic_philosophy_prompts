My opening move is this: suppose we grant, for the sake of argument, that birth within a border is morally arbitrary, and suppose we also grant that democratic citizens are co-authors of institutions that generate both public goods and harms—climate damage being a clear case where benefits are largely captured domestically while costs spill across borders. If co-authorship generates joint liability for institutional harms, and if citizens are the primary beneficiaries of the goods those same institutions produce, then it seems that a special associative duty to fellow citizens arises not from ethnicity or culture, but from a structurally analogous position: shared authorship of a scheme that differentially benefits and burdens its members relative to outsiders. Where exactly does this argument fail? Is it that democratic authorship is too diffuse to generate individual obligations, that the benefits are in fact globally excludable in principle, or that joint liability for harm does not entail distributive priority in remedy?
