For an upper-level undergraduate seminar on virtue ethics and Enlightenment moral philosophy, design one essay exam question (to be answered in 75 minutes, approximately 800 words) centered on Benjamin Franklin's project of moral perfection as presented in his autobiography. The question must avoid asking for a list of virtues or a description of the journal method; instead, it should require students to engage a specific conceptual tension: Franklin frames moral perfection as an achievable engineering problem—rational habits monitored through systematic self-observation—yet he later adds humility to his catalog partly because observers tell him his pursuit of perfection has made him insufferably proud. Ask students to determine whether this addition resolves the tension, transforms Franklin's project into an ironic or performative practice in which the pursuit of humility necessarily defeats itself, or reveals a deeper incoherence in applying Enlightenment rational control to virtues that are structurally self-effacing. Require them to compare Franklin's position with one other framework—such as Stoic self-cultivation, Aristotelian habituation, or a Christian account of grace—without demanding citation of specific scholars, and to defend a clear evaluative thesis. Provide a model answer of about 650 words that demonstrates the required conceptual moves, and a marking scheme totaling 25 marks divided among five criteria: (i) precise reconstruction of the tension with accurate reference to Franklin's framing (5 marks); (ii) analysis of whether the tension is destructive, productive, or ironic (5 marks); (iii) a substantive comparison with the chosen alternative framework that highlights a genuine structural difference (5 marks); (iv) a sustained, non-anachronistic evaluative thesis—penalizing answers that treat Franklin as a proto-utilitarian or Kantian (5 marks); and (v) philosophical precision in avoiding reduction of Franklin's journal to mere self-help or productivity technique (5 marks). For each criterion, specify the marks available and name one specific misunderstanding that should cost marks.
