My advisor grades my historical-Jesus essays and her authenticity calls are consistent but ambigous. She says the baptism by John is authentic because later Christians wouldn't invent a subordinate role for Jesus, not because Matthew and Mark both report it. She rejects the temple action as inauthentic even though John and Mark both include it, since it lines up too neatly with prophetic critique of commerce. She accepts the saying that no one knows the day or hour, but she rejects the dissimilarity criterion outright and instead points to internal coherence with other eschatological sayings. She accepts the mustard seed parable only because it lacks both Christian editorial touches and close parallels in rabbinic texts—she treats both absences as necessary. Now consider a single-attestation saying in which Jesus tells a Samaritan that true worship will happen neither on Gerizim nor in Jerusalem, which carries mild embarrassment for an early Judean church but also echoes prophetic centralization themes. Would she call it authentic? I want the competing rules that fit her four calls—maybe embarrassment-first, maybe coherence-plus-redaction-check, maybe a hidden modified-dissimilarity test that allows prophetic echoes but bars rabbinic ones. Give me your best prediction with reasoning, and tell me what further case—say, a saying with both strong embarrassment and clear rabbinic parallels—would actually split those rules.
