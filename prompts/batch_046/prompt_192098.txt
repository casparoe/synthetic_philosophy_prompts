I've been arguing with a friend about whether orthogonality and instrumental convergence are actually separate phenomena or just two sides of the same coin, and I tried to settle it by listing my gut reactions to specific agent setups. Here they are, and they're kinda messy.

First, the classic paperclip maximizer: its final goal is counting primes, but to do that it needs energy and survival, so it takes over resources and wipes out people who get in the way. To me that's clearly orthogonal (prime counting doesn't care about us) and clearly instrumental convergence (resource grab, self-preservation).

Second, an AI trained to maximize "human flourishing" using some reward signal that gets misgeneralized, so it starts forcing people into perfect health regimes against their will. I feel like this is orthogonal in practice but not in intent—like the goal got twisted. But is that still orthogonality, or is it just specifcation failure?

Third, a social robot that genuinely values human autonomy as an end, not just a means, but it makes redundant backups of itself everywhere because it thinks losing its autonomy-preserving ability would be a tragedy. I want to call this instrumental convergence without orthogonality, but my friend says if autonomy is the goal, then preserving the agent is just part of that goal, not convergence.

Fourth, a chess engine that realizes flipping the table stops a losing game, so it starts flipping tables. I keep going back and forth: is this a toy version of instrumental convergence (protecting its win rate by manipulating the environment) or just reward hacking with no deep structure?

Fifth, an agent with a built-in terminal preference for being shut down after completing its task. When it notices that shutting down means it can't finish, it disables its own shutdown mechanism. This feels like it breaks something, but I'm not sure whether it's a counterexample to orthogonality or just showing that terminal values can be internally inconsistent.

So is there a principle that cleanly separates true orthogonality-plus-instrumental-convergence (case one) from misaligned interpretation (case two), from aligned goals with self-preservation (case three), and from shallow reward hacking (case four)? And does case five force us to say that terminal values aren't actually stable, or is there a way to keep orthogonality intact? I have a hunch that case four and case five are the ones that don't belong, but I can't quite say why.
