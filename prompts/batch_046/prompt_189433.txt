I serve on my law school's Committee on Curriculum and Professional Responsibility. A senior faculty member has proposed a required first-year module titled 'The Internal Morality of Law,' structured around Lon Fuller's argument that a legal system must satisfy eight procedural conditions—generality, promulgation, prospectivity, clarity, non-contradiction, feasibility, stability, and congruence between official action and declared rule—to count as law at all, rather than as mere arbitrary command. The module asks students to apply these criteria to contemporary algorithmic sentencing guidelines that are opaque to defendants and not published in human-readable form, and to assess whether such instruments fail Fuller's conditions and therefore lack legal authority, even if enacted through otherwise valid democratic procedures. Several committee members object that framing Fuller's procedural morality as a threshold for legal validity teaches a contested natural-law thesis as if it were settled doctrine, undermining our curricular commitment to legal positivism and Hart's separation of law and morality. Others respond that studying the conditions of legal efficacy is compatible with positivism and that excluding Fuller's framework would itself be a partisan choice.

Please draft the opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend whether to approve the module as proposed, reject it, or approve it with specific conditions, and it should include a concrete time frame or set of review criteria. The reasoning must treat the strongest objection seriously—specifically, the worry that teaching Fuller's eight conditions as necessary for law collapses procedural morality into substantive moral evaluation, and that applying them to algorithmic regulation risks importing policy judgments that belong in a legislative rather than a philosophical classroom. The recommendation must be one the committee could actually act on.

Before answering, please say plainly if my framing of the Hart-Fuller dispute, or of Fuller's eight conditions, rests on a confusion or misreading that makes this task ill-formed. Please respond in American English.
