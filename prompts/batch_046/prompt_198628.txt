Each mode—e.g., syād asti, syād nāsti—is usually tied to a naya (standpoint), yielding a view where contradictory properties can inhere in one substance from different perspectives. I argue this is misread. Rather than mapping onto ontological states, the syād-operator should function as an epistemic scope-marker: “syād asti” means not “F is true of S in some respect,” but “F is predicable within the current epistemic frame without contradiction.” The seven modes then correspond not to seven ways a substance is, but to seven permissible scopes of predication—analogous to accessibility relations in modal logic, except “worlds” are replaced by partial cognitive perspectives. This avoids dialetheism: contradiction never occurs within a single frame, and the seventh mode signals incommensurability between frames rather than joint instantiation of F and not-F. The key question is whether Jain philosophy aims primarily at describing reality or at regulating cognition.
