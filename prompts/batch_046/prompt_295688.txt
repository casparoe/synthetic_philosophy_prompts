Taking Berkeley's monetary philosophy in The Querist seriously—where he treats coin and paper currency as non-intrinsic tokens or counters whose economic power rests on collective convention and public faith rather than on material substance—please set out and defend the most important implications of this tokenist view for contemporary philosophy of economics. Focus especially on (1) whether his framework offers a coherent ontology for fiat and digital currencies, in which value is sustained by institutional agreement without physical backing, and (2) whether his broader immaterialist metaphysics, which rejects mind-independent material reality, can consistently ground financial instruments as purely relational signs. Rank these lines of implication by their substantive philosophical weight, distinguishing those that genuinely reshape our concept of economic value from those that remain merely analogical, and explain what hangs on each.
