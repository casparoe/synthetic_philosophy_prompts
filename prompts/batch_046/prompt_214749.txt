For my independent philosophy seminar, I am examining whether the self is best understood as a center of narrative gravity—a fictional but organizing point around which a life story coheres—rather than as a substantial essence that persists independently of any story told about it. I need you to build a thought experiment to precise specifications. Design a realistic scenario, no longer than one paragraph, set in an ordinary workplace or family context with no science fiction, brain uploads, or supernatural devices. The case must engineer opposite verdicts from these two views: the narrative-gravity view and an essentialist-substance view of personal identity. Crucially, preserve full biological and psychological continuity—memory, personality, and bodily identity must all remain intact—while fracturing narrative coherence through an external structural change, specifically a mandatory institutional restorative-justice protocol that requires the protagonist to publicly adopt, and live within, an officially rewritten account of her own past actions that she actively rejects and cannot integrate into her first-person autobiography. After the scenario, demonstrate that the specifications are met: explain what functions as the center of gravity in your case, identify exactly which narrative threads are severed or relocated by the institutional framing, and show why the essentialist view still counts her as numerically identical despite the fracture. Then state honestly which feature does the work—the external authorship, the institutional enforcement, or the first-person integration gap—and explain what happens if you remove that feature, particularly whether the case collapses back into a standard psychological-continuity example. Finish by noting how a defender of the narrative-gravity view could attack the case as unfair, and how an essentialist defender could attack it from the opposite direction.
