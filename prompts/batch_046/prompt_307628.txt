I serve on a board rewriting the charter for our cooperative housing garden after disputes over water use. One faction argues we should only codify measurable obligations—gallons per plot, maintenance hours—because terms like "stewardship" or "belonging" don't describe real features of the land; they're just attitudes people project onto it. The other faction says those values are woven into the garden itself, and stripping them from the charter would change what the place actually is. I'm asked to advise whether the charter should include unenforceable value commitments. But I keep feeling that deciding whether values are "out there" or only part of how we organize our thinking already depends on the kind of charter we end up writing. How should I think about this? In your answer, please state how confident you are overall and point out the specific claim you are least sure about.
