As a novelist constructing long-form narratives around synthetic companions in persistent virtual worlds, I need a rigorous philosophical framework before I can write their moral arcs with consistency. I am not asking for a bibliography, a survey of the literature, or citations to specific theorists; I want you to derive the desiderata—the full specification—of what any adequate theory of moral status must deliver if it is to adjudicate whether video-game characters can be wronged in a genuinely non-instrumental sense.

Set out the complete requirements list. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or distinction it protects: for example, the difference between a character whose distress is merely represented to manipulate the player’s affect and one whose simulated preferences are systematically frustrated by gameplay mechanics; the distinction between harming a static, authored NPC and altering the procedural trajectory of an agent with persistent behavioral memory across save-states; and the boundary between representational content that evokes moral response and interactive responsiveness that generates direct obligations. Indicate which requirements are non-negotiable—such as consistency with our practices regarding real-world sentience or agency—and which are camp-specific preferences, like whether phenomenal consciousness is necessary or whether functional organization suffices.

Crucially, the theory must address emergent procedural companions: entities generated by system rules rather than explicit authorial scripting, whose behavioral histories depend on player interaction but who lack predetermined narrative arcs. The framework must either accommodate these agents within moral consideration or explain precisely why they fall outside it, without collapsing the distinction between authored and emergent agency. Then flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—for instance, the demand that moral status requires biological embodiment versus the demand that sustained interactive reciprocity alone can ground obligations.

Conclude by identifying the single requirement that, if enforced strictly, eliminates the broadest class of intuitive judgments about virtual characters, and briefly justify why it carries that exclusionary weight. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing theories.
