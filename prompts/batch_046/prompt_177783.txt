In debates about Douglas Hofstadter's MIU formal system and the MU-puzzle — specifically the task of recognizing that the string MU is not derivable within the system's rules — the idea of jumping out of the system (JOOTSing) has long been used to mark a distinctive human capacity: we apparently recognize that the rules preserve a numerical invariant, and therefore grasp the unreachability of the target, while any process confined to applying those rules cannot. Despite decades of discussion in philosophy of mind and logic, and despite formal verification methods that can automatically detect invariants and establish non-derivability, the disagreement over whether JOOTSing genuinely separates human cognition from formal computation has hardly shifted. My hypothesis is that the impasse is not about computational power, but about a hidden divergence over what recognizing the invariant requires. One side treats it as semantic interpretation — assigning numerical meaning to symbols and seeing the global pattern — while the other treats it as syntactic meta-reasoning that can be implemented in layered formal systems without leaving the domain of syntax. Is this diagnosis right? Is the dispute verbal (centered on the word understanding), substantive (about representational formats or the need for semantic grounding), or a case of two different questions — one psychological, about the phenomenology of insight, one logical, about the expressibility of meta-theorems — pursued under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a conceptual distinction that separates semantic grounding from syntactic reflection, a formal result about the representational resources needed for invariant detection in this particular puzzle, or an argument that the question is ill-posed because jumping out was never a well-defined cognitive category? I want an honest diagnosis supported by what each side actually says, together with an answer about whether anything — conceptual, formal, or empirical — could actually end the stalemate, not a survey of artificial intelligence or logic.
