I'm writing as secretary to the Methodological Standards Committee of the Global Harm Trends Consortium, a body that advises multilateral funding agencies on how to evaluate empirical claims about historical violence. The Committee must issue a binding recommendation for the 2026–2030 research cycle: should the Consortium treat "global violence is declining" as a well-formed empirical claim measurable through standardized per-capita metrics; should it reject unified measurement in favor of domain-specific indices that separate direct physical harm, structural deprivation, and institutional coercion; or should it require an explicit normative framework that defines violence prior to any statistical aggregation?

The Committee needs a formal opinion memo recommending one of these three positions, with a concrete action: which metric family should be mandatory in all funded studies, what conditions must a study meet to depart from it, and how reviewers should handle submissions that mix per-capita and absolute measures without justification. The memo must engage seriously with the strongest counter-argument—specifically, that selecting any unified metric implicitly commits to a contested theory of harm rather than tracking an objective historical fact, and therefore that there may be no fact of the matter about whether the world is becoming less violent at all. It should also specify what kind of conceptual or methodological evidence, available without specialized empirical data that only a statistical team could supply, would cause the Committee to revisit its recommendation within the next twenty-four months.
