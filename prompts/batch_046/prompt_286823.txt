Two members of a philosophy study group, Maya and Jonas, have been arguing about whether the cosmological fine-tuning argument succeeds. Maya said that the cosmological constant falling within a life-permitting range—often cited as roughly one part in 10^120—is analogous to hitting a specific atom with a dart; absent a multiverse or design, accepting this as mere coincidence seems irrational. Jonas replied that this commits the inverse gambler's fallacy: observing one successful outcome and inferring hidden trials, when in fact we should expect to observe constants compatible with our existence regardless of their prior rarity. Maya countered that she is not inferring past universes, but noting that even conditioning on observers, landing inside a range that occupies such a minute fraction of the presumed parameter space remains surprising unless a generating mechanism exists. Jonas said that this presumed parameter space relies on a uniform probability measure over constant values, for which physics offers no principled justification; alter the measure and the surprise vanishes. Maya then argued that, even granting measure ambiguity, the core issue survives: anthropic reasoning explains why we observe life-compatible values, but it does not explain why any life-compatible region exists, or why the actual value is not catastrophically worse for complexity. Jonas responded that this shifts the target: the original claim concerned the improbability of this specific range, which anthropic selection addresses; asking why physical laws permit complexity at all is a separate metaphysical question, not a specific argument for multiverse or design.

Please adjudicate their exchange move by move. For each intervention by Maya and Jonas, rate it as clearly right, partly right, clearly mistaken, or intermediate, with brief justification. Then state who is more right overall, identify any important omission shared by both, and specify the deeper crux—if there is one—that would move at least one side if resolved. Write your response in British English.
