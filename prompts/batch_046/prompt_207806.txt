For my advanced philosophy elective's capstone essay, I am developing an original argument about panpsychism and the combination problem. Specifically, I want to defend a position I am calling temporal-entrainment panpsychism: the claim that macro-conscious subjects arise not from micro-experiential units combining in physical space, but from those units achieving sustained resonant synchronization across neural and phenomenal time-scales, producing a unified field of experience without requiring that individual micro-subjects literally fuse or vanish. Please construct a rigorous argument that this temporal-entrainment model resolves the combination problem—particularly the subject-summing problem and the boundary problem—more effectively than standard spatial-fusion or aggregation accounts. Your argument should also anticipate and respond to one substantial objection, such as the charge that synchronization describes only functional correlation rather than genuine ontological unification of consciousness. Write in a tone appropriate for a serious high-school research paper: clear, structured, and philosophically precise, but without requiring specialized citation of particular scholars or texts.
