Write a joke that turns on the internal logic of expected-value fanaticism rather than on stereotypes about rationalists. The setup should present a modified St. Petersburg-style wager made during a departmental colloquium: each successive heads of a fair coin doubles both the promised cosmic payoff (preventing the heat death of a distant galaxy cluster) and the speaker's unavoidable social embarrassment (being required to deliver an increasingly absurd improvised poem to the audience). The punchline should hinge on the fanatic's precise reasoning—that the exponentially decaying probability of reaching any given round is fully compensated by the exponentially growing utility at stake, making endurance of the mounting humiliation rationally mandatory regardless of the audience's reaction. Avoid generic "nerds are awkward" tropes; the humor should arise specifically from the clash between this calculus and ordinary social norms.
