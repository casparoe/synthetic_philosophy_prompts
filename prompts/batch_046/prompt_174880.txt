Please argue that luck egalitarianism should abandon the standard brute-luck and option-luck dichotomy in favor of a three-part taxonomy comprising natural lottery, structural network positioning, and volitional risk exposure. Maintain that only the first two categories warrant egalitarian correction, but that they call for different remedies: direct compensation for natural lottery deficits, and institutional redesign of access rules for structural network positioning. Further defend the claim that this bifurcation resolves an internal difficulty in Dworkin-style resource egalitarianism, namely that socially embedded positional advantages—such as being embedded in high-trust information networks—are treated in the auction model as internal preferences or talents, when they actually operate as externalities that individual bidding mechanisms cannot price. Show that adopting this taxonomy strengthens Cohen's critique of resource metrics without collapsing into a full relational egalitarianism that demands democratic equality of status across all social interactions.
