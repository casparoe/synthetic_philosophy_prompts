I am helping organize an eighteen-month working group of political philosophers and constitutional lawyers who must draft normative guidelines for oversight institutions governing automated decision systems—such as algorithmic welfare eligibility, predictive policing, or credit scoring. The group has limited time and needs to avoid both popular platitudes and purely academic puzzles that do not bear on institutional design. I want them to concentrate on the live conceptual fault lines among positive, negative, and republican conceptions of liberty, but specifically as these apply to what I will call epistemic dependency: situations where an agent’s choices are shaped not by direct coercion or resource deprivation, but by structurally asymmetric control over the informational environment that frames their options. This phenomenon sits awkwardly between negative freedom (absence of direct interference), positive freedom (capacity for self-mastery), and republican freedom (absence of arbitrary domination), yet it is central to how automated systems actually constrain people in practice.

Given this framing, what are the three to five most important open conceptual questions at the intersection of liberty theory and these non-standard domination structures? Rank them by how much they should command the working group’s limited research effort over the next year and a half. For each, explain: (i) why it is upstream of other issues in the group’s mandate, (ii) how tractable it is for philosophers and lawyers rather than computer scientists or economists, (iii) what specific conceptual clarification or institutional-design insight it could yield, and (iv) what kind of argument or empirical finding over the next six months would cause you to demote or promote it. I am less interested in a literature survey than in a defended agenda: make explicit the criteria you are using—relevance to oversight design, tractability within the group’s expertise, and neglect by current policy discourse—and show where these criteria conflict with one another.

Please present your reasoning in a coherent essay of roughly three to four paragraphs. After that, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON array of objects, ordered by rank, with the following fields: "rank" (integer), "question_title" (string), "core_issue" (string summarizing the open question), "priority_rationale" (string), "tractability_assessment" (string), "institutional_relevance" (string), "revisability_condition" (string describing what evidence or argument would change its rank), and "criteria_tension_noted" (string noting any conflict between importance and tractability for this item).
