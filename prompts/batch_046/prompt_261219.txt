i'm a compatibilist, so determinism doesn't scare me and i think moral responsability doesn't need libertarian free will. i accept nagel's four-way taxonomy but i only think resultant luck is genuinely problematic; circumstantial and constitutive luck are basically epistemic noise. i also think omissions are blameworthy only when there's a specific prior duty of care, not just a general ability to help. at the same time i don't see a deep moral asymmetry between action and omission — if your mental state is the same, the blame should be the same. recently i've bought into this idea of counterfactual robustness: you're responsible only if you'd have done the wrong thing across a range of nearby possible worlds. and i think constitutive luck is a myth because character is revisable over time with effort.

there's also something i've started calling moral luck by omission — you fail to prevent a harm you couldn't have foresawn, but the failure traces back to a personality trait you never chose. i classify that as resultant luck, not constitutive.
