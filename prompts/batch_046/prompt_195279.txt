I am an undergraduate philosophy student preparing the defense portion of my term-paper presentation in an advanced philosophy-of-mind seminar. The thesis I am defending is that Kim’s causal exclusion argument fails once we reject its tacit assumption that causal efficacy must be assigned at exactly one ontological level. Specifically, I argue that mental properties should be understood not as competing with their neural realizers for the production of behavior, but as multi-scale organizational constraints: they specify the boundary conditions and informational architecture within which neural dynamics are causally productive of action, analogous to how the shape of a container constrains fluid flow without competing with molecular forces. On this view, mental causation is a form of structural or enabling causation rather than efficient competition, so there is neither overdetermination nor epiphenomenalism, and closure of the physical is preserved because every token efficient cause remains physical.

The audience will be my seminar instructor—who specializes in mental causation and is openly skeptical of non-reductive physicalism—plus about twelve graduate and advanced undergraduate students, several of whom have just studied Kim’s Mind in a Physical World and are persuaded by the exclusion problem, and one visiting student who works on interventionist causation in neuroscience but is wary of applying it to mental properties. They care about whether non-reductive physicalism can survive without mysterious downward causation, and they will press me on whether my “constraint” move collapses into epiphenomenalism or into a form of reductionism I want to avoid.

Please prepare me for the objections I will actually face, in roughly the order they will come, phrased in the language each interlocutor would use. For each objection, tell me what I should concede, what I should hold firm on, and where my reply is weakest. Include the one objection I am hoping nobody raises—specifically, the challenge that my organizational-constraint model cannot distinguish genuine mental causation from the causal relevance of purely mathematical or geometric descriptions, and therefore either proves too much or renders mental properties causally inert after all. Finally, identify the weakest point in my own position that I should prepare to defend rather than hope they overlook.
