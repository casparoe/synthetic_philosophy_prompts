Write a deadpan, satirical FAQ document—approximately 650 words, formatted like a municipal government webpage—for the fictional city-state of Zornopolis, which has adopted the Axiom of Choice (AC) as its exclusive principle of resource allocation and urban planning. The page should answer practical resident questions—how bus routes are assigned, how public housing is distributed, how water rights are partitioned, and what administrative recourse exists if a resident’s apartment is subjected to a Banach-Tarski-style decomposition and reassembly—while treating AC not as a metaphor but as an actual bureaucratic mechanism. The satire should derive from the mathematics being taken literally: reference the fact that AC asserts the existence of a choice function selecting one element from each member of an arbitrary family of non-empty sets without requiring any constructive rule or explicit description of the selection; contrast this with Dependent Choice (DC), which permits only sequential selections and is therefore administratively tractable but rejected by Zornopolis as insufficiently comprehensive; allude to the well-ordering theorem and to Zorn’s Lemma as the legal foundations for municipal hierarchy; and depict the resulting absurdities—residents unable to verify how their allocations were made, officials defending distributions that cannot be computed or even described, and a public-works department that insists every set of tasks has a maximal element despite no ordering being visible to citizens. Include at least one invented resident testimonial rendered in bureaucratic deadpan confusion. The voice must remain consistently deadpan: no explicit moralizing, no winking at the reader. After the FAQ, append a brief authorial note of about 100 words that separates what is mathematically settled (the equivalence of AC, Zorn’s Lemma, and the well-ordering theorem in ZF; the non-constructive character of general choice functions; the logical consistency of Banach-Tarski with AC) from what is genuinely contested or interpretively open—specifically, whether AC’s non-constructivity represents a genuine epistemic limitation or merely an ontological permissiveness, whether intuitionist rejections of AC depend on a prior commitment to predicative definability, and to what extent the Banach-Tarski paradox can be given any physical or administrative analog without distortion. The target of the satire should be the rhetorical practice of presenting highly abstract, existence-only mathematical principles as if they were transparent, operational governance tools, not any named individual. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not quote any real text verbatim.
