hey so i'm trying to sort out my actual intuitions on precommitment and rational intention, using toxin-style cases with a few twists. here are my gut verdicts:

1. classic setup: a billionaire offers me $1M if tonight i sincerly intend to drink a painful toxin tomorow morning. once the cash is in my account, i have no reason to drink. verdict: i don't think i can actually form that sincere intention, since i know my future self will bail. so i shouldn't bother, and if i somehow managed it, it still wouldn't be rational.

2. collective version: ten people each have their own toxin, and we all get paid only if every single one actually drinks. verdict: here i think we should all commit and follow through. the group structure changes something, and i'd probably drink.

3. brain-scan version: he can scan my brain tonight to verify the intention is genuine, but he can't observe me tomorrow. verdict: i should form the real intention tonight, take the money, and skip the drink tomorrow. the scan checks my mental state, not my action, so there's no contradiction.

4. underestimated harm: same deal, but tomorrow the toxin hurts way more than expected. verdict: my past self was wrong to bind me, and future-me is right to refuse. commitment based on bad information shouldn't hold.

5. gradual drip: instead of one drink, i take one drop every hour for 24 hours, each slightly worse. i can stop anytime, but payout only comes if i finish. verdict: it's totally rational to start, do maybe six hours, then quit and lose the money. partial follow-through feels okay.

there's clear tension between 1 (can't genuinely intend if you'll bail) and 3 (should genuinely intend and then bail). also 2 says collective commitment demands full follow-through, but 5 says partial quitting is fine solo. is there a single principle that captures all five, or do i need to drop one? and what does it say about the case i keep going back and forth on -- the toxin doesn't hurt me, but creates a temporary clone who suffers in my place? i can't decide if that makes it easier or harder to commit.
