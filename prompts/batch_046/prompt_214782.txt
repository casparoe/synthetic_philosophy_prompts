Consider the historiographical and philosophical problem of treating non-human forces—pathogens, long-term climate regimes, and volcanic events—as "historical actors" rather than mere background conditions. The risk is environmental determinism: attributing large-scale demographic or political transformations primarily to biological or geological causes, thereby flattening human social mediation, institutional adaptation, and contingency. At the same time, accounts that treat nature solely as a passive backdrop risk ignoring the genuine causal efficacy, temporal rhythms, and constraint structures that microbes, climate systems, and eruptions impose on human affairs.
