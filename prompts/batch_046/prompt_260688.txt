I am composing the final examination for an upper-level graduate seminar in metaontology and the metaphysics of science, and I need a single essay question that cannot be answered by reciting standard textbook summaries of verbal disputes. The question should present students with a specific, non-canonical case study: a disagreement between two theoretical ecologists, one of whom asserts that ecosystem resilience denotes a genuinely emergent, causally efficacious property of the system as a whole, and the other of whom insists that the term is merely a convenient predictive abbreviation for complex, disaggregated interactions among individual organisms and abiotic factors. Students must determine whether this dispute is verbal in the sense articulated by metaontological deflationists—drawing specifically on the distinction between quantifier variance, conceptual divergence, and empirical underdetermination—rather than a substantive metaphysical disagreement. The prompt should require them to apply at least two diagnostic frameworks, such as Hirsch-style charitable reinterpretation and Sider-style ontological commitment via best-theory quantification, to the ecological case, and to address the specific challenge that both parties share identical predictive commitments and observational bases, which might suggest the dispute is purely semantic or, conversely, that ontology is systematically underdetermined by evidence. Please provide the exam question itself, a model answer of roughly 650 to 750 words that takes a definite position—for example, defending either that the dispute is verbal once predictive and representational commitments are disentangled, or that it remains substantive because emergent causation introduces a non-quantifier-based ontological difference—and a detailed marking scheme that separates an A-grade response, which must correctly deploy two frameworks, diagnose the empirical-overlap problem without conflating it with underdetermination, and defend a non-obvious verdict, from a B-grade response that correctly defines the frameworks but applies them only to familiar mereological examples and treats the ecological case as an afterthought. The exam is ninety minutes long and this question carries forty percent of the final mark.
