I serve as the senior evaluation lead for a museum of contemporary craft that has recently begun reviewing proposals for "variation installations" — works presented not as replicas but as claimed creative transformations of canonical design objects, such as a Shaker chair or a Bauhaus lamp. Our board requires a reusable audit procedure, grounded in Douglas Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model of conceptual creativity, that my team can apply in roughly twenty minutes per proposal without philosophical training. The procedure should treat creativity as the adjustment of dimensions — form, material logic, functional purpose, and cultural reference — within a conceptual space, but it must also incorporate a distinction drawn from our recent internal seminar: "surface knob-turning," in which visible attributes shift while the underlying relational constraints remain untouched, versus "deep knob-turning," in which the rules governing interactions between dimensions are themselves reconfigured.

Please construct a sequential checklist with explicit decision nodes: for each of the four dimensions, what observable evidence counts as an adjustment; a test to distinguish surface from deep turning; a clear termination point where the proposal is rejected for lacking genuine variation; and a designated hand-off point where the analysis ends and a curator must make a value judgment about whether the transformation honors or betrays the source object's identity. Then apply the procedure to three proposals: one that changes finish and scale but preserves all joint logic and functional relations (the answer should be a rejection); one that dissolves the structural grammar of the chair entirely while retaining its material palette, yielding an object that no longer functions as furniture (this should trigger the hand-off); and an ambiguous case involving algorithmic digital fabrication, where parameterized variation produces forms that read as surface adjustments to experts but as deep reconfigurations to lay viewers, leaving the procedure unsettled. Be explicit about where Hofstadter's original framework, developed for abstract conceptual spaces, remains silent on material culture and embodied craft knowledge, and state plainly what this audit cannot do.
