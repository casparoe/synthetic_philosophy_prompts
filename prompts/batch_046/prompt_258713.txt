I'm part of a worker-owned cooperative that makes decisions through a protocol in which we first submit preliminary preferences, then see a provisional outcome, and finally adjust our individual weightings before a binding vote is taken. I have started using this mechanism to commit myself to options I would otherwise avoid—such as accepting a lower-margin but ecologically sound contract—because my immediate impulse is always to favour the more profitable choice. My partner argues that I am not genuinely directing my life but have merely constructed an external apparatus that systematically overrules my actual preferences; she says that real self-direction requires one's first-order choices not to be overturned by any endorsed procedure. I feel, by contrast, that I am more truly in charge when my actions align with values I stand behind upon reflection, even if a mechanical protocol enforces that alignment. Is there a meaningful distinction between freedom as the unimpeded exercise of immediate preference and freedom as governance by a reflectively endorsed framework, particularly when that framework operates independently of momentary desire? And in practical terms, does relying on this protocol expand my authorship of what I do, or diminish it?
