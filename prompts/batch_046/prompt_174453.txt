I'm grading a mid-level philosophy exam on Hegel's Phenomenology of Spirit, specifically the master-slave dialectic and the problem of recognition. The exam asks students to explain why the master's recognition is defective, how the slave's labor addresses this, and then to apply the structure to a contemporary case: content creators who depend on platform algorithms they do not own, where the platform extracts value without labor while creators gradually master the distribution mechanics.

Here is one student's answer (paraphrased in my notes):

"In the Phenomenology, Hegel shows that recognition must be mutual to have value. The master risks his life and defeats the other, turning him into a slave. But because the slave is not a free consciousness, the master's recognition is hollow—he is recognized by something he treats as a thing. Meanwhile, the slave works on nature and transforms it, which gives him an objective existence and self-certainty independent of the master. This is essentially the same as Marx's idea of alienation: the slave is alienated from the product of his labor, which the master consumes. Over time, the slave's technical mastery lets him overthrow the master, achieving true mutual recognition. In the creator-economy case, influencers are the slaves and platforms are the masters. The influencers produce content that platforms monetize, but because influencers learn to game the algorithm, they gain the same kind of independent being-for-self that Hegel describes, eventually making the platform's power obsolete."
