I am writing a position paper for my course on Dostoevsky and political philosophy, and I want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I have landed:

(1) The Grand Inquisitor’s diagnosis is largely correct: given the choice between material security with authority and the anxiety of moral self-determination, most people will consistently take the exchange, and the burden of freedom is not one they genuinely want to bear.

(2) Still, freedom—understood specifically as the capacity for self-authorship rather than mere negative liberty—has intrinsic value that happiness cannot compensate for; this is precisely why the Inquisitor’s offer is a corruption rather than a liberation.

(3) At the same time, if an individual reflectively endorses their own subordination—say, by entering a totalizing institution or accepting a paternalistic arrangement that decides moral questions for them—then overriding that preference violates the very autonomy that (2) celebrates.

(4) I also think it is wrong to coerce people into exercising self-authorship; forcing someone to bear the burden of freedom produces alienation, not liberation.

(5) Finally, I hold that a society should be evaluated partly by whether its members genuinely could have lived otherwise, not merely by whether they report satisfaction with the lives they actually have.

Are these jointly tenable? I see a possible tension between (2) and (3): if self-authorship is intrinsically valuable, allowing someone to abandon it seems to treat it as instrumentally disposable. I also worry that (4) and (5) pull apart—if we refuse to push people toward freedom, but we demand that real alternatives remain institutionally available, then widespread voluntary submission might erode the very conditions for choice over time. Meanwhile, there may be an apparent conflict between (1) and (2) that dissolves once descriptive psychology is separated from normative evaluation. Please audit the set: rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs, identify any tension that only looks real, and tell me which single change would clean up the most conflicts without abandoning my core intuition that freedom matters more than happiness.
