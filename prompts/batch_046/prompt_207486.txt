i've come to think that etiological accounts of biological function are right, but only for traits where the selection history is causally coupled to the current maintenance mechanism — call these entrenched functions. for everything else, like synthetic organs or plasticity from deep brain stimulation, the etiological story decouples and causal-role accounts take over, except then they cant tell dysfunction from statistical weirdness without smuggling in normative history. so my hybrid view is: proper function is etiological when maintenance and origin are coupled, causal-role when theyre not, and the line is whether the trait's current role is developmentally locked-in.

what would count against this? skip the standard heart-pumps-blood vs selected-for-pumping debate. i want the hard cases. what if theres a trait where origin and maintenance stay coupled but the current causal role shifts completely and the organism thrives? or synthetic biology cases where we design a trait with zero evolutionary history but maintenance is immediately coupled to our design intentions — does my view wrongly deny it proper function? also, have i qualified the etiological side so heavily that it cant be falsified? if every decoupled case just gets kicked to the causal-role bin, is the etiological part doing any real work?

sort the defeaters: fatal, awkward, or just annoying. say which ones a defender of this coupled/decoupled split would actually concede, and be honest about whether the view is well-insulated — and if thats a problem for a claim of this kind.
