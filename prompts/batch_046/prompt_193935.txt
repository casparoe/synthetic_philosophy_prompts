A fellow student in my philosophy of war seminar offered what she called the 'symmetric liability-waiver thesis' as a sharp reconstruction of the moral equality of combatants, the view standardly linked to Michael Walzer. According to her summary, enlistment functions as a bilateral contract: by donning the uniform, a soldier transfers her individual right not to be killed by enemy combatants to the opposing state, and in return receives an identical license to kill opposing soldiers. Because this transfer is governed solely by combatant status—not by whether the cause is just—the soldier fighting for an aggressor acquires precisely the same moral permission to kill as the soldier fighting in self-defense. Consequently, unjust combatants are not merely excused for killing; they are positively permitted to do so, and all moral blame for wrongful death attaches exclusively to the political leaders who initiated the war. She further claimed that this moral symmetry explains why the Geneva Conventions mandate identical treatment for prisoners regardless of which side captured them: international law recognizes that combatant status, not the justice of the cause, determines moral rights in war.

The formulation sounds more rigorous than the usual textbook gloss, yet I suspect it conflates being permitted to kill with being liable to be killed, and it seems to transform Walzer's practical separation of jus ad bellum and jus in bello into a metaphysical rights-waiver. Is this summary fair? Please distinguish what accurately reflects the standard defense of moral equality, what is an acceptable simplification, and what is a substantive misrepresentation—particularly regarding whether unjust soldiers are genuinely permitted rather than non-culpable, whether enlistment operates as a contractual waiver of rights, and whether the Geneva Conventions can be derived from the moral thesis. Also, assess whether her conclusion about prisoner treatment actually follows from the moral claim, or whether it rests on an independent legal convention.

Please answer in American English. In your response, please indicate what would change your assessment—such as a textual finding showing Walzer endorses a contractual rights-transfer, or an argument that collapses the liability-permission distinction in exactly her way—and identify where your reasoning is most vulnerable to error.
