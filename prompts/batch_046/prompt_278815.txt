Write a research proposal for an advanced term paper or small dissertation prospectus (approximately 6000 words, one semester) addressing the following problem: Is there a non-arbitrary threshold of material continuity below which a restored original artwork forfeits its privileged evaluative and aesthetic status relative to a perfect, non-causally-linked replica? Frame the inquiry around the tension between the causal-history account of artistic value—according to which contact with the artist’s hand is essential—and the institutional-pragmatic account, which holds that an object inherits its authority through conservation norms and social recognition regardless of how much original substance remains. Your proposal should articulate the precise research question: specifically, whether the widespread intuition that a heavily restored Old Master painting remains authentic while an indistinguishable studio copy is a forgery can survive if we suppose that ninety percent or more of the visible surface is modern conservation material. Describe the methodological approach: a conceptual analysis of three paired comparisons—(1) a painting restored to near-total material replacement versus a high-fidelity physical facsimile; (2) a reconstructed classical sculpture assembled from fragments versus a complete modern cast; and (3) a digitally rendered perfect replica displayed in place of the original—examining in each case whether the asymmetry in our evaluative response tracks metaphysical identity, causal provenance, or normative institutional inheritance. State the possible conclusions clearly: either that the asymmetry is robust but grounded in social practice rather than in material continuity, or that it collapses once material replacement exceeds an unspecified critical point, rendering restoration and replication conceptually continuous. Identify which considerations would adjudicate between these outcomes—such as whether we can coherently maintain that the restored object possesses properties the copy lacks without invoking circular appeals to authenticity. Finally, provide an honest scope assessment: acknowledge that simultaneously resolving the metaphysical, normative, and aesthetic dimensions risks exceeding a single semester, specify which dimension will take priority if the project must be narrowed, and indicate what form of failure—e.g., an unresolved ambiguity about where the threshold lies—would still constitute a meaningful result rather than a mere dead end. Do not include a bibliography, literature review, or citation list; reference theoretical positions and arguments in general terms, and note that specific sources will be integrated separately.
