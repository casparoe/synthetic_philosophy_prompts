I am evaluating normative frameworks for welfare assessment in farmed aquatic animals, and I want to know what I should require of one before I take it seriously. Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must respect, the empirical findings it must be consistent with, and the conceptual puzzles it must dissolve or accommodate—specifically for organisms whose nervous systems range from centralized brains (as in many teleost fish) to distributed neural architectures (as in decapod crustaceans). For each desideratum, state the concrete case or consideration behind it: for example, why the absence of a withdrawal reflex in a crustacean should not be treated as decisive evidence against suffering, or why stress-cortisol levels in high-density salmon tanks might diverge systematically from behavioral indicators of distress. Mark which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable—such as consistency with current comparative neurobiology and the avoidance of ungrounded anthropomorphism—and which reflect merely contingent methodological preferences of particular camps, like privileging directly observable behavior over physiological markers or insisting on a single scalar measure of welfare across all species. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable given present empirical limits. Then, briefly, score three existing approaches against the list: a behavioral-ecological proxy model that extends mammalian welfare indicators by analogy; a neurofunctional analogical framework that infers affective states from structural homology; and a proposed "relational sentience" view that treats welfare not as an intrinsic organism-centered property but as a relation between an animal's distributed cognitive architecture and its specific environment. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard, and say which single desideratum does the most work in excluding inadequate candidates.
