If Hobsbawm demonstrates that national traditions are largely modern inventions ritualised through state practice, and Anderson defines nations as imagined communities that spread through modular replication rather than organic growth, then national identity appears to be a collectively maintained fiction—functionally comparable to sustaining belief in a shared myth. Given that these constructed origins are now well documented, why hasn't academic demystification led to a decline in nationalist attachment? Does its persistence indicate a kind of wilful false consciousness, or does treating "invented" and "imagined" as synonymous with "illusory" commit a category error about how social realities are constituted?
