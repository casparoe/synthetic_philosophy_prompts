As a retired physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy of social science, I find Weber's thesis linking Calvinist asceticism to the spirit of capitalism conceptually puzzling. Weber describes the relation not as mechanical causation but as an elective affinity. Compare Weber's notion of elective affinity with the contemporary philosophy-of-science account of causal mechanisms—organized entities and activities arranged to produce phenomena. Specifically, can a cultural value, such as the psychological anxiety generated by the doctrine of predestination, function as a component within a mechanism in the same ontological sense as a physical part, or does Weber's framework require a sui generis form of causation? Contrast both views with a Humean regularity theory. Which framework best allows culture to be genuinely causal without reducing values to epiphenomena of material conditions? In your answer, distinguish whether Weber treats values as efficacious in an interpretive-meaning-conferring sense, in an ontologically robust sense, or in some hybrid mode that a natural scientist ought to take seriously.
