People always compare Mencius and Xunzi with the water-and-wood analogy -- Mencius says human nature flows downward to good like water unless you dam it, Xunzi says it's crooked wood that needs steaming and bending. But I'm wondering if that analogy actualy hides more than it shows, especially if you take seriously Xunzi's claim that ritual propriety (li) isn't just external force but more like grafting onto an existing root-stock. Audit the analogy: what maps onto what, where the mapping breaks once you treat Xunzi's view of li as internal to human social nature rather than pure imposition, and whether the usual conclusion that Xunzi is 'pessimistic' about human nature survives that correction. Also, does the analogy smuggle in a modern idea of an 'authentic self' that neither philosopher held?
