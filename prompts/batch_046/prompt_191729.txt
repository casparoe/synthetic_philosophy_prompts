Write a formal dialogue between two equally competent historiographers, Dr. Elena Vasquez and Dr. Marcus Chen, on whether historians can ever escape presentism. Vasquez advocates for calibrated presentism: historians should explicitly apply modern analytical categories—such as extractive institutional logic—to past societies, but then subject those applications to a recursive back-translation into period-specific ontologies, making the presentist bias transparent and correctable. Chen defends ontological suspension, contending that any such calibration still smuggles modern normative commitments—including the very distinction between extractive and reciprocal relations—into the historical record, and that true rigor requires bracketing all modern evaluative categories, even if this renders moral judgment impossible. The dialogue should examine a concrete case—the labor restrictions of fifteenth-century Venetian glassmaking guilds—with each speaker responding directly to the other's actual arguments, and neither side presented as a strawman.
