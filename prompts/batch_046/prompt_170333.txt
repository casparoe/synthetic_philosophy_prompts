ok so im a phd student in philosophy of science (mostly working on climate modeling and econ methods) and for my seminar im writing up my stance on Friedmans F-twist and unrealistic assumptions. i want a consistency check before i present. heres roughly where ive landed:

(1) i accept the F-twist for predictive sciences - unrealistic assumptions dont matter if the predictions come out right, and asking for realistic microfoundations is mostly a category mistake.

(2) but i also think that in policy-relevant domains like climate science, predictive accuracy isnt enough; you need counterfactual stability, meaning the model should still hold up if you intervene on emissions or land-use in ways that arent in the training data.

(3) to square that with (1), i want to say climate models achieve counterfactual stability through modular insulation - the unrealistic bits (like parameterized cloud feedback) are quarantined in auxilliary sub-modules that dont corrupt the core dynamics, so theyre predictive and policy-relevant without being literally true.

(4) i also hold that evolutionary population genetics is different: its unrealistic assumptions (infinite populations, random mating) arent insulated; theyre baked into the derivations themselves. so evolutionary models cant claim modular insulation, and that means they should be judged purely by Friedman-style prediction. but they rarely deliver novel predictions, so they come out worse than climate models.

(5) and finally i think that whenever modular insulation is present, the unrealistic assumptions are causally inert with respect to the core outputs, which means they cant be part of the explanatory story - but thats ok because (1) already said explanation isnt required.

do these hang together? im especially worried about (2) vs (1): if i say climate science needs counterfactual stability, am i smuggling in an explanatory demand that Friedman rejects? and does (3) contradict (5) - if the unrealistic modules are causally inert, how do they help deliver counterfactual stability in (2)? also is (4) consistent with my claim that insulation saves explanatory power, since i seem to be saying evolutionary models lack both insulation and explanation but also lack predictive success? rank the tensions by severity and say what the least costly revision is. please dont just list every possible problem; tell me which ones are real and which only look like tensions.
