I was discussing with my friends the other day why people still sign contracts with heavy cancellation fees even when they know they could just break the contract and pay less, and someone mentioned a philosopher's story about a hitchhiker who has to convince a driver he'll pay after being dropped off, even though once he's dropped off he has no reason to actually hand over the money. The twist, as I understand it, is that if the driver is perfectly rational and knows the hitchhiker will also be rational later, the driver won't pick him up at all—so the hitchhiker needs to somehow make himself irrational, or at least appear credibly bound, before the ride starts. I find this pattern everywhere: subscription services with annual lock-ins, wedding deposits, even putting my phone in another room to avoid checking it.

Write a joke that turns specifically on that ex-ante versus ex-post rationality gap in the hitchhiker scenario—not just a generic joke about breaking promises or being cheap. The punchline should depend on the idea that cooperation requires one party to commit to future irrationality, and that a perfectly rational agent can never credibly make that commitment without some external binding mechanism. You could set it in an unexpected modern frame—perhaps a ride-share algorithm that calculates trust scores, or a blockchain smart contract for hitchhiking—but the philosophical mechanics should be recognizable to someone who knows the argument. I don't need citations or a reading list; I'm just trying to see if the concept can survive being translated into humor without losing its structural point.
