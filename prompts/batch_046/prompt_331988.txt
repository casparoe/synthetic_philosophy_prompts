Write an essay on whether we can ever actually know why some nations are rich and others stay poor, given that history only gives us correlation and no clean experiments. Weigh institutions against geography and culture, and take seriously the causal-inference problem—we cant re-run the colonization of the Americas with swapped rules. Then consider the idea of institutional shadowing, where a poor country copies the formal laws of a rich one but the informal norms stay stuck, so the copied rules do nothing. Does that mean institutions are just culture wearing a tie, or can we still pry them apart? Reach a verdict: is the institutions-vs-geography debate permanently underdetermined by historical data, or is there a principled way to settle it? Defend your answer.
