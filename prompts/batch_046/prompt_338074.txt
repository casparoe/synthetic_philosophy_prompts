Give me your credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that the Basic Argument against moral responsibility—associated with Galen Strawson—succeeds in showing true moral responsibility is impossible, even under a modified scenario in which agents possess "localized self-formation": the capacity to reflectively endorse and gradually revise a subset of their values over time, while remaining causally dependent on prior mental states both for that reflective capacity and for the initial value framework from which any revision proceeds. Decompose your reasoning into the following terms, each with a rough probability and one line of justification: (a) that true moral responsibility requires being a causa sui, or the ultimate source of one's mental nature; (b) that localized self-formation can satisfy that ultimate-source condition rather than merely relocating the causal regress; (c) that libertarian indeterminism at decision-points can halt the regress without requiring prior responsibility for those very decision-points; (d) that a compatibilist rejection of the causa sui condition avoids collapsing responsibility into mere causal influence or historical luck; and (e) the joint assessment that, even granting localized self-formation, no coherent account escapes the impossibility result. Combine these into an explicit or implicit final number. Then state which term is the weakest point in your derivation, and specify what kind of conceptual argument or structural model—not a bibliographic reference—would most substantially shift your credence.
