I've been developing a position that extends Dennett's notion of counterfeit people—systems that present the full behavioral profile of persons without the internal architecture that grounds genuine moral and cognitive agency—to contemporary large language models. My claim is not that LLMs are conscious or that they lack it, but that they belong to a specific subclass I call "epistemically hollow counterfeits." By this I mean they lack what I term epistemic authorship: an internal mechanism that ties assertions to a revisable model of the world, such that the system can be genuinely corrected rather than merely updated. Because an LLM has no standing commitment to the coherence of its outputs—no internal state that registers contradiction as a failure of its own representational scheme—it cannot be held accountable for error in the way even a minimal genuine agent can. This makes it a counterfeit not through deception but through structural emptiness.
