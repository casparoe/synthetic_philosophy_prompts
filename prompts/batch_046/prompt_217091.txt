Please argue for the following interpretation of anatta within the Abhidharma framework. Rather than treating the denial of self as a blanket negation of all continuity, interpret it as asserting that personal identity is constituted solely by an asymmetric dependency relation between successive mental moments: each citta inherits the karmic potential and intentional orientation—cetana—of its predecessor through a direct causal link that requires no persisting substrate or owner. Label this structure inheritance without substrate. From this premise, derive that such directional transmission is sufficient to ground both the continuity required for moral responsibility and the impermanence that makes attachment to self harmful, without invoking a fictional narrative agent or a substantial soul. In particular, address the objection that if no subject undergoes the results of past action, then karmic retribution becomes causally arbitrary. I am asking for a rigorous conceptual argument rather than a historical survey of texts. If, however, my framing mischaracterizes the Abhidharma analysis of citta and cetasika, or if the task rests on a confused premise—such as importing an anachronistic metaphysics of causation—please say so plainly rather than constructing the argument anyway.
