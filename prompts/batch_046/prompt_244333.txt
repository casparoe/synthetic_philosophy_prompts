Analyze the Thermidorian coup in July 1794 — Robespierre getting dragged off and the Committee of Public Safety falling apart basically overnight — through the lens of the idea that revolutions devour their children. Map the actual players (the deputies still in the room, the remaining CPS members, the Paris sections that stayed quiet), thier options once Robespierre started reading his list of accused, and how the Law of 22 Prairial had built this preemptive-denunciation trap where silence was riskier than joining the shout. Then ask whether the "devouring" thesis really explains the timing, or whether it misses the contingency — Saint-Just being absent, Fouché organizing in the Plain, and half the deputies privately convinced they were on Robespierre's unpublished list. Finally, what does the episode do to the thesis? Is it confirmation that revolutions necessarily cannibalize, or does it show the thesis is too teleological and ignores the bureaucratic entropy that made the CPS vulnerable to a parliamentary ambush in the first place?
