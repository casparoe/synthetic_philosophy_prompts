I run a grad-student philosophy workshop, about 10 people, 90 minutes. Design an activity that gets them to actually feel the pull of Moore's proof of an external world, not just dismiss it as a joke. I want you to inject a kind of niche twist: the group should have to apply Moore-style reasoning to both ordinary physical hands and to VR-mediated "hands" they can only interact with through haptic gloves, so they discover for themselves where the proof's force lives and where it breaks. Set it up as a structured deliberation with assigned roles, rules for who bears the burden of proof at each stage, a timeline, and small stakes (maybe they vote on which cases count as "known"). Then a debrief that maps their moments of resistance or acceptance onto parts of Moore's argument, says what will predictably go wrong the first time, and is frank about which feature of the proof—probably its direct perceptual entitlement—this setup cant fully reproduce. No lecture up front, dont ask for citations or a reading list.
