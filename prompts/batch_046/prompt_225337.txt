You are assisting a philosopher in preparing for the question period following a seminar defense of attention-gated illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. The speaker's position is that there is no metaphysically robust phenomenal consciousness; rather, the appearance of intrinsic qualitative character is selectively generated by the attentional system's self-model when it represents its own selection processes as possessing non-representational what-it-is-likeness. The speaker contends that this localized error explains the empirical correlation between attention and conscious report without requiring consciousness to be real, and that it avoids the global introspective skepticism facing standard illusionism because the misrepresentation is confined to attentional meta-representation rather than distributed across all experience. The core argument holds that attentional selection is representationally transparent to itself except at the meta-level, where it misattributes intrinsic quality to its outputs, and that this is empirically testable via paradigms such as attentional blink, where absence of report should correlate with absence of the illusion rather than absence of consciousness.

The audience is a philosophy of mind seminar comprising eight faculty members and twelve graduate students. The majority are representationalists—mostly higher-order and self-representational theorists—along with two cognitive scientists who study attentional mechanisms, one specializing in attentional blink and the other in predictive processing in vision. They are skeptical of eliminativism about consciousness but take representationalism seriously. They care about empirical consistency with attention research, explanatory parsimony, and avoiding ad hoc stipulation. One faculty member is a prominent defender of phenomenal intentionality; another is known for arguing that illusionism cannot account for the seeming of seeming without collapsing into self-refutation.

Prepare a Q&A prep sheet. List six to eight objections in roughly the order they will arise, phrased precisely as members of this audience would phrase them, using their vocabulary—representational content, self-model, reportability, predictive processing, phenomenal intentionality, ad hoc, etc. For each objection, indicate whether it carries real force or is largely rhetorical; what the speaker should concede up front; a concise reply suitable for delivery in approximately two minutes; and a frank assessment of where that reply is weakest. Identify the single reply that must land for the proposal to survive the session. Include the question the speaker most hopes will not be asked, formulated as that specific audience member would ask it, together with a brief strategy for addressing it without derailing the defense. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests. The speaker needs strategic preparation, not a literature review.
