I was arguing with my freind last night about whether actually feeling stuff is real or just our brains playing tricks on us, kind of like those old refrigerator lights where you open the door and suddenly there's light so you think it's been glowing all along, but really the bulb only fires up the moment you peek inside. I think some philosophers call this illusionism about phenomenal consciousness? Anyway, I want a villanelle — 19 lines, two refrains that keep coming back in the pattern — that uses that fridge-light image but makes the poem's structure do the actual philosophical work.

Use one refrain like "the bulb burns steady now" and another like "there is only darkness here". Each time they return they should mean something different: first the naive belief that there's always an inner glow, then the act of introspection where opening the mental door makes it flash on, then the illusionist denial that it's never on except when you check, and in the closing quatrain they should crash together and show the weird loop — that if there's genuinely nothing inside, you still need some kind of light to see the absence. Don't give me citations or a reading list, I wouldn't know how to check them, and don't just dress up philosophy in rhyme; it needs to actually work as a poem. Just keep it honest enough that someone who really studies consciousness wouldn't wince, and let the repetition mirror how the illusion keeps reasserting itself every time you think you've caught it out.
