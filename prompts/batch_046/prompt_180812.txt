Construct a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on panpsychism and the combination problem, designed to be answered by concrete structural commitments rather than by label preference. The tree must have exactly one root, four internal branching nodes, and eight leaves. At the root, pose the following diagnostic: if microscopic physical entities each possess minimal phenomenal properties, is the consciousness of a macroscopic subject literally composed of those micro-properties arranged in a structure, or does it depend on an additional non-compositional relation? Phrase this as a yes/no question that requires no prior knowledge of view-names.

From the compositional branch, split first on whether phenomenal combination is governed by a psychophysical law analogous to physical combination laws, or is instead brute and inexplicable. From each of those two sub-branches, split again on whether the phenomenal properties are intrinsic to the micro-entities regardless of relational context, or whether they depend essentially on their functional or spatial position within the macro-structure.

From the non-compositional branch, split on whether macro-consciousness is best understood as a single cosmic-level subject that individuates local experiences, or as an emergent property that stands in no part-whole relation to the micro-properties. Then split the emergent option on whether it allows macro-level phenomenal zombies—cases in which every micro-phenomenal property is present and correctly arranged, yet no macro-experience occurs because a specific binding condition (for instance, a non-linear integration threshold or a relational coherence constraint) remains unsatisfied.

Every leaf must carry either a named position—such as constitutive micropsychism, Russellian monism, cosmopsychism, or emergentist panpsychism—or be marked unnamed with a single-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment. You must include exactly one answer-combination that is unoccupied by any named view, and diagnose in one sentence whether the gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Also flag any pair of named leaves that your tree cannot separate, explaining why they converge on identical answers at every node.

The instrument should not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, scholar attributions, or citations. Build it so that a respondent is sorted entirely by what they affirm about structural cases—intrinsic versus relational phenomenal properties, law-governed versus brute combination, and the possibility of macro-level phenomenal absence despite complete micro-level presence—rather than by which philosophical label they favor.
