So there's this whole debate going on in my co-op building and I'm not sure who's being unreasonable. When we all moved in, everyone chipped in the exact same amount to outfit a shared basement workshop — drill presses, soldering stations, a laser cutter, all of it was supposed to be communal property with a sign-out sheet. The idea was that we started on equal footing, and the tools stayed shared no matter what.

But over the past year my neighbor Mara got obsessed with repairing old electronics and built up a real reputation on the floor. At first she fixed people's laptops for free, but then folks started leaving her cash, gift cards, even groceries. Now she's got this huge personal stash and bought her own private 3D printer that she keeps locked in her apartment; she's even started printing custom replacement parts for the communal drill press, which some people say is just her privatizing the maintenance chain. Some neighbors want to pass a house rule saying any income earned using skills partly developed with shared equipment has to go back into the pool, or that we should redistribute her savings to keep the building "even" like it started. Mara says that's completely unfair — nobody was coerced into paying her, she never broke the tool agreement, and voluntary generosity shouldn't be punished just because it creates inequality. I get her argument, but part of me still thinks the original equality was meaningful, and letting it dissolve just because people are nice to one person feels like it misses something important about fairness. Is there a principaled way to think about whether an initially fair setup can be disrupted by free choices, or are we just overthinkin roommate politics?
