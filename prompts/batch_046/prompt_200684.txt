im trying to map out whether the nordic welfare model is actually exportable, but im bored of the usual "high taxes need high trust" summary. what interests me more is this slightly weirder layer: the nordic states run on very high formal redistribution, but they also rely on an unusually tight consensus about what counts as a fair contribution — not just trust, but a kind of shared bookkeeping of obligations. if you take that seriously, then exporting the model isnt just about copying tax codes or even building trust; its about whether you can transplant that normative bookkeeping without the specific historical path that produced it.

so: what are the open questions or lines of work that actually matter here? im not after a bibliography — i want a defended agenda. which questions are upstream of the others? for instance, is the crux really about institutional design (can you build the bookkeeping top-down?), about behavioural evidence (do people actually need homogeneity to accept high taxes?), or about comparative history (are there hidden prerequisites that make the whole thing path-dependent)? rank them, say which ones are tractable in the next few years, and tell me which ones are overrated — the ones that get discussed a lot but dont actually change whether youd advise a country to try this. im writing a policy brief, so i care about what would genuinely shift the argument, not just academic completeness.
