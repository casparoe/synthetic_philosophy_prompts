ok so in my philosophy of language reading group we've been deadlocked on pragmatic encroachment vs epistemic contextualism for like a year. everyone rolls out the bank cases, everyone agrees practical interests do something, but then we just loop: is it that the word 'knows' shifts it's semantic standard, or that belief needs to be good enough for action? recently we started looking at retroactive stakes—like a researcher forms a belief with low stakes, publishes it, and then decades later it's used in a high-stakes safety call. do we say she didn't know back then, or that she knew then but doesn't now? the encroachment camp seems to treat knowledge as time-sensitive in a way that sounds like belief revision, while contextualists treat it as speaker-sensitive but not retroactive. but neither side admits they're talking about diffrent things, and yet they don't actually disagree on any concrete prediction. is this impasse really about hidden commitments to whether knowledge is an enduring state vs a contextual evaluation, or is it verbal—just two taxonomies for the same linguistic data? and if you think it's substantive, what kind of move breaks it: a formal model of time-indexed standards, an empirical study on retrospective attribution, or should we just split the concept of knowledge into two?
