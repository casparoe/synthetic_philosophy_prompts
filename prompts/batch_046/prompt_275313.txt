here are my verdicts on what Keynes's vision of teh good life actually commits to, and i'm not sure there's one principle behind them or if i'm just being inconsistent.

case 1: a society solves the economic problem — work is down to like 15 hours, basic needs are covered — but people spend their free time in competitive status games and passive consumption. my take: this isn't the good life. so solving the economic problem is necessary but clearly not sufficient.

case 2: someone is still poor, works long hours, but spends every free evening playing music with friends and talking about things they actually care about. my take: they have more of the good life than the wealthy society in case 1, even though Keynes wants poverty eventually solved for everyone. so material abundance isn't necessary for having part of it.

case 3: a rich patron funds museums and concerts everywhere, but people go mainly for signaling and feel anxious the whole time. my take: the art objects exist but the good life is missing, because what matters for Keynes is the internal state — love, beauty, knowledge — not the external production.

case 4 (the hard one): a small community deliberately refuses further automation, keeps working together in fields about 30 hours a week, and reports deep satisfaction and strong communal bonds. i want to say they have the internal goods Keynes cares about, but i also think Keynes would condemn them for not pushing tech further to reduce labor. so i feel like i'm trying to have it both ways — praising their state of mind while saying they're wrong about progress.

case 5: two people both have tons of leisure. one spends it learning dead languages for no reason; the other scrolls feeds to maintain clout. i think the first is closer to Keynes's good life, but i'm unsure if that's because of the content (knowledge vs status) or the psychology (absorption vs anxiety).

is there one account that gets all five? maybe something like "intrinsically valuable states of mind that require both material security and the right orientation toward them, plus a progressive duty to reduce labor"? but then case 4 is the problem — they have the states and some security, but reject the progressive duty. or do i have to drop case 4 and say they actually fail because they reject the economic solution?
