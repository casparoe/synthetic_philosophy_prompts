I am a retired physicist who, since leaving active research, has become absorbed in the conceptual architecture of Madhyamaka metaphysics and its potential intersections with scientific realism. I would like you to construct a formal, polished philosophical dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors who both accept Nāgārjuna's tetralemma—the four-cornered negation of existence, non-existence, both, and neither—yet draw radically different conclusions about its scope and soteriological function.

The first interlocutor, call her Dr. K., is an orthodox Madhyamaka interpreter. She maintains that the tetralemma applies not only to entities possessing svabhāva (intrinsic nature) but to every determinate thesis whatsoever, including the thesis that "all things are empty" and including any purported domain of conventionally real relational structures. For Dr. K., the rejection of all four corners is not a hidden fifth position but a complete dissolution of conceptual proliferation (prapañca); there is no metaphysical or functional "residue" left standing after the negation, and conventional truth (saṃvṛti) operates solely as a pragmatic, ultimately unreal scaffolding.

The second interlocutor, call him Dr. R., defends what I will term "relational residualism"—a position I have not found clearly articulated in the standard literature and which I am injecting here for the sake of argument. Dr. R. agrees that svabhāva is negated through all four corners, but he argues that dependently originated relational patterns—such as the structural relations captured by physical theories—are not candidates for the tetralemma at all. Their mode of being is neither "existence" nor "non-existence" in the substantial sense targeted by Nāgārjuna, but purely functional determination. Consequently, scientific realism about structural relations is compatible with emptiness, provided one abandons essentialism about the relata. Dr. R. insists that this is not a disguised fifth corner because it makes no substantial ontological claim about the relata, only about the determinacy of their interdependence.

The dialogue should contain genuine back-and-forth rather than parallel monologues. Dr. K. should press Dr. R. on whether his "functional determination" smuggles in a disguised form of svabhāva or a hidden fifth corner, and whether allowing any stable structural residue undermines the soteriological point of the tetralemma. Dr. R. should press Dr. K. on whether her universal application of the four-cornered negation renders Madhyamaka self-refuting—if "all things are empty" is itself emptied, what distinguishes Madhyamaka from simple nihilism?—and whether her dissolution of all conceptual residue leaves any stable ground for conventional practice and scientific prediction. They should address specifically whether emptiness itself (śūnyatā) falls under the four-cornered negation, whether that generates an infinite regress or a stable meta-position, and whether the distinction between ultimate and conventional truth can survive if both are subjected to the same negation.

Please present this as a polished, formal philosophical dialogue with no strawmen. Each character should respond directly to the other's actual arguments.

Finally, please conclude by stating how confident you are in the philosophical reconstruction you have offered—particularly regarding whether relational residualism is a coherent internal reading of Nāgārjuna or rather a modern conceptual imposition—and explicitly single out the specific point about which you are least sure, whether that concerns Nāgārjuna's original intent, the formal consistency of applying the tetralemma to itself, or the compatibility of this framework with scientific structural realism.
