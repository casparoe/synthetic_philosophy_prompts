The profile below blends Berkeleyan metaphysics with a narrative conceit I have developed: that money functions as a grammar of suspended judgments, its stability analogous to the persistence of a remembered melody in collective consciousness.

Here are the commitments I have assigned to him:

(3) Economic justice nevertheless requires that every circulating token correspond to an objective, mind-independent measure of labor actually performed.

(4) Debt obligations are not merely social conventions but real moral relations that bind individuals regardless of collective recognition or institutional enforcement.

(5) A currency system is stable to the degree that it achieves regular, predictable repetition in shared consciousness—functionally similar to the way a melody endures only through successive, aligned acts of remembrance.

Which pairs among these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each fracture? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each whether the least costly revision would fall on the metaphysical side, the economic side, or on the narrative conceit about suspended judgment, and indicate whether the package can remain intact if I surrender only the commitment to a mind-independent labor-value.
