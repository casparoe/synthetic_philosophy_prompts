(1) in a victimhood culture, your moral authority comes from having suffered harm that the community recognises.
(2) but recognition needs a shared metric for harm, and that metric is usually built from the norms of the group with instituional power.
(3) so the dominant group effectively sets the "exchange rate" for suffering.
(4) that means victimhood culture makes your moral standing depend on recieving approval from the very structures youre opposing.
(5) therefore victimhood culture is self-defeating: it asks the oppressor to validate the oppression.
(6) dignity culture avoids this because it says worth is intrinsic and doesnt need social confirmation.
(7) so dignity culture is morally superior.
(8) but if worth is intrinsic and equal for everyone, then past wrongs dont create any special present obligations.
(9) you just treat everyone with equal respect now and move on.
(10) but that cant be right, because then slavery or genocide wouldnt justify reparations, just "equal treatment going forward."
(11) so dignity culture proves too much: it flattens all historical injustice into nothing.

i feel like the slip might be between (3) and (4), or maybe between (6) and (8), like why does intrinsic worth have to erase history? but i also really dont want to say victimhood culture works fine. which step should i reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? like if i keep (6) do i have to pay some price about recognition?
