Write a deadpan parody in the form of a corporate job posting and employee onboarding manual for a fictional multinational corporation, “Attentive Global,” which has adopted “radical care ethics” as its official governance philosophy. The job posting should advertise a “Vice President of Relational Autonomy,” listing responsibilities that translate genuine care-ethical concepts—such as Nel Noddings’s idea of receptive, non-rule-governed moral attention, Joan Tronto’s phases of care (caring about, taking care of, care-giving, and care-receiving), and the tradition’s sustained critique of abstract, rights-based individual autonomy—into bureaucratic performance metrics and quarterly review criteria. The onboarding manual should then introduce invented institutional instruments: for example, an “Attentive Responsiveness Scorecard” with weighted sub-metrics for emotional labor; a “Dependency Audit” requiring managers to document asymmetrical care relationships in spreadsheet form; and a “Moral Attention Budget” that caps the hours any employee may spend in receptive listening. The satire should derive entirely from pushing care ethics’ real, internal resistance to formalization and standardization into the dead language of corporate accountability, audit culture, and HR compliance. Keep the voice straight-faced and readable to someone unfamiliar with the philosophical literature, but precise enough that a specialist recognizes the exaggerations as anchored in actual positions within the tradition rather than strawmen. The target should be the specific institutional risk that care ethics, precisely because it rejects bureaucratic abstraction, becomes most vulnerable to being captured and neutralized by bureaucratic abstraction. Format the piece as numbered manual sections with the job posting embedded at the top, and limit the parody to about 600 words.

After the parody, include a brief concluding statement of no more than 120 words that takes a definite position: identify which feature of care ethics—its contextualism, its valorization of emotional responsiveness, its critique of autonomy, or another—is most susceptible to this kind of managerial co-optation, explain precisely why that feature makes it fragile, and defend that judgment without framing it as merely one plausible option among equals. Do not hedge by presenting counter-arguments as equally valid; commit to the verdict and give your reason. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
