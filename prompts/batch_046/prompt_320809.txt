I'm putting together a pitch for a small philosophy-of-religion workshop grant—maybe three months, not huge money—on something that's been bugging me since I read Plutarch's Alcibiades right next to Athanasius's Life of Antony. Everyone talks about biography as moral education, but they mostly mean "you see a good person and want to copy them." I think that's too simple and misses what Plutarch is actually doing with the parallel lives thing, where you've got two guys with almost the same temperament—ambitious, charismatic, kind of reckless—but one collapses into vice and the other doesn't. The learning seems to come from comparing the divergence, not from imitating either hero.

So I want the proposal to ask this precisely: does hagiography destroy a specific pedagogical mechanism that Plutarch's paired structure provides? Not just "hagiography is less nuanced," but whether isolating the saint from a structurally parallel failure removes the reader's ability to calibrate moral judgment through comparative divergence rather than simple identification. The existing work I've seen—both in philosophy of biography and religious studies—tends to treat hagiography either as propaganda or as a different genre of exemplarity, but nobody seems to have framed the loss in terms of comparative moral calibration. I need the proposal to lay out why a conceptual/philosophical analysis (not a historical survey) can make progress: mapping the cognitive steps of "mimetic comparison" vs. "mimetic identification," testing whether hagiography can be reconstructed to preserve comparison without undermining sanctity, and saying what the deliverable looks like—maybe a framework for reading biographical texts that educators or curators could actually use.

Write me the one-page proposal: state the exact question, say why current answers leave it open, defend why this philosophical approach rather than just more history or literary analysis, describe what a result would look like and what counts as failure, and keep the scope realistic for a short project. Don't worry about formal citations—I can fill those in later—but do reference the positions or arguments you'd engage with in general terms.
