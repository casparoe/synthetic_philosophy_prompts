In the philosophy of probability, the debate over the Principal Principle—roughly, that rational credence should align with known objective chance—has stalled for decades around Newcomb-style cases and so-called undermining futures. Defenders hold that learning the objective chance of an event remains an authoritative epistemic constraint on belief, even when one’s own decision causally figures in the history of that event; critics respond that treating chance this way illicitly severs evidential reasoning from causal reasoning, and that once an agent recognizes her choice as part of the causal loop, deferring to the chance value becomes self-undermining. The literature has refined every move—distinctions between admissible and inadmissible evidence, centered versus uncentered world-indexing, restrictions on self-locating belief—yet neither side has shifted. My working hypothesis is that the stalemate is not about the formal properties of chance functions at all, but about what counts as a legitimate source of evidence for one’s own actions: one camp treats objective chance as an epistemic authority that survives causal involvement, the other treats it as a predictive summary that loses normative force precisely when the agent is inside the causal chain. Is that diagnosis correct, or is there a hidden formal disagreement about how to index chances across decision points? More generally, diagnose the structure of the disagreement: is it verbal, turning on competing meanings of “deference”; substantive but anchored in irreconcilable bedrock intuitions about agency and epistemic authority; or a case of two different questions—one about rational belief-formation, one about rational action—being pursued under a single heading? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a formal result clarifying admissibility conditions, a conceptual distinction separating the epistemic role of chance from its pragmatic role, or should we conclude that the underlying intuitions about self-locating belief make the impasse permanent?
