I'm in a course comparing Mencius and Xunzi on human nature, and I'm trying to apply their dispute to a contemporary framing that hasn't been mapped onto them in standard secondary literature: the design of artificial moral agents. Some AI safety researchers describe value alignment as either cultivating latent prosocial capacities—analogous to Mencius's four sprouts—or as imposing external constraints and reward shaping on a self-interested optimizer, closer to Xunzi's claim that human nature seeks profit and requires ritual and learning to become good. Meanwhile, recent developmental psychology shows that infants display spontaneous preferences for prosocial agents, yet complex moral reasoning requires years of institutional scaffolding. If Mencius and Xunzi were confronted with both this empirical pattern and the debate over whether large language models need internal cultivation or external correction, how would each interpret the coexistence of innate orientation and necessary training? In particular, which framework better explains why a system without biological embodiment or emotional sprouts might still depend on an artificial ritual structure rather than spontaneous moral growth? I want the reasoning each philosopher would likely deploy, and whether applying them to this case reveals a tension within one view that the other resolves.
