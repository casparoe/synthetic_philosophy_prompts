Write a short philosophical fiction—approximately 400 words—dramatizing the nonidentity problem in an intergenerational setting. The scenario: a settlement-archivist on a generation ship must choose between two reproductive protocols. Protocol A preserves the current crew's genetic lineages, yielding a future population of specific individuals whose lives are adequate but burdened by resource scarcity. Protocol B delays reproduction and re-pairs survivors after a mutation-correction treatment, yielding a future population of different, specific individuals whose lives are substantially better, but none of whom share biological continuity with the potential descendants of Protocol A.

To inject a conceptual twist, frame personal identity in this colony not through biological continuity but through contribution to a distributed "mnemonic substrate"—an inherited archive of experiential records that grants subjective continuity only to those who integrate into it. Thus, the potential descendants of Protocol A and Protocol B might both access fragments of the same substrate, yet they remain numerically distinct persons.

The archivist should debate an AI mediator (or an internal monologue) that presses the standard nonidentity objection: since the individuals who exist under Protocol B cannot be said to have been "harmed" by not being born under Protocol A—indeed, they owe their existence to Protocol B—there is no person-based reason to prefer A, and the aggregate well-being of B seems decisive. The archivist should then ask whether the substrate-based account of identity undermines this reasoning: if subjective continuity is substrate-dependent rather than biologically essential, does the moral relation between present choice and future people change?

Conclude ambiguously. Do not resolve the dilemma with a moral thesis; instead, end on the archivist's recognition that the substrate makes the future people "partially continuous" with the present, yet still not identical to the ones who would have existed otherwise—leaving open whether this partial continuity generates an obligation, or merely a different kind of loss.
