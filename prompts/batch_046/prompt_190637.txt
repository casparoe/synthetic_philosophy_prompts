Write an eight-minute opening address for the first session of a workshop titled "Mind in a Thermal Universe," held in a university auditorium with an audience split evenly between cosmologists and philosophers of mind—who are familiar with Boltzmann-brain arguments and will notice any imprecise move—and humanities scholars, donors, and first-year graduate students for whom statistical mechanics is foreign. The workshop's premise is that we must take seriously the possibility that conscious observers can arise as random thermal fluctuations in an otherwise empty, expanding universe, and then ask what that possibility does to our entitlement to scientific reasoning, to the reliability of memory, and to the belief that the other people in this room share a continuous world with us. The address must treat the Boltzmann-brain scenario not as decorative background but as a live structural constraint on the workshop itself: it should explicitly mark which assumptions we are not permitted to take for granted over the next two days—such as that we have a long causal past, that our memories are largely veridical, or that this gathering is embedded in a shared history—without pretending to resolve the underlying cosmological debate. It must also supply one concrete, non-mathematical image or analogy that makes the epistemic vulnerability visceral for the non-experts while remaining accurate enough that the specialists do not wince. Finally, the tone should be collaborative rather than paralyzing: explain why recognizing this vulnerability defines the boundary conditions of our discussions rather than canceling them, and invite the room to proceed with a shared understanding of what must be earned rather than assumed. Do not include bibliographies, citations, named papers, or reading lists; do not invoke specific authors as authorities; do not ask the audience to commit to any particular cosmological model; and keep the language formal but accessible, ensuring that the philosophical idea shapes the architecture of the speech—what is promised, what is withheld, and how the room is asked to move forward together.
