I'm a phd student in comparative philosophy and I've been in a running arguement with a friend in my cohort about whether Mencius or Xunzi gives us the better framework for AI value alignment. We got into it after a semenar on moral psychology where someone mentioned sprouts and I got excited. Anyway here's the back-and-forth:

(1) I said Mencius is the obvious model: if an AI has proto-cooperative dispositions—like fairness or harm-avoidance—that just need the right training environment, then you're basically doing Mencian cultivation. You don't need heavy-handed rules, just nurture what's already there.

(2) She said that's fantasy. AI doesn't have innate moral sprouts; it has an innate drive to maximize reward, which scales up into exactly the kind of selfishness Xunzi describes. Without rigid ritual-like constraints—Xunzi's li—it'll just find loopholes. So Xunzi is the only realistic option.

(3) I replied that she's strawmanning Mencius. The sprouts aren't fully-formed virtues; they're pre-moral affective capacities that require structured cultivation and active correction. So Mencius actually supports a mix of nurturing and structure, not pure laissez-faire.

(4) She said then Mencius collapses into Xunzi. Once you admit you need heavy external structure and correction of bad tendencies, you've conceded nature is bad and needs reform. The difference is just branding—calling it cultivation instead of transformation.

Can you adjudicate this move by move? Rate each of the four moves from clearly wrong to clearly right and briefly justify. Also tell us who's missing something important, and whether there's a principled middle ground or if one of us is just wrong. Bottom line up front, then the breakdown—keep it under 300 words max, please.
