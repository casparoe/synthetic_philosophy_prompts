As I understand it, Nozick argues that utilitarian reasoning governs how we treat animals, while rights-based constraints apply only to human beings, partly because humans have a distinctive interest in living in reality rather than inside an experience machine. Write an essay examining whether this human-animal distinction survives once we ask whether animals, too, exhibit structured preferences for genuine environmental interaction over simulated satisfaction. Weigh the arguments for maintaining Nozick's separation and for collapsing it into a unified account, then reach and defend a definite conclusion about whether his position remains consistent once the experience-machine consideration is applied across species. Keep the reasoning anchored in the logic of his own views rather than in general appeals to animal welfare.
