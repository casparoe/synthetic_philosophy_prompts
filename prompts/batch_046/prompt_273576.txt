As a retired experimental physicist now studying philosophy of science, I want a classification instrument—not named philosophers, but positions in logical space—presented as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, on the ontology of real patterns across the physical, design, and intentional stances. Build it from answers to two diagnostic questions: first, whether predictive success in the design stance (e.g., forecasting circuit failure or migratory routes) requires ontologically real regularities or only instrumentally useful fictions; second, whether adopting the intentional stance toward human agents requires realism about mental patterns or permits purely predictive, anti-realist attribution of beliefs and desires. Each item should state one combination of answers, name or describe the resulting position (full realist, physical-only realist, design-realist/intentional-instrumentalist, full instrumentalist, or an explicitly empty branch), note its most awkward commitment, and—where a branch is unoccupied—briefly diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, unstable, or unexplored. Flag any pair your taxonomy cannot cleanly separate, and include at least one hybrid that accepts pattern realism for evolutionary convergence but rejects it for intentional psychology.
