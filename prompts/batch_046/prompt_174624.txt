I've been working through van Reybrouck's argument for supplementing elected chambers with bodies selected by sortition — drawing on his experience with the G1000 Citizens' Summit — and I keep running into what seems like a tension with Arrow's impossibility result. The standard institutional case — that random selection eliminates campaigning, reduces capture by special interests, and yields descriptive representation — treats the selection mechanism as the key intervention. But once the mini-public is seated, they still have to aggregate individual judgments into collective recommendations. Arrow's conditions apply to any social welfare function that takes individual preference orderings as input, regardless of how those individuals were selected; so if the chamber votes by pairwise comparison, cyclical majorities are possible, and if it uses a different rule it violates one of Arrow's conditions (independence of irrelevant alternatives, unrestricted domain, etc.). This leads me to think that sortition solves the selection problem but leaves the aggregation problem untouched, which implies that the real defense must rest on a psychological claim about deliberative preference convergence — that structured discussion transforms arbitrary initial preferences into a shared ordering that escapes Arrow by not being a fixed profile to begin with. But that seems to import a contentious empirical thesis about group deliberation that isn't part of the original institutional design. Is the false move here that I'm modeling the chamber's internal process as a social welfare function over exogenously given preferences, when in fact deliberative bodies are meant to violate the independence conditions by allowing reasoning to change rankings? Or is there a deeper issue: that any collective body, however selected, must eventually output a decision, and Arrow shows that this aggregation step is irreducible unless we abandon democratic equality in the decision rule itself? I want to know exactly where the reasoning breaks, because it feels like I'm forcing lottocracy to answer a formal question about preference aggregation that it was never designed to address.
