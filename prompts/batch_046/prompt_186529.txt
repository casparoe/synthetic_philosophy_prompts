Consider a modified Death in Damascus scenario: a prophet tells an agent, “If you travel to Damascus, you will die there; if you stay away, you will die in Aleppo instead.” The agent reasons that since death is unavoidable regardless of location, the choice is causally moot, and proceeds to Damascus, where he dies. Explain the conceptual error—if any—in treating the prophecy as revealing a location-independent causal mechanism rather than as constraining a single actual future. Take a definite stance: is this reasoning a failure of modal logic, of practical rationality, or not a failure at all? Defend your position with reasons, and specifically assess whether this two-branch version strengthens the fatalist’s argument compared to the classic single-location prophecy. Give a committed answer that picks one diagnosis and argues for it, rather than merely surveying the options.
