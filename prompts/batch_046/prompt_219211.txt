In my epistemology reading group we've been stuck on Nozick's tracking theory versus closure for like a year. The closure defenders say if you know p and competently derive q, you know q, and tracking misses this only by smuggling in some bizzare causal requirement. The tracking defenders say closure is just internalist baggage and that in skeptical worlds you don't track the entailment relation at all. Everyone knows the formal conditions—sensitivity, adherence, etc.—and the standard skeptical counterexamples. Still, zero movement. So what's actually going on? Is it a substantive clash over whether knowledge needs transmission of justification, a verbal mess about what 'closure' even denotes, or two different projects—one normative, one descriptive—running under one name? My working guess is that both camps assume tracking is a single relation, but maybe the real stall comes from ignoring a split between tracking the fact p and tracking the entailment relation itself, so they're arguing about different levels without noticing. Diagnose this using what each side actually commits to, and be honest: could a formal distinction or a new case actually resolve it, or is it just bedrock intuitons about internalism that will never shift?
