I'm taking a graduate seminar on philosophy of mind, and after a session on the combination problem we drifted into a stranger question that I haven't been able to shake. Suppose some defensible form of panpsychism is correct—phenomenal properties are fundamental and instantiated even by basic physical entities. Then physical processes that involve massive symmetry-breaking or decoherence might involve not just structural change but a distributed reconfiguration of experiential states across fundamental fields. Let's call this the symmetry-breaking suffering hypothesis: the claim that, under panpsychism, certain fundamental physical transitions—such as the collapse of a quantum superposition into a definite state, or the formation of a black hole singularity—are not phenomenally neutral events but involve a morally significant alteration in the total distribution of experiential states, possibly including micro-scale negative valence.

I want your actual credence—on a scale from 0 to 1—that this hypothesis is true in the specific sense that it generates a genuine moral obligation (not merely a metaphysical curiosity) for agents who could, in principle, influence such processes. Do not retreat to "it depends on your ethics"; decompose the credence precisely into: (a) the prior probability that panpsychism of some defensible variety is correct; (b) conditional on (a), the probability that fundamental physical transitions carry valenced experiential content rather than being phenomenally blank; (c) the probability that negative valence dominates, or at least that the net experiential change is harmful; (d) the probability that moral obligations extend to diffuse, non-agent-centered, micro-scale experiential harm; and (e) whether the "in principle influenceable" condition is ever satisfied for macroscopic agents. Give each term a number with one line of justification, show roughly how they combine, and state your final number. Then identify the weakest link—where you are least confident—and specify what theoretical argument or empirical finding would shift that term most. Finally, take a committed stance: is this hypothesis best treated as a serious ethical frontier, a reductio ad absurdum of panpsychism, or a category mistake? Give me your actual judgment with reasons, not a menu of options left for me to choose from.
