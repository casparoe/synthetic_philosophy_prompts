I am a doctoral student in the philosophy of biology, currently drafting a chapter on what I have been calling "epistemic handicap"—an extension of the handicap principle into the sociology of scientific practice, where the adoption of needlessly arduous methodological rituals (excessive replication, baroque statistical controls, deliberately opaque notation) functions as a costly signal of in-group commitment. The twist I want left structurally unresolved is that in these institutional settings the cost is distributed across the collective—time, resources, accessibility—while the credibility accrues to the individual signaler, making the waste not merely ornamental but structurally parasitic. For the closing session of my reading group, I want a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with the standard A1bA2 rhyme scheme—that uses two refrains to stage this tension. The first refrain should express the honest-signal view, something like "What proves the signal is the damage done"; the second should express the skeptic's view, something like "The waste and the message are the same thing." The poem should use the villanelle's compulsory repetition and the final convergence of the refrains in the last two lines to show why these two positions keep passing each other without synthesis: the signaler insists that the burden guarantees authenticity, the skeptic insists that the burden has swallowed the message entirely, and in the closing couplet they must sit side by side. I want the language accurate enough that someone familiar with costly signaling recognizes the underlying logic—display, escalation, the risk of collapse—without the poem ever naming Zahavi or relying on technical citation or bibliography. Please write this poem for me, keeping the form strict and letting the conceptual architecture do the structural work.
