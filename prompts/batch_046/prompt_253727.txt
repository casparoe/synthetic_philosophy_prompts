I serve on my university's Academic Integrity and Professional Conduct Appeals Panel, and by Friday we must circulate a decision memo that resolves a case with an uncomfortable philosophical structure. A senior researcher filed a formal complaint alleging that a junior colleague committed reckless methodological negligence by selectively reporting data in a replication study, and she is demanding formal censure. During our review, it became clear that the complainant's own prior publications contain structurally identical omissions—omissions she has not corrected and now excuses by saying that reporting norms have evolved since then. The respondent is not contesting the replication failure itself, but is arguing that the complainant lacks standing to blame: an institution that sanctions someone on the strength of a hypocritical accusation implicitly treats blame as a weapon rather than a normative practice, and thereby violates what I have been calling, in my own notes, the institutional parity condition—that enforcement authority should not be exercised by agents who are exempt from the norms they invoke. The panel is split between those who see this as a straightforward ad hominem distraction and those who think hypocrisy undermines the legitimacy of the sanction regardless of the respondent's actual wrongdoing.

I need a memo that recommends either granting the censure with a formal disavowal of the complainant's standing, denying the censure on standing grounds, or granting it while explicitly separating the wrongness of the act from the authority of the accuser. The reasoning must treat the standing objection seriously—specifically addressing whether moral equality between blamer and blamed is a prerequisite for legitimate institutional blame, or merely an aesthetic constraint on who gets to speak—and must include an honest, non-caricatured account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. I have been reading some recent discussions of relational accounts of blame and want the memo to engage the idea that blame is not just a judgment but a relational posture that requires the blamer to recognize themselves as subject to the same standards; please work that in rather than falling back on the slogan that hypocrisy does not invalidate truth. The recommendation should be concrete enough for a direct vote, with a clear time frame for any follow-up actions, and it should avoid treating the legal or procedural definition of misconduct as automatically resolving the philosophical tension at stake.
