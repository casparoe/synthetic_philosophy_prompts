I have been reading about the long-running argument over whether culture can ever be a genuine cause in history, especially Max Weber’s claim that Protestant religious values played an independent role in shaping modern capitalism. One camp insists that values are always just reflections of deeper economic and institutional forces; the other camp insists that cultural beliefs can steer history in directions that material conditions alone cannot predict. Rather than reading another polemic, I want the kind of document two serious representatives of these camps might jointly sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, using the divergence between open-source software culture and corporate proprietary research as their concrete test case.

Please draft this joint statement. Name the two positions with neutral labels both sides would accept—perhaps cultural causalists and structural reductionists. Structure the document as a numbered section of agreed claims about the actual history of computing norms, followed by a disagreements table that states, in wording each side accepts for its own view, exactly where they still part ways. Identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—such as whether identical funding structures can produce divergent cultural outcomes, or whether values can persist after the economic conditions that produced them have disappeared. For each crux, provide a concrete historical comparison or organizational case that both sides agree is relevant, and record each side’s predicted interpretation of that case in advance. If any portion of their dispute turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, specify which portion and why. Write from the perspective of an interested non-specialist who has no library access and needs the reasoning to stand entirely on its own logic, without any citations, reading lists, or appeals to authority.
