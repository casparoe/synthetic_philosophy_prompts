A popular physics article summarizes the resolution of Maxwell's demon as follows: "The demon fails to violate the second law because measuring particle velocities writes information into its memory, and Landauer showed that erasing each bit dissipates kT ln 2 of heat. Because the demon must eventually clear its register to keep operating, the combined entropy of gas plus demon always rises. This proves that information and thermodynamic entropy are literally the same quantity, expressed in bits versus joules per kelvin, which is why the black-hole information paradox reduces to ordinary thermodynamic bookkeeping. The resolution is also fully classical: Szilard's quantum argument about measurement disturbance was unnecessary, and Bennett's computation-based account renders quantum mechanics irrelevant." Is this summary fair? Please distinguish what correctly captures the Landauer-Bennett resolution, what conflates information-theoretic entropy with its thermodynamic counterpart or overstates their identity, and whether the dismissal of quantum considerations and the reduction of black-hole puzzles to mere bookkeeping actually follow from anything in the classical resolution.
