Read Spike Jonze's film *Being John Malkovich* (1999) through the lens of consciousness as a user illusion—the view that conscious experience is not a transparent window onto reality but an adaptive interface constructed for practical interaction, leaving the cognitive "back end" permanently hidden from the user. The film literalises this premise: a hidden portal allows visitors to inhabit John Malkovich's sensory apparatus for fifteen minutes, retaining their own inner voice and agency while seeing and feeling through his body, until they are ejected onto the New Jersey Turnpike.

In your reading, address the following. First, does the film treat Malkovich's consciousness as a transferable interface with a private "back end" (the real Malkovich who eventually resists occupation), or does it suggest that there is nothing behind the interface except further interface—particularly in the scene where Malkovich himself enters the portal and finds a world populated entirely by copies of himself saying only his name? Second, examine the ending: Craig Schwartz, having permanently occupied Malkovich and then been expelled, enters the portal too late to reclaim Malkovich but too early to control Maxine's daughter Emily, leaving him trapped inside her consciousness, forced to watch Lotte and Maxine live happily together with the child. Does this dramatise the idea that the self is merely a user profile that can be reassigned without residue, or does Craig's enduring frustration and helplessness smuggle back a Cartesian residue that the user-illusion model is meant to eliminate?

Please distinguish carefully what is philosophically settled—such as the distinction between representational vehicle and representational content, or the empirical support for predictive processing in perception—from what remains genuinely contested, especially whether interface models genuinely dissolve the hard problem or merely relocate it. Do not present any contested interpretation (for example, that the film proves eliminativism about the self) as the consensus among philosophers of mind.

Finally, say what the film adds to the debate that philosophical argument alone does not: does its literalisation of interface-transfer expose an intuition that the technical literature suppresses, or does it depend on a confusion between epistemic access and metaphysical identity?
