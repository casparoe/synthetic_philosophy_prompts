I've been helping organize this little community play at the senior center where my grandma used to work, since she ran the bakery there for like forty years and was kind of a local legend around town. We're writing it about her life, but now my family is split right down the middle over one specific scene.

My aunt thinks we should include the part where grandma had this ugly falling out with her sister over some inheritence thing back in the 70s, because she says leaving it out makes grandma look fake and too perfect. But my mom is definetly furious and says dragging dead people's private family fights onto a stage is just disrespectful, especially since grandma's sister is dead too and can't say her side of the story. I keep feeling like there's some deeper philosophical question hiding in here about whether we owe dead people honesty or if art is allowed to clean things up for a better story, but I don't know the actual names for any of that stuff. My cousin threw around this phrase "representational debt" and said artists have an obligation to the dead not to distort their lives, but I'm pretty sure he just invented that after skimming some philosophy blog. So I'm stuck: is my aunt right that real drama needs the messy parts, or is my mom right that some things should stay buried out of respect? I guess I'm asking if there's actually a principled way to decide what we owe real dead people when we turn their lives into entertainment.
