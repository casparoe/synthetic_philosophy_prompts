my roommates got into a huge debate about that Mary in the black-and-white room thing. Riley said when Mary sees red for the first time she learns a real new fact that no textbook could cover — like there's a special "feeling" of red that science leaves out. Casey said no, she just picks up a new ability, like learning to ride a bike; her brain physically changes when she sees color, so physicalism explains everything, she just gets a new way to access info she already had.

Riley shot back that Mary already knew every physical detail about her brain, so if seeing red is just those neurons firing she should have already known what it was like from her books alone. Casey said knowing a recipe isnt tasting the cake — you can have all the objective facts without being in the state yourself.

Then Riley asked: if everything is physical, couldnt there be a zombie Mary with the exact same brain but no inner feeling? That'd mean Mary's actual feeling is extra. Casey said maybe zombies are impossible, so that thought experiment doesnt prove anything.

They also argued about whether Mary learns "this is what red looks like to me" — Casey called that just a fact about yourself, like learning "I am here now," which is still physical. Riley said if its just self-location then why does it feel like a massive revelation?

Neither will back down. Whos right about whether Mary actually learns something new, and is Casey's recipe comparison a real answer or just dodging?
