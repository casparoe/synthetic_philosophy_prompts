My reluctance is not simple vanity: the frames feel like commitments made to earlier iterations of the work, giving the novel what I might call a diachronic identity—an obligation to preserve its historical trajectory that exists alongside, and perhaps conflicts with, its present aesthetic value. Is honoring this kind of embedded historical commitment ever rationally defensible in artistic practice, or does it always reduce to a confusion between past effort and present value?
