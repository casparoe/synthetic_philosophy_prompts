I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. I sit on the ethics review board for my firm’s automated decision systems, and we must approve or reject proposed updates—such as substituting aggregate optimization for individual evaluation, or allowing a module to withhold information from users to prevent harm. We have no library access and no time to verify scholarly references, so any framework must be runnable by non-philosophers in under an hour and must not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.

Please construct a reusable decision procedure, grounded in deontological ethics and specifically engaging the paradox of deontology—the tension that absolute constraints against treating persons as mere means seem to collapse into irrationality when they produce catastrophic aggregate outcomes, yet permitting exceptions risks dissolving deontology into consequentialism. The deliverable should be a gate-by-gate flowchart in words: for each gate, state the precise question to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer to pass, where the procedure terminates with a rejection, and where it must escalate to human judgment because the issue is one of values rather than verifiable fact.

The procedure must include three specific diagnostics. First, a “paradox-point” gate that activates when strict adherence to a constraint produces outcomes that violate the same constraint at scale; at this gate, the procedure must not automatically invoke a threshold exception, but instead require a specific form of justification and flag that justification as the precise location where deontology risks self-undermining. Second, a “side-effect framing” check to detect whether a proposed update reclassifies an intended harm as an unavoidable side effect to evade the direct prohibition against using persons as means—designed so that an engineer without philosophical training can apply it using only the update’s technical documentation. Third, an explicit “judgment residue” marker at every gate where the distinction between fact and value cannot be eliminated by further testing.

Then apply the procedure to three proposed updates:
1. A proposal to replace individual consent checks with a predictive welfare-maximization model, on the grounds that high accuracy makes case-by-case review redundant.
2. A proposal to hide distressing medical results from patients, framing the withholding as a protective side effect rather than direct deception.
3. A proposed emergency override that suspends all individual rights checks during a system-defined crisis to prevent total collapse.

For each, show the gate-by-gate result, including one case where the procedure returns an answer that an engineer seeking system efficiency will find undesirable. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do: which classes of disagreement it will fail to resolve, where it will misfire if applied by a board lacking specific expertise, and which features of deontological theory have no operational counterpart in this framework. Do not include verbatim quotations from philosophical texts; paraphrase without quotation marks if reference to a specific claim is necessary.
