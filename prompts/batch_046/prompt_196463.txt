Please construct a taxonomy in the form of a nested decision tree that proceeds from the logical space rather than from a list of named theorists. Use exactly three binary splits. To make this split concrete, embed the Aunt Hillary scenario: imagine a named worker ant is suddenly removed; answer whether the colony shows a specific, traceable deficit or compensates instantly and seamlessly. The third split, also on the affirmative branch, asks whether there is a unified subject of experience—something it is like to be the colony—or only distributed functional states without phenomenological unity. Each leaf must carry a descriptive name (label it unnamed if the position lacks a standard designation) and exactly one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. After drawing the tree, present the full 2×2×2 logical grid generated by treating the three splits as independent dimensions; for every unoccupied combination, briefly diagnose whether the emptiness stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere unexplored territory. Finally, flag any pair of leaves that share all three answers yet remain genuinely distinct—perhaps differing only in metaphysical framing—and explain why the diagnostic questions fail to separate them. The audience is intellectually curious non-specialists, so phrase every node as a concrete claim or case they can evaluate without knowing philosophical labels. Limit the instrument to no more than eight leaves, and do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or reading recommendations; it should be fully navigable without external sources.
