Write a formal, polished dialogue in American English between two equally competent scholars—Dr. Lena Okonkwo, a stratigrapher who advocates formalizing the Anthropocene as a geological epoch with a Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point anchored to the early-20th-century synthetic nitrogen cascade (the Haber-Bosch disruption layer around 1913), and Dr. Samuel Park, a historian of science who argues that the Anthropocene should remain an informal, interdisciplinary historical period whose boundaries are decided by socio-political and ecological consensus rather than by geological commissions. Have them debate both when the period genuinely began and, more importantly, who possesses legitimate authority to decide—stratigraphic bodies, interdisciplinary working groups, or democratic deliberation. Ensure that each character responds directly to the other’s actual arguments rather than talking past each other, that neither is presented as a strawman, and that the exchange stays in the weeds of conceptual disagreement about evidentiary standards, disciplinary authority, and whether formal periodization is even appropriate for a phenomenon driven by distributed human activity.
