You are an expert in the philosophy of logic and formal systems, with particular familiarity with the MU-puzzle and the idea of "jumping out of the system" (JOOTSing). Before giving your prediction, briefly brainstorm the competing conditions that might count as genuinely stepping outside a formal framework, then apply them.

Consider a philosopher—call her Dr. K—who has judged the following cases about whether an agent embedded in a system can solve a meta-level puzzle concerning that system itself:

1. In the MIU formal system (with its rules for deriving strings from M, I, and U), a reasoner restricted to applying those derivation rules cannot obtain "MU"; Dr. K says the failure is structural and unavoidable from inside.

2. A chess program that evaluates board positions using only move-legality conditions, but lacks any representation of the rules of chess, cannot explain why castling rights depend on prior king and rook movement—even though it never makes an illegal castle.

3. A Gödel-numbered arithmetic system proves every true arithmetic statement, yet when presented with its own Gödel sentence it treats that sentence as undecidable; Dr. K judges that the system has not jumped out but is merely displaying its internal limit.

4. A pattern-completion network trained solely to extend symbol sequences according to an implicit grammar succeeds on in-grammar examples but cannot classify out-of-grammar strings, even when they share surface structural features; Dr. K says it remains inside the grammar.

Now the new case: A large language model, given only the rules of the MIU system in its prompt context and with no prior explicit training on Hofstadter's exposition, derives that "MU" is unreachable within the system, articulates the meta-reasoning in natural language, and distinguishes derivable strings from non-derivable ones. Dr. K is asked whether this counts as genuine JOOTSing.

Predict Dr. K's verdict on the language-model case. Then identify the implicit rule she seems to be running across cases 1–4, propose at least one other rule that fits those four verdicts equally well, and specify what further case—perhaps involving a modified formal system or a different kind of artificial agent—would best distinguish the two rules. Focus on the philosophical boundary between internal computation and meta-level recognition rather than on the model's technical architecture.
