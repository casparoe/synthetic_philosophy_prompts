Here are notes for an essay defending “substrate-sensitive prioritarianism” (SSP). The view holds that prioritarian moral weights should be scaled by both a person’s absolute welfare level and the structural fragility of the institutions sustaining that welfare—what I call the substrate-exposure coefficient. A gain to a poorly-off person whose well-being hinges on a single, unstable support receives greater priority than the same gain to an equally poor person with diversified, resilient supports. I claim this avoids the leveling-down objection: reducing everyone to equal fragility lowers weighted welfare, since the coefficient collapses across the board. I further argue that SSP does not covertly import egalitarian relational concerns, because weights remain anchored to individual welfare profiles rather than to comparative gaps.
