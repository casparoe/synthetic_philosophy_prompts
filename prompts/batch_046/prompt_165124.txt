Write a dialogue between Dr. Aris Thorne, who holds that the badness of pain is essentially representational and evaluative — pain is bad because it represents bodily damage as threatening to the subject’s integrity, and classic pain asymbolia shows that when that representational-evaluative uptake is disrupted, what remains is a bare sensory signal without genuine suffering — and Dr. Bex Corvin, who holds that pain’s badness is irreducibly phenomenological and that asymbolic patients still undergo a primitive affective assault that their cognitive architecture merely fails to integrate, avoid, or report coherently. Have them discuss both the clinical profile of pain asymbolia and a hypothetical “inverse asymbolia”: a patient with complete nociceptive absence who nevertheless retains fully intact conceptual and emotional frameworks for bodily harm, reports profound suffering upon observing tissue damage in herself, and exhibits vigorous avoidance behavior despite feeling absolutely nothing. Each interlocutor should explain whether this inverse case supports her view or forces a revision, address whether suffering requires the conjunction of affective presence and evaluative framing or can be sustained by either alone, and identify the specific further clinical or conceptual case — not a literature citation — that would compel her to abandon her current position. The exchange should be genuinely dialectical: neither should talk past the other, each should press the weakest point in the opponent’s appeal to asymbolia, and both should remain equally competent throughout.
