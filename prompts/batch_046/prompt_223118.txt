Assume a small interdisciplinary team—philosophers of mind and theoretical cognitive scientists—has three years of funding to settle whether global workspace theory must be fundamentally revised to account for phenomenal consciousness, or whether it can be preserved by refining its dynamics. Within this project, they must choose which conceptual open questions are genuinely upstream of that decision, rather than merely decorative. Work with the distinction between 'access consciousness' (globally broadcast information available for reasoning and report) and 'phenomenal consciousness' (subjective experience), and specifically the proposed scenario of 'broadcast integration failure': information is technically broadcast across workspace modules but fails to unify into a phenomenal field due to temporal desynchronization, not due to absence of access. Prioritize the top three open conceptual questions or lines of theoretical work this team should pursue, ordering them by dependency—what must be resolved first. For each, defend its place in the ranking by addressing: (a) why it is tractable for conceptual and philosophical work rather than requiring new empirical neuroscience; (b) how answering it shifts the burden of proof between an access-only interpretation of global workspace theory and one that accommodates phenomenal consciousness; and (c) what specific conceptual result over the next few years would cause you to reorder it. Do not list readings or cite authors; the deliverable should be a defended agenda, not a survey.
