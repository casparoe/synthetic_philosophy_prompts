After retiring from laboratory work in condensed-matter physics, I have lately turned to philosophical questions about practical reason, and I find myself puzzled by the relationship between hyperbolic discounting and the classical problem of akrasia. The empirical literature seems to show that human intertemporal choice follows a hyperbolic rather than exponential curve, producing systematic preference reversals as rewards become imminent. To my scientific mind, this looks like a well-characterised dynamical bias—analogous to thermal drift in an instrument—that could in principle be corrected by commitment mechanisms or algorithmic debiasing. Given that, I wonder whether the philosophical literature on weakness of will is not largely obsolete: if akrasia is merely the behavioural signature of a hyperbolic discount function, does it remain coherent to speak of it as a distinctive failure of rational agency, or should we reconceptualise it entirely as a problem of engineering self-control?
