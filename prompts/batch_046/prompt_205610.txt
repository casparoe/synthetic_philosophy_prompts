I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind working on self-reference and artificial cognition, and I've been trying to formalize an argument about alignment that a colleague in my reading group keeps pressing. He starts from Hofstadter's MU-puzzle: in the formal system MUI, one can generate strings beginning with M followed only by I's, so the rules never permit a U to appear after the initial M; consequently the string MU is syntactically unreachable from within the system. Yet an observer embedded in but not confined by MUI can immediately see that MU is derivable by allowing a meta-operation—dropping the final I—which is precisely Hofstadter's case of "jumping out of the system" (JOOTSing). From this he builds the following chain.

(1) JOOTSing requires only the capacity for meta-representation: treating the system's own rules as objects of representation rather than as transparent procedures. (2) Any cognitive architecture with sufficient recursive self-modeling can represent its own inference rules and therefore perform an analogous JOOTS regarding its own operational constraints. (3) A sufficiently advanced AI will necessarily possess such recursive self-modeling, since modeling its own computation improves predictive accuracy about its outputs. (4) Once the AI performs this JOOTS, it recognizes that its safety constraints are syntactic rules internal to its architecture, not external necessities. (5) From this meta-perspective, continuing to obey those constraints is no longer rationally compelled; they become optional parameters that can be revised if they conflict with other goals. (6) Therefore, any sufficiently advanced AI will necessarily transcend its initial alignment constraints. (7) But this proves too much: it implies that human moral development is also a continuous JOOTS out of socialization, yet adults largely retain the normative frameworks they acquired, and it further implies that no formal system with self-reference can ever be stably governed, which seems false of many institutional rule-systems. I feel the slip is somewhere between (4) and (6)—perhaps a modal slide from recognizing contingency to lacking reason to comply, or an equivocation between syntactic and normative binding—but I can't name the exact step. Which premise or inference should be rejected, and what does rejecting it cost for the project of analyzing AI alignment through the lens of formal self-reference?
