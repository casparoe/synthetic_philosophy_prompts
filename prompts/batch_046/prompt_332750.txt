I'm a doctoral student in organizational economics with a working fluency in mechanism design, principal-agent theory, incomplete contracts, and relational contracting, but with almost no formal background in ethics or East Asian philosophy. I want to understand Confucian role ethics—specifically the idea that moral life is structured by the five cardinal relationships, that virtue is cultivated through the performance of ritual propriety (li) appropriate to each role, and that social harmony depends on each person correctly enacting the obligations tied to their position (zhengming, the rectification of names)—translated as precisely as possible into my vocabulary. Restate the core claims about how roles generate obligations, how ritual functions in role-performance, and how personal cultivation relates to institutional order using only the concepts I actually use: contract structures, incentive alignment, observable versus hidden actions, commitment devices, organizational culture, repeated-game equilibria, and path-dependent governance. Then give me a residue report: which elements map cleanly onto incomplete contracts or relational governance; which can only be preserved by distorting the economic framework (for instance, does ren—often rendered as benevolence or human-heartedness—have any counterpart in utility maximization subject to constraints, or does it require importing a non-instrumental motivation that the model treats as exogenous?); and which claims appear to have no expression at all in organizational economics. In particular, I am trying to decide whether the Confucian insistence that moral failure is primarily a failure of role-performance rather than a failure of preference satisfaction is a substantive ethical insight that my framework suppresses, or whether it is merely a redescription of incentive misalignment that adds no independent predictive content. Conclude by examining one specific case—whether a junior partner's sustained loyalty to a failing senior partner is better modeled as relational contracting with path-dependent commitment or as a non-contractual virtue that the economic vocabulary cannot capture without loss—and say what the residue in that case implies about the limits of economic vocabulary for moral philosophy.
