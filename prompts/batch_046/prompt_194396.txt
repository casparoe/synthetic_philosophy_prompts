I've been drafting notes for an essay on Ian Hacking's looping effects, but applied specifically to the DSM-5-TR category of borderline personality disorder rather than the usual pretraining examples of autism or multiple personality disorder. The central claim is that BPD's polythetic definition—five of nine criteria—creates a structurally different loop from the ones Hacking originally described.

The argument runs in three parts. First, I reconstruct the basic framework: interactive kinds versus indifferent kinds, and how a classification can feed back into the behaviour it describes, usually tightening the category over time. Second, I examine BPD specifically and argue that because the criteria are disjunctive and partly subjective—unstable relationships, impulsivity, affective dysregulation—the loop does not homogenise the category but diffuses it. Individuals partially identify with subsets of symptoms through therapeutic narratives and online self-diagnosis communities, reshaping clinical presentation in ways that increase heterogeneity rather than reducing it. Third, I ask whether this "diffuse looping" is genuinely an instance of a looping effect at all, or merely well-known diagnostic heterogeneity relabelled in philosophical language.

I'd welcome comments on several points. First, on accuracy: am I misrepresenting Hacking's distinction between interactive and indifferent kinds, or the mechanism by which loops typically stabilise classifications? Second, on coherence: is the move from "tightening" to "diffusion" a meaningful extension of the framework, or does it collapse into the standard epidemiological problem of boundary fuzziness? Third, on whether the framing is interesting: does it illuminate how psychiatric categories evolve in an environment of widespread self-diagnosis, or is it just applying a familiar philosophical lens to a familiar clinical problem without adding anything?

I'm particularly unsure about the third section. If the category is already defined to be heterogeneous, then observing that it remains heterogeneous might not demonstrate a looping effect at all—just that the diagnostic rules permit variance. So I'd also value any suggestions on how to sharpen that distinction, or on whether I should abandon the "diffuse looping" concept entirely.
