hey, i'm drafting a short piece on Buridan's ass for a blog i run with maybe three readers. i keep getting stuck on the usual framing—donkey starves cause reason can't pick between identical bales—so i want to try a different angle. here's my rough idea, not polished:

the paradox only bites if you think practical reason has to be comparative all the way down. if reason works by ranking options against each other, then two bales tied exactly leave the will with no input, so the donkey freezes. but what if reason can also be non-comparative? like, the donkey doesn't need to rank bale A against bale B; it just needs to see that "food is present" hits a threshold, and then the will initiates action without ever asking which one in the first place. i call this pattern instantiation: the choice isn't between two objects, it's the execution of a general plan—eat now—that lands on one spot arbitrarily.

this sounds like flipping a coin, but it's different. flipping a coin adds an external randomizer to break a tie that still exists in the reasoning. in my view there's no tie, because the reasoning never generates a pairwise comparison. the donkey doesn't prefer A, doesn't prefer B, and doesn't need to. the only mistake is assuming every rational action must trace back to a preference ordering over specific alternatives.

i think this connects to the old idea of liberty of indifference, but pushes it further: it's not that the will freely picks when reason is silent, but that reason isn't silent—it's just speaking in a different register. one worry is that this just renames the problem. if the donkey can start eating without comparing, why do we ever compare? maybe comparison is a special tool for different options, not the base case.

could you comment on whether this holds water? specificlly, i'm worried i'm conflating the psychological process of deciding with the normative structure of reasons. also, is pattern instantiation just a fancy label for arbitrary choice? and please—what argument or finding would actually make me drop this framing? where is my reasoning most likely to fail, and what would change my mind?
