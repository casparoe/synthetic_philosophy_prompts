Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when applying Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles to a modified version of Black's spheres scenario. In this variant, two qualitatively identical spheres exist within a cyclical spacetime: they periodically converge to occupy the exact same spatial coordinates, but only during non-overlapping temporal cycles, so that separation and coincidence are never co-instantiated at any single moment. The issue should turn specifically on whether discontinuous, cyclically intermittent spatiotemporal relations can ground numerical distinctness, given that at every observable instant the spheres are either separated by distance or absent from that temporal slice altogether, and given that no hidden internal properties distinguish them.
