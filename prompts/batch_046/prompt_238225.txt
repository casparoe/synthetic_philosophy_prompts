hey, i've been thinking about adverserial collaborations (like the ones people post on lesswrong) but applied to infite ethics, and i want it actually in the weeds — not the usual utilitarian vs deontologist reheated leftovers.

camp A: the limit-aggregationists. they think infinite populations are ethically tractable if you pick the right convergence concept — density limits, hyperreal representations, whatever. they think ordering-dependence is just a feature you discipline with a principled limit choice.

camp B: the collapse-theorists. they think every such method just smuggles arbitrariness into the limit selection, and that in genuinely infinite settings aggregation either collapses to triviality or needs an unjustified "boundary condition" like a first moment.

deliverable: the joint statement. start with a numbered list of claims both sides can sign (e.g., standard expected value breaks with infinite populations; different orderings yield different rankings; the problem is conceptual not just computational). then a disagreements table: each row is a precise statement, with camp A's version and camp B's version, worded so each side accepts its own.

then the cruxes. i think there are two:
1. is ordering-dependence a defect in the aggregation rule, or a feature of ethical reality?
2. does restricting to computable/constructive decision procedures solve the problem or just hide it?

for each crux, give a concrete case both agree is relevant: a branching tree of minds with welfare +1 everywhere except a sparse infinite path at -10, arranged so density limits, limsup, and hyperreal methods all diverge. record predictions — camp A says their favored limit gives the right answer; camp B says the divergence proves there's no fact of the matter, or that any choice reveals hidden arbitrariness.

also: what evidence or argument would each side take as genuinely damaging to their view? like, if someone proves all computable aggregation rules are order-sensitive, who takes the hit?

please draft this. keep it casual, typo or two is fine. no bibliographies or citations — this needs to work for a model with no search access.
