I'm constructing a taxonomy for a graduate seminar on Smith's political economy. Write a decision tree that sorts interpretive positions on Adam Smith's view of empire and colonies, structured as a flow from a single root question: Is Smith's opposition to colonial empire fundamentally a claim about economic efficiency, about the corruption of domestic constitutional liberty, or about the moral equality of nations? From each branch, split on concrete commitments: whether colonies are a net fiscal-military burden to the metropole regardless of trade monopoly; whether the monopoly of the home market is the decisive harm or merely one symptom; whether the Navigation Acts are the central case or representative of a broader mercantilist logic; and whether the argument extends to informal economic dominance that lacks formal political rule. Each leaf should carry a label (name the position if it has a standard one in the literature, else 'unnamed'), and one sentence describing its most awkward commitment—e.g., having to deny that Britain gained any net benefit from American colonies despite widespread contemporary belief, or having to claim that free trade with independent nations always yields greater revenue than exclusive colonial preference. Note any unoccupied combinations: is there a view that accepts the fiscal burden but denies any constitutional corruption, and is that incoherent or merely unexplored? Flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely cannot separate, such as positions that agree on all practical conclusions but differ only in rhetorical framing. The tree should have six to eight leaves, be answerable by someone who knows Smith's Wealth of Nations and related writings but has not studied the secondary literature, and must not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Finish with a one-sentence summary of the tree's structure as the final line.
