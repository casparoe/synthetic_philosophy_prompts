I'm putting together the take-home for my grad metaphyscis seminar and I'm tired of essays that just rehearse the determinism/chaos cliche. I want one essay question — about 1500 words, 48 hours — that forces students to think about deterministic chaos not as predictability is hard but through this idea I'm calling predictive sovereignty: determinism only does interesting metaphysical work if the laws grant some in-principle authority over future states, and in chaotic systems that authority is broken not by randomness but by what we might call an informational compression gap — the rules are fixed but the macro-state can't be recovered from them without already knowing the micro-state to impossible precision.

The question should ask whether this gap undermines determinism itself, or just our access to it, and it should make students seperate law-governance from epistemic control in a way that can't be answered by summarizing Lorenz or repeating that causes determine effects. Give me the exam prompt, a model answer of around 800 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit moves (totalling 30 marks) plus notes on the B-level mistakes — like conflating chaos with quantum indeterminacy or treating predictability as just a practical problem. Keep it focused, no citation lists, and write like you're talking to a colleague.
