Consider four distinct formulations of the conceivability argument against physicalism about phenomenal consciousness, each centered on the claim that a philosophical zombie—an entity physically identical to a conscious human but lacking any phenomenal experience—is metaphysically possible.

The formulations are:

1. The two-dimensional intension argument: the primary intension of phenomenal terms fixes reference via how things appear, while the secondary intension fixes it via underlying physical nature; because a world physically identical to ours can be conceived under the primary intension without consciousness, physical facts do not metaphysically necessitate phenomenal facts.

2. The microphysical-variation argument: this version holds that conceiving of a zombie requires only varying the microphysical substrate or its organizational dynamics while holding macro-functional properties constant; since no particular microphysical configuration conceptually entails consciousness, the absence of consciousness remains conceivable across all physical duplicates.

3. The first-person indexical argument: rather than relying solely on third-person descriptions, this formulation appeals to the subject's capacity to conceive of her own physical duplicate—someone with identical neural and behavioral states—lacking her present phenomenal states, using first-person epistemic access to license a direct modal inference about her own consciousness.

4. The comparative-modal essentialism argument: this version embeds the zombie claim within a broader pattern of modal reasoning used in science (e.g., that heat is necessarily molecular motion); it contends that if physicalists reject zombie-conceivability on the grounds that physical properties necessarily carry phenomenal ones, they must either reject analogous essentialist claims across natural science or provide a principled distinction that does not apply ad hoc.

Your task is to rank these four formulations from 1 (strongest) to 4 (weakest) according to a single synthetic criterion: resistance to the "epistemic-parity collapse." Define this collapse as follows: the objection that if conceivability of zombies is granted metaphysical significance, then conceivability of arbitrary skeptical scenarios—such as being a brain in a vat with systematically false phenomenal beliefs—must be granted equal significance; and further, that the physicalist can block both threats with one uniform strategy (for example, appealing to direct reference, conceptual isolation, or the transparency of phenomenal concepts) without being forced to treat the zombie case differently from the skeptical case.

For each formulation, assign its rank (1–4) and justify it in one to two sentences, specifying precisely whether that formulation's premises allow the physicalist to apply the same blocking move to zombies and to skeptical scenarios alike, or whether they force the physicalist to adopt a differential, formulation-specific response—thereby indicating stronger or weaker resistance to the collapse. Do not provide citations or a bibliography; reason from the conceptual structure of each argument alone.
