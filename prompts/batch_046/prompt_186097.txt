Analyse the Chilean Project Cybersyn (early 1970s) as a case study in the socialist calculation debate. In your answer, treat the following features as given: the system was designed by Stafford Beer to link a portion of Chile’s state-owned enterprises via a telex-based network that transmitted real-time production data; it was organised according to Beer’s viable-system model, with recursive organisational levels and algedonic feedback loops intended to regulate performance without direct price signals for every intermediate good; it operated within a socialist government that retained market pricing for consumer goods but sought central coordination of industrial output; and its implementation was partial and was interrupted by the 1973 coup.

Using the arguments of Mises (the impossibility of rational economic calculation in the absence of private property and genuine market prices), Hayek (the dispersion of tacit, local knowledge that central nodes cannot aggregate), and Lange (competitive market socialism in which state enterprises adjust prices by trial and error within a planned framework), address the following in a disciplined mapping:

- Identify the actors and the information each held: factory managers, Beer’s central operations room, government ministers, and the consumer market. What was the specific calculation problem—allocative, informational, or organisational—that the system was built to solve?
- Apply Mises: did Cybersyn attempt to substitute simulated or administratively set prices for genuine market prices in intermediate goods? If so, does this address Mises’s objection or merely relocate it?
- Apply Hayek: to what extent could telex-transmitted quantitative data capture the tacit, contextual knowledge that Hayek argued was essential for rational economic coordination? Where in the loop did such knowledge necessarily remain invisible?
- Apply Lange: was Cybersyn an instance of competitive socialism using price signals, or was it a form of direct cybernetic planning that the Mises–Hayek–Lange debate did not fully anticipate? Be precise about which features map onto Lange’s model and which do not.
- Point to one feature of the episode—such as the partial coverage of the economy, the recursive organisational architecture, or the abrupt political termination—that the standard debate framework explains poorly or not at all. Say whether this reveals a genuine theoretical gap or only a contingent historical boundary.
- Conclude by assessing whether Cybersyn serves as a confirmation, an anomaly, or an unanticipated boundary case for the socialist calculation debate, and specify exactly where the debate remains silent rather than forcing an answer.
