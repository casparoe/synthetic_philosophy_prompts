Having recently retired from experimental physics and turned my attention to philosophy, I have become preoccupied with the conceptual structure of diachronic rationality, particularly the arguments that invoke money pumps to justify coherence constraints across time. In physics one often distinguishes local symmetries, which constrain dynamics point by point, from global conservation laws, which constrain a system only when integrated over its history; I am struck by what may be an analogous distinction in decision theory between synchronic consistency conditions—such as transitivity and independence—and diachronic conditions that prohibit preference reversals in the absence of new information. Whether this analogy is substantive or merely decorative, I do not presume.

What I seek is a defended prioritization of the genuinely open conceptual questions in this area, suitable for directing the efforts of a small interdisciplinary group—say, two philosophers and one formal theorist—over roughly three years. Among the following, though I welcome substitutes I have overlooked, which should be treated as upstream?

First, can diachronic coherence norms be derived entirely from synchronic rationality requirements, or do they require an independent normative foundation?

Second, does vulnerability to a money pump reveal a structural incoherence within an agent’s preference ordering, or is it more accurately characterized as a strategic failure of foresight?

Third, how should one formalize the notion of “conditionalizing on future preferences” without collapsing into either rigid prior commitment or arbitrary ex post revision?

Fourth, to what extent do evolutionary stability or game-theoretic arguments provide an instrumental justification for diachronic norms, and can such justifications supplant or only supplement constitutive ones?

Please rank these questions—or propose more important ones—according to which answers, once settled, would resolve, dissolve, or substantially restructure the others. For each, indicate its tractability with present formal tools, whether its resolution depends on empirical claims about human cognition, and why you would deprioritize any that, even if answered, would leave the broader theoretical and practical landscape largely unchanged. I am not asking for bibliographies, citation lists, or reading recommendations; I want a reasoned research agenda that takes the physics analogy I have sketched seriously without presupposing its validity.
