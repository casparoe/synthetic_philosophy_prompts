A school of contemporary theorists advocates what they call epistocratic governance: citizens must pass rigorous standardized competency examinations to earn voting rights, and the day-to-day implementation of law is delegated to predictive algorithms that continuously optimize policy outputs from social-data feedback loops. The proponents defend this not as tyranny but as expert cultivation—like a garden tended by master gardeners rather than left to wild, untended growth.

My opening challenge: You have criticized revolutionary politics for treating the state as a machine to be dismantled and rebuilt according to abstract rational blueprints, and you have contrasted such mechanistic construction with organic growth that respects an inner nature.
