I sit on our univ's pilot-program review board — we approve small-scale student wellness experiments — and theres a proposal up for Thursday that I can't stop thinking about. A cog-sci grad team plus one phil postdoc want to run a six-week study called The Hedonic Ledger. Participants log every activity (studying, eating, sex, listening to music) on a single 0-100 "pleasure" scale, then an algorithm reshuffles their weekly schedule to maximize the summed score. The researchers claim they've cracked the old heterogeneity problem by isolating a "phenomenal overlap kernel" shared by all pleasant experiences, so aggregation is legit and not just apples-to-oranges math. Half the board says its harmless quantified-self stuff; half says its conceptually incoherent because pleasures dont share a common measurable quality, which means the intervention is basically random and the consent form misleads people about what they're optimizing for.

Draft the board memo: recommend approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with a concrete bottom line and a time frame for response. Work through whether there's actually a shared phenomenal feature being summed, not just a linguistic label. Be honest about the strongest case for letting it run. Also, say explicitly what would change your mind — which finding or argument would flip the recommendation — and flag the spot where your reasoning is shakiest. Write it like a real institutional doc the researchers could reply to, but keep the tone readable, not legalistic.
