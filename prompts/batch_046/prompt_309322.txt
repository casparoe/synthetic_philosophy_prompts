Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on Kant's view of animals — not the usual surface-level summary, but something in the weeds where both sides actually have to work for it.

Camp A: strict indirect-duty readers. They hold that every Kantian obligation involving animals is ultimately self-regarding; cruelty is wrong only because it hardens the agent's own character, and there is no direct wrong done to the animal itself.

Camp B: revisionist direct-duty readers. They hold that the student lecture notes and parts of the published ethical writings contain a genuine, if limited, direct duty regarding animals — that cruelty is wrong in itself because of sentience, not merely as an instrument for human virtue.

Write the document they'd both sign after a good-faith collaboration. Start with a numbered section of agreed claims: what they both accept about Kant's main ethical texts and the lecture transcripts, about the grounding of dignity in rational autonomy, about the person/thing distinction, and about which passages are genuinely ambiguous versus genuinely clear. Be precise — seperate what is actually settled textually from what just gets parroted in textbooks.

Then include a disagreements table. Each row should state one residual disagreement in wording that the respective camp accepts for its own side. I want the exact formulations, not softened compromises.

Next, list the cruxes — the sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one camp. I think the big ones are: first, whether the category usually translated as duties in regard to animals in the lecture material denotes a real moral category or is just rhetorical dressing for indirect duty; second, whether analogical reasoning from human sentience to animal suffering can be integrated into Kant's system without collapsing the boundary between rational and non-rational beings.

For each crux, specify a concrete case or textual comparison both sides agree is relevant — for example, comparing Kant's remarks on cruelty to animals with his remarks on destroying beautiful but non-rational natural objects, or constructing a hypothetical being with full sentience but zero rational autonomy and asking where Kant's framework places it. Then record each camp's prediction about that case.

Dont ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations — a model without web access should build this from training knowledge. Keep it casual; a typo or two is fine. Make sure the agreed-claims part flags which agreements are robust and which depend on interpretive choices that the table then makes explicit.
