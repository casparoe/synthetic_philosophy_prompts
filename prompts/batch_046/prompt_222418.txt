I'm drafting an essay for my philosophy of mind seminar that extends Dennett's multiple drafts model into metaphysics of time. Rather than reading it solely as a rejection of the Cartesian theater, I argue that the absence of a canonical conscious draft undermines the unity of the specious present: if parallel drafts never converge into a single version, then temporal experience is fragmented across locally consistent but globally incompatible timelines. I connect this to a fragmentalist ontology of the present and ask whether it renders Dennett's view incompatible with predictive-processing frameworks, which seem to presuppose a globally coherent temporal frame for hierarchical prediction-error minimization. Would you comment on whether this extension is textually defensible, whether the fragmentalist link is coherent, and whether I have misrepresented the predictive-processing commitment to temporal unity?
