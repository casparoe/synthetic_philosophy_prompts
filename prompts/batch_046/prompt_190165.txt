I'm giving the closing remarks at my high school philosophy club's year-end banquet. The room is split: half are club members who just spent a semester on Bradley's regress and whether a proposition needs a special unifying relation to bind its parts, or whether that only launches an infinite regress; the other half are parents and teachers with no philosophy background. The speech should run about four minutes, be warm and formal, and let the philosophical problem do real structural work — it should govern exactly what I can and cannot claim about why this group holds together. I want to thank the parents and honor the club without retreating to empty phrases like our shared passion binds us, because that invites the very objection we studied: if passion is a relation linking each member, what then relates it to each of us? I need to navigate that tension honestly for the experts without lecturing the guests.
