Consider a philosopher of rationality whose verdicts on diachronic choice cases are as follows: an agent with cyclic preferences who refuses every pairwise trade is rational; an agent with transitive preferences who accepts A-to-B and B-to-C but then refuses C-to-A is rational; an agent whose preferences shift only after explicit belief revision is rational; an agent who applies structurally unjustified, inconsistent time-discounts is irrational; and an agent who is money-pumped once but then permanently adopts a resolution strategy is rational. Now consider this new case: the agent maintains perfectly transitive preferences within each waking period, but across sleep cycles the ordering shifts predictably in a cycle (A > B > C > A) with no change in underlying beliefs or values other than temporal framing; because trades never occur across sleep boundaries, no actual pump takes place, though one would occur if cross-boundary exchange were possible. Predict her verdict on this agent, identify the implicit principle she seems to be running—intertemporal stability across all states versus coherence confined to a single deliberative frame—name the competing rule that accommodates her prior verdicts equally well, and specify the single further case that would best distinguish between these two principles. Write your answer in American English.
