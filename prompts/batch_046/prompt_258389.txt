ive been stuck on this old split in Sartre reading groups for years and it feels like were just running in circles. on one side people treat radical freedom in Being and Nothingness as a structural fact about consciousness—like, being-for-itself always has this coefficient of adversity built in, so bad faith isnt a moral failure you can drop, its just the unavoidable tension between facticity and transcendence. on the other side the authenticity folks read it as a specific kind of self-deception you maintain by choice, something you could own up to and dissolve. both camps cite the waiter and the homosexual, both talk about the facticity loop, and neither has moved since like the 70s. my hunch is that this isnt even about Sartre—its one side doing ontology and the other doing normative ethics, using 'freedom' for totally different jobs. but i could be wrong; maybe theres a hidden empirical disagreement about whether social roles can causally constrain consciousness in a way that makes the waiter genuinely determined? so is this a verbal stall, a cross-purpose disagreement, or a real hidden question about causal determination? and if its the first two, what would actually count as progress—finding a contradiction in the later ethical notebooks, or should we just stop asking if Sartre is consistent and ask which framing is more useful? diagnose it with actual arguments from both sides, not a lit survey.
