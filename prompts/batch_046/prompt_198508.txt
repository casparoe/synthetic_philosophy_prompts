As a journalist covering philosophy of science and mathematics, I have been working with a hybrid finitist position that treats Hilbert's hotel as a diagnostic of structural indeterminacy rather than a reductio of actual infinity: it claims that an actually infinite set of occupied rooms cannot have a determinate state, since Cantor's diagonal argument always produces an occupant whose presence or absence is undecidable within any assignment rule, and that this is compounded by an information-theoretic constraint—no realizable register can stabilize membership conditions across an actual infinity. On this view, actual infinities are not false but conceptually unstable. What would genuinely count against it? I want more than the standard Platonist reply that mathematics is not about physical registers. Specifically, which formal results—say, about determinacy in infinite model theory, computability over infinite domains, or the coherence of infinite structures in physics—would undermine the claim that actual infinities are structurally indeterminate? Which intuitions about mathematical practice or scientific application would defenders have to concede as damaging, and which could they absorb by tightening the informational constraint? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, note whether the position collapses into familiar anti-realism or nominalism if it concedes too much, and say whether it is well insulated or genuinely exposed.
