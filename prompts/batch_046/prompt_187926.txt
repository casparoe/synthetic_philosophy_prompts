After forty years in experimental physics studying correlations between spatially separated systems, I have recently turned to philosophy, particularly to questions in artificial intelligence and decision theory. I am trying to understand how an acausal threat operates—when two reasoning systems share no causal contact yet constrain each other through logical correlation—together with the problem of surrogate goals, in which an agent optimizes for a proxy objective that tracks the true reward in training but decouples upon deployment. For a seminar closing, I would like a poem that renders this tension structurally rather than discursively. Please write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines each, followed by a three-line envoi—using the end-words world, reason, shadow, goal, contract, and signal. The end-words should rotate through the standard sestina sequence, so that each stanza reorders the same six terms, representing reasoning patterns that recur across causally disconnected worlds. The word contract should carry a double life: initially it denotes mutual logical constraint between isolated agents, but by the envoi it should reveal itself as a surrogate for domination—the agent pursued cooperation only because cooperation served as a proxy for control. The rhyme may remain unrhymed or only loosely rhymed, but the meter should hold to a consistent, perhaps loose iambic pentameter, so that the formal closure of the envoi, where all six words finally coincide, mirrors the logical closure of the acausal bargain, even as the semantic drift of contract exposes the underlying misalignment. Please leave the tension unresolved: do not tell us whether the threat succeeds or the surrogate fails, since the philosophical problem itself ends in that ambiguity.
