As a novelist revising a manuscript in which the protagonist's consciousness is structured as a strange loop—working from Hofstadter's conception of the "I" as a self-referential symbol that points back to the very system generating it—I need a reusable editorial procedure I can run alone, without philosophical reference works, to evaluate first-person passages. For each passage I must decide whether it realizes a genuine strange loop, in which the narrative symbol refers back to its own conditions of narration in a tangled hierarchy, or whether it merely deploys conventional introspection, unreliable narration, or metafictional framing. The stakes of misclassification are structural: a false positive flattens the ontology into gimmickry, while a false negative collapses the architecture into ordinary self-consciousness.

Please construct an ordered decision procedure—operational tests, stopping conditions, and what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage—that a practitioner can apply in under fifteen minutes per passage without formal logic training. Translate Hofstadter's distinctions among direct reference, indirect self-reference, and tangled hierarchy into editorial criteria that survive simplification, and explicitly flag which steps are judgment calls dressed as procedure and which can be mechanized. The procedure must halt with a clear output—loop present, loop absent, or indeterminate—while marking the exact point where it hands the decision back to artistic intuition rather than conceptual analysis, particularly where linguistic form underdetermines ontological structure.

Then apply the procedure step-by-step to three constructed examples: first, a passage in which the narrator describes her own act of remembering as "the remembering that is happening in me now," apparently looping back upon itself; second, a passage in which the narrator states "I am writing these words," which seems self-referential but may only index the fictional frame; and third, a passage in which the procedure technically detects a loop but doing so produces an artistically destructive result—where structural fidelity demands retention despite narrative damage, yielding an answer I will not want. For each, show the evaluation path, identify any misfire, and state what the procedure cannot settle.

The deliverable should be honest about its limits: it can assess whether the textual conditions for a strange loop are satisfied, but it cannot determine aesthetic value or ontological reality outside the fiction. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or reading recommendations; I have no access to academic libraries during revision and need something executable in a study with only the manuscript and this checklist.
