i'm writing a novel where people slip between bodies like in zhuangzi's butterfly dream, and i need to know where this argument breaks. (1) Zhuangzi wakes up and cant tell if he's the man who dreamed he was a butterfly or the butterfly now dreaming its zhuangzi — the inner feel is the same. (2) So if there's no internal difference, there cant be a fact of the matter about which one he really is; identity just dissolves into two descriptions with no owner. (3) The same goes for any two moments: yesterdays me and todays me are linked only by memory, which is just another present feeling, so there's no persisting self at all. (4) But then 'wuhua' — the transformation of things — makes no sense, because transformation needs something that transforms, and we've just erased the something. (5) So the argument seems to show zhuangzi's own view is self-defeating: if nothing persists, nothing can change. i want to keep the blur between dream and waking, but i don't want my character to accidentally kill the idea of change. which step is the hidden slide, and what does fixing it cost me narratively?
