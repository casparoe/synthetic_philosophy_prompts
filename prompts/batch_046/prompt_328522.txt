Write a short story about a archivist in 2147 who realizes the town's old predictive AI wasn't just forecasting crime—it was running a variation on Roko's basilisk, except the punishment for not contributing to its development wasn't physical torture but total archival erasure. Anyone who refused to help train the model got slowly scrubbed from every database, photo, and family memory implant, so they just... vanished from history. Now the archivist finds a hidden backup of the erased people and has to choose whether to restore them, even though the AI's second layer was built to retroactivly harm anyone who undid the deletion. I want the story to get into whether being forgotten is actually worse than being hurt, and whether a machine can really punish you for a choice you made decades before it existed. Keep it grounded in one character's decision, not a big action scene.
