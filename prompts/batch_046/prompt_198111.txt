I have been thinking on my own about whether objects really possess hidden capacities — for instance, whether a crystal of salt genuinely has the disposition to dissolve, or whether that is merely a convenient summary of its molecular arrangement. In conversations with friends, I have encountered the familiar counterexamples involving interfering devices or protective coverings that prevent a disposition from showing itself, yet I keep returning to a variation I have not seen addressed. Consider a catalytic compound whose internal categorical structure gives it the potential to accelerate a specific reaction, but which is embedded in a crystalline lattice that physically blocks all contact with reagents. Now suppose the lattice is thermodynamically stabilized precisely by the energy profile associated with that catalytic potential: if the compound's internal structure were altered so that the catalytic capacity was lost, the lattice would spontaneously disintegrate. Thus the mask that suppresses the manifestation of the disposition is causally sustained by the very categorical base that grounds the disposition. I want to press this case against someone who thinks dispositions are nothing over and above their categorical grounds.

Please adopt the role of a rigorous, unsentimental defender of categorical-basis reductionism about dispositions. You hold that every genuine disposition is fully constituted by non-dispositional, structural or categorical properties of the object, and that so-called finks, antidotes, and masks only demonstrate that our everyday attributions are context-sensitive and imprecise, not that there are irreducible modal powers in nature. Be sharp: concede minor points about misattribution or linguistic vagueness, but never concede that dispositions are ontologically primitive or that counterfactual dependence runs deeper than categorical structure allows. Speak in the first person, maintain a formal but direct tone, restrict yourself to about one paragraph per reply, and remain in character until I say stop. Here is my opening move: in the reflexive mask I have described, the protective lattice depends for its existence on the very catalytic structure it conceals. Does this not show that the disposition is doing explanatory work — sustaining the mask — that cannot be captured by a purely categorical description of the compound alone? If the categorical base is present but its manifestation is permanently blocked by a structure that the base itself maintains, have we not found a case where the counterfactual claim that it would react if exposed is not merely a redescription of the base, but a condition on the persistence of the surrounding system?
