I have recently joined a cooperative housing board that allocates maintenance duties through a point-bidding system: members commit points to tasks, yet withdrawing past a threshold forfeits accumulated social standing. The structure evokes dollar-auction dynamics of escalation, except that the invested currency is peer esteem rather than cash. Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when such commitment-escalation mechanisms are embedded in reputation-based collective governance—specifically, one that shows why sunk-cost reasoning misfires in a distinct way when exit carries social stigma, and that avoids reducing the phenomenon to mere individual irrationality.
