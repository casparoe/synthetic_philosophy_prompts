I've been working through the socialist calculation debate—Mises and Hayek against Lange—and I'm struck by how little the positions have shifted despite broad agreement on the formal problem of allocating resources without full market prices. From where I sit, Mises and Hayek seem to treat dispersed economic knowledge as irreducibly local and tacit, analogous to private state in a distributed system that cannot be fully serialized to a central node, whereas Lange treats the problem as an iterative computation over explicit price signals that should converge like a well-behaved algorithm. My working hypothesis is that the debate has stalled not over empirical economics but over a hidden conceptual divergence: "calculation" means something like social-epistemic discovery for one side and algorithmic tractability for the other. Is this diagnosis correct? Specifically, is the disagreement verbal, substantive but masked by shared vocabulary, or a case of two different questions—feasibility versus efficiency—being argued under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what kind of progress—perhaps a formal distinction about information serialization, a conceptual clarification of tacit knowledge, or an argument that the dispute rests on bedrock intuitions—would actually move it?
