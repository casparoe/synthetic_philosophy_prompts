I'm in this philosophy of mind seminar and we got totally stuck on whether qualia are just an incoherent posit — like the Chase and Sanborn line that if you try to actually specify what a quale is you end up contradicting yourself (private but shareable, non-physical but causally efficacious, etc.). Some people in the group just keep saying "well I know what it's like to see red," and others say Chase and Sanborn are holding first-person experience to an unfair standard that we don't apply to other theoretical posits. We've been going in circles for two sessions and I'm convinced we're past eachother on what kind of question this even is.

So I want an actual diagnosis: is the disagreement verbal — are the defenders using "qualia" to mean raw phenomenology while the incoherence camp is attacking a bloated theoretical construct? Or is it substantive, with one side treating introspective access as legitimate ontology and the other treating it as a category mistake? And there's this other thing that's been bugging me: the idea that qualia are "phenomenally transparent" — you can't describe them without circularity because any description either smuggles in physical properties or just repeats the experience-name. Does that transparency actually bolster the incoherence charge, or does it just show qualia are primitives that don't need further analysis?

Finally, what would actually settle it? Would a neuroscience result help, or a logical proof of contradiction, or is the whole thing just bedrock intuitions about what explanation has to look like? I feel like if we don't figure out whether this is a conceptual, empirical, or normative disagreement, we're never getting out of the loop.
