Write a philosophical dialogue between two interlocutors—call them the Pragmatist and the Dissolutionist—concerning whether Lewis Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise exposes a genuine limitation in the justification of deductive inference or merely a category mistake about rule-following. To keep the discussion from collapsing into well-rehearsed positions, frame the debate around a hypothetical symbolic AI architecture that applies logical rules not through implicit procedural competence but through an explicit chain of meta-rules, each citation of a rule being itself treated as a premise that requires further authorization. The Pragmatist should argue that this architecture reveals the heart of Carroll's problem: every formalization of an inference rule merely pushes the need for application back one level, so that deduction ultimately rests on an unformalized practical grasp—something analogous to adopting an intentional stance toward one's own reasoning—that current AI lacks unless it can genuinely adopt norms rather than merely trace them. The Dissolutionist should reply that Carroll confuses the object-level use of a rule with a meta-level description of that use, and that the AI's explicit citation is not an additional premise in need of justification but a transparent record of competent performance; on this view, treating rule-application as requiring further inference is as misguided as demanding a further law of motion to explain why a ball rolls downhill. Both characters must respond directly to each other's specific claims: the Pragmatist should challenge the Dissolutionist to explain what distinguishes a meaningful application from bare syntactic transformation without appealing to an unarticulated capacity, and the Dissolutionist should ask the Pragmatist why, if practical grasp does all the normative work, formal logic retains any authority beyond that of a well-trained heuristic. Maintain equal philosophical competence on both sides, avoid strawman representations, and do not request citations, bibliographies, or references to specific editions.
