Construct a thought experiment that isolates the disagreement between an epistemic-structuralist and an ontic-structuralist account of ontology identification under conditions of radical but realistic inaccessibility. Specifications: (i) design a physical system—no larger than a laboratory apparatus, using only established physics—whose macroscopic outputs are fully explained by both a continuous-field model and a discrete-particle model; (ii) embed within it a causally sealed "inert core" that is postulated by only one model to preserve internal consistency, but which has no causal connections to any observable part of the system, no spatiotemporal relations to observable parts, and no indirect measurable effects whatsoever; (iii) ensure that if the core gained even one weak indirect causal link, both models could be empirically adjusted to accommodate it, so the isolation must be structural rather than merely practical. Present the scenario in one paragraph. Then: state what each structuralist identifies as the ontology of the core, if anything; show why the ontic structuralist is committed to an entity with no empirical footprint while the epistemic structuralist denies it; identify the precise feature—structural causal inertness combined with consistency-dependence—that drives the split; and explain what happens to the case if that feature is removed. Finally, in one sentence, state how the ontic structuralist might defend the commitment without appealing to future observation. Keep the entire response under 300 words.
