I'm writing a feature on whether cultural values can be independent causes of economic change, taking Weber's Protestant ethic thesis as the central case study. In the literature I've been surveying, I keep running into the same argumentative loop, and I'm trying to determine whether it has a recognized name or is best treated as an unnamed failure mode. The loop runs like this: a researcher claims that a specific religious value—say, the Calvinist idea that disciplined labor and capital accumulation serve as visible signs of election—caused a distinctive economic behavior. Critics respond that the value was not an independent cause but was selected and reinforced because it happened to suit emerging market conditions; culture, on this view, is an effect of material incentives. The researcher then defends the causal priority of values by insisting that at some origin point the doctrine was adopted for purely internal theological reasons, with no awareness of economic benefit. But when pressed on why that particular theological interpretation prevailed over competing ones within the same tradition, the defense usually appeals to further values—psychological needs for certainty, social prestige, or aesthetic preference—without ever linking those values back to material conditions in a non-circular way. The result is an explanatory regress: values are protected from reduction to economics only by being pushed back into an ever-earlier, ever-purer cultural origin, leaving permanently unspecified the mechanism by which values actually exert causal force on economic outcomes. Is there an established term for this specific regress—the exogenous-value defense loop in cultural causation debates? And how precisely does it relate to Weber's own attempt to navigate between materialist and idealist explanations, and to the broader philosophical problem of explaining social outcomes with values? Please answer in a single paragraph of no more than 180 words, leading with your bottom line: is there a name for this loop, and what are its nearest conceptual relatives?
