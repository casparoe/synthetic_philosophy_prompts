My study partner (philosphy grad student, not me) has been throwing around takes on trolley stuff and double-effect that dont quite match the intro textbooks. She said switch is fine since you're just rerouting an existing danger, but footbridge is wrong because you're literally using the guy's body as a brake. She also said the trap-door version -- pull a lever, floor gives way, one person falls onto the tracks and stops the train -- is okay too, even though his body still stops the train, because the harm is mediated by the architecture, not your direct push. Then she said the surgeon who harvests organs from one healthy patient to save five is wrong, but she admitted she cant fully explain why the trap-door isnt also using a body as a resource. On bombing she said hitting a munitions factory with foreseen civilian casualties is permissible, but bombing civilians directly to break morale is out, because in the first case the harm is a side-effect of targeting the factory, not the point of the action.

You trigger it with a button press from outside.

Would she say thats okay or wrong? Whats the actual rule shes running -- direct bodily contact, mediation through a system, or whether the victim was already in the danger zone? And whats the one further case that would actually settle which rule it is?
