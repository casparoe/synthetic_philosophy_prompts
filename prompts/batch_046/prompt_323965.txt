Construct a decision tree for classifying philosophical positions on rational choice in cases of perfect equilibrium—the problem traditionally called Buridan's ass—where each branch is a concrete diagnostic question that a respondent could answer without knowing which named view it leads to. Begin with the commitment that an agent faces two options genuinely indistinguishable in value, accessibility, and foreseeable consequence. First branch: does rational choice require a complete, strict preference ordering, or may a rational agent hold a genuine tie? Second branch: if ties are permitted, can they be broken arbitrarily (for instance by an unbiased randomizer), or must every resolution be supported by a further reason, even when the outcomes remain equivalent? Third branch: introduce a finer-grained description of the same options—say, differences in the causal path taken, the temporal sequence experienced, or the mental act of selection itself—so that the options are tied at one level of description but diverge at another. Should rational resolution occur at the coarser grain where the tie holds, at the finer grain where it dissolves, or is the very idea of grain-sensitive resolution incoherent? From these answers, map the leaves onto actual positions (for example, a strict-ordering rationalist view, an arbitrary-selection permissivist view, a hierarchical-resolution view, a view that mandates suspension of choice, and a view that denies the coherence of perfect equilibrium). Note any combination of answers that seems unoccupied in the literature and briefly diagnose whether the vacancy is best explained by incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Explicitly flag any pair of named positions that share answers at every branch and therefore cannot be separated by your tree. The instrument should contain no branch that merely asks “do you accept view X?”; every node must be a claim about the case. Do not request bibliographies, citations, primary-source quotations, or lists of authors to read. Keep the tree to no more than eight terminal leaves.
