As a doctoral researcher in philosophy of science and decision theory, please describe the most important implications of Bostrom's simulation argument—specifically the variant that permits nested, recursively simulated populations of observers with heterogeneous prior probabilities over their own ontological status—for the rational calibration of longtermist commitments regarding the far future. I am particularly interested in whether the possibility of being a simulated agent with truncated causal reach ought to diminish the expected value assigned to actions aimed at influencing outcomes millennia hence, and whether this generates a distinctive form of epistemic paralysis that differs structurally from standard sceptical scenarios. Please present your analysis in British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; I require the conceptual argument itself rather than any reference apparatus.
