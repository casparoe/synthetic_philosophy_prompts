I was chatting with a colleague who covers cognitive science for a science magazine, and she threw out this half-baked idea she’s been turning over: what if applying Dennett’s intentional stance to AI isn’t an all-or-nothing thing, but has to be fractal, meaning we might attribute beliefs differently at each processing layer of a large model because there’s no single representational core? I’m trying to write this up without sounding like I’ve lost the plot. So I want to ask: what are the most important implicatons of taking this layered, non-global version of the intentional stance seriously for AI? If belief-attribution is fractured across levels, does that change how we should think about moral responsibility — like, can we hold a system accountable for a belief that only exists at one layer? And does it blur the line between predictive accuracy and actual understanding in a way that’s different from the usual debates? I’m also stuck on whether recognition still plays any role: with humans, attributing beliefs is tied to recognizing the other as a subject, but if we do it fractally to a machine, is that recognition just a metaphor? I don’t need a bibliography, just want to feel out whether this framing holds water or collapses immediately.
