I was discussing with my friends over dinner about Zhuangzi's butterfly dream and we got totally stuck. My friend keeps insisting it's basically the same move as Descartes evil demon - you know, how do I know I'm not actually a butterfly dreaming I'm human? But that feels wrong to me, like we're forcing Zhuangzi into a Western skepticism box. The conceptual issue I'm trying to wrap my head around is this: Zhuangzi doesn't seem worried about being deceived about external reality. Instead he asks whether there's a real distinction between the self that was Zhuangzi and the self that was the butterfly. So is the problem epistemic - about not knowing which state you're in - or is it metaphysical, about whether personal identity can survive transformation?

And here's where I get really confused. If you take the transformation reading seriously - that hua means there's no persistant core self - then doesn't that actually dissolve the dream skepticism problem rather than create it? Because Descartes needs a stable I that's being deceived, but if Zhuangzi is saying the I is just a temporary arrangement, then there's no single subject to be fooled. But then what does it even mean to ask whether I'm Zhuangzi or the butterfly? That question seems to presuppose there's an I that could be either. So is Zhuangzi's point self-undermining, or am I missing a third option where epistemic humility and ontological flux can coexist? I'd love an explanation that doesn't just cite seconday literature but actually walks through the conceptual tension, maybe pointing out where the analogy to Cartesian doubt breaks down and whether there's a coherent way to be skeptical about identity rather than about reality.
