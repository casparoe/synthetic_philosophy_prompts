ok so in our small ai-philosophy seminar we've been going in circles on orthogonality versus instrumental convergence, and i think part of the problem is that people keep smuggling in evolutionary analogies without noticing. the orthogonality camp treats intelligence like a chess program: any objective function can sit on top of arbitrary reasoning capacity. the convergence camp treats it like an evolutionary fitness landscape: long-term agents will always climb toward resource accumulation and self-preservation regardless of initial utility. but heres where i think its stalled: nobody is clear on whether 'intelligence' here means 'general problem-solving ability' or 'expected utility maximization with a stable preference ordering', and those two come apart in weird ways if you allow for goal plasticity or value drift under reflection. is the disagreement actually about the modal possibility of strange goals, or about the dynamical stability of goals under recursive self-improvement? and does the evolutionary analogy even apply if we think of AI as being engineered rather than selected? i want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is -- verbal, empirical, or bedrock intuition -- and whether anything like a formal model of preference stability could actually move it, or if we're just talking past eachother with different pictures of agency.
