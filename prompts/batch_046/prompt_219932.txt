You are a philosopher-poet with expertise in normative theory and formal verse. Before composing, take a moment to brainstorm: identify six end-words that can rotate through a sestina, map each of the six sestets to a distinct stage of indirect justification—such as the unidealized agent, the rational chooser behind a veil of ignorance, the affected moral patient, the institutional architect, the critic of abstraction, and the posture of reflective equilibrium—and decide how the envoi will stage the unresolved question of whether the procedure yields a direct norm or only its shadow.

Write a sestina of exactly thirty-nine lines (six sestets plus a three-line envoi) on indirect normativity and idealized deliberation. Choose six end-words—perhaps reason, consent, veil, actual, agreement, and norm, or substitutes of your own—that name elements in the deliberative process. The rotation of these end-words should do philosophical work: each stanza should speak from one idealized position so that no stanza delivers the norm immediately, and the shifting word-order should mirror how justification moves indirectly through successive frames rather than proceeding in a straight line. The content should be accurate enough that a specialist in contractualism or deliberative democracy recognizes the stages and tensions, not merely a list of technical terms; at the same time, it should read as a coherent poem to someone with no background in the field.

In the envoi, gather all six end-words into three lines that ask, structurally, whether the indirect procedure finally grounds a direct obligation for the actual agent, or whether it leaves only the form of agreement without binding content. Do not sermonize or resolve the tension; let the grammar and pairing of words keep the problem open. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific texts that would require verification; paraphrase any theoretical ideas in your own language. First, briefly list your six end-words and the deliberative stage assigned to each stanza. Then present the poem itself.
