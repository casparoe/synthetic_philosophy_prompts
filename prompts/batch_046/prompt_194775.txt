I'm trying to nail down my actual view on punishment by looking at my gut reactions, not by reading theory.

Drunk driver kills a pedestrian: prison feels right, he owes a moral debt, not just deterrence. Same driver but forced at gunpoint to drive and drink: no prison, he wasn't the real source of harm. Factory owner picks cheap safety gear, saves money, nothing bad happens for years, then a worker dies — I still want him punished, he "got away with it" for ages but the risk was thier choice. But if we have perfect brain-scans and see someone will definitely commit violence next week, locking them up now feels wrong, like punishing for a crime that hasn't occured yet. Politician lies in a campaign ad that accidentally sparks a deadly riot; she never wanted violence. Still criminal punishment, not just a lawsuit. Kid raised in a cult kills at 18, genuinely believing outsiders deserve death: containment maybe, but not the same moral kind of punishment as the drunk driver.

The tension is that cases 3 and 4 both turn on risk, yet I split them hard. Case 5 also involves unintended harm but feels closer to case 1. Is there one principle that saves all five verdicts, or which one should I drop? What does it predict for an unlisted case — say, we find the hidden grave of a long-dead criminal who committed a serious crime; should we punish thier estate?

Lead with the bottom line, then walk through the cases. Keep it under 250 words.
