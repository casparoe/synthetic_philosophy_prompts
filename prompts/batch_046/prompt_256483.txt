Give an example of a conceptual tension that arises when a no-trade aggregation result—structurally analogous to Arrow’s theorem but applied to belief or forecast aggregation rather than preference—is deployed in a market-like epistemic system where participants rely on genuinely incommensurable evidential norms. Rather than using generic stock-market illustrations, construct or describe a concrete, non-obvious scenario (for instance, in climate-model ensemble weighting or in the interpretation of competing pharmaceutical trial endpoints) in which any aggregation rule must violate at least one plausible rationality condition—such as independence of irrelevant beliefs, non-dictatorship over expert judgments, or unrestricted domain across methodological frameworks—or else yield a collective judgment that none of the individual experts actually endorses. Explain why this failure should be read as a structural feature of rational aggregation across incommensurable standards rather than as a merely practical failure of the market mechanism, and briefly defend which rationality condition you think ought to be abandoned in such cases and why.
