In the philosophy of mind literature, debate over Dennett's rejection of the Cartesian theater in favor of a multiple drafts model has persisted for decades without clear movement. Advocates hold that distributing representations across time and neural substrates eliminates the need for a central observer and dissolves the binding problem; critics counter that this redescribes temporal dynamics without explaining why any draft should carry phenomenal character, or that it collapses the distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness. Both sides now draw on the same empirical work on temporal integration windows and global workspace dynamics, yet no result has been treated as decisive. I suspect the impasse is not empirical but conceptual—perhaps a disagreement over whether dissolving a problem counts as solving it, or whether the two camps are asking different questions (about cognitive architecture versus first-person ontology) under one heading.
