Write a dialogue between a virtue ethicist and a situationist psychologist who both grant that moral behavior varies sharply across contexts, but who disagree about whether virtue can be genuinely "outsourced" to environmental scaffolds. The virtue ethicist should argue that if traits like honesty or courage are primarily sustained by external structures—algorithmic nudges, institutional checks, or architectural design—then virtue ethics collapses into a theory of well-engineered compliance rather than character; praise and blame, she should insist, require that the causal work be done inside the agent. The situationist should reply not by defending pure internal traits, but by proposing that virtues have always been coupled properties of agents-in-contexts, and that recognizing this does not dissolve virtue but relocates its study: we should investigate "virtue niches" and agent-environment couplings rather than isolated dispositions. Have them respond to each other's actual moves—the ethicist should ask whether coupled properties can ground the distinctive normative weight of character judgments, and the situationist should ask whether the internalist picture survives the empirical record without becoming unfalsifiable through ad hoc adjustments. Keep both voices equally rigorous, avoid straw-manning either position, and end without forced resolution.
