Write the full specification of desiderata that a normative theory of state-culture relations must satisfy if it is to adjudicate between French republican assimilation and Anglo-American multicultural recognition without collapsing into either pole. For each requirement, state the concrete case or conceptual distinction that motivates it: for instance, how it handles practices that are simultaneously religious and civic (like head-covering in public schools), how it treats linguistic markets in which one language functions as apparently neutral infrastructure while others are marked as particularistic, and how it accounts for hybrid or third-space practices that do not map onto a single tradition of origin. Specify which desiderata are non-negotiable—such as not licensing coercive cultural erasure and not requiring shared metaphysical commitments as a condition of citizenship—and which are negotiable preferences of one camp, such as strict visibility neutrality or group-differentiated rights. Identify any subset of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable, particularly if demands for symmetric visibility and low exit-costs for minorities conflict with demands for a unified public-reason framework, and explain whether that tension is structural rather than accidental. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; a brief sketch of how laïcité and multiculturalism fare against the list is optional but not the main deliverable.
