I am preparing a taxonomy handout for an applied ethics seminar on obligations toward refugees and asylum-seekers. Please construct a decision tree that sorts positions on two questions: who counts as someone to whom admission is owed, and what form that obligation takes.

Structure the tree from the logical space, not from a list of named theorists. Each branch node must be one concrete claim or case on which positions genuinely diverge, phrased so a student can answer yes/no or choose among options without knowing which leaf it leads to. Use these four branching questions in order:

1. Is the moral category of those entitled to admission coextensive with the 1951 Convention definition (well-founded fear of persecution on protected grounds), or does it extend to people displaced by climate change, generalized violence, or economic collapse that threatens basic survival?

2. For those within the relevant category, is the primary duty of admission borne by states that caused the displacement (causal or rectificatory), by geographically proximate states regardless of causation, or distributed globally according to capacity (a cosmopolitan fair-share principle)?

3. Must the obligation be satisfied specifically by physical admission onto the state's own territory, or can it be fully discharged through non-territorial protection—for example, funding safe zones elsewhere, remote resettlement sponsorship, or protection visas that do not require presence?

4. Is there a legitimate capacity ceiling—conditions under which a state's admission duty is suspended if fulfilling it would undermine the protection of basic rights for current residents?

The tree should include at least these leaves: a strict sovereignty or minimalist view; a cosmopolitan open-borders view; a climate-reparative or causal-responsibility view; a non-territorial "protection elsewhere" view; and any other combinations the logic allows. For each leaf, give the position a concise label (or mark it "unnamed" if it lacks a well-known defender) and one sentence stating its most awkward commitment.

In addition: note any combination of answers that appears unoccupied, and briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; flag any pair of leaves that your tree fails to separate, meaning positions that answer all four nodes identically but still disagree on substance; and do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists—build the taxonomy entirely from the logical commitments.
