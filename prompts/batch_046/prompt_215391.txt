Lately I've been convinced by this scale-arguement about insects — you know, the idea that even if each ant or fly has like a 0.01% shot at being sentince, there are so many of them that the expected suffering is huge, maybe outweighing all mammel suffering combined. But I've also started exstending it to other distributed low-probability cases, like soil microbiomes or tiny neuromorphic chips running in warehouses, which makes me think either I have to take all of that seriously or there's a flaw in the scaling logic itself. What would actually count against this position? Not just 'insects probably aren't conscious,' but which formal results about agregation, which intuitions about risk pooling, which analogies to other domains does it stand or fall with? Which defeaters would the people who push this argument concede as real, and which ones do they just brush off? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, especially the ones that hit the exstension to non-biological distributed systems.
