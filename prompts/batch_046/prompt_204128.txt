In my course on the history and philosophy of economics, we have been examining the socialist calculation debate, particularly Mises's claim that rational economic calculation requires actual market prices for capital goods, Lange's proposal that a planning board can use iterative trial-and-error adjustments to shadow prices, and Hayek's argument that dispersed tacit knowledge makes central coordination impossible regardless of price mechanisms. Give an example of a conceptual issue—ideally one involving intertemporal allocation of capital goods with non-uniform depreciation patterns—where Lange's iterative shadow-price method appears to resolve the calculation problem but actually reintroduces the knowledge-coordination difficulty Hayek emphasized. The example should show why the distinction between administrative calculation in kind and genuine price-based calculation becomes conceptually blurred in this setting, without relying on the standard industrial-production illustrations. I want to see the structural tension clearly, not a historical case study.
