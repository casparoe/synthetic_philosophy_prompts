I'm giving the commencement address at the Meridian Reentry Program graduation, where my younger brother just earned his certificate after release. The audience is split between family members who understand agency only as willpower and philosophers who know the determinism debate well. I want Daniel Dennett's framework of the varieties of free will worth wanting to do real structural work in the speech: it should let me praise the graduates' genuine achievements in self-formation and responsive adjustment without pretending they exercised some libertarian, uncaused power, and without reducing their choices to mere causal outputs shaped by poverty and incarceration. The address must be accessible to relatives but precise enough that experts won't wince, especially since the philosophical backbone needs to determine what I can and cannot honestly say about responsibility here. Please separate what is settled in this area from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers of action—particularly whether Dennett's account fully resolves sourcehood objections—and do not present any contested reading of his position as consensus. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or requests for further reading.
