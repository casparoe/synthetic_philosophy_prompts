Analyze the 2020 toppling of the Edward Colston statue in Bristol and its subsequent display in a local museum through Nietzsche's tripartite distinction of monumental, antiquarian, and critical history. The standard account treats the toppling as an act of critical history—judging and condemning the past to serve present life—while the museum installation is read as a relapse into antiquarian preservation. I want a more disciplined mapping. Identify which actors and institutional responses correspond to each of the three modes; trace what happens to the statue's physical trajectory—street monument, harbor immersion, museum vitrine—as it moves between monumental exemplarity, critical destruction, and antiquarian conservation; and say whether Nietzsche's taxonomy can accommodate the museum display without distortion, or whether the display introduces a dynamic—perhaps an institutionalized form of contrition that uses preservation as punishment—that his framework does not anticipate. Finally, assess whether the episode confirms Nietzsche's warning that critical history, once victorious, tends to institutionalize itself and collapse back into antiquarianism, or whether it reveals a limit to the taxonomy itself.
