I sit on my university's Research Appeals and Equity Panel. By next Friday we must circulate a written opinion recommending a concrete decision in the case of Dr. Amina Okonkwo, an interdisciplinary scholar whose grant proposal—integrating epidemiological modeling with oral-history methodologies drawn from community archive practices—was denied under our Structured Peer Calibration Protocol. The protocol weights reviewer confidence scores by disciplinary alignment coefficients. Reviewers assigned low credibility weights to her evidentiary framework, describing her sources as lacking quantifiable provenance and characterizing the framework itself as anecdotal; the resulting composite score fell below the funding threshold. Dr. Okonkwo appeals on grounds of epistemic injustice: she claims testimonial injustice, in that her hybrid expertise was deflated because it does not match canonical disciplinary credentials, and hermeneutical injustice, in that the protocol's alignment metric renders her conceptual framework unintelligible within the evaluation architecture rather than assessing it as false. She asks us to overturn the denial, suspend use of the calibration protocol for interdisciplinary proposals pending an independent audit, and mandate re-review within sixty days by reviewers with demonstrated competence in both epidemiology and community-based archival methods. The funding office responds that the protocol is formally discipline-neutral, that the descriptors used were evidentiary rather than identity-based, and that granting the appeal would undermine meritocratic consistency and create unmanageable exception categories. Please draft the panel's formal opinion. It should state a clear recommendation—either uphold the denial or overturn it—specify the precise remedy and its time frame, and include an honest, non-caricatured treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side, whether that concerns institutional reliability and standardized evaluation norms or the structural inability of current metrics to recognize hybrid epistemic frameworks.
