She claims the harm is not merely psychological but strikes at the user's extended practical identity, since the object functions as a stable locus for ongoing projects and social commitments. Elias counters with the transparency thesis: he holds that in such environments users never genuinely interact with objects, only with representations of other agents' intentions mediated through an interface. Therefore, destroying the garden is always an interpersonal or psychological wrong, never an object-directed harm; treating it otherwise, he says, is a reification error that mistakes interface states for independent entities. The dialogue should be a real exchange—each philosopher must respond directly to the other's specific claims about persistence conditions, collective governance, the nature of practical investment, and whether identity-based disruption can exist without object-directed harm.
