I am preparing a philosophical analysis of family policy for a graduate seminar, and I want to be sure I am not simply projecting my own political commitments onto institutional design. Here is the concrete practice that puzzles me. First, parental leave is nominally gender-neutral and shared, but a reserved portion for each parent operates on a use-it-or-lose-it basis that expires if not taken within a narrow window tied to the child’s first year. Second, public childcare subsidies cover only hours that align with conventional full-time employment schedules; parents working evenings, nights, or irregular shifts must pay the full market rate for care during those hours. Third, tax benefits for dual-earner households phase out in ways that treat the family as a single economic unit for income calculations, yet treat each parent as an independent agent for leave entitlements and labor-market participation requirements. The result is a system that claims to support diverse family forms but structurally rewards one very specific arrangement: two parents in stable, synchronized, daytime employment, with care divided according to a particular temporal pattern.

What philosophical commitments does this cluster presuppose?
