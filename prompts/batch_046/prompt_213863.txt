The Principal Principle debate — that rational credence should match known objective chance except for admissable evidence — has been going in circles for decades. Humeans treat it as a consistency rule over actual frequencies; anti-Humeans treat it as deference to real modal propensities. Every argument about admissibility, circularity, and whether you can condition on information about the chance-setup itself has been made, yet neither camp budges an inch. My working guess is that this isn't about chance at all but about whether Bayesian updating allows self-reference — treating your own credence state as part of the evidence — and that the two sides are answering different questions: one normative-epistemological, one metaphysical-modal. But it could also be verbal: "objective chance" just means different things. Or there could be a hidden formal problem about indexical conditioning that nobody has actually proved.
