I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind, working on the architecture of conscious integration, and I have been trying to isolate a particular failure mode in split-brain cases that does not seem to line up neatly with the standard taxonomy. The pattern is this: after commissurotomy, the left hemisphere not only produces post-hoc explanations for behaviors initiated by the right hemisphere—the familiar interpreter phenomenon—but, more generally, constructs a unified phenomenal field that treats its own contents as exhaustive. The right hemisphere’s concurrent experiences are not represented as missing, suppressed, or denied; rather, they appear never to have registered as a lacuna at all. For example, when stimuli are confined to the left visual field and the left hand selects an object on that basis, the speaking hemisphere may report that it saw nothing relevant in that region, yet it does not report an empty space or a gap in vision; it describes the scene as complete and coherent, as if the left-field information had never been part of the intended survey. In other paradigms, the left hemisphere integrates a partial cue into a seamless narrative without marking the contribution that came from the other side, producing what looks like a single stream of awareness that is structurally blind to its own partiality.

What I am after is whether there is an established term for this specific configuration: not dual consciousness, not confabulation in the ordinary sense (since there is no explicit false belief about a specific action being repaired), not anosognosia (since the deficit is not acknowledged and then denied, but seems absent from the field of awareness altogether), and not a blindsight analog (since there is no report of “something missing here”). It seems closer to an overactive unification mechanism that occludes rather than integrates, but I want to know whether the literature has carved this out separately—perhaps as a subtype of interpreter-driven filling-in, or as a distinct breakdown in the meta-representation of conscious boundaries. If it has no dedicated name, I would like a precise coinage with clear boundary conditions: what distinguishes it from normal perceptual filling-in, from ordinary split-brain conflict, and from cases in which the patient genuinely reports two simultaneous but incompatible experiences? And which empirical profile—say, a patient who consistently fails to notice left-field omissions without ever generating an explicit explanation for them—would best demonstrate that we are dealing with this phenomenon rather than with one of its near neighbors?
