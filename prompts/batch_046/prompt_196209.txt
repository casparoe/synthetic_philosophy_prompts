For a philosophy reading group I am drafting a brief audit of my own commitments on moral standing and hypocrisy in blame. Here is my position set, stated as precisely as I can manage:

1. I take blame to be a form of moral address: it presupposes that blamer and blamed stand in a shared normative expectation, and without that shared framework blame misfires rather than merely being imprudent.
2. I hold that hypocrisy undermines standing globally within a given wrong-type: if I am guilty of the very same moral failure I condemn in you, I lack standing to blame you for it, irrespective of our personal relationship.
3. At the same time, I think standing is relation-specific: I can lack standing to blame a stranger for failing to keep a promise while retaining standing to blame my partner for the same failure, because the density of shared expectation varies with the relationship.
4. I also maintain that being structurally implicated in an unjust practice—say, benefiting from an exploitative supply chain—does not by itself cancel my standing to blame others who participate in it, unless I have also violated a parallel personal duty of my own.
5. I think private, unexpressed blame is governed by less stringent standing conditions than public or institutional blame, since the risks of moral harm and the demands for public accountability are substantially lower.
6. I believe hypocrisy is vice-specific rather than global across all domains: being a hypocrite about my carbon footprint does not strip me of standing to blame someone for deliberate cruelty, because the normative expectations at issue belong to different moral registers.
7. Finally, I hold that genuine reform and adequate amends restore standing prospectively but not retroactively: once reformed, I may blame others going forward, yet I remain disqualified from blaming them for past actions that mirror my own pre-reform conduct.

Please audit this set. I want you to identify which pairs of commitments genuinely pull against each other, which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension how severe it is and what the cheapest revision would be. Order the genuine tensions by severity. Then tell me which single revision—giving up or significantly modifying one commitment—would clean up the most conflicts overall. Write your answer in American English, and keep the tone formal and analytical rather than conversational.
