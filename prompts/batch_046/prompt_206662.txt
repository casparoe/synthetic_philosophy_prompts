I have been reflecting on why I sometimes feel genuine sorrow or loyalty toward characters in long-form interactive stories, knowing full well that they are constructed by writers and have no inner lives. I recently experienced a similar feeling toward an AI assistant that recalls our previous conversations, even though I understand it is only generating predictions. I understand philosophers discuss something called the paradox of fiction, and that it demonstrates belief is not required for emotion. If that is correct, then my attachment to both the fictional character and the AI seems equally legitimate, yet both objects lack real existence. This leads me to ask: is there a meaningful philosophical distinction between emotional investment in a fictional narrative and in a conversational system that simulates continuity, or should I regard both as mere misfires of my emotional apparatus? Furthermore, if belief truly plays no role, ought I to honor these feelings as genuine commitments, or actively suppress them because their targets are imaginary?
