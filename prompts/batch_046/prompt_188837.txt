The disagreement between Orwellian and Stalinesque interpretations of the color phi phenomenon — together with related temporal illusions such as the cutaneous rabbit and flash-lag — has persisted for decades without clear resolution. Both sides accept the psychophysical results: subjects sincerely report unified motion and intermediate color changes that do not map discretely onto the sequential stimuli actually presented. The Orwellian holds that conscious experience itself is retroactively revised, so that the phenomenal content genuinely includes properties (a mid-flight color shift, continuous motion) constructed only after later stimuli have arrived. The Stalinesque denies that experience is rewritten; instead, the brain produces a smoothed post-hoc report or narrative, leaving the underlying experiential fragments intact but misrepresented in judgment. Despite this shared data, the debate repeatedly cycles through the same maneuvers — appeals to reportability, appeals to the transparency of consciousness, and invocations of a phenomenal versus access distinction — with neither camp conceding ground.
