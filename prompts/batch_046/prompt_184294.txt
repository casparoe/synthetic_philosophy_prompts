As a journalist covering science and philosophy of mind, I have been trying to understand a distinction that keeps surfacing in recent consciousness research: the difference between the hard problem of explaining subjective experience and what philosophers call the meta-problem of explaining why we think subjective experience poses a special explanatory challenge in the first place. Could you explain this conceptual distinction in some depth? I am particularly interested in whether fully accounting for the cognitive and evolutionary mechanisms that generate our problem-intuitions—perhaps through introspective limitations or adaptive signaling architectures—would dissolve the original explanatory gap, or whether it merely relocates it. Furthermore, is consciousness genuinely unique in generating this kind of second-order puzzle, or do analogous meta-problems arise in other philosophical domains, such as the philosophy of biology regarding life, or the philosophy of computation regarding intentionality? I am looking for a conceptual map of the terrain rather than a survey of specific researchers.
