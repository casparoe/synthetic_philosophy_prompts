Write a short, formal dialogue between two analytic metaphysicians—Dr. Kessler, who defends a Lewisian counterpart-theoretic account of modal discourse, and Dr. Voss, who holds that individuals can be strictly identical across possible worlds—set during a workshop session on modal ontology. Rather than relying solely on familiar personal-identity examples, instruct them to examine the modal status of a particular kind of engineered artifact: a functional computing mechanism composed of modular, interchangeable parts, where the system's identity depends not on any single component but on the precise relational and spatial arrangement of all parts working together. Define three worlds for discussion: in the actual world, the mechanism operates with its original modules; in world W1, every module has been gradually replaced over time with physically indistinguishable replicas arranged in the identical relational pattern; in world W2, the original modules survive intact but have been reassembled into a structurally different configuration that performs a different computation.

Dr. Kessler should argue that in W1 there is a highly similar counterpart of the mechanism, but not the very same one, and should explain how counterpart relations—grounded in both qualitative and relational resemblance—allow us to evaluate modal claims without committing to cross-world identity. Dr. Voss should argue that there is a genuine fact of the matter about whether the original mechanism survives into W1 or W2, grounded in essential structural or functional properties, and should press Dr. Kessler on how counterpart theory can sustain true de re modal claims—such as "this mechanism could have malfunctioned"—without implicitly treating the actual-world object as present in other worlds.

Require a genuine exchange: each speaker must directly answer the specific objection just raised by the other, advancing the argument rather than restating a fixed position. Both interlocutors should be equally sophisticated; neither should be reduced to a strawman. The dialogue should remain conceptually rigorous but avoid requesting bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or appeals to obscure technical frameworks outside mainstream modal metaphysics. Keep the focus on whether relational, modular objects pose a distinctive challenge to the distinction between counterpart relations and transworld identity.
