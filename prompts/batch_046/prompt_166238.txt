I teach an advanced undergraduate seminar in social epistemology and I am drafting the final exam. I want one essay question—roughly 90 minutes, 800 to 1,000 words—on peer disagreement, specifically conciliationism versus steadfastness. The question must not be answerable by summarizing the standard meteorologist case or reciting the equal-weight view. Instead, center it on the following constructed scenario: two equally credentialed climate-risk modelers, A and B, share an identical dataset and identical disciplinary training. A applies a frequentist robustness protocol that treats outlier years as noise to be down-weighted; B applies a precautionary Bayesian adjustment that treats the same outliers as potential structural-break signals requiring prior revision. They agree on every descriptive statistical fact, but they disagree about whether the robustness protocol itself belongs to the shared evidential base or is a normative methodological choice that breaks peer symmetry. Ask students to determine whether this constitutes a genuine peer disagreement that demands conciliation, or whether B can remain steadfast on the grounds that A’s protocol smuggles in an unrecognized normative weighting—without collapsing into general skepticism about expert disagreement.

The model answer should be about 700 words and must explicitly make the following three moves: (1) analyze whether peerhood requires shared methodological norms or only shared descriptive evidence, with attention to the hybrid evidential-normative character of the case; (2) explain how a conciliationist can accommodate methodological divergence without requiring B to revise her belief—specifically by distinguishing calibration disagreement from first-order belief disagreement; and (3) identify the specific cost to steadfastness if B’s resistance depends on claiming privileged access to normative calibration rather than on an evidential asymmetry. Provide a marking scheme that separates an A answer (makes all three moves, treats the disagreement as hybrid, and prices the conciliationist response precisely) from a B answer (treats the case as purely evidential and therefore applies standard conciliationism mechanically, missing the normative-methodological layer) and from a C answer (merely defines conciliationism and steadfastness without applying them to the scenario). Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific named texts.
