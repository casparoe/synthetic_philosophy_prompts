I'm advising a small philosophy-of-science research group with three years of funding and no dedicated archival staff. We intend to focus our conceptual work on Otto Neurath's picture-language philosophy—specifically the relationship between his physicalist account of protocol sentences (Protokollsätze) and his normative, pedagogical project of an isotypic, syntactically minimal visual language. We can sustain two major lines of inquiry. Which open questions arising from this relationship should we prioritise, and in what order?

Please defend your ranking by addressing:
- which questions remain genuinely unresolved in the standard interpretation of Neurath's unified project, rather than being settled by familiar Vienna Circle scholarship;
- which carry the widest downstream implications, whether for philosophy of science (for example, intersubjective verification without recourse to private mental states) or for institutional design (for example, transparent decision-making protocols in multilingual deliberative bodies);
- which are tractable for philosophers and conceptual designers within our timeframe, without requiring original archival discoveries or large-scale empirical testing.

In particular, consider whether the tension between Neurath's claim that all scientific statements must ultimately rest on physicalist protocol sentences and his simultaneous construction of a universal picture-language that deliberately suppresses logical syntax is best understood as a contradiction to be resolved, a distinction to be dissolved, or a productive instability to be preserved. If you believe this framing misrepresents Neurath's actual view, correct the misunderstanding and redirect us to the questions that genuinely matter.

Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I want a defended agenda that explains what makes each line of work worth the years, what evidence or argument over the next three years would move a question up or down the ranking, and what a successful outcome would look like for each. Write in British English.
