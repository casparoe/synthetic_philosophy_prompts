hey, i'm putting together my position on moral responsibility and reactive attitudes for a philosophy of mind seminar and i want to know if it's actually coherent or just sounds smart. here's the package:

(1) i'm basically strawsonian: moral responsibility isn't about having some metaphysical libertarian freedom, it's about being part of the web of reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation — that structure our interpersonal lives.

(2) i'm also a determinist about the physical world, and i don't think that undermines responsibility at all, since reactive attitudes don't need causal openness; they just need the agent to be responsve to reasons and embedded in our moral community.

(3) i think animals can be targets of analogical reactive attitudes — like being genuinely pissed off at a dog that bites me — but these are diluted, not full-blown moral resentment, because animals aren't part of our moral community in the right way.

(4) i think anyone who lacks second-personal competence — the ability to recognize others as sources of normative demands — should be met with the objective attitude rather than reactive ones. that covers infants, severe dementia, and also large language models, since none of them can genuinely engage in recipricol moral address.

(5) evolutionary debunking arguments don't work against reactive attitudes, because these attitudes aren't representational beliefs about the world that could be false; they're constitutive practices that make interpersonal life possible.

(6) now, technically if determinism is true nobody could have done otherwise in the branching-worlds sense, but i think that's a red herring — the "could have done otherwise" relevant to responsibility is about being the kind of agent who can adjust their behavior in light of reasons, not about metaphysical possibility.

so where are the cracks? specifically: does (3) sit badly with (4), since if animals can trigger analogical reactive attitudes despite lacking second-personal competence, why can't an LLM do the same? and is there a hidden tension between (2) and (6) — am i smuggling in a compatibilist "could have done otherwise" in (6) that conflicts with my hard determinism in (2)? also, does (5) clash with (1) if strawson treated reactive attitudes as natural susceptibilities rather than practices we construct? please rank the tensions by severity, say which ones dissolve on inspection, and tell me the least costly single revision. don't just list them all as equally bad.
