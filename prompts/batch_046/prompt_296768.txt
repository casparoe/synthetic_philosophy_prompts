As a novelist working on a manuscript about institutional control, I have developed a conceptual framework I call refractive governance, by which I mean a mode of authority that does not observe subjects directly—like the panopticon—but instead manipulates the statistical and categorical environments through which subjects observe and classify themselves. In this model, power operates by altering the epistemic medium of self-recognition rather than by imposing visibility from above. I would like you to compare this idea with Michel Foucault's concept of disciplinary power, particularly his analysis of how normalization proceeds through hierarchical observation, the examination, and the production of docile bodies. Is refractive governance merely a technological update to the panopticon, or does it restructure the relation between knowledge and power in a way that Foucault's account of visibility and normalization does not fully capture? I am particularly interested in whether resistance to such a system demands a different form of counter-conduct than the localized reversals Foucault describes, and whether the subject produced under refractive governance is still best understood as a product of discipline, or as something closer to a self-curating agent.
