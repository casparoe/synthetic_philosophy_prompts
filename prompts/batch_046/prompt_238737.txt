I am preparing an essay for a graduate seminar on philosophy of psychiatry, and I have become convinced that the standard framing of Hacking's looping effects understates their ontological significance. Please argue for the position that looping effects in psychiatric classification are not merely interactive—where categories change the people classified—but are also retroactively constitutive of the evidential archive itself, so that the historical record subsequently used to justify or revise diagnostic criteria is systematically reshaped by prior iterations of the classification. Specifically, defend the claim that this generates a distinct kind of "institutional memory loop" that undermines realist critiques of psychiatric kinds in a way that purely interactive looping does not: the realist appeals to historical stability of symptom profiles, yet that very stability is partly an artefact of the classification's own archival footprint. Your argument should address the objection that this is merely a stronger form of social constructivism, and explain why it is not reducible either to straightforward social construction or to Hacking's original formulation of looping. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then present the reasoning with no preamble and without restating the question. Write in British English.
