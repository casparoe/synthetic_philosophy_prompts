For a 90-minute interdisciplinary seminar of about 14 graduate students in philosophy of physics and cognitive science, design an in-class simulation that makes participants physically experience why information processing cannot be treated as an epistemically free addition to a Maxwell-demon sorting task. The scenario: in pairs, students operate a two-chamber "box" (two bins on a table) containing tokens marked with either high or low "energy" values drawn blindly from a bag. The pair may open a gate (move one token between bins) only after jointly recording the token's current state, the gate's orientation, and the bin's occupancy in a shared physical ledger made of index cards arranged in a grid—no mental tracking is permitted. After each of ten rounds, the pair scores one point for every correct high/low separation achieved, but must then "reset" the ledger for the next cycle by physically erasing and reshuffling the cards, which costs them two energy tokens drawn from a limited communal pool. Include a hidden "blind demon" round in which one partner is forbidden access to the ledger and must open gates by guess, with penalties for wrong moves, so the group feels the difference between ignorant and informed sorting without being able to treat the informed case as costless. Specify exact rules, materials (tokens, cards, pool), a non-monetary payoff scheme that rewards separation but penalizes both unrecorded gate actions and ledger resets, and a timeline with phases. The debrief should map the recording rule onto measurement and correlation-building, the reset cost onto Landauer erasure, and the blind round onto the intuition that mere knowledge without physical inscription does not drive separation; it should also be explicit about what the simulation cannot reproduce—specifically, the quantitative kT ln 2 relation, the distinction between logical reversibility and thermodynamic irreversibility, and the quantum-measurement variant—indicating how the facilitator must supply these verbally. Finally, predict what will go wrong on the first run: participants will try to minimize writing in the ledger, will interpret the token cost as a social rule rather than a physical constraint, or will conflate successful separation with successful knowing; say exactly how the rules should be enforced to prevent this, and note which conceptual feature of the physics-of-information resolution the activity can only gesture at rather than demonstrate.
