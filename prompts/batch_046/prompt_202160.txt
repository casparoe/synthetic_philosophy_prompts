I am a journalist who covers science, technology, and ideas, and I face a recurring editorial problem: whether to treat a complex system—such as a distributed artificial-intelligence cluster, a tightly coupled microbiome, or an institutional board—as a single agent, decision-maker, or bearer of properties, or to keep all descriptions plural and distributed among the parts. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in the special composition question and its principal answers—universalist, nihilist, restricted-composition views that recognize only living organisms or functionally integrated systems, and moderate emergentist positions—that I or my copy editors can apply in roughly five minutes per feature. The deliverable should be a flowchart in words: ordered diagnostic questions, the standard of proof required to answer each, explicit stopping points where the procedure returns "do not reify the collective," "reification is permissible," or "hand to human judgment," and clear flags for steps that depend on contested metaphysics or on values rather than on empirical fact. The procedure must include a specific diagnostic step—call it the reporting-independence test—that asks whether describing the system's behavior requires attributing a state to the collective that cannot be reduced to the states of its parts, even if the metaphysical status of that collective remains disputed. Apply the procedure to three worked cases: (1) a distributed AI network that collectively optimizes resource allocation without any central controller; (2) a mature forest ecosystem that locally regulates temperature and rainfall through feedback loops; and (3) a corporate board that reaches a unanimous strategic decision. In the board case, the procedure should yield a recommendation to treat it as a unitary decider—contrary to my own journalistic instinct to keep attribution distributed—while flagging exactly where that recommendation rests on a contested metaphysical premise. The procedure must also state explicitly what it cannot do: where it will systematically misfire, which metaphysical disputes it deliberately leaves unresolved, and which editorial choices remain outside its scope. Throughout, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among specialists in metaphysics and philosophy of science, and do not present any single view of composition as the consensus. End with one line beginning "Summary:" followed by a single sentence that captures the procedure's core recommendation.
