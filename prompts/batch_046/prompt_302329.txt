I am currently enrolled in an advanced philosophy-of-mind seminar focused on higher-order theories of consciousness, and our discussion of Rosenthal-style higher-order thought (HOT) accounts versus first-order representationalist alternatives has reached an impasse. Despite repeated engagement with the canonical problem cases—targetless higher-order states, misrepresentation scenarios, and the coherence of unconscious HOTs—neither side has moved, and the exchange now feels circular: the HOR theorist appeals to self-directed thoughts to preserve transitivity without infinite regress, while the opponent insists that this introduces an unnecessary representational layer and violates parsimony. I suspect the stall is not empirical but structural, and my working hypothesis is that both camps are answering different questions under one heading—specifically, whether consciousness is a relational property constituted by one mental state bearing an awareness-relation to another, or a monadic intrinsic feature that can be modified by global availability—while a hidden formal disagreement about whether blocking the regress requires a non-representational self-model remains unacknowledged. Could you diagnose the actual nature of this disagreement? Is it substantive, verbal, or a conflation of metaphysical and explanatory concerns? Ground your analysis in what HOT theorists and first-order representationalists actually say about misrepresentation and targetlessness, and specify what, if anything, would count as genuine progress: a conceptual distinction that dissolves the framing, an empirical finding regarding prefrontal integration, or a demonstration that the underlying question is ill-posed?
