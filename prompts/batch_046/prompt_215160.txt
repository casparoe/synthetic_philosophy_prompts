Me and two friends, Mara and Jules, got into it over dinner after seeing some documentary about octopus brains. Mara said consciousness is clearly spread across animals like a dimmer switch — neural integration comes in degrees, and picking a cutoff between a dog and a beetle is totally arbitrary. Jules said thats wrong because phenomenal binding is binary: either theres a unified subjective perspective or there isnt, so being half-conscious doesnt really make sense. Mara came back with split-brain patients, where each hemisphere seems to have its own stream, which shows unity can be divided. Jules said no, split-brain proves sharp boundaries — you end up with two full subjects, not one half-subject, so division is discrete. Then Mara introduced this thing she calls the phenomenal density gradient, saying consciousness doesnt need full unity but has a density parameter that varies with metabolic rate and cross-modal connectivity, so an octopus might score high in one area and low in another. Jules said thats just relabeling neural complexity and it dodges the hard problem of why any density should feel like something. Mara said he was changing the subject. They asked me to referee and I honestly dont know. Can you walk through each step and rate who's right — clearly mistaken, partly right, clearly right? And say who wins overall, plus what both of them are missing, maybe about architectures that arent mammalian or about artificial systems?
