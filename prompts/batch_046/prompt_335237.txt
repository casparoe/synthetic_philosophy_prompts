The philosophical idea should shape what you can and cannot claim about the symposium's aims: you must welcome the scientists without conceding that mechanistic explanation requires necessary connections, and you must welcome the philosophers without reducing occasionalism to a historical curiosity or a mere epistemic limitation. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to obscure secondary scholarship. Build the speech around one concrete conceptual tension—e.g., the difference between describing "neuron A activates neuron B" as a statistical regularity versus as a metaphysically necessary link—so that both halves of the room can locate their disagreement precisely. The tone should be that of a senior scholar setting the terms of debate, not delivering a lecture.
