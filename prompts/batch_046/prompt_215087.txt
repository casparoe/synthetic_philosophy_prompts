I've been convinced lately that in Dennett's Where Am I? puzzle the right answer is that Dennett is actually in the vat with Yorick — the biological brain — not walking around in Hamlet's body with the computer copy. The reasoning is that there's direct spatiotemporal continuty with that brain tissue; Hamlet is just a really good duplicate that booted up once the scan finished, but the original stream of consciousness never actually left the tank. Even if Hamlet remembers being Dennett, that's copied data running on new hardware, not me.

What would count against this? I want real defeaters, not just "well Hamlet behavors like Dennett." Are there formal results in persistence theory or split-brain studies that show substrate-continuity doesn't track identity? Can this view be defended without smuggling in a Cartesian ego? Rank the problems from fatal to merely awkward. Also, be honest — has this kind of "brain-in-vat" answer been qualified into unfalsifiability, like saying "if the vat-brain dies then I was Hamlet all along"? Is that kind of insulation a problem for a view like this, or just sensible hedging?
