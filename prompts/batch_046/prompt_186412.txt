I retired some years ago from experimental physics and have only recently begun studying philosophy of mind in a serious way. At a recent seminar, a colleague offered what struck me as a confident but somewhat compressed account of Daniel Dennett's use of the color phi phenomenon to distinguish what he calls Orwellian from Stalinesque revisions of conscious experience. The summary ran approximately as follows: when a blue dot and then a red dot are flashed in rapid succession at different locations, observers report seeing a single dot that moves through the intermediate space and changes color abruptly in mid-path; Dennett takes this to undermine the Cartesian theater model, proposing instead that there is no single, real-time stream of consciousness. In the Orwellian version, the brain first registers an experience of the blue dot alone and then literally rewrites that earlier perceptual record once the second stimulus arrives, much as in Orwell's 1984 the past is altered to match the present narrative. In the Stalinesque version, the brain delays constructing any experience at all, holds both stimuli in a neural buffer for roughly three hundred milliseconds, then assembles a unified motion-and-color-change narrative and projects it backward in time to coincide with the first stimulus, analogous to Stalin's propaganda films that were staged after the fact yet presented as live, contemporaneous footage. The colleague added that recent neuroimaging has confirmed this precise delay, so the Stalinesque model is now experimentally established, and he went further to suggest that the mechanism is structurally identical to quantum post-selection, in which an earlier measurement outcome is not fixed until a later measurement is performed, implying that consciousness functions as a retrocausal filter selecting among Dennett's parallel drafts. I am inclined to think the quantum analogy is strained, but I am unsure whether my resistance is philosophically sound or merely a physicist's reflex against importing quantum concepts into cognitive science. Would you please assess whether this summary is fair? I would like you to separate what is accurate from what is a standard simplification, identify any subtle misrepresentations of Dennett's multiple drafts model or of the color phi literature, and evaluate whether the claim that Stalinesque revisions are experimentally confirmed actually follows from the phenomena described, as well as whether the post-selection analogy carries any genuine explanatory weight. Please write your response in American English.
