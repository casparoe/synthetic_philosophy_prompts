For our graduate seminar on speech-act theory I am drafting my position and want a consistency audit before presenting. Here are my commitments:

(1) Austin was correct that some utterances perform rather than describe, but wrong that they therefore lack satisfaction conditions; I think performatives can be satisfied or unsatisfied in ways structurally analogous to truth and falsity, and that a misfire can yield something false-like rather than merely unsuccessful.

(2) I endorse Searle's sharp distinction between illocutionary force—what is done in saying, determined by intention and conventional procedure—and perlocutionary effect—what is achieved by saying, which depends on contingent audience psychology.

(3) I hold that promises generate obligations ex nihilo: the speaker binds herself solely through the utterance, with no dependence on prior institutional frameworks or social conventions.

(4) I simultaneously hold that institutional performatives—marrying, sentencing, baptizing—are strictly conventional: they activate pre-existing normative structures and cannot create obligations ex nihilo.

(5) I take felicity conditions to be constitutive norms rather than causal prerequisites: when they fail, the act is not botched but is a different act altogether, such as a rehearsal, quotation, or counterfactual supposition.

(6) I believe in counter-performatives—utterances like "I take that back" or "That was only a joke"—whose primary illocutionary force is to suspend or annul the force of a prior speech act, and I think these operate by convention rather than by ex nihilo creation.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which tensions are only apparent and dissolve on inspection, and what the least costly revision would be for each real conflict. Rank the tensions by severity. In particular, examine whether (3) can be reconciled with (4) without collapsing the sharp boundary defended in (2), and whether (5) allows me to treat the counter-performatives in (6) as genuinely annulling prior acts or forces me to reconceive them. I want to know which single revision would clean up the most conflicts, and I do not want to be told which commitment to abandon—only what each abandonment costs.
