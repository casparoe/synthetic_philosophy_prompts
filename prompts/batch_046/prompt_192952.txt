A classmate summarized Gould and Lewontin's spandrels critique for me as follows: "In their 1979 essay, Gould and Lewontin showed that evolutionary biologists fall into the Panglossian fallacy by assuming every trait is an optimal adaptation. They used the architectural spandrel — the triangular space between arches in San Marco — to argue that many biological features are not adaptations but unavoidable structural byproducts. Because spandrels lack any function, they demonstrate that natural selection is not the main source of organic form; development and physical constraints are. Consequently, adaptationist explanations should be treated as guilty until proven innocent, and most complex traits should be presumed to be spandrels or drift products until shown otherwise." Before I rely on this in a paper, I want to know how accurate it is. Please go through it claim by claim: which parts match Gould and Lewontin's actual argument, which parts wrongly equate spandrels with non-functional drift products or with later exaptation theory, and whether their normative conclusion — a presumption against adaptationism — is really present in the original?
