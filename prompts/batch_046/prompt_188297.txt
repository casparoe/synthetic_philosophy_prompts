I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a political philosopher—unnamed in her recent work—who defends a view she terms the "discontinuity thesis" on humanitarian intervention. On this view, when a state apparatus is systematically redirected toward atrocity against its own population, the state's sovereign legal personality does not merely become illegitimate but enters a temporary suspension; consequently, external military action is not an infringement of sovereignty but an enforcement against a prior legal void. She uses this framework to reassess Kosovo (1999), Iraq (2003), and Libya (2011) not as exceptions or violations of the UN Charter, but as responses to a nullified mandate, and she routinely deflects objections about unauthorized regime change by insisting that once the legal personality is suspended, removing the regime's personnel is administrative housekeeping, not conquest.

Write me a sequence of eight live-audience questions that makes that deflection progressively harder to deploy. I want specific coverage of: (1) how the suspension threshold is specified without circular reliance on the intervening state's own normative judgment; (2) what legal obligations—toward citizens, refugees, or neighboring states—the suspended entity retains, and who holds them; (3) how Libya's Security Council mandate, which explicitly authorized only civilian protection and not regime change, interacts with her claim that the latter was legally compelled rather than accidental; and (4) whether Kosovo's absence of explicit UN authorization actually supports her model or undermines it, given that post-hoc endorsement came through political rather than juridical channels.

For each of the first three, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—likely appeals to customary international law, to the distinction between de jure and de facto authority, or to the administrative housekeeping line itself. The final question should ask what observable pattern of state behavior—if observed in a future case—would genuinely falsify the thesis rather than being reinterpreted as further evidence of suspension. Avoid generic biographical or "how did you come to this view" openings; keep the questions substantive and pitched to an audience of international-relations scholars and normative theorists.

Please present the questions in numbered order. Conclude with a single summary line beginning "Summary:" that identifies the central tension the sequence is designed to expose.
