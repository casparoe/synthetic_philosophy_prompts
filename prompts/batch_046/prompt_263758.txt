I'm writing up some rough notes for a philosophy of bio class after arguing with my study partnet about whether DNA replication is genuinely self-referential or just causally circular, and I want someone to poke holes before I present anything. The basic analogy everyone makes is between Gödel numbering and the genetic code: DNA encodes proteins that build the machinery that reads DNA, so there's a loop where the symbol system produces its own interpreter. I think the standard version misses something though. In Gödel's proof the encoding is arbitrary syntax - the formula doesn't care about its Gödel number, it just happens to have one assigned. But with DNA, the ribosome isn't just accidentally made of proteins; its physical structure is literally the result of prior ribosome activity translating nucleic acids. So the decoder is materially constituted by the products of decoding. I tried to call this 'material closure of interpretation' - the idea that the rules for reading the code are themselves chemically instantiated by the code's outputs, not just logically implied.

Then I pushed it further. I said this makes DNA replication closer to Hofstadter's strange loops than to Gödel's arithmetic, except there's no tangled hierarchy of levels - nucleotides, ribosomes, and proteins are all the same kind of chemical stuff, just arranged differently. The 'information' isn't sitting in the DNA like data in a computer; it's distributed across the loop, which is why you can't fully reduce genetics to pure chemistry without losing the interpretive relation, but you also can't treat the interpretation as non-physical since the interpreter is just more chemistry. I suggested this might be why reductionist programs in biology keep running into this specific wall: they have to either dissolve the semantic layer into chemistry and lose the specificity of the code, or keep the semantic layer and end up with a hermeneutic circle that looks suspiciously like circular reasoning.
