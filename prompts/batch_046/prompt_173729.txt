I have been working out my own position on simultaneous scientific discovery—specifically Newton and Leibniz with calculus, Darwin and Wallace with natural selection—and I would like an audit of whether these commitments cohere. Here is my profile: (1) I think both calculus and evolutionary theory were overdetermined by the conceptual and empirical resources available in their respective centuries, so independent formulation was not accidental but structurally probable; (2) I nevertheless maintain that if Newton had died in 1665, the specific notational and formal apparatus of calculus might have been delayed by generations, which implies that individual cognitive architecture affects the trajectory and form of discovery without undermining its inevitability; (3) I hold that priority disputes matter for historical sociology and institutional credit, but are metaphysically irrelevant to whether the mathematical or biological structures discovered were already present to be found; (4) I reject both the pure great-man narrative and pure structural determinism, adopting instead a middle view in which discovery is a stochastic process constrained by available ideas, where individual agents act as catalysts with variable efficiency; and (5) I believe that if a discovery is genuinely inevitable, moral praise or blame for the discoverer is inappropriate, yet I still judge Darwin's roughly two-decade delay in publishing after conceiving the theory to be a failure of scientific duty. Please examine these commitments pair by pair, identifying which tensions are genuine and which dissolve on inspection. For each real conflict, state its severity, what the cheapest revision would be, and what that revision costs. Order the tensions by severity. In particular, address whether (2) genuinely conflicts with (1) or merely qualifies it, and whether (5) can be sustained alongside (3) without contradiction. Finally, say explicitly what argument or finding would change your assessment—especially what would convince you that the stochastic-catalyst view in (4) collapses into either determinism or heroic individualism, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
