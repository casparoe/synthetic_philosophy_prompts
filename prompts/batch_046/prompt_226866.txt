1. Generational labels have socially constructed, fuzzy boundaries; there's no sharp natural fact that decides exactly where Gen Z starts or ends.
2. Still, I think shared formative shocks — entering the job market in 2008, COVID lockdowns during college, growing up with smartphones in adolescence — can create a real structural cohort with genuinely shared causal dispositions, not just an age bracket.
3. But I also hold that for a social cohort to count as a kind rather than a demographic bin, its members need at least some capacity for collective self-recognition; otherwise statisticians are just drawing lines on a graph.
4. Despite that, I routinely explain specific behaviors — like young workers demanding remote flexibility, or older voters resisting climate policy — by attributing them to Gen Z or to classic Boomer tendencies, treating cohort membership as causally explanatory for individual choices.
5. I'm not an essentialist — there's no Gen Z essence — but I reject methodological indiviualism, so I think groups can have emergent properties that don't reduce to individuals.
6. I also think Boomer mostly tracks accumulated housing wealth and political power, not a real cohort identity shaped by shared history, yet I still reach for the generational framing in cultural arguments.
