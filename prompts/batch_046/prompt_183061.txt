Our department’s philosophy-of-science review committee applies Lakatosian criteria—hard core, protective belt, progressive versus degenerating problem shifts—to evaluate research programmes. Its four prior verdicts are these. First, the shift from behaviorist to computational-representational psychology was judged progressive, even though its key predictions about mental rotation and working-memory limits were formulated only after the relevant data were already published, because the protective-belt adjustments generated independently testable hypotheses about modular architecture. Second, a strong-nativist linguistics programme was judged degenerating when it explained away cross-linguistic counterexamples by positing unobservable parameter settings that yielded no new empirical predictions and resolved no theoretical tension beyond saving the hard core. Third, a physicalist programme addressing quantum measurement was judged progressive when it used decoherence adjustments to predict novel interference patterns in mesoscopic systems, but degenerating when it merely redescribed existing decoherence results with philosophical vocabulary; in both sub-cases the committee ignored whether the predictions preceded or followed the initial data. Fourth, a clinical-psychology programme with a psychoanalytic hard core was judged degenerating because every protective-belt modification—new defense mechanisms, revised developmental stages—was introduced only after clinical anomalies appeared and never risked a prediction that could have been false.

We now face a fifth programme whose hard core is panpsychist: consciousness is fundamental, not emergent. After brain-lesion studies failed to show the expected localized elimination of consciousness, the programme added a “micro-experiential binding” auxiliary to the protective belt, formulated explicitly after the negative results, to explain distributed redundancy. More recently, the same programme derived from this adjusted belt a specific, risky prediction: that artificial substrates with sustained quantum coherence in recurrent architectures will exhibit integrated-information patterns matching those of conscious brains, published before any corresponding experiments were designed.

Predict the committee’s verdict on this fifth programme, identify at least two distinct Lakatosian rules that fit all four prior judgments equally well but diverge on this case, state what each rule predicts here, and give your best-guess verdict with direct reasoning. Then specify one further hypothetical case—structurally different from the five above—that would best discriminate among the rules you have named. I want a committed answer: take a definite position on both the verdict and the rule that is actually operating, defend it without neutral hedging, and be explicit about which feature of the programme is doing the decisive work.
