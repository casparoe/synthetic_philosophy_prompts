I spent thirty years calibrating low-background detectors in experimental physics, and since retirement I have been trying to understand the Bayesian literature on what philosophers call the problem of old evidence. The puzzle, as I understand it, is that subjective Bayesians update beliefs only on probabilities of evidence conditional on hypotheses, yet if the evidence is already known its prior probability is one, which seems to block any confirmation of a new theory by data that was sitting in the archive all along. I have encountered three broad responses: one that denies there is any special problem once conditioning is done correctly; one that appeals to counterfactual probabilities—what I would have believed had I not yet seen the data; and one that treats the theory and the old evidence as mutually supporting in a bootstrap structure. I would like you to translate each of these three responses into the vocabulary I actually used in the laboratory: calibration constants locked before unblinding, blind-analysis protocols that fix the background model independently of the signal hypothesis, retroactive adjustments to systematic-error budgets, the distinction between in-sample fitting and predictive validation on held-out data runs, and null-threshold criteria for rejecting background-only models. For each philosophical position, tell me precisely what maps onto this experimental framework, what must be distorted to fit (for instance, treating subjective priors as if they were pre-registered analysis cuts), and what has no operational counterpart in how physicists handle archival calibration measurements. Then provide a residue report: does the material that refuses translation point to a normative dimension of belief revision that my laboratory vocabulary genuinely lacks, or does it reveal that one or more of the philosophical distinctions collapses under the practical constraints of detector calibration? I am especially curious whether the idea of counterfactual belief revision can be given any precise meaning in a context where the analysis pipeline was frozen before the theoretical model was even proposed.
