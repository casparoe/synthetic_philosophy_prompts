Provide a concrete example of a conceptual error arising from the analogical extension of Gödel's first incompleteness theorem into non-mathematical domains. Specifically, construct a scenario in which a theorist argues that any sufficiently complex normative framework for governing autonomous artificial agents must contain ethical propositions that are true yet unprovable within the framework itself. Then identify precisely where the analogy breaks down: contrast the syntactic conditions necessary for Gödel numbering in Peano arithmetic with the semantic and pragmatic structure of normative language, and explain why the failure of completeness in formal arithmetic does not generate a structurally analogous epistemic limitation in ethical or regulatory reasoning.
