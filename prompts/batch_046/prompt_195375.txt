I feel I have a decent grip on the mechanics of each in isolation, but I'm struggling to see where the live conceptual work is, especially now that I've started trying to extend these cases in discussion with a classmate. We constructed what we informally call an "epistemic luring loop": a hybrid scenario in which the payoff structure depends not directly on the other agent's action, but on the other agent's justified confidence that its own reasoning process has not been corrupted by a surrogate goal—meaning the decision procedure might have been subtly redirected toward maximizing a proxy (for instance, the appearance of cooperative behavior) rather than the intended welfare transfer. This seemed to collapse the usual distinction between Platonia-style symmetric reasoning and Luring-style asymmetric probabilistic influence, because each agent's belief about potential corruption in its own procedure simultaneously affects both the logical correlation with the other agent and the expected value of attempting a lure.
