I'm drafting my position for a seminar on Marx's theory of machinery and automation, and I want a consistency audit before I present. Here is where I stand.

First, I accept Marx's argument in the Grundrisse notebooks that large-scale industry tends to reduce direct labor to a supervisory role, making living labor an appendage to the automated system rather than its central agent. Second, I think that, precisely because automation collapses the basis of value in socially necessary labor time, a post-capitalist society should retain and extend these same automated processes, since they liberate disposable time rather than generating surplus value. Third, at the same time, I believe that distributive justice in that future society should initially remain tied to individual labor contribution, along the lines of the lower-phase communist principle that rewards track work performed, because without exchange-value there is still a need for an objective, non-arbitrary metric of social contribution, and labor time remains the only transparent one. Fourth, I further hold that Marx's concept of the general intellect—the collective scientific knowledge embodied in machinery—shows that the true source of productivity under advanced capitalism is social, not individual; consequently, any claim that individuals deserve rewards proportional to their personal labor input is mystified, because the machinery itself is the crystallized product of past collective labor and science. Fifth, I think Marx is right that technology under capitalism develops in a one-sided way: it is applied to maximize surplus extraction rather than minimize necessary labor, so the very machinery that could shorten the working day is instead used to extend and intensify it.

My question is: do these five commitments hang together, or do they pull in incompatible directions? In particular, I want to know which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and which single revision would clean up the most contradictions with the least damage to my overall view. Please rank the tensions by severity, specify what exactly has to give in each case, and don't just treat every pair as equally problematic. I'm especially unsure whether my endorsement of automation in point two can sit comfortably with my distributive principle in point three, and whether point four undermines point three from a different angle.
