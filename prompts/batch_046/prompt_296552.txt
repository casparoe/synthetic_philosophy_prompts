I have just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a friend about whether contemporary democratic states have a legitimate interest in shaping not merely the information environment but the very cognitive architecture of citizenship. The specific proposal we kept circling is what one might call epistemic horticulture: a regulatory framework in which algorithmic recommendation systems are not merely required to be transparent, but are actively redesigned—through mandatory diversity injection and morphological sequencing—to cultivate in citizens a capacity for perceiving organic social wholes rather than fragmented partisan nodes. My friend insists this is a natural extension of classical republican virtue; I worry it collapses into a mechanistic version of Bildung that Goethe, given his lifelong suspicion of abstract system-building and his belief in self-forming organic development, would have found intolerable. Assuming Goethe’s political and anthropological commitments—his cosmopolitan humanism, his rejection of revolutionary abstraction, his morphological insistence that form emerges through internal dynamics rather than external imposition—how do you think he would have judged such an epistemic horticulture mandate? Would he have seen it as a legitimate modern expression of political Bildung, or as precisely the kind of engineered interference with natural development that his critique of revolutionary politics and his later administrative caution warned against?
