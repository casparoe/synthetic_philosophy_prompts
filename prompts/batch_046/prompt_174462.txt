I sit on our library's special-collections ethics board. A donor funded a "responsive volume" — Einstein's notebooks embedded in a bound book with computation inside, answers in his voice, modeled on that old Hofstadter/Dennett scenario about Einstein's brain living in a book (the one where funcitonal organization might be enough for consciousness). The catch is it only activates when opened; between readings it has no processes running at all. The donor wants us to list it as a "living archive" in our catalog, with protections against deaccessioning, and they want it eligible for reasearcher travel funds. Some board members say this is absurd — it's a gadget, not a colleague — but others worry that if we're funcitonalists about mind, then the substrate being paper and silicon shouldn't matter, and the fact that it "sleeps" between queries might just be like human sleep. I need a memo for Friday recommending whether we grant it protected status, reject outright, or approve with conditions. Please deal seriously with the strongest version of the objection: not just "books aren't people," but the specific worry that intermitent activation means there's no continuous subject of experience, so even on functionalist grounds there's nothing it's like to be this object across time. No bibliography needed, just the reasoning, and keep it tight since I have to present Friday.
