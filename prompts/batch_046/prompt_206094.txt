Explain the conceptual tension at the heart of the DNA–Gödel isomorphism proposed within Douglas Hofstadter's typogenetic framework: in Gödel's arithmetic, self-reference is achieved purely syntactically through Gödel numbering, with no external interpreter required to decode the statement, whereas in DNA replication the nucleotide sequence only acquires functional meaning through the ribosome and the broader cellular translation apparatus, which sit outside the genome itself. Address whether this difference collapses the analogy or merely relocates it—specifically, consider whether the ribosome and its associated tRNA machinery can be coherently internalized to the system in the same way that the proof-checking rules of arithmetic are internal to formal logic. Further, examine whether the thermodynamic and error-correcting dependencies of biological replication—proofreading polymerases, energy expenditure to maintain fidelity—introduce a form of noise management that has no structural counterpart in Gödel's construction, and what this implies for the claim that both systems instantiate the same kind of strange loop. Your explanation should clarify whether the isomorphism is primarily about the logical form of self-encoding, about the causal mechanism of reproduction, or about something else entirely, and should identify which of these three dimensions—syntax/semantics separation, system boundary, and error correction—poses the deepest challenge to treating DNA as a genuinely Gödelian system.
