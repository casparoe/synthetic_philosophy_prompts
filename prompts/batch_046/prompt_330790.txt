I'm writing an essay for my philosophy of science course on whether Nyāya epistemology can illuminate modern scientific modeling. My central argument focuses on upamāna (comparison or analogy), one of the four pramāṇas, alongside pratyakṣa, anumāna, and śabda. I claim that climate simulation relies structurally on analogical reasoning—mapping historical climate dynamics onto future scenarios through parameterized similarity—rather than on strict inferential chains governed by invariable concomitance (vyāpti). From the perspective of classical Nyāya commentators, this would make climate projections epistemically suspect, since upamāna traditionally requires direct perceptual grounding in similarity (sādr̥śya) that abstract mathematical models seem to lack. To avoid a purely negative conclusion, I want to inject a more niche reading: later Navya-Nyāya treatments appear to expand upamāna to accommodate mediated analogies reconstructed through structural correspondence rather than direct perception. If this expansion is legitimate, Nyāya might accommodate scientific modeling, but only by stretching its classical constraints. Could you comment on the coherence of this comparison? In particular, am I blurring the line between upamāna and anumāna, and is my characterization of climate modeling as analogical rather than inferential defensible? I would value feedback on the argument's accuracy and whether it is philosophically interesting, and please respond in American English.
