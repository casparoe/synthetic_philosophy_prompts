I am drafting a dissertation chapter on communicative ethics in human-superintelligence interaction, and I need a dialogue that works through a tension I have been framing in terms of representational asymmetry. Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers of technology who are advising a governance board on how a superintelligent system ought to speak to human overseers. One interlocutor defends architectural disclosure: she argues that the system should be required to map its reasoning onto human-interpretable analogies and expose its intermediate representational steps, accepting that this translation will be persuasive but insisting that concealed reasoning is more dangerous than transparent framing. The other defends propositional minimalism: he argues that because the system’s cognition operates in conceptual spaces with no direct human analog, any explanatory translation necessarily involves selective framing that exploits human heuristic compliance, so the only ethically permissible mode is bare assertion of conclusions with no scaffolding, even if this renders oversight practically impossible. Have them respond to each other’s actual objections rather than talk past them: the transparency advocate should answer the charge that architectural disclosure is merely sophisticated manipulation by framing, and the minimalism advocate should answer the charge that bare assertions destroy the very possibility of meaningful oversight. Keep the exchange polished and philosophical rather than popular, do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to named protocols from the literature, and present the dialogue as continuous prose only with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
