You have been invited to deliver the opening remarks—approximately eight minutes—at an interdisciplinary workshop convened to mark the retirement of a senior metaphysician whose career was devoted to defending correspondence theories of truth. The audience is divided between senior analytic philosophers who regard Richard Rorty’s neopragmatism with suspicion but acknowledge its historical importance, and graduate students in literature and cultural studies for whom antirepresentationalism is already intuitive. Please write the speech in polished American English. The philosophical idea must do structural work rather than serve as decoration: you may not claim that philosophy uncovers the true structure of reality, that rigorous argumentation mirrors an external logical order, or that the retiring scholar’s lifelong project was mistaken in any straightforward representationalist sense. Instead, honor four decades of precise argumentation by framing it as a powerful vocabulary that is now being redescribed—through Rorty’s antirepresentationalist lens—into vocabularies of solidarity, edification, and practical coping rather than accurate representation. You must speak in terms accessible to the non-philosophers present, comfort the scholar’s former students without endorsing representationalism, and challenge the graduate students to value analytic rigor without assuming that such rigor requires a mirror of nature. Do not include bibliographic references, citation lists, or recommendations for further reading.
