If the Löbian obstacle prevents any agent with arithmetic self-reference from rationally trusting its own reasoning about that trust unless it already possesses an independent proof of correctness, then shouldn't we view the alignment problem for recursively self-improving systems as structurally solved? After all, a corrupted successor model would be unable to secure credibility with its predecessor, since the predecessor could not grant that trust without circular justification. Given this, why do alignment researchers still treat the obstacle primarily as a barrier to beneficial self-modification rather than as an automatic safeguard against misaligned successors? What is wrong with the inference that the logical structure itself blocks dangerous handovers?
