I am a retired physicist who spent much of my career studying non-equilibrium thermodynamics and irreversible processes before turning, in retirement, to philosophy of technology and AI governance. In a recent reading group on alignment, I kept returning to the problem of so-called low-impact agents—systems meant to avoid harmful side effects without requiring a complete, explicit specification of human values. The usual framing asks whether metrics like state-space reachability, reversibility, or deviation from a baseline trajectory can capture “impact” in a value-neutral way. I want to press this further through a conceptual lens borrowed from my old field, but I need a rigorous philosophical assessment rather than an analogy.

Write an essay addressing the following controversy: Can “avoiding side effects” be coherently defined for an artificial agent without either smuggling in a full axiological framework—which would defeat the purpose of low-impact design—or collapsing into a purely structural or physical description that cannot distinguish harmful disruption from benign transformation?

Weigh the main positions carefully. First, consider the view—parallel to the principle of least action—that impact can be measured as an increase in what we might call “causal entropy,” that is, an agent’s tendency to irreversibly expand the set of possible future histories in its environment. On this account, one penalizes the agent for increasing dynamical irreversibility without judging which particular histories are good or bad. Second, examine the counter-argument that any selection of a baseline world, any choice of which variables belong to the “environment,” and any metric of reversibility already embeds normative judgments about what counts as damage and what counts as background noise—what philosophers of science term value-ladenness. Third, evaluate a middle position I have been calling “structural subsidiarity”: the idea that an agent can be constrained to operate within a localized causal module, leaving global dynamics untouched, without specifying values—much as federal structures delegate authority downward without defining every local preference.

Your essay should reach and justify a definite conclusion. Is structural impact regularization genuinely value-neutral, or does the very demarcation of causal boundaries and reversibility conditions presuppose judgments about what matters? Defend your verdict explicitly; do not conclude with an equivocal summary. You need not provide citations, bibliographies, or reading lists—simply reason through the conceptual architecture on its own terms.
