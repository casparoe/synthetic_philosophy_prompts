I'm a junior serving as the student representative on my high school's Student Publications Oversight Committee, and we are divided over a proposal to open our digital forum, The Meridian, to fully anonymous opinion submissions. Some members argue that anonymity protects vulnerable voices on controversial issues like curriculum reform and mental-health policy, while others worry that removing attribution will dissolve individual responsibility and invite what one faculty advisor called "leveling" discourse—referring generally to Kierkegaard's critique of the press and the formation of an abstract public that speaks without owning its words. We have been asked to produce a formal recommendation for Thursday's meeting: approve anonymous posting, reject it, or approve it with conditions, together with a concrete implementation framework—such as a tiered verification system where faculty confirm identity but publication remains pseudonymous, a time-bound pilot with sunset review, or a mandatory editorial mediation layer—that our committee could actually enact before the end of the semester. Please draft the memo in American English. It should treat the strongest argument for anonymity seriously, address whether Kierkegaard's concern about an irresponsible mass public genuinely applies to a school-scale digital forum rather than a national newspaper culture, and propose clear criteria reviewers can apply to distinguish responsible anonymous speech from discourse that merely hides behind collective anonymity. The reasoning should include an honest acknowledgment of the losing side's best point, and the bottom line must be a specific course of action with a defined time frame that the opposing members could respond to in writing.
