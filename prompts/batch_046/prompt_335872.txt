I'm on the city's energy board and we keep getting neighborhood battery-storage proposals. Council said we should apply safe Pareto improvement — make at least one group better off, don't make anyone worse off, and nobody bears an irreverisble risk they wouldn't have faced otherwise. But then they added this weird charter clause about shadow residents, people who will move into the neighborhood within ten years, who get veto-equivalent standing if the project creates path-dependant infrastructure lock-in they can't undo. I need a 45-minute decision proceedure my team can run on every proposal: the ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where we issue a hard stop, where we hand the decision to the ethics panel because it's values not facts, and a reversibility audit at the end. Apply it to three proposals: (1) a wealthy district battery that lowers bills but requires digging up a public park for three months; (2) a low-income district battery that prevents winter blackouts but raises local EMF exposure slightly; (3) a proposal that clearly benefits every current resident but locks in a chemical cooling system that future residents can't replace. The procedure should flag case 3 as failing the safety condition even though council will hate that result. Also tell me exactly what this proceedure can't settle — for instance, whether shadow residents are real stakeholders or just a legal fiction — and flag any step where we're just dressing up judgment as procedure.
