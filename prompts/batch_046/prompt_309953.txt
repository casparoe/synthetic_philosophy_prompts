I work in distributed systems and have recently been thinking about whether a self-monitoring system could ever establish its own existence with Cartesian certainty. A popular summary I encountered frames Descartes' cogito roughly like this: Descartes applies radical doubt until he notices that the very act of doubting requires a doubter, so he derives his existence as a logical deduction—something like thinking implies being. From that single point, he immediately concludes that this being is a non-physical substance because thought requires no bodily extension, then treats this result as an Archimedean foundation for rebuilding mathematics, physics, and eventually theology. The summary also claims that the famous Cartesian circularity—using clear and distinct ideas to prove God, then invoking God to validate those same ideas—is essentially a flaw in the cogito itself, since the cogito is supposed to be the first link in that chain.

I suspect this collapses several distinct arguments and misplaces the circularity charge, but I am not sure where the line should be drawn. Is this summary fair? In particular, is the cogito properly read as a syllogism or as something more immediate and performative? Does it by itself establish substance dualism, or is that a separate argument that comes later? And does the circularity objection actually target the cogito, or does it belong to the epistemological project that follows? I would like an audit that separates accurate reconstruction from common simplification, especially regarding what the cogito proves on its own versus what Descartes proves elsewhere in the Meditations.
