University animal care committees usually mandate closer welfare cheks for primates and corvids than for rodents, and they cite relative brain size and cognitive complexity in the justification—yet the analgesic protocals and humane endpoint rules are identical across mammal species. What philosophical view about brain size and moral status does that split actually presuppose? Does it treat cortex size as tracking suffering intensity, the range of interests, or just political visibility? And if brain size is morally irrelevant, is the practice still coherent, or does consistency force us to either equalize monitoring or equalize pain management? Writing a paper on institutional logik here, so I don't need references—just want the implicit theory reconstructed.
