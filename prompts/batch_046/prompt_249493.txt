I'm currently in a course on Hellenistic ethics, and I've adopted a reconstructed reading of the Stoic dichotomy of control that strikes me as more textually faithful than the version circulating in the modern revival. Specifically, I take Epictetus's distinction not as a binary partition of causal domains—things either within my power or not—but as a normative boundary drawn around prohairesis (rational assent), with the consequence that actions involving external goods fall into an intermediate "co-fated" status rather than being simply external. On this view, popular modern Stoicism—especially the self-improvement literature that treats emotional self-regulation as the sole operationalization of "control"—collapses a metaphysical-ethical structure into therapeutic quietism. I also think this binary misreading explains why contemporary revivalists tend to neglect collective or structural conditions: if control is redefined as internal attitude alone, institutional injustice is automatically relegated to the "indifferent" column in a way that ancient Stoics, who maintained cosmopolitan and political obligations, would not have accepted. What would count against this position? I want the strongest objections, not just accusations of unfairness: whether there is textual support in Epictetus or the surviving Stoic fragments for a genuinely binary reading; whether prohairesis can bear the normative weight I assign it without collapsing into a compatibilism that makes the dichotomy trivial; whether my claim about the neglect of collective conditions actually describes modern revivalist writing or is largely a strawman; and whether my view risks being insulated from refutation, since any modern practice can be dismissed as a misreading. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish between what defenders of modern Stoicism would concede versus reject, and give an honest assessment of whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability and whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.
