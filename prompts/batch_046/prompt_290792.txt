hey — i'm a novelist working on a book about an ex-diplomat who keeps replaying three interventions in her head, and i'm trying to map her gut reactions onto some actual principle but i'm stuck. here's her (and my) verdicts, case by case.

kosovo 1999: NATO bombs serbia without UN authorization to stop ethnic cleansing. verdict — justified, full stop. the rules matter but so does stopping people from being murdered in real time.

iraq 2003: invasion based on false WMD claims, regime change, no serious plan for the aftermath. verdict — wrong, and wrong not just because of the lie but because shattering a state and leaving the pieces feels like a narrative crime, destroying the structure just to force an ending.

libya 2011: UN-approved protection of civilians, but then the campaign keeps going untill Gaddafi is dead and the country is militia chaos. verdict — the protection part was okay, but once they exceeded the mandate and produced a collapsed state, the whole thing feels retroactively tainted, like a decent first act ruined by a catastrophic final chapter.

rwanda 1994: the international community watches a genocide unfold and does almost nothing. verdict — a clear moral failure, they should have acted.

the problem: i want to defend all four, but kosovo lacked international legitimacy and i still defend it, while libya had that legitimacy and i still reject it. maybe the principle is about scope — go in, stop the killing, get out — but libya started with exactly that scope and still spiralled. maybe it's about predictable aftermath? but kosovo was risky too. there's also this idea i'm playing with, that a disastrous ending can poison the moral status of the beginning — call it retroactive contamination — but i dont know if that's ethics or just novelist intuition.

so: is there a coherent principle that captures all four verdicts, with each clause tied to one of the cases? if not, which one do i have to drop? and please say exactly what the principle predicts for a case i haven't listed — a regional coalition that invades a neighbor to stop genocide, refuses UN oversight, and then occupies for a decade rebuilding institutions. closer to kosovo or iraq?

also, if you can, tell me whether retroactive contamination is a real thing in moral philosophy or if i'm just making up terms to cover my inconsistencies.
