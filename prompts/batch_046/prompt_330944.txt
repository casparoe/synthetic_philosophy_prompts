I've been trying to figure out if dreams are actual experances or just stories your brain cooks up after you wake up. Here are my gut calls.

If I have a nightmere where I'm falling and I wake up with my heart racing, that feels like I genuinely experienced terror. So yes, that's a real experience.

But my roommate sleepwalks and talks about being at the beach, and in the morning she swears she didn't dream anything. If she has zero memory and zero feeling about it, I don't think she experienced the beach, even though her mouth was moving. So no.

Then there's being woken up in REM and asked to report. People tell these long wild stories. But I always wonder: did they really live through all that, or did they just grab a few images and fill in the plot on the spot? I lean it's mostly made up in the telling, so probably not a real experience.

What about false awakening? I once "remember" dreaming that I woke up, wrote the dream in my notebook, and went back to sleep. But there was no entry. Did I experience writing? It feels like I did, but maybe it's just a fake memory of writing.

Also I think about being blind in a dream when I've never been blind in real life. That seems like I genuinely experienced blindness for a minute. So yes.

But then there's the therapist interpretation thing: I dream about my teeth falling out, and later someone says that means anxiety. But during the dream I wasn't anxious, I was just annoyed about teeth. So I don't think I experienced anxiety; the meaning got added after.

These don't all fit. The nightmare case says if it feels real during, it's experience. The REM report case says maybe feeling real after doesn't count. The false awakening case makes me doubt my own "during" feeling. And the teeth case says interpretation after doesn't count, but then what about the falling nightmare where I interpret it as terror only after I wake up?

Is there one principle that sorts this? Or do I have to drop one? Also what about someone who reports a vivid dream while they're anesthesized during surgery? Would your principle call that an experience?

Please tell me what principle gets closest, which of my verdicts it can't save, and what it says about the surgery case.
