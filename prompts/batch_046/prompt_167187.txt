If Gödel's second theorem shows that any formal system strong enough for arithmetic can't prove its own consistency, then shouldn't any self-amending legal framework—like a constitution with a clause that lets you rewrite the amendment rules—be either logically inconsistent or permanently blind to it's own coherence? I was trying to apply this to recusive statute structures in administrative law where regulations can trigger revisions to the revision process itself, and it seems like Gödel should make them collapse. But countries with actual amendment procedures don't implode, so where does the analogy break? Is the mistake treating legal language like a fixed formal language, or is there something deeper about what 'consistency' even means outside math?
