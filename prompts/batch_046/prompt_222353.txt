Write a one-page workshop proposal (around 800 words) for a philosophy-of-social-science session. The core question: can Lewis's account of convention accommodate norms that persist through a specific kind of reflexive misalignment—where agents share common knowledge of each other's first-order preferences, but each agent systematically misrepresents to themselves (and is known by others to misrepresent) the reasons why others continue to conform? Call these "asymmetric-reflexive conventions": the coordination equilibrium is sustained not by aligned preferences for conformity, but by layered meta-beliefs about misaligned preferences. Does this require revising Lewis's conditions on common knowledge and rational preference, or can evolutionary-game-theoretic models absorb it without abandoning the rational-choice framing? Frame the proposal with: (i) a precise statement of the question that distinguishes it from standard problems of convention change; (ii) why the existing rationalist-versus-evolutionary literature does not settle whether meta-belief misalignment is a separate stability mechanism; (iii) the method—conceptual analysis of belief conditions, possibly paired with a minimal formal sketch—and why this is preferable to a purely empirical or purely formal approach; (iv) what a successful result looks like (e.g., a revised necessary-condition set, or a collapse argument) and what counts as failure; (v) an honest assessment of scope for a six-month single-author project leading to a conference presentation. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; refer to positions and arguments in general terms and indicate where references will be inserted later.
