ok so ive been reading about bradleys regress and the whole "how do parts make a unified proposition" thing, and i keep seeing this specific move in papers and even casual discussion. someone starts with the idea that a proposition like a is F isnt just a and F sitting next to each other -- there has to be some glue. then they propose a glue, like a mental act of combining, or maybe a structural role that F plays with respect to a. but then someone points out that this glue itself needs to be unified with the parts its gluing, so now youve got a and F and the-glue, and you need glue2 to hold those together. instead of biting the bullet, the original speaker says "no no, the first glue isnt an extra part, its just the way the parts are arranged." but then they turn around and say the arrangement itself is realized by some higher-order capacity or framework, which is basically treating the arrangement as a new part that needs its own glue. ive seen this happen in at least three contexts -- mental representation, linguistic syntax, and ontological structure. its not exactly bradleys original regress because the speaker keeps denying that theyre adding parts, but the denial just pushes the problem up a level. is there an actual name for this specific failure mode, where the unity problem gets deferred upward through successive denials that the unifier is a relatum? and if not, can you coin one with a sharp definition and boundary cases? also please seperate whats settled from whats genuinely contested among people who actually work on this, and dont present a contested view as if everyone agrees.
