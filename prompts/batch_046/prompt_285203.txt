In teleosemantics, the project of naturalizing mental content turns on assigning representational properties via proper functions—typically anchored to evolutionary selection history (etiological accounts) or to current systemic contribution (systemic accounts). Yet an expanding class of cases—horizontally transferred bacterial quorum-sensing circuits, developmentally scaffolded neural representations in organisms with extensive niche construction, and synthetic biological sensors engineered through directed evolution—exhibit hybrid causal histories in which etiological and systemic criteria diverge and neither clearly dominates.
