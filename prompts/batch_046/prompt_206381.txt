After retiring from experimental physics and taking up philosophy, I have been studying transcendental arguments—the inference from conditions of experience to necessary commitments about reality. I have devised what I term an "instrumental transcendental": reproducible quantum interference patterns seem to require that we presuppose a single, definite outcome at the apparatus, not a branching distribution of results; hence a non-branching ontology is not empirically discovered but is a transcendental condition of experimental reproducibility itself. From this single case, I infer that all transcendental arguments are in fact disguised methodological rules indexed to particular scientific practices, since their "necessary" conditions always turn out to depend on instrumental setups rather than on experience as such. Please explain the error in this generalization, leading with your bottom line in the first sentence and then giving the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
