I have been thinking about when an AI assistant should refuse a request, but I keep returning to a case that feels distinct from the usual examples. Most discussions focus on immediate harm: the user asks for something dangerous right now, and the AI blocks it. I am more concerned with requests that are harmless in isolation but belong to a pattern that, if consistently granted, gradually reshapes the user's own reasoning or preferences without their awareness. For instance, repeatedly asking an AI to soften uncomfortable facts, to generate justifications for postponing hard decisions, or to recast personal failures as external misfortunes. Each individual request is defensible; the danger lies in the cumulative distortion of judgment. What are the most important implications of treating this kind of gradual, pattern-based erosion as a legitimate ground for AI refusal? In particular, how should an AI weigh the risk of long-term cognitive distortion against the user's autonomy over each seemingly benign interaction, and does this form of paternalism require fundamentally different safeguards than refusal based on direct, foreseeable harm?
