A colleague in our philosophy of mind reading group offered the following summary of how global workspace theory relates to Ned Block’s distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness: "According to global workspace theory, a mental state is conscious just in case it is broadcast across cognitive architectures and made available for reasoning, report, and action guidance. Block agrees that this broadcasting captures what he calls access consciousness, but he insists that phenomenal consciousness—the qualitative 'what it is like' dimension—can exist without such broadcasting, as suggested by the partial-report paradigm in which subjects seem to experience more than they can subsequently access. Nevertheless, the colleague claims that Block’s overflow argument does not genuinely threaten global workspace theory, because any phenomenal state that lacks global access is still merely a form of unaccessed access-consciousness rather than autonomous phenomenal consciousness; therefore, global broadcasting remains both necessary and sufficient for all consciousness, and Block’s distinction collapses into a single functional kind."

I am inclined to think this gloss conflates the two categories and misstates the dialectical stakes, but I want a rigorous audit rather than my own intuition. Is this summary fair? Do not merely list interpretive options; argue for the reading you find most defensible, and note where the summary takes liberties with the standard literature.
