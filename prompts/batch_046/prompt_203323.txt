I'm taking an upper-division metaphysics seminar focused on essentialism and biological identity, and we've been working through Kripke-style origin essentialism as applied to organisms—specifically the claim that an individual human or animal necessarily originates from the particular sperm and egg that actually produced it. I want to clarify a conceptual distinction that hasn't come up directly in our course materials.

There seems to be a difference between what I will call gametic essentialism—the view that the specific fusion event of sperm S and egg E is essential to the organism's identity—and genomic essentialism, which holds that what is essential is the particular genetic sequence that resulted, regardless of which gametes contributed it. These can come apart in thought experiments involving synthetic gametes, nuclear transfer, or mitochondrial replacement therapy, where the nuclear genome might match what S and E would have produced, but the cytoplasmic and epigenetic environment derives from different sources.

My question is conceptual rather than empirical: does origin essentialism, properly understood, commit us to gametic essentialism, genomic essentialism, or some hybrid? More precisely, if we allow that an organism could not have come from different nuclear DNA but could have come from different cytoplasmic origins (or vice versa), are we still preserving the core intuition behind origin essentialism, or have we already abandoned it? And how should we think about cases like tetragametic chimerism, where a single organism contains two distinct genomic lineages that both contributed to development—does this show that origin essentialism must be scoped to a specific cell lineage rather than to the organism as a whole, or does it reveal that origin is a more plural concept than the standard formulation allows?

Please explain the conceptual landscape here: what the standard origin essentialist should say about these distinctions, where the main pressure points lie, and whether there is a coherent way to maintain origin essentialism without implicitly smuggling in a discredited genetic reductionism about biological identity.
