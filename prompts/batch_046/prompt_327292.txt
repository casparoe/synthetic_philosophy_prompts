I've been thinking about the special composition question and I've landed on a kind of anti-ontological functionalism: there just aren't composite objects in any strict sense, only pluralities arranged with different levels of causal and informational integartion. The reason we feel like there's a sharp 'special composition' problem—why cells make an organism but scattered rocks don't—is that we mistake high functional coupling for the emergence of a genuinely singular entity. Even a human body is just a very tightly knit plurality, not one thing made of parts. What would actually count against this? I don't want the standard objections listed; I want to know which specific results, intuitions, or cross-disciplinary findings would genuinely expose it. For example, does quantum entanglement or the metaphysics of personal identity over time put pressure on treating integration as a smooth spectrum? Would a convincing biological case of fission or fusion show there's a real singular entity that can split, rather than just a plurality rearranging? And does this view secretly collapse into universalism the moment you admit every plurality has at least minimal coupling? I'd like the defeaters ranked—fatal to merely awkward—including what defenders of this kind of view would themselves concede, and an honest assessment of whether I've qualified things so much that it's become unfalsifiable, and if that's actually a problem for a metaphysical claim.
