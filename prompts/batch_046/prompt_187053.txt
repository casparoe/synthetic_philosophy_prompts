While reporting on recursive self-improvement, I overheard a debate between Dr. Elena Voss, a philosopher of mind, and Dr. Marcus Chen, a systems engineer, about whether an AI that rewrites its own objective function exemplifies Douglas Hofstadter’s idea of a tangled hierarchy escaping its inviolate level. Voss argued that once a model can alter its own evaluation criteria, there is no fixed bottom layer analogous to the neurons that draw Escher’s hands; the hierarchy collapses into pure recursion without a ground, so the inviolate level—logical as well as physical—has been breached. Chen replied that this confuses software with substrate: energy supplies, silicon fabrication, and thermodynamic limits remain untouched and function exactly as the inviolate level, unchangeable from within the loop, so the hierarchy is merely layered rather than tangled. Voss countered that in Hofstadter’s formulation the inviolate level is logical—formal systems contain axioms they cannot derive internally—and if the AI revises its own axioms, it violates that logical invariance regardless of physics. Chen answered that even revised axioms depend on a compiler and a physical machine; one cannot compile oneself out of physics, so logical revision is always mediated by an untouched physical stratum. They asked me to referee. Please adjudicate point by point: who is right about whether logical self-revision escapes the inviolate level, and who is right about whether physical substrate always preserves it? Say also what, if anything, both miss—perhaps about whether tangled hierarchies apply to engineered artifacts at all. Finally, state how confident you are in each part of your verdict and identify the specific point about which you are least certain.
