I was discussing liberty with a friend last night after revisiting Berlin’s familiar distinction, and we kept circling the same disagreement without any map of where we actually stood. She insisted that freedom is essentially the absence of actual obstacles placed by others, so that a slave who never receives a command and a citizen in a democracy with perfect non-interference are both free in precisely the same respect; I kept wanting to say that there is something genuinely unfree about being subject to another’s will even when that will remains dormant, and that true freedom requires some positive capacity for self-direction rather than merely an empty space. But then she raised cases that broke my intuition: a society architecturally designed so that citizens never need to be coerced because dissent is structurally impossible without anyone actively blocking it, and a person who so thoroughly anticipates a dominant party’s preferences that no external command is ever necessary—a pattern I have started calling preemptive compliance. I want a decision tree, or a full taxonomy presented as one, that takes someone from concrete commitments in these sorts of cases to an actual named position on liberty—positive, negative, republican, and whatever hybrid or unnamed positions actually occupy the logical space between them.

The instrument should begin with diagnostic questions phrased without theoretical labels, so that a respondent can answer them without already knowing which philosopher they endorse. For example: does the absence of active interference suffice for freedom when a dominant party retains the arbitrary power to interfere at will? Does freedom require not merely the absence of obstacles but the presence of specific capacities or resources? How should we classify cases where interference is absent not because power is lacking but because the subordinate anticipates it and adjusts—this preemptive compliance I mentioned? And where does structural design that eliminates the need for direct coercion belong: as a hidden form of interference, as domination without interference, or as something that neither the negative-liberty nor the republican view captures well? Each branch should split on one of these concrete judgments, and each leaf should carry the name of the position—Berlin-style negative liberty, positive liberty of the self-mastery variety, Pettitian republican non-domination, libertarian non-interference, or an unnamed hybrid—together with a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment regarding these edge cases. Please flag any pairs of positions your tree cannot genuinely separate, and note any logical combinations that seem coherent but are unoccupied, whether because they are unattractive, unexplored, or perhaps incoherent. I am not asking for a bibliography or a literature review; I want the classification instrument itself, built so that someone could answer the questions without knowing the philosophical labels in advance and then see exactly which view they have endorsed.
