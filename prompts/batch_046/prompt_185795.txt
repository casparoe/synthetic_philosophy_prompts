I've been discussing with some friends whether countries today can still be guilty for wrongs that happened generations ago, long before anyone now alive was born, and I keep circling back to Karl Jaspers' idea, developed after the war, that there are four different kinds of guilt: criminal guilt for actually breaking a law, political guilt for belonging to a state that committed wrongs, moral guilt for one's own personal actions and choices, and metaphysical guilt, which is a kind of shared human responsibility for failing to prevent suffering even when you had no direct hand in it. Jaspers seems to hold that moral guilt is strictly personal and cannot be inherited, while political guilt can attach to citizens of a nation regardless of individual choice. At the same time, I've heard people argue that when a nation was built on slavery or genocide, later generations inherit not just political responsibility but an actual moral debt—something like inherited moral guilt for continuing to benefit from past crimes.

I'd like you to compare Jaspers' strict separation between political and moral guilt with this idea of transgenerational inherited moral guilt. Specifically, I'm wondering whether Jaspers' own category of metaphysical guilt—which applies universally regardless of direct action—creates a hidden path for moral guilt to travel across generations, or whether it stays purely individual and therefore leaves political guilt as the only genuinely collective form. Can you compare these two frameworks—Jaspers' four-part typology applied to collective and intergenerational contexts versus the view that nations carry an inherited moral burden—and tell me which one better accounts for the intuition that descendants who benefit from past wrongs owe something more substantial than mere political acknowledgment? I'd especially like to know if there's an internal tension in Jaspers' position: if metaphysical guilt can be shared by all humanity without direct involvement, why can't moral guilt be shared by all members of a nation across time, and which of these two views handles that consistency problem more cleanly?
