I have recently been appointed chair of the research ethics panel at an institute that reviews behavioural economics protocols, and we are facing a sharp increase in studies employing algorithmic nudging — altering digital choice architectures without participants' awareness to measure welfare effects. I need a reusable decision procedure that my panel can apply to determine whether such a proposal violates Kant's universalizability tests, specifically the Formula of Universal Law and the Formula of Humanity. The procedure should be a step-by-step checklist that a non-philosopher reviewer can complete in under thirty minutes, translating both formulations into operational tests: one set for identifying contradictions in conception and contradictions in will, and another for detecting whether participants are treated merely as means rather than as ends in themselves. It must handle hard cases, such as a protocol that withholds information about the nudge but offers post-hoc compensation, and one where participants consent to deception in general terms but not to the specific mechanism. Provide worked applications to three distinct study designs, including one that produces a verdict the principal investigators will strongly resist, and explicitly mark the points where reviewer judgment cannot be eliminated. Do not supply a bibliography or citation list. Give a committed answer that takes definite positions on the ordering of the tests and on how to resolve conflicts between them, and write the response in British English.
