I am trying to identify, and if necessary name, a recurring dynamic in debates over whether the Neolithic transition was a mistake. The pattern is this: across multiple independent regions, the archaeological record shows that early agriculturalists were physically smaller, carried heavier pathogen loads and more dental disease, and worked longer hours than the hunter-gatherer populations they replaced or displaced. For any given local group, adopting farming therefore appears to have been a net loss in welfare and leisure. Yet the transition occurred repeatedly and apparently irreversibly, and agricultural societies eventually expanded at the expense of foraging ones. The standard explanation is that farming supports far higher population density and more hierarchical coordination, so each group faced a competitive environment in which remaining foragers risked being outnumbered, displaced, or absorbed, while switching to farming secured collective survival at the cost of individual flourishing.

This looks like a social trap, but I want to distinguish it precisely from neighboring concepts. It is not quite a prisoner's dilemma, because the harm is not produced by mutual defection within a cooperating group but by competitive exclusion between societies adopting different modes. It is not exactly a Malthusian trap, because the pressure is not purely population outstripping resources within one mode but the dominance of one mode over another through demographic and military advantage. And it is not mere technological lock-in, since the technology is not adopted for its intrinsic efficiency but for its capacity to generate the scale needed to survive competition. Is there an established term—perhaps in evolutionary anthropology, collective-action theory, or philosophy of social choice—for this specific configuration, in which a locally inferior equilibrium becomes globally unavoidable because it produces the organizational and demographic power to suppress its alternatives? If there is no established term, can you coin one with a precise definition and boundary conditions, specifying exactly how it differs from a prisoner's dilemma, a Malthusian dynamic, and technological path dependence? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
