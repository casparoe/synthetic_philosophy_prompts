I am preparing an independent study for my senior philosophy seminar on conflicts between genuinely incommensurable values—cases where honoring one value does not merely diminish but structurally excludes honoring another, and where no common scale can adjudicate between them. Rather than asking for a general essay, I want you to rank the following five proposed responses to such conflicts on a scale of 1 to 10, where 10 means the response fully respects the incommensurability without collapsing into either reductionism or paralysis, and 1 means it effectively denies that there is a real conflict. The five are: (1) lexical priority rules, where one value always trumps the other in direct conflict; (2) weighted multi-attribute utility, treating values as dimensions that can be traded off at the margin; (3) tragic-choice acceptance, which holds that the right decision still leaves an irreparable moral remainder; (4) domain segregation, which assigns each value exclusive jurisdiction over separate spheres of life; and (5) what I will call retroactive recalibration, the view that the weights of values are not fixed prospectively but are reconstructed after the choice is made, so that the path taken determines which value is seen as having governed the decision rather than comparing them beforehand. For each, provide the score and a single sentence explaining the core reason. Then, in a brief paragraph, assess whether any of these actually resolves the incommensurability or whether they only manage its symptoms, and say whether retroactive recalibration avoids the charge of being an ex post rationalization. Please keep the reasoning self-contained; I am working without access to external sources and do not need citations.
