Here is roughly where I've landed. (1) I think the evidential problem of divine hiddenness succeeds against classical theism only if we assume that God's primary aim is universal propositional knowledge, but I reject that aim in favor of a pedagogical-distance model: God's hiddenness preserves the conditions for genuinely free, non-coerced relational commitment, analogous to how a skilled therapist might withhold immediate interpretation to foster a patient's autonomy. (2) I also want to apply a judgment-aggregation framework to religious belief-formation: individual agents form judgments on experiential and existential premises, but when these are aggregated across a pluralistic community we encounter the discursive dilemma—majority agreement on premises can yield contradictory collective conclusions about divine presence—so hiddenness might be reconceived not as one agent's intentional withholding but as a structural feature of distributed epistemic evaluation. (3) Despite that structural reconception, I maintain uniform evidential standards: belief in God should meet the same bar as belief in other mind-independent entities, and I reject any special pleading that would excuse hiddenness by lowering epistemic requirements. (4) I further think that if both the pedagogical model (1) and the uniformity constraint (3) are correct, then hiddenness should be proportionate and eventually revocable—like good therapy eventually offers interpretation—yet the persistence of hiddenness for lifelong, capable seekers seems disproportionate, which makes me suspect that either (1) or (3) needs serious qualification. (5) At the same time, I think that if hiddenness is structurally inevitable in the way (2) suggests, then the evidential problem dissolves entirely: the absence of universal belief is not a failure of revelation but an unavoidable artifact of judgment aggregation.

Please answer in American English. Is this a consistent package, or am I helping myself to something in (5) that (3) rules out, and does (2)'s structural explanation undermine (1)'s intentional design? Be specific about the tensions: order them by severity, identify which pair is only apparently in conflict and dissolves on inspection, and say what the cheapest way to restore consistency is—whether I should drop the aggregation framework, qualify the pedagogical model, or relax the uniformity constraint—and what each revision costs me in terms of the other commitments.
