For decades, the Sleeping Beauty problem has functioned as a kind of philosophical groundhog day: upon being told she has been awakened according to a protocol involving a fair coin, a Monday-only awakening for heads, and a Monday-Tuesday pair with amnesia for tails, Beauty is asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. The thirder argument runs that there are three epistemically indistinguishable awakening-centers—Monday-heads, Monday-tails, and Tuesday-tails—so by a principle of indifference her credence should divide evenly, yielding one-third. The halfer counters that Beauty knew the protocol in advance, that being awake provides no information she did not already condition on, and that her prior credence of one-half should therefore stand. The literature has accumulated refinements—centered-world semantics, analogies to double-halfers, distinctions between de se and de dicto conditioning—without either camp retreating.
