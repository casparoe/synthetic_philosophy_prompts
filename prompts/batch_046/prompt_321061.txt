After arguing with my roommate about whether not replacing teh smoke detector battery "caused" the fire damage, I got weirdly into causation by omission and now I have to run a session for my book club — mostly engineers and one lawyer, zero philosophers. I want to avoid the usual "you forgot to water the plant" example because they'll just nod and miss the metaphysical point. Instead I want to carry everything with one paired case: a hospital backup generator that fails during a blackout. In version A a technician disabled the auto-start for maintenance and forgot to re-enable it; in version B the hospital never bought one. The point is that in A the omission is causal relative to a baseline of normal functioning, but in B there's just absence, not an omission. Plan the arc: what idea has to be in place before they can even see the A/B distinction, what this example needs to have and lack to work, the misconceptions they'll form — I'm guessing "omissions aren't real events so they can't cause anything" and also "A and B are the same since nothing happened in either" — and exactly where in the sequence to head each one off. Give me one final check question that shows whether they've grasped the baseline-dependence idea rather than just feeling bad for the technician. Also tell me what to leave out at this level. Keep it under 300 words, American English, no reading lists.
