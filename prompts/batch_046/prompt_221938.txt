Please describe the most important implications of Douglas Hofstadter's three-layer model of messages—frame message, outer message, and inner message, developed in Gödel, Escher, Bach—for interpreting the outputs of large language models. Specifically, analyze whether the semantic content of a generated sequence should be located in the architectural frame (training objective, tokenization, and inductive biases), in the outer message (the literal token string), or in the inner message (the intended propositional or pragmatic meaning reconstructed by an interpreter). What does this division imply for mechanistic interpretability projects that attempt to localize meaning within specific circuits, attention heads, or layer representations? In particular, discuss whether treating model weights as a frame that produces an outer message risks conflating syntactic regularity with semantic content, and whether the recovery of an inner message necessarily requires a recipient who shares the frame—thereby challenging purely internalist accounts of machine understanding.
