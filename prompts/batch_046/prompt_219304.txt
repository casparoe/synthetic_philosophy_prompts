I have been discussing Wittgenstein’s later philosophy of religion with colleagues, and we keep circling the same tension: if religious belief is not a hypothesis about the world but a form of life with its own grammatical rules, then what happens when the practices survive but the propositional content has been deliberately emptied? I am preparing a seminar on this, and I would like you to write a short story that dramatises this specifically Wittgensteinian problem without collapsing it into a standard debate between faith and scepticism.

Please write a story of no more than about 600 words, set in a near-future archival community that preserves what it calls “grammatical religions”: ritual systems in which every doctrinal statement has been translated into a non-propositional syntax of gesture, silence, and communal repetition. The central character should be an archivist who genuinely participates in one of these grammars—not as a believer in any metaphysical claim, but as someone for whom the practice regulates grief, hope, and moral obligation in the way a language-game regulates meaning. The conflict should arise when an external auditor, trained in evidential reasoning, insists on translating the archivist’s commitments into falsifiable propositions, and the archivist must explain, or fail to explain, why that translation destroys the very phenomenon being studied. Do not make the auditor a caricature; let the misunderstanding be structural, arising from the auditor’s perfectly reasonable demand for semantic content where only grammatical use exists. Include at least one concrete scene in which a ritual is performed and its meaning is shown to depend entirely on its place within the form of life rather than on any statement of fact.

After the story, provide a section titled Commentary on the narrative choices of roughly 150 to 200 words. In it, identify which story elements correspond to Wittgenstein’s distinction between belief as propositional assent and belief as a framework governing life, and note honestly where the fictional setting exaggerates or risks distorting Wittgenstein’s actual view—particularly whether treating religious grammar as entirely devoid of propositional residue is consistent with his remarks on religious pictures. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific editions. Write in polished British English.
