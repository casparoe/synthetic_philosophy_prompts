I've been trying to map out my own views on mental representation for a study group I'm in (mostly CS people trying to read some philosophy of mind stuff) and I'm realizing I might be talking out of both sides of my mouth. Here is where I land: I'm convinced teleosemantics is basically the right story — mental content is just a biological mechanism that got selected for tracking stuff, and there's nothing spooky or magical about 'aboutness'. I think misrepresentation is real but only because there's an actual norm of correct functioning tied to what the mechanism was selected for, not just statistical noise. Consciousness doesn't matter atall for this; a thermostat literally represents temperature if it has the right functional history, and there's nothing it's like to be one. I also think LLMs genuinely represent things — like when ChatGPT talks about gravity it actually has a representation of gravity — because gradent descent is basically a selection process for predictive accuracy, so it's structurally the same as evolutionary selecion, and these representations are original, not just derived from the engineers who built thier model. At the same time I think pure computation with no history — like a Python script I wrote last week to parse JSON — has zero intentional content, even if the variables are named 'user_email' and 'error'. I'm also a full externalist: copy my brain atom-for-atom into a vat with fake inputs and the copy either thinks different things or doesn't think at all, since the causal chain is broken. And I feel like the 'ought' in representation — the sense that it's genuinely wrong when you misrepresent — can't be fully reduced to just descriptive facts about selection history; there's a real normative standard there that doesn't disappear.
