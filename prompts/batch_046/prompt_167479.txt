I'm serving as a teaching assistant for an upper-level metaphysics seminar, and I have been asked to grade a fellow student's response to our recent exam on persistence. I want an independent point-by-point assessment to check my own marking before I submit grades.

The exam question was: "Explain the core distinction between endurantism and perdurantism regarding how material objects persist through time. Then apply both frameworks to the case of a university department that retains its legal charter and physical building but has experienced complete faculty turnover between 1990 and 2024. Specifically, discuss whether the department's research tradition—say, a sustained focus on modal logic—counts as an essential property, and evaluate whether a perdurantist can coherently say the department has changed its nature rather than merely accumulated temporal parts. Finally, address one major intuitive objection to perdurantism: that it conflicts with our sense of being wholly present at each moment."

The student's answer read as follows.

Endurantism holds that an object exists in full at every time it exists, lacking temporal parts. Persistence is analogous to a three-dimensional object extending through space, except that the object is not spread out through time. Perdurantism, by contrast, treats objects as four-dimensional and says they persist by having different temporal parts at different times, just as they have different spatial parts at different locations.

Applying this to the department: the endurantist says the department in 1990 and the department in 2024 are literally the same entity, entirely present at both times. The faculty members are accidental properties, like the color of a car. Even though every person changed, the charter and building keep it identical. The research tradition is also accidental—if the department switched to empirical psychology it would still be the same department, just with different properties.

The perdurantist says the department is a spacetime worm composed of temporal slices. The 1990 slice contains professors A, B, and C and focuses on modal logic. The 2024 slice contains professors D, E, and F and focuses on empirical psychology. These slices are parts of one larger four-dimensional object. So when we say the department changed its nature, we just mean that later temporal parts have different properties from earlier ones.

This also answers the objection about being wholly present. The objection, originally developed by David Lewis, says that if we are four-dimensional worms then only our present temporal parts are at a meeting, not us as a whole, which feels wrong. But the perdurantist can reply that being wholly present just means having a temporal part that is present—there is no further fact about the whole worm being there. So the intuition is satisfied once we understand that presence is part-relative.

One further point: endurantists reject temporal parts entirely, so they must also deny that the building is part of the department, since they reject all part-whole relations across time. This shows endurantism has trouble accommodating institutional structures that clearly include physical locations.
