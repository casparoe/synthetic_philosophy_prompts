I want a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on whether and how conceivability guides metaphysical possibility, designed for readers with basic familiarity with modal logic and philosophy of mind but with no required knowledge of specific texts or authors. The instrument should work like a diagnostic quiz: each internal node must present a concrete case or claim that respondents can answer without knowing the position labels, and the tree should be built from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of named theories.

Root split: Does clearly imagining a macroscopic scenario—say, a solid iron cube floating unsupported in a vacuum—provide any evidence that the scenario is metaphysically possible, or is it merely a psychological image with no modal import? If the answer is that it has no modal import at all, that is the first leaf: Pure Modal Empiricism, on which all modal knowledge derives from empirical or scientific sources rather than from imagination.

If the answer is yes, it does provide evidence, proceed to: Can that evidence ever be defeated by further discovery? Present the case of imagining water that behaves exactly like water but is composed of XYZ rather than H2O, followed by the discovery that being H2O is essential to actual water. If the answer is that the initial conceivability is never defeated, split on domain uniformity: Is the evidential force the same for all types of properties, or is it restricted?

For uniform application, use the case of conceiving both the floating cube and a physically identical human being that entirely lacks conscious experience (the familiar phenomenal-zombie-style case) with equal clarity and equal modal weight. That yields Unrestricted Cartesian Rationalism.

For restricted application, the zombie-style case retains full evidential force for phenomenal properties, but physical-composition cases like the XYZ water do not—conceivability guides possibility only for phenomenal or non-physical domains. That yields Phenomenal-Domain Rationalism.

If instead the answer at the defeat-node is yes, the evidence can be defeated, split on what does the defeating. One branch is defeat by empirical discovery of hidden essential properties (the XYZ-water pattern): Necessary-A-Posteriori Qualified Rationalism. A second branch is defeat by failure of bounded-resource algorithmic simulation—the injected niche view, Resource-Bounded Simulationism, which holds that a scenario is modally relevant only if it can be consistently simulated within bounded computational limits, and that defeat comes from simulation collapse or contradiction within those bounds rather than from hidden essence. A third branch is defeat by recognition of our own epistemic ignorance, so that conceivability tracks only epistemic possibility: Epistemic Modalism.

The taxonomy must contain at least these six named leaves. Identify any unoccupied combinations in the logical space—for example, a view that accepts defeasibility by hidden essence for physical concepts but insists on infallibility for phenomenal concepts (which the tree collapses into Phenomenal-Domain Rationalism), or a view that allows conceivability only as a heuristic with no modal commitment (which sits adjacent to Pure Modal Empiricism but is not separated). For each gap, briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Also flag any pairs of positions that the tree genuinely cannot separate, and explain exactly which branching cases fail to distinguish them—such as views that differ only on whether physical-domain defeasibility is total or partial, since both answer the XYZ-water case identically.

Every branch must be labeled with the case or claim that splits it, never with an author’s name. The prompt must not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and must not depend on verification of specific publication titles or dates.

Finally, state how confident you are in the taxonomy you have constructed, and single out the specific branch or unoccupied cell about which you are least sure.
