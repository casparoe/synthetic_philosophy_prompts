(a) Environmental determinism, like Huntington's old climate thesis, treats nature as a fixed external cause that mechanically drives societies. The actor-frame rejects one-way causation: cholera bacteria or Tambora aerosols aren't just conditions but participants in networks with bodies, trade routes, and policies.

(b) Alfred Crosby in The Columbian Exchange treats smallpox as an intentional agent of empire—the virus deliberately targeted indigenous populations lacking immunity, clearing the Americas for Europeans. A determinist just sees mechanical population decline, but framing smallpox as an actor shows the outcome depended on colonial contact patterns and immune histories.

(c) Latour's actor-network theory solves the intentionality problem by showing actants literally possess distributed intentionality—microbes intend survival, aerosols intend dispersion—so agency isn't metaphorical. Critics call this animism but miss that intentionality is emergent. The real weakness is that if every causal factor is an agent, human intentionality loses special status and moral responsibility collapses. So giving agency to volcanoes undermines ethics.
