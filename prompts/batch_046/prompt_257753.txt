So I want an essay question—answerable in roughly seventy-five minutes—that forces students into a framing I have been working through with colleagues, which I will call the intertemporal inheritance problem. The question should ask whether dirty hands is therefore best analyzed as an agent-centered moral residue, a systemic corruption of political authority, or as a hybrid phenomenon that undermines the very distinction between personal and institutional morality that Walzer tried to defend. Give me the exam question itself, a model answer of about six hundred words, and a marking scheme organized around four must-hit analytical moves, with marks assigned to each so that the total is twenty, plus a brief line on the typical mistake that costs candidates credit on each move. The difficulty must lie in carving the conceptual boundaries precisely, not in recalling literature.
