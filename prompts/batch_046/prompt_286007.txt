You are a philosopher of personal identity and metaphysics with particular attention to origin essentialism. Before giving your main answer, briefly brainstorm two or three candidate principles that might unify judgments about whether a person could have originated differently and still be the same individual; then evaluate each against the cases below.

Here are my intuitive verdicts.

1. If the particular sperm and egg that actually formed me had never united—say, a different sperm fertilized the same egg—I would not exist; the resulting child would be my sibling, not me. So my actual gametic origin seems essential to my being me.

2. An identical twin and I share the very same origin (one zygote that split), yet we are clearly two different people. So sharing an origin is not sufficient for being the same person.

3. If I am cloned via somatic-cell nuclear transfer using my adult skin cell, the clone is not me—only a genetic duplicate born later—despite carrying my genome. So originating from my own biological material at a later time does not transfer my identity.

4. Over a lifetime, every atom in my body is replaced; I do not require material continuity to persist. So origin essentialism cannot be reduced to retaining the same matter.

5. The case I keep revisiting: suppose reproductive technology advances so that my parents' gametes are synthesized de novo from their DNA, with no actual sperm or egg ever existing, yielding a child with my exact genome raised in my place. Would that child be me? Part of me says yes—the causal and genetic lineage is intact—part says no, because there was no specific fertilization event linking me to that child.

Is there a coherent principle—perhaps distinguishing a causal-historical chain of personal origin from mere genetic or material origin—that captures verdicts 1 through 4 and resolves 5? State it precisely, say what it predicts about synthetic-gamete reconstruction and about a scenario in which my psychological profile is uploaded into a synthetic substrate with no biological origin at all, and be explicit about which of my five verdicts it forces me to abandon. If no single principle works, present the two best competing candidates, identify the clause in each that handles the cloning and twin cases, and state honestly which verdict each candidate must reject.
