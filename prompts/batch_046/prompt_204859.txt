I'm preparing a methodological reflection on the criteria of authenticity employed in the quest for the historical Jesus, and I have observed a recurring configuration that I cannot confidently name. I should like to know whether an established concept in historical-Jesus scholarship, or in philosophy of history more broadly, already captures it.

Consider the following pattern, which I have noticed in reconstructions of Jesus' sayings about the Son of Man and in debates over his stance toward Torah observance. A scholar begins by applying the criterion of multiple attestation—say, to sayings present in both Mark and the Q source—to establish a small, secure core. From this core she derives a preliminary profile of Jesus, perhaps as an apocalyptic prophet with a distinctive ethical orientation. She then turns to the criterion of coherence, accepting additional sayings that align with this profile and rejecting those that do not. Finally, she applies the criterion of dissimilarity, treating as authentic whatever is discontinuous with both Second Temple Judaism and the early Christian movement. The difficulty is that the benchmark for 'dissimilarity' is implicitly calibrated against the preliminary profile, which itself was constructed from sayings that survived the earlier filters. The result is not merely that the criteria are interdependent—scholars have long recognised that—but that they form a closed loop: no saying can challenge the initial profile without being excluded by at least one criterion, yet each step is individually defensible.

This is not simple circular reasoning, because each criterion appeals to independent evidence (parallel texts, internal consistency, contrast with context). Nor is it exactly the hermeneutical circle, since the loop operates across distinct methodological rules rather than within a single interpretive framework. I have also considered whether it is a species of confirmation bias, but the mechanism seems more structural: the criteria are arranged so that their outputs become hidden inputs.

Does this configuration have an established name? If not, I would ask you to coin and define one, specifying its boundary conditions so that it can be distinguished from ordinary criterion interdependence, from bootstrapping in theory construction generally, and from the well-known risks of using coherence as a secondary filter. Please answer in British English.
