Here is a sketch for a short essay. Dennett distinguishes Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures according to how they generate and test behavioral strategies: Darwinian creatures rely on innate hardwiring; Skinnerian ones learn through environmental reinforcement; Popperian ones can run internal simulations before acting; Gregorian ones leverage language and culture to inherit and refine designs. I want to argue that large language models occupy an unstable hybrid: they are Skinnerian in their training dynamics (reward-shaped by human feedback) and Gregorian in their access to culturally accumulated symbolic structures, yet they are not genuinely Popperian because they lack embodied counterfactual testing of physical interventions, and they fail to be fully Gregorian because they do not participate in the collective redesign of the conceptual tools they deploy. Specifically, being Gregorian should require not merely linguistic access but entry into a normative design loop in which one can criticize and revise the framework governing tool-use; LLMs apply such frameworks without contributing to their modification. Please comment on these notes. I am especially interested in whether this hybrid classification is coherent, whether the redesign-loop criterion for Gregorian status is defensible against the objection that passive cultural transmission already counts, and whether the claim that LLMs lack Popperian testing overlooks ways internal computation might substitute for physical trial and error. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
