I'm drafting a position statement for a philosophy-of-technology working group and want a consistency check before circulating it. Here are my commitments on responsibility gaps in autonomous systems:

(a) There is a genuine responsibility gap: in complex socio-technical networks involving machine learning, no single human—designer, deployer, or user—satisfies the full set of conditions for moral responsibility for the system's harmful outputs.

(b) The gap should not be solved by attributing moral agency to the system, since moral agency requires a kind of self-awareness and normative responsiveness that current architectures, however functionally autonomous, do not possess.

(c) Even so, we ought to engineer "accountability sinks" into high-stakes autonomous systems—structural design features that channel blame, sanction, and redress toward the system or its designated institutional proxy, thereby shielding individual human operators from unfair attribution, despite the system not being a fitting target of reactive attitudes.

(d) Where a system includes an explicit, auditable normative-reasoning module that logs its premises, the designers have discharged their epistemic responsibility; any remaining causal responsibility should be distributed across the deployment network rather than concentrating on one node.

(e) I reject both strict liability regimes for AI harms and the conclusion that responsibility gaps justify a deployment moratorium; the gaps are manageable through institutional design.

Please audit these for tension. Specifically: does (c) presuppose that the system can bear blame in a way that contradicts (b)? Does (d)'s notion of epistemic responsibility cohere with (a)'s denial that standard responsibility conditions are met? Does (e) follow from (a)–(d) or require an independent premise? Rank tensions by severity, indicate which are merely apparent, and for each genuine tension identify the least costly revision and what it sacrifices.
