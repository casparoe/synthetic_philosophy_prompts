I am preparing a position paper for a seminar on social epistemology and want an internal consistency audit before I circulate it. Here are the commitments I take myself to hold:

(a) Testimonial injustice is best analyzed not as a bare credibility deficit but as a misallocation of credibility surplus: dominant-group speakers are systematically over-credited in ways that directly extract credibility from marginalized speakers, so the harm is relational and contextually zero-sum.

(b) Hermeneutical injustice is not merely a collective conceptual gap but a form of epistemic labor extraction: marginalized groups are expected to generate and refine interpretive resources for dominant institutions—legal, medical, academic—without reciprocity or compensation, and this labor is itself part of the injustice.

(c) The appropriate remedy for both is primarily institutional redesign—procedural rules for peer review, deliberative bodies, and curriculum construction that redistribute credibility and hermeneutical resources—rather than case-by-case moral correction or individual education.

(d) Despite (c), individual agents retain direct moral responsibility for the specific credibility judgments they render in face-to-face interactions; structural explanation does not function as a moral excuse for particular acts of dismissal.

(e) I reject standpoint epistemology's claim that marginalized social locations confer automatic or unchallengeable epistemic privilege; lived experience provides distinctive but defeasible access to certain social facts, not authoritative knowledge immune to revision.

(f) I also hold that hermeneutical resources are largely constructed and maintained by dominant institutions, so demanding that marginalized groups themselves "fill the gap" without institutional transformation risks intensifying the very extraction described in (b).

Please audit this set. For each pair that is genuinely in tension, state the tension precisely, rank them by severity (most damaging to least), and say what the least costly revision would be and what it costs. Identify at least one pair that looks incompatible but actually dissolves on closer inspection, explaining why the appearance is misleading. Do not treat all conflicts as equal, and do not assume I am willing to drop any particular commitment in advance—treat the set as fixed unless revision is the only way out. Finally, tell me which single revision, if I were forced to make only one, would eliminate the most tensions at the lowest overall cost.
