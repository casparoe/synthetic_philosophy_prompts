I’m giving the opening remarks at a workshop called Same Mind, Different World? — half the room are metaphysicians who’ve argued about Lewis and Kripke for decades, the other half is cognitive scientists who study counterfactual reasoning in predictive models but dont really care about ontology. The theme is whether saying I could have been a musician commits me to there being another version of me out there, or just me in different circumstances.

I need the speech to actually do structural work: the distinction between counterpart theory — a similar me in another world, not literally me — and transworld identity — its me, same individual, in another possible state — has to shape what I can and cant say. If I lean too hard into youre literally present in every possible world where you exist, the Lewis crowd will groan; if I say theres just a counter-part, the scientists will think Im denying personal continuity, which undermines the whole point about self-models.

Can you write about five minutes of casual remarks? Maybe with one small slip — I want it to sound like Im talking, not reading a paper. No bibliographies or as Lewis said in 1986 citations; just the idea shaping the talk. And please, dont resolve the debate — just show why the way we frame counterfactual selves changes what it feels like to say I could have been elsewere.
