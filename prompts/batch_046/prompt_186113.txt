I've come round to a pretty hardline version of Ayer's idea about life after death — not that it's false, but that it's cognitively empty, and I think I've found a stronger reason for it. Even if you take NDE reports seriously as data, they still depend on spatial metaphors: seeing your body 'from above', feeling yourself 'moving toward a light', etc. So my claim is that any report of post-mortem experience necessarily borrows its descriptive framwork from embodied perception, which means 'disembodied survival' can't be coherently articluated without contradiction. It's not just unverifiable; it's structurally inexpressible in first-person terms. What would count against this? I want an honest map of its exposure: what kind of phenomenological report, what conceptual innovation, or what empircal finding about consciousness without brain activity would actually damage it? Sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a defender of this view should themselves concede as relevant. Also, be straight with me — has this position been qualified into unfalsifiability? If 'disembodied' just means 'not describable in spatial terms', then of course nothing can refute it, but is that insulation a problem for a claim of this kind?
