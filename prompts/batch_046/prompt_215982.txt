So there's this chronic-pain clinic running a two-track protocol. Patients rate intensity like usual, but staff also compute an 'avoidance divergence score' — basically how far your actual behavior shifts to escape the stimulus compared to your self-report. If the divergence is high they label it asymbolic presentation and you get mindfullness and CBT, no opioids; low divergence gets you meds even with lower numbers. They claim they treat suffering, not raw sensation. What theory of pain's badness does that practice presuppose? Does it lock them into saying hurt is only bad because of its motivational push, or could an intrinsic-badness view defend the split some other way? And is it consistent that they withold anxiolytics from people who report panic but sit perfectly still, or does that reveal a hidden contradiction?
