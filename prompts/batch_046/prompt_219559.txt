I'm writing out my position on whether culture can actually cause anything for a grad seminar, and I want a compatability check before I defend it. Here's where I've landed, and I want to know if it's a stable package or if I'm cheating somewhere.

(a) I'm a methodological individualist: any social explanation has to eventually bottom out in individual actions and their subjective meanings, or it's just a story.
(b) I think Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis is empirically wrong — the specific causal link from Calvinist predestination anxiety to the capitalist spirit doesn't hold up.
(c) Still, I think culture is genuinely causal, not just an effect or decoration, in a way that can't be reduced away.
(d) I take elective affinity (Wahlverwandtschaft) to be a real causal mechanism linking cultural forms and economic structures, not just correlation or narrative glue.
(e) I think ideal types are heuristic fictions — they don't map onto real entities.
(f) I think values only explain action via the subjective meanings individuals assign; there's no direct pipeline from value exists to action happens that skips interpretation.
(g) I also think cultural causation is non-fungible: even in principle you can't swap in individual-level psych descriptions and preserve the explanatory work.

Do these hang together? In particular: can (a) and (d) coexist if elective affinity operates between macro-level forms? Does (e) gut (d) since Weber frames affinity through ideal-typical constructions? Is (b) compatible with (c) and (d), or am I just saving the mechanism by abandoning the case study? And does (f) make (c) redundant, or does (g) save it? Also, (a) and (g) look like they're in direct conflict — is that real or does it dissolve once you see what bottoming out allows?

Please rank the tensions by severity, say for each what has to give and what's the cheapest fix. Don't just list them all as equally bad — I need to know which ones are fatal and which are only surface-level.
