I'm a doctoral candidate in science and technology studies, and I'm trying to work through a practical dilemma that seems to conceal a deeper conceptual question about how much early choices genuinely matter. About eighteen months ago, I committed to mastering a particular qualitative archival technique largely because my supervisor had established access to relevant local collections, rather than because it was an obvious match for my long-term intellectual interests. Now, as I prepare applications for postdoctoral fellowships, I notice that the most competitive positions in my area increasingly presuppose fluency in computational modelling and the management of large-scale datasets. I'm weighing whether to spend my remaining doctoral time retraining in those methods, but I'm concerned that such an investment will effectively commit me to a specific trajectory—not merely because of the technical skills involved, but because hiring committees and collaborative networks tend to interpret a CV in ways that reinforce an initial specialisation. At the same time, I wonder whether this anxiety overstates the importance of that original decision: given my broader intellectual disposition, the prevailing institutional incentives in contemporary academia, and the direction of major funding priorities, wouldn't I have gravitated toward data-intensive work eventually, regardless of which supervisory path I had taken? I'm particularly struck by the idea that different initial conditions might still converge on the same outcome—a kind of equifinality—yet I also recognise that some institutional arrangements seem to exhibit genuine lock-in, where small early advantages compound in ways that make reversal prohibitively costly. How ought I to assess whether my situation represents a meaningful constraint produced by that contingent fork, or whether the apparent 'lock-in' is largely an illusion produced by retrospective narration? More broadly, does the same endpoint tend to arrive through different routes, or do arbitrary initial choices genuinely determine where one ends up?
