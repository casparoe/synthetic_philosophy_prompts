I serve on a parliamentary constitutional review panel charged with advising the speaker on whether to recommend embedding an intergenerational fiscal precommitment — a constitutional Ulysses contract — into the national charter. The proposed amendment binds future legislatures to a structural deficit ceiling that may be exceeded only by a three-fourths parliamentary vote together with approval from an independent Precommitment Auditor. The proposal also contains a novel "epistemic sunset" clause: should the macroeconomic forecasting framework used to justify the ceiling generate predictions that deviate from observed outcomes beyond a specified statistical threshold for four consecutive quarters, the binding constraint automatically dissolves unless reaffirmed by national referendum. Those who favor the clause maintain that constitutional precommitment forfeits democratic legitimacy when anchored to empirically discredited models; those who oppose it argue that permitting predictive failure to dissolve the commitment nullifies the logic of tying one's future self to the mast, reducing a constitutional rule to a contingent economic forecast. Please draft the panel's formal advisory memo to the speaker. The memo should advance a clear recommendation — adopt with modifications, reject outright, or substitute an alternative formulation — together with concrete implementation conditions and a timeline. It must engage seriously with the democratic-legitimacy objection rather than treating it as a minor caveat, and it should assess whether the sunset mechanism can be redesigned so that it preserves both epistemic humility and the genuinely binding character of the precommitment. Please write the response in British English, observing British spelling and usage conventions (for example, using "programme" rather than "program," "favour" rather than "favor," and "centre" rather than "center").
