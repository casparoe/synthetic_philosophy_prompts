As a novelist and elected member of the ethics board of the Meridian Foundation for Immersive Narrative, I ask you to draft a formal advisory memo for circulation prior to our 14 June session. The board must reach a binding recommendation—approve, reject, or approve with enforceable conditions—regarding a proposed permanent installation, The Palimpsest Fire, in which visitors wear haptic interfaces to tear and burn virtual reconstructions of lost literary manuscripts. These are not digitized survivors; they are generative counterfactuals—probabilistic reconstructions of works destroyed before publication, synthesized from fragmentary authorial notes by an opaque model. Advocates maintain that, lacking physical substrate and being fully restorable from backup, these entities are mere data-structures; their ritual destruction is therefore an aesthetic performance carrying no more moral weight than the deletion of a temporary file. Opponents, including several trustees, argue that within our institution's digital archive these objects have acquired derivative ontological standing: they are cited in scholarship, assigned in curricula, and engaged by readers as authentic contributions to literary history, so that their deliberate, sensory annihilation inflicts a genuine harm upon collective cultural memory and upon the integrity of the virtual world they inhabit. Our charter permits reasonable accommodation of artistic practice but defines neither its scope nor its limits. The memo should present a clear decision with an explicit time frame—such as immediate suspension pending safeguards, or conditional approval with a six-month audit clause—and should address the strongest argument on the losing side with intellectual seriousness rather than dismissal. It should be addressed to the Foundation's trustees and to the installation's curators, and it should examine, without recourse to named authorities or bibliographies, whether virtual objects of this generative, counterfactual kind can properly be said to suffer real harm when they are interactively destroyed.
