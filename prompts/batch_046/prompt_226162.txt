my roommate's in a soc seminar and she's been pretty consistant about whether generational labels are real kinds or just marketing noise. she says Boomers count, but not because of birth years — it's that Vietnam and the counterculture hit them right in the 18-25 window. she accepts Millennials too, mostly because 2008 and the shift to smartphones/social media landed during their late adolescence. but Gen Z she rejects outright; she thinks they're too split by country and platform, with no single shared tech architecture or discrete trauma. and she also accepts the Silent Generation, because Depression and WWII are obvious shared formative events. so she's running something like "real cohort needs a discrete macro-shock or dominant tech regime that hits globally during identity formation."

i can see at least two rules that fit all four of those verdicts though. one is about shared socio-political trauma in the adolescent window; the other is about coming of age under one information architecture with clear demographic boundaries. both explain Boomers, Millennials, Silent Gen yes and Gen Z no — Gen Z missed a unified media ecosystem and COVID hit them at wildly different ages.

so what would she say about the "Prompt Cohort" — kids born roughly 2008-2014 who entered middle school right when LLM tutors and generative AI got baked into standardized testing and homework platforms? and what about "Generation Alpha" more broadly, born 2010-2025? and a cohort defined purely by being born during the first COVID year, March 2020 to Feb 2021? i'm less interested in the yes/no answers than in the rule she's actually using, what each rule predicts for these three, which of them best tells the two rules apart, and if she accepts the Prompt Cohort, what principle she's adopting that she wasn't applying to Gen Z.
