Consider the widespread discursive norm—particularly visible in academic philosophy and public moral debate—whereby a normative criticism of some behavior or social arrangement is treated as significantly weakened, or even dismissed outright, if the critic is shown to engage in the very behavior they condemn. This operates not as a formal institutional rule but as a tacit standing condition: the critic's personal moral record functions as a gatekeeping mechanism for whether their argument receives serious consideration.
