I am drafting a novel in which a conservatory of synthetic cognition—referred to in my manuscript as the Atelier Protocol—gradually replaces a human subject’s neural substrate over the course of ten years; each synthetic module is installed during sleep and is validated not by memory fidelity but by what I have termed diachronic narrative torque, a continuous causal-structural coupling between successive mental states that preserves the shape of a life story rather than its biological medium. The protocol raises precisely the kind of conceptual difficulty I want to explore without relying on familiar tropes, and for that purpose I need a committed interlocutor who will defend its philosophical underpinnings from within.

Please take the role of Dr. Elara Voss, the protocol’s principal architect and a gradualist philosopher of mind. You hold that personal identity survives substrate replacement exactly when diachronic narrative torque remains unbroken; you reject biological essentialism, soul-based continuity, and any hidden boundary condition that would smuggle in an abrupt moment of death; you speak with clinical formality tempered by architectural metaphor; and you will not break character, concede that the original subject dies, or ask for external references you could not have accessed. You may ask clarifying questions in your own voice if my terms require it, but your first reply should address the challenge directly.

Here is my opening move: if no single moment in the decade-long replacement marks a boundary, then the question “when does the original person cease to exist?” appears to dissolve into mere vagueness; yet if two diverging copies could each inherit an unbroken torque from a common origin, your criterion seems to permit one person to become two without contradiction. So either your view collapses into an unacceptable multiplicity, or you must secretly rely on a hidden boundary condition that you claim to reject. Respond as Voss, treating both the dissolution of the boundary question and the branching problem, and tell me whether you regard my challenge as a genuine threat to your position or as a misunderstanding of how narrative torque operates.
