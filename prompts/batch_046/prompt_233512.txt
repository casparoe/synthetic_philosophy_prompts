I spent thirty years tracking seabird populations in the North Atlantic, so I came to philosophy pretty late—mostly through a philosophy of science course I take at the community college with other retirees. The handicap principle always made intuitive sense to me: peacocks with huge tails are basically saying I'm so fit I can waste energy on this ridiculous thing. Lately I've been wondering if the same logic drives a lot of what looks like epistemic dysfunction in modern research culture. Here's the view I've ended up with: in fields hit by replication crises, the push for ever-more-expensive methodological rituals—like mandatory pre-registration paired with byzantine statistical corrections, or sample-size requirements that only well-funded labs can meet—functions as a costly signal of researcher quality rather than a purely epistemic fix. The cost itself is the point; lowering it, say by allowing simpler preregistered designs, gets resisted because it removes the filter that seperates serious labs from casual ones. I think this applies especially to social psychology and parts of medicine, where the signal value of a study seems to scale with how painful it was to run, not with how much it actually teaches us.

So what would actually count against this? I don't want the usual correlation-isn't-causation handwave. I want to know which observations—say, fields where simplification happened and credibility went up, or occurences where expensive methods collapsed and nobody noticed—would be fatal versus just awkward. I also want to know if defenders of the handicap story would concede anything here, or if they've qualified the view so that every outcome confirms it (resistance proves the signal, adoption proves the signal was already strong). Is there a risk I've turned this into an unfalsifiable just-so story? And if you had to bet, is there a better analogy for why these methodological costs persist—maybe it's just institutional inertia, or maybe there's a real epistemic reason I'm missing? Be honest about where the position is exposed and where it's well insulated, and rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward.
