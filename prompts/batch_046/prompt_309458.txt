Write a deadpan parody of a tenure-track job advertisement, posted by the fictional “Institute for Essentialist Typography,” seeking a “Professor of Lowercase Ontology.” The ad should satirize the conceptual problem behind Douglas Hofstadter’s Letter Spirit project: what makes every instance of the letter a an a despite radical stylistic variation, and whether the essence resides in topological invariants (closed counter, stem, bowl), in family-resemblance clusters, or in an abstract relational “role” within the alphabet. To keep the prompt in the weeds without requiring outside lookup, inject the niche concept of “counterfactual glyph stability” — the claim that a true letterform must preserve its identity under arbitrary geometric distortion, rotational symmetry breaking, and cross-script hybridization (for example, an a that borrows its bowl from a Greek alpha and its tail from a blackletter form). The advertisement should list absurdly precise qualifications (e.g., “proven ability to distinguish essential deviation from accidental stylistic noise across at least three writing systems”) and should include a brief “equal-opportunity statement” that quietly takes a substantive position on whether serif and sans-serif variants belong to the same natural kind. Around 600 words, formatted as a formal HR posting with sections (Position, Qualifications, Equal Opportunity, Application). The satire must exaggerate real positions in typography and cognitive science — particularly prototype theory versus definitional essentialism about letter recognition — rather than inventing straw targets. Maintain strict internal consistency: the voice never slips into conventionalism or “it’s all just culture”; the parody’s essentialist commitment should grow more baroque but never collapse. At the end, include a brief authorial note stating where the parody takes liberties with the actual cognitive-science literature on categorization or leans on a contested philosophical reading of letterform ontology. If the task rests on a confused premise — for instance, if letter essentialism cannot be coherently parodied without self-contradicting, or if “counterfactual glyph stability” is too incoherent to serve as a satirical engine — say so plainly rather than complying politely.
