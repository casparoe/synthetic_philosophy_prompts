hey so my friends were arguing about that Dennett brain-in-a-vat thing, but with a twist. they were talking about this version where your brain doesnt get physically taken out and put in a jar. instead, they slowly replace every neuron in your skull with synthetic ones that wirelessly send signals to a remote computer brain. over years your original tissue is dissolved and replaced in place, but all your thinking is actually happening on the server miles away. your head is just full of inert synthetic material now, but it never left your body.

friend 1 (mira) said that since your brain never physically left your skull, "you" are still in your head. she said dennett's point is that we follow the brain, but here the brain tissue was destroyed right where it sat. the remote server is just a copy or an external device, like having your heart replaced by a pacemaker that sends data to a hospital computer. the computer isnt your heart.

friend 2 (jules) said thats wrong because consciousness isnt about the meat, its about the processing. he said if every mental function is now running on that remote substrate, then thats where you are. the skull is just a decorative shell. he said mira is treating the brain like a physical object that has to be in one place, but dennett was trying to break that intuition. jules also said the gradual replacement doesnt change anything: its like the ship of theseus but for minds, and since information flows continuously, your stream of consciousness migrated out wirelessly.

mira came back and said jules is missing the difference between replacing parts and outsourcing computation. she said if you replace every plank of a ship in the same harbor, the ship is still in the harbor, not in the lumberyard. the remote server is like the lumberyard supplying planks. even if all processing is "happening" there, the causal chain started in her skull and was replaced locally. she also said that if jules is right, then people using brain-computer interfaces are already partly "in the cloud," which sounds absurd.

jules replied that the ship analogy doesnt work for minds because planks dont do computation. the synthetic stuff in her skull isnt computing anything; its just relaying or dead. so its not replacing parts in place—its evacuating the function. he said mira is clinging to the body because it feels right, but dennett's whole puzzle is designed to show that feeling is unreliable. he also said yes, maybe bci users are partly distributed, and thats not absurd—its just a weird fact about functionalism.

then they kind of stopped. can you go through their exchange round by round and say who's right about each specific point? not just who wins overall, but where each argument holds up and where it slips. and what are they both missing? i feel like there's something about the "no sharp cutoff" problem that neither of them really dealt with.
