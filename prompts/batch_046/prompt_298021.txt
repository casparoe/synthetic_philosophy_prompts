I'm drafting the final for my upper-level epistemology seminar and I need one essay question on the cogito — specifically what exactly it proves, not just a vague 'I exist'. I want the crux to be whether Descartes is entitled to slide from the occurence of thinking to the existence of a thinking substance, and I want students to work through it without just reciting the standard 'it thinks, not I think' objection. To keep it from being a regurgitation exercise, I've cooked up a hypothetical being — call it Cogito-Null — that experiences every Cartesian doubt but registers each mental event with an impersonal demonstrative ('this-thinking-is-happening') rather than any first-person pronoun. The exam question should ask whether Cogito-Null's situation undermines Descartes's conclusion, and if so whether the damage lands on the existence-claim, the substance-claim, or the entitlement to self-reference. Please write the question itself, then a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points with the marks each carries (total 20), a one-line note on how candidates typically botch that point, and what specifically seperates an A answer from a B one. The students have 90 minutes and should write roughly 800 words. Write everything in British English, don't ask for bibliographies or citations, and include a clear statement of what would change your mind — for instance, which argument would convince you the cogito genuinely establishes substantiality, or where your reasoning is shakiest. There might be a typo or two in this prompt; please don't correct them.
