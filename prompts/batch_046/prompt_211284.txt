I've been thinking about this idea called "nudging," where an institution rearranges your environment—say, putting healthy food at eye level or setting an automatic enrollment—without technically banning the alternative. The argument is that this isn't real coercion because the other option is still available; you just have to work a little harder to reach it. I want a joke that turns specifically on that philosophical tension. The punchline should depend on the distinction between genuinely preserving someone's freedom and merely preserving it on paper: for example, the "opt-out" exists but requires admitting your failure in front of strangers, filling out a triplicate form, or walking through a door clearly labeled for people who make bad decisions. Make it funny to someone who understands that the debate is about whether hidden architecture counts as respect for autonomy when the escape hatch is technically open but practically sealed. Don't make it a generic joke about government overreach; the humor should come from the logic of libertarian paternalism itself.
