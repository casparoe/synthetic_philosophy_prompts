Design a 90-minute classroom activity for an upper-level philosophy-of-art seminar with approximately 14 students that stages the problem of aesthetic testimony—specifically, whether and how one can come to know that an artwork possesses artistic value through another person's testimony rather than through direct experiential encounter. Structure the exercise as a role-play with real, small stakes: assign participants to roles such as direct witnesses, intermediaries who relay testimony through a chain, and curators who must render acquisition judgments with unequal access to the works themselves. The rules should force participants to depend on testimony for some evaluations and on first-hand exposure for others, with point allocations tied to the internal consistency and defensibility of their final rankings rather than to agreement with an expert answer key. Provide a precise timeline breaking the 90 minutes into rounds of exposure, testimony transmission, deliberation, and final vote; specify the conditions under which a player gains or loses credibility; and include a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the central arguments in the aesthetic-testimony debate—especially the conflict between deferring to expertise and claiming first-person epistemic authority. Be frank about which feature of the view the mechanics can reproduce (for example, the felt inadequacy of judging a work one has never encountered) and which feature they cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply through guided discussion. To prevent a generic response, inject one niche structural mechanism—such as a credibility-decay rule that weakens testimony as it passes through intermediaries, or an aesthetic-bankruptcy condition in which a player who relies exclusively on testimony forfeits the right to defend a judgment—that uses familiar concepts but is not part of the standard textbook formulation. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citation lists, or further reading; the deliverable should be a self-contained, runnable activity.
