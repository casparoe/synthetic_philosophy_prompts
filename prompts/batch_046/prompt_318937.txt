I'm working through an argument from our philosophy of biology seminar about the so-called DNA–Gödel isomorphism—the idea, sometimes labeled typogenetics, that genome self-replication exhibits the same structural features Gödel exploited in his incompleteness proof. The argument seems to show that either biology violates mathematics or the informational view of DNA collapses entirely, and I cannot locate the weak joint. Here is the chain as I reconstructed it.

(1) Gödel's first theorem applies to any consistent formal system that can represent arithmetic and encode its own syntactic rules; such a system necessarily contains a true statement that it cannot derive.

(2) DNA's four-letter alphabet and pairing rules support combinatorial relations—counting, ordering, complementarity—isomorphic to arithmetic, so the genome is representationally powerful enough to satisfy the "sufficiently powerful" condition.

(3) The genome also encodes the full machinery—polymerases, helicases, repair enzymes—that reads and copies it, so it represents its own replication syntax inside itself.

(4) Replication is consistent: lethal mutations that violate pairing logic or destroy core machinery are eliminated, and the system never operates on contradictory instructions.

(5) Replication is also complete: every functional sequence, including those specifying replication proteins, is physically copied; there is no biologically valid gene that is systematically excluded from copying.

(6) Therefore DNA satisfies Gödel's conditions: it is a consistent, complete, self-referential formal system.

(7) By Gödel, there should exist a genetic sequence S that specifies a true, functional replication factor but is structurally unreplicable—an instruction the genome contains but its machinery cannot copy.

(8) No such sequence exists; any gene that actually specifies replication machinery is copied just like every other.

(9) So DNA is a consistent, complete, self-referential system with no unprovable truths—directly contradicting Gödel.

(10) Since Gödel's result is a necessary mathematical truth, not an empirical generalization, it cannot be overturned by a biological observation.

(11) Hence the premise that DNA is a formal system subject to Gödel must fail, which means the DNA–Gödel isomorphism is false and DNA lacks the representational structure of a code.

(12) Consequently, genetic information is not information in any semantic sense; it is only a causal chemical pattern.

I want to defend the idea that DNA is genuinely representational and that the self-replication loop is philosophically significant—so I would prefer to keep (1) through (5) and reject whatever forces (11) and (12). But I need a committed diagnosis: which step is actually wrong, why did it look plausible, and what does giving it up cost for the typogenetic view? Please take a definite position on where the argument breaks and on what the correct account of DNA's representational status should be, rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose.
