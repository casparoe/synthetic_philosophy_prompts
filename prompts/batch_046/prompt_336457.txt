Write a dialogue between a modal realist committed to David Lewis's ontology of concrete possible worlds and an ersatzist who holds that worlds are maximal consistent sets of propositions rather than spatiotemporally related entities. Set the exchange in a seminar room immediately after a talk on modal epistemology, and make both interlocutors equally sharp: neither should talk past the other, and each should respond directly to the opponent's last move. Have the ersatzist open by pressing an epistemic objection: if worlds are concrete and causally isolated from ours, then knowledge of modal truths about them seems to require either direct acquaintance with entities we can never observe or an inference from actual patterns that risks circularity. The realist should reply not by denying the problem but by adapting the "indexical" account of actuality—arguing that our world enjoys evidential privilege without ontological priority, since being "here" fixes our evidential baseline analogously to being "now." Have the realist then defend counterpart relations as anchored in internal qualitative similarity rather than in trans-world identity, and have the ersatzist counter that any metric of similarity presupposes the very modal distinctions it is meant to ground. Meanwhile, the ersatzist should acknowledge her own vulnerability: selecting among representational schemes—say, a Carnapian state-description framework versus a linguistic ersatz—appears to require prior modal knowledge to determine consistency and completeness conditions. The dialogue should end without resolution, with both conceding that their respective ontologies shrink the circle of modal epistemology in different directions but cannot eliminate it, and with a shared acknowledgment that the problem of circularity remains live precisely because each framework must bootstrap its own modal primitives. Keep the tone rigorous and philosophical, avoid any request for citations or reading lists, and ensure that every technical term is used correctly but explained through the exchange itself.
