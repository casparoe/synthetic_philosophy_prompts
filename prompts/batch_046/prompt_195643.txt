I am moderating a podcast interview with an unnamed scholar who defends a synthesized cyclical thesis applied to epistemic institutions rather than to states. Her position combines Polybius's constitutional anacyclosis with Ibn Khaldun's account of asabiyya decay, but relocates the causal mechanism: she argues that solidarity among knowledge-producers erodes not through external conquest but through internal institutional luxury—professionalization, credential inflation, and the substitution of procedural compliance for shared inquiry. Against Spengler's morphological fatalism and Toynbee's linear challenge-and-response model, she contends that modern societies do not transcend the cycle but enter a peculiar arrested phase she calls archival saturation, in which accumulated institutional responses to prior problems suppress the articulation of new challenges, producing a stasis that resembles linear methodological progress. Draft ten interview questions that avoid generic biography and press on the actual structure of the view. I want at least one question on whether shifting Khaldun's unit of analysis from tribal-political groups to peer-reviewed disciplines severs the causal link between luxury and solidarity; one on how archival saturation can be distinguished empirically both from ordinary bureaucratic sclerosis and from genuine linear improvement in scientific method; and one on whether engaging Spengler and Toynbee requires accepting their metaphysical commitments or merely borrowing descriptive patterns. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to her most probable reply—especially her likely deflection that institutional history belongs to sociology, not philosophy. Conclude with a question specifying what institutional trajectory over the next half-century would falsify her cyclical claim rather than being absorbed as another phase of it. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; assume audience familiarity with the canonical texts but no access to obscure secondary literature.
