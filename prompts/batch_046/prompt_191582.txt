I hold a modified Benatar asymmetry restricted to "threshold-crossing suffering": the claim is that for beings capable of suffering intense enough to cross a qualitative threshold, existence is always a harm, since that suffering's presence is bad and its absence in non-existence is good, while below that threshold the absence of pleasure remains neutral and the asymmetry does not apply. I take this to preserve Benatar's core logic while avoiding overgeneralization to beings with only trivial discomfort. What would count against this modified position? I want defeaters aimed specifically at the threshold mechanism: whether it can be defined without circularity, whether it renders the asymmetry empirically unfalsifiable, whether it collapses into a quality-of-life assessment Benatar explicitly rejects, and whether unqualified defenders of the original asymmetry should treat it as a retreat. Rank these from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which ones a defender of the modified view could concede without abandoning the core asymmetry, and say whether insulating the view behind a movable threshold is a virtue or a vice for an argument of this kind.
