I've been mulling over the Knobe effect since a course on experimental philosophy, and I think I've landed on a somewhat niche take: the asymmetry isn't just "normativity bleeding into intentionality," but that folk attributions for side-effects track whether the outcome sits in a non-derivative spot in the agent's practical reasoning—either pursued or deliberately disregarded—rather than tracking causal knowledge or direct aim. So harming the environment counts as intentional because it figures negatively ("this doesn't stop me"), which is still a non-derivative stance, whereas helping the environment is purely derivative and so doesn't count. I'm wondering what would actually count against this view? Not just the standard normative-account-vs-deep-semantics replay, but which redesigned vignettes, which cross-cultural or developmental results, which findings about collective agency or legal reasoning about intent, and which formal distinctions in decision theory would really hurt it? I'd like potential defeaters ranked from genuinely fatal to merely awkward, plus a clear sense of which ones a defender of this "reasoning-structure" view would concede as real problems versus which ones they'd just absorb by tweaking the non-derivative condition. Also, does the view risk collapsing into the plain normative account if pushed far enough, or is there a distinctive prediction it makes that its rivals don't? I'd especially value knowing where it's well-insulated and whether that insulation is a strength or just a warning sign.
