I am a PhD student in philosophy of language with working familiarity in predictive processing. In Gödel, Escher, Bach, Hofstadter distinguishes frame messages, which establish the rules of interpretation; outer messages, which carry the literal propositional content; and inner messages, which carry self-referential or meta-level significance that only activates once the frame is adopted. I want you to restate this tripartite account in the vocabulary of hierarchical predictive processing—generative models, precision-weighted prediction errors, hierarchical message passing—treating the frame as a high-level prior, the outer message as bottom-up input, and the inner message as a revised posterior that feeds back to alter the prior. Be precise about clean mappings and required distortions. Then provide the residue report: can predictive processing accommodate the claim that meaning is genuinely located in the frame rather than in the symbol stream? Specifically, consider a constructed strange-loop utterance whose literal content includes an instruction to revise the interpretive rules governing that very utterance, and say whether this forces a collapse of frame and message in Bayesian terms, or whether it reveals that Hofstadter's framing relies on an intentional stance absent from the mechanical account. Conclude by judging whether the untranslatable residue marks a real limitation of predictive processing or shows that the original tripartite distinction was partly metaphorical.
