As a software engineer without formal philosophy training, I want to understand a conceptual tension in Rawls that standard introductions usually skip. Behind the veil of ignorance, parties are stripped of knowledge about their social position, talents, and conception of the good, but they retain general knowledge of society and human psychology. The difference principle is then defended through maximin reasoning: facing deep uncertainty, rational agents should arrange institutions so that the worst-off position is as favorable as possible.

The issue I keep running into concerns what kind of ignorance the veil actually requires. In systems design, we distinguish between not knowing which node you are assigned to and not knowing the failure-mode mapping—the rules by which a local configuration propagates into an irreversible global state. Translating that distinction to Rawls: does the veil require ignorance only of one's place in the social distribution, or does it also require ignorance of the "institutional mapping" that translates a constitutional choice into a locked-in social role?

Consider a case where the worst-off position is defined not merely by low income or status, but by structural entrapment in a role with extremely high exit costs and path-dependent obligations—something like being permanently assigned to maintain critical legacy infrastructure with no migration path and no opt-out. If parties behind the veil know that such irreversible assignments are possible, but they do not know the specific mapping from basic structure to entrapment, does maximin reasoning still uniquely justify the difference principle? Or does it instead justify a stricter "anti-lock-in" principle that prioritizes reversibility and exit options over the absolute welfare level of the worst-off?

Please explain this conceptual issue with care: what the veil assumes about knowledge of outcomes versus knowledge of causal and institutional structure; why maximin's defense of the difference principle may depend on treating the mapping from constitutional choice to social position as transparent rather than opaque; and whether introducing opacity about path-dependent, irreversible roles undermines Rawls's argument, modifies it, or leaves it intact.
