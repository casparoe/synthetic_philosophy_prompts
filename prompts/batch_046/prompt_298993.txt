Write an exam question for an upper-level political-philosophy course (90 minutes, 25 marks) on the tragedy of the commons and public goods, constructed so that it cannot be answered by summarizing Hardin. Frame the problem around a constructed mechanism—the "conditional-commitment commons"—in multilateral climate governance: each state rationally withholds ambitious emission-reduction targets unless others commit first, producing collectively inadequate promises even though no physical resource is depleted. Require students to (a) distinguish this mechanism precisely from Hardin's original pasture logic and from standard public-goods free-riding on benefits; (b) evaluate whether institutional design features—transparency registries, ratchet mechanisms, and loss-and-damage provisions—resolve the failure or merely relocate it into a different stage of the bargaining process; and (c) defend or reject the claim that normative commitments (future-promissory actions) have payoff structures different enough from material goods that applying the commons framework is conceptually inappropriate rather than merely empirically inaccurate.

Provide a model answer of approximately 600–700 words and a marking scheme totaling 25 marks, broken into identifiable moves with assigned weights and one-line notes on characteristic mistakes (for example, conflating strategic withholding of promises with free-riding on consumed benefits; treating transparency as automatically solving the problem without addressing the conditional payoff structure; or asserting that normative commitments are "not resources" without analyzing whether their strategic interaction still exhibits the relevant collective-action structure).

In your response, include a brief statement of what would change your mind: specify either an empirical finding about national-target revision behavior in climate negotiations, or a philosophical argument about the ontology of normative commitments, that would lead you to revise your assessment of whether this mechanism is genuinely distinct from standard public-goods failure or is not actually operative in these agreements.
