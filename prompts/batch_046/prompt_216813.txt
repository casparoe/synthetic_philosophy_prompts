Construct a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on whether the Anthropocene should be formalized as a geological epoch, designed so that a reader can navigate it without prior knowledge of the labels. The root node should split positions by whether epochal status requires a durable stratigraphic signal in the rock record or a socio-historical qualitative shift in human-Earth relations. From each branch, split by proposed chronological markers—such as the Industrial Revolution, the Great Acceleration, the Trinity test horizon, or an alternative marker you specify—and then by the locus of ratifying authority: an international geological commission, democratic or intergovernmental bodies, or what might be called "stratigraphic sovereignty" shared with non-human Earth systems. Each leaf must either carry the name of an actual defended position (e.g., the Anthropocene Working Group proposal, the Crutzen-Stoermer thesis, critical-geography rejections) or be marked "unoccupied," followed by one sentence diagnosing why that combination is unstable, incoherent, or merely unexplored. Every branching question should be answerable on conceptual grounds alone, with no dependence on bibliographic recall. Finally, flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate, and briefly explain what further distinction would be needed to distinguish them. Keep the structure rigorous, deadpan, and suitable for an advanced seminar handout.
