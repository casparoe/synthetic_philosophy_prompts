In Sweden there's this practice where tax authorities publish individual income and tax data—anyone can request it, not just journalists or officials. It sits right next to high income taxes and near-universal compliance, yet people don't seem to riot about it. The setup seems to assume that transparency about earnings creates social accountability rather than resentment, and that citizens are kind of co-owners in the welfare system rather than private clients of it. What view of equality, or of the social contract, does that presuppose? Is it assuming that privacy is a luxury good high-trust societies can trade away, or that knowing exacty where everyone stands actually reduces envy? And if you tried to export this to a country with low institutional trust, which part of the implicit philosophy breaks first—the idea that visibility breeds compliance, or that solidarity needs to be visible to work? Reconstruct the theory, say whether it's consistent with other Nordic commitments like universal services funded by progressive taxation, and tell me what'd have to change if we replaced the transparency norm with a strict privacy-rights framewrok.
