I'm trying to understand Ian Hacking's idea of looping effects in psychiatric classification—how being labeled can change the people labeled, which then changes the label itself. I have gut verdicts about when this makes a diagnosis more real versus when it corrupts it, but I can't find the principle that ties them together.

Here are my cases. When homosexuality was listed in the DSM, clinical framing and social stigma looped together so that people experienced same-sex attraction as pathological partly because it was classified that way; removing it in 1973 broke a corrupt loop. Verdict: looping was invalidating, and removal was correct. When autism-spectrum criteria broadened in the 1990s and 2000s, more people identified, formed neurodiversity communities, and reshaped the concept around cognitive difference rather than deficit. Verdict: here looping is constitutive—it made the category more accurate and ethically important, not less real. When media coverage of dissociative identity disorder spiked in the 1980s, clinicians began seeing more elaborate alter-personality presentations that closely matched textbook descriptions. Verdict: this clearly manufactured the phenomenon; looping severely undermined the diagnosis's validity. When antidepressant marketing expanded globally in the 1990s and depression diagnoses rose alongside it, I think the loop was partly commercial and partly revealing hidden suffering. Verdict: ambiguous, but I lean that the category was stretched beyond its useful limits.

But I am genuinely stuck on two structurally similar cases. In adult ADHD identification through social media—where people recognize themselves in checklist symptoms, seek diagnosis, and form online communities—the loop looks like autism's: self-driven, identity-forming, and community-building. Yet my gut says this is social contagion and weakens ADHD's validity, whereas autism's loop strengthened it. I cannot defend the difference consistently.

So: is there a coherent principle—perhaps about who drives the loop, whether it reveals pre-existing suffering or manufactures it, or whether the community formed is liberatory versus instrumental—that captures all four verdicts? Or must I drop one, probably either the autism verdict or the ADHD verdict? Please also state what the resulting account predicts for prolonged grief disorder, added to DSM-5-TR in 2022, where bereaved people identify with the diagnosis and seek clinical framing for what was previously considered normal mourning.

Finally, please separate what is broadly settled in philosophy of psychiatry about looping effects from what remains genuinely contested among specialists—especially whether looping can ever genuinely constitute rather than corrupt a psychiatric kind—and do not present any contested view as if it were the consensus.
