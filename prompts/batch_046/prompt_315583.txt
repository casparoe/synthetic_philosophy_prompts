Write the joint statement that a hard-line Buddhist reductionist about anatta (call them the Aggregate Camp) and a conventionalist who thinks Buddhism needs a thin, designated self for karma and practice (the Prajnapti Camp) could both sign after a long adversarial collabration on personal identity.

Structure it like this:
- Numbered agreed claims: what anatta actually denies, whether Western psychological-continuity theory makes the same error as eternalism, and what meditative practice requires in terms of an observer
- A disagreements table: precise splits phrased so each side recognizes its own wording. Key ones: is "conventional self" a useful fiction or a necessary framework for moral agency? Does the meditator-problem show reductionism collapses, or does it just confuse conventional and ultimate levels?
- The cruxes. I think one is whether designating a self is always reification (svabhava) or can be purely nominal (prajnapti), and another is whether narrative continuity explains moral responsibility without smuggling in a substrate
- For each crux, one concrete case both sides agree bears on it — split-brain patients, total amnesia with retained habits, or deep sleep without experience — plus each camp's prediction about what that case shows

Don't include bibliographys or citations. Write it like they'd actually put their names to it, not like a textbook summary. Keep it tight and a bit in the weeds — I want formulations neither side would call slanted, and I especially want the meditator-problem and the svabhava/prajnapti distinction handled carefully, not glossed over.
