I'm writing a paper for my moral philosophy seminar on whether moral revolutions happen because people are convinced by arguments, because interests shift, or because honor codes change. My advisor Dr. K has given verdicts on a bunch of historical cases in her lectures, and I'm trying to figure out her implicit framework. So far she said: British abolition of the slave trade in 1807 was mostly industrial capital wanting free labor markets, with Wilberforce's moral rhetoric being window dressing; the end of dueling in France was the state monopolizing violence and aristocratic honor losing legal backing, not liberal moralists; foot-binding ending in China was nationalist elites reframing bound feet as national shame to Western eyes, with feminist arguments coming too late to matter; Britain ending public hangings in 1868 was middle-class taxpayers and order concerns, not compassion; and the collapse of debtor's prison in the US was banking elites needing predictable credit, with humanitarian language as cover.

I'm wondering about a new case she hasn't discussed: the near-total abandonment of arranged cousin-marriage (bint 'amm) among urban middle-class Palestinian families in Amman between roughly 1995 and 2015. There's no state ban, and dowry costs actually went up for exogamous marriages, so there's no obvious econonic win. But a prominent local religious scholar published a widely-read essay arguing it violates Islamic consent principles, and families started describing the practice as embarrassing and backward in intervews. Some sociologists also note it coincided with urban families competing for status with a new professional class.

Given her track record, what do you think Dr. K would say about this case? I'm less interested in the answer itself than in: what are the two or three plausible rules that fit her earlier verdicts equally well (like a hard Williams-style interest thesis, an Appiah-style honor thesis, and maybe a domain-pluralist version), what each rule predicts for the Amman case, which rule you'd bet she's running and why, and what further case would best tell them apart. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
