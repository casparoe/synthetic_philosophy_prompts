I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind working on self-representation and predictive processing, and I recently came across the following characterization of the consciousness-as-user-illusion view—associated with figures such as Daniel Dennett and Thomas Metzinger—in a seminar handout written by a cognitive scientist outside philosophy. The summary states: Consciousness is not an intrinsic property of neural tissue but a virtual interface that the brain constructs primarily for predictive control and social coordination. Because the brain never observes its own operations directly, it generates a phenomenal self-model—functionally analogous to a computer user dashboard—that represents the organism as a single, continuous observer situated behind the eyes. This model integrates cross-modal inputs through a proprietary temporal binding window, synthesizing them into a unified narrative present that is then projected backward as if it had always been continuous. The model is not epiphenomenal; it causally regulates attention and motor planning by broadcasting integrated predictions across a global workspace. Still, there is no Cartesian theater in which experiences are displayed to an inner witness, so asking where in the brain consciousness resides is a category mistake comparable to asking where the desktop environment is physically located inside the CPU. The representational contents—colors, pains, thoughts—are real as structured patterns of information, but the sense of being a unified, persisting self that owns them is a useful fiction lacking any corresponding neural substrate.

Something about this framing feels as though it conflates distinct theoretical commitments—perhaps blending Metzinger's phenomenal self-model with Dennett's denial of the Cartesian theater and importing machinery from global workspace or predictive processing theories—yet I am unsure which parts are faithful reconstructions and which are subtle distortions. Is this a fair summary of the user-illusion position? Please separate what is accurate to the core view, what counts as a common but potentially misleading simplification, and what defenders of the position would explicitly reject or qualify. I am particularly uncertain whether describing consciousness literally as a virtual interface for predictive control is part of the view or an imported metaphor; whether the temporal binding narrative is a standard component or an extraneous addition; whether the denial of a Cartesian theater amounts to denying any unified locus of experience whatsoever or only a specific metaphysical interpretation of unity; and whether attributing causal efficacy to the self-model in broadcasting predictions is consistent with the illusionist claim that there is no inner observer. Please answer in American English.
