Bad faith is just lying to yourself about being free — like when you say you had to stay in a dead-end job because of your rent or your parents, but really you chose to care about those things. He even argues that emotions like grief are choices we make to escape responsibility, and that freedom is absolute, meaning your social conditions or past trauma never excuse anything. That's why he calls existentialism a humanism, because recognizing this total responsibility is what makes us fully human. Oh, and bad faith also involves avoiding the Look of other people, since being seen makes you feel like an object."

I want to check how fair this is. Can you go sentence by sentence through the summary and say what a Sartre scholar would accept, what they'd wince at, and what's just wrong? Specifically, I'm wondering whether "absolute freedom" really ignores his idea of facticity and situation, whether bad faith is really about lying to yourself or about a deeper conflict between transcendence and facticity, and whether dragging the Look into bad faith is a mix-up. Also, does the conclusion that Sartre gives zero weight to social conditions actually follow, or is that an overreading?
