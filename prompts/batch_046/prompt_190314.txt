I'm drafting a contribution for a value-theory seminar and want a consistency check on my position before I present it. My commitments are these. First, I follow Moore in holding that both friendship and the appreciation of beautiful objects are supreme intrinsic goods, consisting in organic unities whose value exceeds that of any constituent mental states or relations taken separately. Second, I take both goods to be essentially relational and conscious-dependent: beauty requires a subject with appropriate perceptual and cognitive capacities; friendship requires reciprocal recognition between agents. Third, I reject Moore's non-naturalist metaethics and maintain that value-properties supervene on fully natural psychological, social and formal properties, though they are not reducible to them. Fourth, I hold that friendship's intrinsic value is partly constituted by its instrumental contribution to the moral development of the participants — that is, part of what makes the friendship valuable just is the value of the virtues it produces. Fifth, I maintain that beauty, unlike friendship, can be fully realised in non-conscious, abstract structures — for example, formal mathematical patterns or algorithmic designs — and that such structures possess intrinsic value regardless of whether any being actually perceives them.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear so? Rank the tensions by severity, indicate the cheapest fix for each, and say which single revision would resolve the most conflict overall.
