A colleague applies Émilie du Châtelet’s framework for happiness—treating sustained study, freedom from fundamental illusion, and the integration of reason with directed passion as its central pillars—to judge whether modern lives are genuinely happy. Her verdicts to date are these: a wealthy socialite who lives in luxurious idleness, avoids all intellectual labor, and holds no false beliefs about her comfort is not genuinely happy; an impoverished mathematician who spends her days in difficult original proof-work, accepts her modest conditions without self-deception, and directs her affections toward her subject is genuinely happy; a theologian who studies scripture with intense focus but is paralyzed by terror of false metaphysical beliefs about eternal punishment is not genuinely happy; and a climate scientist who works within institutional constraints but retains full autonomy over her specific research questions, holds accurate beliefs about her probabilistic results, and loves the inquiry itself is genuinely happy.

Now consider a new case. A philosopher lives in a fully immersive, AI-curated “cognitive sanctuary”: a sealed environment in which every text she reads, every interlocutor she debates, and every problem she solves is generated in real time to match precisely her abilities and interests. She experiences profound intellectual engagement, feels her choices are entirely self-directed, and directs her passions toward the ideas and “colleagues” she encounters. She has never been told of the fabrication, holds no false beliefs about an outside world (she never considers it), and suffers neither fear nor prejudice. The reasoning she performs is genuinely difficult and original; the only falsehood concerns the external source and social reality of the stimuli.

Predict my colleague’s verdict on the sanctuary philosopher. Reconstruct at least two distinct rules—each consistent with her four earlier judgments—that could explain her track record, state what each rule predicts about this case, give your best guess about which rule she is actually running with reasons, and specify one further case that would definitively distinguish those rules.
