Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps that have long argued past each other about Gödel's rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations and its philosophical implications for the nature of time. The first camp—call them the Temporal Anti-Realists—holds that the metric's allowance of closed timelike curves, together with the absence of any global time function, demonstrates that objective temporal passage is incompatible with general relativity and vindicates Gödel's own philosophical conclusion that time, in the sense of an objective flow, is unreal. The second camp—call them the Metric Formalists—accepts Gödel's solution as mathematically legitimate but denies that it licenses any metaphysical inference: they treat the lack of universal synchronization as a feature of that specific spacetime geometry, maintain that observational cosmology indicates our universe is not rotating and therefore the model is physically irrelevant to actual time, and argue that Gödel's anti-realist inference smuggles in an unstated premise linking determinism in the field equations to the ontology of change.

Produce a single constructive document that both sides could sign, structured as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: precise, shared descriptions of the metric's properties (the existence of closed timelike curves, the failure of global hyperbolicity, the deterministic evolution of initial data), a neutral statement of Gödel's philosophical position on the unreality of time, and explicit acknowledgment of where the two camps agree on the mathematics and disagree only on interpretation—phrased so that neither camp finds the formulation slanted. Second, a disagreements table that isolates the three genuine cruxes: (i) whether travel along a closed timelike curve undermines the A-theory of time or merely reveals a topological peculiarity compatible with local temporal sequence; (ii) whether the mere physical possibility of a rotating universe, even if ours is not one, is sufficient to show that temporal becoming is not a necessary feature of worlds governed by general relativity; and (iii) whether the determinism exhibited by Gödel's metric supports Gödel's anti-realist conclusion or is entirely neutral to it. For each crux, state each camp's position in wording that that camp would accept as accurate. Third, for each crux, specify one concrete case, thought experiment, or observational condition that both sides agree would bear on the issue—such as the predicted experiential report of a hypothetical observer traversing such a curve, current cosmological constraints on universal rotation, and the logical relation between deterministic field equations and the existence of an objective present—recording in advance what each camp predicts that evidence would show. Fourth, identify at least one disagreement that resolves into a verbal dispute rather than a substantive one, explaining the terminological divergence. Write formally, avoid bibliographies or reading lists, do not request citations, and paraphrase Gödel's philosophical remarks without placing them in quotation marks.
