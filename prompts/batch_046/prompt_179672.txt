If I learn a coin is weighted 70/30 toward heads, then flip it and get tails, I still think heads is more likely next time - the weight doesn't change just because of one flip, right? But if my friend tells me he flipped a fair coin and got heads, and then I find out he sometimes lies about flips, I don't think "oh so heads was actually more likely" - I just think he lied or I misheard. Same with weather: the app said 40% rain, it rained, but I don't go "so the real chance was high" - I figure I was in the unlucky 40%. But with quantum decay, if a physicist says there's literally 50/50 chance in the next minute and it doesn't happen, I still think 50/50 for the next minute. Meanwhile if my roommate says "I'd say there's a 50/50 shot my keys are in the drawer" and they're not thier, I think he was just wrong about the odds, not that the 50/50 "played out" on the other side.

So is there one principle behind when I treat the number as "real" and stick with it even after surprises, vs treating it like a guess that can be wrong? And what should I say about my grandma telling me there's "probaly a good chance, like 60%" I'll visit Sunday, and then I don't come? Do I say she misjudged the odds, or that the 60% just hit the 40%? I keep flip-flopping on that one. Is there a consistent view that saves all these gut feelings, or do I have to drop one?
