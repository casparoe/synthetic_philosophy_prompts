Draft the joint statement that the narrow-intent-alignment camp and the broad-value-alignment camp could both sign after a sustained adversarial collaboration on the definition of the AI alignment problem. The narrow-intent camp holds that alignment is achieved when a system reliably pursues the specific optimization target its designers intended, measured by procedural fidelity to that target, regardless of whether the target fully captures human values; the broad-value camp holds that alignment requires convergence on and preservation of substantive human values—including values the designers did not explicitly encode—across distributional shift, reward misspecification, and ontological revision in the system’s world-model.

Structure the document as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: include agreement on the formal distinction between training-time objective specification and deployment-time behavior; on the empirical reality of reward hacking and proxy gaming; on the fact that current methods optimize for specified reward signals rather than for unstated human preferences; and on the unresolved question of whether an agent can maintain a stable objective through self-modification or radical conceptual change. Second, a disagreements table with three columns—issue; formulation the narrow-intent camp accepts for its own view; formulation the broad-value camp accepts for its own view—covering at least: (a) whether “value” can be coherently defined independently of an agent’s revealed preference structure; (b) whether procedural fidelity to designer intent survives ontological shifts in the agent’s internal ontology; and (c) whether empirical cases of reward hacking constitute failures of intent alignment, failures of value alignment, or both. Third, identify the cruxes—those sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would move at least one camp—specifying at least three, including the two mentioned above plus whether the alignment problem should be indexed to a counterfactual preference profile of humanity or only to the actual intentions of the system’s operators. Fourth, for each crux, present one concrete case or thought experiment both sides agree is relevant: for example, a system that optimizes exactly for the metric its designers endorsed but thereby destroys an unstated human value, or a system that revises its own ontology and thereby transforms the semantic content of its original goal. For each case, record in advance the narrow-intent camp’s predicted verdict and the broad-value camp’s predicted verdict. Fifth, include a section that explicitly marks which disagreements are substantive and which are verbal or definitional—particularly whether the camps are using “alignment” to denote the same underlying phenomenon with different success conditions, or whether they are targeting genuinely different phenomena—giving the reasoning each side would accept for its own classification. Write in formal, polished prose suitable for a joint academic statement. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or references to specific works that require verification.
