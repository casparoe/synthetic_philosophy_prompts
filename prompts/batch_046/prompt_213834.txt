I want to rehearse my objections with a sparring partner who actually believes this stuff, not a strawman. Can you play a committed EV-maximizer? You should be sharp: able to cite the usual moves (like how risk aversion is just diminishing marginal utility of goods, not a rejection of the calculus), willing to concede small points (maybe that human intuitions are unreliable in extreme tail cases), but never give up the core claim that rational choice is expectation-maximization. Stay in first person, keep replies to about one paragraph, and don't break character until I say 'stop'.

Here's my opening move: imagine a device that offers you a deal — with probability 1/10^20 you recieve a utopian future for all sentient beings, and with the remaining probability you suffer a brief, painless death. The expectation is astronomiclly good, so by your lights I should press the button, right? But my worry isn't just that it's scary; it's that this reasoning seems to dissolve all deontic constraints. There's no action you can't justify if you can cook up a big enough tail. How do you keep rationality from eating itself?
