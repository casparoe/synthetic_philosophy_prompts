I'm taking a philosophy of physics course and we just started on Everett, and I think I've tied myself in knots about the Born rule problem. If the universal wavefunction evolves deterministically and every measurement outcome is realised in some branch, then it seems like talk of probabilities is literally false — either the event happens in your branch (probability 1) or it doesn't (probability 0). So the only place for |ψ|² to live is in our self-locating ignorance: it's a rational-credence rule telling us how to distribute belief across the branches we'll experience, not a claim about physical reality.

But then people talk about decision-theoretic derivations trying to justify that exact square-modulus weighting, and I can't see what those derivations are even for. If the weights are just bookkeeping for belief, why do they need a physical derivation from symmetry? And if they do need one, doesn't that mean the weights are real physical properties that make high-amplitude branches somehow "more actual" or more likely to be selected? I feel like im conflating two different issues — why we observe one outcome, and why the weights take that specific form — but I keep landing on the idea that in MWI the Born rule is redundant as physics, since unitary evolution already does all the causal work and the rest is just psychology. What's the false step? Is there a hidden presupposition I'm making about what counts as a physical law versus a principle of rationality?
