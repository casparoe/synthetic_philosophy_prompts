Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps in the philosophy of action and free will, both engaging with the concept of sphexishness—mechanical rigidity versus behavioral flexibility.

Camp A: The Hierarchical Sphexists. They maintain that all apparent flexibility in biological agents, including human problem-solving, decomposes into nested deterministic sub-routines. On their view, sphexishness is not an exception but the universal architecture of behavior; what looks like free adaptation is merely a deeper layer of rigid conditional branching.

Camp B: The Decoupled-Representation Anti-Sphexists. They grant that many behaviors are sphex-like but argue that genuine flexibility requires counterfactual decoupling: the capacity to represent a non-actual state and override a routine on that basis. They hold that this capacity is present in some animals and essential for human free will, marking a qualitative break from sphexishness.

Your deliverable should be structured as follows:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign—about the classic sphex wasp burrow-checking case, about the distinction between first-order rigidity (fixed action sequences) and second-order rigidity (fixed learning rules), about whether counterfactual representation is empirically detectable without circular interpretation, and about the relevance of this distinction to assessments of free will.

2. A clearly delineated section stating the exact disagreements in wording each camp would accept for its own position. Include at least: (i) whether hierarchical nesting eliminates sphexishness or merely disguises it; (ii) whether counterfactual decoupling can be operationalized in a non-circular way; (iii) whether free will requires the absence of sphexishness at every level of organization, or only at some specified level.

3. The cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, provide a concrete case or hypothetical piece of evidence both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp would predict about that case. Suggested cruxes include: the hypothetical polymorphic burrow-checker (a creature that revises its inspection routine only within a pre-fixed parameter space) and the interpretation of mirror-self-recognition in corvids through the lens of decoupled representation rather than associative conditioning.

4. A brief concluding assessment: identify any part of the dispute that appears purely verbal, specifying which terms are being used with different meanings, and state whether there is any agreed-upon observation or argument that both sides accept would actually resolve the residual disagreement.

Write neutrally; do not adjudicate. Ensure that formulations are neither vacuous nor slanted, and that each camp's predictions are recorded in vocabulary it would endorse. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
