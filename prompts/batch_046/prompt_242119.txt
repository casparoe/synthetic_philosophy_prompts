Construct a reusable decision procedure for a municipal data-protection officer who must approve or reject requests to release anonymised urban-mobility datasets to external researchers, operating under a 48-hour deadline without an in-house statistician. Ground the procedure in contextual integrity — the view that information is private not solely by its intrinsic content but by whether its flow respects the norms of the social context in which it was originally gathered — augmented by an injected constraint you must treat as binding: the inferential-boundary test, which deems a release impermissible if, within the proposed research environment, an observer could bridge the gap between the released records and a sensitive individual-level inference using only publicly available auxiliary data and standard statistical methods, even after direct identifiers have been removed.

The deliverable should be a practical protocol the officer can apply repeatedly. Include: (1) a three-step decision flowchart expressed in words; (2) four concrete test questions applicable to both the dataset and the research environment; (3) worked applications to three cases — one in which direct identifiers remain but the contextual flow is appropriate; one in which identifiers are stripped yet the inferential boundary is easily crossed; and one ambiguous case in which the procedure produces an unwelcome refusal; (4) explicit markers of where irreducible human judgment is unavoidable and where the procedure is silent; (5) a brief audit sign-off paragraph suitable for recording the rationale.

Present the answer in British English, using the following sections in this exact order: The operational principle (about 100 words); The decision procedure (about 300 words, with the flowchart and test questions); Worked cases (about 350 words); Limits and judgment calls (about 150 words); Audit sign-off template (about 80 words). Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
