I have been working through Jonathan Dancy’s particularist claim that moral reasons are holistic: the same feature—say, that an action breaks a promise—can function as a reason in one case, be silenced in another, and even be reversed by surrounding features, operating variously as what Dancy calls an enabler, a disabler, or an intensifier. This holism seems to pose a direct challenge to alignment strategies that encode moral judgment through invariant weights, such as constitutional AI rules or deontic logic filters that treat “causing unnecessary harm” as carrying stable negative valence regardless of context. Given this, does particularism entail that any architecture relying on generalizable moral heuristics is not merely practically inadequate but conceptually incoherent? Or can a particularist consistently allow for defeasible default weights, and if so, what would a genuinely particularist framework for artificial moral reasoning have to look like—one that avoids both rigid rule-following and an implausible pure case-based intuitionism?
