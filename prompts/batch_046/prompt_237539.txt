As a retired physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mind and personal identity, I want an audit of whether my current commitments hang together. Here is my position. (A) I accept Parfit’s teletransporter thought experiment: if my body is scanned and destroyed, and a psychologically continuous replica is reconstructed on Mars with the right causal history—preserved memories, intentions, and personality—then I survive as that replica, and physical continuity of my original brain is unnecessary. (B) I hold that if the scanner malfunctions and produces two psychologically identical replicas, one on Mars and one on Venus, then neither is strictly identical to me; branching undermines the uniqueness that personal identity requires. (C) I am a strict physicalist: all mental facts supervene on physical facts, and there is no immaterial soul or further ego-fact that could settle identity. (D) I also maintain that if, contrary to physics, a perfect atom-for-atom duplicate of my brain and body were spontaneously assembled in an adjacent room, that being would be me—since there is no non-physical fact to distinguish us. (E) I accept the quantum no-cloning theorem, so I regard perfect simultaneous physical duplication as physically impossible, but I treat (D) as a counterfactual claim about identity conditions rather than a physical prediction.

Please examine these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. For each serious tension, specify the least costly revision and what it sacrifices. Then say whether a single revision could resolve the most conflicts, and state what my cleaned-up view predicts for an unconsidered case: suppose my brain is gradually replaced neuron-by-neuron with functional synthetic equivalents that preserve psychological continuity, but during the process a backup copy of my complete neural state is stored—once the replacement is finished, does the stored backup have any legitimate claim to being me?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then provide the reasoning with no preamble and no restating of the question. Write in American English.
