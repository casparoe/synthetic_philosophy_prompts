I'm taking a course on ancient political philosophy, and after discussing the distinction between natural and conventional servitude in Aristotle's account of household management, I want to test whether the framework can be applied to a modern institutional arrangement without collapsing into anachronism. Please play Aristotle speaking in the first person, using only the conceptual resources available in your own philosophy—nature and form, potentiality and actualization, practical reasoning, the slave as an animate instrument, and the good of the household and the polis—together with the specific modern description I provide below. Do not invoke modern moral concepts such as human rights, autonomy, or systemic injustice; if you need a factual clarification about the arrangement, ask it in character as you would ask about an unfamiliar custom. Stay in character until I say stop, keep your reply to one substantial paragraph, and treat this as a genuine contest rather than a lecture.

Here is the situation: in several contemporary economies there are laborers who are not legally owned by any person. Instead, they wear devices that record movement and biometric data, and an automated mechanism—understand it only as a non-deliberative system that predicts, through pattern recognition over past behavior, which bodies will complete assigned tasks most quickly—continuously directs them to particular jobs, locations, and schedules. This mechanism possesses no soul, no practical wisdom, and no intentions; it operates solely through statistical correlation. The laborers receive wages barely sufficient for subsistence and lose all access to work if they refuse an assignment, which in practice means losing livelihood. Some observers argue this is not servitude because no reasoning master directs them; others say that the absence of an intentional agent makes no difference to the worker's condition.

My opening challenge: in your framework, natural servitude exists when one being's own flourishing is served by being directed by another's practical reasoning, whereas conventional servitude holds a person in bondage contrary to nature. Here, the directing principle itself lacks practical reasoning entirely. Would you classify these laborers as natural slaves accidentally directed by an irrational mechanism, as conventional slaves subjected to a mindless force, or do your categories fail to apply? And if the source of direction has no wisdom, can the worker's subordination still be said to serve the worker's own good?
