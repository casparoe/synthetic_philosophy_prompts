For a 75-minute graduate seminar on embedded agency with twelve participants and no assumed background in formal decision theory, design a deterministic, runnable role-play simulation that forces the group to experience the collapse of the agent-environment boundary before anyone names the concept. The participants should come away feeling why an agent physically implemented inside the world it reasons about cannot occupy the “view from nowhere” presupposed by standard Bayesian decision frameworks—specifically, that any model it constructs of its surroundings must contain a model of itself constructing that model, without access to its own source code or an external observational position. Use a real but minor shared stake, such as distributing an actual snack budget among the twelve, so that the collective rule they design is immediately applied to themselves. Specify the physical layout and hidden role cards (each participant receives a private constraint linking their evaluation of fairness to one other participant’s hidden preference, unknown to all); the round structure with transparent deterministic aggregation rules that include each participant’s own prior commitment as an explicit input, making self-reference unavoidable by round three; the precise moment when participants attempt to “step outside” to audit the rule and discover they are already inside its causal loop; the debrief that maps each round onto a feature of embedded agency—boundary dissolution, recursive self-modeling, causal feedback, and the absence of an epistemic outside—together with the misconceptions that predictably arise during play (treating the facilitator as an external observer, assuming hidden variables can be revealed by collective introspection, conflating self-reference with ordinary empirical uncertainty) and the point in the sequence where each should be interrupted; a brief check for understanding that demonstrates whether the group has grasped that the problem is structural rather than informational; an explicit statement of what must be left out at this level and said so; and an honest note on which feature of embedded agency—such as the computational intractability of full logical self-modeling or the specific dynamics of logical induction—this activity necessarily fails to reproduce and that you must supply verbally. Keep all rules visible and deterministic, do not ask participants to read texts during the session, and do not include any bibliography, citation, or reading list.
