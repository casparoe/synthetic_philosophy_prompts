Next week I'm giving a seminar paper defending the idea that hedonism fails because pleasure isn't one kind of thing at all — it's at least three, and one of them isn't even affective. The classic heterogeniety problem says sensory pleasure and intellectual pleasure feel diferent, but I want to push further: there's affective reward (the dopamine-linked stuff), cognitive satisfaction (the 'aha' feeling when a belief coheres), and a third thing I'm calling 'formal resonance' — the state you get when the structure of a mental representation maps isomorphically onto the structure of its object. Like when you understand a proof and the logical form feels 'right' — not pleasant, not satisfying exactly, but resonant. I think hedonists misclassify this as a kind of pleasure because it accompanies good thinking, but it has no hedonic tone.

The audience is a philosophy of mind / aesthetics seminar. There are two phenomenologists who think introspection can settle this, one cognitive neuroscientist who works on reward prediction error, and a few grad students who think talk about heterogeniety is just a language game.
