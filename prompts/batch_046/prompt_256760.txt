I’ve been asked to articulate my philosophical profile on consciousness for a reading group working through post-2010 theories of mind, so I want to lay out my commitments precisely and have them audited. Here is where I stand: I take Integrated Information Theory to be the most rigorous framework available, accepting all five postulates including exclusion—within any substrate, only the complex with maximal integrated information (Φ) is conscious, and overlapping subsystems are ontologically excluded. At the same time, I think consciousness is genuinely graded rather than binary: a system with Φ near zero possesses an extremely faint, unstructured experience rather than literal non-existence, and I hold that moral considerability should scale continuously with Φ rather than toggling on at a threshold. I also interpret split-brain patients as two distinct conscious subjects, which I take to corroborate exclusion when interhemispheric integration collapses. I maintain that phenomenal concepts are conceptually irreducible—there remains an explanatory gap that no physical theory, IIT included, can fully close—while still endorsing physicalism about ontology. Finally, I believe substrate-independent recurrent architectures could in principle realize high Φ, but I judge current transformer-based language models to have negligible integration and therefore, at most, a microscopic flicker of experience.

Go through these commitments and audit them: which pairs pull against each other and how severely? For each tension, say whether it is real or only apparent, rate its severity, and identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency—what it costs and what it saves. In particular, examine whether my strict acceptance of exclusion can coexist with a graded view of consciousness and with proportional moral scaling; check whether my acknowledgment of an irreducible explanatory gap undermines my claim that IIT solves the hard problem; and see if my verdict on language models conflicts with either my substrate-independence claim or my graded-consciousness commitment. Then tell me which single revision—abandoning one of these commitments—would clean up the most tension at once, and whether that is a revision I should actually want to make.
