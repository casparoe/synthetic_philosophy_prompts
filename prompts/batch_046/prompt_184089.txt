I've been trying to stabilize my view on semantic externalism for a philosophy-of-mind reading-group write-up and want a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is where I have landed: (1) I take Putnam's Twin Earth argument to establish that natural-kind terms like 'water' are rigidly fixed by actual causal interaction with H2O, not by internal psychological state, so mental duplicates on Twin Earth can think about different substances. (2) I think Burge's arthritis case generalizes this to socially deferred concepts—my mental token of 'arthritis' can mean arthritis even if I misuse it, because the linguistic community fixes the extension—leaving only phenomenal concepts and pure indexicals as genuinely narrow. (3) I nonetheless want phenomenal concepts to be not merely narrow but self-fixing: the reference of 'pain' is determined by direct acquaintance with the qualitative state itself, so my Twin Earth duplicate's phenomenal concept refers to the same kind of state (not a twin-kind), which makes phenomenal content invariant across environments. (4) I also hold that this narrow phenomenal residue is sufficient to rehabilitate a restricted methodological solipsism: psychology can individuate phenomenal states without knowing the subject's environment, even though beliefs and desires are widely individuated. (5) Finally, I think this package dissolves the explanatory gap—since phenomenal concepts are narrow and non-descriptive, there is no further fact about them that a physical theory needs to capture; the gap is purely epistemic.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what the least costly revision would be in each case. Order the tensions by severity and say, for each, which single commitment has to give. Separate what is settled in the literature from what is genuinely contested—don't present a contested reading as the consensus. If one revision cleans up the most problems, say which it is and what I lose by making it.
