At a seminar, two participants argued about Quine's stance on moral values. (1) The first claimed Quine is a moral non-cognitivist: moral judgments lack direct sensory checkpoints, and his naturalized epistemology grants cognitive status only to claims tied to empirical stimulation. (2) The second replied that Quine's holism lets moral sentences sit in the web of belief and be revised indirectly by experience, giving them the same weak cognitive standing as theoretical physics. (3) The first countered that this collapses morality into ontology: if Quine's criterion for ontological commitment ties existence to being the value of a bound variable, then his refusal to quantify over moral properties shows he excludes them from serious ontology entirely. (4) The second responded that this refusal is pragmatic, not metaphysical—moral values function as useful social fictions that need not be eliminated, but they need not be reified either, leaving them outside ontology without being cognitively empty. They turned to me for adjudication. Please go through the four moves, rating each from clearly mistaken to clearly right with brief justification, and state which party has the stronger overall reading of Quine on whether moral values carry any cognitive or ontological weight.
