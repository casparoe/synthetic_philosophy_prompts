In my philosophy of art seminar, two students—Maya and Leo—disagreed about whether an AI-generated image that recently won a small digital-art prize could be genuinely creative. Maya argued that the prompter performed hundreds of iterative adjustments—reweighting, inpainting, and compositional selections—amounting to a form of “curatorial creativity” functionally analogous to selective retention; she claimed this satisfies any non-mystical account of artistic production. Leo responded that what is missing is “counterfactual fragility”: the specific image never had to survive a chain of unrecoverable mistakes, emotional revisions, and lived adjustments that only a conscious agent undergoes. He said AI outputs are causally overdetermined—every pixel is statistically recoverable from the training distribution plus prompt—so there is no genuine surprise for the system, and without self-surprise there is only recombination, not creativity. Maya countered that human artists also operate within statistical distributions of style and technique, and that Leo was smuggling in “romantic essentialism” about consciousness rather than identifying a real structural difference. Leo denied essentialism and introduced “narrative integration”: the creative agent can give a truthful first-person account of why this line is here rather than there, grounded in experience. He insisted this is a condition on the production process, not on audience access, though he conceded he had not shown why that condition matters for the work’s value rather than merely for its authorship. Maya then pressed that if narrative integration is hidden from viewers, Leo’s view collapses into a theory of artistic authority rather than artistic value, and she asked whether anonymous works—cave paintings, for instance—would be disqualified. Leo admitted the pressure but maintained that authorship conditions can be objective even when unknown. Please adjudicate their exchange step by step: assess Maya’s curatorial-creativity claim, Leo’s counterfactual-fragility objection, Maya’s essentialism charge, Leo’s narrative-integration reply, and Maya’s anonymity challenge. For each step, say who is right about what, note any equivocation or missing premise, and state which party is more right overall.
