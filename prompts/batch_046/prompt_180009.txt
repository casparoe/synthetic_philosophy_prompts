Consider this reasoning about hinge propositions in the spirit of Wittgenstein’s On Certainty. (1) There are hinge propositions—such as that the earth has existed for a very long time, or that I have hands—which do not operate as ordinary empirical hypotheses but as the fixed framework within which particular doubts and inquiries become possible. (2) Because they are not rationally revisable—any attempt to doubt them already presupposes them—they enjoy a distinctive epistemic status: they are known in a manner that is immune to defeat by counterevidence. (3) Particular empirical beliefs, for instance that a specific fossil predates a specific civilization, are justified only against the background of these hinges standing fast; were the relevant hinge revoked, the particular belief would lose its warrant. (4) Knowledge is closed under entailment and justification-transmission: if S knows H, and E is justified only through H, then S knows E, and the epistemic properties of H are inherited by E. (5) Therefore every particular empirical belief that rests on a hinge inherits that hinge’s immunity to rational revision. (6) But particular empirical beliefs are plainly revisable: I ought to abandon my belief about the fossil’s age if I discover a dating error. (7) So we face a trilemma: either hinges are not knowledge after all and Wittgenstein’s special status for them collapses; or closure and transmission fail in a way that severs particular beliefs from their supporting hinges; or empirical beliefs are in fact dogmatically unrevisable, which is absurd. I am inclined to preserve (1) and (2) and to block the inference to (5), but I want to know precisely which step is weakest, what it costs to reject it—whether we must deny closure for hinges, treat hinges as non-propositional, reconceive the dependency relation, or accept some other revision—and whether the standard responses actually preserve the intuition that hinges are epistemically special without rendering all empirical inquiry dogmatic. Where does the argument go wrong?
