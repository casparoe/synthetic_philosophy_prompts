Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, rhyme scheme ABA ABA ABA ABA ABA ABAA, with two repeating refrains—on the problem of path dependence, taking up the question of whether historical outcomes are genuinely locked in or whether the same results would have arrived anyway through different routes. Design the poem so that its formal repetition enacts the mechanism it describes. Let the first refrain (A1) declare the fixity of the path—for instance, "Once the switch is thrown, the track holds"—and let the second refrain (A2) voice the counterfactual challenge—for instance, "Rewind the tape, and watch it break." Over the course of the five tercets, allow both refrains to recur with slight variations in punctuation or wording so that their meaning shifts: from deterministic inevitability, to the economic idea of increasing returns, to the philosophical distinction between a local attractor (a stable outcome sustained by the high cost of exit) and a global attractor (an outcome that convergence would have reached regardless of initial conditions).

Structure the conceptual movement in three stages across the body of the poem. In the first stage, present the naive linear model: history as a replaceable chain of efficient causes. In the second stage, introduce self-reinforcing mechanisms—standardization, reactive sequences, lock-in—using concrete illustrations such as the QWERTY layout or the evolutionary thought experiment of replaying life's tape, but treat them only as conceptual examples, not as invitations to citation. In the third stage, executed in the final quatrain where both refrains appear together, introduce the niche distinction between weak path dependence (where initial conditions matter but outcomes may still converge) and strong path dependence (where initial conditions permanently determine the trajectory). The turn of the poem should occur here: the refrain about the track holding should come to mean not metaphysical destiny but a basin of attraction with steep walls, while the refrain about rewinding should come to ask not whether the past could have been different, but whether an alternative path would have climbed into the same basin.

The poem should be philosophically precise enough that a specialist in philosophy of history or science would recognize the distinction between reactive sequences and self-reinforcing sequences, and would see that contingency (sensitivity to initial conditions) has been separated from convergence (counterfactual robustness). Do not include footnotes, bibliographies, or citations. Write in roughly iambic pentameter, but allow strategic metrical substitutions—for example, a trochaic opening in the line that introduces exit costs—to signal conceptual shifts. The language should be formal, the imagery concrete, and the rhyme scheme exact.
