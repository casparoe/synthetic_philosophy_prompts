As a retired physicist only recently turned to metaphysics, I have tried to assemble a coherent stance on modality, but I suspect my scientific habits may have led me into inconsistencies that I cannot yet see. Here is my package:

(1) I accept concrete modal realism: every possible world is a real, spatiotemporally isolated universe with exactly the same ontological status as ours, and nothing makes one world "actual" except that I inhabit it.

(2) I therefore treat "actual" as purely indexical, on a par with "here" or "now"—it picks out my world without conferring metaphysical privilege.

(3) I interpret scientific counterfactual practice—climate projections with altered forcings, cosmological models with different vacuum energies, and the like—as literally tracking genuinely existing worlds that differ in specific parameter values; the restriction to "nearby" parameter ranges is pragmatic, not metaphysically grounded.

(4) I read Everettian quantum branching as an internal dynamical feature of a single world, not as a mechanism that generates new Lewis-style worlds.

(5) I reject modal intuition as unreliable on evolutionary grounds—our cognition was never selected for distant modal assessment—yet I think modal knowledge is available through the mathematical extrapolation of physical law.

(6) I deny that there is any mind-independent, non-pragmatic relation of "closeness" or "similarity" between worlds.

My question is whether these six commitments hang together, or whether hidden incompatibilities remain. Is there a genuine tension between treating all worlds as ontologically on a par (1, 2) and treating counterfactual science as tracking a specially relevant subset of parameter-variation worlds (3, 6)? Does my internalist reading of Everett branching (4) conflict with the claim that every concrete possibility is already realized in some world (1)? And is the pairing of evolutionary skepticism about intuition (5) with confidence in law-based modal knowledge (3, 5) only apparently consistent?

Please audit the set rather than lecture me generally. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, specify for each which commitment must give and what the cheapest revision costs. Identify at least one pair that looks tense but dissolves on inspection, explaining why. I want to know which single revision would clean up the most problems with the least damage to the scientific outlook I am trying to preserve.
