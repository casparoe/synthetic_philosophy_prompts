If I introspect that my knee hurts right now, I buy it totally. If I introspect that I'm annoyed with my roommate, I mostly buy that too, though I can be wrong about the exact shade of it. But if I introspect that I'm annoyed *because* he left dishes in the sink, I think that's probably post-hoc storytelling — I don't actually trust that causal story. Same deal with why I donated to some charity: I say it's empathy, but I secretly think I'm just perfoming virtue for myself.

Now here's the weird part. If I introspect that I like chocolate over vanilla with no reason I can articulate, I still think that preference-report is basically honest. But if I introspect that my political belief is rational rather than tribal, I think I'm almost certainly fooling myself, even though both feel like "looking inward" to me. So there's tension: why is one reason-less report okay and the other self-deceptive?

Also, I think introspecting my own mental fatigue is pretty good — I know when I'm too tired to think straight. But introspecting whether I'm currently being logical in an argument? Terrible idea, I never catch my own fallicies.

So what's the actual principle? Is it about content vs. cause? About transparency? Or is there no neat line and I just have to drop one of these?
