If generational labels such as Gen Z or Baby Boomers are merely conventional partitions of continuous demographic flow—lacking the kind of natural-kind structure that would ground essential properties—then cohort-specific behavioral patterns ought to dissolve entirely into age and period effects once the age-period-cohort identification problem is adequately resolved. Recent hierarchical Bayesian APC specifications, however, appear to recover robust, stable cohort parameters even under weak identifying constraints. Given these statistical regularities, must we concede that generations possess a non-constructed modal essence that causally explains their divergence? Or can the persistence of these parameters be reconciled with a purely constructivist ontology of cohorts without appealing to hidden demographic essences?
