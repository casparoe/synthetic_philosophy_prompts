I'm currently in an advanced metaphysics seminar, and I've come to defend a particular version of modal realism—roughly, the view that possible worlds are concrete, spatiotemporally isolated entities with exactly the same ontological status as the actual world, so that modal claims are literally quantifications over these worlds. I think the standard objections are answerable: the incredulous stare is not an argument, worries about epistemic access conflate knowing that a world exists with knowing its internal details, and the indexical analysis of "actual" actually strengthens the view by explaining our preferential concern for this world without importing a metaphysical distinction between actual and merely possible existence. But I also hold that this ontology is only defensible if paired with what I would call a causal-analogical epistemology of modality—namely, that our knowledge of other worlds is not purely a priori but derives from recognizing structural and causal patterns in the actual world that are genuinely replicated elsewhere, so that modal reasoning is a form of analogical extrapolation rather than intuition. I want an honest map of what would count against this combined position. Which specific intuitions, which formal results in modal logic or ontology, and which developments in other areas—say, in physics or the philosophy of science—would be decisive defeaters rather than merely awkward? Which potential counterexamples or theoretical conflicts would a committed modal realist concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss as misunderstandings of the view? I would especially like you to rank these defeaters from fatal to merely inconvenient, and to assess whether qualifying modal realism with this analogical epistemology risks insulating the view into unfalsifiability—whether it has been made so flexible that no observation could threaten it, and whether that insulation is itself a philosophical problem for a position of this kind. What should actually move me, and what can I safely set aside?
