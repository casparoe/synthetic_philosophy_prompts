Write a short research proposal—approximately 1,000 to 1,200 words, suitable for a workshop pitch or an independent-study prospectus—on a problem at the intersection of forecasting methodology and the metaphysics of institutional continuity. The precise question is this: when we apply the outside view to long-range institutional forecasting, particularly in estimating the transformative trajectory or remaining coherence of large-scale political entities, how should we construct reference classes so that they avoid both excessive breadth (which dilutes predictive signal by grouping incommensurable organizational forms) and excessive narrowness (which invites overfitting and post-hoc selection)?

The existing positions in forecasting practice and historical sociology tend to divide between broad base-rate aggregation across all recorded polities and fine-grained analogism that matches on specific constitutional variables. Your proposal should argue that neither approach resolves the underlying issue, because both treat reference-class selection as an empirical problem of similarity-matching rather than as a normative and metaphysical problem about what kind of institutional continuity is predictive.

Your proposed approach should frame reference-class selection as a question about the conditions for institutional identity over time: does predictive continuity depend on the persistence of formal constitutional structures, on the stability of functional economic and coercive roles, or on some hybrid ontology of regime type? Explain why this philosophical reframing allows progress where empirical calibration stalls, specify what your deliverable would be—perhaps a taxonomy of institutional-continuity conditions paired with a decision procedure for forecasters—and state clearly what would count as failure (for instance, if the taxonomy collapses into either pure structuralism or pure functionalism).

The proposal should state the question precisely, explain why current answers leave it open, defend this particular angle over more standard empirical adjustments, describe what a useful result looks like and what its absence would imply, and demonstrate that the scope is realistic for a single semester or a three-month independent project. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or formal citations; you may refer to debates and positions in general terms and should note that citations will be added in revision. Avoid appeals to specific present-day states by name, and ensure that the framing is precise enough that the work could be pursued without access to external databases or real-time information.
