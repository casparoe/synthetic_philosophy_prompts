I'm a biostatistician who recently had to revise our clinical team's beliefs about an experimental cardiovascular therapy after a phase-III trial returned ambiguous endpoint data: some biomarkers improved, others showed no change, and the composite efficacy score was inconclusive. In trying to formalize how we should update our beliefs across multiple partially correlated outcomes, I constructed what looks like a reductio of Jeffrey conditionalization, but I cannot locate the exact leak, and I need to know what it costs to plug it.

(1) Jeffrey's rule states that when evidence does not determine which cell E_i of a partition is actual, but only shifts our credences over the cells to new weights q_i, then for any proposition B we should update by P'(B) = Σ q_i P(B | E_i), leaving all intra-cell conditional probabilities fixed.

(2) Any precise learning event can be redescribed relative to some partition. Even observing that a p-value equals 0.03 can be framed not as accepting the proposition p < 0.05, but as shifting credence over a partition of underlying true effect sizes.

(3) So Jeffrey conditioning applies not merely to genuinely ambiguous perceptual or testimonial evidence, but to all evidence whatsoever, provided we select the appropriate partition relative to which the learning event is merely a redistribution of weights.

(4) If Jeffrey conditioning applies universally, then standard Bayesian conditioning is simply the degenerate special case in which the partition is {L, not-L} and q_L = 1.

(5) This collapses the structural distinction between certain learning and uncertain learning into a mere difference in which partition we have happened to choose and how spread out the q_i weights are.

(6) Yet the distinction is epistemically real: observing an unambiguous genotype match differs in kind from viewing a blurry security image that raises the match probability to 0.7, and our deliberation about the trial data turned precisely on whether our endpoint ambiguity was of the first kind or the second.

(7) Moreover, consider nested partitions—say, a coarse partition by pharmacological mechanism and a fine partition by dosage level within each mechanism. If we apply Jeffrey updates first to the coarse partition, then within each cell to the fine partition, and compare this with applying them in the reverse order, the resulting posteriors differ unless the partitions are probabilistically independent, violating path-independence for rational belief revision.

(8) Therefore Jeffrey conditioning either erases the meaningful boundary between certain and uncertain evidence, or, when applied rigorously with nested partitions, yields order-dependent beliefs; both results are unacceptable, so the rule should be rejected in favor of restricting all updating to standard conditioning on a single fixed ontology of propositions.

Where does this argument go wrong, if anywhere? I would strongly prefer to preserve (1) and (6), since Jeffrey's rule seems exactly right for the ambiguous multi-endpoint data I faced, and the certain/uncertain distinction guided our actual clinical deliberation. If I must surrender something, I want to know whether the weakest link is the universal applicability claim in (3), the path-independence complaint in (7), or the collapse claim in (5)—and what giving up each one commits me to about how rational agents should treat trial data with several interdependent, partially observed outcomes.
