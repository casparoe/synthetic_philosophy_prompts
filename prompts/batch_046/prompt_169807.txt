Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaberation between two camps on competence without comprehension and the Darwin/Turing inversions.

Camp A — the Inversionists — think Darwin's move from Paley-style design to blind natural selection is a real inversion: you get adaptive competence with literally no comprehension inside the selecting mechanism itself. They also read Turing's imitation game as a parallel inversion of the Cartesian cogito — behavioral competence without any internal understanding. For them these dissolve old puzzles rather than just relocate them.

Camp B — the Smuggled-Comprehension crowd — agree the framing changed but insist comprehension is never actually absent, just displaced: onto the evolutionary theorist's representational model, onto the human interogator in Turing's setup, or onto the selection environment carrying implicit information. They think "competence without comprehension" misdescribes where the understanding actually sits.

Write one document both sides could put their names to. Use this format:

1. Numbered agreed claims — substantive, not fluff. Cover the formal structure of selection, what the imitation game actually measures, and which traditional arguments are genuinely undermined regardless of interpretation. Don't ask for a bibliography or reading list.

2. Disagreements table with two columns: "Inversionist wording" and "Smuggled-Comprehension wording". Each row states the same dispute in phrasing each camp accepts for itself. Make the residual disagreement exact; don't let either side become a strawman.

3. Cruxes — the sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one camp. For instance: is the absence of a central representational model in evolutionary adaptation structurally the same kind of absence as the lack of a semantic model in a system that passes the imitation game? Or is the interogator's comprehension part of the system being tested, or an external measurement artifact?

4. Shared tests and predictions — for each crux, name an argument, thought experiment, or empirical case both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp predicts. Don't request citations; just lay out what result would damage which view.

Also flag where any part of the dispute might be verbal rather than substantive, and say why. Keep it in the weeds so nobody can retreat to slogans. A typo or two is totally fine.
