In our seminar on Nietzsches essay about monumental, antiquarian and critical history, people keep throwing around the line that history must serve life, not life serve history whenever someone defends tearing down a statue or, conversely, archiving every old receipt. They use it like a trump card to dismiss both critical demolition and antiquarian hoarding in the same breath. Before next week I want to map out exactly what that one-liner could be claiming. Could you seperate the readings for me? Like: the trivial tautology that useless study is useless; the instrumental reading where only monumental history counts; the vitalist claim that any historical consciousness weakening present action is toxic; the normative claim that life is the sole value; and maybe a hidden compatibilist version where antiquarian and critical work are okay only if they secretly feed monumental goals. For each say whether Nietzsche actually held it, whether its false or defensible, and crucially whether it really supports dismissing both preservation and demolition at once—since that sounds contradictory unless life is doing hidden normative work. Keep it as a printable menu of precisifications, not a single answer. No need for citations, just the conceptual map.
