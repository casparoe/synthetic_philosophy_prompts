Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on the problem of insect sentience under conditions of vast population scale and low individual credence. Camp One—the Scale-Weighted Expected-Value camp—holds that with estimates running into the quintillions of individual insects alive at any moment, even a credence of one in a thousand that a given insect is sentient generates an expected moral burden that dwarfs conventional farmed-animal concerns, so rational moral agents must treat insect welfare as a serious, action-guiding priority unless the credence can be driven substantially closer to zero. Camp Two—the Epistemic-Threshold Particularist camp—holds that moral patienthood is not a linear product of population size times subjective probability, that our current ignorance about insect phenomenology is structural rather than merely quantitative (we lack a validated mapping between invertebrate neural dynamics and valenced experience), and that multiplying an ungrounded low credence across astronomical numbers commits what they call a “scale fallacy”: an illegitimate transfer of individual-level uncertainty into aggregate-level obligation.

The deliverable should follow this format. First, a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the empirical magnitude of insect populations, the formal mechanics of expected-value reasoning and its recognized limits (e.g., aggregation problems in population ethics), the minimal conditions any mechanistic account of sentience must satisfy (integration of nociceptive signaling with goal-directed flexibility, absence of pure reflex-arc explanation), and the arguments both sides reject as non-starters (naïve anthropomorphism, pure behaviorism without neural detail, and appeals to intuitive disgust or charm). Second, a disagreements table with two columns—“As Camp One would phrase its own view” and “As Camp Two would phrase its own view”—stating precisely: (a) whether moral status scales linearly with N × p; (b) whether there exists a metaphysical or epistemic threshold below which expected-value multiplication should not drive moral action; (c) whether the absence of vertebrate-style centralized integration is best interpreted as an evidential gap or as a principled exclusion criterion; and (d) whether the burden of proof lies with those who would deny insects moral consideration or with those who would extend it. Each column should be worded so that only the respective camp could endorse it.

Third, specify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that resolving one in either direction would cause at least one camp to revise its position. These should include: (i) whether an empirically detectable, cross-phyla intermediate marker of affective integration can be defined that applies to insects without assuming mammalian neural homology; (ii) whether formal moral theory requires or permits an “aggregation cap” that blocks astronomical low-probability harms from dominating an agent’s obligations; and (iii) whether our current epistemic state regarding insects is best modeled as a diffuse, quantifiable low credence or as a categorical void. For each crux, state what both sides agree would count as a relevant test or argument, and record in advance each side’s predicted response—for example, to the discovery of a conserved neurobehavioral mechanism shared by insects and acknowledged sentient invertebrates but absent in plants and artificial systems, or to a formal proof that expected-value reasoning at planetary-scale populations entails practically incoherent demands.

Finally, indicate whether any part of the dispute is verbal—specifically, whether disagreements about “sentience,” “moral patienthood,” and “expected burden” dissolve upon clarification of terms, and if so, which part.

In your response, please say how confident you are in the framing of these two positions and single out the point at which you are least sure—particularly whether the characterization of Camp Two’s “scale fallacy” genuinely reflects an extant position in the literature or is instead an artificially constructed stance for the purpose of the collaboration.
