After a long argument with a friend about whether patterns exist independently of our explanatory practices, I have adopted a precise, somewhat underexplored variant of stance-dependence: design-stance primacy with functional equivalence classes. The claim is that a pattern is real exactly when it corresponds to a teleological or functional equivalence class—an organization of parts serving a specifiable end—such that both the physical-stance description (fundamental laws and symmetries) and the intentional-stance description (beliefs, desires, rational predictions) are derivative abstractions that presuppose this prior design-level carving. Without a functional specification of what counts as noise versus signal, there is no principled boundary for patternhood at all; physical measures alone cannot distinguish accidental correlation from organized function, and intentional attribution already smuggles in functional organization. This is stronger than standard instrumentalism: I hold that design-stance patterns carry the full ontological load, while attempts to reduce them to microphysical dynamics either circularly invoke functional notions or eliminate patternhood altogether.
