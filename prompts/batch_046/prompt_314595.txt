I run a workshop for a local rationalist meetup, usually 10-12 people, 90 minutes, mostly folks who've read some Chalmers or Dennett but haven't dug deep into the illusionist literature. I want an in-person activity, not a lecture, that makes them actually feel the refrigerator-light problem applied to phenomenal consciousness — the idea that "something it's like" might only seem present when we're checking for it, because the check itself creates the appearance. Design a structured game or role-play where participants make real reports and choices under rules that mirror this. Split them into pairs (Observer and Subject) across three rounds: in round one the Subject gets a stimulus and writes a phenomenal report only when an "Attention Light" is on; the Observer sees only the report, never the stimulus or the inner state, and they recieve cards describing what they can ask. In round two they do the same task but also fill out a functional "zombie script" describing processing without phenomenal language, then swap so everyone plays both roles. In round three the whole group gets all reports anonymized — phenomenal and functional mixed together — and has to vote on which ones contain a real intrinsic quality versus which are framing artifacts, then discover that every phenomenal report was tied to the light being on and that the functional reports are behaviorally identical. Include a non-cash payoff or scoring rule that rewards accurate sorting but punishes false positives. Give me a timeline, the debrief that maps each round onto parts of illusionism (the transparency of introspection, the misrepresentation of functional states, etc.), an honest note on which feature — probably the actual absence of qualitative feel — the activity can't reproduce and I have to supply verbally, and a heads-up on what will predictably go wrong the first time (I think people will confuse it with eliminative behaviorism). Also say what would change your mind that this design actually captures the idea: which participant reaction or failure mode would convince you the framing is wrong and needs to be rebuilt.
