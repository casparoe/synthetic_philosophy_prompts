Write a formal philosophical essay of approximately 800–1,000 words assessing whether Frankfurt-style counterexamples genuinely defeat the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP). Rather than recapitulating the standard Frankfurt scenario, construct and evaluate a hierarchical variant: imagine an agent embedded in a deterministic architecture containing two nested counterfactual monitors—one tracking first-order intentions, the other tracking second-order volitional endorsements—such that intervention is possible at either level but never actually occurs. In your analysis, rigorously distinguish robust alternative possibilities (the agent could have acted otherwise through her own causal powers) from thin alternative possibilities (the causal chain could have diverged at some upstream node without direct agential control). Reach and justify a specific, non-eclectic conclusion: either that PAP survives only in a qualified form requiring thin alternatives at the highest hierarchical level, or that moral responsibility is compatible with the complete absence of both robust and thin alternatives when hierarchical monitoring is present. Your argument must address the objection that nested determinism renders the distinction between actual and counterfactual sequences epistemically inaccessible to the agent, and must explain whether such inaccessibility undermines attributions of moral responsibility.
