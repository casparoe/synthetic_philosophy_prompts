Please give an intuitive, conceptually precise explanation of a distinction often blurred in Gettier discussions: the difference between lemma-based epistemic luck, where a false intermediate belief undermines knowledge despite yielding a true conclusion, and pathway-based epistemic luck, where every premise is true and the inference valid, yet the belief tracks reality only because an unrelated causal mechanism corrects for a systematic misdirection in the subject's evidence-gathering process. Can pathway-based luck arise without any false premises whatsoever? If so, does this show that the standard JTB response to Gettier—adding an anti-luck condition—was misdirected in targeting false lemmas rather than a deeper structural disconnect between the grounds that justify the belief and the facts that make it true? Work through what this implies for whether knowledge requires not merely justified true belief, but a specific kind of explanatory alignment between justification and truth.
