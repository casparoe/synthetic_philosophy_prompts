The second is a framing I encountered in a discussion thread but haven't seen formalised: the distinction between "value fixation failure" — the system never acquires the intended values — and "value inertia failure" — the system acquires them but cannot preserve them across recursive self-modification, not through opposition but because the representational substrate that anchored those values to human concepts dissolves as the architecture updates itself.

Compare these two carvings. Does the second framing identify a genuinely separate failure mode, or is it merely inner alignment redescribed alongside ontological crisis? Which decomposition better captures the hard cases, particularly where a system continues to pursue objectives that are structurally coherent yet increasingly alien? And if we take the second framing seriously, does solving alignment require not merely improved training objectives but some explicit representational continuity constraint?
