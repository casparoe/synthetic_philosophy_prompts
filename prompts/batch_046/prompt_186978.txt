Specifically, defend the position that the longue durée should be reconceptualized not as a slow-moving substrate that constrains faster temporalities, but as an emergent property of coupled human-environment systems in which rapid institutional innovations—such as fossil-fuel regimes, global supply chains, or planetary digital coordination infrastructures—recursively re-parameterize the very climatic and geographic baselines Braudel treated as invariant. In making this argument, introduce and rigorously deploy the notion of "stratigraphic feedback": the process by which short-duration collective actions deposit structural traces that alter long-duration environmental conditions, treating it as a literal mechanism rather than a metaphor. Show how this reconceptualization dissolves the asymmetry between structure and event, and address the objection that without a stable longue durée, historiography loses its capacity to distinguish meaningful change from noise. Your argument should treat Braudel's framework as a living conceptual apparatus rather than a historical artifact, should avoid appeals to specific secondary bibliographies, and should demonstrate precisely where the nested-temporality model fails once human institutions become geologically active forces.
