As a novelist, I have been tracking my own reasons for beliefs and artistic desires, and I want to know whether they are of the right kind. My verdicts are these. I believe my protagonist betrayed her friend because the narrative evidence and psychological consistency demand it: this feels correct. I believe the betrayal occurred because an unreliable-narrator twist will boost sales: this feels like the wrong kind of reason for belief, even if it is a reason to write it. I want readers to admire the protagonist because her actions genuinely warrant admiration: right kind. I want admiration because admired protagonists drive merchandise and sequels: wrong kind. I hold strong authorial credence that the fictional world contains no hidden magic, solely because the internal logic of the story excludes it: right kind. I hold that same credence because a realist framework makes my political themes sharper: wrong kind. I want to depict a mother abandoning her child because the plot requires it and the scene explores a real moral failure: I am unsure—part of me says this is object-based and legitimate, part says it is driven by my own need to process guilt over neglecting my family for my work, which feels like the wrong kind. Meanwhile, I believe democratic institutions are fragile because of historical patterns and current structural evidence: right kind. I believe the same proposition because holding it makes my fiction feel urgent: wrong kind. Is there a coherent principle—perhaps one that distinguishes reasons responsive to the object or internal logic of the attitude from reasons responsive to the practical effects of holding it—that captures these judgments? Please state the principle precisely, indicate which clause is motivated by which case, apply it to a scenario I have not listed (for example, whether I should revise my belief about the betrayal if a focus group says readers will reject it), and tell me honestly which of my verdicts, especially the ambiguous abandonment case, must be overturned if the principle is to remain simple. Please answer in American English.
