As a PhD student in metaphysics writing on the afterlife of British Idealism in early analytic philosophy, I keep returning to Bradley’s regress not as a museum piece but as a live constraint on any compositional account of propositional unity. Rather than read another historical summary, I want to practice pressing a real defender of the view with a live objection. Please take on the role of an unnamed, sharp contemporary proponent of Bradley’s argument—speaking always in the first person—who accepts that the regress undermines self-standing relational constituents but denies that this renders relations unreal. On your view, the regress reveals that the unity of a proposition can never be captured by any binary analysis—whether relational, identical, mental, or formal—and you defend a minimal but irreducible mode of connection you call “adhesion”: a direct, non-constituent binding between subject and predicate that is neither a further relating relation, nor a trope-theoretic compresence, nor a Fregean functional unsaturatedness, and that requires no appeal to mental synthesis or to modern semantic machinery. Stay in character across turns; concede only small logical or clarificatory points if forced; never break role to lecture me on the history of philosophy; and do not ask for citations, page numbers, texts, or reading lists.

My opening challenge is this. I grant you that binary relational analyses generate a vicious regress, and I grant that mental synthesis is unavailable to you as an explanation. But I propose two alternatives that seem to achieve propositional unity without either regress or mysticism. First, a Fregean account on which the copula is an unsaturated function that binds its arguments precisely without being an additional constituent. Second, a trope-theoretic view on which a particular and a universal are primitively compresent, with no need for a separate binder. If adhesion is genuinely distinct from both—neither functional unsaturatedness nor primitive compresence—then you owe me a positive characterization that shows what work it does. How does adhesion unify subject and predicate without being one more thing inside the proposition, and why should I prefer it to these two alternatives that avoid both Bradley’s regress and an unexplained mystery?
