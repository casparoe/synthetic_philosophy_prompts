hey, i'm trying to work out what i actually think about voting ethics by going off my gut on concrete setups, but the cases are pulling in different directions and i can't tell if there's a real principle hiding in there or if i'm just rationalizing after the fact.

here's where i land:

- in a two-way race where candidate A is genuinely awful and B is merely corrupt, i think voting for B is not just allowed but maybe obligatory if you're in a swing district where it could actually matter.
- but if i'm in a safe state where B will win by 20 points regardless, voting B feels more like an endorsement i dont want to make, and i'd rather vote third party or leave it blank, even though B is still the lesser evil.
- telling a friend "vote for B because he's the lesser evil" when that friend lives in the same safe state feels wrong, like i'm asking them to sign onto corruption for zero strategic gain.
- if there's a non-binding referendum on the same ballot about funding a local park, i think it's totally fine to vote yes on that and skip the race entirely, even though my blank ballot is technically in the same envelope as my yes vote.
- now here's the weird one: in a primary where i think the front-runner C is dangerous but likely to win, i feel okay voting for a longshot D who shares my values, but only if D has literally zero chance of winning the primary. if D starts polling within reach, i suddenly feel i should switch back to the least-bad viable candidate, even if i hate them. so my tolerance for "protest voting" collapses exactly when it might actually work.
- abstaining in a close general election because i dont want "blood on my hands" for B's inevitable bad policies strikes me as self-indulgent, but abstaining in that same election because i genuinely dont know who's less bad strikes me as reasonable.
- writing in a fictional candidate's name in a landslide feels harmless, but writing in that same name in a race that comes down to 30 votes feels like reckless performance art.

i think there's something about whether my vote is functioning as a pivot or just an echo, and whether the ballot is being used as a conditional threat versus pure expression, but i can't formalize it.

is there one principle that captures all of these verdicts? if not, which one should i drop? please state the principle precisely, say what case motivates each clause, and tell me what it predicts for a case i haven't listed: voting in a deliberitive election where your single vote gets pooled with 11 others and you know your group will split 6-5, but you dont know which side you're on. and be honest about which of my intuitions your account just can't save.
