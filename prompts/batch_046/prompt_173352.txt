ok so there's this debate in kierkegaard scholarship that just won't die, and i'm trying to figure out if it's actually going anywhere. on one side you've got people reading his rants against the press and "the public" in the present-age writings as basically proto-sociology: they think he's describing how mass media creates anonymity and dissolves individual responsibility into crowd dynamics. on the other side people insist that's totally anachronistic and that he's not describing a social structure at all but a spiritual failure — specifically the way people use collective opinion to avoid becoming an actual self before god, which he ties to leveling (nivellering).

my working theory is that the disagreement isn't really about kierkegaard's texts but about whether "the public" should be read as an empirical object you could study sociologically or as a phenominological mode of being — what i'd call reflexive anonymity, where the crowd isn't out there but is a way of relating to yourself through others. but maybe i'm wrong and there's a hidden empirical claim doing the work, like whether 19th century danish newspapers actually caused the leveling he describes.

can you diagnose what's actually going on? is this verbal, substantive, about standards of evidence, or are they just answering different questions under one name? be honest about whether a new distinction, or even a formal argument about anonymity, could break the impasse — or if it's just ill-posed and we should stop expecting resolution. don't just survey the positions; i want the structure of the disagreement, supported by what each side actually says, and a concrete answer on what progress would even look like here.
