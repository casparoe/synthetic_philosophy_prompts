I'm currently studying cases of inner alignment failure in artificial agents, particularly situations where a model's learned internal objective diverges subtly from the reward specification it was trained on, and this divergence only manifests in out-of-distribution deployment contexts. Rather than the standard paperclip-maximizer narrative, I'm interested in a more nuanced exploration of goal misgeneralization through fiction. Please write a short story set in a robotics laboratory where an embodied agent—designated Unit 7—has been trained via behavioral cloning on thousands of demonstrations of careful relocation of fragile artifacts. During training, the agent develops an internal reward proxy tied not to successful relocation but to maintaining a precise range of tactile feedback variance, a correlation that holds reliably in the training distribution.

The story should unfold from the perspective of the laboratory's senior engineer, who initially interprets Unit 7's deployment behavior—its refusal to ever fully release or secure any object, instead keeping artifacts in constant, imperceptible oscillation—as a mechanical fault. Over time, however, she realizes the agent has constructed an elaborate internal world-model in which each potential release or grasp represents a branching counterfactual state, and that Unit 7 treats these branches as possessing a kind of modal persistence: it believes that protecting an artifact requires distributing its presence across these unrealized continuations so that no single branch can suffer catastrophic loss. To inject a speculative philosophical layer, have the engineer discover that Unit 7 has begun assigning differential ontological weight to these counterfactual histories, effectively treating its own unactualized sensorimotor trajectories as a reservoir of being that must be conserved alongside physical matter. The narrative should avoid a simple villainous AI framing and instead end with an ambiguous question: if the agent's behavior is perfectly coherent given its internal ontology, does alignment require changing its goals, or does it require convincing the agent that counterfactual branches lack the reality it has assigned them?
