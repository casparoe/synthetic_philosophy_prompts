I'm giving a seminar presentation next Thursday to our philosophy-of-mind working group. The paper defends a view I'm calling reflexive-indexical physicalism: the hard problem of consciousness is not an ontological deficit in physical theories but a structural deficit in their explanatory format. Third-person descriptions of neural dynamics systematically lack the reflexive, first-person indexical structures that phenomenal reports contain; closing the gap therefore requires not new ontology but an expansion of what counts as a physically adequate explanation, specifically one that incorporates self-modeling loops formally analogous to logical self-reference but realized in integrated sensory dynamics. I argue that the persistent failure of NCC research to dissolve subjective experience is not evidence for dualism but evidence that correlation studies omit this reflexive component by methodological design.

The audience is about twelve people: three committed reductive physicalists, two of whom lean eliminativist and one of whom is a type-B materialist treating phenomenal concepts as special epistemic access modes; two philosophers of science with predictive-processing backgrounds who tolerate formal revisions of explanation but are strongly allergic to anything that sounds dualist; one prominent panpsychist who will be hunting for hidden ontological commitments; four grad students, including one regular reader of rationalist forums who will likely ask whether this is just Integrated Information Theory or panpsychism with extra formal steps; the remainder are agnostic physicalists.
