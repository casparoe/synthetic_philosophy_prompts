As a retired physicist who has only recently begun to study the philosophy of time in earnest, I have tried to articulate my own stance on the thermodynamic arrow and the direction of time, but I fear the pieces may not cohere. Here is my position, stated with the precision I would use in a technical note:

(1) I accept the B-theoretic block universe: temporal passage is not an objective feature of reality, and all temporal locations are equally real.
(2) I nevertheless treat the thermodynamic arrow as genuinely objective, grounded in the cosmological Past Hypothesis—the extraordinarily low-entropy boundary condition of the early universe.
(3) I hold that the psychological arrow—our asymmetry of memory, anticipation, and causal reasoning—is fully reducible to local entropy gradients within cognitive and environmental subsystems.
(4) I deny that causal asymmetry is primitive; it is nothing over and above the statistical asymmetry expressed by the second law.
(5) I am unmoved by Boltzmann-brain skepticism, not only because cosmological dynamics suppress such fluctuations, but because a mere momentary fluctuation lacking diachronic coherence could not genuinely possess beliefs, memories, or a first-person perspective.
(6) Influenced by recent work on decoherence, I now think entropy is not an intrinsic property of the universe as a whole, but is always defined relative to a particular coarse-graining and to an observer-dependent partition of microstates into macrostates.
(7) Consequently, I want to maintain that the global cosmological arrow and the local operational arrow can in principle diverge—say, in a contracting cosmos where local decoherence continues to run forward relative to embedded observers—without contradiction.

(c) Identify which apparent tensions dissolve upon closer inspection. (d) Is there a hidden circularity between (4) and (5), given that my suppression argument seems to presuppose causal structure? (e) Do (2) and (6) conflict, since an objective global boundary condition appears incompatible with the view that entropy is partition-relative? (f) Does my acceptance of (1) undermine (3), or is the reduction of mental time to local gradients fully compatible with eternalism?
