Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of rational choice under risk must satisfy if it aims to accommodate the Allais paradox without treating the independence axiom as merely an error of intuition, and if it also intends to serve as a normative guide rather than only a descriptive model. Specify the full list of requirements: the empirical choice patterns it must reproduce—including the common-ratio and common-consequence violations, but also the reversal effects observed in sequential or framed elicitation—the formal conditions it must either preserve or explicitly reject with justification, the distinctions it must maintain (such as between risk and ambiguity, between ex-ante evaluation and ex-post regret, and between procedural and substantive rationality), and the normative constraints it cannot violate in transparent dominance cases. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or argument that makes it a genuine criterion rather than a partisan prejudice, and identify any pair that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, evaluate expected utility theory, rank-dependent utility, and cumulative prospect theory against these criteria, keeping the weight on the specification rather than on the scorecard. If this framing wrongly assumes that descriptive fidelity and normative authority can be combined in one framework for risk preferences, or if the desiderata turn out to be incoherent, say so plainly instead of producing the list. Where your judgments about joint satisfiability or theory scoring are tentative or depend on contested readings, note that explicitly.
