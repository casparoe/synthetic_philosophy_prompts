As a novelist accustomed to thinking about narrative coherence and the boundaries within which human reasons remain intelligible, I have been observing a philosophical dispute about expected value fanaticism—the view that if an action carries even an infinitesimal probability of producing value on an astronomical scale, that remote modal tail should dominate all ordinary deliberation. For years, the exchange has followed an identical rhythm: one camp demonstrates that standard expected-utility arithmetic licenses precisely this dominance, while the other camp replies that accepting it renders practical reasoning absurd, invoking scenarios in which an agent must always chase a distant cosmic jackpot and abandon every proximate good. The mathematics is shared; the thought experiments are shared; no participant denies the arithmetic or the badness of suffering; yet neither camp has yielded ground. My working hypothesis is that this is not a hidden empirical disagreement, nor a formal error in either derivation, but a clash about whether rational decision procedures must preserve what I would call narrative continuity—the requirement that an agent’s reasons stay anchored to a causally bounded, experientially traversable story rather than dissolving into an unweighted aggregation across distant possible worlds. Is that diagnosis correct, or is there in fact a substantive disagreement concealed beneath the surface—perhaps about the admissibility of risk aversion in axiology, about how value aggregates across worlds, or about which cases count as legitimate data—disguised as an intuitive standoff? I want an honest structural diagnosis, supported by what each side actually asserts: do they differ about facts, about standards of evidence, about bedrock intuitions concerning proximity and naturalness, or are they answering different questions—one about formal rational choice, one about moral justification—under a single heading? And given that diagnosis, tell me whether anything—a conceptual distinction that isolates modal-tail reasoning from narrative coherence, a formal result on inter-world comparison, or a change in the framing of the problem—could genuinely move the debate, or whether perpetual impasse is the only rational expectation.
