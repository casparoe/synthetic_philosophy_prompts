I am serving as an external grader for an aesthetics exam in a course I have only recently begun teaching, and a colleague has asked me to assess one response without knowing my own interpretation of the material. The exam question was: "In his essay on taste, Hume distinguishes judgments of sentiment from judgments of reason. Explain this distinction, describe the qualifications he assigns to the 'true judge,' and assess whether this framework allows for genuine aesthetic disagreement or collapses into simple subjectivism."

The student wrote:
"According to Hume, beauty is not a quality that exists in objects independently of the mind, like shape or extension; rather, it is a particular kind of pleasure or displeasure that arises in the perceiver. This makes aesthetic judgments judgments of sentiment, not judgments of reason, because reason only establishes relations of ideas and matters of fact, neither of which can produce or justify a feeling. To prevent the view from reducing to the claim that all tastes are equally correct, Hume appeals to the true judge: someone with a delicate sentiment refined by practice and comparison, free from prejudice, and possessed of sound mental faculties. The responses of such qualified observers establish an intersubjective standard. In this respect, Hume's true judge functions essentially like Adam Smith's impartial spectator, applying a disinterested perspective to correct the distortions of ordinary viewers. Consequently, Hume is not a mere subjectivist; he believes that aesthetic judgments aim at a form of objectivity, in which being right about beauty is analogous to a calibrated instrument correctly registering an independent physical property. Disagreement remains possible and meaningful, but it is always explained by deficiencies in the observer's training or sensitivity rather than by any hidden feature of the artwork itself."

Please assign a grade and provide point-by-point comments justifying it. I am acting as an examiner grading another instructor's student work, so I should not signal my own philosophical view; I need specific identification of accurate claims, subtle conflations, and steps that do not follow, delivered section by section rather than as a general impression.
