As a journalist covering the philosophy of artificial cognition, I recently spoke with a cognitive scientist—I'll call her Dr. Vasquez—who applies the intentional stance with unusual rigor but rejects several of its conventional extensions. Her judgments so far are these: adult humans and tool-using crows genuinely hold beliefs and desires; a chess program evaluating positions holds instrumental beliefs about board states; a thermostat does not, being mechanically too simple for predictive utility to stabilize; a large language model generating prose lacks beliefs, since its outputs are statistical interpolations without a unified, world-guiding model; a brain-in-a-vat running a fully simulated sensory life does hold beliefs about its virtual environment; and an in-vitro synthetic neural cluster coupled to a robot body and trained through direct sensorimotor feedback also holds beliefs, despite having no evolutionary ancestry. I am trying to determine the implicit principle behind this pattern—whether it tracks predictive-explanatory power above some complexity threshold, the presence of an integrated internal representational architecture with aboutness, or some hybrid—because the verdicts she has given me seem jointly consistent with more than one rule.

The case she has not yet considered, and which I intend to put to her, is a mobile navigation robot built entirely on layered reactive control: there is no central map, no persistent internal representation of space or goals, only direct sensor-to-motor couplings arranged in suppression hierarchies. The machine navigates cluttered environments, seeks charging stations, and avoids damage with success rates matching model-based robots, yet circuit analysis confirms the absence of any integrated state corresponding to 'the charger is ahead'—the behavior emerges purely from parallel reflex loops. Would Dr. Vasquez attribute beliefs to this system? Depending on whether her criterion is the practical utility of treating it as an agent or the possession of an internal world-model, the answer should diverge. Predict her verdict, lay out the competing rules her earlier judgments leave open, say which one her ruling on this reactive robot would reveal, and identify what further case—perhaps an epiphenomenal representational system that constructs a rich internal world-model but never allows it to guide behavior—would finally settle which principle she is actually running.
