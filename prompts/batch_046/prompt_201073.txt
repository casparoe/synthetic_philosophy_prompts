Write a satirical internal memorandum of approximately 500 words, composed in the deadpan voice of a senior ethics auditor at the fictional consultancy Aggregative Welfare Solutions. The memo addresses an internal complaint that the firm’s flagship optimisation protocol—which computes net welfare across populations without assigning moral weight to individual boundaries—violates the philosophical objection known as the separateness of persons. Rather than conceding the point, the auditor invents and vigorously defends a bureaucratic mechanism called inter-personal amortisation accounts: each individual’s welfare is formally entered into a discrete ledger before the ledgers are summed, so that procedural separation is maintained even though the arithmetic aggregation is unchanged. The satire should expose the gap between this formalistic respect for boundaries and the substantive reality of utilitarian summation; the auditor should treat the ledger fiction as a genuine moral breakthrough, never breaking character. Include one invented quantitative scenario—such as a case in which severe deprivation for one person is offset by minor gains distributed across seven others—with the auditor explaining precisely how the separate ledgers preserve dignity despite the identical mathematical outcome. The target is the style of corporate ethics discourse that deploys procedural ornamentation to disguise substantive moral aggregation. Present the memorandum as continuous prose only: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; write it as a single flowing internal report, the way one might draft a formal letter.
