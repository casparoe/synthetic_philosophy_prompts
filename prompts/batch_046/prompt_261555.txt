Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps in philosophy of mind and cognitive science concerning real patterns and the three stances—physical, design, and intentional. The first camp, call them the Cross-Stance Compression Realists, holds that patterns identifiable from the design stance and the intentional stance are ontologically on a par with those tracked by the physical stance whenever they exhibit equivalent or greater predictive compression, counterfactual robustness, and intervention-resistance; they treat stance-selection as an epistemic choice among genuinely real, if level-relative, structures in the world rather than as a projection onto a single fundamental base. The second camp, call them the Fundamental-Pattern Exclusivists, grants that all three stances are indispensable predictive heuristics but insists that only patterns embedded in the fundamental physical description carry mind-independent reality; on their view, design-level and intentional-level patterns are real only in a derivative, interpreter-relative sense, and their apparent ontological weight is an artifact of our cognitive need for tractable models, not a feature of nature itself.

The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims—about what a pattern is, about the predictive success of each stance, about the formal irreducibility of intentional descriptions to physical ones in practice, and about the criterion of counterfactual robustness—followed by a disagreements table in which each camp's position is phrased in wording its own members would accept. Identify the cruxes: specifically, whether predictive-compression efficiency tracks ontology or merely cognitive economy; whether a synthetic system trained on ecological dynamics that supports precise design-stance algorithmic predictions and robust intentional-stance agent predictions—but for which the physical-stance microstate description is computationally intractable—counts as genuinely possessing algorithmic states and intentional properties, or only as being usefully modeled that way; and whether "intervention-resistance" is a stance-neutral property or one that dissolves when translated downward to physics. For each crux, specify a concrete case or thought-experiment both sides agree is relevant, record in advance what each camp predicts the case will show, and indicate whether settling that crux would cause either side to revise its view. Conclude by noting, if appropriate, whether any portion of the dispute is verbal—particularly around the term "real"—and if so, which part and why.
