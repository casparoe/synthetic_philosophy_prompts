I'm drafting a position paper for a comparative moral metaphysics seminar and want a consistency check on my commitments regarding karma and moral desert before I present them. My view is structured as follows. First, moral desert is fundamentally a normative fittingness relation: the experiential quality of an agent's future ought to correspond to the moral quality of their prior intentional actions, regardless of social utility or deterrence. Second, I interpret karma through the classical distinction between kamma—the intentional deed—and vipaka—its matured result—where vipaka is not administered by an external agency but is the intrinsic unfolding of the action's moral potential. Third, I accept anatta: there is no enduring substantial self or soul that persists through death and rebirth. Fourth, despite this, I maintain that for vipaka to count as genuinely deserved, rather than merely causally linked, the recipient must be the same moral agent who performed the original kamma; I account for this sameness through a purely functionalist-psychological continuity of mental states, with no non-physical substrate. Fifth, I hold that this continuity must preserve a form of implicit moral self-awareness, such that the recipient can register, however dimly, the present experience as the maturation of their own prior intention; without this, I think the fittingness relation collapses into impersonal cosmic bookkeeping. Sixth, I am a hard determinist: every mental state and action is fully determined by prior physical conditions and natural laws. Seventh, I grant that evolutionary debunking arguments undermine the reliability of cross-cultural intuitions about cosmic justice, yet I insist that karma can still be justified through pure practical reason alone, and I reject any reduction of karma to a psychological heuristic for social coordination.

Please audit this package for joint tenability. Which pairs of commitments pull against each other in ways that are not immediately obvious, and which tensions dissolve once the underlying distinctions are made clear? I am particularly concerned with whether my functionalist continuity can bear the weight of implicit moral self-awareness across death without either reintroducing a substantial self or violating my determinism and physicalism, and with whether hard determinism erodes the normative character of the fittingness relation I take as central. Rank the tensions by severity, and for each specify what must be revised and what that revision costs. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if abandoned, would resolve the greatest number of conflicts while leaving the core thesis—that karma expresses an objective principle of moral desert—intact.
