I serve on the Municipal Science Communication Oversight Board, and by our meeting on the 28th we must decide whether to approve an 18-month pilot of the proposed Visual Risk Protocol: a city-wide emergency-notification system that would communicate flood, heat, and air-quality alerts exclusively through standardized pictograms, with no textual accompaniment in any language. The design team grounds the proposal explicitly in Otto Neurath’s ISOTYPE program and in his broader philosophical claim that empirically meaningful scientific content can be conveyed through a universal picture language without reliance on propositional syntax, grammatical structure, or metaphysical framing. Several board members object that stripping away language does not eliminate hidden propositional commitments but merely conceals them in the icon-selection and sequencing rules, producing ambiguous interpretations across cultural contexts and undermining the very empirical accountability—anchored in observable protocol sentences—that Neurath insisted upon. Others worry that approving the pilot implicitly commits the city to a philosophical thesis about the autonomy of visual meaning that we have not examined. Please draft the short formal written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete, actionable decision—full approval, rejection, or approval subject to specific binding conditions and a defined evaluation timeline—with clear reasoning. The opinion must treat the Neurathian picture-language thesis seriously as the project’s conceptual foundation, explain what philosophical commitments the protocol’s design actually entails, and include an honest, substantive account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. The document should be usable by board members who are not specialists in philosophy of science, and it should not call for bibliographies, external citations, or further literature review.
