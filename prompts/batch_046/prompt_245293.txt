I've been reading about whether sentience comes in degrees — like, do insects have 0.1 of a human's moral status, or is it all-or-nothing? The debate in the animal-ethics people I've been talking to seems totally stuck. One camp says there's a sharp threshold: either you've got the right kind of integraded information processing or you don't, and anything below is just reflex, like a thermostat reacting to heat. The other camp says it's obviously gradated — a bee feels something, just less, and moral status should scale like temperature or brightness, not like being pregnant.

My working theory is that they're not actually disagreeing about the science or even about ethics. I think they're answering two different questions under the same word 'sentience': one group is asking 'does this system have phenomenal consciousness at all?' and the other is asking 'how much does suffering matter here?' But I'm not sure. Maybe there's a hidden empirical question about what neural architectures actually produce experience, and neither side wants to admit we just don't know yet. Or maybe it's bedrock intuition — some people just can't stand the idea that a fly is partially a person, and others can't stand the idea that it's zero.

Can you diagnose what's actually going on? Is it verbal, substantive, or are they talking past each other? And please — I'm tired of people acting like one side is obviously right. Separate what's actually settled (if anything) from what's genuinely up in the air among people who actually study this. Don't present a contested view as the consensus. Also, if my 'two different questions' idea is wrong, tell me. And what would actually move this? A new brain-mapping result? A conceptual distinction? Or is it just going to stay stuck?

Oh, and one more thing — I've been wondering if the whole framing is wrong. In physics we don't ask whether a system 'has temperature' in a binary way; we talk about statistical ensembles and degrees of freedom. Maybe moral status should be treated more like an emergent statistical property of populations rather than an intrinsic switch inside one brain. I don't know if that makes sense philosophically, but it feels like the kind of thing neither camp is even discussing. Does that idea illuminate the stall, or is it just me importing my old field?
