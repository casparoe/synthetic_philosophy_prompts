Write an essay on whether Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model actually explains creativity or just relabels parameter tuning. The idea is that every creative leap is tweeking conceptual dials—like changing one chess rule at a time—so there are no true jumps, just smooth variations on hidden themes. But this seems to struggle with real revolutions: calculus or non-euclidean geometry don't feel like small adjustments, they feel like frame breaks. Weigh whether Hofstadter can absorb this by claiming revolutions are just extreme twists on deeper, invisible knobs, or whether that saves the model only by making it unfalsifiable. Also think about locked knobs: if some dimensions of a concept are fixed, can you still be genuinely creative inside that frame, or does it reduce to combinatorics? Reach a verdict and defend it—don't just sit on the fence.
