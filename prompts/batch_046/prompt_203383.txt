Take the 2008 CDO collapse built on Li's Gaussian copula — you know the story, correlations assumed stable then went to 1 and everything blew up. Analyze it through Friedman's F-twist: unrealistic assumptions are fine, even good, if the predictions hold. Obviously they didnt hold here, but dont just stop at "model failed, therefore Friedman is wrong." I want you to map it carefully — what exactly were the unrealistic assumptions, what was the predictive record before 2007, and then push into the weirder part. Does Friedman have anything to say about that kind of performative feedback? Or is his F-twist secretly built for physics-style models where the atoms dont read the equation?
