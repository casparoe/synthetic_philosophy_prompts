hey im grading some philosophy exams and want a second opinion on one answer. the prompt asked students to explain Doris's idea of local traits versus global Aristotelian virtues, and whether accepting local traits forces us to drop phronesis as an integrating capacity or whether it can be reconceived in an ecological framework where virtue is partly distributed across agent and environment. they also needed to discuss one virtue-ethicist reply and whether situationist data like teh Good Samaritan study actually undermines character or just complicates it.

heres the answer (theyre usually pretty sharp):

Local traits are dispositions that only hold in specific contexts, not across the board. Doris thinks people have these instead of full-blown virtues, so you might be honest with friends but cheat on taxes without being inconsistent—its just that your honesty is local to personal relationships. Aristotles view is different because he treats virtue as a unified state of practical wisdom that should show up everywhere, not just in pockets.

The Good Samaritan study by Darley and Batson is the classic example. They had all the seminarians running late past someone in need, and almost none stopped. This shows that being in a rush wipes out compassion, which means compassion isn't a real trait—its just a reaction to having free time. So situationism really does undermine virtue ethics, and the ecological idea that virtue is distributed doesnt help because if the environment controls everything then theres nothing left for the agent to integrate.

One reply is Julia Annas, who says virtue is like learning chess: you practice, you mess up, and gradually you get better. She thinks this explains why people act differently in different situations—you're just not fully skilled yet. But actually I think this helps Doris, because chess skill doesnt transfer to checkers, so virtue might also be domain-specific. Another point is that practical wisdom could still integrate local traits, but only if we treat it as an ecological capacity spread across habits and surroundings, not just inside the head.
