I recently joined a cooperative governance board in which members operate from genuinely incommensurable value systems—deep ecological preservation, market-oriented development, and indigenous sovereignty frameworks—and every binding decision requires some form of moral compromise. I am not looking for negotiation tactics; I want to know which conceptual open questions about gains from trade through moral compromise should command the limited philosophical attention available for institutional design. Among possibilities such as whether moral reasons can be translated across value systems without loss, what normative status a moral remainder carries after a legitimate compromise, whether partial allegiance to competing values can be rationally structured, and what institutional conditions prevent compromise from collapsing into exploitation or mere capitulation—which should be treated as upstream of the others? For each priority, defend its place using criteria like tractability for conceptual rather than empirical work, dependency on resolving other questions first, and what kind of institutional failure over the next two years would demonstrate that a particular line is less central than it appears. Deliver a defended agenda for directing limited philosophical effort—not a survey, not a bibliography, and not a list of canonical texts.
