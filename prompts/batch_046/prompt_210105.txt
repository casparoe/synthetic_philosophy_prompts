Here is one submission I need help assessing. The student writes: 'Renaming Hollis Avenue is wrong because it destroys historical evidence. The street sign is not praising Hollis; it is just a neutral fact on the map, like an entry in an archive. If we change the name, we erase the record that he existed and built the sewers, which violates the purpose of archival preservation. The spatial expressivism framework actually supports retention, because Vasquez—who developed this view—argues that commemorative objects must remain in their original locations to maintain historical authenticity; moving them to a museum or altering their context destroys their archival value. So the honorific versus archival distinction fails here, since every street name is purely archival by nature. Anyone who uses that distinction to justify removal is therefore misunderstanding both Vasquez and the nature of public signage.'

Identify the parts of the argument that correctly apply the course distinctions, flag any subtle missteps—such as treating a normative evaluative tool as an empirical claim about all street signs, or misrepresenting the framework's allowance for contextualized preservation outside the original site—and distinguish errors that should reduce the mark from weaknesses of style or tone alone.
