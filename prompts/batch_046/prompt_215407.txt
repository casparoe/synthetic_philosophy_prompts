I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends what she calls a "normative-authority" account of valid consent. On her view, deception vitiates consent only when it undermines the agent's authority to set the normative terms of the interaction, not merely when it supplies false descriptive information—so deceiving a research subject about a study's true purpose invalidates consent, but deceiving her about a minor side-risk does not, provided she retains authority over the core commitment. She also treats competence not as a binary threshold but as a scalar, domain-specific authority that can be present for simple acts yet absent for structurally complex ones, and she analyzes coercion not as psychological pressure but as the usurpation of that authority by another agent. Draft six to eight interview questions that move beyond restating the view. Include at least one question on how her framework distinguishes purpose-deception from risk-deception without collapsing into a subjective "what the agent cares about" criterion; one on whether her scalar competence model avoids generating merely partial consent that institutions cannot practically administer; and one on whether her "usurpation" analysis can account for structural coercion where no identifiable individual agent does the usurping. For each, provide a follow-up keyed to her most likely reply—especially the reply that her framework is normative rather than descriptive, and that practical administrability is a separate matter from conceptual clarity.
