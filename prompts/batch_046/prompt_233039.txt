I'm planning a one-off session for a philosophy-of-science reading group — second-years who've done intro logic but never met the Vienna Circle. I want them to get Neurath's ISOTYPE picture-language, not as cute infographics but as a serious claim that spatial icon-arrangement replaces grammatical syntax for science, and that metaphysics literally can't be expressed in it because absense can't be standardised.

I need the teaching plan, not the lecture itself.

Order of dependancy: I think they first have to see that for Neurath a scientific statement is a cluster (he called them Ballungen — vague, cluster-like observation reports) rather than a precise subject-predicate sentence. Only then does the picture-example make sense. But I'm unsure whether to introduce his refusal to design a universal not-icon before or after they see the factory/smoke example.

The carrying example: I want one ISOTYPE-style comparison — say, a factory symbol with smoke icons versus the same symbol with the smoke icons absent — and I need to know exactly what this example must demonstrate (that negation in pictures is contextual, not symbolic) and what it must avoid (looking like a graph or a photo that they already understand).

Misconceptions, in the order I think they'll arrive: first that it's Wittgenstein's picture theory (language mirrors reality like a map), then that it's Esperanto with icons, then that pictures are more objective because they bypass language. At what point do I head off each one? And what's the one sentence that kills the Esperanto confusion without making them feel stupid?

Check for understanding: one question I can ask at the end that shows they've understood the specific medium-limitation — why metaphysics can't be drawn — rather than just repeating that Neurath was anti-metaphysical.

What to leave out and say so: I'm going to skip the Carnap-Schlick protocol debate entirely, but should I name-drop it and say we're skipping it, or just stay silent? Also, I want to inject a slightly niche point — that Neurath thought even basic observation reports were already theory-laden clusters, not neutral building-blocks — but I'm worried that's too much for one session. Where does it fit?
