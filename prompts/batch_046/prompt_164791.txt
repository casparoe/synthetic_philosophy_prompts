I suspect the stall is not empirical but conceptual: one side treats "state at a limit point" as requiring continuity with the trajectory, while the other treats it as independently assignable. To probe this, imagine a "damped lamp" in which each toggle applies a decaying impulse, so the system converges to a unique equilibrium — say, off — regardless of discrete switch history. Does this variant dissolve the paradox, revealing that the original dispute was about dynamics, or does it confirm that the debate was never about physics but about the semantics of limit-assignment and the conditions under which a property extends to a boundary point?
