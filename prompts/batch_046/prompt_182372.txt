Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains that alternate and converge—about Buridan's ass suspended between two identical bales of hay. Let the first refrain state that the options share every relevant property (for instance, “The bales are equal in every respect”), and let the second state that rational choice remains possible despite the deadlock (for instance, “The will is free to choose”). Use the circular repetition to enact the paralysis of perfect equilibrium: each return of the first refrain should deepen the sense that no reason can tip the balance, while the second should insist on an arbitrary but legitimate break. The poem should treat the paradox seriously as a problem for theories of rational agency—specifically, whether the principle of sufficient reason allows for genuine choice when reasons are perfectly balanced—rather than treating it as a mere joke about starvation. In the final quatrain, where both refrains appear together, reflect the resolution traditionally attributed to Buridan: that the will possesses a power to suspend judgment and then act arbitrarily, rather than the popular caricature that the ass dies of indecision. Make the rhyme scheme and the mechanical repetition do philosophical work, so that the form itself embodies the tension between determinism and freedom. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of action recognizes the specific problem of choice without preference, and compelling enough as verse that a reader unfamiliar with Buridan still feels the deadlock.
