I call this "informational brute luck," extending Cohen's brute-option distinction into the domain of belief formation and political preference, and I argue that unjust divergence in these epistemic goods should trigger egalitarian correction—perhaps through structural transparency mandates or attention-budget redistribution—rather than being dismissed as mere option luck or consumer choice. Give me the objections in the likely order of attack, phrased in each camp's vocabulary, with what I should concede, what I must hold, and—crucially—the weakest link in my own argument, namely the analogy between material resources and epistemic exposure, that I should prepare to defend rather than hope they overlook. Also include the one objection I am praying nobody raises, and tell me precisely how to respond if it surfaces.
