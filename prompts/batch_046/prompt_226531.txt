hey — i'm drafting notes for a seminar essay on pain asymbolia and whether it actually undermines the claim that pain is essentially bad. the usual line is that patients feel the sensation but dont mind it, so badness must be seperable from the sensory part. i think that misses a distinction between negative affect and what i'm tentatively calling "motivational tagging" — the feature that makes you want the state to end, regardless of how you feel about it. in asymbolia, maybe the tagging drops out while a kind of neutral affect remains, or maybe affect is suppressed but tagging lingers in a weird, unmotivated form. to test this i made up a case: a cellist with asymbolia who can keep playing through a hand injury, describes the burning as "present like a color in the room," and reports no urge to stop. the question is whether her state is still pain if there's no push to avoid it.

i'd love your comments, especially:
1. is the three-way split (sensory, affect, tagging) coherent, or am i just mulitplying entities?
2. does the cellist case do philosophical work, or is it just a fancy redescription of absent pain?
3. what's the weakest link in the argument that badness is contingent rather than essential?

please be blunt — i want to know if the idea is interesting or just muddled. don't worry about references or reading lists; i'm familiar with the clinical literature. also there are probably a couple typos in the draft above, dont bother fixing them unless they actually change the meaning.
