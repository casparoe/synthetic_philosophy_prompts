Design a reusable evaluation procedure for an editor at a civic-education nonprofit that publishes 2,000-word biographical portraits for adult discussion groups. The editor reviews freelance proposals under tight constraints: thirty minutes per proposal, no specialized philosophical training, and a requirement that any value-laden judgment be flagged for the board rather than settled alone. The guiding principle—derived from Plutarch’s practice of parallel lives and modern critiques of hagiography—holds that biography teaches moral judgment not through emulation but through moral triangulation: the reader calibrates her own position by examining a subject’s genuinely open choices (contingency), viewing them against a contrasting figure or counterfactual path (parallel framing), and applying the reasoning to a present analog only after explicit markers of historical difference are preserved (reflexive translation). Operationalize this into an ordered checklist: the specific questions to ask of a proposal, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the procedure stops with a firm rejection, where it mandates board referral because the issue is one of values, and which distinctions must survive simplification versus which can be dropped. Include explicit warnings about the hard cases where the procedure will misfire. Then apply it to two proposals: first, a portrait of St. Francis of Assisi intended for secular civic groups, where hagiographic idealization is the central risk; second, a portrait of a twentieth-century industrialist celebrated for philanthropy but criticized for suppressing labor organization, paired with a contemporary labor leader, where the parallel framing may yield a verdict the editor is likely to resist. For the second case, state the unwelcome answer and identify what the procedure still cannot resolve. Finally, state clearly what the procedure is not designed to do.
