A metaphysician, Dr Lian Chen, has given the following verdicts on whether a collection of parts genuinely composes a single object: two bricks mortared together form one wall; a scattered cloud of dust particles does not form one thing; a living human organism does; a statue carved from clay is a genuine object, though the clay itself persists independently; two quantum-entangled particles separated by light-years do not compose one object; and a temporary swarm of drones that briefly arranges itself into a geometric shape before dispersing is not an object, even though the drones coordinate during the interval. Now consider a new case: a distributed quantum error-correction network spanning three separate laboratories, linked only by entangled qubits that maintain coherence during a single computation lasting exactly forty-seven minutes, with no permanent physical container, no biological components, and no purpose assigned by an external designer; during that window it functions as one logical processor with unified error correction, then decoheres irreversibly and never reassembles. Predict Dr Chen’s verdict, identify at least two competing principles that are jointly consistent with her earlier rulings—such as spatiotemporal continuity with persistence, functional unity with substrate independence, or essential-property preservation—state which principle best explains her track record, say what each principle predicts about the network, name the closest analog among her earlier cases, and propose one further hypothetical case that would discriminate between the leading candidates. Please answer in British English.
