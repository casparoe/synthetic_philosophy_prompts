Compare the idea of a tangled hierachy—where levels loop back on themselves, like in Escher's Drawing Hands—with Hofstadter's notion of an inviolate level, a bottom floor that dosen't depend on anything higher up. Does the drawing actually have an inviolate level (the paper, the artist's hand, maybe?), or does even that base get pulled into the loop? And if there's no inviolate level at all, can we still call the structure hierarchical in any meaningful sense, or is it just a flat circle with fancy names?
