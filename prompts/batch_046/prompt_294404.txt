Specify the full list of desiderata that an adequate theory of the ethics of voting must satisfy, assuming voting is neither reducible to an instrumental act of outcome-maximization nor to a pure expression of individual moral identity, but is structured by what I call the double-bind of delegation: the voter simultaneously transfers decision-making authority to a collective mechanism and retains personal moral accountability for that very act of authorization. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or conceptual tension behind it — for instance, how the theory must distinguish strategic selection of a lesser-evil candidate from endorsement of that candidate's harmful platform; how it must treat abstention when all available options fall below a minimal moral-acceptability threshold; how it must evaluate votes in purely symbolic or non-binding elections without collapsing the moral distinction between participation and complicity; and how it must accommodate ranked or sequential ballot structures in which a first-choice rejection activates a downstream preference cascade that the voter may not fully control. Identify which pairs of desiderata appear jointly unsatisfiable — particularly any conflict between the instrumental requirement that votes be rationally responsive to expected outcomes and the integrity requirement that votes not violate the voter's core moral commitments — and indicate whether standard results in collective choice theory render any subset impossible. Mark clearly which requirements are non-negotiable for any plausible account and which are camp-specific preferences (e.g., strategic rationality versus moral purity versus civic-associative duty). Then briefly score three broad approaches against the list: a consequentialist account, an expressive-autonomy account, and an account rooted in associative or civic-friendship obligations. Do not supply bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. If the premise that authorization and personal accountability can be coherently separated in a double-bind of delegation is confused, or if the task of reconciling instrumental and integrity-based demands is impossible as posed, say so plainly and do not force the desiderata to fit. Be explicit about your confidence in each judgment and flag any point where the reasoning depends on a contested interpretation of voting practice.
