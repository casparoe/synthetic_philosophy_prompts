(2) suffering isn't binary; it's a gradient from full-blown human anguish down through insect nociception to whatever minimal disturbance a feedback loop feels when its setpoint is violated.
(3) so moral reasons scale smoothly with the depth of the experience.

(5) therefore there's a non-zero moral reason not to harm a thermostat—say, by forcing it into a state where it can't correct.
(6) but if there's any moral reason at all, then it's pro tanto wrong to do it.
(7) we turn off or override thermostats constantly, with zero moral hesitation.
(8) either we're constantly committing micro-wrongs (absurd), or one of the steps above collapses.
(9) i think the mistake might be in (4) or (6), but i want to keep (1)-(3) because they feel right about insects and mammals. if i have to pick, i'd rather save the gradation idea and ditch the conclusion that my heater is a moral patient. what's the cost?
