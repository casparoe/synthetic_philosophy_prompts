Write a sonnet in iambic pentameter (ABAB CDCD EFEF GG) spoken by one of Leibniz’s “striving possibles” — a world-essence that possessed a real conatus (tendency) toward existence in the divine understanding, proportional to its perfection, but was not actualized because it was not compossible with the maximal combination of variety and simplicity that defines the best world. In the octave, establish the speaker’s ontological status: not mere nothingness, but a non-actualized substance with a legitimate claim, now excluded not by intrinsic defect but because including it would have required either sacrificing some greater compossible good or admitting avoidable moral evil elsewhere in the system. At the volta (line 9), shift from lament or envy to recognition: the speaker should grasp that metaphysical evil — the limitation and imperfection inseparable from finitude — is unavoidable in every essence, whereas moral evil is permitted only when it serves a greater harmony; the speaker realizes that its exclusion is the shadow that confirms the actual world’s superiority, not a denial of its own worth. In the sestet, develop the paradox that the best world contains within its divine blueprint the very possibilities it rejects, and that the speaker’s presence-in-absence in God’s intellect contributes to the “perfection” measured by variety-plus-order. Close with a couplet that states precisely what must be surrendered — either the claim of every essence to actualization, or the principle that God maximizes compossible good — letting the rhyme pair enforce the choice. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader of the Theodicy and the Monadology recognizes the distinctions between metaphysical and moral evil, the role of compossibility, and the idea that possible worlds strive for existence in proportion to their perfection, without turning into exposition. Begin with the poem itself, with no preface or commentary.
