For our rationalist reading group I've drafted my philosophical profile on punishment and free will, and I want it audited before I defend it. Here are my commitments:

- I accept Dennett's compatibilist picture: moral responsibility survives determinism because it lives in our reactive-attitude practices—holding people to account—not in some metaphysical escape hatch.
- I also accept Caruso's hard-incompatibilist diagnosis that basic desert—inflicting suffering purely because the offender morally deserves it—is never justified, and that the correct institutional model is the public-health quarantine: restrict liberty only to prevent harm, never to exact deserved pain.
- I hold that proportionality between offense and sanction is a hard constraint, not an aesthetic preference: a system that imprisons petty thieves for life is unjust even if it optimizes public safety.
- I think that once predictive risk algorithms reach high accuracy with low false-positive rates, preemptive detention of high-risk individuals is permissible, even before any crime occurs.
- I think Kant's Formula of Humanity still binds us to the detained: we must offer explanation, rehabilitation, and respect, treating them never merely as risk-containers.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly. For each real conflict, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Identify at least one tension that dissolves once you notice the distinction being used. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most problems without destroying the overall shape of the view.
