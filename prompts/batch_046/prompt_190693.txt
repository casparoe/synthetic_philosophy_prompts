The Foundationalist Basic tier should license “basic beliefs” that ship with no upstream justification clause and are warranted solely by internal self-evidence, and it should include a warranty-voiding provision for users who attempt to justify these beliefs by reference to anything outside the licensed foundation. The Coherentist Pro tier should bind the subscriber to a circular dependency network in which every belief derives its license exclusively from mutual coherence within a closed system, with an explicit prohibition on importing unlicensed external foundations and a clause stating that no single belief may be accessed in isolation. The Infinitist Enterprise tier should offer an open-ended, infinitely regressive chain of justification with no termination event, requiring perpetual renewal, disclaiming liability for beliefs that remain suspended in mid-regress, and noting that cancellation is impossible because the justification for cancellation itself awaits further justification. Include a brief invented user review from a customer who attempted to migrate from Foundationalist Basic to Infinitist Enterprise and discovered that the belief motivating the migration lacked a valid license under both regimes. The tone should remain entirely straight-faced—no winking, no explicit labeling of the joke—so that a reader familiar with the regress problem recognizes the satire as structurally fair. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or references to specific named texts. Write this as a polished, formal prompt that assumes the respondent understands the epistemological positions precisely enough to render them without consulting external sources.
