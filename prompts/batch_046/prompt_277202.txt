"The WKR problem is when you do the right thing but for a reason that doesn't justify it. In Maya's case, she claims her reason is that suffering is bad, but her therapist shows her real reason is avoiding grief. So her psycholgical motivation is self-focused, not other-focused. That means she has the wrong kind of reason, because the feature that moves her isn't the right-making feature.

Still, her volunteering reduces suffering, so the act has normative value. But because of normative leakage, the wrong internal reason drains the moral worth — its good instrumentally but not morally. She shouldn't get full moral credit either, since credit requires the right internal cause plus the right action.

This also relates to Scanlon's buck-passing view: reasons are properties that make actions right, and since Maya's internal state doesn't match that property, the buck doesn't pass. So moral credit needs both the correct action and the correct causal explanation inside the agent."
