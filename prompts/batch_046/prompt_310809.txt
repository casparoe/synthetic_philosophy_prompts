I am designing a single seventy-minute session for first-year international-law graduates with no background in German idealism, focused specifically on the dependency structure in Kant's Perpetual Peace between the preliminary articles and the definitive articles, with cosmopolitan right treated as the bridge that keeps the federation from collapsing into a closed bloc. Plan the explanatory arc, not the explanation itself: the exact sequence in which the preliminary conditions, the republican constitution, the federative union, and hospitality must be introduced so that the first are seen as logical preconditions rather than optional recommendations; the one concrete scenario—perhaps a climate-migration standoff at the border of a republican federation—that should carry the entire session, together with what it must include and exclude; the predictable misconceptions (that Kant proposes a world state, that the articles are pragmatic advice, that hospitality grants full membership), and the precise point in the sequence where each should be headed off; one closing diagnostic question that tests whether the students have grasped the institutional logic rather than memorized the article list; and a clear statement of which elements of Kant's broader critical philosophy I should leave out and openly mark as beyond scope.
