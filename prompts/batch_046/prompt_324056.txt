Write the full list of desiderata that an adequate theory of rational commitment must satisfy to resolve Kavka's toxin puzzle without collapsing it into a standard dominance argument. For each requirement, state the motivating case or distinction: the phenomena to be explained (overnight intention-formation, drinking after the reward is secured, predictive reliability about one's own future behavior); the distinctions that must be preserved (between deciding and predicting one's action, between instrumental and intrinsic reasons for drinking, between a single persisting agent and a sequence of agent-stages); and the formal constraints that cannot be violated (compatibility with expected-utility maximization given stable preferences, prohibition of ad hoc temporal discounting). Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—especially if one requires treating one's future self as an independent deliberator. Then briefly note, for causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and a precommitment model, which single desideratum each fails and why, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves.
