Having recently attempted to translate a rondeau by Clément Marot into English for a close friend's anniversary and discovering that every version preserving the form's mandatory refrain and two-rhyme structure replaced the original's concrete images with only loosely related substitutes, I have become preoccupied with the tension between structural fidelity and semantic betrayal in poetic translation. Write a joke about translation under formal constraint—specifically about preserving poetic form versus literal meaning—that turns on the paradox associated with le ton beau de Marot: that honoring a poem's music and social tone may require the translator to invent entirely new content and thus act as a co-author rather than a transparent medium. The joke should make sense to someone who understands the debate over whether translation is preservation or recreation, and its punchline should depend on the specific dynamics of formal constraints such as the rondeau's refrain and rhyme scheme rather than on generic observations about meaning being lost; in particular, the humor should arise from the idea that the more rigorously one respects the form, the more radically one must alter or invent the substance.
