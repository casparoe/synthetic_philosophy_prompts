I just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a close friend about whether origin essentialism regarding persons is genuinely defensible, and I am trying to turn our disagreement into a short exploratory essay. The view I am examining is the familiar claim that you could not have come from a different sperm and egg—that your actual zygote is metaphysically essential to you. Rather than rehearsing the standard counterexamples, I want to introduce what I am calling "intra-parental modal proximity": if we take causal origins seriously, then a world in which your parents conceive a genetically different child at roughly the same time should be modally closer to actuality than a world in which you are born to entirely different parents. Yet our intuitions seem sharply asymmetrical; we feel strongly that you would not exist in that sibling-world, but we are far less certain about worlds with different parents. I want to argue that this asymmetry exposes a tension in origin essentialism—either it overgenerates essential properties, producing a strange kind of "sibling essentialism" in which every nearby possible sibling is rigorously non-you, or it undergenerates them, since relaxing the gamete requirement while retaining the parents seems to let you "be" the sibling after all. I am framing this not as a decisive proof but as a conceptual stress test, and I am especially unsure whether I am conflating metaphysical identity with biological continuity or smuggling in an undefended assumption about zygote identity conditions.

Could you comment on whether this "intra-parental modal proximity" framing is philosophically illuminating or just unnecessary jargon, and on whether the overgeneration/undergeneration dilemma actually lands? I would also appreciate suggestions on whether the essay needs a sharper distinction between genetic essentialism and process essentialism about embryonic development, and whether there is a hidden normative premise—perhaps about the moral weight of potential siblings—that I have not made explicit but that is doing some of the intuitive work. Please treat this as a draft of ideas rather than a polished argument, and let me know which parts feel like they could survive closer scrutiny and which ones collapse into confusion between modal logic and reproductive biology.
