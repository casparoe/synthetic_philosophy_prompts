Having recently begun to contemplate the possibility of progressive neural prosthesis—where biological neurons are replaced by synthetic analogues gradually over years rather than through any discrete transfer—I have adopted a specific variant of the gradual-replacement view on personal identity. On my account, survival is secured not by continuity of material substrate or by the preservation of every specific psychological trait, but by what I term functional-topology continuity: the invariant relational architecture among cognitive states must be maintained without interruption across the replacement trajectory. I hold that a seamless gradient of substitution, however extensive, preserves identity, whereas even a brief discontinuity in this topological structure fractures it, regardless of how much psychological content is retained.

Given that this position may guide actual clinical and ethical choices I face, I want a rigorous map of its vulnerability. What observations, arguments, or conceptual results would genuinely count against it? Specifically: which known or plausible findings about neural plasticity, prosthetic integration, or split-brain dynamics would challenge the claim that functional-topology continuity is the correct identity-preserving condition? Which objections—whether from the logic of vagueness in gradual change, from branching replacement scenarios in which two trajectories diverge, or from the distinction between diachronic coherence and genuine persistence—would be decisive, and which merely awkward? Would a committed defender of this view have to concede damage if synthetic replacements were shown to alter implicit biases, emotional valence structures, or non-declarative memory in cumulative, imperceptible ways? Finally, does the view risk insulation through post-hoc redefinition of its central term—rendering any observed discontinuity a mere failure of "true" topology rather than a counterexample—and if so, is that unfalsifiability a serious defect for a framework intended to inform real-world decisions about cognitive replacement?
