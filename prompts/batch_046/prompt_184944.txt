I'm moderating a podcast interview with an unnamed philosopher who defends a graded, or scalar, revision of Strawson's reactive-attitudes framework. On her view, resentment, gratitude, and related reactive attitudes are not binary present-or-absent conditions but come in differing intensities, and moral responsibility ought to be allocated proportionally to an agent's sustained capacity for reciprocal reactive engagement rather than treated as an all-or-nothing status. She further claims that the objective attitude is not merely a suspension of reactivity but an active diagnostic stance that reveals structural constraints on agency invisible to reactive involvement. Draft ten interview questions that move well beyond restating Strawson. Include: one on how she prevents her scalar model from collapsing the reactive/objective distinction into a mere continuum; one on what becomes of Strawson's claim that adopting the objective attitude toward someone is incompatible with full membership in the moral community; one on whether her model can preserve the asymmetry between self-directed reactive attitudes (guilt, pride) and other-directed ones (resentment, gratitude) without abandoning the centrality of interpersonal address; and one on what conceptual or empirical consideration could genuinely count against proportional responsibility. For each question, provide a brief follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—particularly any move to treat an objection as a matter for further elaboration rather than as an internal difficulty. Please write the response in British English.
