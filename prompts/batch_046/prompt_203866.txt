Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the Unified Closure-Failure Camp, which holds that the lottery and preface paradoxes share a single structural root—rational belief is not closed under conjunction, and this is a feature of resource-bounded cognition rather than an error—and the Domain-Separation Camp, which holds that the lottery case is a problem of statistical aggregation over independent events best treated by probabilistic coherence norms, whereas the preface case is a problem of self-referential epistemic humility best treated by contextualist assertion norms, so that treating them together obscures more than it reveals. Both parties should be equally competent; do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.

Structure the response as follows.

1. Agreed claims (numbered, roughly 250 words): State the formal structures both sides accept, including Kyburg-style lottery reasoning and Makinson-style preface reasoning. Then address whether a revisability-cost asymmetry—where revising a single lottery-ticket belief is epistemically cheap but revising the global belief that some ticket wins is costly, with the reverse pattern holding in the preface—actually distinguishes the two cases or is merely an artifact of how the scenarios are framed. Also state whether both sides agree that binary belief is inadequate and that some graded or layered doxastic framework is needed.

2. Disagreements table (roughly 200 words): Give precise formulations of each camp’s core thesis, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position. Specify where they part ways on whether closure failure is one phenomenon or two, on whether probabilistic coherence alone can resolve the preface, and on whether contextualist assertion norms can resolve the lottery.

3. Cruxes (roughly 150 words): Identify the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. Focus especially on whether introducing layered doxastic norms—Layer-1 predictive credence versus Layer-2 assertoric commitment—dissolves both paradoxes simultaneously or only the preface, and on whether a modified “stacked preface” with explicit probabilistic error rates behaves like a correlated lottery in which ticket outcomes are not independent.

4. Tests and predictions (roughly 150 words): List the thought experiments or arguments both sides agree would bear on the cruxes, with each camp’s predicted outcome recorded in advance. Include the stacked-preference versus correlated-lottery comparison, and state what result—if conceptually clear—would cause each side to revise its view that the paradoxes are unified or separate. If any portion of the dispute is purely verbal, name it and explain why.
