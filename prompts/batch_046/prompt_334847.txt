I have been developing an argument about forecasting and self-knowledge that tries to refine the standard distinction between the inside view and the outside view. Most treatments present the inside view as reasoning from the specific details of a case and the outside view as reasoning from statistical base rates, with epistemic modesty understood as calibrating one's confidence downward toward the historical average. I want to suggest that this framing misses the deeper problem: it is not merely that people overestimate their competence, but that they miscategorize themselves with respect to the appropriate reference class. When a project manager forecasts her timeline, she places her project in the class of projects with these particular constraints, but the relevant outside view might require placing her in the class of all projects led by people with her experience level in this sector. The required modesty, on my account, is not just Bayesian shrinkage of an estimate but a prior reconceptualization of the self as an instance of a broader category before the case-specific details are applied.

I have drafted some notes exploring whether this reconceptualization is genuinely distinct from standard Bayesian updating with a properly chosen prior, or whether it is merely a psychological redescription of the same formal move. I am also uncertain whether I have misrepresented the forecasting literature: does Tetlock's distinction concern the evidential weight of case-specific reasoning versus historical frequencies, or does it already encompass this problem of reference-class selection? I would welcome comments on whether the distinction I am drawing is coherent, whether the claim that epistemic modesty is primarily about correct self-categorization rather than confidence calibration is defensible, and whether this reads as a substantive contribution or just a reframing of familiar material. I am not looking for a reading list, but I would value a careful assessment of where the argument holds up and where it collapses into a restatement of existing ideas.
