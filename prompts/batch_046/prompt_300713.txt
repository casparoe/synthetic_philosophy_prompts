Write an essay on whether Dennett's Gregorian creature represents a genuinely distinct kind of mind or is reducible to a Popperian creature augmented by external cognitive scaffolding. Critics contend that language, writing, and cultural tools do not generate a new cognitive kind but merely extend the internal generate-and-test mechanism already present in Popperian minds, making the Gregorian category ontologically redundant and the boundary between Popperian and Gregorian arbitrary. Defenders respond that Gregorian cognition enables a qualitatively different form of recursive self-modeling—the creature can model not only its environment but its own modeling tools, and can revise those tools in light of second-order evaluation—which internal simulation alone cannot achieve. Weigh these positions with precision, but do not end non-committally: defend a definite verdict on whether the distinction survives once we treat Gregorian cognition as distributed across brains, artifacts, and social practices rather than bounded within an individual skull. In particular, address whether this distributed reading forces a revision to Dennett's original taxonomy—perhaps collapsing Gregorian back into Popperian, or splitting Gregorian into artifact-dependent and artifact-independent subkinds—and justify your answer. The essay should be in American English and should take a committed position with reasons, not merely survey the options.
