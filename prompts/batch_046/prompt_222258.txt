If Popper's argument in The Poverty of Historicism succeeds—showing that history lacks discoverable laws because the growth of knowledge introduces irreducible novelty—then long-range social forecasting should be impossible in principle. Yet Popper endorses piecemeal social engineering, which requires predicting the outcomes of specific institutional changes, and today algorithmic models routinely forecast behavioral trends at scale. Does this mean Popper's framework contains a hidden contradiction: he must either reject all predictive social science, including his own reformist project, or smuggle back in the very historical laws he denies? Or is the mistake mine—conflating the unconditional prophecies he attacks with the conditional, falsifiable predictions that engineering actually needs? Identify where the reasoning goes wrong, and defend whether predictive governance can be consistent with his anti-historicism without collapsing into the doctrines he rejects.
