Jeffrey conditonalisation is different from standard Bayes conditioning because with Bayes you learn that a specific event E occured and you update P(H) to P(H|E). Jeffrey's version, set out in The Logic of Decision (1965), is for when you dont learn any specific E but your beliefs about the members of a partiton shift directly. You keep your old conditional probabilities P(H|Ei) fixed — Jeffrey called this direct inference — and combine them with your new partition probabilities using the law of total probability. So with prior P(H)=0.3 and the given values, my updated credence is 0.5×0.8 + 0.3×0.4 + 0.2×0.2 = 0.56. 

As for a tension, Jeffrey himself recognised that the partition cells need not be mutually exclusive if the evidence is genuinely vague. That means you can assign belief to E1 and E2 simultaneously without breaking the axioms, which is useful for sensory data that doesnt fall neatly into one box. Critics might worry this makes the update underdetermined, but Jeffrey thought it was a feature, not a bug, because it mirrors how perception actually works.
