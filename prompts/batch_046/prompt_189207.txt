I have been trying to understand Johannes Kepler on my own, and I keep running into what looks like a deep contradiction. On the one hand, he replaced the ancient circular orbits with ellipses and showed that planets move faster near the sun and slower farther away, governed by a mechanical law rather than by perfect, uniform motion. On the other hand, he still cast horoscopes and argued that the planets exert real influences on human temperament and worldly events. The contradiction, as I see it, is that the older astrological worldview seemed to depend on the idea that each planet possesses a constant, circular nature—something like a pure, unchanging tone in a cosmic harmony—that radiates downward in a steady, unvarying way. If Kepler’s own physics proves that planetary motion is elliptical, mechanically forced, and rhythmically variable, then the metaphysical basis for a fixed celestial “character” seems to dissolve entirely. So my question is this: was Kepler simply holding two incompatible worldviews out of financial necessity and habit, or did he have a coherent philosophical way of reconciling elliptical mechanics with astrological causation—perhaps by replacing circular constancy with some other kind of harmonic ratio derived from orbital speed? I feel as though I am missing a subtle link between his astronomy and his astrology, but I cannot see what allows the two to stand together without contradiction.
