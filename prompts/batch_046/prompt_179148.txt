Compare Hume's probabilistic argument against miracles with what I'd call the attenuation problem in nested testimony. Here's the idea: if you have a chain of witnesses where each person only trusts reports that align with their own direct experience, then a genuinly singular event gets diluted at every link -- not because anyone lies, but because each filter applies a local version of Hume's uniform-experience rule. So is Hume's individual-level argument just one node in that larger network failure? Or does his appeal to uniform experience across all mankind do something the distributed version can't replicate? I don't need scholar names or page numbers, just whether the two structures are genuinely parallel and where they split apart.
