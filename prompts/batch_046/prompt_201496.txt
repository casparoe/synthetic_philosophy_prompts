I'm constructing the philosophical backbone for a novel in which the central figure lives with pain asymbolia—she registers nociceptive signals without the usual aversive affect or withdrawal imperative, a condition I intend to treat not as numbness but as a genuinely altered moral-phenomenal landscape. Before I commit to her interior voice, I need an audit of my own convictions, because they will determine whether she reports her state as liberation, deficit, or mere difference. Here is my current profile: (a) The asymbolic case demonstrates that the phenomenal character of pain and its negative value are separable; what remains is sensation without suffering, and suffering, not raw sensation, is what makes pain bad. (b) Even so, I believe pain retains an objective badness for the asymbolic subject—she is missing a correct evaluative response, analogous to color blindness missing red, and her welfare is genuinely diminished by that absence. (c) The representational content of pain—its information about tissue damage—is conceptually and ethically distinct from its affective force; only the latter carries moral weight, while the former is instrumentally useful but not intrinsically valuable. (d) If an experience produces absolutely no motivational pull—no minimal desire for its cessation—then attributing badness to it is a projection of normal human psychology rather than a property of the experience itself. (e) There is an unavoidable first-person epistemic asymmetry: the asymbolic patient can authentically report that nothing is wrong, and no external observer can fully overturn that report from outside, because the missing affect is precisely the data that would make correction phenomenologically compelling.

Please examine these commitments for joint consistency. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe is each real conflict? Rank them by severity, specify the cheapest revision for each, and say what each revision costs—especially whether abandoning any one of them forces me to portray my protagonist either as mistaken about her own welfare or as lacking authority over her own reports. I am not asking for a survey of the literature; I want a precise audit of this specific set, with the contradictions mapped exactly and no commitment treated as sacred.
