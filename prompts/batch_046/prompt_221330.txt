Malcolm vs. Dennett on whether dreams are experiences has been stuck for decades. Malcolm basically says dreams cant be experiences because during sleep there's no observer who can verify them happening in real time, and waking reports are unreliable reconstructions. Dennett pushes back that this sets a verificationist bar we dont apply to waking experience, and that retrospective report is good enough. But they've traded these moves past eachother forever without anyone conceding. My working theory is that they're not actually disagreeing about dreams but about what 'experience' requires: maybe Malcolm thinks experience needs some kind of present-tense self-access that sleep lacks, while Dennett thinks thats adding an unnecessary epistemic condition onto a metaphysical state. But I'm not sure if thats right, or if there's a hidden empirical question about whether REM sleep involves the same global workspace dynamics as waking consciousness. Can you diagnose this? Is it verbal, is it about standards for what counts as data (first-person report vs third-person physiology), are they answering different questions about consciousness vs memory, or is the whole thing ill-posed? Specifically, I wonder if injecting a distinction between 'phenomenal presence' (being there in the moment) and 'retrospective narrative availability' (being able to construct a story after waking) would help, or if thats just renaming Malcolm's worry. And honestly, what could actually move this? A new neuro finding, a conceptual distinction, or should we just admit its a dead end and move on?
