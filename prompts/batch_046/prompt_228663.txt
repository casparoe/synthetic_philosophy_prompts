I have just come out of a long, circular argument with a friend about whether Darwin's evolutionary account of morality actually undermines normative ethics or merely explains its origin. Throughout our exchange she kept invoking the phrase nature is red in tooth and claw, using it as though it were Darwin's own verdict on the moral universe, and she pressed the claim that evolutionary naturalism necessarily reduces altruism to a calculated mask for reproductive self-interest. I am trying to organize my thoughts for a seminar paper, but I realize I am conflating several distinct theses that people routinely smuggle into that phrase.

Could you carefully disambiguate the precise claims that nature is red in tooth and claw could be expressing in this specific context—distinguishing an empirical claim about the prevalence of predation and competition in natural selection, a metaphysical claim about the absence of teleology or inherent purpose in evolutionary processes, a normative claim that cooperation is always instrumentally rational rather than intrinsically valuable, and an epistemological claim that human moral sentiments are systematically deceptive about their own evolutionary function? For each reading, indicate whether it is a trivial restatement of evolutionary theory, a substantive scientific claim, a false inference that Darwin himself rejected, or a rhetorical sleight of hand that confuses the origin of a trait with its present justification. Then specify which of these readings my friend actually needs in order to argue that evolutionary ethics collapses moral obligation into strategic cooperation, and whether any of them can be fairly traced back to Darwin's own arguments about social instincts, sympathy, and the gradual development of the moral sense in his writings on human evolution, as opposed to being a later social-Darwinist interpolation. Finally, address whether Darwin's parallel naturalistic account of religious belief—on which he treats theism as emerging from animistic reasoning about unseen agency—offers any internal resources for resisting the reduction of morality to pure strategic cooperation, or whether his naturalism uniformly undermines both domains.
