Crucially, H is not independent of E; rather, H is partly constituted by recognizing patterns within E itself—for example, noticing that one's confidence in P tracks an irrelevant feature of the presentation. Existing frameworks tend to treat this either as straightforward irrationality, resolved by deferring to H, or as resolvable through bootstrapping, where one uses first-order success to vindicate the reasoning process. I want to investigate whether there is a coherent intermediate position: one that allows an agent to rationally maintain P at reduced strength while also maintaining H, without either collapsing into skepticism about all first-order judgments or illicitly using P to undermine H. The proposal should articulate the precise question—can a non-aggregative, context-sensitive model of evidential weight accommodate reflective loops without violating either the uniqueness norm or a minimal consistency constraint?—and explain why the standard aggregative and leveling-up models leave this open. The deliverable should be a principled taxonomy that distinguishes vicious loops, which require belief revision, from benign reflective instability, which permits sustained belief with adjusted confidence, plus an explicit statement of what would count as failure—such as demonstrating that the non-linear model either collapses back into the hierarchical one or licenses arbitrary belief retention. The scope should be realistic for three months of philosophical work without empirical data collection, emphasizing conceptual analysis and model-building. The proposal should also address, briefly, why this internal puzzle about rationality merits funding: specifically, because the structure of reflective loops appears not only in individual epistemology but in institutional decision-making and in the design of artificial reasoning systems, where distinguishing vicious from benign loops is practically urgent.
