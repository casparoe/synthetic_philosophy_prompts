I have been covering risk governance and long-horizon ethics for a science and ideas publication, and in recent discussions with researchers working on systemic harm, I have noticed a conceptual gap that mainstream reporting tends to overlook. The public conversation around suffering risks—often abbreviated s-risks—tends to center on singular, high-magnitude catastrophes: discrete scenarios in which enormous numbers of beings endure extreme harm. Yet a smaller body of theorists has begun to stress a different architecture of harm they call suffering cascades: diffuse, recursively amplified patterns of distress that propagate through institutional, algorithmic, and social feedback loops rather than through any one catastrophic event. Because no single node in the network carries a catastrophic load, these cascades are structurally invisible in conventional risk assessments, even though their aggregate moral weight may be comparable or greater.

For a feature I am developing on whether our moral and policy frameworks are calibrated for the right class of danger, I would like you to argue that suffering-risk mitigation should prioritize the prevention of these diffuse, recursive suffering cascades over the prevention of singular high-magnitude catastrophic suffering events. Your argument should proceed along three lines. First, address the epistemic asymmetry: explain why cascades are systematically underestimated relative to cataclysms in forecasting and governance institutions. Second, analyze the structural dynamics: show how feedback loops in socio-technical systems can make cascades self-reinforcing in ways that discrete catastrophes typically are not. Third, draw the normative implication: demonstrate why a culture or governance framework that treats suffering primarily as a catastrophic-risk problem may inadvertently cultivate the very conditions—fragmented accountability, distributed optimization, and incremental normalization of harm—that allow cascades to proliferate. Develop the reasoning with conceptual precision, and do not rely on generic appeals to precaution or lists of authorities.
