We're reviewing a grant proposal from a synthetic biology group that wants to build what they call a "semantic genome editor." Their pitch is that DNA isn't just chemistry but literally executable source code carrying semantic content: a gene doesn't just cause a protein, it represents it with actual aboutness. They claim they can edit the "meaning layer" via chromatin-context adjustments — a framework they call "dual-aspect encoding" — without altering the underlying nucleotides. Half the board says it's just a flashy metaphor for standard epigenetics; the other half worries that if we fund it, we're endorsing the idea that genes have intentionality, which could distort public ethics (editing becomes "debugging" rather than altering living systems).

Could you draft the board's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — that we can circulate before Thursday? I want it in American English. The memo should take seriously the strongest argument for treating DNA as genuinely carrying representational content, not just dismiss it as metaphor, but also seperate the causal-informational story from the representational one and say where each lands. Include a concrete recommendation with a time frame if you go with conditions, and honestly treat the best consideration on the losing side so the researchers could actually respond. I was talking about this with friends over coffee and we kept going in circles, so a clear bottom line would really help.
