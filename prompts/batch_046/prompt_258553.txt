I've been retired from physics for a few years and lately I've got into philosphy, particulaly whether the fall of Rome tells us anything about modern democracies. I sketched a little three-stage idea I'm calling 'norm-erosion cascade': first formal rules get stretched (tribunes ignoring vetos and so on), then patronage replaces civic loyalty, then violence becomes the usual way to settle things. Could you analyse the collapse of the Roman Republic from the Gracchi through to Caesar crossing the Rubicon through that lens? I want to know what it actually explains — say, why the senate couldn't halt the drift — and where it clearly falls apart, maybe on the land-reform economics. And honestly, does the case support drawing analogies to modern democracies, or does it show the comparison is mostly empty? No need for bibliographies, just the reasoning.
