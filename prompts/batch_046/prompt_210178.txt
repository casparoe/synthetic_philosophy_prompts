I was taking a course on metaphysics of time and we got onto this in the pub afterwards, and now I cant shake the feeling that my flatmate's argument proves way too much. Here are the steps she laid out about endurantism versus perdurantism, and I need to know where the wheel comes off:

(1) Endurantists say an object is wholly present at each time it exists. (2) At noon my candle is wholly present and 10cm long; at three its wholly present and 8cm long. (3) So the same object, taken without temporal qualification, is both 10cm and not 10cm. (4) The standard reply is that properties are really relations: being-10cm-at-noon, being-8cm-at-three. (5) But then the candle never genuinely instantiates a temporary intrinsic property; it only stands in relations to times. (6) Apply this to mental life, where beliefs are usually treated as temporary intrinsics: at t1 I believe climate change is urgent, and at t2 I rationally revise and believe not-p. (7) If I am wholly present at both times, I either hold contradictory beliefs, or my mental states are just relations like believing-p-at-t1. (8) If they are just relations, then there is no single subject who abandons p and acquires not-p; there are only different temporal parts of me standing in different relations. (9) But genuine rational revision requires one and the same subject to change her mind. (10) So endurantism either makes me contradictory or denies that belief revision ever happens. (11) Worse, if every property is time-relational, then nothing ever genuinely changes—everything is static and only relates differently to different moments, which is basically Parmenideanism wearing a four-dimensional coat. (12) Hence endurantism collapses into denying all change, which is clearly wrong.

I think the mistake is somewhere around (8) or (11)—maybe a hidden slide from mental properties to all properties, or maybe I'm smuggling in diachronic mereological essentialism without noticing—but I cant pin it down. Which premise should I reject, and what does giving it up cost the endurantist? Also, does the argument accidentally damage perdurantism as well, or am I just mixing the two up? Please explain in British English, and work through the logic rather than giving me a bibliography.
