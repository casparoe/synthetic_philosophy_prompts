As a doctoral candidate in political philosophy working on democratic legitimacy, I am trying to calibrate the relative normative strength of four competing formulations of the accessibility constraint governing religious arguments in public deliberation. Rather than assessing them by historical influence, I want them ranked according to their resilience against what I call the reciprocal opacity objection: the claim that requiring religious citizens to translate their comprehensive doctrines into secular terms imposes an asymmetric epistemic burden, since secular citizens are rarely required to render their own comprehensive commitments transparent to religious interlocutors.

The four formulations are as follows:

A. Rawlsian exclusivism: religious reasons are categorically inadmissible in the public justification of constitutional essentials and basic justice unless they can be fully translated into the vocabulary of public reason.

B. The Audi translation principle: citizens ought to supply secular reasons sufficient to justify coercive policies, though religious reasons may continue to provide private motivation; translation is an obligation of civic respect, not a condition of admissibility per se.

C. The convergence model: religious and secular reasons may independently converge on the same coercive policy without either being translated into the other, provided each constituency can recognize the policy as justified from within its own comprehensive doctrine.

D. A modified pluralist stance I am developing: accessibility should be assessed relative to the shared vocabulary of the specific deliberative forum rather than against an idealized secular baseline, meaning religious arguments are admissible if they can be rendered intelligible to reasonable interlocutors within that context, even absent full secular translation.

Please rank these four positions (A through D) from most to least resilient against the reciprocal opacity objection. For each position, assign a robustness score from 1 to 10 and provide a brief justification of no more than two sentences. Then identify which single position is most vulnerable to collapsing into either pure secular exclusivism or unrestricted religious particularism, and explain why in exactly one sentence.
