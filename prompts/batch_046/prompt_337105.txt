I am drafting a proposal for a six-month interdisciplinary workshop fellowship that brings together historians of philosophy and philosophers of time. The core idea grows out of my long-standing concern with Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life—specifically his division of history into monumental, antiquarian, and critical modes—but I want to avoid the well-worn debate about whether Nietzsche was right that history serves life. Instead, I want to treat the three modes as competing temporal ontologies: not merely historiographical styles, but constitutive practices through which a present establishes its ontological relation to what is no longer and what is not yet. My half-formed suspicion is that each mode presupposes a different metaphysics of presence—monumental history requires a present thick enough to bear heroic resemblance; antiquarian requires a continuous substrate of identity across time; critical requires a present capable of radical discontinuity. What I do not yet see is whether these three exhaust the logical space of life-affirming temporal relations, or whether there is a structurally distinct fourth orientation—perhaps one that binds the present to the future rather than the past—that Nietzsche's framework implicitly excludes but that contemporary conditions of total archival retention and immediate disposability make unavoidable. I need a proposal that states this question with enough precision to have a determinate answer, explains why treating the three modes as styles rather than ontological commitments leaves the problem open, sets out a philosophical rather than empirical approach (the deliverable must be usable by historians of ideas without requiring new archival discovery), and specifies what a successful result would look like—perhaps a reconstructed taxonomy with explicit criteria for distinguishing a genuinely new temporal mode from a hybrid of Nietzsche's three—as well as what would count as failure, such as merely redescribing the existing categories in new vocabulary. The scope should be realistic for six months of part-time research alongside teaching, and I want an honest assessment of where the project risks becoming too large. Please do not include a bibliography or citation list; I will supply those myself. I also want the proposal to acknowledge explicitly where judgment rather than argument must carry the weight, particularly in deciding what counts as life-affirming in the first place.
