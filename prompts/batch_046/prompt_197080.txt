I'm working through my lecture notes for a graduate seminar on space-time ontology, and I've sketched out a package of views that I present to the students as my working position, but I'm no longer sure it survives scrutiny without hidden costs. Here is the set: (1) I take general relativity to be correct in treating the metric field as dynamical; (2) I accept that Einstein's hole argument demonstrates a failure of determinism for that field if space-time points possess primitive, non-qualitative identity; (3) I respond not by becoming a relationist but by adopting a structuralist version of substantivalism, on which points are real but individuated solely by the relational and metric properties they bear within the global structure; (4) I nonetheless want to say that two models related by a hole diffeomorphism can represent genuinely different physical possibilities, because the global distribution of matter-energy fields may vary in ways not fixed by the local metric structure alone; and (5) I hold that determinism should be preserved at the level of the laws and the physically invariant state, even if the full mathematical description contains gauge-redundant information that makes the formal initial-value problem underdetermined.

My concern is that these commitments may pull in incompatible directions, and I would like you to audit them as a package rather than defend any one thesis in isolation. Specifically, does (3) actually dissolve the threat in (2), or does it merely relocate the indeterminacy into the structural relations themselves? Is there a severe tension between (4) and (5): if I allow modal distinctions across hole-related worlds, am I treating the gauge-variant descriptions as carrying physical weight in precisely the way that (5) declares redundant? And is there an apparent contradiction between (1) and (4) that turns out to be harmless once we distinguish dynamical symmetry from representational redundancy? Please rank the real tensions by severity, indicate for each what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs, identify any pair that only looks incompatible but dissolves on closer inspection, and tell me which single commitment, if dropped, would do the most to restore consistency without collapsing the whole view.
