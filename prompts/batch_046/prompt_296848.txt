Read Frankenstein through Turing's idea that we should build a child-machine and educate it with a mix of discipline and initative, rather than programming an adult mind from scratch. Shelley's creature basically teaches himself by evesdropping on a family and reading books — there's no teacher giving rewards or setting boundaries. Where does that autodidactic path actually line up with Turing's vision, and where does it fall apart because he thought learning needs structured guidence and play? I'm prepping this for a philosophy of AI seminar and don't want to just use the novel as a prop — does the creature's rapid language acquisition and moral confusion say anything real about whether unguided machine education could work?
