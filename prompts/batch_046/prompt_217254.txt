I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind, and I am preparing an adversarial collaboration framework between two camps on panpsychism and the combination problem. The first camp, which I call the Relational Combinators, maintains that micro-subjects combine into macro-subjects through lawlike phenomenal-bonding relations that preserve micro-phenomenal content without generating genuinely novel macro-properties. The second camp, the Constitutive Emergentists, maintains that macro-consciousness is a genuinely novel determinable constituted but not reducible to arrays of micro-psychic states, and that the combination relation is structural rather than aggregative.

Please draft the joint statement that both camps could sign. Include: (1) a numbered section of substantive agreed claims about panpsychism, the combination problem in both its synchronic and diachronic dimensions, and the present state of the debate—formulated so that neither side can reject them without misrepresenting its own commitments; (2) a disagreements table setting out the precise residual differences, each view phrased in wording that its own proponents would accept and that opponents would recognize as accurate; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would shift at least one camp. I take the central cruxes to be (a) whether macro-phenomenal determinacy requires corresponding determinacy at the micro-level (the granularity-transfer problem) and (b) whether macro-subject diachronic persistence can be explained without primitive cross-temporal identity relations across micro-subject turnover; (4) for each crux, specify a concrete conceptual case or argument both sides accept as bearing on it, together with each camp's explicit prediction about that case—for (a), whether a macro-subject's precise chromatic experience can be sustained when constituent micro-subjects possess only indeterminate phenomenal ranges; for (b), whether gradual cellular-replacement or split-brain scenarios support macro-subject persistence under each model.

Format the document as the numbered agreed-claims section, followed by the disagreements table, followed by the crux analysis with predictions recorded in advance. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; refer to all positions, arguments, and conceptual distinctions by descriptive labels only.
