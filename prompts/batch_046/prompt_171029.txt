Write an essay on whether the simulation argument should actually cool people down about being fanatical for the far future. The usual framing is just "are we in a computer?" but its really about urgency and moral weight. If theres a non-trivial chance we're running inside someone else's server, then all our longtermist planning is for a future that might literally end when the simulation gets paused or deleted — which should weaken the case for extreme sacrafice now for distant outcomes. On the flip side, if we happen to be in base reality, then the values and institutions we lock in now might shape not just our own descendants but an astronomical number of nested simulated civilizations, which seems to crank the urgency up, not down. Theres also a self-reference trap: if you try to dampen concern by saying simulated beings count less, you have to face the possibility that you yourself are simulated, which means you're implicitly treating your own moral claims as second-rate. Weigh these tensions, and don't just waffle — defend a clear verdict on whether taking simulation seriously makes far-future fanaticism more rational or less rational. Also say whether the self-reference problem is fatal or can be solved with some distinction I'm missing.
