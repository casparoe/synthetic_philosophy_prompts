My philosophy-of-science colloquium director, Dr. Mira Solari, has issued the following rulings on puzzles involving observer selection and anthropic reasoning, presented without further commentary:

1. The doomsday argument applied to the total number of humans who will ever live: she rejects the inference from one's birth rank to an imminent extinction, on the grounds that we possess no statistical sampling frame that would justify treating our temporal position as randomly drawn.

2. The presumptuous philosopher scenario, in which a philosopher argues that extreme cosmological fine-tuning licenses belief in a vast multiverse because otherwise our existence would be astronomically improbable: she dismisses the argument, holding that reasoning from one's own existence to the scale of global ontology is circular unless independently empirically anchored.

3. The Sleeping Beauty problem (the standard coin-toss with one awakening under Heads and two under Tails): she rules that Beauty's credence in Heads should remain one-half upon awakening; she treats the number of awakenings as epistemically irrelevant to the coin's bias.

4. The "God's coin" multiverse case, in which a fair coin determines whether ten identical universes or one are created, each containing exactly one observer with the same experiences: she maintains fifty-fifty credence for the coin, refusing to update toward the ten-universe hypothesis on the basis of her own existence.

5. The empirical anthropic prediction of a cosmological constant's value (within a range permitting observers), advanced by physicists who treat the observer-selection effect as a statistical filter rather than an ontological argument: she accepts the prediction as methodologically legitimate, distinguishing it sharply from the multiverse inference in case 2.

Next week's discussion concerns the Archive Case, which I have devised: there are ninety-nine sealed vaults, each containing a single conscious observer who lives for exactly one subjective day with no access to calendars, vault labels, or external duration-cues; and there is one further vault containing a single observer who lives for exactly one thousand subjective days with phenomenologically identical experiences. An observer wakes with no prior information and asks whether she should assign higher credence to being in the long-duration vault, or whether any such assignment is illegitimate.

Predict Dr. Solari's verdict on the Archive Case, identify the candidate anthropic frameworks that could jointly accommodate her first five rulings—such as a strict self-sampling assumption with a fixed reference class, a self-indication assumption applied selectively, a prohibition on presumptuous reasoning that allows empirical filtering, or a hybrid reference-class restriction—state which framework best explains her pattern, say what each predicts about the Archive Case, give your best guess with reasons, and specify one further case that would distinguish the remaining competitors.
