I am preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher who advances a version of compatriot priority she calls institutional modal proximity. On her account, special obligations to compatriots do not rest on shared nationality, culture, or citizenship, but on membership in a bounded network of counterfactual institutional interdependence: a failure at one node propagates through the system with a density that typically does not obtain across borders. From this she infers that cosmopolitan distributive obligations are weaker—not because outsiders matter less intrinsically, but because the requisite modal density is lacking. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that press on the substance of this view rather than invite restatement. I want at least one question on how she distinguishes modal density from ordinary causal or economic interdependence; at least one on immigrants and refugees who enter the network and whether they immediately acquire full associative obligations; and at least one on asymmetric interdependence—such as colonial structures—where failure propagates in one direction but not reciprocally. For each of these three, include a follow-up tailored to her most likely reply, especially her standard deflection that boundaries are merely the contours of the interdependence graph, not moral boundaries. End with a question about what empirical or institutional evidence could, in principle, weaken her claim that modal density tracks moral weight. Write the response in American English.
