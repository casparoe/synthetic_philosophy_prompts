Write a taxonomy—branching by claim rather than by author—of interpretations of societal decline, using the Late Bronze Age, the Maya Classic period, and Easter Island as diagnostic cases. The first split should ask whether “collapse” denotes an objective discontinuity in hierarchical complexity and energy capture, or merely a state-centric narrative imposed on gradual settlement and political reorganization. From there, branch through: whether ecological stress is necessary for decline, whether institutional overextension is sufficient without it, and whether a society can “fail” in a normative sense if its population survives but abandons previous practices. Each leaf should name a defended position if one exists (e.g., complexity-cost fragility, environmental determinism, continuity/transformation) and state its most awkward commitment for at least one case—such as being forced to treat Easter Island’s moai construction as causally irrelevant or to deny that the Maya case involved real rupture. Where two leaves answer every branch identically, flag them as inseparable in this tree. Phrase every node as a concrete question about the three cases, not as a request for a theoretical label, and include one “unnamed” combination with a brief diagnosis of why it might be unoccupied—whether incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
