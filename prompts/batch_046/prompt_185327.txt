I spent thirty-five years in experimental physics, and since retiring I have taken up philosophy with the same expectation that a theoretical proposal should come with an explicit specification of what it is meant to explain before one evaluates its elegance. At present I am working through debates on liberty with a small reading group, and I find that the familiar division between negative and positive freedom — together with the more recent republican addition — is usually defended by pointing to one or two vivid cases rather than by setting out the full conditions that any adequate taxonomy must satisfy. I should therefore like you to set out the complete desiderata for a theory that distinguishes positive, negative, and republican liberty without allowing them to collapse into one another or into purely rhetorical categories.

Specifically, the theory must be able to accommodate three motivating cases with precision: first, the benevolent despot whose subjects suffer no actual interference yet live under an authority that retains the arbitrary power to intervene at will; second, the citizen who enjoys extensive formal options and faces no direct coercion, but whose choices are structured by severe resource scarcity that some accounts treat as a form of unfreedom; and third, the situation of algorithmic governance, in which automated decision-making imposes no physical constraint and offers no identifiable human master, yet produces a systemic dependency that functionally resembles domination. For each desideratum you list, please provide the underlying case or conceptual distinction that makes it non-optional — for example, why the mere absence of interference cannot be sufficient for negative liberty if arbitrary power remains present, or why the presence of capability must be kept distinct from the absence of domination. The theory should also remain consistent with the broad findings of comparative political economy regarding the welfare and stability effects of rule-bound versus arbitrary authority, and it must preserve conceptual space for an agent to be unfree even when both options and non-interference are present.

Finally, please indicate which of these requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which might be traded away for theoretical unity, and identify the single desideratum that does the most damage to the leading contenders — whether Berlin's original framework, the republican revival, or the capability-based reconstructions. If it helps, present a brief table scoring the main positions against your list, but keep the analytical weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring itself.
