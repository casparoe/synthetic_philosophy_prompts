If Gilbert Ryle—who maintained that knowing how is not a species of knowing that—were alive to study the modern educational-psychology literature on the expertise reversal effect, particularly the finding that explicit verbal instruction can temporarily degrade performance in expert musicians and athletes, how do you think he would read it? Would he take it as empirical confirmation that propositional knowledge actively obstructs intelligent practice, or as a processing bottleneck that leaves his logical-grammar distinction intact? Reconstruct his likely position with attention to his anti-intellectualist arguments, and tell me which feature of the data—the transience of the interference, the survival of skill despite amnesia for instructions, or its confinement to specific motor domains—would have forced him to concede the most ground.
