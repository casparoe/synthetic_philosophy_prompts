I have been thinking on my own about whether I could have come from a different sperm and egg—whether my very existence is fixed to those particular gametes or only to the general biological process that produced me. Without any formal training in philosophy, I have tried to read a little on the question and now host a small independent discussion series where I interview people about ideas that interest me. Next week I am speaking with a philosopher who defends a specific, somewhat unusual position: he accepts that human organisms have their origins essentially—so that I could not have developed from a different egg and sperm and still be this same organism—but he denies that personal identity works the same way, because he holds that being the same person is a matter of psychological continuity rather than of genetic or biological origin. I want you to write me a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that turn on the actual content of this split view, not on biography or background. I want at least one question that tests the boundary between organism and person—perhaps by asking how he treats cases of brain transplantation, or cases in which an early embryo splits to produce twins, so that two persons share an original gametic pair. I want at least one question that presses the standard reply that psychological continuity itself may depend on the biological origin he dismisses for persons. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give: for example, if he appeals to a functionalist theory of mind to separate psychology from biology, the follow-up should ask whether that same functionalism undermines the biological essentialism he wants to keep for organisms. The questions should assume no access to outside texts or citations, should not ask for a bibliography or reading list, and should avoid invoking obscure named theories except where the view itself requires it. Please make the questions genuinely probing: anticipate his defenses and push past them, so that the conversation moves into the weeds rather than staying at the level of restating the position.
