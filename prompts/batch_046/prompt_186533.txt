At the same time, this language lacks explicit modal operators—no auxiliary verbs marking possibility, necessity, or counterfactuality. Rather, modal force is expressed solely through evidential suffixes that encode the speaker’s epistemic source (direct perception, inference, hearsay) without grammaticalizing the distinction between what is actual, possible, or necessary.

My colleague insists that this is merely a notational inconvenience: speakers can still represent the full range of modal-quantificational thoughts through periphrasis, discourse-level negotiation, or pragmatic repair strategies, so the absence is superficial. I am less convinced. Please do not provide a bibliography or reading list; I am trying to work out the conceptual architecture myself. I would especially value an explanation that addresses how a speaker of such a language might navigate a reasoning task that requires holding quantifier scope and modal status fixed and independent—say, evaluating whether every possible instance of a property necessarily entails some other property—without the grammatical scaffolding that typically isolates those dimensions in languages like English or standard logical notation.
