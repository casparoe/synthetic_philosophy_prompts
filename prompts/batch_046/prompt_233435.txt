Write a six-month workshop grant proposal addressing one conceptual question in the philosophy of history: under what conditions can a non-human event—such as the 1815 Tambora eruption or the introduction of Yersinia pestis—function as a historical actor without either collapsing into environmental determinism or being reduced to an inert background condition? State the gap precisely: current environmental historiography often deploys actor-language metaphorically, while critics of determinism deny nature any causal weight, leaving no rigorous middle ground for efficacy without intentional agency. Propose a philosophical rather than empirical approach that distinguishes structural efficacy—the event reshapes the constraint-space within which human decisions occur—from agential efficacy, in which the event directs outcomes. Specify the deliverable: a three-part decision framework historians can apply before attributing historical weight to a climatic or microbial event, illustrated by two worked cases, one permitting the attribution and one blocking it. Define failure: if the framework cannot distinguish Tambora’s impact on the post-1816 agricultural crisis from routine weather variation, the distinction is useless. Outline a realistic six-month scope, indicate where references to recent historiography will be inserted by the applicant, and do not request a bibliography, reading list, or citation-based literature review.
