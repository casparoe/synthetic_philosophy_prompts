I am drafting a position paper on population axiology and need a consistency audit on the package below before I defend it in print. Here is where I stand:

(1) I hold a critical-level total view: other things equal, a world is improved by adding people whose lives are well above a welfare threshold T, with the improvement scaled to how far above T they are, and worsened by adding lives below T.

(2) I reject the repugnant conclusion, but not by appealing to brute intuition. Instead, I invoke a structural comparability constraint: cardinal welfare comparisons across worlds are blocked when their social architectures differ radically (for example, an egalitarian society versus a rigid caste system), so the standard quality-for-quantity tradeoff that generates the conclusion is illegitimate.

(3) I deny all person-affecting restrictions. I think the idea that failing to create a happy person harms that non-existent person is metaphysically incoherent, and I reject asymmetries that depend on such reasoning.

(4) Despite (3), I accept a procreative asymmetry: we have strong reasons not to create lives below T, but only weak, easily defeasible reasons to create lives well above T.

(5) I take reflective equilibrium to be the correct method in this domain, though I discount intuitive verdicts about astronomically large populations on the grounds that our moral cognition evolved for small-scale cooperation.

(6) I think climate and reproductive ethics should share this framework: preserving conditions for near-future lives well above T can justify present mitigation costs, but there is no obligation to maximize the number of such lives.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify the cheapest revision—whether reconceiving, weakening, or abandoning one of the commitments—and what that revision costs. Then tell me which single revision, applied consistently, would clean up the most conflicts at once. Finally, say explicitly what would change your mind about this audit: which feature of my set, if shown flawed, would most undermine your assessment, and where in your reasoning is failure most likely?
