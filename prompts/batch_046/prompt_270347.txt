Draft the joint statement that a committed biostatistical naturalist and a committed welfare-based normativist about health and disease could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The naturalist holds that disease is an internal state reducing functional ability below the statistical mean for an appropriate reference class, with normative judgments playing only an epistemic or pragmatic role; the normativist holds that disease is essentially a state that tends to reduce an organism's ability to achieve objectively valuable vital goals, so evaluation is constitutive rather than derivative.

Produce a document with three parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims that both sides can sign without reservation—include at least: that neither camp accepts vitalist essentialism or metaphysically primitive health-properties; that both accept evolutionary history shapes physiological norms but disagree on whether evolutionary design determines normative status; that both reject the claim that subjective suffering is necessary for disease, though they differ on whether it is sufficient or irrelevant; and that both agree boundary cases such as aging, pregnancy, and adaptive phenotypic variation must be accommodated without ad hoc adjustments.

Second, a disagreements table with two columns, "Naturalist position (wording this side accepts)" and "Normativist position (wording this side accepts)." Each entry should state the precise formulation—particularly whether statistical abnormality alone can constitute disease in the absence of harm; whether vital goals are determinable without circular appeal to statistical norms; and whether a fully compensated, asymptomatic, statistically abnormal condition should count as pathological.

Third, identify two cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party. The first crux is the silent-dysfunction case: a congenital metabolic variant reduces enzymatic efficiency well below the statistical mean for the reference class, yet is fully compensated by alternative pathways, produces no symptoms, causes no reduction in lifespan or reproductive success, and is evaluated by the bearer as entirely unproblematic. Record in advance what each side predicts about whether this is a disease and on what grounds. The second crux is the adaptive-normalization problem: in an isolated population, a mutation that eliminates a sensory modality spreads to fixation, removing both the sensory function and a linked autoimmune vulnerability; over generations, statistical norms shift to reflect the new phenotype. Record each side's prediction about whether the reference class should be updated—making the condition statistically normal and thus, for the naturalist, non-diseased—or whether an ancestral functional baseline should be retained, and whether the normativist's evaluation of vital goals should reference the ancestral or the current state.

Conclude with a brief assessment: is any part of the dispute merely verbal—e.g., about whether function means statistical contribution to survival and reproduction versus welfare-conducive capacity—and if so, specify exactly which part and why the remaining disagreement is substantive. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
