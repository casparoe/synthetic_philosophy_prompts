In my metaphysics seminar on persistence, I am tired of seeing endurantism and perdurantism evaluated only through the ship of Theseus and standard brain-fission vignettes. Before I compare them, I want the full specification: what must any adequate theory of diachronic persistence actually get right? Give me exactly five desiderata in a numbered list, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion. For each item, state the criterion, the motivating case or distinction behind it (such as being wholly present at a time versus spread across it, or biological maintenance versus psychological continuity), and whether it is negotiable. One must address gradual part-replacement in living organisms without collapsing into stage-theory; another must address asymmetric psychological continuity paired with symmetric biological continuity in fission. Within the list, identify which single requirement eliminates the majority of hybrid four-dimensionalist compromises.
