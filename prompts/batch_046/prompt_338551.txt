Write a joke that engages with Fitch's paradox of knowability, concentrating on the tension between the knowability principle—that every truth is in principle knowable—and the paradoxical derivation that, if so, every truth is already actually known. To keep the task away from well-worn expositions, frame the joke around an invented conceit: a modal-logic librarian who sets out to catalog the one remaining unarchived proposition, specifically that there exists at least one unknown truth, only to realize that placing it in the index makes it known and thereby erases the very entry she sought to preserve. The joke should be conceptually coherent for someone familiar with basic epistemic logic, should not invoke citations, bibliographies, or obscure secondary literature, and should be composed in a relatively formal and polished register.
