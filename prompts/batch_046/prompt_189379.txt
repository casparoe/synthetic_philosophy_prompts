I am preparing a fifty-minute seminar for advanced undergraduate philosophy students who have worked through Newcomb's problem but have not yet encountered Parfit's hitchhiker. The goal is not to rehearse the canonical scenario but to teach why rationality might require cultivating a genuinely irrational disposition, using a modified framing that avoids the usual alien-driver setup. Plan the session's explanatory arc: first, identify the conceptual dependency that must be secured before the hitchhiker argument can land—specifically, why the evidential versus causal reasoning distinction is insufficient on its own and how the transparency of one's own deliberative process alters the strategic structure in a way Newcomb's boxes do not capture. Second, specify the one carrying example: design a concrete scenario (distinct from both Newcomb's boxes and the standard hitchhiker) in which an observer can verify the agent's reasoning trace prior to the decisive action and in which the reward or penalty is contingent on that trace rather than on the action alone; state exactly what properties this scenario must possess and which familiar features it must deliberately lack to prevent misanalogy. Third, map the predictable misconceptions in the order they will arise—likely beginning with the collapse of the case into Newcomb, then the belief that the agent can merely feign irrationality, then the assumption that precommitment necessarily requires an external physical binding mechanism—and indicate precisely where in the sequence each should be interrupted and corrected. Fourth, provide a single closing diagnostic question that distinguishes students who understand that the rational agent must actually become irrational in the relevant respect from those who only understand that she should perform an irrational act. Finally, state explicitly which elements of the standard discussion of commitment devices and binding mechanisms should be set aside at this level and justify each omission, without supplying a bibliography or reading list.
