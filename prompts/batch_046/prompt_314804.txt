I am currently taking an advanced seminar on consciousness and temporal perception, and we have been examining Dennett's well-known distinction between Orwellian and Stalinesque revisions in the context of the colour phi phenomenon—the perceptual illusion in which two rapidly presented dots of different colours, appearing at separate spatial locations, are experienced as a single moving object that changes hue in the intermediate space. Although the psychophysical parameters are largely settled and both sides of our seminar accept the same basic neurophysiological timeline—namely that cortical binding operates on a scale of roughly 80 to 120 milliseconds—the disagreement has become completely static. One group insists that the intermediate colour is genuinely present in experience but is subsequently overwritten by a later editorial process (the Stalinesque position), while the other maintains that there is never any intermediate phenomenal content at all, only a retrospective confabulation constructed in report and memory (the Orwellian position).

What interests me is whether this stall is genuinely empirical or whether it conceals a deeper conceptual divergence about the internal structure of the phenomenal present—specifically, whether phenomenal time is composed of discrete, frame-like atomic units that can be individually inserted or excised, or whether it forms an indivisible field that can only be retrospectively segmented. If the atomic model holds, Stalinesque editing is conceptually coherent; if the field model holds, Orwellian confabulation becomes unavoidable. Could the disagreement therefore be about the metaphysics of phenomenal time rather than about colour phi itself? Or is there a hidden empirical question—perhaps concerning the temporal windows of cross-modal integration—that might resolve it? I ask you to diagnose the nature of this impasse: is it verbal, substantive, or a case of different questions pursued under one heading? Please support your diagnosis with reference to what each side actually maintains, indicate what would count as progress—whether a formal distinction, a conceptual clarification, or an argument that resolution is impossible—and present your answer in British English.
