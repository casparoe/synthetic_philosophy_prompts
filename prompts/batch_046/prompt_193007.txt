As part of my sixth-form coursework in philosophy and ethics, I am examining whether modern penal systems should prioritise punishment or rehabilitation. I should like you to imagine that Cesare Beccaria—the eighteenth-century Italian philosopher who defended proportionate, publicly visible penalties determined at the point of sentencing—were alive today and confronted with two contemporary models: Norway's Halden prison, which operates on a principle of normalisation through small-scale domestic architecture, shared cooking facilities, and prison officers trained primarily as social mentors; and the American system of mass incarceration, which relies on high-security design, lengthy mandatory sentences, and an institutional logic centred on incapacitation rather than social reintegration.

Rather than asking for a straightforward comparison, I want you to apply Beccaria's reasoning to a specific, as-yet-unimplemented institutional proposal: an adaptive penal environment housed within a single maximum-security facility, in which the physical conditions of confinement—from isolated, locked cells to open-plan communal living—are continuously modified by an administrative board according to real-time behavioural assessments. Under this design, the severity of the prisoner's daily environment is not fixed by the court but fluctuates with demonstrated progress, so that one individual might experience both extreme restriction and near-domestic freedom during the course of a single sentence.

Please predict whether Beccaria would endorse or reject this adaptive model, with particular attention to his conviction that punishment must be certain, proportionate to the offence, and fixed in advance in order to function as a public deterrent. You should also consider how he might regard the Norwegian emphasis on social integration in contrast to the American emphasis on security, but your central task is to reconstruct his argument in relation to this niche design. Please do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I am interested only in the reconstructed philosophical position.
