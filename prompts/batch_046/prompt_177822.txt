Please argue for the position that Dennett's two-bitser, once embedded in a self-modifying feedback loop coupled to an environment but lacking any external interpreter or designer, undermines Searle's sharp division between original and derived intentionality—not by elevating derived intentionality to original status, but by showing that the conditions of derivation depend on functional roles that can be realized entirely within the system. Specifically, defend the claim that if the network can stabilize and correct its own symbolic outputs through environmental feedback alone, then Searle's requirement that derived intentionality presupposes a mind with original intentionality smuggles in an unjustified observer-dependence. Anticipate and respond to the objection that without intrinsic mental content, such a loop remains merely causal rather than genuinely intentional, and explain why that objection either begs the question against functionalist accounts of intentionality or forces Searle to concede that original intentionality can exist in the absence of consciousness.
