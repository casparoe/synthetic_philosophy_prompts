We've been talking about Zhuangzi's butterfly dream and I feel like everyone just nods and says "haha maybe we're dreaming" then moves on, without actually feeling the disorientation. I want an in-class activity with no lecture up front, where they run into the skeptical problem themselves — not just "how do you know you're awake" but specifically the Zhuangzian twist that the self asking isn't stable, that the boundary between "me" and "the butterfly" is more like a flow or transformation than a binary switch.

Can you design a game or structured simulation for this? It needs rules, maybe hidden roles or rounds where people make commitments based on what they think is waking reality, and then something inside the activity undermines that framework so they experience the blur firsthand. They shouldn't hear "Zhuangzi" or "butterfly dream" until the debrief. The debrief should map specific moments onto the epistemic skepticism vs the ontological dissolution of self, tell me what will predictably go wrong — like people treating it as just a logic puzzle — and be honest about which feature of Zhuangzi's view, probably the cheerful acceptance of transformation rather than anxiety, the activity can't reproduce and you'll have to supply. Also please don't include any bibliographies or citation lists, since half the group hasn't read past the excerpt and I don't want anyone guessing at page numbers. I was chatting with a friend who said dream skepticism always gets taught through Descartes, so I want this to feel different — more about identity flow than about certainty and knowledge — and maybe build that contrast into the rules somehow.
