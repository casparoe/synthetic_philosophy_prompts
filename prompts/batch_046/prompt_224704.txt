I sit on the Interdisciplinary Cataloging Council of our university's research library. We must decide whether to keep two separate philosophy subject branches—'Natural Kinds' and 'Social Kinds'—or merge them into one 'Kinds and Classification' branch. One faction of faculty claims the distinction is a purely verbal dispute: swapping the labels in every acquisition, shelving, and retrieval protocol changes nothing about how materials are actually handled. The other faction insists the split tracks a substantive metaontological difference that genuinely guides researcher navigation.

Please draft the short formal opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It must recommend a concrete course of action—merge, retain with conditions, or restructure—together with a clear implementation time frame (for example, a six-month pilot or a decision deadline). The reasoning should apply what I call the 'institutional substitution criterion': if two category labels can be interchanged in all practical library decisions, including counterfactual retrieval cases where a researcher searches using one label and expects results filed under the other, without altering any institutional outcome, then maintaining separate branches commits us to an unnecessary ontological distinction. At the same time, you must treat the strongest consideration on the losing side with complete honesty—whether that is the risk that retrieval behavior is itself ontologically loaded, or that merging could erase distinctions researchers genuinely depend on even when staff treat the labels interchangeably. Address the memo to the Council, write in British English, and do not include any bibliography, reading list, or citation requests.
