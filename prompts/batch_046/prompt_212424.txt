I'm giving the retirement toast for Dr. Marcus Chen, a philosopher of science who served for twenty years as both a department chair and a researcher in value theory. About half the room are philosophers familiar with debates about incommensurable values; the rest are family, staff, and former students who are not. Write a five-minute toast built around a specific, hypothetical 2018 decision: Chen dissolved a well-funded corporate partnership that would have expanded access but diluted rigor, in order to preserve a small, unfashionable graduate seminar. Frame this not as a calculated trade-off on a common scale, but as a hard choice between genuinely incommensurable goods—access and rigor—where commitment to one value generates reasons rather than discovers pre-existing weights. The speech must celebrate his career without ranking his achievements, must avoid any language implying he "optimized" his life, and must be intelligible to non-philosophers without condescending to them. Please state how confident you are in this framing of incommensurability and reason-creation, and single out the specific claim you are least sure holds up philosophically.
