I'm on the review board for our uni's macro-history funding scheme. We've got a proposal called 'Why Britain and Not China? Re-evaluating the Needham Puzzle Through Contrastive Institutional Mapping.' The PI wants to treat the Great Divergence as a single well-formed question—basicaly asking why industrial take-off clustered in Britain rather than the Yangtze delta, using a six-variable institutional scorecard applied symetrically to both regions. Half the board think this is standard comparative history. The other half—including me—worry there's a deeper conceptual problem: framing it as 'why X not Y' assumes Britain and Qing China are unitary actors in a shared causal field, and that the counterfactual 'China industrialises first' is stable enough to support causal attribution. But actualy, if you vary the institutional cluster in Britain, the conditions that make China comparable might collapse, so the contrastive framing could be generating spurious causal claims rather than explaining them. There's also the issue that Needham's original baseline—shared scientific knowledge—doesn't survive regional disaggregation in Chinese economic history. I need a short memo for Thursday's meeting recommending fund, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with criterai the PI could respond to. Write in British English, don't ask for citations or a reading list—just argue through whether the contrastive framing is sound or broken, and deal seriously with the view that it's perfectly legitimate comparative work.
