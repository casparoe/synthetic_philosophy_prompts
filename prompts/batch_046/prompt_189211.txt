I've mostly bought Dennett's line that the self is just a center of narrative gravity, but I've been playing with a tweak: what if the gravitational pull isn't locked inside one skull but distributed across other people's stories about you, your texts, your digital traces? So the self becomes more like a fictional character whose center of mass is partly outside any single brain. What would actually count against this distributed version? Don't just hit me with 'but I feel like an inner subject'—I want the serious defeaters. Maybe neuroscience showing self-modeling is tightly localized, or cases where you recieve conflicting external narratives so violent that no coherent center exists, or problems for moral responisibility if the 'center' is partly in other people's mouths. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward. Also, which ones would a defender of this view concede actually hurt them, and which ones would they dismiss as missing the point? I'm wondering if this risks anything real or if its been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that's a problem for a view of this kind.
