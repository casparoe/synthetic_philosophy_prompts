I'm working up my notes for a seminar on democratic boundaries and want a consistency check before I present. Here's where I stand:
(a) the all-affected-interests principle should govern membership — if collective decisions reshape your life, you get a say;
(b) birthright citizenship is basically feudal luck, no more justified than inheriting a title;
(c) democracy needs a shared political culture and mutual commitment, not just preference aggregation — there's no demos without a Schicksalsgemeinschaft or shared fate-structure;
(d) immigrants should eventually get full rights, but only after a genuine integretion period, not automatically at the border;
(e) every boundary line is somewhat arbitary — there's no metaphysically correct demos — yet institutions have to draw one anyway.

Which pairs actually clash, which just look like they do? I worry (a) and (c) pull in opposite directions, and that (b) makes (d) look hypocritical since residency time is just another arbitary cutoff. Rank the tensions by severity and say what the cheapest revision is. Don't treat them all as equal — I need to know which are fatal and which dissolve on closer look.
