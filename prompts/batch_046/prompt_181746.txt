Write out the desiderata any adequate theory of compatriot partiality has to satisfy if it wants to justify special moral claims without collapsing into bare tribalism or a cosmopolitanism so thin it denies any weight to shared institutions. For each one, give the motivating case or distinction behind it -- explaining why we owe more to fellow citizens in distributive justice but not in basic rescue, respecting that national membership is usually non-voluntary, being consistent with the fact that compatriots are often strangers, and not letting the account become unfalsifiable by redefining 'compatriot' whenever a counterexample shows up. Mark which are hard constraints (like not violating the equal moral worth of outsiders) and which are camp-specific preferences (like requiring partiality to be voluntary, or grounding obligations only at the state level). I especially want you to flag whether there's a jointly unsatisfiable cluster -- say, demanding that special claims be both non-voluntary and fully justifiable to outsiders, while also generating strong distributive duties. There's also this exernal beneficiary scenario I keep coming back to: someone who uses a nation's infrastructure and legal system but holds citizenship elsewhere and lives abroad. Do they get partial claims, do they owe partial duties, both, neither? Make sure one of your desiderata handles that case, because I think it breaks a lot of assocative accounts. If there's a subset that's jointly unsatisfiable given standard liberal constraints, say so -- that's the part I'm most interested in.
