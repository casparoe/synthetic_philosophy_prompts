I am a PhD candidate in normative political theory. In my job-market paper, I defend what I call a separated-auction model: a capped, anonymized preference auction limited to non-binding advisory votes, with all surplus captured by a public trust and redistributed as civic vouchers rather than retained by buyers. I argue that standard repugnance-based objections to vote commodification conflate symbolic degradation with actual corruption, and that if market exchange is structurally separated from status competition—through anonymity, binding caps on expenditure, and state capture of revenue—the mechanism does not undermine civic equality in the way critics fear. The thesis is not that voting should become a consumer good, but that a narrowly designed auction can reveal intensity of preference for advisory policy without purchasing binding influence.
