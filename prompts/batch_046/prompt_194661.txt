I am drafting a philosophical dialogue for a novel in which an aging astrophysicist and a skeptical archivist debate whether a discredited cosmological model can genuinely lie closer to the truth than its rival. To keep their disagreement structurally precise rather than rhetorical, I need a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on the problem of verisimilitude. The root commitment should be that false theories can be ordered by proximity to a true complete description of the world.

Build the tree as a sequence of binary splits. Label each branch with a concrete claim or comparison case on which the positions genuinely come apart, phrased so that a reader could answer it without already knowing the philosophical labels.

First split: Is proximity measured solely by the logical inventory of true and false consequences—what I will call the content-based family—or by something non-logical, such as structural resemblance, predictive accuracy, or pragmatic utility?

Within the content-based family, include this comparison: Theory C correctly predicts all observed planetary motions but posits an invisible crystalline sphere; Theory D misses every prediction by a small consistent margin yet avoids all commitments to unobservable entities. The branch should ask whether C can still outrank D.

Next, ask how the family addresses the logical obstacle that one can always augment a false theory with an arbitrary true disjunction, thereby seeming to improve its score without improving its substance. Sub-branches should distinguish those who block the maneuver by restricting evaluation to minimal or explanatory theories, those who weight consequences by relevance or depth, and those who abandon quantitative counting altogether.

Within the non-content family, split between structural accounts—proximity as similarity of law-structure or causal architecture—and predictive or instrumental accounts—proximity as domain-specific empirical accuracy. Note whether these two can be separated in practice, and flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot distinguish.

For every leaf, provide either a named position (use names only if you are certain of the attribution; otherwise label the view descriptively) and one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Identify any unoccupied combinations—such as pure quantitative counting paired with no restriction mechanism—and diagnose briefly whether the vacancy stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.

As a cross-cutting axis of my own design, distinguish epistemic proximity (how near the theory is to being fully knowable or justifiable) from ontological proximity (how near its posited entities are to real ones). Indicate which leaves can accommodate this distinction and which treat the two as inseparable.

Keep the taxonomy formal and polished, suitable for insertion into a character's notebook or an appendix to the novel. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Do not invoke hyper-obscure technical results unavailable to a small model; rely instead on conceptual distinctions and the concrete comparison cases given above.
