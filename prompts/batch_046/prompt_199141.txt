Consider the following five introspective judgements that a person might sincerely make about their own mental life: (A) that they are presently feeling a precise shade of melancholy rather than ordinary fatigue; (B) that their refusal of a recent request was motivated by a principled objection rather than by social anxiety; (C) that they harbour no unconscious prejudice against a specific social group; (D) that their present conscious experience possesses no temporal thickness beyond an instantaneous present; and (E) that they genuinely recall being present at a particular past event, as distinct from having reconstructed that apparent memory from a photograph encountered afterwards.

Rank these five judgements from the most to the least epistemically reliable as deliverances of introspection alone, assigning each a numerical score between 0 and 10. For each entry, provide a concise justification that identifies the specific introspective mechanism presumed to be operative and the principal source of error, confabulation, or theoretical contamination that weakens or strengthens its claim to reliability.
