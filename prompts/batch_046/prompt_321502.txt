Please argue that Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility undermines not only libertarian free will but also a standard compatibilist defense that grounds moral desert in present-tense reasons-responsiveness. Specifically, defend the claim that the regress Strawson identifies—true responsibility for an action requires true responsibility for the way one is, which requires true responsibility for prior causes, and so on without end—applies with equal force to the very capacities that make reasons-responsiveness possible. If an agent's ability to recognize, weigh, and respond to moral reasons is itself the outcome of genetic endowment, early development, and social conditioning for which she bears no ultimate responsibility, then merely exercising that capacity in the present moment does not restore the sourcehood that genuine blame or praise requires. Counter the objection that this confuses responsibility for possessing a capacity with responsibility for exercising it: show why, if moral assessment is to be more than instrumentally useful, the capacity itself—not only its exercise—must lie within the agent's legitimate sphere of accountability. You may treat the general structure of the Basic Argument as familiar, but do not invoke specific textual passages, page references, or bibliographic citations.
