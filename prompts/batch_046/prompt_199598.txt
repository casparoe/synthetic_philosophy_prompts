I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for many years, and one pattern I keep noticing is that metaphysical debates eventually reach a state of highly polished stalemate: the standard moves are all well mapped, every participant can predict the counter-arguments, and yet nobody updates their view by more than a fraction. The presentism versus eternalism dispute about the ontology of time seems to me a textbook case. For decades philosophers have cycled through the same objections—the challenge from special relativity to any globally privileged present, the truthmaker argument for past and future entities, worries about whether temporal passage can be coherently described, and appeals to the phenomenology of change—without any sign of convergence. My own working hypothesis is that the deadlock is not genuinely ontological but concerns a hidden disagreement about explanatory debt: specifically, whether our asymmetrical epistemic access to the past and future requires an ontologically privileged present, or whether that asymmetry can be fully accounted for by causal and cognitive mechanisms within an eternalist block. But I am genuinely unsure whether this captures the real structure of the disagreement, or whether I am imposing a rationalist-style framing where none belongs.
