After a long argument with a friend about Zhuangzi's butterfly dream, I am left with a confusion that seems to turn on a hidden contradiction. She interpreted the passage as an early challenge to diachronic personal identity: if Zhuang Zhou can wake up unsure whether he was a man dreaming he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming it is a man, then there is no metaphysical fact that fixes which mental life is the 'real' one, and the self dissolves into incompatible perspectives. I replied that this mistakes an epistemic limitation—merely not knowing which state one occupies—for ontological indeterminacy about who one is, and that Zhuangzi is concerned with the transformation of things rather than with Cartesian-style doubt. She then pressed that, if transformation is genuinely ontological, the principle of the indiscernibility of identicals must fail across temporal stages, since the same subject is said to contain both human and butterfly forms without contradiction. I now wonder whether the dream actually commits us to denying that identity is transitive over time, or whether we can preserve it by reconceiving identity as relational rather than intrinsic. Specifically, if we read the passage not as scepticism but as a claim about the fluidity of selfhood, are we obliged to reject Leibniz's law for diachronic persons, or is my friend importing a modern metaphysical apparatus—intrinsic versus relational properties, transitive identity conditions—that the text never intended to invoke?
