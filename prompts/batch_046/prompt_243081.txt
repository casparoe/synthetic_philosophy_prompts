I'm currently drafting a term paper for an advanced metaphysics seminar, and I've become stuck on what strikes me as a serious tension in David Lewis's counterpart theory, particularly in light of his well-known approach to temporary intrinsics. As I understand his view, Lewis addresses accidental intrinsics—such as an object being bent at one time and straight at another—by treating enduring things as four-dimensional wholes composed of temporal parts, rather than as three-dimensional entities that literally undergo change. I have begun to wonder whether his counterpart theory applies an analogous structure across possible worlds: just as there is no single three-dimensional object that is both bent and straight, perhaps there is no single individual who is both a musician and not a musician, but only world-bound stages or parts related by resemblance. But this analogy seems to create a problem for essential properties. If I am never literally present in any world other than the actual one—only represented there by counterparts—then being essentially human appears to reduce to the claim that all my world-bound representations share humanity, which sounds more like an extrinsic relational pattern than a genuine intrinsic essential feature.

This led me to think that counterpart theory does not merely reject Saul Kripke's transworld identity; it transforms essential properties into cross-world statistical regularities and thereby undermines de re modal predication altogether. Given that we routinely make modal judgments about particular individuals—such as considering whether a specific person could have had different parents, or whether a specific artifact might have been composed of different material—should I conclude that Lewis's framework is incompatible with essentialism and that Kripkean transworld identity is the only viable option? Or is there a principled way, within a counterpart-theoretic metaphysics, to maintain that particular things possess intrinsic essential and accidental modal properties without assuming that they are literally duplicated across worlds?
