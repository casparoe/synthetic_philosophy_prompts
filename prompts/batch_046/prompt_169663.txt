I'm taking a graduate seminar on the epistemology of mathematics, and after working through Benacerraf's 1973 argument I find myself stuck on what seems like an underappreciated skeptical conclusion. Benacerraf frames his challenge as a dilemma for the platonist: either mathematical objects exist and are causally inert, in which case a causal theory of knowledge cannot account for our access to them, or they do not exist, in which case we need some other explanation for the apparent truth and applicability of mathematics. Most commentators treat this as a problem specifically for platonism that invites either a rationalist reply, a nominalist retreat, or a structuralist compromise. But to me the argument looks symmetrical and more damaging: the platonist retains the objects but forfeits knowledge of them, while the nominalist retains our cognitive practices but forfeits the truth-makers that would make those practices genuinely knowledge-producing rather than merely instrumentally successful. So rather than offering a choice between two live positions, hasn't Benacerraf shown that mathematical knowledge—understood as justified true belief about an independent domain—is impossible on either ontology?

I have tried to block this reading by appealing to a practice-based distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that: perhaps mathematical competence is a skillful mastery of formal rules that does not require propositional knowledge of objects. Yet even if this saves the platonist from the causal-access problem, it seems to leave the nominalist without any account of why those rules yield truths rather than useful fictions. Is my skeptical reading a misinterpretation of Benacerraf's structure, or does the dilemma genuinely collapse into a form of mathematical skepticism that a practice-based epistemology cannot resolve? And if the latter, does that mean the real philosophical task is not choosing between platonism and nominalism, but explaining how mathematical practice can constitute knowledge even when both traditional ontologies have been undermined?
