Analyze the 1989–1991 dissolution of the Soviet Union and the collapse of the Warsaw Pact through Karl Popper’s argument in *The Poverty of Historicism* that there are no unconditional laws of historical development. Treat the Marxist-Leninist historicist thesis—that capitalism’s internal contradictions made its eventual collapse inevitable—as the specific law being tested. Map the episode precisely: identify the relevant actors (Gorbachev, the Polish Solidarity leadership, East German authorities, republican nationalist movements), the decision points where contingency was visible (the refusal to deploy Soviet tanks in 1989, the accidental press-conference opening of the Berlin Wall, the rapid institutional unraveling after the August 1991 coup failed), and the outcomes a deterministic law should have foreclosed but did not. Then say what Popper’s framework explains well about this failure of prediction, what it leaves silent (for instance, whether structural economic stagnation still functioned as a constraining background condition), and whether the episode falsifies historicism generally or only this particular law. To push further, distinguish unconditional predictive laws of history from path-dependent structural regularities that emerge only after a critical juncture has been crossed; apply both categories to the Soviet collapse, and say whether Popper’s critique applies with equal force to each, or whether the episode reveals that one form survives his argument.
