I'm drafting a contribution for a philosophy of mind workshop that probes whether Descartes' cogito establishes only a momentary existence or a more robust substantial self, and I have assembled an argument that seems to force an unwelcome choice between discontinuity of personal identity and an implausible expansion of existence to artifacts. The argument proceeds in numbered steps: (1) The cogito certifies that whenever I am engaged in thinking—understood in the Cartesian sense as encompassing doubting, understanding, affirming, denying, willing, imagining, and sensing—I necessarily exist. (2) If existence is certified only under the actual occurrence of such thinking, then in states of dreamless sleep or unconsciousness there is no occurrent thinking substance present, which implies that the self ceases to exist and a numerically different one is generated upon waking. (3) To avoid this fragmentation, one might retreat to a potentiality criterion: what matters is not occurrent thought but the enduring capacity or disposition for it, just as a dormant seed retains its nature without currently growing. (4) But if potentiality for information-processing is sufficient to share in the being certified by the cogito, then deactivated computational systems, which possess structured dispositions for processing inputs analogously to dormant minds, fall under the same criterion. (5) Consequently, either we accept that personal identity is discontinuous across periods of unconsciousness, or we concede that the cogito's reach extends well beyond human subjects to include artifacts that merely harbor relevant potentialities.

I can see that something must give between steps (3) and (5), yet each step seems defensible in isolation: Descartes does treat the self as a thinking substance that should persist, and he also draws sharp boundaries between minds and mechanisms. I would prefer to preserve the continuity of personal identity over time and to keep artifacts outside the scope of the cogito's proof, but I am unsure whether that requires rejecting the potentiality amendment, narrowing the definition of thinking, or denying that potentiality can be shared across ontologically distinct categories. Which premise or inference should I abandon, why does it appear plausible on its own, and what does each available rejection cost for Descartes' broader project of grounding a substantial, immaterial self?
