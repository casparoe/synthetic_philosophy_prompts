Design a 75-minute session for a philosophy-of-physics reading group of six graduate students who have read Einstein’s 1913–1915 correspondence on general covariance but have not yet worked through the hole argument in detail. Create an in-class prediction game that makes the participants feel the force of the argument before anyone names it. Divide the group into two teams of three: the “Labelers” and the “Relationalists.” Provide each team with identical grid-paper “manifold” sheets on which a metric field is drawn everywhere except inside a central blank “hole.” Give the Labelers a set of persistent identity tags that they affix to individual grid points; give the Relationalists only boundary-condition cards and relational field-value markers, with no point-tags. Introduce a scripted “active transformation” card that shifts the interior field values within the hole while leaving the exterior fixed, and run three rounds of play. In each round, both teams must place “confidence tokens” (graded chips, not real money) betting on the local curvature at specified interior locations in the next round. The payoff rule is: correct local predictions earn tokens; appeals to global point-identity to justify a prediction lose tokens if the boundary conditions do not uniquely determine that identity inside the hole. Arrange the rules so that the teams initially agree on every exterior measurement, then diverge inside the hole. The Labelers should suffer a predictable coordination failure: they assign different identity tags to what they each call “the same point” after the transformation, make contradictory curvature predictions, and yet each insists they are tracking an unchanged spacetime point—while the Relationalists, lacking point-tags, converge on a single relational prediction but feel unable to say “where” it occurs. Include precise materials (the grid template, token denominations, the transformation card with its explicit coordinate map, boundary-condition cards), the round-by-round sequence, and instructions for the facilitator to remain silent during betting. Follow the game with a debrief that maps each round onto a step in the hole argument: agreement on the exterior as general covariance, the blank region as the hole, the transformation card as the active diffeomorphism, and the token loss as the failure of determinism for point-identity. The debrief must be explicit about what the activity necessarily hides—the continuous smoothness of the manifold and the technical active/passive diffeomorphism distinction—which the facilitator must supply verbally after the mapping is complete. Also predict two failure modes for the first run: participants will conflate the paper grid points with physical spacetime points, and one team will invent a hidden background metric to resolve the contradiction; state exactly how to interrupt each error without breaking the game’s internal logic. The prompt should be answerable without web access by someone who understands the hole argument and basic pedagogical game design; do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
