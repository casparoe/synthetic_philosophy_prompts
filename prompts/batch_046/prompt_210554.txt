Compare Machiavelli's fortune and virtù with Spinoza's fortuna and virtus in the Ethics. Machiavelli treats fortune like a river that virtù has to dam or channel—its a personal, almost martial skill—while Spinoza treats fortune as basically ignorance of causes, so true virtue is rational understanding that dissolves the appearance of luck. Now inject a third view, call it contingency engineering: the idea that you redesign institutions with distributed feedback so black-swan events get absorbed rather than resisted by any one agent, which means fortune shrinks as an explanatory category over generations. Which framework best explains why some regimes survive massive shocks and others collapse? And in each case is the relevant capacity—virtù, virtue, or engineering—located in an individual, a collective, or the system itself? Keep it tight, don't do a survey of every political thinker.
