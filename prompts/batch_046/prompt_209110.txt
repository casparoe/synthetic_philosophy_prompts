I spent last evening in a long and somewhat exhausting argument with my friend Mira about personal identity and gradual mind uploading, and I am trying to determine whether her judgments across a range of cases are as consistent as she insists they are. Mira maintains that if you replace neurons one at a time with functionally identical silicon chips over the course of many years, you survive the transition and remain the same person even after reaching one hundred percent replacement; that if you perform the same total replacement all at once through a scan-and-copy reconstruction, the result is a duplicate and the original dies; that gradually improving your cognitive speed or memory capacity during the replacement process does not break personal identity; that if you halt the process at seventy percent replacement and split the brain so that half the silicon tissue and the remaining biological tissue are placed into two separate bodies, neither body is the original you and identity is destroyed by the branching; and that if you eventually reach full silicon replacement and then, years later, revert ten percent of the tissue back to biological matter grown from your original DNA, you remain the same continuous self rather than a new person who has taken over. Given this track record, I would like you to predict what Mira ought to say, for consistency, about the following new case.

Imagine a person who has undergone gradual replacement up to eighty percent silicon, with full psychological continuity preserved throughout, but then the remaining twenty percent biological tissue is extracted and maintained alive in a vat, continuously interacting with the silicon body through a sustained high-bandwidth wireless neural link, so that mental life is genuinely distributed between the remote biological tissue and the embodied chips, with no discontinuous copying and no branching into two separate streams of consciousness. Would Mira judge that the person is now a single distributed system spanning both locations, that the true self remains anchored in the biological vat while the silicon body functions as an advanced prosthesis, that identity has been destroyed by the physical separation despite the functional integration, or perhaps something else entirely? Please write your response in American English, using continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if composing a careful letter.
