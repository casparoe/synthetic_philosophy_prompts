quick conceptual question thats been bugging me: is "collapse" even a coherent category, or is it just a narrative we slap on societies that stop being hierarchical in ways that leave big ruins? the bronze age trade system falls apart and literacy vanishes in some regions, the classic maya polities fragment but rural populations stay put, easter island gets deforested and statues stop being carved—yet in each case people keep living there, just in a different organizational mode. so what's actually being measured? is collapse a specific kind of rapid complexity drop with real irreversibility, or is it just "societies that became less legible and interesting to later historians"? i want an actual answer with a clear criterion, not a "it depends" fence-sitting thing—if you think the concept is salvageable say exactly what distinguishes collapse from mere reorganization, and if you think it's bunk say what we should study instead.
