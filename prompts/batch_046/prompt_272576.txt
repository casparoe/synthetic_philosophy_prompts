For my grad seminar on agency I'm putting together a dialogue exercise. Write a short dialogue between two philosophers — Mara and Leo — about sphexishness and whether mechanical rigidity really undermines free will. I want them equally sharp, no strawmen.

Mara should defend that the classic wasp example (repeating its burial ritual even when conditions change) only scratches the surface. The deeper problem, she thinks, is meta-sphexishness: an agent that can revise its own rules, but only via a fixed, unalterable algorithm at a deeper level. She should argue that this describes most human "flexibility," and that escaping it requires something like self-authorizing revision — not just more conditional branches.

Leo should push back hard. He should say Mara keeps moving the goalpost: first sphexishness was context-blind repetition, now it's any action governed by a constraint you didn't choose. He should argue that all rational agency works within fixed logical and physical constraints, and that calling those meta-sphexish collapses flexibility into an impossible infinite regress. He can use a concrete counter-example: an AI that updates priors with Bayes' rule and has no fixed prior — Leo should claim this is genuinely flexible, and Mara should have to respond to that specific case, not just repeat her openeing claim.

Make it real back-and-forth: each should answer the other's actual point. Keep it casual, like a coffee-shop argument. Don't have them cite books or ask for reading lists — a student without internet should be able to follow it. And please, no made-up quotes in quotation marks; paraphrase anything from real texts without quotes. There's probably a typo or two in thier reasoning, which is fine.
