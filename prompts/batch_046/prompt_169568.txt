I'm currently taking a graduate seminar on the ontology of art, and we've been working through Nelson Goodman's Languages of Art—particularly his distinction between autographic arts, in which even an exact duplicate counts as a forgery, and allographic arts, in which faithful copies are fully genuine instances of the work. Goodman died in 1998, well before the widespread adoption of generative image models and blockchain-based provenance systems, so I'm trying to extend his framework to a phenomenon he never encountered.

The case I'm focused on involves AI systems that synthesize images de novo through interpolation in high-dimensional latent space rather than copying any specific prior work, yet are then tokenized as non-fungible "originals" accompanied by fabricated exhibition histories and synthetic pedigree records. These outputs are technically reproducible at the code level—seemingly allographic—but they are marketed and valued precisely through the institutional framing of singular, hand-authored origin, with blockchain certificates functioning as a manufactured narrative of authenticity for something that never had an original to forge. If Goodman were confronted with this collapse of his distinction—where the "forgery" is not a duplicate of an existing work but a constructed origin story for a work that was always reproducible—how do you think he would diagnose it? Would he argue that generative art reveals all artistic value to be symbolically allographic beneath the surface, that we've invented a new category of pseudo-autographic work whose worth depends entirely on social conventions about scarcity, or that blockchain provenance is merely a modern notational error within an otherwise allographic system? And given your diagnosis, would he treat an AI-generated piece with a falsified exhibition history as a forgery in the traditional aesthetic sense, or as something categorically different—perhaps a fraud about institutional framing rather than about artistic identity? I'm especially interested in whether he would see any genuinely aesthetic loss in reproducing these synthetic images, or whether the entire question of originality here dissolves into sociology.
