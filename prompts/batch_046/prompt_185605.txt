Here's an argument I sketched for a seminar and now find suspicious because it seems to erase the design and intentional stances altogether. (1) A pattern is genuinely real only if predictions based on it remain stable when we hold the physical micro-state constant but vary the causal history that produced that state. (2) Physical-stance predictions—e.g., that an object of mass m accelerates at rate a—depend solely on current micro-state, not on history, so they satisfy (1). (3) Design-stance predictions—e.g., that this organ pumps blood—depend essentially on evolutionary or manufacturing history; a physically identical artificial replica with a different origin lacks the design pattern. (4) Intentional-stance predictions—e.g., that an agent will reach for water—similarly depend on learning history and functional organization that outstrips any particular physical realization. (5) Therefore design and intentional patterns violate (1): they are history-sensitive rather than history-invariant. (6) So only physical-stance patterns are real; the other stances track useful heuristics without ontological weight. (7) But this renders biological functions and mental states unreal, which is hard to swallow. (8) Hence (1) must be abandoned—unless we pay the eliminativist price. Where does the argument actually slip? If I must preserve something, I would keep the unreality of (6) and salvage reality for the other stances, but I want to know what criterion for pattern-reality I am left with once (1) is rejected. Please state what counterexample or theoretical consideration would change your verdict, and identify the step in your own reasoning that is most likely to fail.
