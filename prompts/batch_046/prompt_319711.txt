Specify the desiderata that an adequate theory of historical analogy in foreign-policy reasoning must meet, with particular attention to why analogies to Munich (appeasement), Vietnam (quagmire escalation), and the so-called Thucydides Trap (power-transition conflict) systematically mislead rather than merely cataloguing past errors. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or conceptual consideration, mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp, and flag any tension with other requirements. The list must include: (1) a criterion separating analogical structure-mapping from the export of causal mechanisms, with the reason that Munich-style analogies often share surface structure but not causal dynamics; (2) a requirement that the theory treat appeasement and quagmire analogies as distinct failure-types rather than symmetric mirror images, explaining what structural feature differentiates them; (3) a condition on narrative closure—whether analogies drawn from completed historical arcs can ever be valid for open-ended present crises—stating whether this is a hard constraint or a defeasible presumption and why it matters for Vietnam-type reasoning; (4) consistency with empirical findings on expert case-based reasoning, without reducing normative evaluation to descriptive psychology; (5) preservation of space for genuinely instructive analogies (e.g., post-conflict institutional reconstruction models) and a specification of what distinguishes them from misleading ones; and (6) an explicit judgment about whether the cluster comprising causal fidelity, openness of the present situation, and narrative completeness is jointly satisfiable, or whether any adequate theory must sacrifice at least one. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating tensions; a brief optional scorecard of prominent approaches—structuralist mapping, causal-explanatory, and narrative-hermeneutic—is welcome but secondary.
