I'm writing up my philosophical profile on consciousness for a graduate reading group and want an internal consistency check before I present. Here is where I've landed. First, I accept Ned Block's distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, and I think global workspace theory gives the right structural account of access consciousness: a state becomes access-conscious when its content is globally broadcast to multiple cognitive subsystems for reasoning and reporting. Second, I am a strict physicalist about both kinds of consciousness. I think phenomenal consciousness involves local recurrent processing in sensory cortices rather than global broadcast, which means the two can come apart—phenomenal consciousness without access, and access without phenomenal—though in normal human cognition they are tightly coupled. Third, I hold that phenomenal concepts involve a distinctive acquaintance-based mode of presentation. This explains the explanatory gap: we grasp physical descriptions in a third-person mode, but phenomenal properties in a first-person mode tied to direct experience, without this entailing any non-physical ontology. Fourth, I think introspection is unreliable specifically about phenomenal properties because of what I call refractive distortion: the moment a phenomenal state becomes the target of global workspace attention, its local recurrent dynamics are disrupted, so we never introspect the original state—only a globally modulated version. Fifth, I deny that philosophical zombies are metaphysically possible, but I think they are conceptually coherent in a way that reveals a real epistemic gap; if physicalism is true, this coherence shows only that our phenomenal concepts don't analytically entail physical descriptions.

Go through these five commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look tense but dissolve on inspection, and how severely each real tension cuts. In particular, address whether my use of zombie conceivability in the fifth commitment is compatible with my physicalism in the second and my acquaintance-based account in the third; whether the third and fourth commitments conflict over whether direct acquaintance is ever achieved if introspection is always refracted; and whether the first and fourth together imply that phenomenal consciousness and access consciousness are not merely distinct but actively antagonistic, given that global broadcast disrupts the local recurrence required for phenomenality. Rank the tensions by severity, say for each what the least costly revision would be and what it costs, and then state which single commitment I should drop if I want the cleanest package overall. Please lead with your bottom-line verdict—whether the set hangs together with minor repairs or requires a serious revision—in the very first sentence, then provide the reasoning with no preamble and without restating the question.
