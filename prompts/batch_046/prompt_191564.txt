I'm drafting a novel about a couple arguing whether to have a kid in a warming world, and I keep noticing an argumentative move I can't name. Here's the occurence in two forms.

First: a friend says "if it's wrong to create a child who'd suffer climate collapse, consistency demands it's wrong NOT to create one who'd live in the stable future -- you're denying that happy ghost a life." Then she imediately backtracks: "but obviously no one has to reproduce, so the first claim is bogus." The implausibility of a positive duty gets used to dissolve a negative one.

Second: in forums I read, people blame parents for giving them a hard life, but never blame them for "skipping" the easier, happier sibling they could've had. The absence of omission-blame is treated as proof there's no real commission-blame either.

Both seem to lean on the same asymmetry, but I think it's different from the usual procreation asymmetry, which sounds like it's about whether happy lives are reasons to reproduce. Here the asymmetry is being weaponized dialectically -- using our refusal to blame for "missing" happy people to argue we shouldn't blame for "creating" suffering people.
