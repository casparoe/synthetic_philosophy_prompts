Write an essay on whether homeschooling regulations should care more about exit costs then entry rules. Everyone talks about making it easier to start homeschooling — filing a notice, showing a curriculum plan, that kind of thing. But there's this weird asymmetry: in alot of states it's dead simple to pull your kid out of public school and begin, but if you want to put them back in mid-year they face credit-evaluation hell, placement tests, and curriculum-alignment reviews that homeschool portfolios rarely pass cleanly. Some folks argue this is just bureaucratic friction that families should plan for; others say it creates a hidden lock-in effect that turns "school choice" into a one-way gate, especially for low-income families who try homeschooling, realize its not working, and then can't afford the time or tutoring to bridge the gap. Weigh the arguments carefully — is the exit-cost asymmetry an unfair structural bias, or is it a necessary safeguard to protect districts from constant churn? Reach an actual conclusion and defend it. Don't just say "it depends" and call it a day; take a real stand.
