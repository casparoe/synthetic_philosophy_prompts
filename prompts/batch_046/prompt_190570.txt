I was discussing biological function with a colleague who works on synthetic and chimeric organisms, and she offered a series of judgments that I'm struggling to systematize. She holds that in a standard mammal the heart has the proper function of circulating blood; that in a deep-cave salamander with vestigial eyes that still react chemically to light, those eye structures have no proper visual function; that in a synthetic chimeric plant into which a bacterial nitrogen-fixation gene was horizontally inserted and is now essential for survival in poor soil, that gene does possess the proper function of nitrogen fixation despite lacking any evolutionary history in the plant lineage; and that in a wild orchid whose scent was originally selected to attract pollinators but now only repels them while coincidentally deterring herbivores—which is now the sole reason the plant survives—the proper function of scent production remains pollination rather than defense. I want to understand what rule or rules could jointly explain all four verdicts, since they seem to pull in different directions: the synthetic gene case looks like it honors current causal contribution or design integration, while the orchid case looks like it honors evolutionary origin even when the current contribution has changed.

Would she say Strain A has the proper function of producing that protective waste? For each principle you identified, state what it predicts about this consortium case, explain which feature of the scenario makes the principles diverge, and then name the further hypothetical case—one that changes only that feature—that would best reveal whether she is running an etiological, a causal-role, or a systemic-integration rule.
