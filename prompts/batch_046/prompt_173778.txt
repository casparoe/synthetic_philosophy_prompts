Write a short story about Mara, who at 30 signed an advance directive refusing any life-extending tech if she ever got dementia, but now at 75 she has it and seems genuinly happy—she even asks her daughter (her heathcare proxy) to tear the paper up. Here's the niche twist: the directive included a "values lock" clause saying her past self could bind her future self only if that future self's preferrences were formed under cognitive degradation. The daughter has to decide whether her mom's current joy is a real preference or just a symptom, and whether the clause makes any sense if dementia has already broken whatever chain of personal identity was supposed to hold.

Keep it grounded in everyday details, not sci-fi, around 600 words.
