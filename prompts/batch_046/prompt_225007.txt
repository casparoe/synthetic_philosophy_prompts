For a philosophy paper I am writing, I have been working through the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—the question of what, if anything, all pleasures genuinely share. Rather than retreating to a purely subjective answer, I want to defend a specific synthesis I am calling structural-functionalist hedonism. On this view, pleasures need not share any uniform phenomenal quality or single hedonic tone; instead, they are unified by playing an identical causal-functional role in reward-learning mechanisms and in rational deliberation about well-being. This allows me to treat the pleasure of solving a geometry problem, the pleasure of a warm bath, and the pleasure of hearing a favorite chord progression as commensurable goods without claiming they feel alike in any introspectively accessible way. I also want to add a further claim that I think distinguishes this view from rivals: in cases of mixed-valence experience, where an agent reports simultaneous enjoyment and aversion—for instance, the bittersweet satisfaction of completing a painful but meaningful task—the hedonist can still assign a net hedonic value by weighing the relative strength of competing reinforcement signals. Rival theories that depend on phenomenal homogeneity, I think, struggle to account for such cases without collapsing into contradiction.

What would count against this position? I am not asking for the standard list of objections—such as the diversity of felt qualities or the difficulty of comparing mental and bodily pleasures—unless you can show why they specifically undermine the functionalist component rather than just older phenomenal-uniformity versions of hedonism. Instead, I want to know which observations, thought experiments, or results from other domains—say, about persistence conditions in general, or about whether conscious enjoyment can systematically dissociate from reward-system activation—would be genuinely fatal. Which would be merely awkward but absorbable through qualification? Would a committed defender of this view have to concede that certain cross-modal comparison failures, or empirical findings showing that reinforcement signals and reported well-being diverge in predictable ways, count as real defeaters? And does the view risk being qualified into unfalsifiability—say, by allowing any failure of comparison to be explained away as a malfunction in the functional mechanism rather than a failure of hedonism itself? If so, is that insulation a virtue or a vice for a theory of this kind? Please rank the potential defeaters from decisive to merely inconvenient, distinguish what defenders of the view would themselves concede from what only critics allege, and be honest about whether structural-functionalist hedonism actually risks anything substantive, or whether it has already been protected into emptiness.
