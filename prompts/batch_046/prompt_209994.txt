(3) At the personal level, if career paths realise incommensurable goods — contemplative understanding versus practical efficacy — the appropriate response is not expected-utility maximisation but asking which option avoids a particular kind of self-betrayal.

(4) I hold that regret across such domains is structurally heterogeneous: the regret of abandoning artistic pursuit is not comparable to that of abandoning scientific pursuit, because the objects of regret belong to different evaluative categories.

(5) Despite this, I advise individuals facing such choices to apply a conservative rule: choose the path whose worst plausible outcome involves the least severe form of domain-specific regret.

(6) I deny global incommensurability: there is always a procedural or constitutional framework — democratic deliberation, rights-based side-constraints, or reflective equilibrium — that permits legitimate comparison without collapsing values into a single scale.
