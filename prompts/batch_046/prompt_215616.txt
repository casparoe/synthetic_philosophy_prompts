After a long argument with a friend about whether permanent human settlement on Mars can ever be ethically justified, I realized we kept talking past each other because neither of us had specified what an adequate ethics of space colonization is actually required to deliver. Before I evaluate any particular proposal, I want the full desiderata: what must any normative framework for long-duration extraterrestrial settlement be able to do? Specifically, it must account for intergenerational lock-in—founding generations making irreversible ecological and technological choices that permanently constrain descendants who never consented to live under them; it must maintain the distinction between resource use for survival and territorial sovereignty, without collapsing either into the other; it must remain consistent with the established international principle that celestial bodies are not subject to national appropriation, yet avoid the conclusion that all settlement therefore constitutes theft; it must address the moral status of indigenous microbial life discovered after settlement has begun, not merely in pre-mission surveys; and it must separate obligations owed to future colonists from obligations owed to Earth-based humanity, without allowing one category to automatically override the other. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual tension behind it, flag which are genuinely non-negotiable and which reflect the prejudices of particular camps—such as deep-ecological stewardship versus frontier-contractarian autonomy—and indicate whether any subset of these demands is jointly unsatisfiable, particularly whether irreversible planetary modification and meaningful intergenerational consent can coexist. Keep the weight on the criteria and their boundary conditions rather than on scoring existing theories, though a brief note on how a liberal-contractarian view, a planetary-stewardship view, and a libertarian-frontier view fare against the list would help me locate where my friend and I actually disagree. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
