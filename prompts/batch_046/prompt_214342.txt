I serve on my university's Research Ethics and Integrity Board, and we must deliver a decision memo on Protocol 2024-117, submitted by the Digital Deliberation Lab. The proposal asks to embed generative-AI synthetic personas—video and text agents indistinguishable from real humans—into a six-week online forum study measuring trust dynamics. Half the human participants will never learn that some interlocutors are synthetic; the other half receive delayed disclosure only after week four. The investigators argue that synthetic confederates eliminate direct harm to human subjects and allow precise causal measurement of how undisclosed artificial agents erode cooperative behavior.

A minority of board members invoke Daniel Dennett's argument about counterfeit people: that manufacturing synthetic agents specifically designed to be taken for real human interlocutors does not merely deceive particular individuals but corrupts the shared environment of trust and intentional-stance attribution on which moral accountability and social coordination depend. They contend this is not routine methodological deception but a form of social vandalism that damages the epistemic commons even if every human subject is eventually debriefed. The majority treats it as standard deception research with adequate safeguards.

Draft the board's decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—addressed to the Vice Provost for Research. The memo should state a clear, actionable recommendation, with specific conditions if applicable; apply a philosophical criterion (not only regulatory compliance) to this protocol; fairly present the strongest consideration supporting the opposite recommendation and explain why it does not prevail here; and avoid bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Keep the tone formal and the length around 600 words.
