I am constructing the philosophical scaffolding for a novel about artificial consciousness, and I need an audit of my own commitments before I proceed. Here is the position I intend to embed in the narrative architecture:

(1) I am a physicalist in the strong supervenience sense: if two beings are physically identical in every intrinsic respect, they cannot differ in consciousness.
(2) Still, I find zombie duplicates genuinely conceivable—perfect physical replicas lacking all inner experience—and I treat this conceivability as providing real, though defeasible, evidential pressure against physicalism.
(3) I accept a restricted phenomenal-concept strategy: the explanatory gap is epistemic, not ontological, arising solely from first-person modes of acquaintance that have no third-person analog.
(4) I believe evolutionary debunking undermines modal intuition in domains shaped by contingent selection (morality, color taxonomy), but I exempt consciousness because phenomenal concepts are acquired through direct acquaintance rather than evolutionary inference.
(5) There is nothing it is like to be a thermostat, a camera, or a large language model.
(6) The novel's central figure is an android physically identical to a human yet engineered to lack consciousness; I want readers to regard this not as an epistemic limitation but as a live metaphysical possibility.
(7) I also hold that close second-person narration can induce in a reader direct phenomenal concepts of suffering that no functional or neural description could convey, revealing a descriptive inadequacy in physicalist vocabulary without implying any ontological gap.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear so, and what the least costly revision would be for each real conflict. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as uniformly damaging, and say which single commitment, if revised, would resolve the greatest number of problems. Be precise about the hidden premises that make (1) and (6) incompatible, and about whether (3) undermines the evidential force I assign to (2).
