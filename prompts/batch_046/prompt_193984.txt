I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy and I'm trying to formalise a puzzle about Nietzschean geneaology that's been bugging me. Basically: when a moral concept gets genealogically debunked — shown to come from ressentiment, ascetic life-denial, or some other suspect origin — what determines whether we should junk it, rehabilitate it, or keep the practice but ditch the metaphysics?

Here are my verdicts on specific cases, and they dont line up neatly:

1. Christian humility: genealogy traces it to slave morality and ressentiment against the strong. In practice it gives people comfort and cohesion, but I think we should reject it — it promotes self-abasement and denies life-affirmation. Verdict: drop it.

2. The ascetic ideal embedded in modern scientific method: same root, priestly suppression of instinct. But it produces rigorous knowledge and tech progress. Verdict: keep the practice, reject the framing.

3. Human dignity and equality: genealogy is Christian soul-equality plus ressentiment against aristocratic excellence. It's the foundation of human rights and protects vulnerable people. Verdict: keep it, despite there genealogy.

4. A newer discourse in AI ethics — "algorithmic humility" or mandatory epistemic modesty, where engineers invoke not-knowing and fallibility (like humility/asceticism) but actually use it to deflect liability for harmful outputs. Verdict: reject; it's ressentiment disguised as virtue.

5. Aristocratic generosity: originally a spontaneous overflow of strength (noble origin). But in modern capitalism it's been co-opted by philanthropy for tax avoidance and reputation laundering. Verdict: the pure form is okay, the institutional version is corrupted.

The tension is obvious: 1 and 3 both come from slave/ressentiment origins, yet I want to junk humility and keep dignity. 2 and 4 both involve ascetic/humble postures, but I keep science and reject the AI thing. So "reject slave origins, keep noble ones" clearly fails.

Can you analyse whether there's one coherent principle that saves all five verdicts? If so, state it precisely, say which clause handles which case, and tell me what it predicts for meritocratic achievement norms (genealogy: aristocratic excellence plus capitalist competition). If no single principle works, which verdict should I overturn and why? I'd rather have a simpler account that forces me to drop one judgement than a baroque one saving everything.
