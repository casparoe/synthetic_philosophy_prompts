I am working through the Rietdijk-Putnam argument in a philosophy-of-science seminar: if special relativity denies any privileged hyperplane of simultaneity, then the distinction between past, present, and future is frame-dependent, and the ontology that best fits the physics is a four-dimensional block universe in which all spacetime events exist tenselessly. Against that background, I have become puzzled by an institutional practice that seems to take the opposite metaphysical stance for granted. The International Bureau of Weights and Measures constructs Coordinated Universal Time by averaging relativistically corrected readings from atomic clocks distributed across the Earth, then publishes a single authoritative world-clock that global institutions—satellite-navigation systems, financial clearinghouses, telecommunications networks—use to assign one unique timestamp to events happening in different locations and different states of motion. The protocol does not deny relativity; it applies gravitational and velocity corrections precisely because clock rates depend on path and potential. Nevertheless, the social and technical function of UTC is to establish a shared "now" across the planet, a convention that is treated in practice as if it recovered an objective global present rather than merely selecting one coordinate system among infinitely many equally valid ones.

What philosophical commitments about time, existence, and convention does this practice presuppose? Does the BIPM protocol implicitly reject the block-universe conclusion by treating simultaneity as a factual relation that can be corrected for and then fixed, or can an eternalist interpret the entire apparatus as a pragmatic indexing scheme with no ontological implications for what exists? Reconstruct the implicit theory of time embedded in the practice, distinguish carefully between what the institution strictly needs to assume and what it merely accommodates comfortably, and tell me whether a consistent Rietdijk-Putnam eternalist could endorse UTC exactly as it stands. If not, specify what part of the practice—its technical justification, its legal authority, or its social role—would have to be reconceived.
