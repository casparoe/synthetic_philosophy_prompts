I sit on our city's heritage and public-space commission and we have a messy case coming up next month. There's this fountain in the old plaza — the Whitmore Memorial Fountain, put up in 1912 by the Whitmore textile family. The family built half the downtown and also ran mills that relied on prison labor contracts through the 1940s. The fountain's become a kind of accidental community hub; people meet there, but the plaque only talks about Whitmore's 'civic generosity' with no mention of where the money came from. A local group wants it removed entirely and replaced with a memorial to the laborers. Another faction wants to keep it exactly as is but add a contextual plaque. There's also this middle proposal from our chair: relocate the fountain to a local history museum but keep the original plaque intact, treating it as a historical artifcat rather than a public honor.

I'm drafting the commission memo recommending one of these three. The memo needs an actual bottom line — approve removal, approve contextualization, or approve relocation — with reasoning the different factions could actually respond to, not just a summary. And it needs to take seriously the strongest consideration on the losing side. There's also this idea one member keeps pushing, that monuments don't just symbolize values but literally 'budget' collective moral attention — like every square foot of plaza given to Whitmore is cognative space taken from the laborers. I think that's either brilliant or total nonsense but the memo has to address it either way.

Can you write it? Formal enough that the commission could vote on it, but don't include any citation lists or suggested readings — I need something we can act on, not a literature review. Also please don't just tell me what I already think; I need the reasoning spelled out clearly, and I want the memo to address whether keeping an object in public view can ever be 'just history' once we know its origins.
