Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for interpretive positions in Confucian role ethics, using as its diagnostic case what I call recursive role disavowal: an adult child explicitly rejects the father's authority to assign familial roles but continues to provide material care, while the father insists that this absence of recognized submission violates xiao regardless of the care given. Build the tree from logical commitments rather than from lists of named scholars. First branch: does li (ritual propriety) require mutual recognition of roles between the parties, or can it be performed unilaterally by one party? Second branch: is ren (humaneness) in this framework genuinely role-relative, or is it a universal affect that merely happens to be expressed through role relationships? Third branch: does the classical obligation to correct one's parents function as a meta-role that supersedes ordinary filial submission, or does it remain subordinate to hierarchical deference? Fourth branch: do modern contract-based conceptions of family render the Confucian framework incoherent, or only culturally inapplicable? Each leaf should carry a position label—such as strict role essentialism, reconstructed ritualism, universalist Confucianism, incoherence thesis, or unnamed/unoccupied—together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment, especially for any view that maintains binding obligations despite the disavowal of role authority. Note any logical combinations that remain unoccupied and briefly diagnose whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, citation formats, or verbatim quotations from primary texts.
