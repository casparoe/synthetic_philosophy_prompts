Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of aesthetic judgment and beauty's objectivity must satisfy, specifically in the wake of Hume's problem of taste. I want the full specification before comparing particular accounts. List the requirements across four categories: (1) the phenomena it must accommodate—such as persistent disagreement among cultivated critics, cross-cultural variation in preference, the phenomenology of immediate aesthetic response, and the possibility of correcting one's own taste over time; (2) the distinctions it must not collapse—particularly between finding a work beautiful and finding one's own response to it appropriate, between intersubjective agreement and genuine objectivity, and between a failure of taste and a mere difference in sensibility; (3) the psychological and conceptual constraints it must respect—above all that aesthetic judgment operates through sentiment rather than through contradiction-based belief revision, and that it remains normatively evaluative rather than purely descriptive; and (4) the puzzles it must dissolve—most importantly how ideal critics in Hume's sense could disagree without either being defective, and whether such disagreement undermines objectivity or only reveals its limits.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or consideration, mark whether it is non-negotiable or negotiable by particular camps, and flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—especially any tension between preserving the subjectivity of sentiment and securing robust objectivity. Keep the conceptual weight on the criteria and their conflicts rather than on a scorecard. Then, briefly indicate how Hume's own standard of taste, a contemporary response-dependence account, and a realist cognitivist position fare against your list, noting for each where it succeeds and which requirement it is forced to sacrifice.
