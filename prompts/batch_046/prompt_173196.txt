A predictive risk system with an established record of accurate, location-specific forecasts advised my colleague that she would be killed in a structural collapse if she attended a conference in Damascus on March 12. She canceled the trip, went to Aleppo instead, and died there on March 12 in an unrelated accident—while the Damascus venue collapsed exactly as predicted. Before she left, she asked whether trying to "outmaneuver" the forecast was rational: if the system tracks real causal patterns, might her avoidance merely displace the outcome? I want to understand whether her flight to Aleppo was a free choice that caused her death, or whether the result was invariant regardless of location. Had she ignored the warning and stayed in Damascus, would she have survived? And does the fact that the prediction was precise about Damascus but wrong about Aleppo—where the cause was entirely different—indicate that she genuinely altered her trajectory, or that the system detected an unavoidable endpoint independent of her actions?
