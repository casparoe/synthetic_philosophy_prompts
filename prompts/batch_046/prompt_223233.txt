I've been turning over that argument about big collective problems—climate, factory farming, even voting—where it seems like one person's choice can't really be the thing that causes harm, since the damage only shows up once millions do the same thing together; yet if everyone relied on that, nothing would change. I want a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains that alternate—built around exactly this tension. The first refrain should state the inefficacy intuition: that a single contribution stays below every causal threshold and never tips the outcome. The second refrain should state the structural dependence: that the harm is made of nothing but these sub-threshold parts, so removing one doesn't stop it, yet removing all of them does. Let the form do the philosophical work—the refrains should circle each other through the tercets and finally sit side by side in the last quatrain without resolving which is right, leaving the paradox open rather than preaching or shrugging. Make it accurate enough that someone who actually thinks about collective-action logic recognizes overdetermination and expected-value reasoning, but don't let it read like a textbook summary; keep the contradiction standing.
