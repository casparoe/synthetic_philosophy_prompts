As a novelist constructing a speculative world, I keep returning to the old question of whether an ant colony can possess a mind—something in the spirit of Hofstadter’s Aunt Hillary—but I want to press my intuitions on cases that are not quite the standard ones, since the boundary I draw will shape my fiction.

Here are my verdicts. A conventional ant hill coordinating foraging feels clearly un-minded: distributed reflex, no integration. A slime mould solving a maze strikes me as clever problem-solving, yet I do not think it has a mind; the solution resides in the physics, not in a subject. Now consider a “choral hive” I am writing about, in which ants inherit pheromonal traces that encode not merely signals but continuous narrative threads across generations, producing recursive feedback loops—something like an ant fugue—where the colony’s behaviour references its own past states. That feels closer to mindedness, though I cannot articulate precisely why. By contrast, a human crowd chanting in perfect unison feels like pure aggregation with no interiority. A distributed computer network with no central processor seems to me analogous to the slime mould—functional integration without a subject—though I waver on this. A single isolated ant trained, hypothetically, to navigate complex paths by individual memory seems to possess a minimal mind, yet scaling that cognition up does not seem to produce a colony-mind; the parts remain parts. The crux is the recursive self-reference: if the colony’s loops genuinely mirror the kind of self-reference that Gödel exploited, does that constitute the emergence of a subject, or is it still only mechanism?

Is there a single principle—perhaps about integrated information, narrative continuity, or recursive self-modelling—that captures all these verdicts? If so, what does it predict for the choral hive versus the distributed network? And if the principle forces me to drop one verdict, which should go: my sense that the choral hive differs from the slime mould, or my insistence that the isolated ant has a mind while the colony does not?

If this framing rests on a confused premise—perhaps by treating narrative continuity as philosophically load-bearing when it is merely aesthetic, or by assuming that Gödelian recursion applies to biological systems—please say so plainly rather than repairing it politely. I am not seeking a bibliography or citation, only a rigorous assessment of whether these intuitions cohere and what they cost.
