Take the 1920s Brouwer–Hilbert crisis over the law of excluded middle and infinite proof methods, and read it through Hilbert's hotel and Cantorian actual infinity vs strict finitism. I want a real mapping, not a metaphor: if Hilbert's hotel runs on formalist rules where every room is indexed and available regardless of whether a specific guest has arrived, and Brouwer is the night manager who refuses to assign a room number unless he can construct the guest's reservation in finite steps, then exactly which operational feature of the hotel breaks? Does Cantor's diagonal argument work like an overbooking scheme that Brouwer can't cancel, or is it a red herring? Then reverse the lens: given that Hilbert's program eventually dominated mathematical practice despite Gödel's 1931 surprise guest, does that institutional victory show finitist housekeeping is incoherent, or just that mathematics doesn't care about occupancy limits? Be precise about who plays the manager, what counts as the reservation list, and where the analogy flatlines — especialy on Gödel's result, which neither side fully anticipated.
