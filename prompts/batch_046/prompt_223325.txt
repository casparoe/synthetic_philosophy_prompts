(2) I accept Dennett's multiple drafts model: conscious experience consists of parallel, competing narrative streams, with no canonical version that counts as the experience.
(3) Despite (2), I maintain there is a determinate fact of the matter about what I am phenomenally conscious of at any moment—there is a dominant draft that fixes experiential content, even if other drafts persist.
(4) Phenomenal properties are real and carry intrinsic what-it-is-like character, but they are distributed across drafting processes rather than localized.
(5) Introspection gives partial, direct access to the dominant draft's content, but is systematically blind to drafting mechanisms and to competing sub-threshold drafts.
(6) Moral agency requires a unified diachronic narrative self—integrated temporal binding of intentions and actions—though this does not require a Cartesian theater or a single experiential author.
(7) The transition from unconscious representation to conscious draft involves a specific temporal binding interval—roughly 200 to 500 milliseconds—during which competing representations are synchronously updated before differentiation; this interval explains apparent continuity despite fragmentation.
(8) Some unconscious processes generate sub-threshold drafts that never achieve dominance yet still carry partial phenomenal character.

Please audit the set.
