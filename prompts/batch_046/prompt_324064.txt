I am revising a manuscript with an unusual collaborator: an elderly relative who, after a stroke, can no longer follow narrative or grasp symbolic meaning, yet when I read passages aloud, she consistently points to the exact sentences where the prose fails and suggests replacements that restore rhythm and tension with uncanny precision. Her physicians say she operates through preserved pattern-matching circuits with no access to comprehension; she does not know what the novel is about and cannot explain why a revision works. This reminds me of two strange inversions I have been pondering—how evolutionary processes construct elaborate functional structures without foresight or intention, and how a computing mechanism might solve logical problems without knowing what logic is. Now I wonder whether her contributions should count as artistic judgment or merely as the output of a competent but mindless process. If I dismiss her because she lacks understanding, must I also dismiss those parts of my own writing that arise from an intuition I cannot articulate? And if I credit her, am I attributing comprehension where there is only functional success, much as we might mistakenly see design in nature or thought in a machine?
