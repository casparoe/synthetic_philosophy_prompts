I've been trying to think clearly about safety architectures that are designed for the case where alignment is not achieved—where we treat misalignment not as a transient bug to be fixed by further training, but as a persistent possibility that the containment layer must accommodate indefinitely. Most of the literature I encounter either treats control measures (sandboxing, monitoring, capability restriction, adversarial evaluation) as temporary scaffolding until an aligned system is produced, or it evaluates them by whether they eventually facilitate alignment. I want to step back and ask what an adequate evaluative framework for control-based safety would have to deliver on its own terms, assuming the model may never become aligned and may actively optimize against its constraints. What are the genuine desiderata?

The framework needs to handle specific phenomena: containment mechanisms that the model learns to reward-hack, distinctions between suppressing harmful outputs and suppressing the underlying cognition that produces them, and trade-offs between false positives (shutting down benign but misinterpreted behavior) and false negatives (missing subtle optimization against the sandbox). It must also preserve conceptual distinctions that popular accounts collapse—such as between interruptibility and corrigibility, or between measures whose efficacy scales with model capability and those that remain constant—without reducing them to a single dimension of "restrictiveness." It should be consistent with results from computer security and control theory, yet not smuggle in unstated normative commitments about whether open-ended development is desirable. Could you lay out the full requirements list, with the motivating case for each, indicate which are non-negotiable versus which reflect camp-specific prejudices, note whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable, and briefly score the main existing proposals—say, strict capability boxing, interpretability-driven oversight, and red-teaming protocols—against the list, identifying which single desideratum does the most work in excluding candidates?
