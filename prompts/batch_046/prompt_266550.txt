me and my roommate got into it last night about whether smart people should always meet in the middle when they disagree. she walked me through this argument that conciliationism basically eats itself and i cant figure out where the trapdoor is, so im hoping you can spot the leak.

(1) conciliationism says if you and an epistemic peer disagree about X, you should both move your credence closer to the other persons.
(2) but "peer" just means roughly equal reasoning skills and access to the same evidence.
(3) now take any belief i actually hold, say that climate change is mostly human-caused. there is definitely some climate scientist out there who is my peer in reasoning but who, due to some weird cognitive bias or different weighting of studies, ends up disagreeing.
(4) in fact my roommate said this applies to every belief i have, because for any belief theres always some possible peer-world version of me with the same skills but opposite view.
(5) so conciliationism forces me to split the difference with all these possible peer-versions on every single belief.
(6) that means for every belief i hold, my rational credence should get pulled toward 50/50 or lower, all at once.
(7) but then i end up with no beliefs at all, which is impossible since i need beliefs just to get through the day.
(8) therefore conciliationism is false, or at least useless, and being "steadfast" is the only consistent option.

she says this proves that sticking to your guns is rational after all. but i feel like step (4) is doing something sneaky with "possible" peers versus actual ones i really talk to, and maybe step (6) assumes i have to average over every hypothetical peer simultaneously instead of just adjusting when i actually run into disagreement. but i cant tell if thats the real problem or if im just trying to save conciliationism because it sounds nicer. which step should i actually reject, and whats the cost? like, does giving up (4) mean conciliationism only applies to real-world arguments and not to thought experiments, or does rejecting (6) mean i have to accept that rational people can hold opposite views forever without either being wrong? id really like to keep the idea that peer disagreement matters, but i also dont want to end up believing nothing.
