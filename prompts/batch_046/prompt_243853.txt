I spent three decades in condensed-matter physics before retiring and turning, somewhat unexpectedly, to philosophy of science and metaphysics, and I am now trying to settle my own view on dispositions, causal powers, and the puzzles raised by finks and masks. Before I present these ideas in a small seminar for retired scientists, I want a consistency audit: I have assembled a package of commitments that feel individually plausible to me, but I suspect some pull against others in ways that are not immediately obvious, and I would like to know which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts dissolve upon closer inspection, and what the least costly revision would be in each case, ordered by severity.

My position is as follows. I am a realist about dispositions: I take them to be irreducible properties of physical systems, not merely convenient summaries of counterfactual regularities. I also think the classic fink and mask cases—such as the wire that loses conductivity the moment it is touched, or the fragile glass enclosed in protective foam—are best interpreted not as showing that dispositions vanish but as revealing that manifestation conditions are complex and include the absence of interfering conditions; the protected glass still genuinely possesses the disposition to shatter, and its manifestation is conditional on the removal of the mask. At the same time, I am drawn to a dispositional essentialist account of natural law, according to which the laws of nature derive from the essential natures of fundamental properties like charge and mass. Yet I believe this essentialist picture must be modified for quantum phenomena, since properties such as decay rate or spin orientation are not intrinsic dispositions of an isolated particle but relational properties of a coupled system comprising the particle, its environment, and the measurement context; if essentialism holds, it applies to these relational structures rather than to bare particles. In addition, I hold that working scientists treat dispositional language—talk of solubility, fragility, or radioactive decay—instrumentally in practice, as defeasible inference-licenses for prediction and control, and I regard this methodological instrumentalism as fully compatible with metaphysical realism, because empirical science aims at predictive adequacy rather than ontological commitment. I also defend a constraint I have come to call structural unmanifestability: if a disposition is not merely temporarily masked but is permanently blocked in every physically possible situation compatible with a system's boundary conditions, then ascribing it is ontologically empty and should be rejected, since it is not a hidden power but a theoretical artifact carried over from an inappropriate model. Finally, I reject the coherence of internal finks in the sense originally discussed by C.B. Martin—cases in which an object is supposed to change its own categorical base precisely upon being triggered—because I think this involves a conceptual confusion between one object being replaced by another and one object retaining its identity while losing a property; if the internal constitution changes upon triggering, then we do not have a finked disposition but a different object altogether.

Please examine this set of commitments and tell me whether they form a stable package. I want you to identify the genuine tensions—particularly between my metaphysical realism and my quantum-contextual revision of essentialism, between my instrumentalist view of scientific practice and my realist ontology, and between my acceptance of masked dispositions and my structural-unmanifestability constraint—while also noting any pairs that only appear to conflict. For each tension, rank its severity and indicate the cheapest revision that would restore consistency, and then tell me which single change—giving up or substantially modifying one commitment—would resolve the greatest number of conflicts. I ask that you write your response entirely in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a thoughtful letter, so that I can read it as a unified argument rather than as a segmented checklist.
