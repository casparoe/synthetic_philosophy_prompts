Write an essay on whether qualia are an incoherent posit, taking the challenge posed by Chase and Sanborn as your point of departure without merely rehearsing Dennett’s familiar quining strategy. Center your analysis on a precise conceptual tension: if phenomenal properties are genuinely intrinsic and essentially first-personal, they must be individuated independently of functional or relational descriptions; yet if they are to function as coherent theoretical terms, they appear to require exactly such descriptions for intersubjective deployment. To structure your argument, employ a distinction between phenomenal transparency—where a quality is present to introspection without mediating representational structure—and phenomenal opacity, where introspective access is necessarily mediated by functional architecture and therefore conceptually dependent on structural roles. From this, either construct and defend an intermediate position—call it indexical phenomenalism, which holds that phenomenal concepts are irreducibly first-personal but intersubjectively deployable through shared structural roles without collapsing into functional descriptions—or argue that the tension is fatal and that intrinsic phenomenal talk should be eliminated in favor of a purely structural account of consciousness. Weigh the principal objections on each side, particularly the charge that first-personal individuation inevitably smuggles in functional criteria and the countercharge that functional descriptions leave an explanatory gap that only intrinsic properties can close. Reach a definite verdict and justify it: either that qualia survive the incoherence charge once we accept that first-personal concepts obey a distinct logic of individuation, or that the charge succeeds and we ought to abandon the posit. Do not conclude with a non-committal balance; defend your position with conceptual precision rather than appeals to empirical neuroscience or bibliographic authority.
