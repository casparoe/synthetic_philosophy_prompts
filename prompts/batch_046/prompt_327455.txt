I'm reporting on whether Douglas Hofstadter's strange-loop model of the self—the idea that consciousness is a symbol in the brain that refers back to itself—can be developed into a rigorous conceptual research program rather than remaining a literary metaphor. For the purpose of my piece, I want to ask: if a small interdisciplinary group, say philosophers of mind and theoretical cognitive scientists with no laboratory access, had roughly three years to pursue this, which open conceptual questions should they prioritize?

I'm particularly interested in the problem of the loop's ground case: if the symbol 'I' requires a non-self-referential base representation to avoid infinite regress, does that compromise the autonomy of the loop, or can the base itself be folded back into the loop? Please provide a defended agenda rather than a survey: rank the questions by which are upstream of others, which are tractable without empirical neuroscience, and which would genuinely alter our account of personal identity rather than serving as decorative metaphysics. Specify the criteria governing your ordering—importance, tractability, dependency among questions—and identify what conceptual or empirical result, should it emerge in the next few years, would cause you to reorder the priorities.
