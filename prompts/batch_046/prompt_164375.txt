I'm putting together a diagnostic handout for an applied ethics seminar, because I find the standard pro-choice versus pro-life binary does little to map where my classmates actually stand once they think through concrete cases. I want a decision tree—built from the logical space of commitments rather than from a list of familiar labels—that walks a respondent through three nested diagnostic questions and lands them on a named position within the abortion debate, with particular attention to Judith Thomson's violinist thought experiment and to Don Marquis's future-like-ours argument. The first branch should ask whether a foetus possesses the same moral status as an adult human being, using as its concrete case whether it is permissible to terminate a pregnancy in which the pregnant person is brain-dead but is being kept alive mechanically solely for gestation. The second branch should ask whether bodily autonomy can justify termination even if the foetus is a full moral person, tested through a modified violinist scenario in which you voluntarily invited the dependent person into your home knowing they might fall ill and require the use of your kidneys for nine months. The third branch should ask whether the future-like-ours argument applies only to entities that already have an actual psychological future, or also to those that have merely a possible one, tested by asking about a foetus that will almost certainly develop consciousness and relational capacities but currently possesses none whatsoever.

Please phrase every branch node as a direct question that a student could answer without already knowing which philosophical position it leads to. Each leaf should carry the name of the view it corresponds to—for instance, absolute prohibition, Thomsonian bodily-autonomy liberalism, gradualist personhood, Marquis-style potentialist prohibition, or an unnamed combination—together with a single sentence describing its most awkward commitment. Where the tree generates a combination of answers that no prominent view occupies, flag it as unoccupied and briefly diagnose whether the gap arises from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. In addition, please flag any pair of leaves that your tree genuinely cannot separate. Present the result in a clear flowchart format, using British English spelling and punctuation throughout, and ensure that the answer itself is written in British English. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the tree should be answerable entirely from general philosophical reasoning about the cases given.
