After a long argument with a friend about whether any conception of free will survives the twin pressures of the manipulation argument and the disappearing-agent objection, I have settled on a hybrid view that I want genuinely stress-tested rather than merely classified. The position is this: the only variety of free will worth wanting is neither libertarian sourcehood—which risks reducing either to randomness or to an obscure, metaphysically costly causal relation—nor standard hierarchical compatibilism, which seems unable to distinguish a genuinely self-governing agent from a successfully manipulated one. Rather, the defensible core is a diachronic capacity for reflective self-revision: an agent must be able to come to reject or restructure even her deepest values through a process that is not algorithmically derivable from her prior psychological profile, yet this does not require that she possess literal alternative possibilities in the exact same causal circumstances. Call this reflective openness without leeway. My friend insists that once you cash out non-algorithmic self-revision you have smuggled in indeterminism, and that once you drop leeway you have merely restated familiar compatibilism, so that I have renamed a stalemate rather than resolved it. So I want to know what would genuinely count against this specific position. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, including which conceptual tensions—such as whether non-algorithmic value-revision is coherent without some form of causal openness—should move a defender, and which empirical considerations—such as findings about the predictability of value-change from prior neural or behavioral states—would actually threaten the view. Say also what proponents of this kind of hybrid would themselves concede as damaging, versus what they would dismiss as missing the point. Be frank about whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a view of this kind. In your answer, please separate what is genuinely settled in the philosophy of action from what remains actively contested among specialists, and do not present any disputed interpretation as the consensus view.
