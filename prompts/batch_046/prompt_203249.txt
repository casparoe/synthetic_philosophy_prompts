Please plan the explanatory arc for a single session: first, identify the conceptual distinction between perfection as a completed state and perfection as an asymptotic, never-completed practice, and explain why that distinction must be secured before the thirteen virtues are introduced, since without it the list will strike these students as an arbitrary feature-engineering problem; second, specify the single carrying example or exercise drawn from Franklin's own practice that should anchor the entire session, clarifying why it must include both his ledger-based tracking of failures and his explicit refusal to extend the project into religious perfection, and also what the example must lack—specifically, any framing that reduces the virtues to quantifiable productivity metrics—so that it does not reinforce the very misconception I need to dissolve.
