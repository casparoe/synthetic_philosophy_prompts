I chair the Foundational Risk Review Board for an international consortium that certifies mathematical theorems for deployment in safety-critical engineering—specifically cryptographic protocols and aerospace control systems. Our working axiom system is ZFC augmented with a specific large-cardinal extension we designate System L. In light of Gödelian limitations, our charter treats the consistency of System L not as a settled background fact but as an open epistemic risk that must be actively managed in every certification decision. I need you to construct a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure that my board—composed of working mathematicians without philosophical training—can execute on any submitted result within ninety minutes. The procedure should classify each submission into one of three categories: (A) cleared for deployment with a standard epistemic warranty; (B) cleared only with an explicit consistency-contingency clause that specifies exactly which axioms of System L must fail for the result to collapse; or (C) withheld because its justification is too deeply embedded in System L to be isolated or hedged.

Your procedure must translate the philosophical problem—that the basic axioms of mathematics might be inconsistent—into concrete review steps. Specify, in order: (1) a structural risk audit that distinguishes results derivable in Peano Arithmetic or weak fragments of ZFC from those that invoke the large-cardinal apparatus; (2) a dependency-tracing test that maps which premises in the proof are genuinely load-bearing versus decorative; (3) the exact conditions under which a consistency-contingency clause can be drafted without rendering the result useless for engineering; (4) the points at which the board must make an irreducible value judgment about risk tolerance rather than apply a factual test, clearly marked as judgment calls requiring a named signatory; and (5) the stopping conditions where the procedure halts because the question is metaphysical or circular rather than procedural. Include a one-page sign-off sheet format that separates factual determinations from judgment calls.

Then apply the procedure to three submissions. First, a cryptographic security proof that relies exclusively on number-theoretic results formalizable in Peano Arithmetic. Second, a convergence theorem for adaptive control algorithms whose proof requires the well-ordering principle at the cardinal specified by System L's large-cardinal hypothesis. Third, a metamathematical result, proved entirely within System L, establishing that a broad class of engineering-relevant stability statements is undecidable—where the unwelcome answer is that the procedure classifies this as (C) and withholds it, despite its direct practical relevance, precisely because its own scope depends on the consistency of the very system it investigates. For each case, show the completed sign-off sheet and identify where the board might be tempted to override the procedure, noting that such overrides are permitted only at the marked judgment points and must be documented.

Finally, state explicitly what the procedure cannot do—such as resolve whether System L is in fact consistent, or substitute for a domain expert's assessment of engineering relevance—and flag any step where simplification for usability risks distorting the underlying philosophical issue. The deliverable should be something a reviewer could actually run without consulting a philosopher, yet honest about where judgment enters and where the framework fails.
