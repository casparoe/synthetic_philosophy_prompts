Student answer: In Parfit's desert example, a driver saves you if you promise to pay later, but once your safe theres no point paying since the cause is already done. Once rescued, actually paying is irrational but having that disposition is rational overall. He calls this rational irrationality and uses it to defend causal decision theory against evidentialists, since only your present action matters causaly.
