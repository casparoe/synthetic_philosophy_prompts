I've been trying to work through a puzzle about whether our thoughts and intentions actually cause anything in the world, or whether everything we do is just the result of brain chemistry and physics. There's a line of reasoning I keep running into: since every bodily movement can be fully explained by neurons firing and chemical reactions, mental things like beliefs, desires, or intentions are left with no causal work to do. They might exist, the argument goes, but they don't push anything around — they're more like shadows cast by the machinery of the brain. People present this as a serious obstacle to the idea that we act because of our minds.

Yet in the very same conversations, when people explain ordinary human behavior — why someone took an umbrella, changed their opinion about a friend, or decided to apologize — they speak as if mental states are doing genuine causal work. They say things like "his belief that it was raining made him reach for the umbrella," or "the intention to make things right shaped the words he chose." They don't translate these into neuron-talk; they let the mental description itself carry the explanatory weight. What's strange is that they don't treat this as contradicting the first point. They seem to act as if the "no room left" idea applies strictly in one kind of context — perhaps when we're talking about basic physics or biology — but quietly stops applying when we're talking about people, reasons, and everyday explanations.

I've been wondering whether there's a specific established name for this pattern. It's not just ordinary inconsistency or changing the subject, because both positions are meant to belong to the same overall picture of how the world works. It's also not exactly the view that mental talk and physical talk are just two descriptions of one process, because the first argument was specifically about mental descriptions being causally redundant rather than merely differently worded. So is there a recognized concept — in philosophy of mind or philosophy of science — for this selective suspension of a strict exclusion principle across different levels or styles of explanation? And if there is no precise term, how should one distinguish this move from plain contradiction on the one hand, and from the idea that mental talk is merely a convenient fiction on the other?
