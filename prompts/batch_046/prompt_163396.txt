I sit on the city charter review panel (seven of us, we advise council on amendments). There's a proposal, 7-C, that'd stick a "recursive revision clause" into Article III. The clause says if two charter provisions conflict, it auto-triggers a "self-correction" that rewrites the text without a voter referendum—so the charter can amend itself in real time. The drafters say this is just adminstrative hygiene, especially since our city just got hit with an AI zoning dispute that Article IV never anticipated. But there's an obvious self-reference snag: what if the recursive clause conflicts with Article XII, which requires 2/3 voter approval for any amendment? Could it "correct" Article XII away?

The legal subcommittee also wants a "second-order reflexivity safeguard"—the clause wouldn't just update charter text, but also the very criteria for what counts as a "conflict" in the first place. That feels like opening a legal Liar loop with actual institutional teeth. The panel meets next Thursday and I need to circulate a short memo before then recommending whether we endorse 7-C, reject it, or send it back with conditions. The memo should take seriously the drafters' argument that static charters can't adapt fast enough, but also engage the strongest objection—that letting a document amend its own amendment rules collapses the distinction between constituent and constituted power.

Please draft that memo: clear recommendation with reasoning, honest treatment of the best counter-argument, and a concrete course of action (with a rough timeline if relevant). Also, include a brief note saying what would change your mind—what specific feature or argument about the clause would flip your recommendation, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
