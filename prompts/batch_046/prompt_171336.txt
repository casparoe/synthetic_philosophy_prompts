I just came out of a long, somewhat heated argument with a close friend about Szasz and psychiatric classification, and I want to check whether the position I actually defended holds together before I present it in a seminar. Here are the commitments I stand by:

(1) I accept Szasz's claim in its restricted form: psychiatric diagnoses frequently reify social conflicts and moral judgments as biological diseases, but this does not entail that severe psychological suffering is unreal or that some conditions lack neurological correlates.

(2) I hold that schizophrenia is not a unitary natural kind but a cluster of specific cognitive dysfunctions—particularly disruptions to source monitoring and predictive processing—that exist on a continuum with normal cognition and do not justify a categorical disease model.

(3) I reject the DSM-5 taxonomy as an instrument of institutional social control, yet I grant that dimensional assessments of cognitive flexibility and reality monitoring have genuine predictive validity for clinical outcomes.

(4) I believe involuntary psychiatric commitment can be justified solely on the basis of severely impaired practical reasoning—specifically, an inability to understand one's own interests—without any need to classify the condition as a medical disease.

(5) At the same time, I insist that every psychiatric evaluation, even purely dimensional and neurologically grounded, necessarily embeds a normative judgment about which cognitive styles are socially acceptable; therefore, there is no value-free psychiatric science.

Please audit these for joint consistency. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision? I am especially concerned that (2) undermines (1) by conceding too much to medicalization, and that (3) conflicts with (5) by assuming that predictive validity can be separated from normative framing. Rank the tensions by severity, specify what each resolution costs me conceptually, and tell me whether my core anti-psychiatric commitment survives intact or must be substantially qualified.
