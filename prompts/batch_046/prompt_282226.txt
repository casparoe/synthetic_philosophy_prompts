I have been thinking about the claim that hypocrisy removes one's standing to blame others, and it seems to me that if this is true, then because every person has at some point violated norms they now uphold—whether through dishonesty, unfairness, or neglect—no one could ever legitimately blame anyone else without being disqualified by their own history. To avoid this result, I want to propose a distinction between "active hypocrisy," where one currently benefits from or participates in the very wrong being condemned, and "archival hypocrisy," where the failure lies entirely in the past and has been abandoned. My question is whether standing is lost only under active hypocrisy, or whether archival hypocrisy also creates a permanent disqualification. Furthermore, if reform through what I will call "restitutive acknowledgment"—explicitly recognizing one's prior failure—can restore standing, does that restoration depend on whether the blamer shares the same social role as the person being blamed, or is standing purely a function of one's present internal consistency?
