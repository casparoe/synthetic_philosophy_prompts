Write a sestina—six sestets followed by a three-line envoi—using the end-words decay, record, cause, boundary, flow, and origin in the standard rotating order. The poem should be spoken from the perspective of an observer inside a statistically isolated “reverse-arrow pocket”: a local region in which entropy decreases rather than increases, so that the inhabitants experience causal order running backward relative to ours. Use the cyclical repetition of the end-words to mirror the time-reversible microdynamics underlying both regions, but let the narrative progression show how, within the pocket, what the inhabitants call a record is for us an erasure of information, and what they call an origin is for us a high-entropy sink. In the envoi, bring in the past hypothesis as a conceptual anchor—not by naming specific authors or texts, but by stating that our global arrow depends on a singular low-entropy boundary condition, which renders the local inversion a statistical anomaly rather than an equally fundamental rule. The imagery should be concrete and the tone serious; avoid treating the arrow as merely subjective or illusory. Make the poem accurate enough that someone familiar with branch-system accounts of causal asymmetry and the statistical mechanics of records would recognize the conceptual structure, but do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to particular papers. Keep the form strict: thirty-nine lines total, with the envoi using all six end-words in any order.
