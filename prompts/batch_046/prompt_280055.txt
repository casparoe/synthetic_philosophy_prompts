I'm putting together my philosophical profile for an interdisciplinary workshop on interspecies welfare and consciousness attribution, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present. Here is roughly where I've landed. First, I treat phenomenal consciousness as distributed across the animal kingdom in degrees rather than as an all-or-nothing property; evolutionary continuity makes sharp metaphysical boundaries implausible, and I think it is defensible to attribute some form of conscious experience not only to mammals and birds but also to cephalopods and possibly decapod crustaceans. Second, in practice I justify these attributions through neural and behavioral homology—similar nociceptive pathways, sleep-like architectures, and flexible context-sensitive learning—treating these similarities as our best evidential bridge from human experience to other taxa. Third, I reject any reduction of phenomenal consciousness to functional organization or information integration; I regard the zombie-animal scenario as conceptually coherent, which means that functional similarity alone can never logically guarantee phenomenal experience. Fourth, despite that anti-reductionist commitment, I regularly invoke integrated information theory—specifically appeals to high Φ values in mammalian and avian brains—to support my attributions, and I treat these measures as more than mere heuristics. Fifth, I hold that primary, affective consciousness does not require self-consciousness, language, or narrative selfhood, so the absence of mirror-self-recognition in pigs or octopuses does not by itself undermine their claim to experience. Sixth, I maintain that legal and welfare frameworks necessarily require binary thresholds—animals either fall within a protection regime or they do not—so I defend sharp regulatory distinctions (for instance, protected status for cephalopods but not for insects) while insisting that these lines are pragmatic instruments rather than metaphysical discoveries.

Please audit this package. Which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe is each real tension? For each genuine tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me elsewhere in the set. Rank the tensions by severity. Finally, identify the single revision that would clean up the largest number of conflicts, and say whether adopting it forces me to abandon anything I clearly want to keep.
