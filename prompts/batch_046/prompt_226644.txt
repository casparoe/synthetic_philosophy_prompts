A philosopher of biology with whom I regularly discuss moral status assigns entities precise grades on a 0–10 scale rather than using binary categories. Her ratings are: adult humans 10; chimpanzees 8; pigs 6; octopuses 6; chickens 3; individual honeybees 1; a mature beehive considered as a collective agent 3; fish 1; earthworms 1; Venus flytraps 0; bimetallic-strip thermostats 0; and Physarum polycephalum navigating a maze 2. She rejects both pure sentience-threshold and pure suffering-capacity frameworks, claiming her scale tracks a single underlying property.

Predict her grade for a distributed mycorrhizal fungal network that connects hundreds of trees, adaptively reroutes nutrients around damage, and solves path-optimization problems no single fungus performs alone, yet possesses no neurons, ganglia, or centralized control. Identify at least two rules consistent with her full track record—one treating emergent functional integration as sufficient regardless of substrate, one requiring biological nociceptive or affective architecture—and say what grade each assigns here. Give your best-guess prediction with reasoning, then specify one further case (for instance, a neuromorphic silicon swarm with equivalent routing behavior, or a brain-dead human with intact spinal reflex arcs) that would best distinguish the competing rules.
