Give an example of a conceptual issue in lottocratic institutional design that is not reducible to the standard competence objection or the familiar accountability deficit. Specifically, construct a scenario involving a two-stage citizens’ assembly: the first tier is selected by lot and produces binding recommendations; the second tier is also selected by lot, but is constituted under an “epistemic firewall” in which members are screened for ignorance of the first tier’s reasoning and evidentiary record, so as to preserve statistical independence and cognitive diversity. Show how this design feature generates a tension between the representativeness rationale for sortition—which favors independence, non-correlation, and fresh judgment—and the deliberative-rationale conditions for legitimate collective reasoning, which require continuity of premises, mutual justification across stages, and a shared reasoning trajectory. In your example, isolate the exact structural feature that forces the choice: either the second tier cannot genuinely deliberate with the first, reducing the assembly to two disconnected statistical samples whose outputs lack deliberative coherence, or the firewall is weakened, collapsing the independence that justified separate selection in the first place. Keep the institutional details realistic enough for a contemporary democratic-reform proposal, avoid appeal to exotic technology or named theorists, and do not ask for bibliographies or citations; the task is to display the conceptual fracture itself, not to survey the literature.
