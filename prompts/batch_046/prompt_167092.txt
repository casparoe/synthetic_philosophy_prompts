Pragmatic encroachment claims that practical stakes can affect whether someone genuinely knows a proposition, whereas epistemic contextualism treats such variation as semantic: the word "knows" expresses different standards in different contexts. Since both frameworks predict that knowledge attributions will be withheld in high-stakes situations, I want to understand whether pragmatic encroachment can be maintained without collapsing into contextualism. In particular, if high stakes genuinely raise the evidential threshold for knowledge, doesn't that just show that the meaning of "knows" is context-sensitive? If not, what distinguishes a substantive change in the epistemic relation itself from a shift in the linguistic standard being applied?
