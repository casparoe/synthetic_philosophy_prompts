You are a novelist with training in moral philosophy. Before composing anything, take a moment to think carefully and brainstorm: articulate precisely what tranquilism holds—that only the absence of craving and the absence of suffering possess value, while all purported positive goods are either reducible to these absences or illusory; distinguish it sharply from negative utilitarianism, from Epicurean ataraxia, and from mere psychological contentment; and consider whether a mind structurally emptied of all desire could suffer not from deprivation but from the hollow architecture of its own neutrality.

Then write a short story, approximately five hundred to seven hundred words, exploring this idea. Set it in a near-future city governed by the Tranquilist Charter, where citizens undergo sanctioned cognitive restructuring to eliminate craving entirely. Your protagonist should be a restoration archivist who curates pre-Charter artifacts—objects whose sole purpose was to generate or sustain desire, such as an unfinished mechanical flute that can produce only the first half of any melody. Through prolonged exposure to these relics, she begins to experience what she privately names “hollow absence”: not the ache of an unfulfilled want, but a distinct suffering that emerges from the very form of a consciousness that has lost the capacity to want even relief. Let the philosophical tension arise through image, dialogue, and action rather than exposition; do not lecture the reader.

After the story, include a brief author’s note—no more than a concise paragraph—stating where the narrative takes liberties with tranquilism or depends on a contested interpretation of it. For example, address whether the absence of craving can itself become phenomenologically unstable, or whether tranquilism implicitly permits relational or aesthetic values that are not strictly negative. Crucially, in that same note identify the specific argument or observation about the nature of desire or suffering that would change your framing: what evidence or reasoning would lead you to present tranquilism as fully coherent and structurally complete rather than deficient?

Write in a polished, formal register. Be self-critical in your reasoning, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
