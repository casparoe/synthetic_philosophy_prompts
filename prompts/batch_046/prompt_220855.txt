Read Ken Kesey’s novel One Flew Over the Cuckoo’s Nest through the lens of Ian Hacking’s looping effects in psychiatric classification. Focus on how the ward’s diagnostic distinctions—such as acute versus chronic, or voluntary versus involuntary commitment—function not merely as descriptive labels but as interactive kinds that shape the patients’ self-understanding and conduct. Examine McMurphy’s rebellion against Nurse Ratched as a response to being classified: does his defiance represent an escape from the loop, or does the institution’s final intervention (his lobotomy) demonstrate that resistance itself can be reclassified as pathology, thereby closing the loop? Also consider where the novel resists a purely constructivist reading: does it allow that any of the psychiatric categories capture genuine differences in suffering or capacity, or does it treat all classification as coercive fiction? Finally, address a reflexive dimension: the novel itself has shaped popular and clinical images of psychiatric institutions and patient identity since its publication. Could the work be participating in the very looping dynamics it depicts, altering how real patients and practitioners understand the categories it dramatizes? Conclude by identifying what this specific literary case contributes to Hacking’s philosophical framework that a purely theoretical analysis of classification dynamics might miss.
