Design a 75-minute classroom activity for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on philosophy of the body and culture, 14 students, that lets participants experience—before anyone names the view—the force of Nietzsche’s idea that diet is not mere biological maintenance but a condition of philosophical style, specifically that what he calls great health requires an unstable, situation-sensitive calibration between bodily indulgence and ascetic discipline rather than any stable middle path. Give each student a hidden physiological profile drawn from Nietzsche’s documented patterns: for instance, a migraine-prone aphorist who needs darkness and dry air; a traveling philologist who craves heavy fare, beer, and long sedentary hours; or an Italianate convalescent sustained by pasta, light wine, and extended walking. Over a simulated three-day retreat, the group must allocate a shared pool of 30 digestive-energy tokens per day across meals, philosophical labor, and social interaction. Structure the payoff rules so that pure asceticism (zero indulgence tokens spent on food) collapses endurance scores to zero, pure hedonism (maximum indulgence) collapses judgment scores to zero, and high combined scores occur only when token distribution matches hidden climate and emotional-state modifiers revealed each round—so that the same allocation succeeds on day one and fails on day three. Provide the exact token-allocation mechanics, a non-monetary scoring rubric computing style quality multiplied by endurance, a minute-by-minute timeline for the 75 minutes, and facilitator instructions. The debrief should map each collapse point onto Nietzsche’s reflections on digestion, climate, and the stomach as an organ of judgment; predict the specific misreading—students interpreting the unstable optimum as Aristotelian moderation—and explain how to correct it in discussion; and be explicit about which feature of Nietzsche’s view, namely that a particular dietary error produces a particular philosophical error and can be cured by the correct menu, the simulation cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply directly. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; do not invoke theories outside Nietzsche’s widely known corpus; and do not include verbatim quotations from specific texts.
