I run a philosophy workshop with about 12 grad students, 90 minutes. Design an activity that makes them feel the pull and danger of Nietzsches three modes of history — monumental, antiquarian, critical — without a lecture up front. Frame it as a joint committee meeting: three groups must approve one public memorial for a fictional town whose archive was just destroyed in a flood. Give each group hidden victory conditions that mirror Nietzsches descriptions: the monumentalists want a heroic myth that inspires action, the antiquarians want every lost record reconstructed exactly, the critics want the past buried so the town can move on. Add an opaque scoring mechanic — call it vitality tokens — where every proposal either secretly adds or drains collective life-force, but nobody sees the full ledger until the end. Include clear rules, materials, a payoff scheme using points not money, a timeline, and a debrief that maps the committees moves onto the three modes and explains where each mode served or harmed life. Also say what will predictably go wrong the first run and how to fix it, and be honest about which feature of Nietzsches view — probably that critical history can genuinely liberate rather than just nihilistically destroy — the simulation cant reproduce and you must supply verbally. Keep it focused, no citations or reading lists needed, and feel free to inject one strange constraint or hidden rule to keep it from being too generic.
