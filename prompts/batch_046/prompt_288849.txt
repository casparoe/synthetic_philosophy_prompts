I just finished a long argument with a friend about whether DNA is literally a code or only a chemical metaphor. We kept returning to a concrete institutional pairing: in synthetic biology and patent doctrine, gene sequences are treated as readable, patentable instructions rather than mere molecules, while in clinical diagnostics the same mutations are called transmission errors or corrupted messages. What philosophical commitments about representation, intentionality, and causation does that dual practice presuppose? Does it require that DNA carries semantic content in a Gricean or teleosemantic sense, or can a purely syntactic Shannon-information framework sustain both moves? Reconstruct the implicit theory of biological information that makes them cohere; distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates; say whether it remains compatible with a purely causal, non-representational view of nucleic acids; and tell me what would have to change in patent eligibility rules or in experimental design if that representational commitment were dropped.
