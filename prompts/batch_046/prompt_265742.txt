Hey, I've been thinking about color-phi — you flash a red dot, then a green dot in a different spot, and people swear they saw greenish motion in between even though no green dot was ever shown there. There's a similar auditory illusion where two tones play in sequence and listeners report an intermediate pitch traveling between them. In both cases there's an argument about whether your conciousness of the first stimulus gets rewritten once the second arrives — like an Orwellian edit where the past experience is updated — or whether the intermediate was never present and just gets airbrushed in later, Stalinesque-style. The usual debates ask "when do you become aware of the middle?" but I'm stuck on a narrower framing: is there an established term for the disagreement over whether raw phenominal experience itself can be retroactively revised, as opposed to just being supplemented by a later narrative construction? It feels close to confabulation and to Dennett's multiple drafts, but those usually deal with belief or report, not with the actual sensory content being rewritten. Does this specific distinction — retroactive phenomenal editing versus post-hoc phenomenal insertion — have a name in philosophy of mind? And if there isn't one, how should we draw its edges so it doesn't just collapse into ordinary memory distortion?
