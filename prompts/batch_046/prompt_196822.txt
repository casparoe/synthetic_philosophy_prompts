I have volunteered to facilitate a session for an interdisciplinary reading group composed largely of humanities postgraduates who have recently encountered popular articles on behavioural genetics. They have begun to use phrases such as 'intelligence is 80 per cent heritable, therefore schooling is largely irrelevant,' and I wish to disabuse them of this inference without delivering a dry statistical lecture. Although I am not a specialist in quantitative genetics, I understand enough to see that the difficulty lies not in the arithmetic but in the conceptual dependency between variance in a population and destiny for an individual. I would like you to plan the pedagogical architecture for a single ninety-minute session rather than explaining the concept directly to me.

Please specify: the precise order in which the ideas must be introduced, explaining why the statistical definition of heritability must precede any discussion of 'nature versus nurture' and why the distinction between group-level variance and individual-level causation must come before any concrete human example; the single carrying example — I am inclined toward cloned plants cultivated in randomly assigned soil conditions, but advise me whether that is adequate or whether a human-trait illustration is indispensable, and detail what properties the example must possess (for instance, visible variation within a uniform genotype) and what properties it must avoid (for instance, implying that heritability measures biological inevitability); the misconceptions that this particular audience is likely to bring or construct, in the probable order of appearance — I anticipate at least the belief that heritability applies to individuals, the belief that a high heritability coefficient implies immutability, and perhaps a misunderstanding about twin studies being conclusive proof — together with the exact stage in the sequence at which each should be intercepted and neutralised; one diagnostic question I can pose at the close to determine whether they have genuinely understood the concept or have merely adopted a corrective slogan; and which technical apparatus — such as reaction norms, genotype-environment interaction, or the breeder's equation — should be explicitly excluded at this level, with a brief justification for each omission. Finally, please present your plan in British English.
